'''''The Jerk''''' is a 1979 American [[comedy film]] directed by [[Carl Reiner]] and written by [[Steve Martin]], [[Carl Gottlieb]], and [[Michael Elias]] (from a story by Martin and Gottlieb). This was Martin's first starring role in a feature film. The film also features [[Bernadette Peters]], [[M. Emmet Walsh]], [[Catlin Adams]], [[Maurice Evans (actor)|Maurice Evans]], and [[Jackie Mason]]. Critical reviews were mostly positive, and ''The Jerk'' was a major financial success.

==Plot==
Navin Johnson, a homeless person sleeping in a stairwell in [[Los Angeles]], addresses the camera directly to tell his life story.

The white adopted son of black [[Sharecropping|sharecroppers]] in [[Mississippi]], Navin grows to adulthood naïvely unaware of these circumstances. On his birthday his mother prepares him his favorite meal of tuna salad with mayonnaise on white bread, a Tab, and twinkies with candles. She prepares a traditional southern meal for the rest of the family. She then reveals he is adopted. He is unable to dance in rhythm to the [[Pick a Bale of Cotton|spirited folk songs]] played by the family but finds that he can do so perfectly to a [[Lawrence Welk|champagne-style song]] on the radio. Seeing this moment as a calling, he excitedly decides to leave home and travel to [[St. Louis]], where the broadcast originated. Along the way, he adopts a dog and names it "Shithead" after angering the guests at a motel by waking them up in the middle of the night, having misinterpreted the dog's barking at his door as a warning of a fire.

Navin finds a job as a gas station attendant, where he attempts to detain some thieves but accidentally destroys a nearby church. Later, a madman chooses his name at random from the telephone book and decides to kill him. As the gunman waits for an opportunity, Navin solders a brace to a customer's eyeglasses to stop them from slipping down his nose. The customer, Stan Fox, is an inventor who promises to try to market the device and split any profits with Navin. The gunman opens fire at Navin but misses, and Navin flees to the grounds of a traveling carnival.

Navin is hired as a weight guesser and is brusquely seduced by Patty Bernstein, an intimidating [[motorcycle stunt riding|daredevil motorcyclist]]. Later, while operating a [[ridable miniature railway|miniature railway]], he meets a [[cosmetology|cosmetologist]] named Marie Kimble and arranges a date with her. When a jealous Patty interrupts and starts to beat Navin, Marie easily knocks her unconscious. The two begin a relationship, and Navin decides to ask Marie to marry him. Before he can do so, though, she leaves him because he cannot provide financial security. Devastated, Navin takes Shithead and travels to Los Angeles. There, the gunman who tried to kill him—now sane and working as a [[private investigator]]—tracks him down and gives him a letter from Stan requesting a meeting.

Stan has been able to market Navin's device, now branded as the Opti-Grab, and gives him a check for $250,000 as the first installment on his share of the profits. Navin finds and marries Marie, and the two adopt a life of extravagant spending as his wealth continues to grow. However, Navin is soon named as defendant in a [[class action]] lawsuit brought by director [[Carl Reiner]] and millions of other Opti-Grab customers who have become permanently cross-eyed after using the device. Navin loses the suit and is ordered to pay $10 million in damages, leaving him broke, and he storms out into the street after an argument with Marie.

Having finished his story, Navin resigns himself to living in poverty, only to be found by his adoptive family, who have brought Marie and Shithead with them. The family has become wealthy by investing the money Navin sent them from time to time, and they take him and Marie home to live in their new house, which is nearly identical to their old shack but larger to scale and sturdier. Once again, Navin dances on the porch to folk songs, this time with perfect rhythm.

==Production==
[[File:Carl Reiner-1976.jpg|thumb|Director Carl Reiner (1976 photograph)]]
By 1977, comedian [[Steve Martin]] was experiencing wild success. He wanted to cross over to a film career, believing it promised more longevity.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=188}} Basing his film proposal on a line from his act — "It wasn't always easy for me; I was born a poor black child" — he fleshed out his ideas into a series of notes he intended to deliver to studios. With confidence in his budding standup career, he imagined it would not be difficult to break into Hollywood. Instead, he found it more difficult than expected.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=188}} Bill McEuen was acquainted with [[Paramount Pictures]] president [[David Picker]], and convinced Picker to sign Martin for a three-year contract at the studio after showing him one of his routines at the Boarding House comedy club in San Francisco.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=The Jerk |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/65077-THE-JERK?cxt=filmography |access-date=2023-04-21 |website=AFI Catalog}}</ref>

Martin passed along his notes, which the studio read carefully. It described a series of odd jobs lead character "Steve" would hold in his saga. Martin developed this concept into the first draft of ''The Jerk'', but Paramount passed on the project after Picker left the studio.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=189}} However, Picker convinced his new employer Universal Studios to sign a distribution deal for the film, which would leave Martin in creative control of the project. Martin was able to pick which director he wanted to work with. Martin initially chose [[Mike Nichols]], but Nichols then "bowed out" of the project in 1978. Martin's next choice was [[Carl Reiner]], famous for his work on ''[[The Dick Van Dyke Show]]''.<ref name=":0" />{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=190}}

The duo met constantly, and the film's title grew out of their conversations. Martin recounts in his memoir:<blockquote>It needs to be something short, yet have the feeling of an epic tale. Like [[Dostoyevsky]]'s ''[[The Idiot]]'', but not that. Like ''The Jerk''.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=190}}</blockquote>

Martin wrote the part of "Marie" with [[Bernadette Peters]] in mind.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Martin |first=Frank W.|url=http://www.people.com/people/archive/article/0,,20075645,00.html |title=The Jerk Made Detractors Eat Crow |date=January 21, 1980 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081020113428/http://www.people.com/people/archive/article/0,,20075645,00.html |archive-date=October 20, 2008 |magazine=[[People (magazine)|People]] |url-status=dead}}</ref> He adapted several bits of his standup act to fit within the film, such as a monologue in which he emotionally exits a scene, remarking "I don't need anything", but nevertheless picking up each object he passes on his way out.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=190}} In co-writing the script with [[Carl Gottlieb]] and [[Michael Elias]], their goal was to provide a laugh on each page of the screenplay.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=191}}

Principal photography took 45 days using 85 locations in [[Greater Los Angeles]], on a $4.6 million budget. This included [[Mohammed al Fassi]]'s mansion in [[Beverly Hills, California|Beverly Hills]], [[Pasadena, California|Pasadena]], [[Westlake Village, California|Westlake Village]] and [[Devonshire Downs]] in [[Northridge, Los Angeles|Northridge]].<ref name=":0" /> In shooting the film, Reiner "ran a joyful set", according to Martin, with the cast and crew eating lunch together each day.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=192}} Martin's favorite moment of the film, as he detailed in his 2007 memoir ''[[Born Standing Up]]'', was the scene in which he and Peters sing "Tonight You Belong to Me". Martin felt the moment was touching, and waited in anticipation at the film's premiere screening in [[St. Louis]]. Unfortunately, much of the audience left during the scene to buy more popcorn.{{sfn|Martin|2007|p=192}}

===Deleted scenes===
A scene in which [[Bill Murray]] was to have made a cameo was cut from the final film.<ref>{{cite web|last=Locke|first=Greg W.|title=The Top 25 Roles Bill Murray Didn't Take|date=26 August 2011|url=http://www.zecatalist.com/lists/the-top-25-roles-bob-murray-didnt-take/|access-date=2015-05-25|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111125110247/http://www.zecatalist.com/lists/the-top-25-roles-bob-murray-didnt-take/|archive-date=25 November 2011 |website=Ze Catalist}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Evans|first=Bradford|title=The Lost Roles of Bill Murray|date=17 February 2011|url=http://splitsider.com/2011/02/the-lost-roles-of-bill-murray/|access-date=25 May 2015|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150520115025/http://splitsider.com/2011/02/the-lost-roles-of-bill-murray|archive-date=20 May 2015 |website=Splitsider}}</ref> An alternate, comic introduction of Marie (Peters), near the train ride Navin was running at the carnival, was shot. When her nephew takes off on the train, Navin rescues him, and in returning the boy to Marie, has the bill of his engineer's cap pulled down over his eyes so he cannot see the toy village he (Navin) destroys like a lunatic. 

Another scene that was cut featured [[Gailard Sartain]] as a Texas oil millionaire who tearfully begs Navin for money to replace the cracked, dried-out leather seats on his private jet. Navin grants his request and he gratefully states: "Now I can fly my friends to the [[Super Bowl]] like a MAN, and not some damned BUM!"<ref>{{Cite AV media |date=Sep 10, 2013 |first=Jeff |last=Whitehead |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h76RntKeWu0 |title=Gailard Sartain in The Jerk - Lonnie Don McGinty |access-date=January 3, 2020 |archive-date=February 14, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210214112309/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h76RntKeWu0 |url-status=live |via=[[YouTube]] }}</ref> 

The television version features a scene in which a forlorn Navin, trying to forget Marie, spends six hours on the [[Round Up (ride)|Round Up]] carnival ride. The boss orders the ride stopped, and Navin is removed by two carnival workers, who sit him down on the ride's stairs. "What are you looking at?", he asks them. "Haven't you seen a man so broken he needed to spin?"

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film was a hit, earning over $73 million domestically,<ref name="box office mojo">{{cite web |url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=jerk.htm |title=Box Office Information for ''The Jerk'' |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=2012-01-28 |archive-date=January 28, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120128183848/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=jerk.htm |url-status=live }}</ref> and making the movie the [[1979 in film|eighth highest-grossing of 1979]] and $100 million worldwide, having been produced on a relatively low budget of $4 million.<ref name="brummel" />

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 82% based on 44 reviews, with an average rating of 6.9/10. The site's critical consensus reads: "Crude, crass, and oh so quotable, ''The Jerk'' is nothing short of an all-out comedic showcase for Steve Martin."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/jerk/ |title=The Jerk |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=16 January 2024 |archive-date=25 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220425004017/https://rottentomatoes.com/m/jerk |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film received a score of 61 based on 14 reviews, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Jerk |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-jerk |website=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref>

Janet Maslin of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote that ''The Jerk'' "is by turns funny, vulgar and backhandedly clever, never more so than when it aspires to absolute stupidity. And Mr. Martin, who began his career with an arrow stuck through his head, has since developed a real genius for playing dumb&nbsp;... Even when it's crude—which is quite a lot of the time—it's not mean-spirited&nbsp;... Mr. Martin and his co-star, Bernadette Peters, work very sweetly together, even when they sing a duet of '[[Tonight You Belong to Me]],' carrying sweetness to what could easily have become an intolerable extreme."<ref>{{cite web |last=Maslin |first=Janet |author-link=Janet Maslin |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1979/12/14/archives/movie-steve-martin-stars-in-the-jerkbirthday-surprise.html |title=Movie: Steve Martin Stars in 'The Jerk': Birthday Surprise |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=December 14, 1979 |access-date=July 30, 2020 |archive-date=September 25, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200925183014/https://www.nytimes.com/1979/12/14/archives/movie-steve-martin-stars-in-the-jerkbirthday-surprise.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of ''The Chicago Sun-Times'' gave the film a mixed review, stating ''The Jerk'' was "all gags and very little comedy" and built of "gag situations [which] are milked for one-time laughs. They don't grow out of his character, or contribute to it."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-jerk-1979 | title=The Jerk movie review & film summary (1979) &#124; Roger Ebert }}</ref> In a retrospective review published in 2000, the [[BBC]] rated the movie two out of five stars and described the film as an "early watered-down version of the crude comedy the Farrelly Brothers would later take to new extremes" and made references to it having similar themes to the early 1980s parody film ''[[Airplane!]]''<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2000/11/21/the_jerk_1979_review.shtml |title=The Jerk (1979) |last=Haflidason |first=Almar |date=21 November 2000 |website=[[BBC]] }}</ref> [[MusicHound|VideoHound's Golden Movie Retriever]] reviewed the film for its book and rated the movie as being two and a half stars. The author of the review referred to Steve Martin's silly, exaggerated acting as complementary to the early comedian [[Jerry Lewis]].<ref>{{cite book|chapter=The Jerk|title=VideoHound's Golden Movie Retriever|first1=Jim|last1=Craddock|publisher=2007 ed., Gale, 2006|page=457}}</ref>

==Legacy==
''The Jerk'' has been praised as not only one of Martin's best comedic efforts, but also one of the funniest films ever made. In 2000, readers of ''[[Total Film]]'' voted ''The Jerk'' the 48th greatest comedy film of all time. This film is No.&nbsp;20 on [[Bravo (U.S. TV network)|Bravo]]'s ''100 Funniest Movies''<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.boston.com/ae/movies/bravo-funniest-movies-all-time/zD1lgXmwCYi6WZUm1JQ53H/article.html#slide-7 |title=Bravo's 100 Funniest Movies of All Time |work=[[Boston.com]] |date=2016 |access-date=August 22, 2016 |archive-date=March 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305005616/http://www.boston.com/ae/movies/bravo-funniest-movies-all-time/zD1lgXmwCYi6WZUm1JQ53H/article.html#slide-7 |url-status=dead }}</ref> and No.&nbsp;89 on the [[American Film Institute]] list [[AFI's 100 Years...100 Laughs]].<ref>{{cite web |title=AFI's 100 Years...100 Laughs |url=http://www.afi.com/Docs/100Years/laughs100.pdf |publisher=[[American Film Institute]] |date=2002 |access-date=August 22, 2016 |archive-date=March 16, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130316140859/http://afi.com/Docs/100Years/laughs100.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref>  ''[[IGN]]'' ranked the film as the tenth top comedy film of all time.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ign.com/articles/2012/03/13/top-25-comedies-of-all-time?page=16|title=Top 25 Comedies of All-Time, page 16|website=[[IGN]]|date=March 13, 2012|access-date=March 13, 2013|archive-date=March 9, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130309011343/http://www.ign.com/articles/2012/03/13/top-25-comedies-of-all-time?page=16|url-status=live}}</ref> 
''[[Premiere (magazine)|Premiere]]'' voted Steve Martin's performance of Navin Johnson No.&nbsp;99 on their list, "The 100 Greatest Performances of All Time".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://www.filmsite.org/100greatperformances.html|title=100 Greatest Movie Performances of All Time |magazine=[[Première (magazine)|Première]]|via=[[Filmsite.org]]|access-date=February 19, 2015|archive-date=March 3, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303225539/http://www.filmsite.org/100greatperformances.html|url-status=live}}</ref> A BBC poll of more than 250 critics rated the film as the 99th greatest comedy of all time.<ref>{{Cite web| url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20170821-the-100-greatest-comedies-of-all-time| title=The 100 greatest comedies of all time| website=BBC| date=August 22, 2017| access-date=August 29, 2017| archive-date=January 11, 2018| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180111175543/http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20170821-the-100-greatest-comedies-of-all-time| url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Christiane Kubrick]] hailed it as one of [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s favorite films of all time.  It led to Kubrick meeting with Martin to discuss working together on his film ''[[Eyes Wide Shut]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Barfield |first=Charles |date=July 6, 2018 |title=Steve Martin Discusses Being Pitched 'Eyes Wide Shut' By Stanley Kubrick |url=https://theplaylist.net/steve-martin-discusses-pitched-eyes-wide-shut-stanley-kubrick-20180706/ |access-date=June 30, 2020 |website=The Playlist }}</ref>

==Sequel==
''The Jerk'' had a [[television film]] [[sequel]], ''[[The Jerk, Too]]'' (1984), starring [[Mark Blankfield]] as Navin and co-starring [[Stacey Nelkin]]. Steve Martin was executive producer, but did not write the screenplay.<ref>{{cite web|first=Erik|last=Davis|url=http://blog.moviefone.com/2010/04/02/yes-these-exist-splash-too-and-the-jerk-too/|title=Yes, These Exist: 'Splash Too' and 'The Jerk, Too'|website=[[Moviefone]]|date=2010-04-02|access-date=May 25, 2012|archive-date=September 17, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120917065935/http://blog.moviefone.com/2010/04/02/yes-these-exist-splash-too-and-the-jerk-too|url-status=dead}}</ref>
