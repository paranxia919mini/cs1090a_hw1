'''''Yen and Ai-Lee''''' ({{zh|t=小雁與吳愛麗|link=no}}) is a 2024 Taiwanese [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] directed and written by [[Tom Lin Shu-yu]]. Starring [[Kimi Hsia]], [[Yang Kuei-mei]], [[Sam Tseng]], and [[Ng Ki-pin]], the film explores themes of [[domestic violence]] and follows the reconciliation between a young woman (Hsia), who murders her abusive father, and her mother (Yang).

Marking his fifth feature film, director-screenwriter Tom Lin Shu-yu conceived the project to collaborate with his wife Kimi Hsia, with the protagonist's character arc basing on Hsia's real life experiences. Lin began writing the screenplay in 2022 and production began in 2023, primarily filming in [[Kaohsiung]] and shot entirely in [[black-and-white]].

The film had its world premiere in competition at the [[29th Busan International Film Festival]] on 5 October 2024, where it won the [[Kim Jiseok Award]], making it the first Taiwanese film to win in this category. It was then release theatrically in Taiwan on 10 October. The film received eight nominations in the [[61st Golden Horse Awards]], with Yang Kuei-mei winning [[Golden Horse Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]].

== Plot ==
The film begins in [[Meinong District|Meinong]] with a [[long take]] of Yen soaking in blood as she cycles to a police station to turn herself in after murdering her abusive father to protect her mother Ai-Lee. Eight years later, Yen is released from prison and returns to a new house where her mother welcomes her and shows her the new bedroom. She then meets her mother's new boyfriend, Ren, a rude local thug whom Yen immediately despises. A young woman (who looks identical to Yen) attends a drama class at a [[community college]] and introduces herself as Allie. Befriending some classmates, she learns that their drama teacher's real job is as a "[[filial piety]] daughter", actresses hired by families to [[Filial mourning|mourn at funerals]]. Allie excels in her class and gains confidence through her performances.

Yen is contacted by a woman claiming to be her deceased father's mistress, who urges her to take care of her younger half-brother, Wei, as she must leave town for a month. Although Yen refuses, the woman leaves Wei outside Ai-Lee's grocery store. Yen initially drives Wei back and leaves him outside his home, but feeling pity for the boy, she returns and takes him to the house of Cheng, a former classmate she has recently reunited with, asking Cheng to care for him. Although Cheng agrees, his father is dissatisfied and asks Yen to take the boy away as soon as possible. From uncollected letters left outside the mistress's home, Yen discovers the woman used to work at a community college and tries to inquire about her there, only to find out she resigned over a month ago. While leaving, Yen is drawn to a drama class advertisement. Although reluctantly, Yen begins to bond with Wei while caring for him.

A month passes, and Ren brings his friends to Ai-Lee's grocery store. He takes some [[scratchcard]]s hidden inside a cabinet, claiming that those are real while the ones in the cupboards are fake. Yen confronts Ai-Lee, asking why she sells fake scratchcards. Ai-Lee claims this is to make money and insists she is not wrong, instead blaming Yen for ruining their family. Angered, Yen packs her things and leaves home. Wei refuses to go with her, believing his mother will come to pick him up the next day since it marks a full month. When Wei's mother does not show up, the boy finally realizes that she has abandoned him. Unwillingly, Ai-Lee feels sorry for Wei and begins to take care of him. Meanwhile, Yen moves to [[Kaohsiung]] and finds a job at a laundry factory. She changes her appearance from a rural girl to a city girl, restyling her hair which resembles Allie's, revealing that they are the same person.

Ren arrives at the grocery store and discovers that Ai-Lee has stopped selling fake scratchcards. He threatens Ai-Lee to continue selling them, but she firmly refuses and slaps him. Triggered, Ren tries to kill Ai-Lee, and is only stopped by a passing policeman. Meanwhile, in the drama class, students are randomly assigned costumes and must perform accordingly. Allie is assigned a [[Mourning#East Asia|white mourning cloth]]. Initially wanting to refuse, she is encouraged by the teacher and continues to perform. Allie soon becomes overwhelmed with emotion, imagining herself talking to her deceased father at the funeral and expressing that she does not want to hate him or her mother but does not know how to move on without the hatred. After this emotional performance, she finally finds peace within herself and reveals her real name, Yen, to the teacher.

Upon learning her mother is at the police station, Yen returns to Meinong and bails her. At last, Ai-Lee tells Yen that Ren has moved out, revealing that she stood up to him in hopes of bringing her back home, while Yen finally dares to discuss her father with Ai-Lee.

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] took place in 2023 in [[Kaohsiung]], Taiwan, spanning approximately three to four weeks.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.ctwant.com/article/367397/ |title=《小雁與吳愛麗》影展首映口碑熱烈 導演將高雄美濃美景帶到國際 |date=5 October 2024 |website=[[Want China Times]] |author=吳庭宜 |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh |archive-date=5 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241005132122/https://www.ctwant.com/article/367397/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="FilmaholicOctober2024" /> The film was shot by Indian cinematographer [[Kartik Vijay]], who previously collaborated with Tom Lin Shu-yu on ''[[The Garden of Evening Mists (film)|The Garden of Evening Mist]]'' (2019). Lin invited Vijay to join the project to present Taiwan from a foreign perspective and to leverage Vijay's keen attention to detail.<ref name="FilmaholicOctober2024" /> The shoot took place during Kaohsiung's rainy season, which required adapting many scenes to a rainy setting, including rewriting the opening sequence as a [[long take]] due to rain during filming, and production was delayed for two days due to a typhoon.<ref name="TatlerOctober2024" /><ref name="FilmaholicOctober2024" /> Lin also proposed to shoot the climax scene in long takes, filming it in seven versions with different dialogues, while noting that the number of lines decreased version by version because he found them inappropriate for the performance.<ref name="PTSOctober2024" /> Most of the filming took place in the [[Meinong District]],<ref name="TatlerOctober2024" /> with additional location shoots in the city center and [[Cishan District]].<ref name="TaiSoundsOctober2024">{{Cite web |url=https://www.taisounds.com/news/content/107/151718 |title=100%高雄拍攝！《小雁與吳愛麗》楊貴媚、夏于喬化身母女檔感動說愛 |date=11 October 2024 |website=Tai Sounds |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref>

== Release ==
''Yen and Ai-Lee'' had its world premiere in competition for the [[Kim Jiseok Award]] at the [[29th Busan International Film Festival]] on 5 October 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/global/busan-festival-brillante-mendoza-rima-das-tom-lin-1236119105/ |title=Brillante Mendoza, Rima Das, Tom Lin Latest Titles Light up Busan's Jiseok Section |date=26 August 2024 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |last=Frater |first=Patrick |access-date=14 October 2024}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.cna.com.tw/news/amov/202410050143.aspx |title=「小雁與吳愛麗」細膩詮釋弒父女孩心境 與創傷和解 |date=5 October 2024 |website=[[Central News Agency (Taiwan)|Central News Agency]] |author=廖禹揚 |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh |archive-date=7 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007020324/https://www.cna.com.tw/news/amov/202410050143.aspx |url-status=live }}</ref> Winning the award, the jury described the film as "an unfinishing and bold portrayal of a traumatic mother and daughter relationship with powerful, beautiful performance" in the awards announcement statement.<ref name="FocusTaiwanOctober2024">{{Cite web |url=https://focustaiwan.tw/culture/202410110018 |title=Taiwanese film wins top prize at Busan Int'l Film Festival |date=11 October 2024 |website=[[Focus Taiwan]] |last1=Liao |first1=Yu-yang |last2=Lai |first2=Sunny |access-date=15 October 2024}}</ref> The film had an early screening on 8 October,<ref name="CNAOctober2024" /> and was released theatrically in Taiwan on 10 October.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.chinatimes.com/realtimenews/20240917002655-260404?chdtv |title=專訪／夏于喬合作導演尪嗆「根本不會導戲」 曝與母親愛恨並存 |date=17 September 2024 |website=[[China Times]] |author=李承陽 |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref>

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Yen and Ai-Lee'' grossed only NTD$195,000 on its opening day, which lead actress Kimi Hsia described as "discouraging and disappointing".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.setn.com/news/1546339?utm_campaign=viewallnews |title=夏于喬新片入圍金馬票房卻慘兮兮！她深夜發文痛哭：看過的人少之又少 |date=14 October 2024 |website=[[SET News]] |author=張尚辰 |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.chinatimes.com/realtimenews/20241014002427-260404?chdtv |title=夏于喬新作入圍8項金馬票房卻奇差 氣餒哭了：知道的人少之又少 |date=14 October 2024 |website=[[China Times]] |author=黃鈺晴 |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref> Following positive reviews, the box office performance in the second week saw a surge of 20%, bringing the total gross to NTD$2.1 million.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://stars.udn.com/star/story/10090/8306234 |title=看完「小雁與吳愛麗」全場哭！夏于喬謝恩：顏色不重要 |date=21 October 2024 |website=[[United Daily News]] |author=陳穎 |access-date=22 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://ent.ltn.com.tw/news/breakingnews/4837875 |title=《小雁與吳愛麗》口碑逆襲票房升！戲后楊貴媚加持「全場掌聲」 |date=21 October 2024 |website=[[Liberty Times]] |author=鍾志均 |access-date=22 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
Nikki Baughan of ''[[Screen International]]'' commended ''Yen and Ai-Lee'' as a tragic tale that "benefits greatly from performances from Kimi Hsia and Yang Kuei-mei" and "monochromatic visuals throw the emotion of the story into sharp relief", particularly the "black-and-white aesthetic" and "warm production design", throughout which "Lin paints evocatively in light and shadow, emphasising moments of pain but also of hope as the pair learns to reconnect and slowly overcome the mistakes of the past".<ref name="ScreenDailyReview">{{Cite web |url=https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/yen-and-ai-lee-busan-review/5197973.article |title='Yen And Ai-Lee': Busan Review |date=9 October 2024 |website=[[Screen Daily]] |last=Baughan |first=Nikki |access-date=14 October 2024 |archive-date=10 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241010033327/https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/yen-and-ai-lee-busan-review/5197973.article |url-status=live }}</ref> Alan Chu of ''[[United Daily News]]'' also wrote on the film's choice of shooting in black-and-white, stating that it "intentionally creates visual and narrative purity" and that Tom Lin "worked on the visuals, directly imbuing the film with a gray, melancholy and oppressive atmosphere", "as if the characters are struggling to survive in this world, desperately searching for the colors of life".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://reading.udn.com/read/story/7044/8273754 |title=《小雁與吳愛麗》影評：夏于喬迎來演技巔峰，楊貴媚、曾國城動人傳神 |date=8 October 2024 |website=[[United Daily News]] |last=Chu |first=Alan |access-date=14 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref>

Chang Wan-hsuan, writing for ''[[The News Lens]]'', described the film as "unexpectedly good" and praised Kimi Hsia's performance in the drama lesson scenes as "Golden Horse Award-worthy", noting that the scene's "claustrophobic close-up shots and large [[Headscarf|headscarves]] limited the actress's performance to the center of the frame", "making the audience watch how Kimi Hsia transforms from playing Yen into becoming the character herself, using a very full shot to explode the emotional tension of the character's repression".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.thenewslens.com/article/243249 |title=【影評】《小雁與吳愛麗》：讓我從「表演課」說明，為什麼夏于喬值得一座金馬獎最佳女主角？ |date=16 October 2024 |website=[[The News Lens]] |last=Chang |first=Wan-hsuan |access-date=16 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref> Cain Noble-Davies of ''[[FilmInk]]'' rated the film 8/10 and also considered the film "a powerful look at trauma, forgiveness, and the many roles that people play in life", writing that "Tom Lin Shu-yu's writing and Kimi Hsia's magnificent dual performances to present what feels like an honest, earnest, and relentlessly inquisitive snapshot of a real world, where hope can be discarded like flakes from a scratchie ticket, or soak itself in tears from under a funeral veil".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.filmink.com.au/reviews/yen-and-ai-lee/ |title=Yen and Ai-Lee |date=27 July 2025 |website=[[FilmInk]] |last=Noble-Davies |first=Cain |access-date=11 April 2026}}</ref>

== Awards and nominations ==
''Yen and Ai-Lee'' is the first Taiwanese film to win a [[Kim Jiseok Award]] at the [[Busan International Film Festival]].<ref name="FocusTaiwanOctober2024" /><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.rti.org.tw/news/view/id/2223688 |title=興奮《小雁與吳愛麗》釜山影展奪大獎 李遠力推：所有父母、子女都該看 |date=11 October 2024 |website=[[Radio Taiwan International]] |author=江昭倫 |access-date=15 October 2024 |language=zh}}</ref> It also received eight nominations in the [[61st Golden Horse Awards]], making it the second most-nominated film of the year.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://focustaiwan.tw/culture/202410020019 |title=Taiwanese horror comedy leads Golden Horse nominations |date=2 October 2024 |website=[[Focus Taiwan]] |last=Lin |first=Sean |access-date=16 October 2024 |archive-date=7 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007101807/https://focustaiwan.tw/culture/202410020019 |url-status=live }}</ref>
