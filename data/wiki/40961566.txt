'''''Something Special''''' (also known as '''''Willy/Milly''''' and '''''I Was a Teenage Boy''''')<ref>{{cite news|title=Rancid attitudes give shape to the fantasy in 'Special'|url=http://nl.newsbank.com/nl-search/we/Archives?p_product=AT&p_theme=at&p_action=search&p_maxdocs=200&p_topdoc=1&p_text_direct-0=0EB7C261B0B93DE9&p_field_direct-0=document_id&p_perpage=10&p_sort=YMD_date:D&s_trackval=GooglePM|access-date=3 November 2013|newspaper=The Atlanta Journal and The Atlanta Constitution|date=November 21, 1986}}</ref> is a 1986 comedic fantasy film directed by [[Paul Schneider (director)|Paul Schneider]], based upon a short story by the same name by [[Alan H. Friedman]]. The film follows the title character of Milly Niceman, a young [[tomboy]] who is unhappy with the traditional expectations for her gender and longs to become a boy so she can act however she wants. Initially released as ''I Was a Teenage Boy'' in 1986, the film was later re-titled to ''Something Special'' for a 1987 limited theatrical release and for a VHS release.<ref name="LAT" /><ref>{{cite web|title=I Was a Teenage Boy (1986)|url=https://www.nytimes.com/movies/movie/45581/I-Was-a-Teenage-Boy/overview|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131114110048/http://www.nytimes.com/movies/movie/45581/I-Was-a-Teenage-Boy/overview|url-status=dead|archive-date=14 November 2013|department=Movies & TV Dept.|work=[[The New York Times]]|author=Paul Brenner|date=2013|access-date=4 November 2013}}</ref>

==Plot==
Milly Niceman is a fourteen-year-old girl who has grown frustrated with her mother's attempts to curtail her tomboyish nature. Her mother, Doris, believes that Milly's actions are inappropriate for a girl, and that she'd be better off showing more interest in dances and dresses. When her best friend's younger brother, Malcolm, sells her a spell that will grant her deepest wish, that will only work during the upcoming eclipse, Milly jumps at the chance. She performs the spell, and believes that it has failed. The next morning, Milly discovers that not only was the spell successful, but it turned her into a boy. She tells her family about what happened, and to her surprise, her father encourages her to explore her new masculinity.

As a result, Milly changes her name to "Willy", and begins attending a new school as a transfer student. Milly initially takes well to her new persona and gender, but eventually becomes conflicted when she begins to realize that life isn't necessarily easier as a boy and that she has feelings for her friend Alfie, who is equally confused about his feelings for "Willy". By the film's end, Milly decides to become female once more. Malcolm sells Milly a wishing stone that she is to throw at her favorite star at 12:03 during a star shower. She turns back into a girl again.

==Reception==
''[[Providence Journal]]'' commented that the film could have worked it if had been "played for hilarious farce a la ''[[Tootsie]]'', or as an examination of the psychological traumas that [Milly]/Willy undergoes".<ref>{{cite news|last=Janusonsis|first=Michael|title=Boy, oh boy Milly goes Willy in fantasy film that's too silly|url=https://providencejournal.newsbank.com/doc/news/15252D72DAF64090|newspaper=Providence Journal|date=Jul 22, 1986}} {{subscription required}}</ref> The ''[[Sun-Sentinel]]'' panned the film as "so shallow, boring and half-baked that one wonders if anyone looked at the finished product before distribution".<ref name="Hurlburt">{{cite web|last1=Hurlburt |first1=Roger |title=Comedy 'Willy/Milly' Really Silly |url=https://www.sun-sentinel.com/news/fl-xpm-1986-05-02-8601260901-story.html |website=Sun Sentinel |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200322030010/https://www.sun-sentinel.com/news/fl-xpm-1986-05-02-8601260901-story.html |archive-date=22 March 2020 |language=en |date=May 2, 1986}}</ref> The ''[[Daily News of Los Angeles]]'' and ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' both gave mixed reviews for the ''Willy/Milly'', with the ''Los Angeles Times'' remarking that while the film "doesn't really function well as a comedy" it did have some appeal.<ref name="LAT">{{cite news|last=WILMINGTON|first=MICHAEL|title='Something'--almost Something Special|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-04-06-ca-80-story.html|access-date=3 November 2013|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=April 6, 1987}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title='SOMETHING SPECIAL': PREDICTABLE BUT CUTE|url=http://nl.newsbank.com/nl-search/we/Archives?p_product=LA&p_theme=la&p_action=search&p_maxdocs=200&p_topdoc=1&p_text_direct-0=0EF513A36759BFDB&p_field_direct-0=document_id&p_perpage=10&p_sort=YMD_date:D&s_trackval=GooglePM|access-date=3 November 2013|newspaper=Daily News of Los Angeles|date=April 8, 1987}}</ref>
