'''''I Saw the TV Glow''''' is a 2024 American [[psychological horror film|psychological horror]] [[psychological drama|drama]] film written and directed by [[Jane Schoenbrun]]. The film stars [[Justice Smith]] and [[Jack Haven]]{{efn|name=Jack}} as two troubled high school students whose connection to their favorite television show drives them to question their reality and identities. The supporting cast includes [[Helena Howard]], [[Snail Mail (musician)|Lindsey Jordan]], [[Conner O'Malley]], [[Emma Portner]], Ian Foreman, [[Fred Durst]], and [[Danielle Deadwyler]].

Actress [[Emma Stone]] and her husband, comedian [[Dave McCary]], are among the film's producers under their [[Fruit Tree (company)|Fruit Tree]] company. The film premiered at the [[2024 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 18, 2024. It was given a limited theatrical release by [[A24]] in the United States on May 3, with its theatrical release expanding to Canada and nationwide in the US on May 17. ''I Saw the TV Glow'' received positive reviews from critics and received five nominations at the [[40th Independent Spirit Awards]], including [[Independent Spirit Award for Best Film|Best Feature]].

The film marks the second entry in what Schoenbrun refers to as their{{efn|Schoenbrun uses "[[they/them]]" pronouns.}} ''Screen Trilogy'', following the film ''[[We're All Going to the World's Fair]]'' (2021) and preceding ''[[Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma]]'' (2026),<ref>{{Cite web |last=Castillo |first=Monica |date=August 7, 2026 |title=Jane Schoenbrun has finally found their audience and themself over their Screen trilogy |url=https://www.avclub.com/jane-schoenbrun-screen-trilogy-sex-and-death-worlds-fair-tv-glow |access-date=2026-08-29 |website=AV Club |language=en-US}}</ref> and is thematically tied to their novel ''Public Access Afterworld''.<ref name="SchoenbrunVariety">{{cite magazine |last=Chapman |first=Wilson |date=April 14, 2022 |title=Director Jane Schoenbrun on the Extremely Online, Gender Dysphoric Coming-of-Age Journey in ''We're All Going to the World's Fair'' |url=https://variety.com/2022/film/news/jane-schoenbrun-were-all-going-to-the-worlds-fair-1235220775/ |access-date=July 2, 2024 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |archive-date=June 13, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240613031127/https://variety.com/2022/film/news/jane-schoenbrun-were-all-going-to-the-worlds-fair-1235220775/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Zilko |first=Christian |url=https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/jane-schoenbrun-announces-debut-novel-public-access-afterworld-1235012440/ |title=Jane Schoenbrun Sets Debut Novel 'Public Access Afterworld' at Hogarth Books |website=[[IndieWire]] |date=June 5, 2024 |access-date=August 30, 2024 |archive-date=August 30, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240830214422/https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/jane-schoenbrun-announces-debut-novel-public-access-afterworld-1235012440/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Plot==
In 1996, isolated teenagers Owen and Maddy bond over the [[young adult]] television show ''The Pink Opaque'', which follows teenagers Isabel and Tara as they use their [[psychic]] connection to fight supervillain Mr. Melancholy, who has the power to warp time and reality. Due to the show airing past his bedtime and his father's disapproval of him watching it, Owen sneaks out to Maddy's house to watch it with her; she also provides him with [[Videotape|tapes]] of the show when he misses it. Maddy feels deeply connected to ''The Pink Opaque'', claiming that it feels more real to her than her actual life. Two years later, while watching ''The Pink Opaque'' with Owen, Maddy begins sobbing inexplicably, later explaining that she has resolved to run away. She expects Owen to join her, but he finds himself unable to go through with it and stays behind. Later, Maddy goes missing, Owen's mother Brenda dies of cancer, and ''The Pink Opaque'' is canceled after five seasons. 
 
In 2006, Owen still lives with his father, Frank, and works at a local movie theater. Maddy suddenly reappears one night and takes Owen to a bar where it's "safe" for them to talk. She rebuffs Owen's pleas for her to contact the police, instead questioning what Owen remembers of ''The Pink Opaque'' and claiming to have spent the past two years inside the show itself. At her urging, Owen rewatches the final episode, in which Mr. Melancholy captures Isabel and Tara and imprisons their consciousnesses in a [[pocket universe]] known as the "Midnight Realm" which bears a strong resemblance to the real world. Mr. Melancholy's minions then bury Isabel and Tara alive before the episode abruptly ends. After finishing the episode, Owen suffers a nervous breakdown and attempts to stick his head through the TV screen before Frank pulls him out.
 
The next night, Maddy explains to Owen that, after running away, she continued to feel unsatisfied and paid a man to bury her alive; after suffocating, she awoke in ''The Pink Opaque'' as Tara. She explains that they are in fact in the Midnight Realm and that their true identities are those of Tara and Isabel, respectively; the memories of their actual lives have manifested as episodes of a TV show. Maddy then encourages Owen to allow her to bury him alive so that they may return to their reality. She leads Owen to a football field to be buried.  To her disappointment, he pushes her away and rushes home. While Owen never sees her again, he remains haunted by her claims. 
 
By 2010, Owen is living alone after Frank's death from a [[stroke]]. One night, he rewatches ''The Pink Opaque'' on a [[Streaming media|streaming service]], but finds it to be more fatuous, saccharine, and juvenile than he remembered. Over the next twenty years, both Owen's depression and [[asthma]] increasingly worsen. The movie theater where he worked is closed down, and his supervisor secures him a job in a [[family entertainment center]]. During a child's birthday party, a now middle-aged Owen abruptly screams that he is "dying" and begs for help, though nobody reacts, appearing instead to freeze in place as Owen cries out for his mother. Owen locks himself in the bathroom and cuts his chest open with a box cutter, smiling when he sees a TV screen inside of himself. He then returns to work and timidly offers apologies to partygoers for his outburst.

''I Saw the TV Glow'' is heavily influenced by<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kay |first=Jeremy |date=February 18, 2024 |title=How ''Buffy The Vampire Slayer'' helped inspire Sundance hit ''I Saw The TV Glow'' |url=https://www.screendaily.com/features/how-buffy-the-vampire-slayer-helped-inspire-sundance-hit-i-saw-the-tv-glow/5190709.article |url-access=limited |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240403060146/https://www.screendaily.com/features/how-buffy-the-vampire-slayer-helped-inspire-sundance-hit-i-saw-the-tv-glow/5190709.article |archive-date=April 3, 2024 |access-date=June 16, 2024 |website=[[Screen Daily]] |quote=A major influence was cult US TV series ''Buffy The Vampire Slayer'', which ran from 1997-2003. 'It was an obsession and a real balm for me in my adolescent years,' they reflect. 'I rewatched my tapes over and over again, and like Owen and Maddy do with ''The Pink Opaque'', I thought of the characters like family. Buffy will always be an influence on everything I create.'}}</ref><ref name="VultureInt" /> and draws parallels to ''[[Buffy the Vampire Slayer]]'' (1997–2003). This is reflected in the film's fictional TV show ''The Pink Opaque'', which mirrors ''Buffy'' through its strong female leads and a mix of mythic and [[Villain of the week|monster-of-the-week]] episodes. Owen is introduced to this "''Buffy''-esque" show that becomes a refuge for him until its narrative starts affecting his reality as the story explores loneliness and the search for something real through a fixation. The cameo by Benson, who played [[lesbian]] character [[Tara Maclay]] on ''Buffy'', "felt healing in a way" for Schoenbrun,<ref name="VultureInt">{{Cite web |last=Zuckerman |first=Esther |date=May 3, 2024 |title=Jane Schoenbrun Saw the TV Glow |url=https://www.vulture.com/article/jane-schoenbrun-on-i-saw-the-tv-glow-and-buffy.html |access-date=June 16, 2024 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |archive-date=June 12, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240612155625/https://www.vulture.com/article/jane-schoenbrun-on-i-saw-the-tv-glow-and-buffy.html |url-status=live }}</ref> who relied on the show as a coping mechanism during adolescence.<ref name="Polygon">{{Cite web |last=Robinson |first=Tasha |date=May 10, 2024 |title=A small, important cameo in ''I Saw the TV Glow'' is meant as justice for Tara on ''Buffy the Vampire Slayer'' |url=https://www.polygon.com/24153069/i-saw-tv-glow-amber-benson-cameo-fixing-buffy-vampire-slayer |access-date=June 16, 2024 |website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] |archive-date=June 16, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240616103533/https://www.polygon.com/24153069/i-saw-tv-glow-amber-benson-cameo-fixing-buffy-vampire-slayer |url-status=live }}</ref>  

The film has been compared to ''[[The Adventures of Pete & Pete]]'' (1991–1996), exploring the theme of children living in a "semi-magical, frequently scary" world unnoticed by adults.<ref name="AVClub" /> Maronna and Tamberelli, lead actors of ''Pete & Pete'', appear as ghostly neighbors to explore the uncanny nature of aging. Schoenbrun emphasized that the cameos are meant to deepen the film's ideas.<ref name="Polygon" />

According to ''The AV Club'', Owen's story demonstrates "what happens to a trans person when the world makes the prospect of transitioning too terrifying to ever look at straight-on".<ref name="AVClub" /> Owen and Maddy represent pre- and post-realization stages of identity, mirroring Schoenbrun's own journey of [[self-discovery]]. It delves into how people perceive media differently as they grow, with changes in tone reflecting shifts in understanding, and feelings of shame and [[Gender dysphoria|dysphoria]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pulliam-Moore |first=Charles |date=May 21, 2024 |title=For the director of ''I Saw the TV Glow'', ''Buffy the Vampire Slayer'' was just the start |url=https://www.theverge.com/24159054/i-saw-the-tv-glow-jane-schoenbrun-interview |access-date=June 16, 2024 |website=[[The Verge]] |archive-date=June 15, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240615190525/https://www.theverge.com/24159054/i-saw-the-tv-glow-jane-schoenbrun-interview |url-status=live }}</ref> Owen experiences a sense of claustrophobia and disconnection from reality, mirroring the struggle to retain one's sense of wonder and magic in the face of adulthood. The film ends with Owen when the "world is content to ignore his screams that something inside of him is dying", and highlights the importance of "a little magic, borrowed from art, is one of the only ways to survive, and to remind yourself that—as the film's most haunting line reminds us—there is still time".<ref name="AVClub">{{Cite web |date=June 12, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' boldly channels the melancholy magic of ''The Adventures of Pete & Pete'' |url=https://www.avclub.com/i-saw-the-tv-glow-buffy-the-adventures-of-pete-pete-1851533917 |access-date=June 22, 2024 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |first=William |last=Hughes |archive-date=June 22, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240622014048/https://www.avclub.com/i-saw-the-tv-glow-buffy-the-adventures-of-pete-pete-1851533917 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film also draws inspiration from Schoenbrun's recurring dream about the ending of ''[[Twin Peaks]]'' (1990–1991) and aims to capture a "[[David Lynch|Lynchian]] terror".<ref name="VultureInt" /> Inspired by its followup ''[[Twin Peaks: The Return]]'' (2017), the film intertwines themes of TV series endings and revivals.<ref name="Polygon" />

The visual aesthetics were inspired by what Schoenbrun considered "to be the most beautiful films" (''[[A.I.: Artificial Intelligence]]'', ''[[Blade Runner]]'', "a lot of animation" and ones that "felt like they had the audacity to paint in really vibrant, rich and heightened colors").<ref name="shimmeringmemoryof90sTV"/>

==Reception==

===Critical response===
On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 85% based on 236 reviews, with an average rating of 7.6/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "With a distinctive visual aesthetic that enhances its emotionally resonant narrative, ''I Saw the TV Glow'' further establishes writer-director Jane Schoenbrun as a rising talent."<ref>{{Cite web |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/i_saw_the_tv_glow |access-date=March 16, 2025 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |archive-date=August 9, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240809204021/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/i_saw_the_tv_glow |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], the film has a score of 86 out of 100, based on 48 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{Cite web |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/i-saw-the-tv-glow/ |access-date=August 10, 2024 |website=[[Metacritic]] |archive-date=August 24, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240824183418/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/i-saw-the-tv-glow/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

To Guy Lodge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', ''TV Glow'' is "both promising [[psychological drama|psychodrama]] fodder on its own terms, and of a piece with the particular fixations Schoenbrun has established across their small oeuvre thus far".<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Lodge |first=Guy |date=January 19, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' Review: Jane Schoenbrun's Eerie Ode to Adolescent Television Obsessions |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/reviews/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-1235877715/ |access-date=January 19, 2024 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |archive-date=January 19, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240119204349/https://variety.com/2024/film/reviews/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-1235877715/ |url-status=live }}</ref> David Ehrlich of ''[[IndieWire]]'' wrote, "Schoenbrun's astonishing second feature manages to retain the seductive fear of their micro-budget debut and deepen its thrilling wounds of discovery even while examining them at a much larger scale".<ref name="IndieWire">{{Cite web |last=Ehrlich |first=David |date=January 19, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' Review: Jane Schoenbrun's Second Feature Is a Haunting Look at What We See in Our Media |url=https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/movies/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-1234946004/ |access-date=January 19, 2024 |website=[[IndieWire]] |archive-date=January 19, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240119204350/https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/movies/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-1234946004/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Some reviewers also praised the film for the authenticity with which it conveyed transgender themes, with [[Richard Brody]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' calling it "a profound vision of the trans experience", and Veronica Esposito for ''[[The Guardian]]'' saying it "speaks to '90s trans teens".<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Brody |first=Richard |authorlink=Richard Brody |date=May 3, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' Is a Profound Vision of the Trans Experience |url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/i-saw-the-tv-glow-is-a-profound-vision-of-the-trans-experience |magazine=[[The New Yorker]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Esposito |first=Veronica |date=May 4, 2024 |title=Nostalgia horror ''I Saw the TV Glow'' speaks to 90s trans teens like me |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/article/2024/may/04/i-saw-tv-glow-movie-trans-teen |access-date=June 16, 2024 |newspaper=[[The Guardian]] |archive-date=October 5, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241005202118/https://www.theguardian.com/film/article/2024/may/04/i-saw-tv-glow-movie-trans-teen |url-status=live }}</ref>

Amy Nicholson of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' criticized the film as a "collection of leaden scenes that might make the audience want to claw out of its own skin", noting that it "invents a new emotion: passionate ambivalence".<ref>{{cite news |last=Nicholson |first=Amy |date=May 2, 2024 |title=Review: ''I Saw the TV Glow'' gets stranded in a glum gaze from which it never stirs |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2024-05-02/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-jane-schoenbrun |accessdate=May 17, 2024 |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |archive-date=May 16, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240516232017/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2024-05-02/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-jane-schoenbrun |url-status=live }}</ref> Nicolas Rapold of ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' agreed, writing that "there's the awed sense of a blueprint or roadmap that is insisted upon without entirely being executed and fulfilled".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Rapold |first=Nicolas |date=January 30, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'': a rough portrait of fanatical obsession |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/i-saw-tv-glow-rough-portrait-fanatical-obsession |magazine=[[Sight & Sound]] |accessdate=May 17, 2024 |archive-date=May 18, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240518022844/https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/i-saw-tv-glow-rough-portrait-fanatical-obsession |url-status=live }}</ref> Dylan Roth of the ''[[The New York Observer|Observer]]'' acknowledged this discomfort, noting that the "challenge with ''I Saw the TV Glow'' is that almost everything I disliked about it is done on purpose, and effectively. As a piece of art, I can't deny that it works".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Roth |first=Dylan |date=May 6, 2024 |title=''I Saw the TV Glow'' Review: Moody, Interesting, No Fun |url=https://observer.com/2024/05/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-moody-interesting-no-fun/ |access-date=June 16, 2024 |website=[[The New York Observer|Observer]] |archive-date=October 5, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241005202113/https://observer.com/2024/05/i-saw-the-tv-glow-review-moody-interesting-no-fun/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Filmmaker [[Martin Scorsese]] praised the film as "emotionally and psychologically powerful" and said that he liked it "a great deal".<ref name=Scorsese>{{cite web |last=Coyle |first=Jake |date=November 14, 2024 |title=Martin Scorsese on 'The Saints,' faith in filmmaking and what his next movie might be |website=apnews.com |url=https://apnews.com/article/martin-scorsese-saints-fox-df1b10e984c32c96a8eb0d0515b1d7c1 |access-date=November 18, 2024 }}</ref> After viewing the film, [[Paul Schrader]] called Schoenbrun "hands down the most original voice in film in the last decade," but "also confounding."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Schrader |first=Paul |author-link=Paul Schrader |date=May 5, 2024 |title=JANE SCHOEBRUN is hands down the most original voice in film in the last decade. ''Sight and Sound'' put the film at 11 on their list of the best 50 movies of 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |date=December 6, 2024 |title=The 50 best films of 2024 |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/50-best-films-2024 |access-date= |website=BFI |language=en}}</ref> In June 2025, ''[[IndieWire]]'' ranked the film at number 28 on its list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)."<ref>{{cite web |date=June 16, 2025 |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)|url=https://www.indiewire.com/lists/best-movies-2020s/ |access-date=June 23, 2025}}</ref>

== Potential sequel ==
In May 2024, in an interview with ''[[USA Today]]'', Schoenbrun stated that a sequel to ''I Saw the TV Glow'' was possible, saying that they would be open to approaching the story from a different perspective.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zilko |first=Christian |date=May 18, 2024 |title=Jane Schoenbrun Teases Potential 'I Saw the TV Glow' Sequel: 'There's Another Side of the Prism I'd Like to Glance Through' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/i-saw-the-tv-glow-sequel-jane-schoenbrun-1235006635/ |access-date=September 26, 2024 |archive-date=September 27, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240927031046/https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/i-saw-the-tv-glow-sequel-jane-schoenbrun-1235006635/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In September 2024, Schoenbrun made a post to [[Twitter|X]] (formerly Twitter), in which they mentioned having an idea for a sequel to ''I Saw the TV Glow'' that they want to make "in a few years".<ref>{{Cite tweet |number=1839317305660256705 |user=sapphicspielbrg |title=The other day I was telling my boyfriend the idea I have for a TV GLOW sequel I wanna make in a few years, beat-by-beat. After I finished I asked him if he liked it. He paused for a while and then said "not really." |first=Jane |last=Schoenbrun |author-link=Jane Schoenbrun |access-date=September 26, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241005202113/https://x.com/sapphicspielbrg/status/1839317305660256705?s=46&t=nvS0DJay8o8RqFJClBNcjQ |archive-date=October 5, 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref> In an August 2026 interview with Douglas Greenwood, Schoenbrun further reaffirmed their intention to make a sequel to ''I Saw the TV Glow'', stating that they have an idea “meaty" and relevant enough to warrant the large amount of time that would be spent to create the film.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Jane Schoenbrun on Sex, Filmmaking, and Camp Miasma |url=https://i-d.co/article/jane-schoenbrun-filmmaker-interview/ |access-date=2026-08-15 |language=en-GB}}</ref>
