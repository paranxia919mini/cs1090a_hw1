'''''Sleepaway Camp'''''<!--Alternative titles in countries aside from the film's native country do not need to be in lead--> is a 1983 American [[slasher film]] written and directed by [[Robert Hiltzik]], and starring [[Mike Kellin]] (in his last on-screen appearance), [[Katherine Kamhi]], and Paul DeAngelo alongside [[Jonathan Tiersten]], [[Felissa Rose]], [[Christopher Collet]], and Karen Fields. The original entry in the [[Sleepaway Camp (film series)|''Sleepaway Camp'']] film series, it focuses on serial killings which occur at a [[summer camp]] for teenagers.

Filmed in [[upstate New York]] in the fall of 1982, ''Sleepaway Camp'' was released the following year by United Film Distributors. It earned approximately $11 million at the box office, but was met by largely unfavorable reviews from critics, many of whom deemed it exploitative and derivative of such films as ''[[Friday the 13th (1980 film)|Friday the 13th]]'' (1980).

In the years since its release, ''Sleepaway Camp'' has gone on to develop a [[cult following]], a more positive reappraisal from critics, as well as garnering notoriety for its [[twist ending]], which has been named as one of the most shocking and unforgettable in cinematic history.<ref name=kumar>{{cite news|url=https://thecinemaholic.com/best-horror-movie-plot-twists-time/|last=Kumar|first=Sharath|title=25 Best Horror Movie Twist Endings - Best Twist Endings in Horror Movies|work=The Cinemaholic|date=July 28, 2017|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514083838/https://thecinemaholic.com/best-horror-movie-plot-twists-time/|archive-date=May 14, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=cotter>{{cite web|work=[[Screen Rant]]|url=https://screenrant.com/sleepaway-camp-ending-best-twist/|title=Why Sleepaway Camp's Ending Is Best Slasher Movie Twist|last=Cotter|first=Padraig|date=March 11, 2019|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514095627/https://screenrant.com/sleepaway-camp-ending-best-twist/|archive-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref><ref name=yahoo>{{cite news|work=[[Yahoo! It was followed by four sequels: ''[[Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers]]'' (1988), ''[[Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland]]'' (1989), ''[[Sleepaway Camp IV: The Survivor]]'' (1992), and ''[[Return to Sleepaway Camp]]'' (2008).

==Plot==
In 1975, John Baker and his boyfriend Lenny take John's children, Angela and Peter, on a boating trip. After the boat capsizes, John and the children try to swim to shore, but they swim into the path of a reckless motorboat and are struck. John and one of the children are killed.

Eight years later, Angela, the surviving child, is traumatized and lives with her eccentric aunt, Dr. Martha Thomas, and cousin Ricky Thomas. Angela and Ricky are sent to Camp Arawak. Angela's [[Extraversion and introversion|introverted]] nature makes her a target for bullying by fellow camper Judy and camp counselor Meg, but Ronnie Angelo and Susie are nice to her. The head cook, Artie, attempts to sexually assault her, but Ricky intervenes. Later, an unseen figure causes Artie to be severely scalded by boiling water. Artie is rushed to the hospital, and camp owner Mel Kostic dismisses the incident as accidental.

Campers Kenny and Mike mock Angela, prompting Ricky and his friend Paul to confront them. Paul befriends Angela. Later, after Kenny is out at night on the lake and capsizes his canoe, an unseen figure confronts Kenny. Kenny is found the next morning, drowned, and Mel again rules the death an accident. Paul asks Angela to go to the movies with him and kisses her. Campers Billy and Jimmy throw a water balloon at Angela, then Billy is killed when someone traps him in a public toilet with a beehive, exploiting his allergy. Mel begins to suspect a killer is at the camp.

Tensions rise as Angela and Paul's relationship becomes strained. When Paul kisses her again, she has a flashback to witnessing her father in bed with Lenny. Judy seduces Paul, and Angela finds them kissing. Paul tries to explain, but Judy goads Meg into carrying Angela to the pier and throwing her into the water. Mel stops Ricky from helping by holding him by the arms while accusing Ricky of harming campers to protect Angela; Ronnie intervenes. While Ricky helps Angela out of the water and back to the beach, four small children throw sand at Angela. Ricky comforts her and swears revenge. Right after dinner, Meg's back is split open during her shower in one of the vacated cabins, killing her.

During the camp social, Paul apologizes to Angela, and she tells him to meet her by the water. Meanwhile, Mel discovers Meg's body, and camp counselor Eddie finds the bodies of the four children who threw sand at Angela, brutally murdered in their sleeping bags. Soon after, Judy is killed by being vaginally penetrated with a hot electric [[Hair iron|curling iron]]. Panic spreads through the camp. Believing that Ricky is the killer, Mel beats him unconscious, then runs into the woods, encountering the real assailant. Horrified, Mel seemingly recognizes the attacker before being killed by an arrow to the throat. Police begin searching for missing campers. Paul is on the beach with Angela, who suggests they go for a swim, while Officer Frank Breton finds Ricky unconscious but alive.

Ronnie and Susie discover a naked Angela humming and clutching a hunting knife alongside Paul's severed head. They are shocked to realize that "Angela" is actually a boy. It is then revealed that Angela is actually her presumed-dead brother Peter. The real Angela had died in the boating accident, while Peter was the survivor. After Martha had gained custody of him, she decided to raise Peter as "Angela", the girl she had always wanted, which mentally affected him. The nude, blood-covered Angela/Peter stands before the horrified Susie and Ronnie, hissing and growling.

==Production==
===Development===
Writer-director [[Robert Hiltzik]], a [[New York University]] film school graduate, began developing the idea for ''Sleepaway Camp'' in the 1970s.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=16}} He was inspired by his own experiences attending Camp Algonquin in [[Argyle (town), New York|Argyle, New York]] as a child.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=16}}<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/robert-hiltzik-talks-the-making-of-slasher-classic-sleepaway-camp/|title= Robert Hiltzik Talks the Making of Slasher Classic Sleepaway Camp|last=Kaye|first=Don|date=June 3, 2014|work=Den of Geek|archive-date=May 14, 2023|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514084315/https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/robert-hiltzik-talks-the-making-of-slasher-classic-sleepaway-camp/}}</ref> He was also inspired to make the film following the success of ''[[Friday the 13th (1980 film)|Friday the 13th]]'',<ref name="Reeves 2024">{{cite news|last=Reeves|first=Rachel|title=SponsoredCamp Blood: 7 Retro Summer Camp Horror Movies [Friday the 13th Week]|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/sponsored/3830803/camp-blood-friday-the-13th/|date=September 10, 2024|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="FilmInk">{{cite news|last=Free|first=Erin|title=Unsung Auteurs|url=https://www.filmink.com.au/unsung-auteurs-robert-hiltzik/|date=July 24, 2025|work=[[FilmInk]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251122093020/https://www.filmink.com.au/unsung-auteurs-robert-hiltzik/|archive-date=November 22, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> though he has denied that the film was a direct inspiration on the screenplay.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|pages=16–17}}

Hiltzik privately raised funding for the film through friends, family, and other "connections," eventually amassing a budget of $335,000 to make the film.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=18}}

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] of ''Sleepaway Camp'' took place at Camp Algonquin (which Hiltzik attended himself as a child) in Argyle, New York.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|pages=16, 25}} Filming began in September 1982 and lasted five weeks.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=25}} Hiltzik dedicated the film to his mother, who died in a car accident before production began.<ref name="AFI"/><ref>{{cite news|last=Greenblatt|first=Lowell|title=Jeff Hayes Talks To Macabre Daily About His New Book About "Sleepaway Camp!"|url=https://www.macabredaily.com/articles/jeff-hayes-talks-to-macabre-daily-about-his-new-book-about-sleepaway-camp|date=December 5, 2023|website=Macabre Daily|archive-date=January 13, 2026|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260113203242/https://www.macabredaily.com/articles/campfire-stories-inside-the-new-book-sleepaway-camp-making-the-movie-and-reigniting-the-campfire|url-status=live}}</ref> The film had been storyboarded but after the first day of filming, the film was already behind schedule. The storyboards could not be used and were thrown out.<ref name="SCtrivia"/> The trees, with their leaves turning, belie the summer setting of the film.

Several crew members, including costume designer Eileen Sieff and mechanical effects technician Ed Fountain, were hired by production manager Carl Clifford; Clifford, Fountain and Sieff had previously worked on [[George A. Romero]]'s ''[[Creepshow]]'' (1982).{{sfn|Hayes|2023|pages=26–27}}

===Special effects===
The practical and makeup effects were done by Ed French,{{sfn|Collum|2004|pages=140–142}} Diane Lawrence, and Suzen Poshek. Jonathan Tiersten performed Angela's killing scenes since Felissa Rose's contract stated that she would not do the killings on-screen due to her young age.<ref name="SCMTiersten"/><ref name="SCtrivia"/>{{sfn|Collum|2004|pages=138–144}} A lifecast of John E. Dunn was used for Kenny's death scene.<ref name="SCMDunn"/> The effects used for Billy's death were achieved using a dummy with sugar mixture on its face to attract the bees.<ref name="SCMSallahian"/>{{sfn|Hayes|2023|pages=48–50}}

For the scene in which "Angela" is revealed to be Peter Baker, Rose was originally going to wear a prosthetic penis.{{sfn|Collum|2004|p=140}} A local college student was paid $200 to fill in for Rose, and had to be drunk before filming the scene. He shaved his body and stood naked on the waterfront, wearing a mask of Angela's face.<ref name="SCtrivia"/>{{sfn|Collum|2004|pages=140–141}} The mask, designed by French, featured glass eyes and was crafted out of [[Poly(methyl methacrylate)|dental acrylic]].{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=60}}

==Reception==
===Box office===
Upon its New York City release over the [[Thanksgiving (United States)|Thanksgiving]] week in 1983, ''Sleepaway Camp'' grossed $430,000 from 85 theaters.{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=78}} The film earned $90,000 during its opening weekend in Los Angeles in May 1984, screening at 15 theaters.<ref name="AFI"/>{{sfn|Hayes|2023|p=78}} By June 10, 1984, the film ranked among the top-twenty highest-grossing films at the U.S. box office that week.<ref>{{cite news|date=June 10, 1984|page=B6|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/sunday-news/124636408/|work=Sunday News|title=The week's top money-making movies|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> By the end of its theatrical run, it had gone on to gross a total of $11 million.{{sfn|Hafdahl|Florence|2020|p=47}}

===Critical response===
====Contemporary====
Upon its original release, the film was frequently compared to ''[[Friday the 13th (1980 film)|Friday the 13th]]'' due to their shared settings and [[whodunit]] plot structure.<ref name=bdrev>{{cite news|author=Bloody Disgusting staff|title=Sleepaway Camp|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/reviews/108376/sleepaway-camp-2/|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=June 30, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120607082324/https://bloody-disgusting.com/reviews/108376/sleepaway-camp-2/|archive-date=June 7, 2012}}</ref> A review in ''[[The Courier-Journal]]'' characterized the film as a "low-budget [[Slasher film|slasher]] in the ''Friday the 13th'' mold, with teen-age mayhem at a summer camp",<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Courier-Journal]]|page=22|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/109693293/|via=Newspapers.com|series=The Movie Page|title=Sleepaway Camp}}</ref> while Rick Lyman of ''[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]]'' uniformly lambasted the film, criticizing its performances, writing, and twist ending.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-philadelphia-inquirer-sleepaway-camp/8892049/|work=[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]]|date=January 23, 1984|last=Lyman|first=Rick|page=3-F|title=Film: More teenage mayhem in 'Sleepaway Camp'|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Linda Gross of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' felt the film was derivative and gruesome, but conceded that its pacing was adept and that director Hiltzik portrayed the often cruel and abusive behavior of teenagers towards another young person.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/124636192/|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|last=Gross|first=Linda|date=May 28, 1984|page=3|via=Newspapers.com|title=Gruesome, Crude 'Camp' Story}}</ref>

In the ''[[The Star-News (Chula Vista)|Chula Vista Star-News]]''{{'}}s review, the film was deemed "a tasteless picture about mysterious murders at a summer youth camp that obscenely blends beheadings, stabbings, pubescent impulses, homosexuality, and [[transvestism]]" with a cast of junior-high-school actors.<ref>{{cite news|newspaper=[[The Star-News (Chula Vista)|Chula Vista Star-News]]|page=24|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/130644072/|date=June 10, 1984|title=Movie Views|author=Maleska, Eugene T.|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> George Williams of ''[[The Sacramento Bee]]'' made similar criticisms of the acting, and described the film as "mindless" and "dirty".<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Sacramento Bee]]|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-sacramento-bee-sleepaway-camp-review/118180378/|last=Williams|first=George|date=March 21, 1984|page=21|title='Sleepaway' Hasn't Got a Single Redeeming Feature|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Paul Willistein of ''[[The Morning Call]]'' described the film as "simply horrible", writing that its [[camp (style)|campy]] sensibility is unsuccessful as it is "intended to be a bonafide horror film".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-morning-call-sleepawaycampissimpl/19520491/|work=[[The Morning Call]]|date=January 26, 1984|page=D11|last=Willistein|first=Paul|title='Sleepaway Camp' is simply horrible|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

''[[The News-Press]]'' gave it a favorable review, calling it "a shockingly good slasher film, if you use the relatively fine, first ''Friday the 13th'' as a measuring stick... it's just another crazed killer stalking nubile summer campers. But, this time, there are some truly creative killings and interesting plot twists".<ref>{{cite news|newspaper=[[The News-Press]]|date=April 21, 1989|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/217326195/|title=New Releases|via=Newspapers.com|page=89}}</ref> The ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]''{{'}}s Peter Stack also praised the film as a "great scuzz movie from top to bottom" and a "little masterpiece of hostility comedy," noting that "there's not one likable character, a factor that should serve as an excellent release valve for all the people who absolutely hate teenagers."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/san-francisco-chronicle/188812898/|work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|last=Stack|first=Peter|date=March 26, 1984|p=39|title='Sleepaway Camp' a Teen Hater's Delight|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Richard Martin, writing for the ''[[Ottawa Citizen]]'', found the film's screenplay underdeveloped and lacking suspense, but praised its conclusion as effective and frightening, noting: "The very last image of ''Sleepaway Camp'' is so shocking and deeply disturbing it's given me goosebumps every time I recall it."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-ottawa-citizen-sleepaway-camp-review/118179853/|date=October 1, 1984|last=Martin|first=Richard|work=[[Ottawa Citizen]]|p=B9|title=Suspenseless film has shocking ending|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

====Modern assessment====
In the years since its release, ''Sleepaway Camp'' has developed a [[cult following]] and garnered critical reappraisal. Its twist ending has been distinguished by audiences and critics as one of the most shocking in the slasher genre's history,<ref name=kumar/><ref name=cotter/><ref name=yahoo/><ref name=harrington>{{cite news|work=[[The Mercury News]]|url=https://www.mercurynews.com/2013/10/17/13-fright-flicks-sleepaway-camps-ending-will-kill-you/|date=October 17, 2013|title=13 Fright flicks: Sleepaway Camp's ending will kill you|url-status=live|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514100055/https://www.mercurynews.com/2013/10/17/13-fright-flicks-sleepaway-camps-ending-will-kill-you/|last=Harrington|first=Jim}} {{closed access}}</ref> as well as in [[History of film|cinema history]] in general.<ref name=digest>{{cite web|work=[[Reader's Digest]]|url=https://www.readersdigest.co.uk/culture/film-tv/10-of-the-most-shocking-film-plot-twists|title=10 of the most shocking film plot twists|last=Stanley|first=Ben|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514100752/https://www.readersdigest.co.uk/culture/film-tv/10-of-the-most-shocking-film-plot-twists|archive-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref><ref name=stolworthy>{{cite web|work=[[The Independent]]|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/movie-twists-best-explained-b2312379.html|title=The 35 most mind-blowing twists in film history, explained|last=Stolworthy|first=Jacob|date=April 1, 2023|url-status=live|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514101813/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/movie-twists-best-explained-b2312379.html}}</ref>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|82|6.5|28|''Sleepaway Camp'' is a standard teen slasher elevated by occasional moments of [[John Waters]]-esque weirdness and a twisted ending.|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}<ref>{{cite web|title=Sleepaway Camp (1983)|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/sleepaway_camp|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|accessdate=August 22, 2022}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film was reviewed by four critics and got a rank of 58 out of a 100, which indicates "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/sleepaway-camp|title=Sleepaway Camp (1983)|website=[[Metacritic]]|accessdate=July 12, 2021}}</ref>

The website [[Bloody Disgusting]] gave the film a positive review, praising [[Felissa Rose]]'s performance and the film's twist ending calling it "one of the most shocking seen since, possibly, [[Alfred Hitchcock|Hitchcock]]'s ''[[Psycho (1960 film)|Psycho]]''".<ref name=bdrev/> [[AllRovi|AllMovie]] wrote in its review on the film: "While most of the gender-bending story's sexual confusion is ultimately half-baked... ''Sleepaway Camp'' is distinctive enough to warrant required viewing for genre enthusiasts".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://allmovie.com/movie/sleepaway-camp-v45145/review|title=Sleepaway Camp - Review|last=Beldin|first=Fred|work=[[AllRovi|AllMovie]]|accessdate=July 12, 2021}}</ref>

Film scholar [[Bartłomiej Paszylk]] called the film "an exceptionally bad movie but a very good slasher".{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=176}} Commenting on the performances in the film, film scholar Thomas Sipos wrote that "[the film] feels odd due to its contrasting acting styles. Most of the cast performs in a naturalistic manner, whereas [[Desiree Gould]]'s performance as Aunt Martha is strikingly stylistic: broadly overplayed to the point of caricature".{{sfn|Sipos|2010|p=39}}

In his book ''The Pleasure and Pain of Cult Horror Films: A Historical Survey'' (2009), Paszylk characterized ''Sleepaway Camp'' as "elevat[ing] the kids to the position of the movie's mass protagonist and becomes a tricky metaphor of the unspeakable pains and anxieties of growing up".{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=177}} He further commented on the film's conclusion: "The epiphanous ending brings ''Sleepaway Camp'' further away from the likes of ''Friday the 13th'' and closer to such 1980s 'slashers with a twist' as ''[[Happy Birthday to Me (1981 film)|Happy Birthday to Me]]'' (1981) and ''[[April Fool's Day (1986 film)|April Fool's Day]]'' (1986), but Hiltzik's movie goes even further than that: in this case the denouement doesn't just add a new dimension to everything we saw up to this point, but it pushes its way deep into our minds and stays with us forever".{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=177}}

The film was featured on episode 48 of the [[podcast]] ''[[How Did This Get Made?]]'' where hosts [[Paul Scheer]], [[Jason Mantzoukas]], [[June Diane Raphael]], and guest host [[Zack Pearlman]] struggled to decipher the film's opening moments, due to the ambiguous relationships established at the beginning of the film. Scheer later recalled that it had been the most fan-requested film "by a landslide".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://avclub.com/paul-scheer-picks-his-favorite-how-did-this-get-made-e-1798236164|title=Paul Scheer picks his favorite ''How Did This Get Made?'' episodes|last=Rabin|first=Nathan|date=February 7, 2013|website=[[The A.V. Club]]|url-status=live|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514091610/https://www.avclub.com/paul-scheer-picks-his-favorite-how-did-this-get-made-e-1798236164}}</ref>

====Criticism====
''Sleepaway Camp'' has garnered contemporary criticism for its representation of a [[transgender]] villain.<ref name=abley/><ref name="Maclay 2015">{{cite web|url=https://cleojournal.com/2015/08/10/how-can-it-be-shes-a-boy-transmisogyny-in-sleepaway-camp/|title="How Can It Be? She's a boy". Transmisogyny in Sleepaway Camp|last=Maclay|first=Willow|date=August 10, 2015|website=Cléo|access-date=August 31, 2021}}</ref> Willow Maclay, a transgender writer for ''Cléo'' magazine, criticized the film for its "equation of mental instability with having grown up in a [[gender role]] not concurrent with your identity. Nearly every single transgender person grows up being raised in a gender role that does not fit, and that doesn't mean that they are mentally ill or seriously violent".<ref name="Maclay 2015" /> BJ Colangelo, in an editorial for [[Dread Central]], similarly felt the film had transphobic and homophobic implications due to its representation of Angela (Rose) and Angela's gay father John Baker (Dan Tursi), but conceded that the film has metaphorical merit for showing the unfavorable and violent consequences for a character who is forced into [[gender role]]s that do not align with their identity.<ref name="Colangelo 2019">{{cite web|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/295274/going-back-to-sleepaway-camp-revisiting-the-problematic-classic/|title=Going Back To Sleepaway Camp: Revisiting The Problematic Classic|last=Colangelo|first=BJ|date=June 5, 2019|website=[[Dread Central]]|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514084409/https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/295274/going-back-to-sleepaway-camp-revisiting-the-problematic-classic/|archive-date=May 14, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[File:Calpernia Addams.jpg|thumb|right|upright|Transgender activist [[Calpernia Addams]] argues that the character of Angela is not actually transgender, but has been forced to live as a girl despite being designated as a male.]]
[[Calpernia Addams]], a transgender author and activist, commented in a 2021 interview with ''[[Fangoria]]'' that the character of Angela "is not even really trans... This whole situation with Angela is a child who was forced into this quote-unquote transition".<ref name=abley/> She compared the character to [[David Reimer]], a Canadian man who, as a child, was forced by his parents to live as a girl following a botched [[circumcision]].<ref name=abley/> Addams further stated that the film should be assessed in the context of the period in which it was made: "I would just say that I enjoy ''Sleepaway Camp'' for what it is, which is schlocky '80s horror with a unique twist ending. And I think it's the worst possible portrayal of a supposedly trans storyline, ''à la'' [[Buffalo Bill (character)|Buffalo Bill]], or ''[[Dressed to Kill (1980 film)|Dressed to Kill]]'', or any of those types of films. But at the same time, I don't want it censored or canceled. And if you just sit back and let yourself, it can be an enjoyable watch".<ref name=abley>{{cite web|work=[[Fangoria]]|url=https://www.fangoria.com/original/problematic-films-in-defense-of-sleepaway-camp/|title=Problematic Films: In Defense Of SLEEPAWAY CAMP|last=Abley|first=Sean|date=September 28, 2021|url-status=live|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514044234/https://www.fangoria.com/original/problematic-films-in-defense-of-sleepaway-camp/}}</ref>
 
Transgender writer Alice Collins of ''Bloody Disgusting'' said that ''Sleepaway Camp'' "is steeped in [[queerness]], especially when compared to its contemporaries. In its day it took a deeper look into the subject matter than that of other films. Angela and Peter's dad is a [[closeted]] gay man, there's forced [[gender bending]] (which is abuse rather than queer but people will see it as such), and the majority of the scantily clad people in the film are men with all those very short shorts that leave little to the imagination while there is little skin shown of the feminine variety".<ref name="Collins 2020">{{cite news|last=Collins|first=Alice|title='Sleepaway Camp': The Elephant in the Room [Trapped By Gender]|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3625816/sleepaway-camp-elephant-room-trapped-gender/|date=August 21, 2020|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=December 1, 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514084153/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3625816/sleepaway-camp-elephant-room-trapped-gender/|archive-date=May 14, 2023}}</ref> Collins argues that Angela is a transgender girl, noting that, in the film's [[Sleepaway Camp (film series)|sequels]], Angela is retconned as a woman who uses feminine [[gender pronoun|pronouns]]: "So despite Aunt Martha being insane, she just happened to stumble upon a person who was already a girl; and it was an accident that her brainwashing worked".<ref name="Collins 2020" />

Actress Felissa Rose defended the film in a 2023 interview: "I absolutely don't feel like it's transphobic. I feel as though Angela was a typical adolescent trying to find her gender identification and sexual orientation and I thought that was extremely exciting for 1982. It was ahead of its time. You can see with her father and his lover as well as her relationship with Paul [her camp crush], who she was trying to understand her relationship with, I feel like it was an adolescent story of a young person coming of age".<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Dread Central]]|date=February 7, 2023|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/446261/felissa-rose-discusses-the-queer-horror-of-sleepaway-camp-interview/|title=Felissa Rose Discusses the Queer Horror of 'Sleepaway Camp'|url-status=live|archive-date=May 14, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514043715/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/446261/felissa-rose-discusses-the-queer-horror-of-sleepaway-camp-interview/}}</ref>

=== Sequels ===
In the late 1980s, Hiltzik sold the rights to the successive ''Sleepaway Camp'' films, after which Michael A. Simpson directed two sequels, ''Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers'' (1988) and ''Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland'' (1989).{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=177}} In them, Angela (now played by [[Bruce Springsteen]]'s younger sister, [[Pamela Springsteen]]) resurfaces at a nearby summer camp, but this time masquerading as a counselor after a [[sex reassignment surgery]].{{sfn|Stine|2003|p=277}} Much like at the previous camp, she gleefully tortures and kills anyone who misbehaves or annoys her. These films had more of a [[satire|satirical]] [[comedy|comic tone]] than the original.{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=165}}{{sfn|Sipos|2010|p=65}}

Another sequel, ''Sleepaway Camp IV: The Survivor'', directed by Jim Markovic, was partially filmed in the early 1990s but left incomplete.{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=177}} In 2002, the unfinished footage was released and made available as an exclusive fourth disc in Anchor Bay/[[Starz Entertainment]]'s ''Sleepaway Camp'' [[DVD]] boxed set. In 2012, the film was completed using archival footage from the first three films and released on DVD and [[Amazon Prime Video|Amazon]] Video on Demand.

A fifth film, ''Return to Sleepaway Camp'', was completed in 2003 and initially struggled to have its visual effects completed. It was directed by Robert Hiltzik, the director of the original 1983 film. According to ''Fangoria'', the digital effects were redone from 2006 to 2008. The film was released in 2008.{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=177}}

=== Unofficial sequel ===
Karen Fields reprised her role of Judy from ''Sleepaway Camp'' in the 2014 short film ''Judy'', although it wasn't technically a sequel. The Jeff Hayes-directed film was included in the collector's edition Blu-ray release of the original ''Sleepaway Camp''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rowsdowr.com/2014/03/12/sleepaway-camp-collectors-edition-bluray-art-special-features/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20210818101011/https://www.rowsdowr.com/2014/03/12/sleepaway-camp-collectors-edition-bluray-art-special-features/|url-status=dead|archive-date=2021-08-18|title=Sleepaway Camp Collector's Edition Blu-ray Art & Special Features|work=Rowsdowr|date=March 12, 2014|access-date=August 18, 2021}}</ref>

=== Proposed remake ===
The purportedly final film in the ''Sleepaway Camp'' series, titled ''Sleepaway Camp Reunion'', was also announced to be in the works. Distribution had been arranged via [[Magnolia Pictures]]. Creator Robert Hiltzik, who recovered the rights to the franchise, has stated that he would make the film if his budget was met. However, Hiltzik and ''Return To Sleepaway Camp'' producer Jeff Hayes later announced themselves as having started work on a reboot that would retain the key characters and elements of the original film with additional storyline elements and a dose of modernizing. As of Summer 2014, Hiltzik was reportedly tweaking the script. In addition, Michael Simpson, the director of ''Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers'' and ''Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland'', wrote a script for an additional film called ''Sleepaway Camp: Berserk''.

By December 2020, another ''Sleepaway Camp'' installment was announced to be in development, starring Felissa Rose.<ref>{{cite news|date=December 3, 2020|title=New 'Sleepaway Camp' Movie Is in the Works Says Original Star Felissa Rose|url=https://movieweb.com/sleepaway-camp-reboot-plans-felissa-rose/|website=[[MovieWeb]]|last=Chand|first=Neera|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230514084552/https://movieweb.com/sleepaway-camp-reboot-plans-felissa-rose/|archive-date=May 14, 2023|language=en-US}}</ref>

In September 2025, it was announced that Kenan Thompson's production company Artists for Artists (AFA) has acquired the rights to remake ''Sleepaway Camp'' with original writer/director Robert Hiltzik.<ref>{{cite news|last=Grobar|first=Matt|date=September 18, 2025|title=Kenan Thompson's AFA Developing Reimagining Of '80s Slasher 'Sleepaway Camp'|url=https://deadline.com/2025/09/sleepaway-camp-kenan-thompson-developing-reimagining-1236548085/|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260113052224/https://deadline.com/2025/09/sleepaway-camp-kenan-thompson-developing-reimagining-1236548085/|archive-date=January 13, 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>
