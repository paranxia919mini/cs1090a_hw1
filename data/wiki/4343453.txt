'''''The Slumber Party Massacre''''' (also known as '''''The Slumber Party Murders''''' in the UK) is a 1982 American [[slasher film]] produced and directed by [[Amy Holden Jones]] and written by [[Rita Mae Brown]]. It is the first installment in the [[Massacre (franchise)|''Slumber Party Massacre'' series]], and stars Michelle Michaels, [[Robin Stille]], and [[Michael Villella]]. The film follows a high school senior who gathers her friends for a slumber party, unaware that an escaped power drill-wielding killer is loose in the neighborhood.

The film was originally written by Brown as a [[parody]] of the slasher genre but was shot as a straightforward horror film instead. As a result, it contains more humor, both intended and unintended, than usual for the genre at the time.

''The Slumber Party Massacre'' grossed $3.6 million at the box office on a budget of $220,000, and received mixed reviews from critics. Despite the reception, it has obtained a large [[cult following]] since its release. Two direct [[sequel]]s, ''[[Slumber Party Massacre II]]'' and ''[[Slumber Party Massacre III]]'', followed in 1987 and 1990, respectively, with [[Slumber Party Massacre (2021 film)|a fourth film]] following in 2021.{{Sfn|Shary|2014|p=182}} Two [[Spin-off (media)|spin-off]] film series forming a wider [[Massacre (franchise)|''Massacre'' franchise]] were also produced: the ''[[Sorority House Massacre]]'' [[trilogy]] (1986–1990), and the ''[[Cheerleader Massacre]]'' films (2003–2011), the first film of the latter series having been filmed as ''Slumber Party Massacre 4'' before being renamed.

==Plot==
In [[Venice, Los Angeles|Venice]], Trish Deveraux, an 18-year-old high school senior, decides to throw a [[slumber party]] while her parents are away. Their neighbor, Mr. David Contant, is given the job of checking on the girls. She awakes and gets dressed shortly before going to school. Meanwhile, Russ Thorn, an escaped serial killer, kills a telephone repairwoman and steals her van. Trish meets up with her friends Kim, Jackie, and Diane. She invites the new girl, Valerie, to the party, but Valerie declines. After school, one of their classmates, Linda, goes back into the school to retrieve a book but gets locked inside. She is attacked and presumably killed by Russ, armed with a [[drill|power drill]]. As the party begins that night, the girls use [[marijuana]] and [[alcohol (drug)|alcohol]], while Valerie babysits her younger sister, Courtney, across the street. Two boys from school, Jeff and Neil, arrive and spy on the girls while they change clothes. Russ kills Mr. Contant outside with his power drill. Diane asks Trish permission to go with her boyfriend, and goes to his car to find him decapitated. She is then murdered as well.

The girls order pizza and, while on the phone with their coach, Rachel Jana, the girls answer the door and find the [[pizza delivery]] man with his eyes drilled out. The teens arm themselves with knives as Jeff and Neil run for help, but both boys are killed. Russ gains entry to the house and murders Jackie. Trish and Kim barricade themselves in Trish's bedroom, but Russ enters through a window and kills Kim as Trish flees. Valerie and Courtney enter the house to find Kim dead and hide from Russ. Coach Jana, having become concerned after the earlier phone call, arrives and is confronted by Russ, who [[disembowelment|disembowels]] her with the drill. Valerie chases Russ with a machete, eventually severing his hand before slicing his stomach. Russ falls into a swimming pool and sinks beneath the water, only to emerge moments later and attack them again, but Valerie finally kills him with the machete. Valerie and Trish break down in tears as Courtney looks on in [[Acute stress disorder|shock]].

==Production==
===Conception===
Author and feminist activist [[Rita Mae Brown]] wrote the original screenplay, titled ''Sleepless Nights'', as a parody of the [[slasher film]]. Producers repurposed Brown's script to make a "serious" slasher film against her wishes.{{Sfn|Nashawaty|2013|p=187}}

Amy Holden Jones, a film editor,<ref name=timeout/> wanted to direct and asked [[Frances Doel]] for advice. Doel gave Jones a number of scripts. Jones chose the script that would become ''The Slumber Party Massacre'', then going by the title of ''Don't Open the Door'', and decided to film the first three scenes. Her husband, cinematographer [[Michael Chapman (cinematographer)|Michael Chapman]], acquired equipment and film and hired actors from the [[University of California, Los Angeles]],<ref name=kart>{{cite news|title=Why one tough woman turned down 'E.T.' for 'Slumber Party Massacre'|work=The Guardian|location=London|author=Kart, Larry|page=14|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/22376079/chicago_tribune/|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> and they shot the scenes at their house over a weekend for $1,000. She showed the result to [[Roger Corman]], who agreed to finance the film.<ref name=kart/> Jones had to turn down a job editing [[Steven Spielberg]]'s ''[[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial|E.T.]]'' (1982) as a result.{{Sfn|Nashawaty|2013|p=185}} The soundtrack was composed on a [[Casio MT-40]].{{Sfn|Nashawaty|2013|pages=184–85}}

===Filming===
Filming began in the summer of 1981.<ref name=afi/> The film was shot on location in [[Los Angeles, California]], mainly in [[Venice, California|Venice]].{{Sfn|Muir|2012|p=279}}

==Release==
===Box office===
Distributed by [[New World Pictures]], the film premiered in Los Angeles on September 10, 1982,<ref name=afi>{{cite web|url=http://www.afi.com/members//catalog/DetailView.aspx?s=&Movie=56875|work=The American Film Institute (AFI)|title=The Slumber Party Massacre|access-date=December 29, 2016}}</ref> and was given a limited release in New York City on November 12.<ref name=afi/> It grossed $3.6 million at the box office on an estimated budget of $220,000.<ref name=afi/>

===Critical response===
''The Slumber Party Massacre'' received mixed reviews from critics. [[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote of the film: "''The Slumber Party Massacre'' is just the usual cavalcade of corpses, all of them dispatched by a maniac who wields a power drill. At the end of the movie, a woman who has miraculously survived the carnage breaks his drill in half. That's feminism for you, and symbolism too".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9803E6DB103BF931A25752C1A964948260?|first=Janet|last=Maslin|title='The Slumber Party'|website=[[The New York Times]]|date=November 12, 1982|access-date=February 4, 2017|author-link=Janet Maslin}}</ref> ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' gave the film a middling review, noting: "Despite the unlikely script credit for Rita Mae Brown, Jones's debut feature is little more than a ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' clone, reliant on buckets of blood and sudden surprise rather than suspense."<ref name=timeout>{{cite web|url=https://timeout.com/london/film/the-slumber-party-massacre|work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|location=London, England|title=The Slumber Party Massacre, directed by Amy Jones|date=10 September 2012 |access-date=February 4, 2017}}</ref> David Hinckley of the ''[[New York Daily News]]'' awarded the film 1.5 stars out of 4, noting that the performances are "uneven" and "the special effects are not special".<ref>{{cite news|author=Hinckley, David|date=November 12, 1982|work=[[New York Daily News]]|title='Slumber Party Massacre': Sleeping sickness|page=4|via=Newspapers.com|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/22376165/daily_news/}}</ref>

''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]''{{'}}s published review, however, praised the film: "Besides its obviously catchy title, ''Slumber Party Massacre'' is an entertaining terror thriller, with the switch that distaff filmmakers handle the 'young women in jeopardy' format."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/1981/film/reviews/slumber-party-massacre-1200425125/|work=Variety|author=Variety Staff|date=March 31, 1982|access-date=February 4, 2017|title=Review: 'Slumber Party Massacre'}}</ref> [[Dave Kehr]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' also gave the film a positive review, noting its even pacing and direction by Jones.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/the-slumber-party-massacre/Film?oid=1073704|work=The Chicago Reader|first=Dave|last=Kehr|author-link=Dave Kehr|title=The Slumber Party Massacre |date=26 October 1985|access-date=December 22, 2016}}</ref> Leonard Klady of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' also noted the film's pacing, writing in a retrospective that the film boasted a "darkly humorous vision and a breathtaking pace".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-10-16-ca-9632-story.html|work=The Los Angeles Times|first=Leonard|last=Klady|date=October 12, 1987|title=Movie Reviews: 'Slumber Party Massacre II'|access-date=February 4, 2017}}</ref>

Dale Schneck of ''[[The Morning Call]]'' deemed the film a "rousing thriller" that "delivers as many vicarious thrills as one could want from this sort of cinematic mayhem".<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Morning Call]]|location=Allentown, Pennsylvania|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/22375958/the_morning_call/|last=Schenck|first=Dale|title=This 'Slumber Party' will keep you awake|date=May 6, 1983|page=D3|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|48|4.9|25|ref=yes|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 51 out of 100, based on 8 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Slumber Party Massacre'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-slumber-party-massacre |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=11 November 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Despite the critical reception, the film has a large [[cult following]] among [[Slasher film|slasher]] fans.<ref>{{cite web|title=The Slumber Party Massacre (1982) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/slumber_party_massacre |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=August 5, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180430172625/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/slumber_party_massacre |archive-date=April 30, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Home media===
It was released on [[VHS]] and [[Betamax]] by [[Embassy Pictures|Embassy Home Entertainment]] in 1983.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0084695/companycredits|title=Company Credits for The Slumber Party Massacre|website=imdb.com|access-date=April 12, 2011}}</ref>

The film has been released on [[DVD]] three times in North America. The first release came from [[New Concorde|New Concorde Home Entertainment]] in September 2000.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdempire.com/Exec/v4_item.asp?item_id=15949|title=The Slumber Party Massacre (DVD)|publisher=dvdempire.com|access-date=April 12, 2011}}</ref> The company subsequently re-released the film on a [[double feature]] DVD alongside ''Slumber Party Massacre II'' in July 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdempire.com/Exec/v4_item.asp?item_id=476758|title=Slumber Party Massacre/Slumber Party Massacre II (DVD)|publisher=dvdempire.com|access-date=April 12, 2011}}</ref> Both these versions are [[out of print]]. [[Shout! Factory]] released all three films in the [[Massacre (franchise)|series]] on a two-disc [[special edition]] DVD set in October 2010. It contained the full-length documentary ''Sleepless Nights: Revisiting the Slumber Party Massacres'', reuniting cast and crew of the original trilogy and directed by [[Jason Paul Collum]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Barton |first=Steve |date=July 13, 2010 |title=Shout! Factory Bringing Home the Slumber Party Massacre Collection! |url=https://dreadcentral.com/news/38504/shout-factory-bringing-home-slumber-party-massacre-collection |url-status= |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230427033040/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/18333/shout-factory-bringing-home-the-slumber-party-massacre-collection/ |archive-date=April 27, 2023 |work=[[Dread Central]]}}</ref>

[[Shout! Factory]], under their subsidiary label [[Scream Factory]], released ''The Slumber Party Massacre'' on [[Blu-ray]] on March 18, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Slumber-Party-Massacre-Blu-ray/79560/|work=Blu-ray.com|title=The Slumber Party Massacre Blu-ray|date=March 18, 2014|url-status=live|archive-date=April 27, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230427032754/https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Slumber-Party-Massacre-Blu-ray/79560/}}</ref> The company later re-released the film on Blu-ray in a limited edition steelbook with new extras in January 2020, followed by a double-feature set on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K UHD Blu-ray]] alongside ''[[Slumber Party Massacre II]]'' on February 21, 2023.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Slumber-Party-Massacre-and-Slumber-Party-Massacre-II-4K-Blu-ray/328286/|work=Blu-ray.com|title=The Slumber Party Massacre / Slumber Party Massacre II 4K Blu-ray|date=February 21, 2023|url-status=live|archive-date=April 27, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230427032926/https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Slumber-Party-Massacre-and-Slumber-Party-Massacre-II-4K-Blu-ray/328286/}}</ref>

In the United Kingdom, the [[British Board of Film Classification]] (BBFC) re-titled the film ''The Slumber Party Murders'', as the word "massacre" was felt to be too suggestive.{{Sfn|Harper|2004|p=166}} In the U.K., it has had two releases on DVD, with both editions containing no special features.

==Sequels==
There have been two [[sequel]]s to the film, ''[[Slumber Party Massacre II]]'' (1987) and ''[[Slumber Party Massacre III]]'' (1990). Jason Paul Collum directed the documentary ''Sleepless Nights: Revisiting the Slumber Party Massacres'' (2010).<ref>{{Cite web |title='Slumber Party Massacre' DVD Series Getting Re-Released |url=http://www.horrorbid.com/blog/blog1.php/2010/02/05/slumber-party-massacre-dvd-series-gettin |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120227200003/http://www.horrorbid.com/blog/blog1.php/2010/02/05/slumber-party-massacre-dvd-series-gettin |archive-date=2012-02-27 |access-date=2025-11-11 |website=www.horrorbid.com |language=en-US}}</ref> Other films in the [[Massacre (franchise)|''Massacre'' film series]] includes a second trilogy: ''[[Sorority House Massacre]]'' (1986), ''[[Sorority House Massacre II|Sorority House Massacre II: Nighty Nightmare]]'' (1990), and ''[[Sorority House Massacre III: Hard to Die]]'' (1990), and a third series: ''[[Cheerleader Massacre]]'' (2003) and ''Cheerleader Massacre 2'' (2011), the former filmed as ''Slumber Party Massacre 4'' before being renamed during post-production.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.retroslashers.net/interviewwynorski.htm |title=Interview with Director Jim Wynorski - Retro Slashers |website=www.retroslashers.net |access-date=January 12, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071022122748/http://www.retroslashers.net/interviewwynorski.htm |archive-date=22 October 2007 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
