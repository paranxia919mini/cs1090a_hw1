'''''Spider-Man: No Way Home''''' is a 2021 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[Marvel Comics]] character [[Spider-Man]]. Produced by [[Columbia Pictures]], [[Marvel Studios]], and [[Pascal Pictures]], and distributed by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]], it is the sequel to ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017) and ''[[Spider-Man: Far From Home]]'' (2019), and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 27th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Jon Watts]], written by [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]] and [[Erik Sommers]], and stars [[Tom Holland]] as [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] alongside [[Zendaya]], [[Benedict Cumberbatch]], [[Jacob Batalon]], [[Jon Favreau]], [[Jamie Foxx]], [[Willem Dafoe]], [[Alfred Molina]], [[Benedict Wong]], [[Tony Revolori]], [[Marisa Tomei]], [[Andrew Garfield]], and [[Tobey Maguire]]. In the film, Peter asks Dr. [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]] (Cumberbatch) to use magic to make his identity as Spider-Man a secret again after it was revealed to the world at the end of ''Far From Home''. When the spell goes wrong due to Peter's actions, the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]] is broken open and several visitors from alternate realities are brought into Peter's universe.

A third MCU Spider-Man film was planned during the production of ''Homecoming'' in 2017. Negotiations between Sony and Marvel Studios to alter their deal—in which they produce the Spider-Man films together—ended with Marvel Studios leaving the project in August 2019, but a negative fan reaction led to a new deal between the companies a month later. Watts, McKenna, Sommers, and Holland were set to return, and filming took place from October 2020 to March 2021 in New York City and Atlanta. ''No Way Home'' serves as a [[Crossover (fiction)|crossover]] between the MCU and the previous [[Spider-Man in film|''Spider-Man'' films]] directed by [[Sam Raimi]] and [[Marc Webb]]. Several actors reprise their roles from those films, including previous Spider-Man actors Maguire and Garfield. The pair's involvement was the subject of wide speculation and numerous leaks despite Sony, Marvel, and the cast's efforts to conceal their involvement until the film's release.

''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' premiered at the [[Fox Village Theater]] in [[Westwood, Los Angeles]], on December 13, 2021, and was theatrically released in the United States on December 17, as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU. The film received positive reviews from critics and grossed $1.921{{nbsp}}billion worldwide, surpassing its predecessor as the highest-grossing film released by Sony Pictures at the time. It became the [[highest-grossing film of 2021]], the [[List of highest-grossing films#Highest-grossing films|<!--peak position, not current, not to be updated constantly-->sixth-highest-grossing film]] at the time of its release, the highest-grossing Spider-Man film at the time<!--prior to Brand New Day-->, and set several other box office records, including those for [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema|films released during the COVID-19 pandemic]]. The film received a nomination for [[Academy Award for Best Visual Effects|Best Visual Effects]] at the [[94th Academy Awards]], among [[List of accolades received by Spider-Man: No Way Home|numerous other accolades]]. An extended version, subtitled ''The More Fun Stuff Version'', was theatrically released in September<!-- Do not add a specific date here as it was released globally throughout the month.--> 2022. An additional planned film trilogy with Holland began with ''[[Spider-Man: Brand New Day]]'', which was released in July 2026.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words. -->
After [[Quentin Beck (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quentin Beck]] frames [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker]] for his murder and reveals that Peter is [[Spider-Man]], the [[Department of Damage Control]] arrests Peter; his girlfriend, [[MJ (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Michelle "MJ" Jones-Watson]]; his best friend, [[Ned Leeds (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ned Leeds]]; and his aunt, [[May Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|May Parker]]. Lawyer [[Matt Murdock (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Matt Murdock]] gets Peter's charges dropped, but the group grapples with negative publicity. After Peter's, MJ's, and Ned's [[MIT]] applications are rejected, Peter goes to the [[New York Sanctum]] to ask Dr. [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]] for help. Strange starts casting a spell that would make everyone forget Peter is Spider-Man, but it is corrupted when Peter repeatedly requests alterations to let his loved ones retain their memories. Strange contains the corrupted spell.

At Strange's suggestion, Peter tries to convince an MIT administrator to reconsider MJ's and Ned's applications. He is attacked by [[Otto Octavius (film character)|Otto Octavius]], who rips [[nanotechnology]] from Peter's [[Iron Spider (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Iron Spider]] suit. This bonds with Octavius's mechanical tentacles and allows Peter to control them. As [[Norman Osborn (2002 film series character)|Norman Osborn]] arrives and attacks, Strange teleports Peter back to the Sanctum and locks Octavius in a cell next to [[Curt Connors (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Curt Connors]]. Strange explains that the corrupted spell summoned people from other universes within the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]] who know Spider-Man's identity. He orders Peter, MJ, and Ned to find and capture the others; they locate and retrieve [[Max Dillon (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Max Dillon]] and [[Flint Marko (Sam Raimi film series)|Flint Marko]] at a military research facility.

Osborn reclaims control of himself from his split [[Green Goblin]] personality and destroys the Goblin mask. He gets help from May until Peter retrieves him. While discussing their battles with Spider-Man, Osborn, Octavius, and Dillon realize they were pulled from their universes just before their deaths.{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' (2002), ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' (2004), and ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man 2]]'' (2014), respectively}} Strange prepares to reverse the contained spell and send the villains back to their respective universes, but Peter argues that they should first help each villain to possibly change their fates upon their return. When Strange refuses, Peter steals the spell, traps Strange in the [[Mirror Dimension]], and takes the villains to [[Happy Hogan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Happy Hogan]]'s apartment. He uses [[Stark Industries (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stark Industries]] technology to cure Octavius. Before Peter can cure anyone else, the Goblin persona retakes control of Osborn. The Goblin convinces the other villains to betray Peter and fatally wounds May as Dillon, Marko, and Connors escape; before she dies, May tells Peter that "[[with great power, there must also come great responsibility]]".

Ned discovers that he can create portals using Strange's [[sling ring]], which he and MJ use to try to locate Peter. They instead find "[[Peter Parker (2002 film series character)|Peter-Two]]" and "[[Peter Parker (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Peter-Three]]", [[Alternative versions of Spider-Man|alternate versions of Peter]] who were also summoned from the villains' universes by Strange's spell. The group finds this universe's Peter ("Peter-One"), who is mourning May and ready to send the villains home to die. The alternate Peters share stories of losing [[Uncle Ben]]{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' (2002)}} and [[Gwen Stacy (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Gwen Stacy]]{{efn|As depicted in ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man 2]]'' (2014)}} and encourage Peter-One to fight in May's honor. The three Peters develop cures for the villains and lure them to the [[Statue of Liberty]]. Peter-One and Peter-Two cure Connors and Marko while Octavius helps cure Dillon. Ned accidentally frees Strange from the Mirror Dimension. The Goblin unleashes the contained spell, breaking the barriers between universes and pulling in countless others who know Peter's identity. Strange attempts to hold them off while an enraged Peter-One tries to kill the Goblin. Peter-Two stops him and Peter-Three helps Peter-One inject the Goblin with his cure, restoring Osborn's sanity.

Peter-One realizes that the only way to protect the multiverse is to erase himself from everyone's memory and requests Strange do so while promising MJ and Ned that he will find them and remind them who he is. Strange reluctantly casts the spell, and everyone returns to their respective universes—<!--DO NOT MOVE THIS TO A SEPARATE PARAGRAPH-->including [[Eddie Brock (Sony's Spider-Man Universe)|Eddie Brock]],{{efn|Brock was teleported to the MCU by Strange's first spell as seen at the end of [[Sony's Spider-Man Universe]] (SSU) film ''[[Venom: Let There Be Carnage]]'' (2021).}} who unknowingly leaves behind a piece of the [[Venom (Sony's Spider-Man Universe)|Venom]] [[Symbiote (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|symbiote]].<!--DO NOT MOVE THIS TO A SEPARATE PARAGRAPH--> Two weeks later, Peter visits MJ to reintroduce himself to her and Ned, but decides against it. While mourning at May's grave, he has a conversation with an unknowing Hogan and is inspired to carry on, making a new Spider-Man suit and resuming his vigilantism.<!-- Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], do NOT add the Multiverse of Madness teaser here, as that is what it is: a teaser trailer added to the end of the film. == Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Tom Holland & Jon Watts (28620388656).jpg|thumb|250px|Lead actor [[Tom Holland]] and director [[Jon Watts]] in 2016]]
During production on the film ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017), two sequels were being planned by [[Marvel Studios]] and [[Sony Pictures]].<ref name="/FilmSMHSetVisit" /> In June 2017, star [[Tom Holland]] explained that each film would take place during a different year of high school for [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]], with the third being set during the character's senior year.<ref name="FFHSequelSeniorYear" /> Marvel Studios President [[Kevin Feige]] noted in July 2019 that the third film would feature "a Peter Parker story that has never been done before on film" due to the ending of the second film, ''[[Spider-Man: Far From Home]]'' (2019), which publicly reveals that Peter is Spider-Man.<ref name="FeigeFFHSequel" />

Development on third and fourth MCU [[Spider-Man]] films had begun by August 2019, with Sony hoping Holland and director [[Jon Watts]] would return for both;<ref name="NoNewSonyDealDeadline" /> Holland was contracted to return for one more film, while Watts had completed his two-film deal and would need to sign on for any more films.<ref name="SonyNoPlaybook" /><ref name="WattsDeadlineAug2019" /> By then, Marvel Studios and its parent company [[Walt Disney Studios (division)|The Walt Disney Studios]] had spent several months discussing expanding their deal with Sony. The existing deal had Marvel and Feige produce the Spider-Man films for Sony and receive 5% of their revenue. Sony wanted to expand the deal to include more films while keeping the same terms of the original agreement. Disney expressed concern with Feige's workload producing the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) franchise already and asked for a 25–50% stake in any future films Feige produced for Sony.<ref name="NoNewSonyDealDeadline" /><ref name="NoNewDealSonyResponse" /><ref name="WattsDeadlineAug2019" /> Unable to come to an agreement, Sony announced that it would be moving forward on the next Spider-Man film without Feige or Marvel's involvement. Their statement acknowledged that this could change in the future, thanked Feige for his work on the first two films, and said they appreciated "the path [Feige] has helped put us on, which we will continue."<ref name="NoNewDealSonyResponse" /> [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]] and [[Erik Sommers]] were writing the screenplay for the third film by the time of Sony's announcement, after also doing so for ''Far From Home'', but Watts was receiving offers to direct large films for other studios instead of returning to the franchise, including potentially working on a different property for Marvel Studios and Feige.<ref name="WattsDeadlineAug2019" />

In September 2019, Sony Pictures Entertainment chairman [[Tony Vinciquerra]] said that "for the moment the door is closed" on Spider-Man returning to the MCU and confirmed that the character would be integrated with Sony's own shared universe—[[Sony's Spider-Man Universe]] (SSU)—moving forward. Responding to backlash from fans following the announcement, Vinciquerra added that "the Marvel people are terrific people, we have great respect for them, but on the other hand we have some pretty terrific people of our own. [Feige] didn't do all the work ... we're pretty capable of doing what we have to do here."<ref name="VinciquerraMCUSep2019" /> However, after this fan reaction continued at Disney's biennial convention [[D23 Expo|D23]], and at the urging of Holland who personally spoke to [[Disney]] CEO [[Bob Iger]] and [[Sony Pictures Motion Picture Group]] chairman [[Tom Rothman]],<ref name="HollandTHROct2019" /> the companies returned to negotiations.<ref name="DeadlineSonyMarvelDealSM3" /> Sony and Disney announced a new agreement at the end of September which would allow Marvel Studios and Feige to produce another MCU Spider-Man film for Sony with [[Amy Pascal]], scheduled for July 16, 2021.<ref name="SonyMarvelDealSM3" /> Disney was reportedly co-financing 25% of the film in exchange for 25% of its profits, while retaining the character's merchandising rights.<ref name="SonyMarvelDealSM3" /><ref name="DeadlineSonyMarvelDealSM3" /> Feige said he was "thrilled" that the character would remain in the MCU and said "all of us at Marvel Studios are very excited that we get to keep working" on the franchise. The agreement also allowed Holland's Spider-Man to appear in a future Marvel Studios film as well as crossing over to Sony's own shared universe,<ref name="SonyMarvelDealSM3" /> with the latter interaction described as "a 'call and answer' between the two franchises as they acknowledge details between the two in what would loosely be described as a shared detailed universe". Sony described their previous films with Marvel Studios as a "great collaboration", and said "our mutual desire to continue was equal to that of the many fans."<ref name="DeadlineSonyMarvelDealSM3" /> At the time of the new agreement, Watts was in final negotiations to direct the film.<ref name="WattsNegotiations" />

Discussing the new deal in early October, Iger attributed it to the efforts of Holland as well as the fan response to the end of the original deal. He felt that both Sony and Disney had initially forgotten that "there are other people who actually matter" while they were negotiating.<ref name="IgerSonyMarvelOct2019" /> Rothman said the deal was a "win-win-win. A win for Sony, a win for Disney, a win for the fans." He felt the initial reports on the negotiations did not necessarily line up with the actual discussions that were taking place, and said the final deal would have eventuated without the reports and fan discourse.<ref name="THRProducerRoundtable" /> By the middle of the month, [[Zendaya]] was confirmed to be reprising her role as [[MJ (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Michelle "MJ" Jones-Watson]] from the previous films in the sequel.<ref name="Zendaya" /><ref name="FullNames" />

=== Pre-production ===
McKenna and Sommers began working on the script in earnest by December 2019.<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021THR" /><ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" /> They considered featuring [[Kraven the Hunter]] as the film's main antagonist,<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021THR" /> an idea that Watts had expressed interest in and [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] to Holland,<ref name="KravenVillain" /><ref name="HollandKravenIdea" /> but was abandoned because Sony wanted to first feature the character in [[Kraven the Hunter (film)|a solo film]] before any other projects.<ref name="KravenUse" /> The plot subsequently gravitated towards a story idea similar to the film ''[[It's a Wonderful Life]]'' (1946) in which Peter makes a wish regarding his now public identity. That idea introduced Dr. [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]] to the story, and the duo began exploring the idea of the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]] and potentially revisiting characters from past ''Spider-Man'' films. Initially, they thought this would just be a tease for fans, but they ultimately decided to fully integrate the past characters into the plot. Sommers said, "Once it was collectively decided that we were going to take this swing, we had to commit and we had to do what was right for the story." They optimistically wrote for all the characters they wanted in the film before those actors were confirmed to be returning.<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021THR" /> Initial drafts of the film included every major character from past ''Spider-Man'' films returning, but this was narrowed down because the pair felt they had "bit[ten] off more than [they] could chew". The duo worked hard to prevent the film from just being "a bunch of fan service" by using the returning characters to help tell Peter Parker's story instead of just creating "curtain calls for everybody".<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" /> [[Norman Osborn (2002 film series character)|Norman Osborn / Green Goblin]] from [[Sam Raimi]]'s [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' trilogy]] was not the main villain of the film in the first version of the script, despite still appearing as an antagonist; after the film lost "other characters", McKenna and Sommers concluded that the Green Goblin "had to be" the main villain and rewrote the script to give him a second chance to replicate his actions in the film ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' (2002), but in a darker way related to Holland's version of Spider-Man.<ref name="GoblinPromoted" /> [[America Chavez (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|America Chavez]] had been considered to appear in the film at one point to serve in the sorcerer's apprentice-type role that eventually became part of [[Ned Leeds (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ned Leeds]]'s role in the film; she would go on to appear in the MCU film ''[[Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness]]'' (2022).<ref name="AmericaChavez" />

By the end of 2019, filming was expected to begin in mid-2020.<ref name="UnchartedFilmingSchedule" /> By March 2020, McKenna and Sommers were still working on the first draft of the script.<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" /> In April, Sony rescheduled the film's release to November 5, 2021, due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="November2021Date" /> Although the film was originally meant to be set after the events of ''Multiverse of Madness'', release date changes meant the film would be released first which meant aspects of the plot had to be rewritten,<ref name="HollandNov2021GQ" /> including Strange no longer having firsthand knowledge of the multiverse. McKenna felt this made it "even more frightening, to start fooling around with these things, because it's the fear of the unknown".<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" /> In June, [[Marisa Tomei]] confirmed that she would return as [[May Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|May Parker]] along with Watts as director. She was hopeful that May's work as a community organizer would be featured in the film.<ref name="TomeiWatts" /> Also in June, [[Charlie Cox]], who portrayed [[Matt Murdock (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Matt Murdock / Daredevil]] in [[Marvel Television]]'s [[Marvel's Netflix television series|Netflix series]], was contacted by Feige about reprising his role in upcoming Marvel Studios projects starting with appearances in ''No Way Home'' and the [[Disney+]] series ''[[She-Hulk: Attorney at Law]]'' (2022).<ref name="CoxTHR" /><ref name="CoxVarietySH" /> While working on the script, McKenna and Sommers discussed the possibility of giving Murdock a larger role in the film than they had originally planned, though the duo ultimately decided against this to avoid detracting the film's focus on Peter.<ref name="McKennaSommersDaredevilPlans" /> The next month, Holland said production was planned to take place from late 2020 to February 2021,<ref name="FilmingTimeline" /> and Sony shifted the film's release date to December 17, 2021.<ref name="December2021Date" /> [[Tony Revolori]] was also confirmed to be reprising his role as [[Flash Thompson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Flash Thompson]].<ref name="Revolori" /> In early October, [[Jacob Batalon]] and [[Benedict Cumberbatch]] were revealed to be reprising their MCU roles of Ned Leeds and Strange, while [[Jamie Foxx]] was set to return as [[Max Dillon (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Max Dillon / Electro]] from [[Marc Webb]]'s film ''[[The Amazing Spider-Man 2]]'' (2014), with filming beginning later in the month.<ref name="Foxx" /><ref name="Cumberbatch" /> Immediately prior to the beginning of filming, several other key actors in the film had yet to sign on. According to Holland, the film needed "all or none" of the actors to be produced.<ref name="HollandNov2021GQ" />

=== Filming ===
[[Second unit]] filming occurred from October 14 to 16, 2020, in New York City,<ref name="2ndPrincipalFilming" /><ref name="FilmingStart" /><ref name="QueensFilming2" /> under the [[working title]] ''Serenity Now'',<ref name="WorkingTitle" /><ref name="FilmingStart" /> to capture visual effects plates and [[establishing shot]]s.<ref name="2ndPrincipalFilming" /> Filming occurred in the [[Astoria, Queens|Astoria]], [[Sunnyside, Queens|Sunnyside]], and [[Long Island City]] neighborhoods in Queens.<ref name="QueensFilming" /><ref name="QueensFilming2" /> On October 23, filming occurred in [[Greenwich Village]] in Manhattan.<ref name="GreenwichVillageFilming" />

The production moved to Atlanta by October 25, with Holland, Batalon, and Zendaya joining for [[principal photography]],<ref name="HollandFilmingStart" /><ref name="ZendayaAtlantaFilming" /> after Holland finished shooting the Sony film ''[[Uncharted (film)|Uncharted]]'' (2022) two days earlier.<ref name="HollandUnchartedFilming" /><ref name="2ndPrincipalFilming" /><ref name="HollandDec2020" /> [[Mauro Fiore]] served as cinematographer on the film,<ref name="Fiore" /> replacing [[Seamus McGarvey]],<ref name="McGarvey" /> who had to leave the production after contracting [[COVID-19]]. McGarvey also had a conflict with the film ''[[Cyrano (film)|Cyrano]]'' (2021) following the third MCU ''Spider-Man'' film's pandemic-caused production delay,<ref name="McGarveyLeave" /> as principal photography was originally planned to start in July 2020.<ref name="FilmingLocations" /><ref name="ProductionDelay" /><ref name="FilmingTimeline" /> Shooting in Atlanta occurred at [[Trilith Studios]], with strict safety measures in place on the soundstages to prevent exposure to COVID-19.<ref name="AtlantaStageFilming" /> To reduce interactions between cast and crew members on set during the pandemic, the production reportedly relied on "innovative new technology" that scanned actors into a visual effects system that can apply make-up and costumes to actors during post-production.<ref name="FilmingVFXScan" /> A light system was also in-place to signal when the cast could take off their masks for filming and when masks would be required for the cast and crew members to wear while set work was being done.<ref name="FilmingLightSystem" /> Cumberbatch began shooting his scenes in Atlanta by late November, before beginning work on ''Multiverse of Madness'' which began filming that month in London.<ref name="CumberbatchAtlantaFilming" /><ref name="CumberbatchFilming" /> Filming ran for seven-to-eight weeks using the working titles ''Serenity Now'' and ''The November Project'', before a break during the 2020 [[Christmas season]].<ref name="HollandFeb2021" /><ref name="HollandDec2020" /><ref name="WorkingTitlesDec2020" />

By December 2020, [[Alfred Molina]] was set to reprise his role as [[Otto Octavius (film character)|Otto Octavius / Doctor Octopus]] from Raimi's film ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' (2004),<ref name="Molina" /> with [[Tobey Maguire]] reprising his role as [[Peter Parker (2002 film series character)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] from Raimi's ''Spider-Man'' trilogy and [[Andrew Garfield]] returning as [[Peter [[cameo appearance]] of Maguire and Garfield alongside Holland as their respective Spider-Men had previously been considered for the [[Sony Pictures Animation]] film ''[[Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse]]'' (2018), but was cut then because Sony felt at that moment that it would be too risky and confusing.<ref name="ITSV Attempt" /> At that time, ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]'' reported that Maguire and Garfield would appear in the film alongside [[Kirsten Dunst]] as [[Mary Jane Watson (2002 film series character)|Mary Jane Watson]] from Raimi's ''Spider-Man'' film trilogy and [[Emma Stone]] as [[Gwen Stacy (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Gwen Stacy]] from the ''Amazing Spider-Man'' films.<ref name="DunstStone" /> The first draft of the script included other characters from the ''Spider-Man'' and ''Amazing Spider-Man'' films, such as [[Sally Field]]'s [[May Parker]], but the writers felt that including too many characters would negatively affect the story.<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" /> The rumors about returning actors led to speculation and commentary, with Richard Newby of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' believing that a They had not been able to focus on those scenes earlier because they were busy during filming of much of the first and second acts in the previous months.<ref name="McKennaSommersDec2021Variety" />

Feige confirmed in December 2020 that the film would have connections to ''Multiverse of Madness''.<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /> A month later he discussed the fact that the film's title had not yet been announced, acknowledging that it was being referred to by some as ''Spider-Man 3'' and revealing that Marvel was referring to it as ''Homecoming 3'' internally.<ref name="FeigeNotSpiderMan3" /> Cox had shot material for the film by then.<ref name="Cox" /> An Atlanta set photo indicated that the film would be set during the Christmas season.<ref name="Christmas" /> Filming occurred at [[Douglass High School (Atlanta)|Frederick Douglass High School]] from January 22 to 24.<ref name="AJCFilming" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Ramakrishnan |first=Anagha |date=December 27, 2021 |title=Did you catch the Atlanta spots that 'Spider-Man: No Way Home' filmed? == Marketing ==
In May 2020, Sony entered a promotional partnership with [[Hyundai Motor Group]] to showcase their new models and technologies in the film;<ref name="Hyundai" /> Hyundai later released a commercial, titled "Only Way Home", that was directed by Watts to promote the film and the [[Ioniq 5]] electric-powered [[SUV]] (which appears in the film along with the [[Hyundai Tucson]]), with Holland and Batalon starring.<ref name="HyundaiCommercial" /> In late February 2021, Holland, Batalon, and Zendaya released three stills featuring their characters from the film alongside fake logos with the titles ''Spider-Man: Phone Home'', ''Spider-Man: Home-Wrecker'', and ''Spider-Man: Home Slice'', respectively.<ref name="CNETTitlesStills" /> The film's official title was announced the next day with a video showing Holland, Batalon, and Zendaya leaving Watts's office (where they supposedly received the fake titles). Batalon and Zendaya note that Holland could not be trusted with the actual title since he had "accidentally" revealed the second film's title. The video ends on a whiteboard showing the film's true title, among various other titles featuring the word "home" that were apparently considered.<ref name="NoWayHomeIGN" /> Jennifer Bisset of ''[[CNET]]'' suggested the fake titles and logos could represent the villains in the film, including Foxx's Electro and Molina's Doctor Octopus,<ref name="CNETTitlesStills" /> while ''[[TheWrap]]''{{'}}s Umberto Gonzalez called them funny "[[bait-and-switch]] fakes", and noted the ''Phone Home'' title referenced a line from ''[[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial]]'' (1982).<ref name="TheWrapTitlesStills" /> Gregory Lawrence of ''Collider'' felt the ''Home-Wrecker'' title could point to the film feeling like a 1990s [[thriller film]], and said the fake titles were a "solid goof" to excite fans. He also compared the stills to the "terrifying/awe-inspiring wonder" of [[Steven Spielberg]] films and ''[[The Goonies]]'' (1985),<ref name="ColliderTitlesStills" /> while Germain Lussier from ''[[io9]]'' said they gave off "subtle ''[[National Treasure (film series)|National Treasure]]'', ''[[Indiana Jones]]'' vibes".<ref name="io9TitlesStills" /> In July 2021, Marvel revealed various toys and figurines for the film, including [[Funko Pop]]s, [[Marvel Legends]] figures, and [[Lego]] sets.<ref name="JulyMerchandise" />

At the end of August 2021, when asked about the lack of trailer and official images or descriptions for the film, Feige stated that the film was not being "any more or less secret than any of our other projects" and reaffirmed that a trailer would be released before the film's premiere in theaters.<ref name="FeigeTrailerAug2021" /> Though Sony handles the marketing for the film, their marketing team is in coordination with Disney's to ensure each knows when the other is releasing MCU-related content so it is a "win-win for everybody".<ref name="AyazJun2021" /> On August 22, an apparent leak of the first trailer was shared on social media,<ref name="TrailerLeakTHR" /><ref name="TrailerLeakEW" /><ref name="TrailerLeakTotalFilm" /> which ''The Hollywood Reporter'' deemed "legitimate" after Sony worked to have various copies of the trailer taken down.<ref name="TrailerLeakTHR" /> Adam Chitwood at ''Collider'' noted the built-up online "fervor" surrounding the trailer and felt that it would not be able to "live up to the hype fans have built up in their minds". Chitwood continued that the other 2021 film releases from Marvel Studios had not seen a similar level of demand as ''No Way Home'', pointing out that all of the rumored castings had positioned the film to be a "once-in-a-lifetime moviegoing experience" if they were accurate. He also wondered if Sony was wary of committing to marketing that noted the film's December 2021 release date amid the resurgence of the [[COVID-19 Delta variant]].<ref name="ColliderMarketingExpectations" />

The film's [[teaser trailer]] was officially released on August 23 during Sony's [[CinemaCon]] 2021 panel.<ref name="THRTrailer" /> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'}}s Devan Coggan noted that the trailer confirmed the multiverse's role in the film, including elements from the Raimi and Webb films,<ref name="EWTrailer" /> while Ethan Anderton from ''/Film'' called the trailer "nothing short of thrilling" since it confirmed many of the previous rumors about the film.<ref name="/FilmTrailerBreakdown" /> Austen Goslin at ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'' conversely felt much of what had been rumored did not get revealed in the trailer, feeling that the rumors were either false or Marvel was still intending to keep them a secret.<ref name="PolygonTrailer" /> Anderton's colleague Joshua Meyer called the trailer "a doozy... packed with jaw-dropping moments" and noted how the film would be adapting the "[[One More Day (comics)|One More Day]]" comic book storyline;<ref name="/FilmTrailer" /> Newby had previously noted the apparent adaption of "One More Day" and "[[One Moment in Time (comics)|One Moment in Time]]" storylines following the reveal of Cumberbatch's casting.<ref name="NewbyTHRCastings" /> Many commentators noted the possible teases of Spider-Man villains Sandman and the Lizard in the trailer as an indication of the [[Sinister Six]] forming in the film.<ref name="TotalFilmTrailerEEs" /><ref name="ColliderTrailer7Things" /><ref name="EWTrailer22Things" /> Vinnie Mancuso at ''Collider'' was excited to see the return of Molina and the potential for Dafoe's involvement, but called it a "cheap pop" since it was doing "a disservice to the stories you're trying to tell in the present by reminding the audience how much better things used to be". He also felt the trailer played into "Marvel's ongoing reluctance to let Tom Holland's Spider-Man star in his own Spider-Man movies" since the trailer gave Peter "zero memorable moments" among all of its elements.<ref name="MancusoColliderTrailer" /> The trailer had 355.5{{nbsp}}million global views in its first 24 hours, becoming the most viewed trailer in that time period. This surpassed the record of ''Avengers: Endgame'' (289{{nbsp}}million views) and more than doubled the views for the ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' trailer (135 million). It also generated the largest 24-hour social media conversation volume of all-time globally with 4.5 million mentions, made up of 2.91 million in the United States, and 1.5 million internationally; these both exceeded ''Avengers: Endgame''{{'}}s mentions (1.94 million in the United States, 1.38 million internationally).<ref name="TrailerViews" />

The second official trailer premiered at a fan screening at the [[Regal Cinemas|Regal Sherman Oaks]] theater in Los Angeles on November 16, 2021.<ref name="TrailerFanScreening" /> Goslin felt the trailer "reveals the full extent of Marvel's Spider-Man multiverse",<ref name="GoslinPolygonSecondTrailer" /> while his colleague Matt Patches noted the missing appearance of Maguire's or Garfield's Spider-Man in the trailer but felt that it was "entirely possible the actors appear in ''No Way Home''".<ref name="PatchesPolygonSecondTrailer" /> Jason Robbins of ''Collider'' was disappointed by the trailer, saying it was "what we expected, but less", since it just showed the returning villains and had no confirmation of Maguire or Garfield or "further insight into the multiverse".<ref name="ColliderSecondTrailer" /> Some commentators said parts of the trailer appeared as though Maguire and Garfield had been edited out of the footage, such as a shot in which the Lizard appears to be struck by an invisible force.<ref name="IGNSecondTrailerEdits" /><ref name="CBRSecondTrailerEdits" /> On November 24, Sony began releasing several videos on [[TikTok]] as part of their ''[[The Daily Bugle (web series)|The Daily Bugle]]'' [[viral marketing]] campaign featuring Simmons and Rice.<ref name="DailyBugleTikTok" /> In December 2021, the first minute of the film premiered exclusively on ''[[Late Night with Seth Meyers]]'',<ref name="NoWayHomeOpeningScene" /> while a [[newsstand]] for ''The Daily Bugle'' was set up in New York City in partnership with [[Liberty Mutual]] to promote the film.<ref name="TheDailyBugleNewsstand" /> Other marketing partners include the video games ''[[Fortnite]]'' and ''[[PUBG Mobile]]'', which featured special in-game Spider-Man themed props and costumes, [[Asus]], whose [[Republic of Gamers]] (ROG) laptop is used by Ned in the film, [[Xiaomi]], [[BBK Electronics|iQOO]], [[Continental AG]], and [[Tampico Beverages]]. The film had a total promotional marketing value of $202{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="DeadlineMarketing" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical ===
''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' had its world premiere at [[Fox Village Theater]] in [[Westwood, Los Angeles]], on December 13, 2021.<ref name="WorldPremiereUPI" /><ref name="WorldPremiereVariety" /> The film was released in the United Kingdom and Ireland on December 15,<ref name="UKRelease" /> and in the United States on December 17,<!--DO NOT LIST RELEASE FORMATS WITHOUT EXPLICIT SOURCING!--> where it opened in 4,325 theaters, including in [[RealD 3D]],<ref>{{cite tweet |url=https://x.com/RealD3D/status/1457763625326690308 |title=The Multiverse unleashed. |number=1457763625326690308 |user=RealD3D |date=November 8, 2021 |access-date=April 22, 2025}}</ref> [[IMAX]], and other [[premium large format]]s.<!--DO NOT LIST RELEASE FORMATS WITHOUT EXPLICIT SOURCING!--><ref name="DeadlineProjections" /> It was initially set for release on July 16, 2021,<ref name="SonyMarvelDealSM3" /> but was pushed back to November 5,<ref name="November2021Date" /> before it was further pushed back to the December 2021 date due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="December2021Date" /> It is part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" />

In August 2021, Sony and CJ 4DPlex announced a deal to release 15 of Sony's films over three years in the [[ScreenX]] format, including ''No Way Home''.<ref name="ScreenX" /> In November 2021, the film was reported to be getting a theatrical release in China, making it the first Phase Four film to do so as ''[[Black Widow (2021 film)|Black Widow]]'', ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'', and ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' were not released in the country,<ref name="ChinaRelease" /> though by February 2022, there had been no release date,<ref name="ChinaNoReleaseDateFeb2022" /> in part because of diplomatic tensions between the country and the United States,<ref name="ChinaNoReleaseDate" /><ref name="ChinaNoReleaseDateFeb2022" /> which stemmed in part from [[Concerns and controversies at the 2022 Winter Olympics#United States|the U.S. diplomatic boycott of the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics]].<ref name="ChinaNoReleaseDate" /> Chinese authorities requested for the [[Statue of Liberty]] to be removed from the film for it to be released in the country, but Sony refused.<ref name="StatueofLiberty" />

In March 2024, Sony announced that all of their live-action Spider-Man films would be re-released in theaters as part of Columbia Pictures' 100th anniversary celebration. ''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' was re-released on June 3, 2024.<ref name="Columbia100th" />

==== ''The More Fun Stuff Version'' ====
[[File:Spider-Man No Way Home – The More Fun Stuff Version poster.jpeg|thumb|upright=0.95|Theatrical release poster for ''The More Fun Stuff Version''. It was considered an improvement from the original poster as it featured all the characters previously kept secret with the marketing of the original release.<ref name="ColliderMFSPoster" /><ref name="GameRantMFSPoster" /><ref name="ComicBookMFSPoster" />]]

An [[extended cut]] of the film, subtitled ''The More Fun Stuff Version'', was announced in June 2022. The re-release celebrated 60 years of Spider-Man in comics and 20 years of [[Spider-Man in film]],<ref name="NWHExtendedVersion" /> and featured approximately 13 minutes of additional and deleted scenes. The additions included: an introduction by Holland, Maguire, and Garfield; additional scenes of Peter and May being interrogated by the Department of Damage Control; the scene with Holland's brother Harry as a thief that was cut from the original release; additional scenes of Peter at school; Brant interviewing Peter, his teachers, and his classmates; ''Daily Bugle'' reports of Peter's first day back at school and the arrivals of Dillon and Marko; additional scenes in the basement of the New York Sanctum; a scene featuring May, Peter, and the villains in an elevator while on the way to Hogan's apartment; an additional scene with Murdock and Hogan; additional scenes of the three Peters; and a new post-credits scene, replacing the teaser trailer of ''Multiverse of Madness'', which showcases the effects of Strange's second spell.<ref name="MoreFunStuffAddedMaterial" />

''The More Fun Stuff Version'' was released in theaters beginning August 31, 2022, in Indonesia, followed by the United States and Canada, among other countries, on September 1, and later in several other markets through October 6.<ref name="MoreFunStuffReleaseSchedule" /><ref name="MoreFunStuffReleaseSchedule2" /> The poster for the extended cut was considered an improvement on the original release's marketing, since it was able to feature all of the characters who were previously kept secret in the lead up to the film's release.<ref name="ColliderMFSPoster" /><ref name="GameRantMFSPoster" /><ref name="ComicBookMFSPoster" /> Shrishty Mishra at ''Collider'' said it was an "''Avengers''-style poster [with] a perfect blend of multiverse feels, combined with the '[[Where's Waldo]]' effect".<ref name="ColliderMFSPoster" /> Anthony Lund of ''[[MovieWeb]]'' called it an "epic poster that [the fans] have been calling out for".<ref name="MovieWebMFSPoster" /> ''[[ComicBook.com]]''{{'}}s Russ Burlingame said the poster was what "we've all been waiting for" and "a big change in the way [Sony] initially promoted the movie" since they "continued to play coy" on the cameos after the film had released.<ref name="ComicBookMFSPoster" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' grossed $814.9{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $1.107{{nbsp}}billion in other territories for a worldwide total of $1.921{{nbsp}}billion.<ref name="BOM" /><!--Update / change the amount only according to Box Office Mojo or The Numbers. DO NOT add any other sources here and DO NOT change the box office amount according to anybody other than these two.--> It became the [[highest-grossing film of 2021]],<ref name="Highest2021" /> the [[List of highest-grossing films|sixth-highest-grossing film of all time]],<ref name="HighestGrossingAllTime" /> the [[List of highest-grossing films in the United States and Canada|third-highest-grossing film in the United States and Canada]],<ref name="HighestGrossingUS" /> the highest-grossing ''Spider-Man'' film, and the highest-grossing film released by Sony.<ref name="HighestGrossingSony" /> ''No Way Home'' is also the first film since ''[[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker]]'' (2019) to gross over $1{{nbsp}}billion,<ref name="BillionCNBC" /> becoming the third-fastest to reach that milestone and the first to do so during the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="1BillionVariety" /> It surpassed ''[[Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle]]'' (2017) to become Sony Pictures' highest-grossing film in North America.<ref name="HighestGrossingSony" /> Based on a final projected worldwide gross of $1.75{{nbsp}}billion (a figure that was surpassed), ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' estimated the film's final net profit at $610{{nbsp}}million, accounting for production budgets, marketing, talent participations, and other costs versus box office grosses and home media revenues.<ref name="NetProfitDeadline" /> It returned the global revenue of [[Cineworld]], the world's second-largest cinema operator, to 88% of 2019 levels.<ref name="Cineworld" />

In the United States and Canada, ''No Way Home'' earned $121.85{{nbsp}}million (which included $50{{nbsp}}million from its Thursday night previews) on its opening day, becoming the second-highest opening film after ''Endgame'' ($157.4{{nbsp}}million) and the highest-opening film for a December release. In its opening weekend, it grossed $260{{nbsp}}million, surpassing ''Infinity War'' ($257.7{{nbsp}}million) to become the [[List of box office records in the United States and Canada|second-highest domestic box office opening]] of all time.<ref name="DeadlineNWHPreviewRecord" /> {{As of|2022|1|9|df=US}}, an estimated 54.4{{nbsp}}million people have seen ''No Way Home'' in theaters in the United States and Canada.<ref name="4thWkndDeadline" /> The film remained at the top of the box office for three additional weekends until it was dethroned in its fifth weekend by ''[[Scream (2022 film)|Scream]]''.<ref name="2ndWkndDeadline" /><ref name="3rdWkndDeadline" /><ref name="4thWkndDeadline" /><ref name="5thWkdDeadline" /> In its sixth weekend of release, ''No Way Home'' reclaimed the top spot at the box office.<ref name="6thWkndVariety" /> The film once again held onto the number one spot in its seventh weekend of release.<ref name="7thWkndDeadline" /> In March 2022, the film surpassed $800{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, becoming the third film to do so after ''[[Star Wars: The Force Awakens]]'' (2015) and ''Endgame''. [[Comscore]] Senior Media Analyst Paul Dergarabedian called the film "arguably the most important movie to the movie theater industry ever", as it helped show the viability of the movie theater industry and the benefit of theatrical release windows while the industry recovered from the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="800Million" /> The film dropped to the 11th spot on its 15th weekend.<ref name="15thWkndBoxOfficeMojo" /> ''No Way Home'' returned to first place at the box office, earning $1.8{{nbsp}}million on the first day of the release of ''The More Fun Stuff Version'',<ref name="MoreFunStuffTopBO" /> and went on to finish third with $6.5{{nbsp}}million over the four-day [[Labor Day]] weekend, finishing behind ''[[Top Gun: Maverick]]'' (2022) and ''[[Bullet Train (film)|Bullet Train]]'' (2022).<ref name="MoreFunStuffBoxOffice" /> It was the highest opening weekend box office gross for a re-released MCU film, surpassing ''Endgame'' ($5.5{{nbsp}}million) and ''Far From Home'' ($4{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="MoreFunStuffBoxOfficeRecord" />

''No Way Home'' earned $43.6{{nbsp}}million from 15 markets on its opening day, with Sony holding the best opening-day record in South Korea, the United Kingdom, Mexico, Italy, and Taiwan. In South Korea, the film grossed $5.28{{nbsp}}million on its opening day, thereby beating ''Far From Home''{{'}}s opening day in the country by over 11% and the biggest day-one figure for any film during the pandemic. In the United Kingdom, the film beat ''[[No Time to Die]]''{{'}}s opening box office record at £7.6{{nbsp}}million ($10.1{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="NWHBoxOfficeDec15" /> In India, the film's box office on its opening day was {{INRConvert|320|m|year=2021}} to {{INRConvert|345|m|year=2021}}, beating ''Endgame'' and the Indian film ''[[Sooryavanshi]]'' (2021).<ref name="IndiaOpeningDay" /> In its five-day opening weekend, the film grossed $340.8{{nbsp}}million from 60 markets.<ref name="IntlOpeningWkndDeadline" /> In [[Latin America]] as of January 16, 2022, it became the all-time highest-grossing film in Mexico ({{US$|72{{nbsp}}million|long=no}}), and the second all-time highest in Brazil ({{US$|50.4{{nbsp}}million|long=no}}), [[Central America]] ({{US$|12.6{{nbsp}}million|long=no}}) and Ecuador ({{US$|7.9{{nbsp}}million|long=no}}).<ref name="LatinAmericaBO" /> {{As of|2022|2|27|df=US}}, the film's largest markets are the United Kingdom ($127.3{{nbsp}}million), Mexico ($76.2{{nbsp}}million), Australia ($67.9{{nbsp}}million), France ($65.2{{nbsp}}million), and South Korea ($63.1{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="TopMarkets" />

==== Pre-sale ticket records ====
Tickets went on sale the midnight of November 29, 2021, with several ticket websites such as [[Fandango Media|Fandango]] and [[AMC Theatres]] crashing due to the high influx of users attempting to purchase tickets.<ref name="PresaleTicketCrash" /> Ticket sales on Fandango surpassed those for ''Black Widow'' in just two hours, and by the end of the day it became the best first-day advance ticket sale since ''Endgame'',<ref name="PresaleDeadline" /> while also surpassing the 24-hour ticket sales of ''Infinity War'', ''[[Star Wars: The Last Jedi]]'' (2017), ''Far From Home'', ''The Rise of Skywalker'' and ''[[Rogue One]]'' (2016).<ref name="24HrPresaleDeadline" /> ''No Way Home'' had the second-highest one-day ticket sales on AMC, with CEO [[Adam Aron]] attributing this to Spider-Man-themed [[non-fungible token]]s (NFTs).<ref name="24HrPresaleDeadline" />

The film also set records in Mexico with $7{{nbsp}}million in the first-day ticket sale, which was 40% above ''Endgame''. In the United Kingdom, the film outsold ''No Time to Die'' three times in the same twelve-day span before their release, while Brazil's ticket sale was 5% above ''Endgame'' at the same point. The film also passed the presale records of ''The Rise of Skywalker'' in Poland and ''No Time to Die'' in Portugal. Other markets with the best presale records include Spain, Brazil, and Central America.<ref name="OverseasPresale" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of {{RT data|score}}, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews. The site's critical consensus reads: "A bigger, bolder ''Spider-Man'' sequel, ''No Way Home'' expands the franchise's scope and stakes without losing sight of its humor and heart."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=spider_man_no_way_home |type=m |title=Spider-Man: No Way Home |access-date=October 21, 2024}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 71 out of 100, based on 60 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=spider-man-no-way-home |type=movie |title=Spider-Man: No Way Home |access-date=January 9, 2022}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a rare "A+" grade on an A+ to F scale, the first live-action Spider-Man film and the fourth MCU film overall to earn the score after ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012), ''[[Black Panther (film)|Black Panther]]'' (2018), and ''Avengers: Endgame''.<ref name="DeadlineNWHPreviewRecord" /> [[PostTrak]] reported 96% of audience members gave it a positive score, with 91% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="DeadlineNWHPreviewRecord" />

Amelia Emberwing of ''[[IGN]]'' gave the film 8 out of 10, stating that its "impact on the universe as a whole, as well as the overall emotional beats, all feel earned" while praising the performances of Dafoe, Molina, and Foxx.<ref name="EmberwingIGNReview" /> Pete Hammond of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' praised Watts's direction and wrote, "Holland, Zendaya, and Batalon are a priceless trio, and the various villains and 'others' who pop in and out make this pure movie fun of the highest order. Fans will be in heaven".<ref name="HammondDeadlineReview" /> Peter Debruge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised Garfield's and Maguire's performances and felt the film "provides enough resolution for the past two decades of Spider-Man adventures that audiences who've tuned out along the way will be rewarded for giving this one a shot".<ref name="MaguireGarfieldConfirmed" /> Writing for ''[[Den of Geek]]'', Don Kaye gave the film 4 out of 5 stars, for its action sequences, performances and chemistry of the cast, stating that "''No Way Home'' channels the entire spectrum of Spider-Man movies while setting the character on a course all his own at last".<ref name="KayeDenOfGeekReview" /> Jennifer Bisset of ''[[CNET]]'' praised the action sequences, performances, and story, writing: "A Russo Brothers influence can almost be felt ushering Holland's third Spider-Man movie into new, weightier territory. If the character is to become the next Tony Stark, this is the way to etch a few more scars into a more interesting hero's facade. If you came for the biggest movie of the year, you'll definitely leave satisfied".<ref name="BissetCNETReview" /> In a 4 out of 5 review, [[Sandra Hall (writer)|Sandra Hall]] of ''[[The Sydney Morning Herald|Sydney Morning Hall]]'' stated that "it's a form of breaking the fourth wall, inviting the audience in to share a joke with the actors, and verges on parody. Yet somehow they carry it off without diluting our sense of involvement with Peter and those he loves".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.smh.com.au/culture/movies/this-spider-man-is-the-superhero-we-need-right-now-20211215-p59hoz.html|title=This Spider-Man is the superhero we need right now}}</ref>

[[Kevin Maher (writer)|Kevin Maher]] of ''[[The Times]]'' gave the film 4 out of 5 stars, saying that it was "As satisfying to watch as it is perilous to discuss", and described it as "a dynamite blast of smarty-pants postmodernism that never once abandons its emotional core".<ref name="TheTimesReview" /> Benjamin Lee of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film 3 out of 5 stars, praising Watts for "bringing back numerous baddies from the previous Spider-Man universes, delivering a propulsive, slickly choreographed adventure that will appease a broad fanbase this Christmas" but feeling that the script "lacks the expected fizz, that sense of shaggy fun struggling to break through a more robotic plot".<ref name="LeeTheGuardianReview" /> Kate Erbland of ''[[IndieWire]]'' gave the film a "B−", feeling that Watts's work was "satisfying, emotional, and occasionally unsteady". She found that the script spent "far too long dwelling on the machinations of people and plans we already know, throwing in some awkward misdirection and simply delaying the inevitable".<ref name="ErblandIndieWireReview" /> ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]''{{'}}s John DeFore felt that the inclusion of "multiversal mayhem" addressed the "Iron Man-ification of the character" that made Holland-centric films "least fun".<ref name="DeForeTHRReview" />

[[CNN]]'s Brian Lowry praised the humor and wrote, "What's already apparent, though, is that this movie was conceived to be savored and enjoyed. And in what has become an increasingly elusive phenomenon, that will include whoops and hollers from appreciative fans in theaters, where ''Spider-Man'' will first reveal its secrets, and then, more than likely, shows off its legs."<ref name="CNNReview" /> [[Richard Roeper]] of ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film 3 out of 4 and praised the performances of Holland and Zendaya, writing: "There's nothing new or particularly memorable about the serviceable CGI and practical effects, but we remain invested in the outcome in large part because Holland remains the best of the cinematic Spider-Men, while Zendaya lends heart and smarts and warmth to every moment she's onscreen. We continue to root for these two to make it, even if the multiverse isn't always on their side."<ref name="ChicagoSun-TimesReview" /> In contrast, Bilge Ebiri of ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'' called the film "aggressively mediocre", criticizing the action, comedy and writing, but praising Dafoe – describing him as "once again gets to have some modest fun with his character's divided self" – and Garfield, calling him a "genuine delight" and naming his the film's best performance.<ref name="VultureReview" /> Hannah Strong of ''[[Little White Lies (magazine)|Little White Lies]]'' criticized the film for poor character development particularly regarding Holland's Peter Parker character stating, "It's grating seeing the same character repeatedly fail to learn any lessons or show even a modicum of personal growth". The same review was also generally critical of the film prioritizing fan service over good storytelling.<ref name="LWLReview" />

== Future ==
{{Main|Spider-Man: Brand New Day{{!}}''Spider-Man: Brand New Day''}}
By August 2019, a fourth film in the franchise was in development alongside ''No Way Home''.<ref name="NoNewSonyDealDeadline" /> In November 2021, Pascal said there were plans for another trilogy of Spider-Man films starring Holland, with work on the first of those about to begin.<ref name="PascalNov2021Fandango" /> The next month, Feige confirmed that he and Pascal, along with Sony and Disney, were actively beginning to develop the story for the next ''Spider-Man'' film following Peter's "momentous decision" at the end of ''No Way Home''.<ref name="NYTimesFeigePascalDec2021" /> He said that ending was a promise to audiences that they would see Holland as a "proper Spider-Man" for the first time, with the character on his own and fighting street-level crime in New York.<ref name="FeigeBNDJul2025" />

The fourth film, ''Spider-Man: Brand New Day'', was released on July 31, 2026, with [[Destin Daniel Cretton]] directing from a screenplay by McKenna and Sommers.<ref name="BrandNewDay" /> Holland, Zendaya, Batalon, and Tomei reprise their roles, with [[Michael Mando]] returning as [[Mac Gargan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mac Gargan / Scorpion]] from ''Homecoming'', while [[Jon Bernthal]], [[Florence Pugh]], and [[Mark Ruffalo]] reprise their respective MCU roles as [[Frank Castle (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Frank Castle / Punisher]], [[Yelena Belova (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Yelena Belova / Black Widow]], and [[Bruce Banner (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Bruce Banner / Hulk]]. They are joined by [[Sadie Sink]] as [[Jean Grey (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Jean Grey]] and [[Tramell Tillman]] as [[William Metzger (character)|Bill Metzger]].<ref name="DeadlineBNDCast" />
