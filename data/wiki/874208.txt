'''''Ravenous''''' is a 1999 [[black comedy]] [[Horror Western|horror western]] film{{sfn|Aquila|2015|p=318}}{{sfn|Grant|2011|p=190}}{{sfn|Hutchings|2009|p=332}} directed by [[Antonia Bird]] and written by [[Ted Griffin]]. Set in the 1840s, the film stars [[Guy Pearce]] as John Boyd, a US Army captain who is exiled to a remote military outpost in the [[Sierra Nevada|Sierra Nevadas]], where he and his colleagues encounter a sadistic [[Human cannibalism|cannibal]], played by [[Robert Carlyle]]. The film also stars [[Jeffrey Jones]], [[David Arquette]], [[Jeremy Davies]], [[Stephen Spinella]], [[Neal McDonough]], and [[John Spencer (actor)|John Spencer]] in his final film role.<ref name=":0">{{cite magazine |last1=Ehrlich |first1=David |date=October 27, 2015 |title=Why 'Ravenous' Is the Greatest Cannibal Western Ever Made |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-news/to-serve-man-why-ravenous-is-the-greatest-cannibal-western-ever-43674/ |url-status=live |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220707145233/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/to-serve-man-why-ravenous-is-the-greatest-cannibal-western-ever-43674/ |archive-date=July 7, 2022}}</ref>

Griffin's screenplay combines elements from the [[Donner Party]] and that of [[Alfred Packer]], the real-life "Colorado Cannibal" who survived by eating five companions after becoming snowbound in the [[San Juan Mountains]] in the 1870s. An international co-production between United Kingdom, United States and Mexico, ''Ravenous'' had a troubled production history. Issues over budget and shooting schedules were still ongoing when filming was about to start in [[Slovakia]]. After the original director [[Milcho Manchevski]] was fired three weeks into production, he was replaced by Bird at the suggestion of Carlyle. The score was composed by [[Michael Nyman]] and [[Damon Albarn]], who used period-accurate instruments and utilized several [[minimal music]] techniques.<ref>{{cite web |last=Draper |first=Sam |date=May 6, 2020 |title=Ravenous – Damon Albarn & Michael Nyman |url=https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-features/41584/ravenous-damon-albarn-michael-nyman/ |website=www.lovehorror.co.uk}}</ref>

Released by [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]] in the spring of 1999, ''Ravenous'' was a [[box-office bomb]], grossing $2 million against a $12 million budget. However, despite initial reception being mixed when released, it has since garnered a reputation as a [[cult film]].<ref name=":1">{{cite web |last=Evangelista |first=Chris |date=October 11, 2019 |title=31 Days Of Streaming Horror: 'Ravenous' Is A Cannibal Cult Classic |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/569691/streaming-horror-ravenous/ |website=[[/Film]]|archive-date=September 6, 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20250906194304/https://www.slashfilm.com/569691/streaming-horror-ravenous/|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Plot ==
<!-- Per [[MOS:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. -->During the [[Mexican–American War]], [[United States Army|US Army]] [[second lieutenant]] John Boyd plays dead as his unit is destroyed by Mexican troops. Unaware Boyd is still alive, the Mexican troops send him on a cart of American dead to the Mexican headquarters. Throughout this journey, blood drips into Boyd's mouth, giving him the strength to capture the headquarters. His heroism earns him a promotion to [[Captain (United States O-3)|captain]], but Boyd's superior [[General (United States)|General]] Slauson sends Boyd to Fort Spencer, a remote military outpost in the [[Sierra Nevada]] under the command of [[Colonel (United States)|Colonel]] Hart, as punishment for perceived cowardice.

The [[Post-traumatic stress disorder|traumatized]] Boyd joins the fort's seven-man garrison. A stranger named Colqhoun arrives after his [[wagon train]] became lost in the mountains by their guide, a colonel named Ives who misrepresented his experience. After eating their horses and pack-animals, the men resorted to [[Human cannibalism|cannibalism]] to avoid starvation and Colqhoun fled from Ives' particularly voracious appetite. The fort assembles a rescue party to search for survivors, and the fort's [[United States Army Indian Scouts|Indian scout]], George, warns them of the [[Wendigo]] myth: anyone who [[Human cannibalism|consumes the flesh of a human]] absorbs their strength but becomes a demon cursed by an insatiable hunger for more human flesh.

Finding the cave that had sheltered Colqhoun's party, Boyd and Reich discover human remains and clothing. They discover a Colonel's uniform below one of the skeletons, revealing that it is in fact Colqhoun who murdered and consumed his five companions. Colqhoun now kills the rescue party one by one, leaving Boyd the sole survivor. He breaks his leg when he escapes by jumping off a cliff and hides in a pit next to the body of Reich, eating some of Reich's flesh to survive, as Colqhoun butchers and devours the other members of the rescue party.

Boyd limps back to Fort Spencer delirious and plagued by cravings for human flesh. The soldiers who never met Colqhoun do not believe Boyd's wild tale. A second expedition to the cave finds neither bodies nor any trace of Colqhoun. A temporary commander is assigned to the fort and turns out to be Colqhoun, presenting himself as the murdered Colonel Ives. Boyd's fellow soldiers refuse to believe that "Ives" is the killer, especially since he bears no sign of the wounds supposedly inflicted on him during the fight at the cave.

That evening, Ives privately tells Boyd that he used to suffer from [[tuberculosis]]. After an Indian scout informed him of the Wendigo myth, he killed and ate the scout, which cured his illness and revitalized him. He now plans to use the fort as a base to cannibalize other passing [[Manifest destiny|travelers headed westward]] and wants Boyd, who is fighting his cannibalistic cravings, to join him.

Boyd is suspected of murder after Private Cleaves mysteriously dies. He is incarcerated and watches helplessly as Major Knox is murdered by the real killer, Colonel Hart, whom everyone believed to be dead. Ives reveals he saved Hart by feeding Hart his own comrades and now Hart too is addicted to human flesh. Frustrated by Boyd's stubbornness, Ives critically wounds him and forces Boyd to make a choice: once again consume human flesh or die. Eventually Boyd gives in and eats a stew made out of human flesh, healing his injuries.

Rather than join the two men in their attempt to convert General Slauson, who is shortly due to arrive at the fort, Boyd convinces Hart to free him so he can kill Ives. Hart complies but asks that he be killed as he no longer wants to live as a cannibal. Boyd and Ives wound each other but do not die easily due to their recuperative powers. Finally, in an outhouse, Boyd forces Ives into a bear trap and springs it, pinning them both together. Ives expires first, and Boyd refuses to save himself by eating Ives' body and dies on top of his adversary. Martha, George's sister, finds the bodies of both Ives and Boyd, closes the door, and walks away. Slauson eventually arrives. While his aide looks around the dilapidated fort, Slauson tastes the stew that was left simmering on the fire.

== Production ==
{{expand section|date=February 2025}}

=== Development ===
The script for ''Ravenous'' was one of three screenplays written by [[Ted Griffin]] that he sold to a studio in a week.<ref name=":3">{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2014-03-19 |title=Unsung Anniversaries #6: Ravenous |url=https://thatshelf.com/unsung-anniversares-6-ravenous/ |access-date=2023-05-26 |website=That Shelf |language=en-US}}</ref> The script was loosely inspired by the [[Donner Party]], as well as the story of [[Alfred Packer]], an American prospector and [[Union army]] veteran who murdered a group of traveling companions, ate them and claimed to law enforcement that he did so out of self-defense and survival.<ref name=":3" /> In September 1997, Macedonian director [[Milcho Manchevski]] was announced to direct the film for [[Fox 2000 Pictures|Fox 2000]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Petrikin |first=Chris |date=September 19, 1997 |title='Rain' man Manchevski bites to helm 'Ravenous' |url=https://variety.com/1997/film/news/rain-man-manchevski-bites-to-helm-ravenous-1116674303/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230527005327/https://variety.com/1997/film/news/rain-man-manchevski-bites-to-helm-ravenous-1116674303/|archive-date=May 27, 2023|url-status=live |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
The film was shot on location in the [[Tatra Mountains]] in [[Slovakia]] and [[Durango]] in Mexico.<ref name=":4">{{Cite news |date=March 19, 1999 |title='Ravenous' for Fun: Dark Humor is Lifeblood of Tale on Cannibalism |work=[[The Morning Call]] |url=https://www.mcall.com/1999/03/19/ravenous-for-fun-dark-humor-is-lifeblood-of-tale-on-cannibalism/ |url-status=live |access-date=May 26, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230526205738/https://www.mcall.com/1999/03/19/ravenous-for-fun-dark-humor-is-lifeblood-of-tale-on-cannibalism/ |archive-date=May 26, 2023}}</ref> One week before production, Manchevski reportedly submitted new [[storyboard]]s, which would have required an additional two weeks of shooting.<ref name="manchevski">{{Cite news |last=Busch |first=Anita M. |date=April 10, 1998 |title='Creative differences' shake up Hollywood |work=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |url=https://ew.com/article/1998/04/10/creative-differences-shake-hollywood/ |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201127024926/https://ew.com/article/1998/04/10/creative-differences-shake-hollywood/|archive-date=November 27, 2020}}</ref> Fox 2000 eventually agreed to an additional week, with complaints that Manchevski had refused to attend production meetings with the producers.<ref name="manchevski" /><ref name=":5">{{Cite web |last=Stepenberg |first=Alejandro |date=2015-06-26 |title=The Best Movie You Never Saw: Ravenous |url=https://www.joblo.com/the-best-movie-you-never-saw-ravenous-527/ |access-date=2023-05-26 |website=JoBlo |language=en-US}}</ref> Manchevski claimed Fox 2000 executive [[Laura Ziskin]] micromanaged the production by vetoing his chosen technicians and casting against his wishes.<ref name="bird" /><ref name=":5" />

Shooting was delayed on the first day, as Manchevski and the production were still negotiating over the production budget and shooting schedule. As filming commenced, Manchevski says Ziskin sent him notes on the [[Dailies|rushes]] "every day", complaining about the amount of dirt on the costumes and the number of [[Close-up|closeups]].<ref name="bird">{{Cite news |last=Clarke |first=Roger |date=September 3, 1999 |title=Film: They all but ate me alive! |work=[[The Independent]] |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/film-they-all-but-ate-me-alive-1115720.html |access-date=April 4, 2012}}</ref> Screenwriter Ted Griffin was on hand for "constant rewrites" during the shooting.<ref name="manchevski" />

Three weeks into filming, Ziskin arrived to the set to dismiss Manchevski, and have him replaced with director [[Raja Gosnell]].<ref name="manchevski" /><ref name="bird" /> Though Manchevski left the production, the cast was said to have been unhappy with Gosnell, and were rumored to have held a mutiny on the set.<ref name="bird" /> [[Robert Carlyle]] then recommended [[Antonia Bird]], his frequent collaborator and business partner, to take over.<ref name="bird" /> Bird had a previous business relationship with Ziskin, and admired the script.<ref name=":4" />

Following ten days of negotiations, Bird arrived in [[Prague]] to take over the production. She, too, would criticize the circumstances under which the filming was to take place, describing the allocated studio space as "horrible" and the scheduling of the shoot "manipulative".<ref name="bird" /> She also went on to say that her predecessor, Manchevski, should not be blamed for the problematic production.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hanley |first=Ken W. |date=March 19, 2015 |title=FANGO Flashback: 'RAVENOUS' |url=http://www.fangoria.com/new/fango-flashback-ravenous/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170830014833/http://www.fangoria.com/new/fango-flashback-ravenous/ |archive-date=August 30, 2017 |access-date=August 29, 2017 |website=Fangoria.com}}</ref>

=== Post-production ===
Bird suggested the final theatrical cut had elements introduced without her approval, such as the voiceover narration and explanatory quotes. Bird felt these elements were superfluous, and expressed a desire in recutting the film for the European market.<ref name="bird" />

== Themes ==
The film uses its period setting and the act of cannibalism to critique [[manifest destiny]], [[American imperialism|colonialism]] and [[capitalism]].<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":2">{{cite web |last=Abrams |first=Simon |date=June 18, 2014 |title=Forgotten Flick Ravenous Is the Best-Ever Manifest Destiny Cannibal Comedy |url=https://www.villagevoice.com/2014/06/18/forgotten-flick-ravenous-is-the-best-ever-manifest-destiny-cannibal-comedy/ |website=[[The Village Voice]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250428044125/https://www.villagevoice.com/2014/06/18/forgotten-flick-ravenous-is-the-best-ever-manifest-destiny-cannibal-comedy/|archive-date=April 28, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Chaw |first=Walter |date=2022-09-20 |title='Ravenous' Is A Fabulously Gory Horror Film About Cannibalism … But Also Capitalism |url=https://decider.com/2022/09/20/walter-chaw-ravenous/ |access-date=2023-05-26 |website=[[Decider (website)|Decider]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Said Bird: "As a European, observing early Californian history and making a film about that — I kind of believe Europeans were responsible for a lot of stuff that happened here. Robert Carlyle's character represents that. The Europeans practiced [[Genocide of indigenous peoples|genocide]]. I think the good things about America — we're (Europeans) not responsible for."<ref name=":4" />

Though Bird said the film is "more about social misfits than the whole seductive nature of power", she stated, "I would like to think that someone who enjoyed it (''Ravenous'') only as [[Black comedy|gallows humor]] would start to think. The metaphor of power and a society where we're encouraged to be competitive is, to me, not a great society."<ref name=":4" /> Thus, cannibalism can be seen as a critique of "contemporary America where the way of life seems to be more and more a matter of consume or be consumed".<ref name=":4" /> In addition, she said that cannibalism can be interpreted as an addiction to [[drug]]s or a yearning for eternal youth, which manifests in modern-day Western society as the consumption of [[junk food]] or the obsession with [[plastic surgery]].<ref name=":4" /> "Robert Carlyle's character is the ultimate drug pusher and Guy Pearce's character is the ultimate [[Addiction|junkie]]," said Bird.<ref name=":4" />

== Soundtrack ==
{{Main|Ravenous (soundtrack)}}The score was composed by [[Michael Nyman]] and [[Damon Albarn]] of the band [[Blur (band)|Blur]]. Instruments used for the score were from the film's historical period and included the violin, guitar, banjo, [[Jew's harp|jaw harp]] and [[Accordion|squeeze box]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Schulte |first=Tom |title=''Ravenous'' Original Soundtrack |url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/ravenous-mw0000049828 |access-date=May 26, 2023 |website=[[AllMusic]]}}</ref> Nyman and Albarn reworked versions of American patriotic songs and old [[Methodist Episcopal Church|Methodist]] hymns (including those of [[Stephen Foster]], known as the "father of American music") to be intentionally out of tune and off-kilter.<ref name=":4" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Ravenous'' earned $1,040,727 over its opening weekend in the United States, finishing eighteenth for the weekend.<ref name=BOM>{{cite web |title=Ravenous (1999) |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=ravenous.htm |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250906191127/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl913147393/weekend/|archive-date=September 6, 2025|url-status=live|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> The film went on to gross $2,062,405 in North America.<ref name=BOM/> It was regarded as a [[box-office bomb]], failing to recoup its $12 million budget.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.joblo.com/ravenous-wtf/|work=[[JoBlo.com]]|title=Ravenous (1999) – WTF Happened to This Horror Movie|date=December 16, 2023|last=Black|first=Emilie|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240701235724/https://www.joblo.com/ravenous-wtf/|archive-date=July 1, 2024}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has a 52% rating based on 66 reviews and an [[Weighted arithmetic mean|average rating]] of 6/10. The site's critical consensus reads: "''Ravenous'' tries bringing cannibal horror into an Old West setting, ending up with an uneven blend that will fail to satisfy most fans of either genre".<ref>{{cite web |title=Ravenous |url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/ravenous/ |access-date=February 12, 2024 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 46 out of 100, based on 23 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Ravenous'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ravenous |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=June 12, 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "D" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemascore.com|title=Find CinemaScore|format=Type "Ravenous" in the search box|publisher=[[CinemaScore]]|access-date=November 25, 2025}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] rated the film three out of four stars, and stated that it was "the kind of movie where you savor the texture of the filmmaking, even when the story strays into shapeless gore."<ref name=":6">{{cite news |author=Ebert |first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert |date=March 19, 1999 |title=Ravenous |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ravenous-1999 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250326085543/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ravenous-1999|archive-date=March 26, 2025|url-status=live|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> He noted the film is "more interested in atmosphere than plot", but conceded Bird "shows she's a real filmmaker...and has an instinct for scenes like the one where a visiting general savors the broth of a bubbling stew".<ref name=":6" /> Ebert concluded that ''Ravenous'' "is clever in the way it avoids most of the cliches of the [[Vampire film|vampire movie]] by using cannibalism, and most of the cliches of the cannibal movie by using vampirism. It serves both dishes with new sauces."<ref name=":6" /> [[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' reviewed the film negatively, and said: "a potentially strong cast makes its way in deadly earnest through material that's often better suited to a [[Monty Python]] skit".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Maslin |first=Janet |date=March 19, 1999 |title='Ravenous': His Favorite Dessert? Lady Fingers, of Course! |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/031999ravenous-film-review.html |access-date=2023-05-26 |website=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

Audiences, particularly in the US, were said to be confused about the tone of the film,<ref name="bird" /> which combines various genres such as [[Horror film|horror]], [[black comedy]], [[satire]] and [[film noir]].<ref name=":4" /> Said Carlyle: "Because the subject matter is so gruesome and the visuals are so distasteful, there are going to be people who are not going to be too happy to watch this one. They've said all along that it's going to be a hard sell. It's a period piece. I think that's why there's humor in the film. I like the fact that it's unusual and that it is hard to place. I think that's a good thing — probably not for the people who are trying to make their money back."<ref name=":4" />

The film has garnered [[Cult following|cult status]] since its release.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dee |first=Jake |date=March 20, 2019 |title=The Test of Time: Ravenous (1999) |url=https://www.joblo.com/the-test-of-time-ravenous-1999/ |url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241214122853/https://www.joblo.com/the-test-of-time-ravenous-1999/|archive-date=December 14, 2024 |website=[[JoBlo.com]] |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name=":1" /> In a retrospective review in ''[[Rolling Stone]]'', David Ehrlich wrote, "''Ravenous'' butchers the fantasy that the United States is a banquet with room for everyone at the table. This is a landscape where 'manifest destiny' becomes a handy euphemism for all sorts of horrors, and a reminder that progress was never possible without savagery; the frontier was the [[The Hunger Games|''Hunger Games'']], and it always has been. That may not be breaking news, but the film isn't interested in telling you something that you don't know, only showing it to you in a way so giddy and gruesome that you'll never be able to forget it."<ref name=":0" />
