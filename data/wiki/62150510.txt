'''''Candyman''''' is a 2021 [[supernatural horror film]] directed by [[Nia DaCosta]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Jordan Peele]] and [[Win Rosenfeld]]. It is a direct sequel to [[Candyman (1992 film)|the 1992 film]] and the fourth film in the [[Candyman (film series)|''Candyman'' film series]], based on the short story "[[Books of Blood#"The Forbidden"|The Forbidden]]" by English author [[Clive Barker]], and set between the films ''[[Candyman: Farewell to the Flesh]]'' (1995) and ''[[Candyman 3: Day of the Dead]]'' (1999). The film stars [[Yahya Abdul-Mateen II]], [[Teyonah Parris]], [[Nathan Stewart-Jarrett]], and [[Colman Domingo]]. [[Vanessa Estelle Williams|Vanessa Williams]], [[Virginia Madsen]], and [[Tony Todd]] reprise their roles from the original film.

Plans for another ''Candyman'' film began in the early 2000s, with original director [[Bernard Rose (director)|Bernard Rose]] wanting to make a [[prequel]] film about Candyman and Helen's love. However, the studio turned it down and the project entered [[development hell]]. By 2018, Peele signed on as producer for a new film using his company [[Monkeypaw Productions]]. In November that same year, [[Universal Pictures]] and [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Pictures]] became involved, and it was confirmed that Peele would produce the film with Rosenfeld, while DaCosta signed on as director. [[Principal photography]] for the film began in August 2019 and wrapped in September 2019 in [[Chicago, Illinois]].

''Candyman'' was theatrically released in the United States on August 27, 2021, by Universal Pictures. Its release date was delayed three times from an original June 2020 date due to concerns regarding the [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema|COVID-19 pandemic]]. The film received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised DaCosta's direction, visual style, and the blend of social commentary with horror.<ref>{{cite web |last=Auty |first=Dan |title=Review Roundup For Candyman |url=https://www.gamespot.com/articles/review-roundup-for-candyman/1100-6495565/ |website=[[GameSpot]] |date=August 25, 2021 |access-date=August 25, 2021}}</ref> It grossed $77 million worldwide against a $25 million budget.

== Plot ==
<!--- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be 400-700 words --->
In 1977, at [[Chicago]]'s [[Cabrini–Green Homes|Cabrini–Green]] housing projects, a young boy witnesses the killing of Sherman Fields, a homeless African American man with a hook for a hand. Suspected of giving a white child a [[Poisoned candy myths|razor blade in a piece of candy]], Sherman is beaten to death by police but later proven innocent when more cases of razor blades in candies come up.

In 2019, artist Anthony McCoy lives in Chicago with his girlfriend, gallery director Brianna Cartwright. Seeking inspiration, Anthony explores the projects after Brianna's brother Troy and his boyfriend tell them the story of [[Candyman (1992 film)|Helen Lyle]], who is believed responsible for a killing spree and briefly kidnapping a baby in Cabrini-Green. Stung on the hand by a bee, Anthony meets [[Self-service laundry|laundromat]] owner William Burke, the boy who witnessed Sherman's death. William introduces him to the urban legend of the [[Candyman (character)|Candyman]], which Anthony tells Brianna and jokingly performs its curse: by saying "Candyman" five times to a mirror, Sherman's spirit will appear and kill the summoner.

Inspired by Sherman's death and the Candyman legend, Anthony creates an elaborate art piece titled "Say My Name" for a show curated by Brianna and her colleague Clive, but his work is disparaged by critic Finley Stephens and other attendees. Later that night, Clive and his girlfriend Jerrica unwittingly summon the Candyman, who brutally murders them; Brianna discovers their bodies, triggering childhood memories of witnessing her father's suicide.

Anthony compulsively paints gruesome portraits of unknown men, becoming obsessed as he investigates Helen Lyle and her own Candyman research, and has a vision of Sherman's ghost in an elevator. Interviewed by Finley, Anthony goads her into summoning the Candyman herself, and has another vision in a mirror of himself as Sherman. Joining Brianna at a business dinner, he leaves abruptly when he learns Finley has been murdered.

Anthony goes to William, who explains that the legend originated in the 1890s with Daniel Robitaille, an artist who had an interracial affair with a white client's daughter. After the daughter claimed Robitaille raped her, he was mutilated and burnt alive. The legend has been renewed for generations with the souls of other [[Hate crime|murdered]] innocent black men joining the Candyman "hive"; these are the subjects of Anthony's paintings. Anthony attempts to protect Brianna, but only frightens her with his increasingly erratic behavior.

Meanwhile, a female teenager, who previously attended Anthony's show with her parents, and her friends are killed after summoning the Candyman in their school bathroom, with one of their classmates, who wasn't involved in the summoning, having arrived after and survived. Anthony undergoes a physical transformation, spreading from the [[bee sting]] on his hand across his entire body. He confronts his mother, Anne-Marie, after visiting a hospital and learning that she lied to him: he was born near Cabrini-Green, and was the baby taken the night Helen died. Anne-Marie admits the Candyman was responsible for the bloodshed blamed on Helen, and the community vowed never to speak his name.

Brianna seeks out William, who abducts her to an abandoned church, where Anthony is in a [[fugue state]]. Shortly after Sherman's death, William witnessed his return as the Candyman when his sister and her friend summoned him and were killed. William plans to resurrect the Candyman "hive" as a form of retaliation against gentrifiers. Sawing off Anthony's hand and replacing it with a hook, William calls the police to have Anthony wrongfully gunned down as another vengeful spirit to join the hive. Chased into the Cabrini-Green row houses, Brianna stabs William to death with a [[pen]]. She is confronted by Anthony, who collapses in her arms and is shot dead by the police.

Detaining Brianna in the backseat of a car, the police try to [[witness tampering|intimidate]] her into agreeing that Anthony was the killer and that his shooting was justified. Instead, she uses the rearview mirror to summon the Candyman, who appears as Anthony and massacres the police. Swarming with bees, his face transforms to Daniel Robitaille, instructing Brianna to "tell everyone" what she has witnessed, as more police officers arrive.

The film's end credits feature a [[shadow puppet]] montage of members of the growing Candyman hive, including Daniel, Sherman, [[Lynching of Anthony Crawford|Anthony Crawford]], [[Murder of James Byrd Jr.|James Byrd Jr.]], [[George Stinney]], and Anthony himself.

=== Music ===
{{Main|Candyman (soundtrack)}}
The original score soundtrack was composed by Chicago musician Robert Aiki Aubrey Lowe, who used solo compositions based around voice and extended modular synthesis techniques. He also used [[Location shooting|field]] recordings of Cabrini Green to capture the essence and spirit of the neighborhood and layered it as textural elements on top of the main instruments. In January 2022, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported that the [[film score]] briefly made the [[Shortlisted|shortlist]] for the [[94th Academy Awards|2022 Academy Awards]] in the category of [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]]; however, it did not make the official final ballot list.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Burlingame|first=Jon|date=2022-01-10|title=Oscars: 'Candyman' Marks the Rare Horror Film to Compete for Best Original Score|url=https://variety.com/2022/film/awards/oscars-candyman-horror-film-original-score-shortlist-1235150644/|access-date=2022-01-11|website=Variety|language=en-US}}</ref> Phillips Glass' song ''"Helen's Theme/Music Box"'' was also reimagined by Lowe as a new interpolation on the soundtrack as well as in one scene and end credits in the film. Additionally, [[Playboi Carti]] recorded the song [[H00dByAir]] in 2021 for the film's soundtrack, revealed in 2024 roughly nine months after the release of the track.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Buford|first=Jayson|date=2024-09-03|title=One Wild Night With Playboi Carti: Rage on Stage, All-Night Sessions & 'Burnt Music'|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/playboi-carti-cover-story-interview-1235764638/|access-date=2024-09-03|magazine=Billboard|language=en-US}}</ref>

==Release==
''Candyman'' was originally scheduled to be released by [[Universal Pictures]] on June 12, 2020, but due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], it was pushed to September 25, 2020,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/universal-moves-candyman-september-release-1288440|title=Universal Moves 'Candyman' to September Theatrical Release|work=The Hollywood Reporter|date=April 3, 2020|access-date=May 10, 2020|archive-date=April 19, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200419233458/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/universal-moves-candyman-september-release-1288440|url-status=live}}</ref> and then again to October 16, 2020, taking the previous release date of ''[[Halloween Kills]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2020/07/blumhouse-universal-move-halloween-kills-forever-purge-more-to-later-release-dates-1202980411/|title=Blumhouse & Universal Move 'Halloween Kills', 'Forever Purge' & More To Later Release Dates|website=Deadline Hollywood|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|date=July 8, 2020|access-date=July 8, 2020|archive-date=September 27, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200927205739/https://deadline.com/2020/07/blumhouse-universal-move-halloween-kills-forever-purge-more-to-later-release-dates-1202980411//|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was then delayed to August 27, 2021.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/universals-candyman-finds-2021-release-date|title=Universal's 'Candyman' Finds 2021 Release Date|website=The Hollywood Reporter|first=Aaron|last=Couch|date=October 21, 2020|access-date=October 27, 2020|archive-date=January 2, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102170405/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/universals-candyman-finds-2021-release-date|url-status=live}}</ref> It was released on-demand on September 17, 2021.

''Candyman'' was released for purchase on [[Video on demand|digital video on demand]] with new exclusive early access to [[director's cut|bonus material]] featurette clips including an alternate ending, extended scenes and behind the scenes interviews on streaming platforms such as [[Vudu]], [[Apple TV app|Apple TV]], among others on November 2, 2021,<ref>{{Cite web|last=Squires|first=John|date=2021-10-26|title='Candyman' Comes to Digital and Blu-ray in November With Alternate Ending and Deleted Scenes|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/home-video/3689079/candyman-comes-digital-blu-ray-november-never-seen-alternate-ending-deletes-scenes/|access-date=2021-11-01|website=Bloody Disgusting!|language=en-US}}</ref> and was released on [[DVD]], [[Blu-ray]] and [[4K UHD]] [[Movies Anywhere|digital code]] release including all bonus material and [[Special edition#Disc products|special features]] on November 16, 2021.<ref>{{Cite web | url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Candyman-Blu-ray/264937/#Overview | title=Candyman Blu-ray (Blu-ray + DVD + Digital HD) }}</ref>

=== Marketing ===
{{One source section
| date = September 2021
}}
Summarizing the film's marketing results, RelishMix wrote that viewers were debating whether it was a remake or a sequel and that "with Jordan Peele on board, fan expectations run high in anticipation of the return of this classic horror villain, who's described as a 'Black [[Freddy Krueger|Freddy]] or Black [[Jason Voorhees|Jason]]', as the film explores racial issues. Plus, fans are looking at the journey into the fine art world, woven into artists' creations, as they are influenced by demons and ghosts." By August 2021, the film's promotional content was viewed 144.1 million times, 40 percent higher than the average horror film; the first and second trailers accumulated 75 and 60 million views, respectively. Additional marketing tactics about the film's premise included a [[Snapchat]] filter, a stunt activation in Chicago, and Peele daring viewers to tweet <nowiki>#Candyman</nowiki> five times, resulting in the film trending online.<ref name="opening" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Candyman'' grossed $61.2 million in the United States and Canada, and $16.2 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $77.4 million.<ref name="BOM">{{Cite Box Office Mojo |id=9347730 |title=Candyman (2021) |access-date=November 3, 2021}}</ref><ref name="NUM">{{Cite The Numbers |id=Candyman-(2020)#tab=summary |title=Candyman (2021) |access-date=November 3, 2021}}</ref>

In the United States and Canada, ''Candyman'' was projected to gross around $15 million from 3,569 theaters in its opening weekend.<ref name=VarietyPreview>{{cite web|last= Rubin |first=Rebecca |date=August 25, 2021|title= 'Candyman' to Slash Box Office Competition |url= https://variety.com/2021/film/box-office/candyman-box-office-opening-weekend-projections-1235048754/ |access-date=August 25, 2021|website= Variety }}</ref> The film took in $9.1 million on its first day, including $1.9 million from Thursday night previews. It went on to debut to $22 million, topping the box office. The audience was made up of 53% male, with African Americans (37%) and Caucasians (30%) making up a majority. The top markets in the U.S. were Los Angeles ($1.3 million) and New York ($1.1 million).<ref name="opening">{{cite web |url= https://deadline.com/2021/08/candyman-posts-sweet-thursday-night-with-1-9m-1234823246/ |title= 'Candyman' Makes The Box Office Taste Good With $22M+ Opening |date=August 29, 2021 |first=Anthony |last=D'Alessandro |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=August 29, 2021}}</ref>

DaCosta became the first Black female director to have a film finish number one at the box office.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://deadline.com/2021/08/candyman-nia-dacosta-box-office-record-first-black-female-filmmaker-1234824013/ |title= 'Candyman': Nia DaCosta Becomes First Black Female Filmmaker To Open Pic To No. 1 At Domestic B.O. |date=August 29, 2021 |first=Anthony |last=D'Alessandro |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=August 29, 2021}}</ref> The film dropped 53.2% in its sophomore weekend to $10.3 million (and a total of $12.5 million over the four-day Labor Day frame), finishing second behind newcomer ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=September 7, 2021 |title='Shang-Chi' To The Moon: Marvel Asian American Superhero Movie Wraps Labor Day Weekend With Record $94M+ & Best Monday During Pandemic |url=https://deadline.com/2021/09/shang-chi-opening-weekend-box-office-1234826987/ |access-date=September 8, 2021 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> In its third weekend the film grossed $4.8 million, finishing fourth.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://deadline.com/2021/09/shang-chi-strong-second-weekend-with-31m-malignant-dying-1234831053/ |title= 'Shang-Chi' Strong Second Weekend With $31M+; 'Malignant' Dying |date= September 12, 2021 |first=Anthony |last=D'Alessandro |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date= September 12, 2021}}</ref>

Worldwide, ''Candyman'' was released in 51 markets and made $5.23 million; the top countries were the United Kingdom ($1.48 million), Spain ($356,000), Mexico, Russia, and Germany.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2021/08/free-guy-china-global-international-box-office-1234823823/|title='Free Guy' Flies To $100M+ Overseas With China Assist, Global Now $180M – International Box Office|date=August 29, 2021|first=Nancy|last=Tartaglione|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210829220314/https://deadline.com/2021/08/free-guy-china-global-international-box-office-1234823823/|archive-date=August 29, 2021|url-status=live|access-date=August 29, 2021}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|84|7.3|335|''Candyman'' takes an incisive, visually thrilling approach to deepening the franchise's mythology—and terrifying audiences along the way.}}<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/candyman_2021 |title= Candyman (2021) |work= [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher= [[Fandango Media]] |access-date= October 30, 2022 |archive-date= August 25, 2021 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210825045010/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/candyman_2021 |url-status= live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 72 out of 100 based on 54 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/candyman-2020 |title=Candyman (2021) Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Red Ventures]] |access-date=September 12, 2021 |archive-date=August 15, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210815175237/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/candyman-2020 |url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, while [[PostTrak]] reported 72% of audience members gave it a positive score, with 56% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="opening" />

Reviewing the film for ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Manohla Dargis]] wrote that "DaCosta plays with perspective, shifting between Anthony's and the intersecting, sometimes colliding worlds of more-successful artists, urban-legend propagators and, touchingly, profoundly scarred children." She points to the interspersed bits of shadow puppetry as a reflexive writing device that emphasizes ''Candyman'' is fundamentally about storytelling, writing: "We tell some fictions to understand ourselves, to exist; others we tell to turn other human beings into monsters, to destroy."<ref>{{Cite news|last=Dargis|first=Manohla|author-link=Manohla Dargis|date=2021-08-26|title='Candyman' Review: Who Can Take a Sunrise, Sprinkle It With Blood?|language=en-US|work=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/08/26/movies/candyman-review.html|access-date=2021-08-27|issn=0362-4331}}</ref> Odie Henderson, reviewing the film for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', praised DaCosta's visual style, writing that she "stages the kill scenes with a mix of pitch-black humor, misdirection, and clever framing, fully acknowledging that what you don't see—or think you saw—can be a lot worse than what you did see."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Henderson|first=Odie|title=Candyman movie review & film summary (2021)|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/candyman-movie-review-2021|access-date=2021-08-27|website=www.rogerebert.com/|language=en}}</ref>

In her review of ''Candyman'' for ''[[The A.V. Club]]'', Anya Stanley wrote that the film's various interests are "more than a 91-minute movie can adequately explore," but conceded "there are worse crimes for a movie to commit than having too many ideas." She explained: "Where Bernard Rose spoke on White anxieties and the image of the scary Black man in 1992, DaCosta expands the conversation, relocating the horror from one man to the many structures that foment brutality upon the Black populace."<ref>{{Cite web|title=A new Candyman reconsiders the bogeyman|url=https://www.avclub.com/a-new-candyman-reconsiders-the-bogeyman-1847553096|access-date=2021-08-27|website=[[The A.V. Club]] |date=August 25, 2021 |language=en-us}}</ref>

In her review for ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'', [[Angelica Jade Bastién]] called ''Candyman'' "the most disappointing film of the year so far," writing that it limns "not only the artistic failures of the individuals who ushered it to life, but the artistic failures of an entire industry that seeks to commodify Blackness to embolden its bottom line."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Bastién|first=Angelica Jade|author-link=Angelica Jade Bastién|date=2021-08-25|title=Candyman Is a Soulless, Didactic Reimagining|url=https://www.vulture.com/article/candyman-2021-movie-review-nia-dacosta-jordan-peele.html|access-date=2021-08-27|website=Vulture|language=en-us}}</ref> Robert Daniels expressed similar disappointment with the film in his review for ''[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]]'', describing it as "cluttered, preachy, and not nearly scary enough." He took particular issue with the way the film fails to convey the geographical importance of Cabrini–Green, writing that the "lack of a visual metaphor makes the film's exploration of gentrification more of an assemblage of nonspecific dialogue. It talks about what gentrification is, and not what it looks like."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Daniels|first=Robert|date=2021-08-25|title=The new Candyman was modernized for the wrong audience|url=https://www.polygon.com/reviews/22641277/candyman-review-2021|access-date=2021-08-27|website=Polygon|language=en}}</ref>

David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' wrote: "Director Nia DaCosta, working from a script she wrote with Jordan Peele and Win Rosenfeld, uses Bernard Rose's 1992 film as a jumping-off point for bone-chilling horror that expands provocatively on the urban legend of the first film within the context of Black folklore and history, as well as the distorting White narrative that turns Black victims into monsters."<ref name="THR">{{Cite web|last=Rooney|first=David|date=August 25, 2021|title=Yahya Abdul-Mateen II in 'Candyman': Film Review|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/yahya-abdul-mateen-ii-in-candyman-1235001315/|access-date=August 25, 2021|website=The Hollywood Reporter|language=en-US|archive-date=August 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210826022112/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/yahya-abdul-mateen-ii-in-candyman-1235001315/|url-status=live}}</ref> Reviewing the film for ''[[TheWrap]]'', Elizabeth Weitzman  said: "DaCosta, Peele, and Rosenfeld are playing with us—the victim is rendered less sympathetically than Candyman—as much as they are with notions of history, culture, art and appropriation. They bring in actors from the first film (including Tony Todd and Vanessa Estelle Williams) but not always in ways we expect. They build on canon while simultaneously dismantling it."<ref>{{Cite web |last= Weitzman |first= Elizabeth |date=August 25, 2021| title='Candyman' Film Review: Nia DaCosta Deconstructs and Re-Creates a Horror Classic |url=https://www.thewrap.com/candyman-film-review-2021-nia-dacosta-jordan-peele/ |access-date=August 25, 2021 |website=[[TheWrap]] |language=en }}</ref> Aedan Juvet of ''[[Screen Rant]]'' also named the movie: "The Most Rewatchable Horror Film of 2021."<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-12-22|title=10 Most Rewatchable Horror Movies Of 2021, Ranked|url=https://screenrant.com/most-rewatchable-horror-movies-of-2021-ranked/|access-date=2022-02-07|website=ScreenRant|language=en-US}}</ref>
