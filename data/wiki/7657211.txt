'''''The Bad Seed''''' is a 1956 American [[psychological horror]] [[thriller film]] directed by [[Mervyn LeRoy]] and starring [[Nancy Kelly]], [[Patty McCormack]], [[Henry Jones (actor)|Henry Jones]] and [[Eileen Heckart]], about an eight-year-old girl whose mother begins to suspect that she might be a [[psychopathy|psychopathic]] killer.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=5 of the Best Portrayals of Cinematic Sociopaths |url=https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/horror-movies/movie-sociopaths-best-portrayals-psychopath-mental-illness-empathy-actors-roles |access-date=2025-03-09 |website=Paste Magazine |language=en-US |archive-date=2024-11-14 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241114121018/https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/horror-movies/movie-sociopaths-best-portrayals-psychopath-mental-illness-empathy-actors-roles |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film adapts the [[The Bad Seed (play)|1954 play of the same name]] by [[Maxwell Anderson]], which in turn is based on [[William March]]'s 1954 novel [[The Bad Seed|of the same name]]. The screenplay was written by [[John Lee Mahin]]. Kelly, McCormack, and Heckart each garnered [[Academy Awards]] nominations for their performances, as well as [[Harold Rosson]]'s cinematography.

==Plot==
Kenneth and Christine Penmark dote on their eight-year-old daughter, Rhoda, pristine and proper in her [[pinafore dress]] and blonde [[pigtail]]s. Kenneth leaves on military duty. Neighbor and landlady Monica Breedlove is fond of Rhoda. Rhoda tells her about a [[penmanship]] competition that she lost to her schoolmate Claude Daigle. Rhoda then leaves for her school picnic at a lake.

While lunching, Christine and friends hear a radio report that Claude has drowned in the lake. Christine worries that her daughter might be traumatized, but Rhoda is unfazed by the incident. Rhoda's teacher, Miss Fern, visits Christine, revealing that Rhoda was seen grabbing at Claude's penmanship medal and was with the boy just prior to his death. She hints that Rhoda might have some connection to Claude's death, adding that Rhoda will not be welcome at the school after the current term ends. Claude's parents barge in; Mrs. Daigle is distraught and drunk, accusing Miss Fern of withholding information. When Christine finds the medal hidden in the lining of Rhoda's jewel case, she demands an explanation. Rhoda tells her that she paid Claude fifty cents to wear it for the day and only kept it in the confusion after Claude's death.

During a visit from her father, journalist Richard Bravo, Christine confronts him about haunting and confusing memories of her own childhood. She tells him that she always suspected that she was adopted. Initially he denies it, but when Christine recalls details, he reluctantly confirms that she was adopted as a toddler. Christine is horrified to discern that she is the biological daughter of a notorious Australian [[serial killer]]. She worries that her origin is the genetic cause of Rhoda's [[antisocial personality disorder|sociopathy]]. Richard tries to convince her that it is [[Nature versus nurture|nurture, not nature]], that primarily influences such behavior.

Christine catches Rhoda trying to dispose of her [[tap shoe]]s in the apartment incinerator. She realizes that Rhoda must have hit Claude with the shoes, which had left odd crescent-shaped marks on his face and hands that could not be identified. Alternately feigning tears and angrily blaming Claude, Rhoda admits that she killed the boy for his medal. Rhoda also confirms Christine's suspicion that, to acquire a keepsake, she had previously murdered an elderly neighbor when they had lived in [[Wichita, Kansas]]. Christine orders Rhoda to burn the shoes in the incinerator.<ref>''Cruel Children in Popular Texts and Cultures''; {{ISBN|978-3-030-10179-4}} p. 294</ref>

The next day, janitor Leroy Jessup, who has previously seen Rhoda's malice behind her demure pose, teasingly tells Rhoda that he believes she killed Claude. After Rhoda angrily tells him that she burned her shoes, Leroy opens the incinerator and finds the remains. A drunk Mrs. Daigle returns and tells Christine that she believes that Rhoda knows what happened to her son.

Realizing Leroy knows the truth, Rhoda sets his [[Wood wool|excelsior]] bedding ablaze and locks him in the basement. After men break open the basement hatch, Leroy runs into the yard aflame, ultimately burning to death. From the window, Christine and Monica see him die; Christine becomes hysterical. That night, a strangely calm Christine tells Rhoda that she dropped the penmanship medal into the lake where Claude's body was found, then gives her daughter a lethal dose of [[sleeping pills]]. Christine shoots herself in the head; however, the gunshot alerts the neighbors, and Rhoda and Christine are rushed to the hospital. They both survive, although Christine lingers in a coma. Kenneth arrives and takes Rhoda home.

At bedtime, Rhoda excitedly tells Kenneth that she will inherit Monica's pet [[lovebird]]. She mentions that she and Monica plan to sunbathe on the roof. When Kenneth tells her that lovebirds don't live as long as people, Rhoda becomes pensive about Monica's lifespan and her chances of getting the lovebird. Christine regains consciousness and is expected to make a full recovery. She calls Kenneth and tells him that she must pay for her "dreadful sin," but Kenneth assures her that they will work on their problems together.

At night, Rhoda sneaks away during a thunderstorm and attempts to retrieve the medal from the lake using a [[hand net|dip net]]. A sudden bolt of lightning strikes her, ultimately causing her death.

==Music==
The soundtrack, composed by [[Alex North]], was released by RCA Victor on vinyl upon the movie's release in 1956, however, it only included eleven songs.<ref>{{Citation |title=Alex North - The Bad Seed |date=1956 |url=https://www.discogs.com/release/3647312-Alex-North-The-Bad-Seed |access-date=2025-10-08 |language=en}}</ref> In 2011, those same songs were digitally released<ref>{{Cite web |title=Alex North - The Bad Seed (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) |url=https://boomkat.com/products/the-bad-seed-original-motion-picture-soundtrack |access-date=2025-10-08 |website=Boomkat}}</ref> and it was not until 2017 that La-La Land Records, Columbia Records and Sony Music put out all sixteen songs on CD for the first time.<ref>{{Cite web |title=BAD SEED, THE: LIMITED EDITION |url=https://lalalandrecords.com/bad-seed-the-limited-edition/ |access-date=2025-10-08 |website=La-La Land Records |language=en}}</ref> Excluded from the soundtrack, in multiple scenes Rhoda plays [[Au clair de la lune]], a French folk song, on the piano.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Bad Seed Movie Script |url=https://www.scripts.com/script/the_bad_seed_19713 |access-date=2025-10-08 |website=www.scripts.com}}</ref>

==Production==
[[File:Harbor Drive-In Ad - 25 October 1956, National City, CA.jpg|thumb|[[Drive-in theater|Drive-in]] advertisement from 1956]]
After the success of the book, Geoffrey Shurlock of the [[Production Code Administration]] (PCA) sent a letter to [[Jack Warner (film production executive)|Jack Warner]] stating that "the property violated the spirit and letter of the Code." Shurlock's office wrote to [[Paramount Pictures]], [[Columbia Pictures]] and [[Universal Pictures]] to caution them against the property. Although the studios had not yet formally inquired about it, [[Buddy Adler]], [[Frank McCarthy (producer)|Frank McCarthy]] and [[Dore Schary]] were interested in producing the film. After a bidding war, Warner purchased the film rights for $300,000 (equivalent to ${{Inflation|index=US|value=300000|start_year=1956|end_year=2021|r=-5|fmt=c|cursign=$}} in 2021).<ref name="AFI">{{cite web |title=The Bad Seed |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/51748 |website=AFI |publisher=American Film Institute |access-date=27 May 2021 |archive-date=28 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240628125128/https://catalog.afi.com/catalog/moviedetails/51748 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Inflation Calculator |url=https://www.dollartimes.com/inflation/inflation.php?amount=300000&year=1955 |website=Dollar Times |publisher=H Brothers, Inc. |access-date=27 May 2021 |archive-date=4 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240604160101/https://www.dollartimes.com/inflation/inflation.php?amount=300000&year=1955 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[United States Pictures]] stated that it would only produce the film for [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] upon approval by the PCA. Adler contacted Shurlock demanding to know why approval had been given. Shurlock responded that director [[Mervyn LeRoy]] had devised a [[Film treatment|treatment]] "that seemed to do what the office thought was impossible."<ref name="AFI" />

Although the novel and play conclude with Christine dying and Rhoda surviving, the [[Motion Picture Production Code]] did not permit perpetrators of crime to remain unpunished.<ref>''Writing the Horror Movie'' {{ISBN|978-1-441-19618-7}} p. 28</ref> The film's ending thus reverses the deaths of the mother and daughter, with Christine's life saved and Rhoda killed by a bolt of lightning. In another move to appease the censors, Warner Bros. added an "adults only" warning to the film's advertising.<ref name="TCM">[https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/67988/the-bad-seed#articles-reviews "The Bad Seed"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240601164426/https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/67988/the-bad-seed#articles-reviews |date=2024-06-01 }}, [[Turner Classic Movies]]; retrieved June 1, 2024.</ref> The film softens the shocking ending with a segment in which an announcer introduces the members of the cast. It concludes with Kelly lightheartedly spanking McCormack for her character's misdeeds. However, Le Roy claims that the ending was not forced by the Motion Picture Production Code and that it was done on his own accord, as he felt as though it was more appropriate.<ref>{{Cite web |last=LeRoy |first=Marvin |date=September 9, 1956 |title=ON TRANSPLANTING 'THE BAD SEED'; Director Lists Hurdles Faced in Adapting Play to Film Synthesis Motion Formula |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1956/09/09/archives/on-transplanting-the-bad-seed-director-lists-hurdles-faced-in.html |access-date=October 7, 2025 |website=The New York Times}}</ref>

==Reception==
''The Bad Seed'' was one of the larger hits of 1956 for Warner Bros., earning the company $4.1 million in theatrical rentals in the U.S. against a $1 million budget. The film finished among the year's top 20 at the box office in the United States and among the ten most popular box-office draws in Britain in 1956.<ref name="TCM"/><ref>BRITISH. FILMS MADE MOST MONEY: BOX-OFFICE SURVEY<br />''The Manchester Guardian'' (1901–1956), 28 Dec 1956: pg. 3</ref>

The film received favorable reviews from critics, and review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reports an approval rating of 64% based on {{nowrap|28 reviews}}, with a rating average of 7.0/10.<ref name="rtomatoesrating">{{cite web|title=The Bad Seed (1956) - Rotten Tomatoes|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1001572-bad_seed|website=Rotten Tomatoes.com|publisher=Rotten Tomatoes|access-date=September 23, 2025|archive-date=June 18, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240618184840/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1001572-bad_seed|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Influence and legacy==
McCormack starred in the 1995 low-budget film ''[[Mommy (1995 film)|Mommy]]'' as a psychopathic mother and in ''House of Deadly Secrets'' in 2018. Some consider both films as unofficial sequels to ''The Bad Seed''.{{citation needed|date=April 2018}}

The first act of the 1992 off-Broadway musical ''[[Ruthless!]]'' is inspired by the film.{{Citation needed|date=February 2023}}

The 1993 film ''[[The Good Son (film)|The Good Son]]'' is partly inspired by ''The Bad Seed''.<ref name=ref1>{{cite web|url=http://www.nick-cave.com/_biography.php|title=Nick Cave Online|access-date=2009-06-25|publisher=nick-cave.com|archive-date=2018-03-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180317102140/http://www.nick-cave.com/_biography.php|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2024, ''[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]'' listed Rhoda 3rd among "the best portrayals of cinematic sociopaths", in 2011 ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' called Rhoda "the perfect psychopath", and ''[[Psychology Today]]'' in 2023 wrote that the novel on which the movie is based "exposed the risk of [[psychopathy]] in children".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Roake |first=Jessica |date=2011-12-12 |title=Cute Little Psychokillers |url=https://slate.com/culture/2011/12/bad-seed-films-history-of-the-horror-film-genre.html |access-date=2025-03-08 |work=Slate |language=en-US |issn=1091-2339 |archive-date=2024-12-29 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241229222820/https://slate.com/culture/2011/12/bad-seed-films-history-of-the-horror-film-genre.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=The Genesis of William March's "The Bad Seed" {{!}} Psychology Today |url=https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/surviving-the-female-psychopath/202308/the-genesis-of-william-marchs-the-bad-seed |access-date=2025-03-08 |website=www.psychologytoday.com |language=en-US}}</ref><ref name=":0" />

==Remakes==
''The Bad Seed'' was [[The Bad Seed (1985 film)|remade for television]] in 1985, adapted by George Eckstein, directed by Paul Wendkos and starring Carrie Welles, [[Blair Brown]], [[Lynn Redgrave]], [[David Carradine]], [[Richard Kiley]] and [[Chad Allen (actor)|Chad Allen]]. The remake employs the original ending of the March novel and its stage production but was panned by critics and poorly received by its television audience.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/live-feed/lifetimes-next-tv-movie-a-805681|title=Lifetime's Next TV Movie: A Remake of 'The Bad Seed' (Exclusive)|website=The Hollywood Reporter|date=29 June 2015 |access-date=Sep 18, 2019}}</ref>

In June 2015, it was announced that [[Lifetime (TV network)|Lifetime]] would produce a remake. In December 2017, ''Deadline'' reported that [[Rob Lowe]] was to direct and star in the remake with [[Mckenna Grace]], Sarah Dugdale, Marci T. House, [[Lorne Cardinal]], Chris Shields, [[Cara Buono]], and a special appearance by McCormack as Dr. March. [[The Bad Seed (2018 film)|The film]] aired in September 2018.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/bad-seed-review-1140535|title='The Bad Seed': TV Review|website=The Hollywood Reporter|date=7 September 2018|access-date=Sep 18, 2019|archive-date=12 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191112023514/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/bad-seed-review-1140535|url-status=live}}</ref>

Four years later in September 2022, a sequel to the Lifetime TV movie, titled ''[[The Bad Seed Returns]]'', aired. It again featured [[Mckenna Grace]] as Emma and [[Patty McCormack]] as Emma's therapist, Dr. March.
