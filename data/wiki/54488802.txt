"'''Part 8'''", also known as "'''Gotta Light?'''",{{efn|Episodes did not originally air with titles, but the promotional quotes for each episode were later used as titles in the Blu-ray release of this season}} is the eighth episode of the [[Twin Peaks season 3|third season]] of the American [[Surrealist cinema|surrealist]] [[Mystery fiction|mystery]]-[[Horror fiction|horror]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama]] television series ''[[Twin Peaks]]''. It was written by series creators [[Mark Frost]] and [[David Lynch]], directed by Lynch, and stars [[Kyle MacLachlan]]. "Part 8" was first broadcast on June 25, 2017, on [[Showtime (TV network)|Showtime]], and was watched by an audience of 246,000 viewers in the United States.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Metcalf |first=Mitch |date=June 27, 2017 |title=UPDATED: SHOWBUZZDAILY's Top 150 Sunday Cable Originals & Network Finals: 6.25.2017 |url=http://www.showbuzzdaily.com/articles/showbuzzdailys-top-150-sunday-cable-originals-network-finals-6-25-2017.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170628042417/http://www.showbuzzdaily.com/articles/showbuzzdailys-top-150-sunday-cable-originals-network-finals-6-25-2017.html |archive-date=June 28, 2017 |access-date=June 27, 2017 |website=Showbuzz Daily}}</ref> The episode is composed of lengthy, surreal scenes,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lehrer |first=Adam |date=July 7, 2017 |title=Ep. 8 Of 'Twin Peaks' Is David Lynch's Purest Marriage Of Television And Video Art |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamlehrer/2017/07/07/ep-8-of-twin-peaks-is-david-lynchs-purest-marriage-of-television-and-video-art/#28ae4752526b |access-date=July 14, 2017 |website=[[Forbes]] |location=New York City}}</ref> with very little dialogue throughout, and was largely shot in [[black and white]]. Critics and audiences overwhelmingly praised the episode for its visuals, sound design, tone, and thematic depth,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Twin Peaks: The Return: Season 1, Episode 8 {{!}} Rotten Tomatoes |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/twin_peaks_the_return/s01/e08 |access-date=2025-11-19 |website=www.rottentomatoes.com |language=en}}</ref> but some viewers found the narrative's abstract nature confusing and lacking in coherence.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Miller |first=Liz Shannon |date=2017-06-26 |title='Twin Peaks' Review: Part 8 Aims for Maximum Weirdness and Succeeds |url=https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/shows/twin-peaks-2017-review-episode-8-part-8-season-3-david-lynch-showtime-spoilers-recap-1201847015/ |access-date=2025-11-19 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Keene |first=Allison |date=2017-06-26 |title=Twin Peaks Recap: "Part 8" - Gotta Light? |url=https://collider.com/twin-peaks-recap-part-8/ |access-date=2025-11-19 |website=Collider |language=en}}</ref>

== Plot ==

{{blockquote|Gotta light?|Woodsman (used as a promotional tagline for the episode)}}

===Background===
The small town of Twin Peaks, Washington, has been shocked by the murder of schoolgirl [[Laura Palmer]] ([[Sheryl Lee]]) and the attempted murder of her friend [[List of characters in Twin Peaks#Ronette Pulaski|Ronette Pulaski]] ([[Phoebe Augustine]]). [[FBI]] special agent [[Dale Cooper]] ([[Kyle MacLachlan]]) has been sent to the town to investigate<ref>{{Cite episode |title=Pilot |episode-link=Pilot (Twin Peaks) |series=[[Twin Peaks]] |last=David Lynch (writer and director) |author-link=David Lynch |last2=Mark Frost (writer) |author-link2=Mark Frost |network=[[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] |date=April 8, 1990 |season=1 |number=1}}</ref> and has discovered that the killer was Laura's father, [[Leland Palmer]] ([[Ray Wise]]), who acted while possessed by a demonic entity—[[Killer BOB]] ([[Frank Silva]]).<ref name="ep16">{{Cite episode |title=Episode&nbsp;16 |episode-link=Episode 16 (Twin Peaks) |series=Twin Peaks |series-link=Twin Peaks |last=Tim Hunter (director) |author-link=Tim Hunter (director) |last2=Mark Frost (writer) |author-link2=Mark Frost |network=[[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] |date=December 1, 1990 |season=2 |number=9}}</ref> At the [[Episode 29 (Twin Peaks)|end]] of the [[Twin Peaks|original series]], Cooper was trapped in the [[Black and White Lodges|Black Lodge]], an extra-dimensional place, by BOB, who let out Cooper's doppelgänger to use him as his physical access to the world.<ref name="ep29">{{Cite episode |title=Episode&nbsp;29 |episode-link=Episode 29 (Twin Peaks) |series=Twin Peaks |series-link=Twin Peaks |last=David Lynch (director) |author-link=David Lynch |last2=Mark Frost (writer) |author-link2=Mark Frost |network=[[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] |date=June 6, 1990 |season=2 |number=22 |author3=Harley Peyton (writer) |author3-link=Harley Peyton |author4=Robert Engels (writer) |author4-link=Robert Engels}}</ref> Twenty-five years after those events, Cooper manages to escape the Lodge through a portal between worlds; during this process, he was supposed to replace the doppelgänger, but instead he takes the place of a second doppelgänger (known as Dougie Jones), fabricated by the first as a patsy for the exchange. Cooper's doppelgänger, exhausted from the process, crashes his car and passes out, allowing the police to capture him;<ref name="pt3">{{Cite episode |title=Part 3 |episode-link=Twin Peaks season 3#ep33 |series=Twin Peaks |series-link=Twin Peaks |last=David Lynch (director & writer) |author-link=David Lynch |last2=Mark Frost (writer) |author-link2=Mark Frost |network=[[Showtime (TV network)|Showtime]] |date=May 28, 2017 |season=3 |number=3}}</ref> at the station, he uses the information in his possession to blackmail Warden Murphy ([[James Morrison (actor)|James Morrison]]) into releasing him and his partner, Ray Monroe ([[George Griffith (actor/director)|George Griffith]]).<ref name="pt7">{{Cite episode |title=Part 7 |episode-link=Twin Peaks season 3#ep37 |series=Twin Peaks |series-link=Twin Peaks |last=David Lynch (director & writer) |author-link=David Lynch |last2=Mark Frost (writer) |author-link2=Mark Frost |network=[[Showtime (TV network)|Showtime]] |date=June 18, 2017 |season=3 |number=7}}</ref>

===Events===
Monroe and Cooper's doppelgänger are traveling in a car Murphy prepared for their escape. Using his phone, the doppelgänger spots several trackers on the car, which he sends to a nearby truck to confuse the wardens. When the doppelgänger asks him whether he wants to go to "the farm", Ray responds affirmatively; when asked about Darya's fate, the doppelgänger lies and tells Ray that she's still alive, awaiting their call. On their way through, Ray stops to urinate; the doppelgänger uses this occasion to attempt to extort the information he was seeking from him, and, when he refuses to cooperate, tries to kill him, but the gun provided by Warden Murphy has had the firing pin removed and does not work.

Ray then shoots the doppelgänger twice, but as he is preparing to deliver his final hit, ghostly men (the "woodsmen") appear and begin to tear at the doppelgänger's body, revealing an orb that emerges from his wound with [[Killer Bob|Killer BOB's]] grinning face within. Other ghostly figures run circles around Ray in a ritualistic manner, prompting Ray to flee, terrified; on his drive away, he sends [[Phillip Jeffries]] a message that the doppelgänger may have survived the attack. In the Roadhouse, an MC (JR Starr) introduces "the" [[Nine Inch Nails]]; the group performs their song "[[She's Gone Away]]". Later, the doppelgänger awakens where he lay, seemingly fully restored.

In 1945 New Mexico, [[Trinity (nuclear test)|the first atomic bomb is detonated]]. Woodsmen circle around and inside a building labeled "convenience store" that appears burnt out. Floating in a void, the Experiment (Erica Eynon), a white humanoid form, spews a stream of primordial/ectoplasmic fluid; among various ova in the fluid one darker globule has BOB's visage. Red and gold imagery follows like burning embers, a fireworks radiation of atomic energy. In an imposing windowless building atop a craggy outcrop amid a purple sea, Señorita Dido ([[Joy Nash]]) sits next to a metallic-bell shaped machine, listening to a phonograph. When the machine begins to buzz loudly, [[The Giant (Twin Peaks)|the Fireman]] ([[Carel Struycken]]) enters, inspects the machine, switches the siren off, then moves upstairs to a room like a small vintage movie theatre with a film projector. The Fireman watches as images from the detonation, the convenience store, and the Experiment sprouting BOB play on the screen. He then begins to levitate, light and tendrils of energy emanating from his head like a forming galaxy, as stars are projected onscreen. Señorita Dido enters, and the Fireman emits a golden crystal/light orb containing Laura Palmer's face; the orb floats down to her. After kissing the orb, she sends it to Earth (which appears on the screen) through a golden tube contraption emanating from the whirring luminaire ceiling.

In 1956 New Mexico, an unearthly amphibi-insectoid creature hatches from an egg on the bomb's explosion site and crawls through the desert. A boy ([[Xolo Maridueña]]) and a girl (Tikaeni Faircrest) pass a gas station as he walks her home from a date; the girl finds a face-up penny and contemplates the good luck it might bring. Two woodsmen manifest and descend on a rural road, stopping a couple's (Tad Griffith and Leslie Berger) car. One woodsman ([[Robert Broski]]), cigarette in hand, repeatedly asks them, "Got a light?", which prompts them to flee, terrified. Meanwhile, the young couple reaches the girl's home; they share a brief first kiss before she walks in and he departs elated. The Woodsman enters a radio station. He asks the receptionist (Tracy Phillips) for a "light", then crushes her skull, killing her instantly; overpowering the disc jockey (Cullen Douglas) and dislodging [[The Platters]] single "[[My Prayer]]", he repeatedly broadcasts the words: "This is the water and this is the well. Drink full and descend. The horse is the white of the eyes and dark within." During the broadcast, numerous listeners fall unconscious, including the young girl, through whose room's open window the creature enters and climbs down her throat. The woodsman kills the disc jockey with another one-handed skull crush and leaves. As he walks into the desert, the neighing of a horse can be heard.

== Production ==

=== Writing ===
"Part 8", like the rest of the limited series, was written by [[Mark Frost]] and [[David Lynch]].{{Citation needed|date=September 2024}} Of the writing of this episode, Frost said:

<blockquote>The idea, obviously—or, well, not obviously—was that we'd never done anything close to what you might describe as a ''Twin Peaks'' origin story, [showing] where this pervasive sense of darkness and evil had come from. On the page, we wrote it in great detail. I think it was maybe 12, 15 pages. But as we were putting down the descriptions, I knew David was going to take that as the blueprint for something extraordinary. He ran with it and elevated it to a whole other level [...] the atomic explosion was probably half a page as written, but I knew that, in David's hands, it could run as long as 10 or 12 minutes, and it would be riveting. It was certainly a narrative departure from what we had done before. There was no question about that. But it needed to stand apart, and it needed to blow your mind. So mission accomplished.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Reed |first=Ryan |date=November 7, 2017 |title=The last word on "Twin Peaks" by David Lynch's co-creator Mark Frost. |url=https://www.salon.com/2017/11/07/the-last-word-on-twin-peaks-by-david-lynchs-co-creator-mark-frost/ |access-date=May 21, 2018 |website=[[Salon (website)|Salon.com]] |publisher=Salon Media Group |location=San Francisco, California}}</ref></blockquote>

In his autobiography ''Room to Dream'', Lynch called the insectoid creature a "frog-moth", and said the idea for it came from his [[Early life of David Lynch#Art student|travel in Europe]] with [[Jack Fisk]] during the mid-1960s:

<blockquote>[T]he frog-moth [...] came from [[Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia|Yugoslavia]]. When Jack and I were in Europe, we caught the ''[[Orient Express]]'' in [[Athens]] to take us back to [[Paris]], so we're going up through Yugoslavia and it's really, really dark. At a certain point the train came to a stop and there was no station but we could see people getting off the train. [...] When I got off the train I stepped into this soft dust that was like eight inches deep and it was blowing, and out of the earth these huge moths, like frogs, were leaping up, and they'd fly and flip and go back down again. So that was the frog-moth—things just sort of show up in the world of ''Twin Peaks''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Cruz |first=Gilbert |date=June 13, 2018 |title=David Lynch on Michael Jackson and That Crazy Frog From 'Twin Peaks' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2018/06/13/arts/television/david-lynch-room-to-dream.html |access-date=June 14, 2018 |publisher=The New York Times (website)}}</ref></blockquote>

On May 9, 2018, a page from the episode's script was shown in a documentary about Dean Hurley, the series' sound designer.<ref>{{YouTube|id=CPeiUFybVUs}}</ref> The page (numbered 195) translates on-screen to the woodsman breaking inside the radio station: differently from the final cut, the spelling of the episode's titular line was "Got a light?" and the woodsman was supposed to "speak disturbing, atonal word-like mechanical sounds into the mic, going out over the air in a strange monotone", part of which composed the mantra that was performed in the final episode. The page does not resemble the script the cast members received.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dom |first=Pieter |date=May 10, 2018 |title=Revealed: Actual Twin Peaks Season 3 Script Pages From Part 5 And Part 8 ("Got A Light?") |url=https://welcometotwinpeaks.com/news/twin-peaks-season-3-script-pages-part-5-part-8/ |access-date=May 21, 2018 |website=WelcomeToTwinPeaks}}</ref>

=== Filming ===

"Part 8" was directed by Lynch, who had also directed the rest of the limited series and six episodes of the original series—the "[[Pilot (Twin Peaks)|Pilot]]", "[[Episode 2 (Twin Peaks)|Episode 2]]", "[[Episode 8 (Twin Peaks)|Episode 8]]", "[[Episode 9 (Twin Peaks)|Episode 9]]", "[[Episode 14 (Twin Peaks)|Episode 14]]" and "[[Episode 29 (Twin Peaks)|Episode 29]]".<ref name="lynch">{{Cite web |title=David Lynch movies, photos, movie reviews, filmography, and biography |url=https://www.allmovie.com/artist/david-lynch-p100454/ |access-date=October 28, 2017 |website=[[AllRovi]] |publisher=[[Rovi Corporation]]}}</ref> The scenes inside the Fireman's house were filmed inside the [[Tower Theatre (Los Angeles)|Tower Theatre]] in [[Los Angeles]], a location Lynch had used in ''[[Mulholland Drive (film)|Mulholland Drive]]'' (2001). On set, Lynch showed Joy Nash how to hold the [[Laura Palmer]] orb, kissing it and giving it "so much love" before letting it go; he also demonstrated to her how to walk through the theatre, with bouncing steps like a "little cherub."<ref>Jason S. (director) (December 5, 2017). "The Number of Completion". Impressions: A Journey Behind the Scenes of Twin Peaks. Season 1. Episode 4. [[CBS Home Entertainment]].</ref> The scenes at KPJK were filmed in a specially constructed set in New Mexico. Upon visiting the set for the first time, Lynch said that he had envisioned a simple set for one shot, but instead they had built an authentic radio station, which he joked could be on the air by the following week.<ref>Jason S. (director) (December 5, 2017). "Two Blue Balls". Impressions: A Journey Behind the Scenes of Twin Peaks. Season 1. Episode 3. [[CBS Home Entertainment]].</ref>

=== Music ===
Almost every episode of the 2017 ''Twin Peaks'' series features a live performance by a band at the Roadhouse. In this episode, American rock band [[Nine Inch Nails]] performed "[[She's Gone Away]]" (2016). Nine Inch Nails frontman [[Trent Reznor]] collaborated with Lynch on the [[Lost Highway (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] for ''[[Lost Highway (film)|Lost Highway]]'' (1997),<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kiefer |first=Halle |date=June 25, 2017 |title=Nine Inch Nails Stop By the Roadhouse on Tonight's Twin Peaks |url=https://www.vulture.com/2017/06/nine-inch-nails-stop-by-the-roadhouse-on-twin-peaks.html |access-date=July 13, 2017 |website=Vulture}}</ref> and Lynch directed the video for the Nine Inch Nails song "[[Came Back Haunted]]".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Phillips |first=Amy |last2=Pelly |first2=Jenn |date=June 19, 2013 |title=David Lynch to Direct Nine Inch Nails' "Came Back Haunted" Video |url=https://pitchfork.com/news/51231-david-lynch-to-direct-nine-inch-nails-came-back-haunted-video/ |access-date=June 20, 2013 |website=[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]] |publisher=[[Pitchfork Media]] |location=Chicago, Illinois}}</ref> Reznor and [[Atticus Ross]] originally submitted a different track for the episode, but Lynch rejected it and requested they create "something that feels menacing and unpleasant". The original track later became "This Isn't The Place", featured on the Nine Inch Nails EP ''[[Add Violence]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wampler |first=Scott |date=2022-06-24 |title=Trent Reznor On TWIN PEAKS: THE RETURN PART 8, Five Years Later |url=https://www.fangoria.com/trent-reznor-on-twin-peaks-the-return-part-8-five-years-later/ |access-date=2025-06-27 |website=www.fangoria.com |language=en-US}}</ref>

Uniquely, the performance takes place during the episode, preceding the New Mexico scenes, rather than at its end. During the scene in which Monroe shoots Cooper's doppelgänger, a severely slowed-down recording of [[Ludwig van Beethoven]]'s ''[[Piano Sonata No. 14 (Beethoven)|Moonlight Sonata]]'' plays.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Dom |first=Pieter |date=June 28, 2017 |title=To Score The Haunting Woodsmen Scene, David Lynch Severely Slowed Down Beethoven's "Moonlight Sonata" And Mixed It With Monkey Screeches |url=https://welcometotwinpeaks.com/music/woodsmen-beethoven-moonlight-sonata/ |access-date=December 1, 2017 |website=WelcomeToTwinPeaks}}</ref> The atomic bomb sequence is underscored by [[Krzysztof Penderecki]]'s ''[[Threnody to the Victims of Hiroshima]]'' (1961).<ref name="ew" /> Two original recordings are used in the episode, during the scenes in the structure over the purple sea: "Slow '30s Room", a remixed excerpt of the seventh movement in Lynch and Dean Hurley's 2007 album ''[[The Air Is on Fire (album)|The Air Is on Fire]]'', and "The Fireman" by [[Angelo Badalamenti]]; both were released on the 2017 album ''[[Twin Peaks: Limited Event Series Original Soundtrack]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Twin Peaks: Music from the Limited Event Series |url=https://www.rhino.com/product/twin-peaks-music-from-the-limited-event-series |access-date=October 28, 2017 |publisher=Rhino Records}}</ref> [[The Platters]]' "[[My Prayer]]" is used in the final scene.<ref name="nytimes" /> Coincidentally, one of the group's founding members is a singer named David Lynch.<ref name="rock hall">{{Cite web |title=The Platters |url=https://www.rockhall.com/inductees/platters |access-date=August 26, 2014 |publisher=Rock & Roll Hall of Fame}}</ref>

==Reception==
"Part 8" received critical acclaim. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the episode received a 100% rating based on 29 reviews. The critical consensus reads, "'Part 8' adds yet another masterful chapter to ''Twin Peaks''{{'}} return—and arguably one of the finest hours of creator David Lynch's incredible career."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Twin Peaks - The Return, Episode 8 |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/twin_peaks_the_return/s01/e08 |access-date=July 13, 2017 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |location=San Francisco, California}}</ref> Writing for ''[[IndieWire]]'', Liz Shannon Miller gave the episode a "B", expressing disappointment at the "disturbingly retro" special effects in the opening sequence and calling the Trinity Nuclear Test sequence "beautiful nonsense", while calling the scenes with Señorita Dido and the Fireman "captivating". She called the episode "truly polarizing" and "one that challenges viewers to appreciate its beauty, even if we don't understand it. Wherever you land on it, there's one thing that can't be argued: You've never seen this before on television."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Miller |first=Liz |date=June 25, 2017 |title=Twin Peaks 2017 Review: Episode 8 (Part 8) Aims For Maximum Weirdness |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2017/06/twin-peaks-2017-review-episode-8-part-8-season-3-david-lynch-showtime-spoilers-recap-1201847015/ |access-date=July 12, 2017 |website=[[IndieWire]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |location=Los Angeles, California}}</ref>

''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} Noel Murray gave the episode a positive review, drawing favorable comparisons to the end sequence of [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey (film)|2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' and [[Jonathan Glazer]]'s ''[[Under the Skin (2013 film)|Under the Skin]]'', calling the episode "phenomenal".<ref name="nytimes">{{Cite news |last=Murray |first=Noel |date=June 26, 2017 |title='Twin Peaks' Season 3, Episode 8: White Light White Heat |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2017/06/26/arts/television/twin-peaks-season-3-episode-8-recap.html |access-date=July 12, 2017 |work=[[The New York Times]] |location=New York City}}</ref> In his recap for ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', [[Jeff Jensen]] called Part 8 "a mesmerizing rush of pure-cut WTF."<ref name="ew">{{Cite web |last=Jensen |first=Jeff |date=June 26, 2017 |title=Twin Peaks recap: 'The Return: Part 8' |url=https://ew.com/recap/twin-peaks-season-3-episode-8/ |access-date=July 12, 2017 |publisher=[[Meredith Corporation]] |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |location=New York City}}</ref>

In her positive review of the episode, ''[[The A.V. Club]]''{{'}}s Emily L. Stephens gave the episode an "A", writing that she might not have been as impressed with it as a discrete [[experimental film]] but "both as a piece of ''Twin Peaks ''backstory and as an episode of television, 'The Return, Part 8' is as unexpected, as shocking, as thrilling as anything I've ever seen."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Stephens |first=Emily L. |date=June 26, 2017 |title=Twin Peaks swerves into uncharted territory |url=https://www.avclub.com/twin-peaks-the-return-recap-season-3-episode-8 |access-date=July 12, 2017 |website=[[The A.V. The A.V. Club reacts to the boldest Twin Peaks yet |url=https://www.avclub.com/got-a-light-the-a-v-club-reacts-to-the-boldest-twin-p-1798288423 |access-date=October 2, 2017 |website=[[The A.V. ===Accolades===
The episode was nominated for several awards. Along with "[[Part 1 (Twin Peaks)|Part 1]]" and "Part 15", the [[Art Directors Guild]] nominated it for the award for One-Hour Contemporary Single-Camera Series.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Giardina |first=Carolyn |date=January 4, 2018 |title=Art Directors Guild Awards: 'Dunkirk,' 'Shape of Water,' 'Blade Runner 2049' Among Nominees |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/2018-art-directors-guild-nominations-1067883/item/one-hour-contemporary-single-camera-series-1067961 |access-date=February 11, 2018 |publisher=[[Eldridge Industries]] |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |location=Los Angeles, California}}</ref> The [[Cinema Audio Society Awards|Cinema Audio Society]] nominated it for Outstanding Achievement in Sound Mixing for Television Movie or Mini-Series<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pedersen |first=Erik |date=January 10, 2018 |title=CAS Awards Nominations Announced For Film & TV Sound Mixing |url=https://deadline.com/2018/01/cas-awards-nominations-2018-last-jedi-game-of-thrones-veep-1202239554/ |access-date=February 11, 2018 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |location=Los Angeles, California}}</ref> and the [[Motion Picture Sound Editors]] nominated it for a Golden Reel Award for Outstanding Achievement in Sound Editing.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pedersen |first=Erik |date=January 22, 2018 |title=Motion Picture Sound Editors Reveal Golden Reel Nominations |url=https://deadline.com/2018/01/golden-reel-nominations-2018-list-motion-picture-sound-editors-1202266360/ |access-date=February 11, 2018 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |location=Los Angeles, California}}</ref>

For the [[70th Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards]], the episode was nominated in the Limited Series categories for Outstanding Cinematography, Outstanding Single-Camera Picture Editing, Outstanding Sound Editing, and Outstanding Sound Mixing.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Twin Peaks |url=https://www.emmys.com/shows/twin-peaks |access-date=July 14, 2018 |website=Emmys.com}}</ref>
