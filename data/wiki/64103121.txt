'''''The Painter and the Thief''''' ({{Langx|no|Kunstneren og tyven}}) is a 2020 [[Norwegian language|Norwegian]] [[documentary film|documentary]] film directed by [[Benjamin Ree]].

==Synopsis==
The documentary film follows Barbora Kysilkova, a talented [[Hyperrealism (visual arts)|hyperrealist]] artist, forming a friendship with Karl-Bertil Nordland, a man convicted of stealing her work from an Oslo art gallery, spanning a period of three years.

* Barbora Kysilkova

==Background==

In an interview quoted in ''[[The Guardian]]'', director [[Benjamin Ree]] stated, “The questions I would like to explore here are: what do humans do in order to be seen and appreciated? And what it takes of us to help and see others.” <ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/may/20/the-painter-and-the-thief-art-documentary|title=The Painter and the Thief: behind the year's most moving documentary|website=[[The Guardian]]|date=May 20, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=February 8, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210208052950/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/may/20/the-painter-and-the-thief-art-documentary|url-status=live}}</ref>

The idea for the film came when Norwegian filmmaker Benjamin Ree read news reports about the robbery of two large oil paintings from [[Oslo]]’s ''Galleri Nobe'', in 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/art-theft-true-crime-friends-documentary|title=How an Artist Befriended the Thief Who Stole Her Painting|website=[[Vanity Fair (magazines)|Vanity Fair]]|date=May 21, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204052413/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/art-theft-true-crime-friends-documentary|url-status=live}}</ref> He contacted the artist, Barbora Kysilkova, a Czech now living in Norway, and began documenting her reaction to the theft of her artwork, two paintings entitled ''Chloe & Emma'' and ''Swan Song''. It took a while to convince Karl-Bertil Nordland to participate, and Ree began filming both of them together on the fourth time they met.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://directorsnotes.com/2020/10/28/benjamin-ree-the-painter-and-the-thief/|title=Benjamin Ree Reinvents the Documentary Form with the Boundary Breaking 'The Painter and the Thief'|website=[[Directors notes]]|date=October 28, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=February 12, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210212034359/https://directorsnotes.com/2020/10/28/benjamin-ree-the-painter-and-the-thief/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film started as a [[Short film|short]] documentary with the filmmakers not knowing what would happen, where the story would go. It was decided to make it a feature film after seeing Nordland's moving first reaction to his portrait by Kysilkova. It was filmed from 2016 to 2019.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://moveablefest.com/benjamin-ree-barbora-kysilkova-and-karl-bertil-nordland-painter-and-the-thief/|title=Interview: Benjamin Ree, Barbora Kysilkova and Karl-Bertil Nordland on Shifting Perspectives with "The Painter and the Thief"|website=[[Movable Feast]]|date=May 23, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=February 6, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210206133025/http://moveablefest.com/benjamin-ree-barbora-kysilkova-and-karl-bertil-nordland-painter-and-the-thief/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The [[Stock footage|archival footage]] in the documentary was mostly contributed by a friend of Kysilkova, who had been filming the artist since 2014, including stills and video from the making of the two paintings later stolen. The friend was also at the exhibition, and she participated in the trial. The featured courtroom proceedings are genuine; Kysilkova, unfamiliar with the Norwegian language, had taken a hidden audio recorder to obtain a copy of the trial for translation afterwards. Inadvertently left running, the tape had also captured Kysilkova and Nordland's first meeting — seeking to redress the loss felt from her work being stolen, and “a sort of obligation to continue the story”, the artist had approached the accused during a break to suggest that he be a subject for her future painting.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ehrlich |first=David |date=2020-01-24 |title='The Painter and the Thief' Review: A Tender Psychosexual Tale of Art and Ownership |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2020/01/the-painter-and-the-thief-sundance-review-1202205354/ |access-date=2022-12-01 |website=IndieWire |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Galvin |first=Nick |date=2021-03-23 |title='I felt confused yet flattered': He stole from her, she embraced him |url=https://www.smh.com.au/culture/art-and-design/i-felt-confused-yet-flattered-he-stole-from-her-she-embraced-him-20210323-p57ddf.html |access-date=2022-12-01 |website=The Sydney Morning Herald |language=en}}</ref> Kysilkova was also intrigued as to why anyone would steal her relatively unknown work, and Nordland's answer of “Because they were beautiful” piqued her curiosity.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-05-20 |title=The Painter and the Thief: behind the year's most moving documentary |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/may/20/the-painter-and-the-thief-art-documentary |access-date=2022-12-01 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref> The film also uses the actual [[Closed-circuit television|CCTV]] footage of the robbery, which was the main evidence in the trial.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://observer.com/2020/02/the-painter-and-the-thief-documentary-barbora-kysilkova-benjamin-ree/|title=An Artist Meets the Thief Who Stole Her Work in an Astonishing New Documentary|website=[[Observer.com|Observer]]|date=February 5, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=January 21, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210121051115/https://observer.com/2020/02/the-painter-and-the-thief-documentary-barbora-kysilkova-benjamin-ree/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Kysilkova spoke [[English language|English]] in the film, as she didn't understand Norwegian.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://directorsnotes.com/2020/10/28/benjamin-ree-the-painter-and-the-thief/|title=Benjamin Ree Reinvents the Documentary Form with the Boundary Breaking 'The Painter and the Thief'|website=[[Director's notes]]|date=October 28, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=February 12, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210212034359/https://directorsnotes.com/2020/10/28/benjamin-ree-the-painter-and-the-thief/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Ree stated, “For me, filmmaking is about asking intellectually stimulating and emotionally engaging questions through observing human behaviour. That’s why I’ve made a movie that asks the complex questions around such a unique and complex relationship as Barbora and Karl-Bertil.”<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2020-11-06 |title=The Painter and the Thief: An Exploration of Human Connection |url=https://norwegianarts.org.uk/the-painter-and-the-thief-an-exploration-of-human-connection/ |access-date=2022-12-01 |website=Norwegian Arts |language=en-GB}}</ref> Ree chose to present the film in an unconventional structure: "I wanted a film that tries something new that I haven’t seen in this kind of observational documentary before".<ref name="Bacon">{{Cite web |last=Bacon |first=Redmond |date=2020-10-28 |title=The Painter and the Thief by Benjamin Ree // Documentary // WeAreDN |url=https://directorsnotes.com/2020/10/28/benjamin-ree-the-painter-and-the-thief/ |access-date=2022-12-01 |website=Directors Notes |language=en-GB}}</ref> "First of all, dramaturgy in a documentary is not only an artistic choice, it’s also an ethical choice. The impression the audience gets of Barbora and Karl Bertil is something they have to live with for the rest of their lives. I really wanted to portray Bertil as charismatic, intelligent, dark and funny, to really show his complexity and get to know him. I thought the only way to do that was to show the world from his point of view. How you portray people in a documentary is closely connected to the ethics of it".<ref name="Bacon"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2020/05/the-painter-and-the-thief-nonfiction-neon-oscar-contender-1202232983/|title=Why 'The Painter and the Thief' Is Neon's New Oscar Doc Contender|website=[[IndieWire]]|date=May 22, 2020|access-date=February 14, 2021|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204115839/https://www.indiewire.com/2020/05/the-painter-and-the-thief-nonfiction-neon-oscar-contender-1202232983/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
''The Painter and the Thief'' received positive reviews from film critics, mainly for its direction, story, structure and emotional weight. It holds a 'fresh' {{RT data|score}} approval rating on [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], based on {{RT data|count}} critic reviews, with an average of {{RT data|average}}. The site's critical consensus reads, "''The Painter and the Thief'' uses the unlikely bond between a criminal and his victim as the canvas for a compelling portrait of compassion and forgiveness."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_painter_and_the_thief|title=The Painter and the Thief|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date|df=dmy}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200530010553/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_painter_and_the_thief|archive-date=30 May 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film is currently assigned a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]] score of 79 out of 100, based on 33 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|title=The Painter and the Thief|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-painter-and-the-thief|access-date=May 28, 2020|website=[[Metacritic]]|archive-date=May 23, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200523142914/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-painter-and-the-thief|url-status=live}}</ref>

Cath Clarke from ''[[The Guardian]]'' awarded the film 4 stars out of 5, describing it as "astonishing, emotionally electric" and writing, "What an engrossing film – and the gender reversal of a male muse inspiring a female painter has got to be one small step for art-world equality,"<ref>{{Cite news|last=Clarke|first=Cath|date=30 October 2020|title=The Painter and the Thief review – astonishing portrait of two lost souls|work=[[The Guardian]]|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/oct/30/the-painter-and-the-thief-review-barbora-kysilkova-portrait|url-status=live|access-date=28 March 2021|archive-date=9 December 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201209101939/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/oct/30/the-painter-and-the-thief-review-barbora-kysilkova-portrait}}</ref> while Adrian Horton called the film a "remarkable documentary" that "plays more like a twisting narrative film than real-life portrait."<ref>{{Cite news|last=Horton|first=Adrian|date=2020-05-20|title=The Painter and the Thief: behind the year's most moving documentary|language=en-GB|work=[[The Guardian]]|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/may/20/the-painter-and-the-thief-art-documentary|url-status=live|access-date=2020-07-14|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200629024419/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/may/20/the-painter-and-the-thief-art-documentary|archive-date=2020-06-29|issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

In his review, Nick Schager from ''[[The Daily Beast]]'' describes the story as "a true-crime tale reconfigured into a unique relationship saga, replete with twists, turns, heartbreak, failure and redemption that’s as surprising as it is well-earned." He also praised the film's ending as "unbelievably unexpected, poignant, and altogether perfect."<ref>{{Cite news|last=Schager|first=Nick|date=2020-05-18|title=The Unbelievable True Story of an Artist Who Formed a Lasting Bond With Her Thief|language=en|work=[[The Daily Beast]]|url=https://www.thedailybeast.com/hulus-the-painter-and-the-thief-tells-the-amazing-true-story-of-an-artist-who-befriended-her-thief|url-status=live|access-date=2021-03-28|archive-date=2020-06-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200602145921/https://www.thedailybeast.com/hulus-the-painter-and-the-thief-tells-the-amazing-true-story-of-an-artist-who-befriended-her-thief}}</ref>

Peter Debruge wrote for [[Variety (magazine)|''Variety'']] magazine that its direction and editing "don’t give us all the information we might need to form a clear understanding of their subjects’ actions, yet their approach is not only more artistic but somehow more representative of real life", also noting its "time-bending nonlinear structure and various sleight-of-hand techniques to deliver information when it’s most effective."<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Debruge|first=Peter|date=2020-01-24|title='The Painter and the Thief': Film Review|url=https://variety.com/2020/film/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief-review-1203472656/|url-status=live|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|language=en-US|access-date=2021-03-28|archive-date=2021-03-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210302201538/https://variety.com/2020/film/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief-review-1203472656/}}</ref>

Paul Byrnes wrote in his review for ''[[The Sydney Morning Herald]]'', "The level of trust required to allow a filmmaker to document their lives is in itself moving, but it mirrors the relationship they have with each other," adding that "The slow revelation of [Barbora's] pain makes clear just how artful Ree’s work on structure has been in this achingly beautiful true story."<ref>{{Cite news|last=Byrnes|first=Paul|date=24 March 2021|title=Achingly beautiful true story defies belief|work=[[The Sydney Morning Herald]]|url=https://www.smh.com.au/culture/movies/achingly-beautiful-true-story-defies-belief-20210324-p57dik.html|url-status=live|access-date=28 March 2021|archive-date=27 March 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210327000711/https://www.smh.com.au/culture/movies/achingly-beautiful-true-story-defies-belief-20210324-p57dik.html}}</ref>

Brian Tallerico from ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' awarded ''The Painter and the Thief'' 3 stars out of 4, writing that it "illuminates a great deal about the human condition" and that some sequences "are going to stick with me for a long time." He added, "Ree has a very cinematic language, shooting long shots down hallways, trailing his subjects like a [[French New Wave]] director would follow his fictional creations down a sidewalk." However, he felt that the film "does kind of fizzle out in the third act ... I was hoping for something to take the film to the next level in the final section, but the opposite almost happens. A question that feels like it should have been asked long before is saved for the 'climax', and I was reminded of the construction of the film in a negative way."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Tallerico|first=Brian|date=22 May 2020|title=The Painter and the Thief movie review (2020)|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief-movie-review-2020|url-status=live|access-date=2021-03-28|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|language=en|archive-date=2021-01-24|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210124201203/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief-movie-review-2020}}</ref>

Writing for the magazine ''[[Little White Lies (magazine)|Little White Lies]]'', Leila Latif commended the director for tackling the film's themes "with gentle curiosity, never pushing the subjects to probe this dynamic too forcefully, but rather allowing it to slowly unveil itself."<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Latif|first=Leila|date=30 October 2020|title=The Painter and the Thief|url=https://lwlies.com/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief/|url-status=live|access-date=28 March 2021|magazine=[[Little White Lies (magazine)|Little White Lies]]|archive-date=25 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210125222844/https://lwlies.com/reviews/the-painter-and-the-thief/}}</ref>

David Ehrlich from [[IndieWire]] gave the film the grade B−, calling it a "a tender psychosexual tale of art and ownership" and a "nuanced and beguiling new documentary about the various things we all take from each other." He also wrote, "Ree doesn’t pat himself on the back for his efforts to humanize both of his title characters. Instead, ''The Painter and the Thief'' recognizes how art — ostensibly, empathetic art most of all — has a nasty habit of flattening its subject in order to fulfill its audience, and the film does what it can to complicate the privileged gaze of looking at someone like they can’t look at you back."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Ehrlich|first=David|date=2020-01-24|title='The Painter and the Thief' Review: A Tender Psychosexual Tale of Art and Ownership|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2020/01/the-painter-and-the-thief-sundance-review-1202205354/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210328122245/https://www.indiewire.com/2020/01/the-painter-and-the-thief-sundance-review-1202205354/|archive-date=2021-03-28|access-date=2021-03-28|website=[[IndieWire]]|language=en}}</ref>
