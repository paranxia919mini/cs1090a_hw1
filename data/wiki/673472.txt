'''''The Lost Boys''''' is a 1987 American [[comedy horror]] film directed by [[Joel Schumacher]] and written by [[Jeffrey Boam]], Janice Fischer, and James Jeremias. Based on a story by Fischer and Jeremias, it follows two teenage brothers who move with their divorced mother to the fictional [[California]] town of Santa Carla, which they discover is a haven for vampires. The film stars [[Corey Feldman]], [[Jami Gertz]], [[Corey Haim]], [[Edward Herrmann]], [[Barnard Hughes]], [[Jason Patric]], [[Kiefer Sutherland]], and [[Dianne Wiest]].

The title is a reference to the [[Peter Pan (character)|Peter Pan]] and [[Neverland]] characters, the [[Lost Boys (Peter Pan)|Lost Boys]], who (like vampires) never grow up. Produced by [[Harvey Bernhard]] for the studio Richard Donner Production, with [[Richard Donner]] himself serving as executive producer, the film was primarily shot in [[Santa Cruz, California]].

''The Lost Boys'' was released by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] on July 31, 1987, and was a critical and commercial success, grossing $32.5 million on a budget of $8.5 million. It has since been described as a [[Cult film|cult classic]]. Over two decades after its release, it spawned [[The Lost Boys (franchise)|a franchise]] with two low-budget sequels (''[[Lost Boys: The Tribe]]'' and ''[[Lost Boys: The Thirst]]'') and two comic book series. A [[The Lost Boys (musical)|musical adaptation]] of the movie opened on Broadway in April 2026, winning [[79th Tony Awards|4 Tony Awards]] and receiving 12 nominations, including Best Musical.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Grein |first=Paul |date=2026-06-07 |title=Here Are the 2026 Tony Awards Winners: Full List |url=https://www.billboard.com/lists/2026-tony-awards-winners-list/ |access-date=2026-06-09 |website=Billboard |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Plot==
Teenager [[Michael Emerson (The Lost Boys)|Michael Emerson]], his younger brother Sam, and their recently divorced mother Lucy move to the seaside town of Santa Carla, [[California]]. They live with Lucy's eccentric father, to whom they refer solely as Grandpa. Lucy takes a job at a video store owned by Max, who takes a romantic interest in her. At the local comic book store, Sam meets brothers Edgar and Alan Frog, self-proclaimed vampire hunters who believe vampires have infested the town.
 
Michael becomes drawn to Star, a beautiful girl he meets on the boardwalk. He notices her spending time with [[David (The Lost Boys)|David]], the charismatic leader of a local gang that includes Paul, Dwayne, and Marko. Sensing Michael's interest in Star, David challenges him to keep up with them on their motorcycles, leading him to their hideout in a former luxury hotel buried during the [[1906 San Francisco earthquake]]. There, David manipulates Michael's perception, making his food appear as maggots or worms, before offering him a bottle of red liquid. Although Star warns him that it contains blood, Michael drinks it. David's gang then leads him to a railway bridge, where they hang suspended over a vast drop, each member eventually letting go and disappearing into the fog. Michael, unable to hold on, falls, but he inexplicably lands safely in his bed.

Michael begins to change; he becomes sensitive to light, finds normal food revolting, and notices his reflection fading. He develops a craving for blood and nearly attacks Sam, but Sam's dog, Nanook, intervenes and stops him. Though terrified, Sam agrees to help Michael and deduces that he is turning into a vampire. His transformation will only be complete, though, if he feeds on human blood, and it may be reversible if they kill the head vampire. Sam, Edgar, and Alan suspect Max is the head vampire, but after Max is invited to dinner at Lucy's house, they notice he has a reflection and has no reaction to garlic or holy water.

David and his gang attempt to push Michael into killing by revealing their true monstrous faces and demonstrating their ability to fly as they murder a group of partygoers. Michael resists the temptation to feed. Later, Star confides in Michael that Laddie, a young member of the gang, and she are also only partly transformed, as they, too, have refused to kill. She admits that David wanted her to kill Michael to complete her transformation. Determined to save her, Michael leads Sam and the Frog brothers to the gang's lair during the day while the vampires are sleeping. They stake and kill Marko, whose pained screams awaken the others. The boys narrowly escape with Star and Laddie, as David vows to hunt them down that night.

Michael, Sam, and the Frog brothers prepare for the gang's impending assault, arming themselves with holy water, a longbow, and stakes. As night falls, David's gang breaks into the Emerson home. In the ensuing fight, Sam, Nanook, and the Frog brothers kill Paul and Dwayne, while Michael takes on David. Michael resists David's urging to accept his transformation and impales him on Grandpa's collection of antlers. Despite David's death, Michael's transformation is not undone, leading the group to realize that David was not the head vampire.

Lucy and Max return home from their date. Spotting David's lifeless body, Max confesses he is the head vampire and reveals he passed the boys' earlier vampire tests because he had been invited into their home, making him immune to those vulnerabilities. Max explains his desire to create a vampire family and that he ordered David to turn Michael and Sam to compel Lucy to join the family as mother to the boys. To save Sam from being killed on the spot by Max, Lucy agrees to Max's demands. Before Max can turn her, though, Grandpa crashes his truck through the wall, impaling Max with a large wooden fence post and causing him to explode. With Max's death, Michael, Star, and Laddie revert to normal. Grandpa then grumbles, "One thing about living in Santa Carla I never could stomach—all the damn vampires."

==Production==
===Background===
A March 5, 1985, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' news item announced that the independent production company [[Producers Sales Organization]] (PSO) bought first-time screenwriters Janice Fischer and James Jeremias' ''Lost Boys'' script for $400,000 on February 20, 1985.<ref name="afi"/> PSO announced their acquisition of the project at the [[American Film Market]] in 1985. [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] later joined the project, taking over domestic distribution and some foreign territories.<ref name="afi">{{cite web |title=The Lost Boys (1987) |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67055 |website=[[AFI Catalog of Feature Films]]}}</ref>

The film's title is a reference to the [[Peter Pan's Lost Boys|characters]] featured in [[J. M. Barrie]]'s ''[[Peter Pan (character)|Peter Pan]]'' stories, who—like vampires—never grow old. Jeremias said, "I had read [[Anne Rice]]'s ''[[Interview with the Vampire]]'', and in that there was a 200-year-old vampire trapped in the body of a 12-year-old girl. Since Peter Pan had been one of my all-time favourite stories, I thought, 'What if the reason Peter Pan came out at night and never grew up and could fly was because he was a vampire?'"<ref name="Empire"/> According to academic William Patrick Day, the central theme of ''The Lost Boys'', "organized around loose allusions to ''Peter Pan''", is the tension surrounding the Emerson family and the world of contemporary adolescence.<ref>{{cite book|last=Day|first=William Patrick|url=https://archive.org/details/vampirelegendsin0000dayw/page/28/mode/2up|title=Vampire Legends in Contemporary American Culture: What Becomes a Legend Most|publisher=[[University Press of Kentucky]]|location=Lexington|year=2002|page=29|isbn=978-0-8131-9250-5|url-access=registration}}</ref>

The film was originally set to be directed by [[Richard Donner]], and Fischer and Jeremias' screenplay was modeled on Donner's recent film ''[[The Goonies]]'' (1985).<ref name="indiewire1">{{cite news| url=https://indiewire.com/2012/08/5-things-you-might-not-know-about-the-lost-boys-on-its-25th-anniversary-251374/| title=5 Things You Might Not Know About 'The Lost Boys' on Its 25th Anniversary| first=Oliver |last=Lyttelton| website=[[IndieWire]]| date=2012-08-01| access-date=2018-02-23}}</ref> In this way the film was envisioned as more of a juvenile vampire adventure with 13 or 14-year-old vampires, while the Frog brothers were "chubby 8 year-old Cub Scouts" and the character of Star was a young boy.<ref>{{cite video| url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OryXT5-F2RY |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/OryXT5-F2RY |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live| title=The Lost Boys dvd promo| year=2004| via=[[YouTube]]| access-date=2018-02-17}}{{cbignore}}</ref><ref name="indiewire1"/> When Donner committed to other projects, [[Joel Schumacher]] was approached to direct the film, although Donner eventually received credit as an executive producer. He came up with the idea of making the film sexier and more adult, bringing on screenwriter [[Jeffrey Boam]] to retool the script and raise the ages of the characters.<ref name="Empire">{{cite magazine |last=Godfrey |first=Alex |title=The Lost Boys: Joel Schumacher On Making The Coolest Vampire Movie Of All Time |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/lost-boys-joel-schumacher-making-of-interview-feature/ |access-date=28 May 2023 |magazine=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |date=October 28, 2022}}</ref><ref name="retrospective">{{cite video| title=The Lost Boys: A Retrospective| medium=DVD| publisher=Warner Bros. Home Video| year=2004}}</ref>

===Casting===
Schumacher said he had "one of the greatest [casts] in the world. They are what make the film." Most of the younger cast members were relatively unknown. Schumacher and [[Marion Dougherty]] met with many candidates.<ref name="retrospective"/> [[Jason Patric]] was approached early on by Schumacher to play Michael Emerson, but Patric had no interest in doing a vampire film, and turned it down "many times". Eventually, he was won over by Schumacher's vision and his promise to allow the cast "creative input" in making the film. According to Kiefer Sutherland, Patric "was really instrumental" in adapting the script with Schumacher and shaping the film.<ref name="auto">{{cite video| url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MQIMz2R0f5c |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/MQIMz2R0f5c |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live| title=The Lost Boys Panel - Jason Patric and Kiefer Sutherland| publisher=Renegade Geek|via=[[YouTube]]| date=2019-05-27| access-date=2020-02-08}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

Schumacher envisioned the character of Star as being a waifish blonde, similar to [[Meg Ryan]], but he was convinced by Jason Patric to consider Jami Gertz, who had just worked with Patric in ''[[Solarbabies]]'' (1986). Schumacher was impressed, but only at Patric's insistence did he finally cast Gertz.<ref name="retrospective"/> Schumacher was surprised when his first choice for the role of Lucy, Dianne Wiest, accepted the role, as she had just recently won the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress]] for ''[[Hannah and Her Sisters]]'' (1986) at the [[59th Academy Awards]].<ref name="retrospective"/>

After seeing Kiefer Sutherland's portrayal of Tim in ''[[At Close Range]]'', Schumacher arranged a reading with him at which they got on very well. Sutherland had just completed work on ''[[Stand by Me (film)|Stand by Me]]'' when he was offered the role of David. Schumacher said Sutherland "can do almost anything. He's a born character actor. You can see it in ''The Lost Boys''. He has the least amount of dialogue in the movie, but his presence is extraordinary."<ref name="retrospective"/>

===Principal photography===
Most of the film was shot in Santa Cruz, California, starting on June 2, 1986, and ending on June 23, 1986, after 21 days of filming. Locations include the [[Santa Cruz Boardwalk]], the [[Pogonip (park)|Pogonip open space preserve]], and the surrounding [[Santa Cruz Mountains]]. Other locations included a cliffside on the [[Palos Verdes Peninsula]] in [[Los Angeles County]], used for the entrance to the vampire cave, and a valley in [[Santa Clarita, California|Santa Clarita]] near [[Six Flags Magic Mountain|Magic Mountain]], where introductory shots were filmed for the scene where Michael and the Lost Boys hang from a railway bridge.<ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.fast-rewind.com/locations_lostboys.htm| title=The Lost Boys (1987) Movie Filming Locations| website=The 80s Movies Rewind| access-date=2018-02-17}}</ref> Stage sets included the vampire cave, built on Stage 12 of the Warner Bros. lot, and a recreation of the interior and exterior of the Pogonip clubhouse on Stage 15, which stood in for Grandpa's house.<ref name="retrospective"/>

Sutherland broke his right wrist while doing a [[wheelie]] on his motorcycle and had to wear gloves on set to conceal the cast. His motorcycle for the movie was adapted so he could operate it with his left hand only.<ref name="auto"/>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Lost Boys'' opened at number two during its opening weekend, with a domestic gross over $5.2 million. It went on to gross a domestic total over $32.2 million against an $8.5 million budget.<ref name=BMojo>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0093437/ |title=The Lost Boys (1987) |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=June 21, 2024}}</ref><ref name=TheNumbers>{{cite web|url=https://the-numbers.com/movie/Lost-Boys-The#tab=summary |title=The Lost Boys (1987) |website=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] |access-date=June 10, 2017}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 75% based on 80 reviews, with an average rating of 6.4/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "Flawed but eminently watchable, Joel Schumacher's teen vampire thriller blends horror, humor, and plenty of visual style with standout performances from a cast full of young 1980s stars."<ref>{{cite web |title=The Lost Boys (1987) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/lost_boys/ |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=March 20, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a weighted average score of 63 out of 100 based on 16 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Lost Boys |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-lost-boys |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=2020-05-04}}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= CinemaScore |publisher= [[CinemaScore]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film two-and-a-half out of four stars, praising the cinematography and "a cast that's good right down the line", but ultimately describing ''Lost Boys'' as a triumph of style over substance and "an ambitious entertainment that starts out well, but ends up selling its soul."<ref>{{cite news |date=July 31, 1987 |last=Ebert |first= Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert | title=The Lost Boys| url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-lost-boys-1987 | newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> [[Caryn James]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' called Dianne Wiest's character a "dopey mom" and [[Barnard Hughes]]'s character "a caricature of a feisty old Grandpa." She found the film more of a comedy than a horror and the finale "funny".<ref>{{cite news|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|title=Film: 'The Lost Boys'|last=James |first=Caryn|author-link=Caryn James|date=July 31, 1987|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1987/07/31/movies/film-the-lost-boys.html}}</ref>

[[Elaine Showalter]] commented that "the film brilliantly portrays vampirism as a metaphor for the kind of mythic male bonding that resists growing up, commitment, especially marriage."<ref>{{cite book |last=Showalter |first=Elaine |author-link=Elaine Showalter |url=https://archive.org/details/sexualanarchygen00show_0/page/183/mode/2up |title=Sexual Anarchy: Gender and Culture at the Fin de Siècle |location=New York |publisher=[[Viking Press|Viking]] |year=1990 |page=183 |isbn=978-0-670-82503-5 |url-access=registration}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' panned the film, calling it "a horrifically dreadful vampire teensploitation entry that daringly advances the theory that all those missing children pictured on garbage bags and milk cartons are actually the victims of bloodsucking bikers."<ref>{{cite magazine |year=1987 |title=The Lost Boys |url=https://variety.com/1986/film/reviews/the-lost-boys-1200427072/ |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>

The film won a [[Saturn Award for Best Horror Film]] at the [[15th Saturn Awards]] in 1987.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://theenvelope.latimes.com/extras/lostmind/year/1987/1987sat.htm |title=1987 15th Saturn Awards |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |access-date=June 21, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060524134120/http://theenvelope.latimes.com/extras/lostmind/year/1987/1987sat.htm |archive-date=May 24, 2006}}</ref>

The Frog brothers make a cameo in [[Jenny Colgan]]'s 2001 novel ''Looking for Andrew McCarthy'', in which they are now police officers and make a brief, ominous reference to their past work with "the supernatural".<ref>{{cite book|first=Jenny|last=Colgan|title=Looking for Andrew McCarthy|publisher=[[HarperCollins]]|year=2001|isbn=0007105533|pages=256–260}}</ref>

==Adaptations==
===Novelization===
Due to his past fantasy novels and horror short stories, [[Craig Shaw Gardner]] was given a copy of the script and asked to write a novelization to accompany the film's release. At the time, Gardner was, like the Frog brothers, managing a comic-book store, as well as writing.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://comicsalliance.com/batman-89-novelization-adaptation-craig-shaw-gardner-interview/|title=Novelist Craig Shaw Gardner On Adapting Batman '89 For Prose [Interview]|first=Patrick A.|last=Reed|website=[[ComicsAlliance]]|date=July 3, 2014}}</ref>

The novelization released in paperback by [[Berkley Books|Berkley Publishing]] is 220 pages long.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.fantasticfiction.com/g/craig-shaw-gardner/lost-boys.htm|title=The Lost Boys by Craig Shaw Gardner|website=Fantastic Fiction}}</ref> It includes several scenes later dropped from the film, such as Michael working as a trash collector for money to buy his leather jacket. It expands the roles of the opposing gang, the Surf Nazis, who were seen as nameless victims of the vampires in the film. It includes several tidbits of vampire lore, such as not being able to cross running water and salt sticking to their forms.

===Comic books===
David made a reappearance in the 2008 comic-book series ''[[Lost Boys: Reign of Frogs]]'', which serves as a sequel to the first film and a prequel to ''[[Lost Boys: The Tribe]]''.

In October 2016, [[Vertigo (DC Comics)|Vertigo]] released a comic-book miniseries, ''[[The Lost Boys (comic book)|The Lost Boys]]'', wherein Michael, Sam, and the Frog brothers must protect Star from her sisters, the Blood Belles.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://variety.com/2016/film/news/lost-boys-sequel-comic-book-vertigo-1201814669/|title='The Lost Boys' Sequel Comic in the Works from Vertigo (EXCLUSIVE)|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|last=Prudom|first=Laura|date=July 15, 2016}}</ref>

===Broadway musical===
A [[The Lost Boys (musical)|musical adaptation]], with music and lyrics by [[The Rescues]] and directed by [[Michael Arden]], opened in April 2026 at the [[Palace Theatre (New York City)|Palace Theatre]]. Reviews ranged from favorable<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rizzo |first=Frank |title=‘The Lost Boys’ Broadway Review: Musical Adaptation of ‘80s Teen Vampire Flick Is Rich in Imagination, Filled With Spectacular Effects |url=https://variety.com/2026/legit/reviews/lost-boys-broadway-review-musical-teen-vampire-movie-spectacular-effects-1236730779/}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Hempstead |first=Pete |title=Review: The Lost Boys Breaks the Curse of the Vampire Musical on Broadway |url=https://www.theatermania.com/news/review-the-lost-boys-breaks-the-curse-of-the-vampire-musical-on-broadway_1834562/}}</ref> to mixed<ref>{{Cite web |last=Shaw |first=Helen |title=‘The Lost Boys’ Review: Live, Die, Reprise |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/26/theater/the-lost-boys-review.html}}</ref>. The show received twelve [[Tony Awards|Tony Award]] nominations and won four - [[Shoshana Bean]] for Best Supporting Actress in a Musical, [[Ali Louis Bourzgui]] for Best Supporting Actor in a Musical, Best Scenic Design of a Musical, and Best Lighting Design of a Musical<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Lost Boys |url=https://www.tonyawards.com/shows/the-lost-boys/}}</ref>.

==Sequels==
Kiefer Sutherland's character, David, was impaled on antlers, but does not explode or dissolve as do the other vampires. He was intended to have survived, which would be picked up in a sequel, ''The Lost Girls''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Topel |first=Fred |url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/joel-schumacher-lost-in-lost-boys-sequel/ |title=Joel Schumacher Lost in "Lost Boys" Sequel |date=2007-02-04 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=2017-10-23}}</ref> Scripts for this and other sequels circulated over the years; Joel Schumacher made several attempts at a sequel during the 1990s, but nothing came to fruition.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://slashfilm.com/kiefer-sutherland-talks-lost-boys-prequel-tells-the-tribe-to-fo/ |title=Kiefer Sutherland Talks Lost Boys Prequel, Tells The Tribe to FO |last=Stephenson |first=Hunter |date=2008-08-13 |website=[[/Film]] |access-date=2017-10-23}}</ref><ref name="indiewire1"/>

A [[direct-to-video|direct-to-DVD]] sequel, ''[[Lost Boys: The Tribe]]'', was released in 2008. Corey Feldman returned as Edgar Frog, with a cameo by Corey Haim as Sam Emerson. Kiefer Sutherland's half-brother [[Angus Sutherland (actor)|Angus Sutherland]] played the lead vampire, Shane Powers.<ref>{{cite web |author=Caffeinated Clint |url=http://moviehole.net/200711700wanna-know-who-the-lost-boys-are |title=Wanna know who The Lost Boys are? |date=2007-08-29 |website=Moviehole |access-date=2017-10-23 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20171024043241/https://moviehole.net/200711700wanna-know-who-the-lost-boys-are |archive-date=2017-10-24}}</ref>

A third film, ''[[Lost Boys: The Thirst]]'', was released on DVD on October 12, 2010. Feldman served as an executive producer in addition to playing Edgar Frog, and Newlander returned as Alan Frog.<ref>{{cite web |last=Wigler |first=Josh |url=http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2009/03/18/lost-boys-threequel-on-the-way-corey-feldman-to-return/ |title='Lost Boys' Threequel On The Way, Corey Feldman To Return |website=[[MTV Movies Blog]] |date=March 18, 2009 |access-date=April 30, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090322055848/http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2009/03/18/lost-boys-threequel-on-the-way-corey-feldman-to-return/ |archive-date=2009-03-22}}</ref> Haim, who was not slated to be part of the cast, died in March 2010. A fourth film was discussed, as well as a Frog brothers television series,<ref name="movieweb">{{cite web |url=https://movieweb.com/the-lost-boys-4-is-dead-and-the-frog-brothers-tv-series-is-homeless/ |title=The Lost Boys 4 Is Dead and the Frog Brothers TV Series Is Homeless |last=Orange |first=B. Alan |date=2012-11-23 |website=[[MovieWeb]] |access-date=2017-10-23}}</ref> but with the dissolution of [[Warner Premiere]], the projects never materialized.<ref name="movieweb"/>

In September 2021, a new film was announced, to be directed by Jonathan Entwistle, from a script by Randy McKinnon, starring [[Noah Jupe]] and [[Jaeden Martell]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Kit |first=Borys | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/new-lost-boys-movie-noah-jupe-jaeden-martell-1235015779/ | title=New 'Lost Boys' Movie in the Works with Noah Jupe, Jaeden Martell to Star (Exclusive) | magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] | date=September 17, 2021}}</ref>

[[Thomas Newman]] wrote the [[film score|original score]] as an eerie blend of orchestra and organ arrangements.

The music soundtrack contains a number of notable songs and several covers, including "[[Good Times (Easybeats song)|Good Times]]", a duet between [[INXS]] and former [[Cold Chisel]] lead singer [[Jimmy Barnes]] which reached No. 2 on the Australian charts.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.drummercafe.com/education/premium-resources/transcriptions/jon-farris-inxs-jimmy-barnes-good-times.html|title=Jon Farris: INXS & Jimmy Barnes – &quot;Good Times&quot;|website=Drummer Cafe |last=Elliott |first=Bart |date=May 14, 2015 |access-date=November 1, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211101133447/https://www.drummercafe.com/education/premium-resources/transcriptions/jon-farris-inxs-jimmy-barnes-good-times.html |archive-date=November 1, 2021}}</ref> This cover version of a 1960s Australian song by [[the Easybeats]] was originally recorded to promote the [[Australian Made]] tour of Australia in early 1987, headlined by INXS and Barnes.

Tim Cappello's cover of [[The Call (band)|the Call]]'s "I Still Believe" was featured in the film and on the soundtrack. Cappello makes a cameo appearance in the film playing the song at the Santa Cruz boardwalk, with his saxophone and bodybuilder muscles on display. Cappello frequently appears at fan conventions to perform the song.

The soundtrack features a cover version of [[the Doors]]' song "[[People Are Strange]]" by [[Echo & the Bunnymen]]. The song as featured in the film is an alternate, shortened version with a slightly different music arrangement.

[[Lou Gramm]], lead singer of [[Foreigner (band)|Foreigner]], recorded "Lost in the Shadows" for the soundtrack, along with a video, which featured clips from the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://80smusicchannel.blogspot.com/2008/10/lou-gramm-lost-in-shadows-1987.html|title=Lou Gramm: Lost in the Shadows (1987)|first=Jack|last=Cabbage|date=October 27, 2008}}</ref>

The theme song, "[[Cry Little Sister]]", was originally recorded by Gerard McMahon (under his pseudonym Gerard McMann) for the soundtrack, and later re-released on his album ''[[G Tom Mac (album)|G Tom Mac]]'' in 2000. In the film's sequel ''Lost Boys: The Tribe'', "Cry Little Sister" was covered by a [[Seattle]]-based rock band, [[Aiden (band)|Aiden]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://80smusicchannel.blogspot.com/2008/10/gerard-mcmann-cry-little-sister-1987.html|title=Gerard McMann: Cry Little Sister (1987)|first=Jack|last=Cabbage|date=October 26, 2008}}</ref> and appeared again in the closing credits of ''Lost Boys: The Thirst''.

"The Lost Boys" Soundtrack album was certified Gold by the RIAA on 9/30/87.
