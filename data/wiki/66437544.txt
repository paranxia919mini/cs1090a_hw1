'''''X''''' is a 2022 American [[slasher film]] written, directed, produced and edited by [[Ti West]]. It stars [[Mia Goth]] in dual roles: a young woman named [[Maxine Minx|Maxine]] and an elderly woman named [[Pearl (X)|Pearl]]. The film also stars [[Jenna Ortega]], [[Martin Henderson]], [[Brittany Snow]], [[Owen Campbell (actor)|Owen Campbell]], Stephen Ure, and [[Kid Cudi|Scott Mescudi]]. Set in 1979, the film follows a cast and crew who gather to make a [[pornographic film]] on an elderly couple's rural Texas property.

[[A24]] announced ''X'' in November 2020. Principal photography occurred in February–March 2021, primarily in [[Fordell, New Zealand]]. Goth underwent extensive prosthetic makeup for Pearl, and special effects were employed to depict violent scenes in the film. Characterized as a contemporary take on [[psycho-biddy]], ''X'' draws inspiration from [[horror film|horror]], [[Exploitation film|exploitation]], and pornographic films, and emphasizes the interplay between beauty, aging, and self-worth. The score was composed by [[Tyler Bates]] and [[Chelsea Wolfe]].

''X'' had its world premiere at [[South by Southwest]] on March 13, 2022, and was theatrically released in the United States five days later by A24. It received generally positive reviews from critics. It is the first installment of the [[X (film series)|''X'' film series]], followed by the prequel film ''[[Pearl (2022 film)|Pearl]]'' (September 2022) and the sequel ''[[MaXXXine]]'' (July 2024).

== Plot ==
In 1979, police officers arrive on a lonely farm in rural Texas and find numerous dead bodies wrapped in bags. They then enter the farmhouse and find something shocking in the basement.

Twenty-four hours earlier, aspiring adult film star [[Maxine Minx|Maxine]] is on a trip to shoot a pornographic film with her producer fiancé Wayne, fellow [[burlesque]] dancer Bobby-Lynne and her Marine Corps veteran boyfriend Jackson, director RJ, and RJ's timid girlfriend Lorraine. The group arrives at a farm owned by World War veteran Howard and his wife [[Pearl (X)|Pearl]], an elderly couple who have a guesthouse they rented for the shoot. Howard expresses his disdain to the group, but Wayne excuses this due to not informing Howard of their intentions.

While Bobby-Lynne and Jackson act out a scene, Pearl discreetly observes Maxine nude swimming in a nearby lake, before inviting her into the farmhouse. Pearl expresses an unusual fixation on her, as Maxine looks similar to Pearl in her youth. Howard returns home and Pearl rushes Maxine out, urging her to keep it a secret. Maxine returns to the guesthouse and shoots a sex scene with Jackson in the barn, as Pearl secretly watches and imagines herself in Maxine's spot. Later, Pearl attempts to seduce Howard but he turns her down, citing heart problems. That night, Lorraine participates in the shoot, upsetting RJ. When everyone is asleep, an angry RJ attempts to drive away and strand the group at the farm, but finds Pearl in the driveway. After he rebuffs her advances, Pearl stabs him repeatedly in the neck, decapitating him.

Noticing RJ's absence, Lorraine and Wayne search for him. In the barn, Wayne steps on a large nail and is stabbed in the eyes by Pearl with a pitchfork. Meanwhile, Howard lures Lorraine into their house, claiming Pearl has gone missing. He asks Lorraine to retrieve a flashlight from the basement. When she goes down, Howard locks the door and she discovers a naked male corpse hanging from the ceiling. Howard goes to the guesthouse and asks Jackson to help find Pearl. When they split up to scout the lake's perimeter, Jackson finds a car submerged in the water. After failing to draw Jackson into the water with his flashlight, Howard confronts him and shoots him in the chest. Meanwhile, Pearl sneaks into the guesthouse, undresses, and climbs into bed with Maxine, caressing her body, who awakens in horror to find Pearl lying next to her, her scream waking Bobby-Lynne.

Back in the house, Lorraine breaks through the basement door with a hatchet but Howard breaks her fingers. Bobby-Lynne finds Pearl standing on the lake's edge and tries to help her. Pearl insults Bobby-Lynne and pushes her into the water where an alligator kills her. Pearl and Howard reunite and enter the guesthouse where they have sex. Maxine escapes to the van and arms herself with a pistol. She frees Lorraine, who runs out of the front door, only to be shot by Howard. As the couple moves her body inside the house, Lorraine's corpse twitches, startling Howard and causing him to have a fatal heart attack.

Maxine retrieves the keys to Howard's truck and attempts to shoot Pearl, but finds the pistol is empty. Pearl shoots at Maxine with Howard's gun, but she dodges it and the recoil knocks Pearl back onto the porch, breaking her hip. As Maxine gets into Howard's truck, Pearl berates and insults her. Maxine reverses the truck, crushing Pearl's head before driving away. It is revealed that Maxine is the daughter of a [[Televangelism|televangelist]] frequently heard throughout the film. The police discover RJ's camera, with the sheriff speculating that it contains footage of "one goddamn fucked-up horror picture."

==Themes and influences==
Nate Roscoe of ''[[Fangoria]]'' wrote in an essay on the film that ''X'' exemplifies a modern take on the [[psycho-biddy]] horror subgenre, in which aging or elderly women portray grotesque, violent characters. Roscoe also notes that the film's primary theme revolves around aging, youth, and longing over the past.<ref name=roscoe>{{cite web|work=[[Fangoria]]|last=Roscoe|first=Nate|url=https://www.fangoria.com/x-marks-the-thot-that-hag-over-there/|title=X Marks the THOT (That Hag Over There)|date=March 23, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220324012346/https://www.fangoria.com/x-marks-the-thot-that-hag-over-there/|archive-date=March 24, 2022}}</ref> "Snatching its inspo from the shadiest recesses of art and exploitation, it is the relationship between beauty, aging and self-worth that creeps most conspicuously through the architecture of ''X''."<ref name=roscoe/> He also notes that the film presents its antagonist—the murderous [[Pearl (X)|Pearl]]—in a manner that is sympathetic, writing that, at moments, "one can't help but feel crushingly sorry for this tragic figure."<ref name=roscoe/>

Critics noted the influence of several films on ''X'', with multiple commentators observing [[Homage (arts)|homage]]s to the 1974 film ''[[The Texas Chain Saw Massacre]]''.<ref name="Roeper 2022">{{cite web |last=Roeper |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Roeper |date=March 17, 2022 |title='X' marks a smart, inventive take on gory horror and old-school porn |url=https://chicago.suntimes.com/movies-and-tv/2022/3/17/22981888/x-review-movie-mia-goth-kid-cudi-horror-slasher |access-date=March 28, 2022 |website=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |archive-date=March 8, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065409/https://chicago.suntimes.com/movies-and-tv/2022/3/17/22981888/x-review-movie-mia-goth-kid-cudi-horror-slasher |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Gleiberman 2022">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2022/film/reviews/x-review-sxsw-1235203851/|title='X' Review: '70s Horror Meets '70s Porn in the Rare 'Chain Saw' Homage That Earns Its Fear|last=Gleiberman|first=Owen|author-link=Owen Gleiberman|date=March 14, 2022|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 8, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065352/https://variety.com/2022/film/reviews/x-review-sxsw-1235203851/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Samarov 2022">{{cite web|url=https://chicagoreader.com/film/x-film-2022/|title=X|last=Samarov|first=Dmitry|date=March 18, 2022|website=[[Chicago Reader]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 8, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065406/https://chicagoreader.com/film/x-film-2022/|url-status=live}}</ref> Other films cited by critics as having an influence on ''X'' include ''[[Psycho (1960 film)|Psycho]]'' (1960), ''[[Easy Rider]]'' (1969), ''[[Hardcore (1979 film)|Hardcore]]'' (1979), ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'' (1980), ''[[Alligator (film)|Alligator]]'' (1980),<ref name="Gleiberman 2022" /> and ''[[Boogie Nights]]'' (1997). [[Richard Roeper]] wrote that ''X'' also contains "echoes" of such pornographic films as ''[[Blue Movie]]'' (1969) and ''[[Debbie Does Dallas]]'' (1978).<ref name="Roeper 2022" />

===Special effects===
Goth donned extensive [[prosthetic makeup]] to portray the elderly Pearl.<ref name="Gingold 2022" /> Describing her experience, Goth stated, "It was a good 10 hours in the makeup chair, and then I'd go and do a 12-hour day on set, and the makeup artist, Sarah Rubano, who was incredible, would constantly be touching me up and making sure my contacts were all right and all those sorts of things."<ref name="Gingold 2022" />

The scene in which Pearl stabs RJ in the neck involved the use of a retractable prop knife, a prosthetic neck with a slit in it, and tubing to allow the passage of [[stage blood]] through the slit.<ref name="Navarro 2022">{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/interviews/3710670/x-hair-makeup-designer-sarah-rubano-breaks-down-two-of-the-gnarliest-kills/|title='X' Hair & Makeup Designer Sarah Rubano Breaks Down Two of the Gnarliest Kills|last=Navarro|first=Meagan|date=April 13, 2022|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=April 14, 2022|archive-date=March 8, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065349/https://bloody-disgusting.com/interviews/3710670/x-hair-makeup-designer-sarah-rubano-breaks-down-two-of-the-gnarliest-kills/|url-status=live}}</ref> The effect of RJ's subsequent decapitation was accomplished using a dummy head of RJ, with a stunt performer and a false floor; the stunt performer lay on his back, with his head and shoulders beneath the false floor and concealed by a prosthetic upper body.<ref name="Navarro 2022" /> The performer then twitched his body during the filming of the scene, which, when paired with the disembodied dummy head, creates the illusion of RJ's body continuing to twitch after death.<ref name="Gingold 2022" /> For the scene in which Pearl stabs Wayne in the eyes with a pitchfork, a dummy of Wayne's upper body and head was constructed by the Netherlands-based MimicFX Studio.<ref name="Gingold 2022" />

== Reception ==
===Box office===
''X'' grossed $11.8 million in the United States and Canada, and $3.3 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $15.1 million, against its $5.3 million budget.<ref name="NZFilmRebate">{{cite web |title=Approved Screen Production Rebate |url=https://www.nzfilm.co.nz/sites/default/files/2024-02/Approved%20Screen%20Production%20Rebates%20-%20International%20(1%20Jan%202010%20-%2031st%20March%202024)).pdf |website=New Zealand Film Commission |access-date=March 11, 2025 |archive-date=July 13, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240713185718/https://www.nzfilm.co.nz/sites/default/files/2024-02/Approved%20Screen%20Production%20Rebates%20-%20International%20%281%20Jan%202010%20-%2031st%20March%202024%29%29.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref>

This film was long rumored to have been made for $1 million,<ref>{{cite web |title=Pearl: Why the Secretly Filmed Prequel to Ti West's X is a Brilliant Idea |url=https://movieweb.com/pearl-prequel-to-ti-wests-x/ |website=MovieWeb |date=July 4, 2022 |access-date=July 4, 2022}}</ref> but as executive producer, Peter Phok, stated back in 2022,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Molloy |first1=Tim |title=No, Pearl Did Not Cost a Mere $1 Million |url=https://www.moviemaker.com/no-pearl-did-not-cost-a-mere-1-million/ |website=MovieMaker |date=October 5, 2022 |access-date=October 5, 2022}}</ref> that was not true. And when the New Zealand Film Commission released their Approved Screen Production Rebate numbers,<ref name="NZFilmRebate" /> it was clear that both X and its sequel, Pearl, were each made for more than the rumored $1 million.

In the United States and Canada, ''X'' was released alongside ''[[Jujutsu Kaisen 0 (film)|Jujutsu Kaisen 0]]'', ''[[The Outfit (2022 film)|The Outfit]]'', and ''[[Umma (2022 film)|Umma]]'', and was projected to gross $2–5 million in its opening weekend.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficepro.com/weekend-box-office-forecast-the-batman-eyes-third-1-frame-as-jujutsu-kaisen-0-the-movie-the-outfit-umma-and-x-debut/|title=Weekend Box Office Forecast: ''The Batman'' Eyes Third #1 Frame as ''Jujutsu Kaisen 0: The Movie'', ''The Outfit'', ''Umma'', and ''X'' Debut|date=March 16, 2022|first=Shawn|last=Robbins|website=[[Boxoffice Pro]]|access-date=March 17, 2022|archive-date=March 16, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220316181626/https://www.boxofficepro.com/weekend-box-office-forecast-the-batman-eyes-third-1-frame-as-jujutsu-kaisen-0-the-movie-the-outfit-umma-and-x-debut/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film earned $4.3 million from 2,865 theaters in its opening weekend, finishing fourth.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W11/|title=Domestic 2022 Weekend 11|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=April 5, 2022|archive-date=March 24, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220324005709/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W11/|url-status=live}}</ref> Men made up 55% of the audience during its opening, with those in the age range of 18–34 comprising 73% of ticket sales. The ethnic breakdown of the audience showed that 50% were Caucasian, 22% Hispanic and Latino Americans, 12% African American, and 16% Asian or other.<ref name="opening">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2022/03/box-office-jujutsu-kaisen-0-the-batman-x-a24-crunchyroll-1234981855/|title=''The Batman'' Hitting $300M+ Today, Crunchyroll Anime Pic ''Jujutsu Kaisen 0'' Solid With $17M+ – Sunday AM Update|date=March 20, 2022|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=March 20, 2022|archive-date=March 19, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220319212306/https://deadline.com/2022/03/box-office-jujutsu-kaisen-0-the-batman-x-a24-crunchyroll-1234981855/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film made $2.2 million in its second weekend and $1 million in its third.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W12/|title=Domestic 2022 Weekend 12|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=April 5, 2022|archive-date=May 31, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220531022951/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W12/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W13/|title=Domestic 2022 Weekend 13|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=April 5, 2022|archive-date=April 5, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220405074525/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W13/|url-status=live}}</ref> It dropped out of the box office top ten in its fourth weekend with $359,067 (a drop of 65%).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W14/|title=Domestic 2022 Weekend 14|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=April 12, 2022|archive-date=April 11, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220411132240/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2022W14/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 94% based on 229 reviews, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The website's critics consensus reads: "A fresh spin on the classic slasher formula, ''X'' marks the spot where Ti West gets resoundingly back to his horror roots."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/x_2022|title=X (2022)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=September 6, 2022|archive-date=March 20, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220320214901/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/x_2022|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], the film has a score of 80 out of 100 based on 35 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/x-2022|title=X (2022) Reviews|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[Red Ventures]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 20, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220320084603/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/x-2022|url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[PostTrak]] gave the film a 68% positive score, with 45% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="opening" />

Reviewing the film following its SXSW premiere, [[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it "a deliberate, loving, and meticulous homage" to 1974's ''The Texas Chain Saw Massacre'', as well as "a wily and entertaining slow-motion ride of terror that earns its shocks, along with its singular quease factor, which relates to the fact that the demons here are ancient specimens of humanity who actually have a touch of... humanity."<ref name="Gleiberman 2022" /> John DeFore of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' commended the film's cast and noted that, "Before the gore begins (and even mid-action), West seems to truly consider the pain of irretrievable youth, and feel for those whose final years are consumed by it."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/ti-wests-x-sxsw-2022-1235110997/|title=Ti West's 'X': Film Review {{!}} SXSW 2022|last=DeFore|first=John|date=March 14, 2022|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=February 21, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230221135832/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/ti-wests-x-sxsw-2022-1235110997/|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[The A.V. Club]]''{{'}}s Todd Gilchrist gave the film a grade of "B+", writing that it "examines the way that youth in others seems to bring out the feeling and impact of age in ourselves, not to mention how we resist or respond to that when it happens," and calling it "bloody, ballsy fun".<ref name="Gilchrist 2022">{{cite web |last=Gilchrist |first=Todd |date=March 14, 2022 |title=Ti West works more genre-manipulating magic with ''X'' |url=https://www.avclub.com/ti-west-works-more-genre-manipulating-magic-with-x-1848644775 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065349/https://www.avclub.com/ti-west-works-more-genre-manipulating-magic-with-x-1848644775 |archive-date=March 8, 2023 |access-date=March 28, 2022 |website=[[The A.V. Club]]}}</ref> Abby Olcese, writing for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', gave the film a score of three out of four stars, concluding: "''X'' is plenty of fun; it also feels like a trifle that could easily have been much more."<ref name="Olcese 2022">{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/x-movie-review-2022|title=X movie review & film summary (2022)|last=Olcese|first=Abby|date=March 14, 2022|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 8, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065422/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/x-movie-review-2022|url-status=live}}</ref>

Upon release, ''[[The Atlantic]]''{{'}}s David Sims called the film "a modern classic", comparing it with 2022's ''[[Texas Chainsaw Massacre (2022 film)|Texas Chainsaw Massacre]]'', which he felt failed creatively compared to ''X''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2022/03/x-movie-review/627111/|title=The Slasher Film 'X' Is a Modern Classic|last=Sims|first=David|date=March 19, 2022|website=[[The Atlantic]]|language=en|access-date=March 21, 2022|archive-date=February 21, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230221135835/https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2022/03/x-movie-review/627111/|url-status=live}}</ref> Richard Roeper of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' awarded the film three-and-a-half out of four stars, calling it "the kind of movie that has you reeling in disgust at certain moments, then laughing at the blood-spattered absurdity of it all. It's a new twist on the period-piece slasher movie, smart and strange and fantastically depraved."<ref name="Roeper 2022" /> [[A. O. Scott]], in a review of the film for ''[[The New York Times]]'', wrote that ''X'' "isn't shy about appealing to [[voyeurism]]. There's nothing coy or arty about the bloodletting. [...] West, unlike his pornographers, has things to say as well as bodies to show. Most of all, he has an aesthetic that isn't all about terror or titillation. ''X'' is full of dreamy, haunting overhead shots and moments of surprising tenderness."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/17/movies/x-review.html|title='X' Review: Trash, Art and the Movies|last=Scott|first=A. O.|author-link=A. O. Scott|date=March 17, 2022|website=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 8, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230308065431/https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/17/movies/x-review.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

Valerie Complex of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' referred to the film as "a new love letter to the slasher film genre", writing: "I give West credit for having a vision and sticking to his influences. He knows what he wants to do and how to execute it unapologetically. ''X'' is surface-level entertainment [...] but still a satisfying piece of indie horror filmmaking that's worth taking a chance on."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2022/03/ti-west-love-letter-x-movie-1234982179/|title='X' Film Review: Director Ti West Delivers A Love Letter To Slasher Cinema|last=Complex|first=Valerie|date=March 18, 2022|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=March 28, 2022|archive-date=March 28, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220328050303/https://deadline.com/2022/03/ti-west-love-letter-x-movie-1234982179/|url-status=live}}</ref> Dmitry Samarov of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' gave the film a mostly negative review, writing that "even the most casual horror fan won't miss" the references that ''X'' makes to 1974's ''Texas Chain Saw Massacre'', but that, "unlike [[Tobe Hooper]]'s masterpiece, which has a point to make about economic desperation and cultural clash in 70s America, West just wants to punish everyone involved in gory ways played for laughs."<ref name="Samarov 2022" />

==Prequel and sequel==

In March 2022, it was revealed that a prequel film, ''[[Pearl (2022 film)|Pearl]]'', was secretly shot back-to-back with the first film. West directed and co-wrote the film with Goth.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.statesman.com/story/entertainment/arts/2022/09/15/pearl-movie-review-mia-goth-x-prequel-ti-west-austin-tx/68154884007/|work=[[Austin American-Statesman]]|date=September 15, 2022|title=The star of Texas slasher 'Pearl' doesn't shoot; she stabs|last=Webb|first=Eric|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220916182935/https://www.statesman.com/story/entertainment/arts/2022/09/15/pearl-movie-review-mia-goth-x-prequel-ti-west-austin-tx/68154884007/|archive-date=September 16, 2022}}</ref> Principal photography took place in New Zealand, and upon official announcement was already in the post-production stage. Goth reprises her role as a younger-aged [[Pearl (X)|Pearl]]. A24 produced the project, with Jacob Jaffke, Harrison Kreiss and Kevin Turen serving as producers, and West, Goth, Mescudi and [[Sam Levinson]] as executive producers.<ref name="Future_Deadline">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2022/03/x-prequel-a24-ti-west-sxsw-premiere-1234977817/|work=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|title=A24's ''X'' Has Shot A Prequel, Ti West Reveals At Pic's SXSW Premiere|author=Fleming, Mike Jr.|date=March 14, 2022|access-date=March 15, 2022|archive-date=April 6, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220406012523/https://deadline.com/2022/03/x-prequel-a24-ti-west-sxsw-premiere-1234977817/|url-status=live}}</ref> A sneak preview was also shown in ''X'''s [[post-credits scene]] for North American releases only.{{citation needed|date=March 2022}} ''Pearl'' was released theatrically in North America on September 16, 2022, approximately six months after the release of ''X''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://gamerant.com/pearl-everything-we-know-ti-west-prequel-x/|work=[[Screen Rant|Game Rant]]|title=Pearl: Everything We Know About Ti West's Prequel To X|last=Turner|first=Danni|date=August 16, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220818013014/https://gamerant.com/pearl-everything-we-know-ti-west-prequel-x/|archive-date=August 18, 2022}}</ref>

A third installment in the series, ''[[MaXXXine]]'', was announced shortly before the release of ''Pearl''. It was released on July 5, 2024, and focuses on Maxine in 1980s [[Los Angeles]], following the events depicted in ''X''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/movie/3731491/maxxxine-a24-announces-third-movie-in-ti-wests-x-franchise-teaser/|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|title='MaXXXine' – A24 Announces Third Movie in Ti West's 'X' Franchise!|last=Squires|first=John|date=September 13, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220914080256/https://bloody-disgusting.com/movie/3731491/maxxxine-a24-announces-third-movie-in-ti-wests-x-franchise-teaser/|archive-date=September 14, 2022}}</ref>
