'''''House on Haunted Hill''''' is a 1958{{efn-lr|Though sometimes classified as a 1959 film, ''House on Haunted Hill'' was released theatrically in late 1958.<ref name=Team/>}} American [[horror film]] produced and directed by [[William Castle]], written by [[Robb White]] and starring [[Vincent Price]], [[Carol Ohmart]], [[Richard Long (actor)|Richard Long]], [[Alan Marshal (actor)|Alan Marshal]], [[Carolyn Craig]], [[Julie Mitchum]], and [[Elisha Cook Jr.]] Price plays an eccentric millionaire, Frederick Loren, who, along with his wife Annabelle, has invited five people to the house for a "haunted house" party. Whoever stays in the house for one night will earn $10,000. As the night progresses, the guests are trapped within the house with an assortment of terrors. 

''House on Haunted Hill'' was first released in a limited number of cities on December 17, 1958,<ref name=Team>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-team-march-beacon/197637014/|work=The Team March Beacon|date=December 12, 1958|title=Theater Two|page=12|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref><ref name=Wings/> before premiering in San Francisco on January 14, 1959.<ref name="auto"/><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-san-francisco-examiner/60551633/|title=''House on Haunted Hill''|work=[[San Francisco Examiner]]|page=17|date=January 14, 1959|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> In February, 1959, it played on a double bill with ''[[The Cosmic Man]]'' (1959).{{sfn|Warren|1986|page=736}} This film is famous for employing one of Castle's [[Movie gimmick|promotional gimmick]]s, "Emergo,"<ref name=":0" /> a skeleton that was flown over the audience during screenings to frighten patrons.
 
In the years since its release, the film has developed a [[Cult film|cult following]]{{sfn|Mathijs|Sexton|2011|pages=243–252}}{{sfn|Simpson|2010|page=39}} and was [[House on Haunted Hill (1999 film)|remade]] in 1999.

==Plot==
Millionaire Frederick Loren invites five people to a party he is throwing for his fourth wife, Annabelle, in an allegedly [[haunted house]] he has rented. He promises to give each guest $10,000 with the stipulation that they stay the entire night in the house after the doors are locked at midnight. All the windows are barred, there is no electricity and no phones or radios to use. The guests are test pilot Lance Schroeder; newspaper columnist Ruth Bridges; psychiatrist Dr. David Trent; Nora Manning, who works for one of Loren's companies; and the house's owner, Watson Pritchard. All are strangers to both the Lorens and each other.

The Lorens have a tense relationship. Frederick is convinced that Annabelle tried to poison him to acquire his wealth, which Annabelle denies, attributing his suspicions to paranoia and jealousy. Watson believes that the house is genuinely haunted by the [[ghost]]s of those murdered there, including his own brother; he claims to have spent one night there before and "was almost dead" when found the next morning. He gives a tour of the house, including a vat of acid in the basement, which a previous resident used to kill his wife. When Lance and Nora remain behind to further explore the basement, Lance is locked in an empty room and struck on the head, while an apparent ghost confronts Nora.

Annabelle privately warns Lance that her husband is scheming something and that she suspects him of murdering his second and third wives after his first wife disappeared. The guests learn the party's rules downstairs, and each is given a gun for protection. Having encountered further apparitions, Nora decides against staying the night, but the caretakers lock the doors five minutes early, taking that option out of the guests' hands.

Hearing a scream, Lance and David find Annabelle's corpse, suspended to suggest that she had [[Suicide by hanging|hanged herself]]. However, the absence of a perch immediately arouses suspicions of murder. Nora confronts Lance and tells him that an unseen assailant strangled her and left her for dead. In light of Annabelle's warnings, they both suspect Frederick. He tells her to remain out of sight so that her attacker will still think that she is dead. Lance and David propose that everyone stay in their rooms and shoot anyone who enters to survive the night. Thus the innocents will have no reason to leave their rooms and the killer must stay put or admit guilt.

Nora is chased from her room into the basement by Annabelle's ghost. Aroused by the ghostly sounds, David concludes that the killer is about and proposes that he and Frederick split up to search the house. Lance uncovers a secret room at the end of the second-floor hall, but the door shuts behind him once he enters, trapping him. David instead meets with Annabelle, who faked her death using a hanging harness and sedatives. Secretly lovers, the two of them have orchestrated the various mishaps to manipulate Nora into killing Frederick. Nora, seeing Frederick enter the basement with a gun in his hand, shoots him. After she flees, David slips in to dispose of Frederick's body in the vat of acid, and the lights go out.

Annabelle walks to the basement to confirm that her husband is dead. A skeleton rises from the acid, accuses her in Frederick's voice, and shoves her into the vat. Frederick emerges from the shadows, holding the puppeteer control unit that he used to manipulate the skeleton and revealing that he had known their plot all along.

After Nora, Watson, and Ruth release Lance from the secret room, Nora admits that she shot Frederick. When they arrive in the cellar, Frederick explains that he loaded her gun with blanks, that his wife and David plotted to kill him, and that they both died in the vat of acid. He says that he is ready for justice to decide if he is innocent or guilty. Watson remains convinced that the house is haunted, with David and Annabelle now added to its ranks of ghosts, and that he will be the next victim.

==Production==
===Casting===
In July 1958, Vincent Price was in talks with director [[William Castle]] to star in the film,<ref>{{cite news|work=[[Houston Chronicle]]|date=July 8, 1958|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-houston-chronicle/197670444/|title=Work on 'The Glenn McCarthy Story' Begun By Three Texans|last=Parsons|first=Louella|author-link=Louella Parsons|page=21|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> and his casting was officially announced in August 1958.<ref name=Hopper>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/san-francisco-chronicle/197669903/|work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|date=August 9, 1958|last=Hopper|first=Hedda|author-link=Hedda Hopper|title=Hedda Hopper in Hollywood|page=14|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Carol Ohmart's casting was announced the following month.<ref name=Bacon>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/courier-post/197670184/|work=[[Courier-Post]]|author-link=James Bacon (author)|date=September 10, 1958|last=Bacon|first=James|title=Carol Ohmart Returning to Screen Roles|page=35|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-san-francisco-examiner/117279943/|work=[[San Francisco Examiner]]|date=October 19, 1958|last=Parsons|first=Louella|author-link=Louella Parsons|title=Carol Ohmart to Play Lead In 'House on Haunted Hill'|page=10|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> The film marked Ohmart's first major screen role in several years, following her publicized divorce from actor [[Wayde Preston]].<ref name=Bacon/>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] of ''House on Haunted Hill'' took place in the late summer of 1958.{{sfn|Senn|2019|page=139}} Exterior shots of the house were filmed at the historic [[Ennis House]] in [[Los Feliz, California]], designed by [[Frank Lloyd Wright]] and built in 1924.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.architectmagazine.com/design/frank-lloyd-wright-homes-with-haunted-histories_o |title=Frank Lloyd Wright Homes with Haunted Histories |last=Blahut |first=Chelsea |date=2014-10-31 |website=[[Architect (magazine)|Architect]] |access-date=2017-10-07 |archive-date=15 November 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191115075113/https://www.architectmagazine.com/design/frank-lloyd-wright-homes-with-haunted-histories_o |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.austinchronicle.com/daily/screens/2009-07-30/now-you-too-can-own-the-house-on-haunted-hill/ |title=Now You, Too, Can Own the House on Haunted Hill! |last=Savlov |first=Marc |date=July 30, 2009 |website=[[The Austin Chronicle]] |archive-date=  November 15, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191115075049/https://www.austinchronicle.com/daily/screens/2009-07-30/now-you-too-can-own-the-house-on-haunted-hill/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The bulk of the film was shot on sound stages, depicting the interior of the house in a combination of styles, including 1890s [[Victorian architecture|Victorian]], with gas chandeliers and sconces.{{sfn|Eisner|2013|page=79}} The poster for the film included an illustration of a house in yet a third style, that of a fanciful four-story [[Romanesque architecture|Romanesque structure]]. Several art pieces owned by Vincent Price were used as set decoration in the film.<ref name=Hopper/>

The [[film trailer|theatrical trailer]] promoted the film as ''The House on Haunted Hill'', although all advertising material and the title on the film itself were simply titled ''House on Haunted Hill''.

==Release==
[[File:Baseline Drive-in Ad - 13 May 1959, Highland, CA.jpg|thumb|upright|[[Drive-in theater|Drive-in]] advertisement from 1959 for ''House on Haunted Hill'' and co-feature, ''[[The Cosmic Man]]''.]]
''House on Haunted Hill'' was originally released theatrically by [[Monogram Pictures|Allied Artists]]. It had a limited release in [[Riverside, California]]<ref name=Team/> and [[Hoopeston, Illinois]] on December 17, 1958.<ref name=Wings>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-wings/197637927/|work=The Wings|date=December 12, 1958|title=Movie Guide...|page=9|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> It opened in San Francisco at the [[Golden Gate Theatre]] on January 14, 1959.<ref name="auto">{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-san-francisco-examiner/60551633/|title=World Premiere Starts Today!|work=[[San Francisco Examiner]]|page=17|date=January 14, 1959}}</ref>

==="Emergo" gimmick===
''House on Haunted Hill'' is known for a promotional gimmick used in the film's original theatrical release called "Emergo".{{sfn|Law|2000|pages=66–67}} In some theaters that showed the film, exhibitors rigged an elaborate pulley system near the theater screen which allowed a plastic prop skeleton to be flown over the audience during a corresponding scene late in the film.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://allmovie.com/movie/v23381 |title=House on Haunted Hill (1958) |author=Erickson, Hal |work=[[AllMovie]]|archive-date=November 15, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191115075049/https://www.allmovie.com/movie/v23381 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/battle-creek-enquirer/155459239/|date=February 1, 1959|title='Emergo' Emerges Today|work=[[Battle Creek Enquirer]]|page=6|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

Thanks in part to Castle's gimmickry, the film was a huge success. [[Alfred Hitchcock]] took notice of the low-budget film's performance at the box office and made his own low-budget horror film, which became the critically acclaimed hit ''[[Psycho (1960 film)|Psycho]]'' (1960).<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20101005006944/en/Stars-Mystery-Science-Theater-3000-Give-Vincent#.VgR84JcTA8A | title=The Stars of Mystery Science Theater 3000 Give the Vincent Price Classic House on Haunted Hill the RiffTrax Treatment on the Big Screen This Halloween | work=[[Business Wire]] | date=October 5, 2010 | archive-date=September 24, 2015 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924045809/http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20101005006944/en/Stars-Mystery-Science-Theater-3000-Give-Vincent#.VgR84JcTA8A | url-status=dead }}</ref> Castle was himself a Hitchcock fan and imitated Hitchcock's work in later films such as ''[[Homicidal]]'' (1961).{{sfn|Law|2000|pages=66–67}}

==Reception==
===Box office===
''House on Haunted Hill'' was a financial success,{{sfn|Senn|2019|page=139}} having grossed $3 million in the United States by July 1959.<ref name=HopperLAT/>

===Critical response===
Numerous critics praised ''House on Haunted Hill'' for its classical style, likening it to old-fashioned haunted house films.{{efn-lr|Attritbuted to multiple critical reviews.<ref name=Knickerbocker/><ref name=Oregonian/><ref name=Walrath/>}} Paine Knickerbocker of the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' wrote that the film's "Emergo" gimmick was "not an important element of the picture," adding: "The film is far better than one might suspect from its title, and yet it actually pretends to be no more: it is merely an old-fashioned mystery suspense film and a pretty good one."<ref name=Knickerbocker>{{cite news|work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|date=January 14, 1959|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/san-francisco-chronicle/197659930/|last=Knickerbocker|first=Paine|title='Haunted Hill' at Golden Gate|page=39|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> ''[[The Oregonian]]'' similarly compared the film favorably to classic horror films such as ''[[The Bat (1926 film)|The Bat]]'' (1926) and ''[[The Cat and the Canary (1927 film)|The Cat and the Canary]]'' (1927), heralding it as a "ghost story of the highest excellence" and "pure entertainment from the first reel forward."<ref name=Oregonian>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-oregonian/197704169/|work=[[The Oregonian]]|date=February 27, 1959|title=Orpheum Has Ghost Story|page=17|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Jean Walrath of the ''[[Democrat and Chronicle]]'' similarly praised the film's "classic approach" and commended the performances by Price, Carol Ohmart, and Alan Marshal.<ref name=Walrath>{{cite news|last=Walrath|first=Jean|date=March 12, 1959|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/democrat-and-chronicle/197704286/|work=[[Democrat and Chronicle]]|page=40|title=Horror Fills 'House on Haunted Hill'|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

Kate Cameron of the ''[[New York Daily News]]'' awarded the film two and a half out of four stars, praising Price's performance.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/daily-news/197704231/|work=[[New York Daily News]]|date=February 19, 1959|title=Film With a Gimmick at Albee|via=Newspapers.com|last=Cameron|first=Kate|page=71}}</ref> [[Howard Thompson (film critic)|Howard Thompson]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' alternately panned the film, deeming it "a stale spook concoction."<ref>{{cite news|title=Screen: New Double Bill; ' Legion of Doomed' and 'Haunted' House Here|work=[[The New York Times]]|last=Thompson|first=Howard|author-link=Howard Thompson (film critic)|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1959/03/12/archives/screen-new-double-bill-legion-of-doomed-and-haunted-house-here.html|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120172942/https://www.nytimes.com/1959/03/12/archives/screen-new-double-bill-legion-of-doomed-and-haunted-house-here.html|archive-date=November 20, 2023|date=March 12, 1959}}</ref>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|80|7.2|44|consensus=Campy by modern standards but spooky and atmospheric, ''House on Haunted Hill'' is a fun, well-executed cult classic featuring a memorable performance from genre icon Vincent Price.|access-date=May 16, 2026|ref=y}} [[AllMovie]] has praised the film retrospectively, writing, "Campy and creepy in equal measures, ''House on Haunted Hill'' deserves its status as a horror classic."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://allmovie.com/movie/house-on-haunted-hill-v23381/review |title=House on Haunted Hill (1958) |author=Beldin, Fred |work=[[AllMovie]] |archive-date=10 May 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120510171510/http://www.allmovie.com/movie/house-on-haunted-hill-v23381/review |url-status=dead }}</ref>

On September 28, 2011, the estate of William Castle released an annotated screenplay from ''House on Haunted Hill'' which is a copy of the shooting script along with Castle's "margin notes" and the leather-bound style Castle used for his shooting script. This edition includes introductions from [[Joe Dante]] and Castle's daughter Terry. It also features its own version of "Emergo" in which the skeleton appears to readers via a "flip page" method.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fangoria.com/new/event-report-house-on-haunted-hill-1959-at-alamo-drafthouse-yonkers/ |title=Event Report: "HOUSE ON HAUNTED HILL" (1959) at Alamo Drafthouse Yonkers! |work=[[Fangoria]] |archive-date= April 2, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402092941/http://www.fangoria.com/new/event-report-house-on-haunted-hill-1959-at-alamo-drafthouse-yonkers/ |url-status=dead|date=March 30, 2015|last=Hanley|first=Ken W.}}</ref> [[Mondo Media]] re-released the film 2015 as part of his Mondo X Chiller series on 28 March 2015 in the [[Alamo Drafthouse Cinema|Alamo Drafthouse]] in [[Yonkers, New York]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.fangoria.com/new/mondo-premieres-house-on-haunted-hill-poster-fango-staffer-to-host-ny-drafthouse-screening/ |title=Mondo Premieres "HOUSE ON HAUNTED HILL" Poster; FANGO Staffer to host NY Drafthouse Screening! |work=[[Fangoria]]|archive-date=March 10, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150310022451/http://www.fangoria.com/new/mondo-premieres-house-on-haunted-hill-poster-fango-staffer-to-host-ny-drafthouse-screening/ |url-status=dead|last=Hanley|first=Ken W.|date=March 5, 2015 }}</ref>

In 2024, ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' ranked ''House on Haunted Hill'' at number 15 in a list of the 22 greatest haunted house film of all time.<ref name=EW>{{cite web|url=https://ew.com/movies/best-haunted-house-films-of-all-time/|title=The 22 best haunted house films of all time, ranked|last1=Thrash|first1=Steven|last2=Jacobsen|first2=Kevin|date=October 9, 2024|url-status=live|work=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251202080314/https://ew.com/movies/best-haunted-house-films-of-all-time/|archive-date=December 2, 2025}}</ref>
