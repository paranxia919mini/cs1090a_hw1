'''''The Others''''' ({{langx|es|'''Los otros'''|links=no}}) is a 2001 [[psychological horror|psychological]] and [[Gothic film|Gothic]]<ref name=":0" /> [[psychological horror|horror]] film written, directed and scored by [[Alejandro Amenábar]], starring [[Nicole Kidman]], [[Fionnula Flanagan]], [[Christopher Eccleston]], [[Elaine Cassidy]], [[Eric Sykes]], [[Alakina Mann]] and [[James Bentley (actor)|James Bentley]]. Set in 1945 in [[Jersey]], it focuses on a woman and her two [[Photosensitivity in humans|photosensitive]] children who experience supernatural phenomena in their manor after the arrival of new servants.

''The Others'' was theatrically released in the United States on August 10, 2001, by [[Dimension Films]], and in Spain on September 7, 2001, by [[Warner Sogefilms]]. It was a major critical and commercial success, grossing $210 million worldwide on a $17 million budget. Critics praised the atmosphere, performances (particularly Kidman), and [[plot twist]].

At the [[16th Goya Awards]], ''The Others'' earned a leading fifteen nominations and won in eight categories, including [[Goya Award for Best Film|Best Film]], [[Goya Award for Best Director|Best Director]], and [[Goya Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]]. It was the first English-language film without Spanish dialogue to win Best Film at Spain's [[Goya Awards]]. It received six nominations at the [[28th Saturn Awards]], winning [[Saturn Award for Best Horror Film|Best Horror Film]], [[Saturn Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] (for Kidman), and [[Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] (for Flanagan). It was nominated for [[BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role|Best Actress in a Leading Role]] (for Kidman) and [[BAFTA Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]] at the [[55th British Academy Film Awards]], and for [[European Film Award for Best Film|Best Film]] at the [[14th European Film Awards]]. Kidman was nominated for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama|Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama]] at the [[59th Golden Globe Awards]].

==Plot==
In 1945, Grace Stewart resides in a remote country house in [[Jersey]], a Channel Island formerly [[German occupation of the Channel Islands|occupied by the Germans]]. As her young children, Anne and Nicholas, suffer from a severe [[sensitivity to light]], Grace keeps the home darkened with heavy curtains. One day, Mrs. Bertha Mills, Edmund Tuttle and the [[Muteness|mute]] Lydia arrive seeking employment. Grace hires them as the housekeeper, gardener, and maid, and learns they worked in the house years earlier.

Anne claims to be visited by a young boy named Victor, his parents, and an elderly blind woman. Grace believes this is a fantasy, but after she hears footsteps and voices, she orders the house to be searched for intruders. In a storage room, she finds a nineteenth-century album containing [[photographs of corpses]]. Mrs. Mills recounts that many left the house in 1891 due to an outbreak of [[tuberculosis]]. Grace begins to fear there are supernatural entities in the house, but struggles to reconcile this with her Catholic faith.

Grace witnesses a piano playing itself and becomes convinced that the house is haunted. She runs outside in search of the local priest to bless the house and instructs Tuttle to check the gardens to see if a family has been buried there. Mrs. Mills instructs Tuttle to conceal gravestones with leaves. In the woods, Grace runs into her husband, Charles, whom she thought was killed in [[World War II]], and brings him back to the house.

One day, Grace checks on Anne playing. To her horror, she finds an old woman wearing Anne's veiled [[First Communion|communion dress]] who speaks in Anne's voice. Grace attacks the woman but finds she has actually attacked Anne. Charles tells Grace he must return to the [[Western Front (World War II)|front]], rejecting her insistence that the war is over. He leaves the next morning.

Grace is horrified to find all of the curtains in the house have been removed, exposing Anne and Nicholas to sunlight. She accuses the servants and expels them. That night, the children discover that the headstones in the cemetery belong to the 
servants, and flee when the servants approach them. Grace finds a postmortem photograph of Mrs. Mills, Tuttle and Lydia, who all perished during the 1891 tuberculosis outbreak. Mrs. Mills tells Grace to talk to the "intruders".

Grace discovers that the elderly blind woman is a [[Mediumship|medium]] holding a [[séance]] with Victor's parents. They have discovered via [[automatic writing]] that Grace, despondent after Charles died in the war, smothered her children with a pillow and shot herself. Aghast, Grace realizes that the intruders are the living family, and that she, her children and the servants are haunting the house.

Embracing her children, Grace admits to her act of [[murder–suicide]]: she awoke after her suicide and believed that God had brought everyone back to life for a second chance. Victor and his family move out. Anne and Nicholas realize that sunlight no longer hurts them and enjoy it for the first time. The house goes up for sale and Mrs. Mills informs the Stewarts that they will have to learn to cohabit with future inhabitants. Grace and the children affirm that the house is theirs and that they will not leave.

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Others'' was released in the United States and Canada by [[Dimension Films]], opening on August 10, 2001 in 1,678 theaters. It grossed $14 million its opening weekend, ranking fourth at the U.S. box office behind ''[[American Pie 2]]'', ''[[Rush Hour 2]]'' and ''[[The Princess Diaries (film)|The Princess Diaries]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Karger|first=Dave|title=''American Pie 2'' comes out on top|url=https://ew.com/article/2001/08/15/american-pie-2-comes-out-top/|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230813184033/https://ew.com/article/2001/08/15/american-pie-2-comes-out-top/|archive-date=August 13, 2023|url-status=live|date=August 15, 2001}}</ref> It stayed in fourth place for three more weeks, expanding to more theaters. During the weekend of September 21 to 23, it was second at the box office, grossing $5 million in 2,801 theaters.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=others.htm |title=The Others (2001) - Weekend Box Office |access-date=October 26, 2007|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> The film, which cost $17 million to produce, grossed $96.5 million in the United States and Canada. It grossed $24 million in Spain, becoming the [[List of highest-grossing films in Spain|highest-grossing Spanish film of all time]], beating the record set earlier that year by ''[[Torrente 2: Mission in Marbella]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=October 22, 2001|title=Romance, laffs boos o'seas B.O.|last=Groves|first=Don|page=12}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|page=7|date= December 24, 2001|title=Homegrown pix gain in Europe|last=Hopewell|first=John}}</ref> It grossed $113.4 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $210 million.<ref name="boxofficemojo"/>

===Critical response===
[[File:Nicole Kidman 10 (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=0.7|[[Nicole Kidman]]'s performance received acclaim.]]
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|84||174|consensus=''The Others'' is a spooky thriller that reminds us that a movie doesn't need expensive special effects to be creepy.|ref=yes|access-date=October 8, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240207003300/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1109257-others|archive-date=February 7, 2024|url-status=live}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 74 out of 100, based on 29 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Others'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-others |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=February 12, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231103225214/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-others/ |archive-date=November 3, 2023 |url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Cinemascore |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date=2018-12-20}}</ref>

[[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote: "''The Others'' is a flawed if interesting vehicle. The anxious indeterminacy of the first section proves hard to sustain, and as Mr. Amenábar moves away from elegant minimalism, the story begins to become cluttered and confusing, rather than spare and enigmatic." Scott highlighted Kidman's performance, writing that she "embodies this unstable amalgam with a conviction that is in itself terrifying. The icy reserve that sometimes stands in the way of her expressive gifts here becomes the foundation of her most emotionally layered performance to date."<ref>{{cite news |last=Scott |first=A. O. |author-link=A. O. Scott |date=August 10, 2001 |title=FILM REVIEW; Now, Which of You Are Dead? |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/08/10/movies/film-review-now-which-of-you-are-dead.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220709184737/http://www.nytimes.com/2001/08/10/movies/film-review-now-which-of-you-are-dead.html |archive-date=July 9, 2022 |access-date=February 12, 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave ''The Others'' two and a half out of four, writing: "Alejandro Amenábar has the patience to create a languorous, dreamy atmosphere, and Nicole Kidman succeeds in convincing us that she is a normal person in a disturbing situation and not just a standard-issue horror movie hysteric." However, he felt that "in drawing out his effects, Amenábar is a little too confident that style can substitute for substance".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-others-2001 |title=The Others (2001) |date=August 10, 2001 |last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/wip/FX2Dn|archive-date=August 13, 2023|via=RogerEbert.com| work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]}}</ref>

Neil Smith of the [[BBC]] awarded ''The Others'' four out of five, writing: "Shot in oppressive [[Sepia (color)|sepia]] amid near-darkness (Grace's children having a rare ailment that precludes exposure to sunlight), Amenábar racks up the tension to unbearable levels."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[BBC]]|title=The Others – Movie Review|last=Smith|first=Neil|date=October 29, 2001|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/10/16/the_others_2001_review.shtml|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230814012022/https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/10/16/the_others_2001_review.shtml|archive-date=August 14, 2023}}</ref> ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' praised it as "confident and controlled...&nbsp;Absence makes the heart beat faster: the absence of light, the corporeal absence of loved ones. Shrewdly cast, Kidman is pitch perfect. It's a clammy, ingenious film, one of the best studio movies of the year."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|title=The Others (2001), directed by Alejandro Amenábar|url=https://www.timeout.com/movies/the-others|date=February 18, 2014|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201110152457/https://www.timeout.com/movies/the-others|archive-date=November 10, 2020}}</ref>

[[Kenneth Turan]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' cited Kidman's performance as the greatest strength, writing that she "has thrown herself into her role as if it were [[Lady Macbeth]] on the London stage, with formidable results. Though Kidman doesn't hesitate to make Grace high-strung and as tightly wound as they come, she also projects vulnerability and courage when they're called for. It's an intense, involving performance, and it dominates and energizes a film that would be lost without it."<ref>{{cite news |last=Turan |first=Kenneth |author-link=Kenneth Turan |date=August 10, 2001 |title=Kidman Proves Haunting |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-aug-10-ca-32555-story.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20230814012527/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-aug-10-ca-32555-story.html |archive-date=August 14, 2023 |work=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref> In 2025, ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' named ''The Others'' the 14th-greatest horror film of the 21st century.<ref>{{cite web |date=July 23, 2025 |title=25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century, Ranked|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/25-best-horror-movies-of-the-21st-century-ranked/sinners-2025/ |access-date=2025-08-02}}</ref>

==Planned remake==
In April 2020, Sentient Entertainment acquired the remake rights to ''The Others'', with the company planning to revamp the film by setting it in the present day.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Wiseman |first1=Andreas |title=Sentient Wins Remake Rights To Nicole Kidman Horror 'The Others', Alejandro Amenabar's Timely Self-Isolation Chiller Which Made $200M+ |url=https://deadline.com/2020/04/sentient-wins-remake-rights-to-nicole-kidman-horror-the-others-alejandro-amenabars-timely-self-isolation-chiller-which-made-200m-1202878443/ |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=April 8, 2020 |access-date=  May 9, 2020}}</ref> Later that year, it was announced that [[Universal Pictures]] will co-produce and distribute the film with Sentient.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Kroll |first1=Justin |title='The Others' Remake In The Works As Universal Pictures & Sentient Entertainment Partner On New Movie |url=https://deadline.com/2020/10/the-others-remake-universal-sentient-1234595985/|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=October 12, 2020 |access-date=  January 12, 2021}}</ref>
