'''''Wild Things''''' is a 1998 American [[erotic thriller]] film directed by [[John McNaughton]] and starring [[Matt Dillon]], [[Kevin Bacon]], [[Neve Campbell]], [[Denise Richards]], [[Theresa Russell]], [[Robert Wagner]], and [[Bill Murray]]. It follows a high school guidance counselor in [[South Florida]] who is accused of rape by two female students and a series of subsequent revelations after a police officer begins investigating the alleged crimes. ''Wild Things'' was released by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]] on March 20, 1998 and received mixed to positive reviews from critics while grossing $67.2 million against a $20 million budget. The film was followed by two television film sequels ''[[Wild Things 2]]'' (2004) and ''[[Wild Things: Diamonds in the Rough]]'' (2005), and a [[Direct-to-video|direct-to-DVD]] standalone sequel ''[[Wild Things: Foursome]]'' (2010). Since its release, the original film has been regarded as a [[cult classic]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://facts.net/movie/41-facts-about-the-movie-wild-things/ |title=41 Facts About The Movie Wild Things |last=Persaud |first=Ellen |date=December 19, 2023 |publisher=Facts.net |quote=Wild Things is a gripping thriller that has captivated audiences since its release in 1998 }}</ref>

==Plot==
In the upscale [[Miami]] suburb of Blue Bay, handsome high school [[School counselor|guidance counselor]] Sam Lombardo, who is known for his popularity among the female students as well as some of the older women in the area due to his [[sex appeal]], holds a school assembly on [[Sex and the law|sex crimes]]. Afterwards, popular and wealthy [[Cheerleading|cheerleader]] Kelly Van Ryan accuses Lombardo of [[raping]] her; outcast student and juvenile delinquent Suzie Toller, who comes from a poor family in the [[Everglades]], makes a similar accusation. Kelly's vindictive mother, real estate heiress Sandra, goes on a relentless crusade to take Sam down. Sam faces harassment, is placed on [[administrative leave]], gets [[blacklisting|blacklisted]] from various local hot spots, is run off the road in his car, and is beaten by Sandra's pool boy, Frankie Condo, before being jailed. Sam hires attorney Kenneth Bowden to defend him.

During the trial, Suzie succumbs to pressure during [[cross-examination]] and admits that she and Kelly concocted false allegations to get revenge on Sam. Suzie was seeking revenge for Sam's failure to bail her out of jail on a minor drug charge, and Kelly was retaliating out of jealousy for Sam's affair with her mother. Both girls face charges of [[perjury]]. Sam and Kenneth negotiate an $8.5 million settlement for [[defamation]], which Sandra pays out using funds from a [[Trust law|trust]] Kelly would receive only upon Sandra's death. In spite of the win, Sam loses his home and his job, but after the payout, it is revealed that Sam and the girls were accomplices, using the trial to extort money from Sandra.

Police detective Ray Duquette suspects the trio of working a scam. Against the wishes of the district attorney, he continues investigating Sam. He tells Kelly and Suzie that Sam has already transferred the money to an off-shore account. Suzie panics and goes to Kelly, who comforts her. Kelly, however, calls Sam and tells him they may have to get rid of Suzie. Suzie attacks Kelly in the pool, and they fight, but end up having sex, unaware Ray is watching them. A few nights later, Sam bludgeons Suzie to death on the beach while Kelly waits nearby. The two then drive to the swamp, where Sam disposes of a plastic-wrapped body.

Ray and his partner, Detective Gloria Perez, investigate Suzie's disappearance. Her blood and teeth are found at the beach, while her car is located abandoned at a bus terminal. The D.A.'s office again insists that Ray drop the case, but he asks Gloria to watch Sam. Sam shows Gloria his counseling file on Kelly, which suggests she is troubled and violent. Meanwhile, Ray goes to the Van Ryans' guest house to confront the scared and upset Kelly, but they end up shooting each other. Sandra rushes over as Ray stumbles out of the house with a gunshot wound to the shoulder, while Sandra discovers Kelly dead from two shots to the chest. Ray claims he was forced to shoot Kelly in self-defense. No charges are filed against him, but he is discharged from the force for disobeying orders.

It is revealed that Sam is in cahoots with Ray. Although Sam is displeased that Ray killed Kelly instead of simply framing her for Suzie's murder, he agrees that they now have fewer loose ends to deal with. The two go sailing on Sam's boat, where Sam attempts to kill Ray. When Ray fights back, he is shot and killed with a [[speargun]] by Suzie, who staged her murder with Sam. Suzie reveals she was motivated to kill Ray to avenge the murder of her best friend, Davey, whom Ray wrongly shot, then staged as a self-defense killing. Sam reluctantly accepts a drink from Suzie, who assures him she will not double-cross him; however, upon drinking it, he realizes she has poisoned it before Suzie knocks him overboard and sails into the sunset, leaving Sam's body to float in the ocean. In the Everglades, Gloria learns the truth about Duquette’s corruption and his crimes.

Suzie was the ultimate mastermind of the plot. Upon finding out that Sam and Kelly were in a sexual relationship, Suzie [[blackmail|blackmailed]] Sam with photographs of the two using drugs during sex, convincing him to help with her scheme. Suzie subsequently orchestrated the meeting between Sam and Ray at a local bar. During her staged murder on the beach, Suzie pulled out her own teeth with pliers to make her death appear legitimate. Ray shot Kelly first before shooting himself in the shoulder to pretend he killed her in self-defense. With Kelly, Ray, and Sam all dead, Suzie is met by Kenneth, who gives her a briefcase full of cash that he describes as "just walking-around money" and a check for millions of dollars. As she leaves, he tells her to "be good" before taking her drink.

==Analysis and themes==
Literary scholar John Thorburn notes that ''Wild Things'' is loosely based on several figures in Greek tragedies, namely [[Medea]], whom he describes the character of Suzie as a "modern-day version of".{{sfn|Thorburn|2010|p=113}} [[Neve Campbell|Suzie]] is met by police, Duquette and Perez, while reading ''[[Death on Credit|Death on the Installment Plan]]''. He also notes that Kelly functions as a [[Phaedra (mythology)|Phaedra]]-like figure, while Sam exemplifies both [[Jason]] and [[Hippolytus of Athens|Hippolytus]].{{sfn|Thorburn|2010|pages=113–114}} Thorburn suggests that the film's "most under-appreciated element is screenwriter Stephen Peters’s obvious debt to classical mythology, tragedy and, especially, two [[Euripides|Euripidean]] plays, ''[[Medea (play)|Medea]]'' (431 BC) and ''[[Hippolytus (play)|Hippolytus]]'' (428 BC)."{{sfn|Thorburn|2010|p=114}}

==Production==
===Development===
The film's screenplay was written by screenwriter Stephen Peters, who had previously written the independent film ''Dead Center'' (1993).<ref name=revisiting>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|url=https://ew.com/movies/2018/03/20/wild-things-20th-anniversary/|title=Pervy or Priceless? Revisiting Wild Things on its 20th anniversary|last=Nashawaty|first=Chris|date=March 20, 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201105070511/https://ew.com/movies/2018/03/20/wild-things-20th-anniversary/|archive-date=November 5, 2020}}</ref> [[John McNaughton]], who had garnered acclaim for the horror film ''[[Henry: Portrait of a Serial Killer]]'' (1986), became involved with the project as he was seeking to make a more mainstream feature.<ref name=money2/>
{{quote box|width=28%|bgcolor=light-grey|quote="I was at a point in my career where I needed to do a commercial picture, and that was one of the key reasons I chose the film. I really liked the script, but it was also me asking myself 'What sells? Sex and violence. You want sex and violence? Well, here you go. How much can you take?'"|source=–Director John McNaughton on his motivation to direct the film<ref name=money2/>}}
Kem Nunn was appointed to perform some rewrites of Peters's original version.<ref name=money2>{{cite web|work=Money Into Light|last=Rowlands|first=Paul|url=http://www.money-into-light.com/2016/10/an-interview-with-john-mcnaughton-part_27.html|title=An Interview with John McNaughton (Part 2 of 3)|date=October 2016|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210415180301/https://www.money-into-light.com/2016/10/an-interview-with-john-mcnaughton-part_27.html|archive-date=April 15, 2021}}</ref> McNaughton commented that Peters's original draft "is brilliant on plot and we didn't change any of it, but I felt Kem Nunn was stronger on texture and character and place. The producer, Peter Guber, sent us off to Florida, where none of us had spent much time and we spent ten days there getting shepherded around to places and meeting people who were like people in the story. When I read the script I thought 'As crazy as it is, I do believe it could happen in the world that we live in.' Once I believe that a story can happen in the real world, then I know how to direct it."<ref name=money2/> Kevin Bacon, who also executive produced, described the script as "the trashiest thing he had ever read ... Every few pages, there was another surprise."<ref name="spring-preview">{{cite magazine |author=<!--Not stated--> |date=February 7, 2014 |title=Spring Movie Preview: March 1998 |url=https://ew.com/article/2014/02/07/spring-movie-preview-march-1998/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250520131424/https://ew.com/article/2014/02/07/spring-movie-preview-march-1998/ |archive-date=May 20, 2025 |access-date=December 15, 2025 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]}}</ref>

The original screenplay featured a [[gay men|gay]] scene between Sergeant Duquette and Sam Lombardo near the end of the film, in which the men kiss in the shower, revealing that—similarly to Suzie and Kelly—the two had a homosexual relationship that allowed Lombardo to prey on Duquette in order to manipulate him and ultimately con him out of the money.<ref name=totaldillon>{{cite web|work=[[Total Film]]|url=https://www.gamesradar.com/the-total-film-interview-matt-dillon/|title=The Total Film Interview: Matt Dillon|date=September 1, 2005|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191004132016/https://www.gamesradar.com/the-total-film-interview-matt-dillon/|archive-date=October 4, 2019|author=''Total Film'' Staff}}</ref> According to Kevin Bacon, the scene was modified to eliminate any suggestion of a sexual relationship between the men, as the film's financiers "didn't like the idea of men making out. They felt it went too far."<ref name=totalbacon>{{cite web|work=[[Total Film]]|author=''Total Film'' Staff|url=https://www.gamesradar.com/the-total-film-interview-kevin-bacon/|title=The Total Film Interview: Kevin Bacon|date=March 1, 2005|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190824052141/https://www.gamesradar.com/the-total-film-interview-kevin-bacon/|archive-date=August 24, 2019}}</ref> However, in an interview in 2023, the director said that the modification occurred because one of the two actors had objected to the sexual aspect of the scene just before the cameras rolled.<ref name="y921">{{cite web | last=Alter | first=Ethan | title='Wild Things' at 25: Director John McNaughton on the steamy sex scene he could never make today | website=Yahoo Entertainment | date=March 17, 2023 | url=https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/wild-things-john-mcnaughton-neve-campbell-denise-richards-kevin-bacon-matt-dillon-142115212.html?guccounter=1 | access-date=May 24, 2026}}</ref>

===Casting===
{{Anchor|Casting}}
[[Robert Downey Jr.]] was the first choice for the role of Sam Lombardo, which ultimately went to Matt Dillon. Downey was considered because of his highly publicized drug problems, and although he was in recovery he was seen as too great an insurance risk. Producer Rodney Liber said "we couldn't make it work", and the production company even offered to put up some of the money but "There were just too many lawyers and insurance people and bond-company people involved."<ref name="spring-preview" />

John McNaughton said Denise Richards' first audition was good but her much improved second audition convinced them to cast her.<ref>{{cite magazine |date=April 10, 1998 |first=Benjamin|last=Svetkey |title=Denise Richards takes a 'wild' turn |url=https://ew.com/article/1998/04/10/denise-richards-takes-wild-turn/ |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210622013407/https://ew.com/article/1998/04/10/denise-richards-takes-wild-turn/|archive-date=June 22, 2021|url-status=live}}</ref> Richards' lawyer negotiated a detailed contract about how much nudity would be filmed, including the option to use a body double. Richards did not use a double and filmed the scene herself after drinking a pitcher of margaritas with Neve Campbell.<ref name="revisiting" /><ref>{{cite web |date=July 31, 2011 |last=Carter |first=Andrew |title=12 Stories From Denise Richards' New Memoir |url=https://www.thedailybeast.com/12-stories-from-denise-richards-new-memoir |website=[[The Daily Beast]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201124185431/https://www.thedailybeast.com/12-stories-from-denise-richards-new-memoir |archive-date=November 24, 2020 |url-access=registration |url-status=dead }}</ref> Campbell's contract had a strict no-nudity clause.<ref name=revisiting/> Campbell took on the role wanting to challenge herself, to do something different from her ''[[Party of Five]]'' character, and to avoid being [[Typecasting|typecast]].<ref>{{cite magazine |date=September 18, 1997 |last=Dunn |first=Jancee |title=Interview: Neve Campbell |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-features/interview-neve-campbell-235077/ |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220125183621/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-features/interview-neve-campbell-235077/|archive-date=January 25, 2022}}</ref> [[Natasha Lyonne]] also auditioned for Suzie.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Riley |first=Jenelle |date=2024-09-20 |title='His Three Daughters' Star Natasha Lyonne Recalls An Early Audition for 'Wild Things': 'I Was Definitely Not Getting That Part' |url=https://variety.com/2024/awards/actors/his-three-daughters-wild-things-1236151939/ |access-date=2024-10-06 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> Bacon also had a no nudity clause in his contract but without giving it much thought allowed McNaughton to use the shot that he thought looked best, and a moment of frontal nudity was included in the film. He was surprised by how many questions he got about it at the American press showing, and noted that the European press did not ask about it at all.<ref>{{cite web |date=August 25, 2014 |last=Branch |first=Chris |title=Kevin Bacon Dishes On Doing Full-Frontal Nudity |url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/kevin-bacon-wild-things-frontal-nudity_n_5711619 |website=[[HuffPost|The Huffington Post]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210623202351/https://www.huffpost.com/entry/kevin-bacon-wild-things-frontal-nudity_n_5711619|archive-date=June 23, 2021 }}</ref>

===Filming===
The film was shot in the spring and summer of 1997, between April 21 and July 16.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/444211/wild-things#notes | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240826042035/https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/444211/wild-things#notes | archive-date=August 26, 2024 | title=Wild Things }}</ref> It is unclear whether or not Neve Campbell was still working on the film in the latter stages of shooting, since her film ''[[Scream 2]]'' began shooting on June 16, 1997, whilst ''Wild Things'' was also still shooting.<ref>{{cite book | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5u-i03aazJwC&dq=%22june+1997%22+%22scream+2%22&pg=PA34 | isbn=978-0-7864-1923-4 | title=Wes Craven: The Art of Horror | date=March 9, 2004 | publisher=McFarland |last=Muir|first=John Kenneth|page=34}}</ref> Filming in the [[Everglades]] proved difficult due to severe weather conditions. ===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]] the film has an approval rating of 64% based on reviews from 121 critics, with an average rating of 5.80/10. The site's consensus states: "''Wild Things'' is a delightfully salacious, flesh-exposed romp that also requires a high degree of love for trash cinema."<ref>{{cite web |title=Wild Things (1998) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/wild_things/ |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=February 21, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 51/100 based on reviews from 20 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{Metacritic film|title=Wild Things}} {{Retrieved|access-date=October 8, 2020}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a grade "C+" on scale of A to F.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= WILD THINGS (1998) C+ |work= [[CinemaScore]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= December 20, 2018 }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three stars out of four, praising McNaughton's directing and the plot twists. He described it as "lurid trash, with a plot so twisted they're still explaining it during the closing titles. It's like a three-way collision between a [[softcore pornography|softcore sex film]], a [[soap opera]] and a B-grade noir. I liked it."<ref>{{Cite web |date=March 20, 1998 |first=Roger |last=Ebert |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Wild Things |website=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/wild-things-1998|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211222063042/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/wild-things-1998|archive-date=December 22, 2021}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] gave the film a marginal recommendation.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Siskel|first=Gene|authorlink=Gene Siskel|url=https://siskelebert.org/?p=1694 |title=Wild Things, Niagara Niagara, Mr. Nice Guy, Wide Awake, Fireworks, 1998|work=Siskel and Ebert Movie Reviews |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210514220404/https://siskelebert.org/?p=1694|archive-date=May 14, 2021|date=1998}}</ref> [[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]''  praised Campbell and Richard's performances, and also McNaughton's direction for adding "a decadent gloss to this far-fetched, quintuple-crossing tale", although she criticized the plot as being "loony".<ref>{{Cite web |date=March 20, 1998 |first=Janet |last=Maslin |author-link=Janet Maslin |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1998/03/20/movies/film-review-schoolgirls-make-alligators-look-like-ingenues.html |title=FILM REVIEW; Schoolgirls Make Alligators Look Like Ingenues |website=[[The New York Times]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220422164915/https://www.nytimes.com/1998/03/20/movies/film-review-schoolgirls-make-alligators-look-like-ingenues.html|archive-date=April 22, 2022 }}</ref>

In ''[[The Washington Post]]'', [[Desson Howe]] described the film as "clearly a crock", and although it "may not have a single redeeming feature, but it doesn't have a dull moment, either."<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1998/03/20/wild-things-a-hot-pursuit/8ee8578f-4915-495c-8f4e-763780af2492/ |title='WILD THINGS': A HOT PURSUIT |first=Desson |last=Howe |date=March 20, 1998 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170827042616/https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1998/03/20/wild-things-a-hot-pursuit/8ee8578f-4915-495c-8f4e-763780af2492/ |archive-date=August 27, 2017 |url-status=bot: unknown |access-date=December 21, 2020 }}</ref> In the same newspaper, [[Stephen Hunter]] described the film as being "as tawdry as someone else's lingerie, yet not without a certain prurient watchability".<ref>{{Cite news |date=March 20, 1998|last=Hunter|first=Stephen|authorlink=Stephen Hunter |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/wildthingshunter.htm |title='Wild Things' (R) |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] }}</ref> ''[[The Orlando Sentinel]]'' said that the film, overall, missed the mark, but that Murray in his small role manages to steal the show.<ref>{{cite web |date=March 20, 1998 |author=Boyar, Jay |title=Twisty 'Wild Things' Just Misses Its Mark |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1998-03-20-9803200824-story.html|work =Orlando Sentinel|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210111003917/https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1998-03-20-9803200824-story.html|archive-date=January 11, 2021}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised the casting of Dillon, Bacon, Campbell, Richards, Russell, Murray, and Snodgress, stating "you have an ensemble that appears to be enjoying the challenge of offbeat roles and unusual material. There's not a wrong note struck by the game group of players", and also praised the film as "original" with a "glossy, unreal quality that nicely dovetails with the pulse of the drama".<ref>{{cite web |date=March 18, 1998 |last=Klady |first=Leonard |title=Wild Things |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/reviews/wild-things-1200453105/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] }}</ref>

==Legacy==
In a retrospective on the film celebrating its twentieth anniversary, ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' writer Chris Nashawaty wrote that ''Wild Things'' marked a peak in lurid sex-themed thriller films in the late-1990s, summarizing: "As a rule, movies like ''Wild Things'' fight an uphill battle with critics who would want to seem above titillation. But this was one of those rare films whose underlying smarts couldn't be denied."<ref name=revisiting/> McNaughton commented in 2018 that he considered ''Wild Things'' his "most political film" due to its focus on [[social class]], concluding: "Who wins? The girl from the [[trailer park]]! She’s all alone on the ninety foot sail boat, out on the Caribbean. Pretty much everyone else is dead. That was the nineties, with the concentration of wealth. But the girl from the trailer park takes ’em all down. You know, I’m from the striving working class. A lot of the kids I grew up with, the parents didn’t care if their kid dropped out of school. But some of us had parents who insisted their children have an education, go to college, escape all that. So that’s where my heart always lies."<ref name="flashback">{{cite magazine|magazine=The Flashback Files|url=https://www.flashbackfiles.com/john-mcnaughton-interview|title=How Dare You? An Interview with John McNaughton|year=2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220527084721/https://www.flashbackfiles.com/john-mcnaughton-interview|archive-date=May 27, 2022}}</ref>

===Related works===
The film was followed by three sequels: ''[[Wild Things 2]]'' and ''[[Wild Things: Diamonds in the Rough]]'' both premiered on [[Starz Encore|Encore Mystery]] in 2004 and 2005, respectively and ''[[Wild Things: Foursome]]'' was released on DVD in 2010. The sequels recycled much of the plot, dialogue, and direction of the first film, albeit with different actors. All three films take place in Blue Bay and its high school, and include the Blue Bay Police Department (BBPD).<ref name="McLevy">{{cite web |date=2018 |author=McLevy, Alex |title=Wild Things gave us Kevin Bacon's wang and a threesome of sequels |url=https://www.avclub.com/wild-things-gave-us-twists-kevin-bacon-s-wang-and-a-t-1823634659|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210120203829/https://www.avclub.com/wild-things-gave-us-twists-kevin-bacon-s-wang-and-a-t-1823634659|archive-date=January 20, 2021 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=June 21, 2018 |author=Rob Hunter |title='Wild Things' is a Blackly Comic Gem; Its Three Sequels Are Also Movies Set in Florida |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/wild-things-sequels-dtv-descent/2/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210110130258/https://www.slashfilm.com/wild-things-sequels-dtv-descent/2/|archive-date=January 10, 2021|website=[[/Film]] }}</ref> In 2006, the producers tried to develop a [[spiritual successor]] and John McNaughton was in talks to again direct a script by Stephen Peters titled "Backstabbers". Richards and Campbell were also in talks to star, but the movie was never made.<ref>{{cite web |date=February 15, 2006 |last=Gardner |first=Chris |title=Mandalay 'Wild' about pic reteam |url=https://variety.com/2006/film/markets-festivals/mandalay-wild-about-pic-reteam-1117938205/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] }}</ref>
