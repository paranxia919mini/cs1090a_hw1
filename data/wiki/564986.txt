'''''The Princess Diaries''''' is a 2001 American [[comedy film]]<ref>{{Cite web |date=November 11, 2022 |title=The Princess Diaries |url=https://www.afi.com/news/the-princess-diaries-afi-movie-club/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230131062406/https://www.afi.com/news/the-princess-diaries-afi-movie-club/ |archive-date=January 31, 2023 |access-date=July 19, 2024 |website=[[American Film Institute|AFI Movie Club]] |quote=THE PRINCESS DIARIES (2001) is a modern day coming-of-age Cinderella story}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Grobar |first=Matt |date=November 15, 2022 |title='The Princess Diaries' Threequel Written By Aadrita Mukerji In Works At Disney |url=https://deadline.com/2022/11/the-princess-diaries-threequel-in-works-at-disney-1235173279/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230520024616/https://deadline.com/2022/11/the-princess-diaries-threequel-in-works-at-disney-1235173279/ |archive-date=May 20, 2023 |access-date=July 18, 2024 |work=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |quote=The Princess Diaries is a coming-of-age comedy}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Boardman |first=Madeline |date=March 14, 2023 |title=The Princess Diaries cast: Where are they now? |url=https://ew.com/gallery/princess-diaries-where-are-they-now/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250314122917/https://ew.com/gallery/princess-diaries-where-are-they-now/ |archive-date=March 14, 2025 |access-date=July 18, 2024 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |quote=Disney's 2001 coming-of-age comedy}}</ref> directed by [[Garry Marshall]] from a screenplay by Gina Wendkos. Produced by [[Walt Disney Pictures]], it is based on [[Meg Cabot]]'s 2000 novel [[The Princess Diaries|of the same name]] and stars [[Anne Hathaway]] (in her film debut), [[Julie Andrews]], [[Héctor Elizondo]], [[Heather Matarazzo]], [[Mandy Moore]], [[Caroline Goodall]], and [[Robert Schwartzman]]. In the film, shy American teenager [[Mia Thermopolis]] (Hathaway) learns she is [[crown princess]] of Genovia and receives tutelage from her estranged grandmother Clarisse Renaldi (Andrews), Genovia's [[regent|Queen regent]], as she decides whether to claim or [[Abdication|renounce]] her title.

Development on the film began after Cabot's agent, believing in the novel's feature film potential, pursued [[Debra Martin Chase]] to adapt it: Chase eventually secured a deal with Disney, and the film was [[Green-light|greenlit]] in August 1999, being co-produced with [[Whitney Houston]]'s [[Whitney Houston|BrownHouse Productions]]. Wendkos collaborated with Chase on the screenplay, which differs from Cabot's novel, with certain themes softened and other aspects introduced or removed, as Marshall sought to create a [[Motion Picture Association film rating system#G|G-rated]] film with broad appeal to adults and children. Hathaway was cast in 2000 while Andrews (who was then semi-retired from acting), joined due to Marshall's involvement, marking her first Disney film since ''[[Mary Poppins (film)|Mary Poppins]]'' (1964). The rest of the cast, which included the return of Marshall's frequent collaborator Elizondo and Marshall's family in minor roles, was rounded out soon thereafter. [[Principal photography]] took place from September to December 2000, with filming locations including [[Walt Disney Studios (Burbank)|Walt Disney Studios]] in [[Burbank, California|Burbank]] and in [[San Francisco]]. The film's costumes were designed by [[Gary Jones (costume designer)|Gary Jones]] and the music was composed by [[John Debney]].

''The Princess Diaries'' premiered at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Los Angeles]] on July 29, 2001, and was theatrically released in the United States on August 3, by [[Buena Vista Pictures Distribution]]. A [[sleeper hit]], the film grossed $165.3 million worldwide, defying industry expectations of an underperformance due to its G-rating and subject matter. Upon release, ''The Princess Diaries'' received mixed reviews from critics, with praise for Hathaway's and Andrews' performances (establishing Hathaway as a [[bankable star]] and reviving Andrews' career), but criticism for its plot. It received retrospective praise for its themes of self-discovery and identity and its centering of female characters. A sequel, ''[[The Princess Diaries 2: Royal Engagement]]'', was released in 2004, and a third installment is in development.

==Plot==
<!-- Please see WP:FILMPLOT before editing. Per key guideline, the plot summary should be within the desired length of 400-700 words. -->
Grove High School student [[Mia Thermopolis]] resides with her single mother, Helen, in a refurbished [[Fire station|firehouse]] in [[San Francisco]]. Unpopular among her peers, Mia suffers from a [[Glossophobia|fear of public speaking]] while harboring a crush on Josh Bryant, and is often teased by his popular girlfriend, Lana Thomas. Mia's only friends are political activist Lilly Moscovitz and her older brother, Michael, who secretly harbors feelings for Mia.

Mia is invited to meet her estranged paternal grandmother, Clarisse, on a diplomatic visit to San Francisco. Mia learns that she is the sole heir to the small European kingdom of [[Genovia]], having inherited the position from her recently deceased father, Philippe. Clarisse is determined to make Mia into a refined princess so that she may one day rule the kingdom, which Clarisse currently presides over as Queen regent of Genovia. Overwhelmed at the discovery, Mia initially refuses until her mother convinces her to attend her grandmother's "princess lessons" based on the condition that she need not make her final decision until the Genovian Independence Day Ball in three weeks' time.

Mia receives a glamorous makeover and a limousine chauffeured by Joe, the queen's head of security and confidant, who becomes a father figure to her. Mia's transformation causes her schoolmates to treat her differently, while her increasingly hectic schedule strains her relationship with Lilly. To appease her best friend, Mia tells Lilly the truth and swears her to secrecy. However, the public soon learns that Mia is a princess after the secret is sold to the press by Paolo, the beautician responsible for Mia's makeover, and paparazzi begin to pursue her relentlessly. Although Mia embarrasses herself at her first [[state dinner]], Clarisse admits that she found her clumsiness endearing and suggests that they spend quality time together, canceling their lessons for the afternoon. While bonding, Clarisse explains that although Mia's parents loved each other, they divorced amicably in order to pursue their own passions: Philippe remaining in Genovia to eventually become king, and Helen returning to America with Mia to offer her a "normal" childhood.

As Mia's popularity grows, Josh invites her to attend the end-of-school beach party with him. Mia accepts, causing her to neglect Lilly and cancel plans she made with Michael. The paparazzi ambush Mia at the beach party. Josh publicly kisses Mia to get his [[15 minutes of fame]], while Lana exposes Mia wearing only a towel; both photographs are printed in the newspaper the following day. Finding the photos inappropriate for a princess, Clarisse admonishes Mia for her behavior, after which a humiliated Mia promises to renounce her title. Joe reminds Clarisse that Mia is still both a teenager and her granddaughter, implying that the queen reacted too harshly.

After making amends with Lilly, Mia gets back at Josh during PE and finally stands up to Lana for bullying a schoolmate. Mia invites both Lilly and Michael to the ball but Michael declines, still heartbroken over Mia's initial dismissal. After Clarisse apologizes to Mia for scolding her, she states that Mia must publicly renounce the throne at the ball. Terrified by the prospect, Mia plans to take a trip to Colorado until she discovers a touching birthday letter from her late father and relents. Mia's car malfunctions while driving to the ball, stranding her in a downpour until Joe rescues her in his limo, with the help of Mia's neighbor, Mr. Robutusen.

When they finally arrive, Mia, still wet and untidy from the rain, accepts her role as Princess of Genovia while Clarisse, Helen, and Lilly look on proudly. After changing into a gown, Mia accompanies Clarisse into the ballroom where Michael, who has accepted Mia's apology, invites her to dance before proceeding to the courtyard, where they confess their feelings for each other and share their first kiss. In the final scene, Mia travels to Genovia on a private jet with her pet cat Fat Louie, and writes in her diary that she plans to relocate to Genovia with her mother.

=== Further writing ===
Additional adjustments were made to the script once the cast was finalized. Marshall was constantly trying to make the film funnier because Disney had challenged him to incorporate humor into the G-rated project.<ref name="Eliza-2016" /><ref name="Caro-2001" /> Marshall drew from experience writing for the family-oriented shows ''[[Happy Days]]'' and ''[[The Odd Couple (1970 TV series)|The Odd Couple]]'',<ref name="Caro-2001" /> whose [[Teleplay|scripts]] he revisited for inspiration.<ref name="EW-2001" /> Matarazzo described the set as a collaborative and familial environment fostered by Marshall,<ref name="Eliza-2016" /> consisting of birthday parties for cast and crew members, including Marshall himself.<ref name="Setoodeh-2016" /><ref name="Knolle-2016" />

Mia's grandmother, nicknamed "Grandmere" in the novel, is depicted as considerably kinder in the film.<ref name="Writers Write-2019" /> Disney's decision to have Mia's father be deceased is among the film's most significant deviations from its source material, in which he is both alive and plays an integral role.<ref name="Kirby-2017">{{Cite web|url=https://hellogiggles.com/reviews-coverage/movies/princess-diaries-dad-mia-julie-andrews/|title=There's a reason Mia's dad was killed off in "The Princess Diaries" movies — and it's because of Dame Julie Andrews|last=Kirby|first=Meaghan|date=August 3, 2017|website=[[HelloGiggles]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190106153423/https://hellogiggles.com/reviews-coverage/movies/princess-diaries-dad-mia-julie-andrews/|archive-date=January 6, 2019|access-date=January 6, 2019}}</ref> The producers decided to [[kill off]] Mia's father to expand Clarisse's role, for which they had wanted Andrews cast from the beginning.<ref name="Kirby-2017" /> Once she learned of Andrews' involvement, Cabot approved that Mia's father be eliminated, much of whose dialogue was re-written for Mia's grandmother.<ref name="Kirby-2017" /> Upon Marshall's request, Andrews suggested that the fictional country of Genovia be famous for its pears, after which the set was decorated with [[Fake fruit|artificial pears]] and pear-shaped statues.<ref name="Adhav-2016" /> Marshall wrote Hathaway's childhood struggles with wearing a [[Retainer (orthodontics)|retainer]] into the film; ==== Costume design ====
Costume designer [[Gary Jones (costume designer)|Gary Jones]], who had worked with Marshall on prior films, was drawn to ''The Princess Diaries'' due to the wide range of costumes required for its characters, describing the project as "a costume designer's dream come true".<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> Jones worked closely with Andrews to design Clarisse's costumes, drawing inspiration from [[Chanel]], [[Bill Blass]], and [[Christian Dior]].<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> The gown Andrews wears to the state dinner was handmade in China, and is an homage to her role as ''My Fair Lady''<nowiki/>'s [[Eliza Doolittle]].<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> Jones envisioned Mia as a character who is shy about her body at first, opting to dress her in layers consisting of long sleeves and loose-fitting garments.<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> The character's [[Periwinkle (color)|periwinkle]] state dinner gown was inspired by [[Victoria, Crown Princess of Sweden]],<ref name="Rosseinsky-2016" /> with Jones describing the dress as an homage to the [[Renaissance]] period and ''[[Romeo and Juliet]]'', accessorizing it with an 18-carat diamond ring.<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" />

Both Hathaway and Andrews's tiaras were designed and custom-made for them, with the designers ensuring that both characters' crowns were appropriate for their age.<ref name="Stein-2012a" /> The tiara and jewelry Andrews wears during the final scene of ''The Princess Diaries'' consists of half a million dollars’ worth of diamonds, loaned to the production by jeweler [[Harry Winston]],<ref name="Rosseinsky-2016" /> with whom Jones worked closely to obtain several unique jewels.<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> A security guard followed Andrews at all times to both protect her and ensure all jewelry was returned at the end of each day.<ref name="Stein-2012a" /><ref name="Nepales-2001" /> Andrews's peach [[taffeta]] ballgown was accessorized with a 100-carat necklace comprising four rows of diamonds.<ref name="CinemaReview-2019" /> Hathaway's tiara was considerably less expensive,<ref name="Rosseinsky-2016" /> consisting of [[cubic zirconia]] as opposed to authentic jewels.<ref name="Sorokach-2016" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Tinubu |first=Aramide |date=May 4, 2016 |title=17 Things You Never Knew About 'The Princess Diaries' |url=https://www.hollywood.com/movies/17-things-you-never-knew-about-the-princess-diaries-60575998 |access-date=January 22, 2019 |website=[[Hollywood.com]]}}</ref> The crowns and tiaras worn by both actresses are preserved by the [[Walt Disney Archives]], into which they were inducted in 2016 to commemorate the film's 15th anniversary.<ref>{{Cite web |date=August 3, 2016 |title=The Crowns of The Princess Diaries |url=https://d23.com/the-crowns-of-the-princess-diaries/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240302205450/https://d23.com/the-crowns-of-the-princess-diaries/ |archive-date=March 2, 2024 |access-date=January 7, 2019 |website=D23}}</ref>

Hathaway donned false eyebrows and a wig to make her character's makeover more dramatic.<ref name="Gupta-2016" /> Hathaway's [[Toupée|hair piece]] was nicknamed "The Beast", while her eyebrows required one hour to apply;<ref name="Brown-2016" /> each strand of hair was individually glued to her brow.<ref name="Rosseinsky-2016" /> At times Hathaway was required to leave the set in full costume, claiming that she "never felt so alone in [her] entire life".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Falk |first=Ben |date=Dec 11, 2001 |title=Anne Hathaway – The Princess Diaries |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/12/11/anne_hathaway_princess_diaries_interview.shtml |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231116211337/https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/12/11/anne_hathaway_princess_diaries_interview.shtml |archive-date=November 16, 2023 |access-date=January 4, 2019 |website=[[BBC Online]]}}</ref>

== Themes ==
[[File:Julie Andrews My Fair Lady.JPG|left|thumb|Due to its themes, ''The Princess Diaries'' has been heavily compared to the play ''Pygmalion''; the play served as the basis for the 1956 stage musical ''[[My Fair Lady]]'', in which Andrews played the main character Eliza Doolittle. ]]
Andrews explained that the film is as much "about what you are inside and the responsibility and just plain old hard work that goes into being a princess" as it is "about the trappings of being a princess".<ref name="Stein-2012a" /> Hathaway identified "remaining true to yourself" among the film's core values, describing Mia's transformation as both emotional and [[Psychology|psychological]], "empowerment story", identifying "the power to be anything that you want to be" as its core message.<ref name="Zook-2001" /> Chase elaborated, "In the beginning, Mia looks in the mirror and doesn't think she's princess material at all" but ultimately "comes to believe that she is".<ref name="Zook-2001" /> Houston echoed that being a princess "doesn't mean they have to come from royalty" but Houston and BrownHouse Productions were heavily involved in curating music for the film, which Kristal Brent Zook of ''The Washington Post'' observed "displays more girl power and ethnic flavor than the film does".<ref name="Zook-2001" /> Dawn Soler served as the film's music supervisor,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Andreeva |first=Nellie |date=January 6, 2007 |title=Touchstone TV playing music with Soler |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/touchstone-tv-playing-music-soler-127393 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190425115004/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/touchstone-tv-playing-music-soler-127393 |archive-date=April 25, 2019 |access-date=February 4, 2019 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]}}</ref> and Moore recorded a cover of [[Connie Francis]]' "[[Stupid Cupid]]" for the soundtrack.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Minton |first=Melissa |date=February 16, 2018 |title=Mandy Moore Posted a Throwback From The Princess Diaries of Herself With Super-Thin Brows |url=https://www.allure.com/story/mandy-moore-90s-throwback-the-princess-diaries |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230423211003/https://www.allure.com/story/mandy-moore-90s-throwback-the-princess-diaries |archive-date=April 23, 2023 |access-date=January 4, 2019 |website=[[Allure (magazine)|Allure]]}}</ref> Composer [[John Debney]] was recruited to score the film. Long-time friends with Disney executive Bill Green, Green felt that Debney would complement the film and personally recommended him to Marshall.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Goldwasser |first=Dan |date=October 16, 2001 |title=John Debney – Interview |url=https://www.soundtrack.net/content/article/?id=85 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230127215943/https://www.soundtrack.net/content/article/?id=85 |archive-date=January 27, 2023 |access-date=January 29, 2019 |website=[[Soundtrack.Net]]}}</ref> Debney identified ''The Princess Diaries'' as one of the films he is most proud to have worked on, explaining that the project holds an "emotional connection" because it reminds him of his mother.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ciafardini |first=Marc V. |date=June 19, 2014 |title=Interview…Film Composer John Debney Contemporizes History with the Music to 'Bonnie & Clyde' |url=http://goseetalk.com/interview-film-composer-john-debney/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230423154029/http://goseetalk.com/interview-film-composer-john-debney/ |archive-date=April 23, 2023 |access-date=January 29, 2019 |website=GoSeeTalk.com}}</ref>

The [[The Princess Diaries soundtracks|official soundtrack]] was released by [[Walt Disney Records]] on July 24, 2001.<ref name="Phares-2001">{{Cite web|url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-princess-diaries-mw0000011309|title=Original Soundtrack – The Princess Diaries|last=Phares|first=Heather|date=July 24, 2001|website=[[AllMusic]]|access-date=January 24, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419024646/https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-princess-diaries-mw0000011309|archive-date=April 19, 2023}}</ref> Described as largely a collection of [[pop rock]], [[teen pop]], and [[dance-pop]] tracks,<ref name="Phares-2001" /> the soundtrack features contributions from artists [[BBMak]], [[Aaron Carter]], [[Backstreet Boys]], [[Myra (singer)|Myra]], [[Hanson (band)|Hanson]] and [[B*Witched]].<ref name="Walsingham-2018" /> [[AllMusic]]'s Heather Phares reviewed that the album consists of mostly "slick, virtually interchangeable singles ... a few tracks, for better or worse, are particularly distinctive", and felt that the album could have benefited from more originality as the Backstreet Boys' track "What Makes You Different (Makes You Beautiful)" implies.<ref name="Phares-2001" /> A soundtrack consisting exclusively of the film's orchestral score was released on December 11, 2001, credited to Debney.<ref>{{Cite web |date=December 11, 2001 |title=John Debney – The Princess Diaries [Original Score] |url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-princess-diaries-original-score-mw0000214916 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230423061434/https://www.allmusic.com/album/the-princess-diaries-original-score-mw0000214916 |archive-date=April 23, 2023 |access-date=January 29, 2019 |website=AllMusic}}</ref>

To commemorate the film's 10th anniversary in April 2012,<ref name="Stein-2012a" /> the film was released on [[Blu-ray]] to coincide with Disney's National Princess Week and the release of Andrews's book ''The Very Fairy Princess: Here Comes The Flower Girl!''.<ref name="D23-2012" /> A double-disk paired with ''[[The Princess Diaries 2: Royal Engagement]]'', the release was sold exclusively at [[Target Corporation|Target]].<ref name="D23-2012" />
