'''''The Outsiders''''' is a 1983 American [[Coming-of-age film|coming-of-age]] [[crime drama film]] directed by [[Francis Ford Coppola]] (billed as Francis Coppola), based on [[The Outsiders (novel)|the 1967 novel]] by [[S. E. Hinton]]. Jo Ellen Misakian, a librarian at [[Lone Star Elementary School]] in [[Fresno, California]], and her students were responsible for inspiring Coppola to make the film.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.zoetrope.com/zoe_films.cgi?page=films&action=show_one&film_id=25 |title= American Zoetrope: Films |publisher=Zoetrope.com |access-date=June 4, 2012 |archive-date=May 6, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120506223558/http://www.zoetrope.com/zoe_films.cgi?page=films&action=show_one&film_id=25 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The film is notable for its ensemble cast including [[C. Thomas Howell]] (who garnered a [[Young Artist Award]]), [[Matt Dillon]], [[Ralph Macchio]], [[Patrick Swayze]], [[Rob Lowe]] (in his feature film debut), [[Diane Lane]], [[Emilio Estevez]], [[Tom Cruise]], and [[Leif Garrett]]. In addition, Dillon starred in two more films based on Hinton novels: ''[[Tex (film)|Tex]]'' (1982), with Estevez; and Coppola's ''[[Rumble Fish]]'' (1983), with Lane. Estevez also wrote and starred in the Hinton adaptation ''[[That Was Then... This Is Now]]'' (1985).<ref>{{cite web|title=Movies - S.E. Hinton|url=http://www.sehinton.com/movies/|ref=2|access-date=September 16, 2014|archive-date=October 17, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141017020203/http://www.sehinton.com/movies/|url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Outsiders'' was released by [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] in the United States on March 25, 1983. The film received mostly positive reviews from critics, particularly for its performances, and grossed $25.8 million on a $10 million budget. Over the years, the film has earned a [[cult following]] and has spawned a [[The Outsiders (American TV series)|1990 sequel television series]] and a successful [[The Outsiders (musical)|2023 stage musical]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Phillips|first=Patrick|date=August 25, 2021|title=Is The Outsiders Based On A True Story?|url=https://www.looper.com/585437/is-the-outsiders-based-on-a-true-story/|access-date=November 4, 2021|website=Looper.com}}</ref>

==Plot==
In 1965 [[Tulsa, Oklahoma]], Darry Curtis is left to raise his younger brothers, Sodapop and [[Ponyboy Curtis|Ponyboy]], after the deaths of their parents in a car crash eight months earlier. Youth subculture in the city is centered around two gangs — the affluent [[Ivy League (clothes)|Socs]] and the poorer [[Greaser (subculture)|Greasers]]. Amongst the latter are Ponyboy, Johnny Cade, and their friend Dallas Winston, who go to a movie at the local [[drive-in theater]]. There, Dallas unsuccessfully flirts with Soc Cherry Valance and leaves in a rage after she insults him by splashing him with [[Coca-Cola]]. Cherry and her friend Marcia invite Ponyboy and Johnny to sit with her and her friend, later joined by Two-Bit Matthews. After the movie, the Socs' boyfriends, Bob Sheldon and Randy Anderson, take umbrage and confront them, prompting Two-Bit to pull his [[switchblade]], so the girls leave with the Soc guys to avoid escalation.

Ponyboy and Johnny walk to an abandoned lot to help Johnny avoid his parents' bickering and [[domestic violence]]. He confesses the hopelessness of his life and considers suicide. Ponyboy falls asleep, and Johnny wakes him a few hours later. Ponyboy rushes home, where Darry confronts him and slaps him. Ponyboy runs off to a local park with Johnny, where Bob, Randy, and three other Socs attack them. Johnny is beaten, and when Ponyboy is almost drowned in the park's fountain, Johnny fatally stabs Bob in self-defense. Ponyboy and Johnny find Dallas, who gives them money for food and a loaded firearm. They flee to Windrixville after hopping on a train and hide in an abandoned church, where they read ''[[Gone with the Wind (novel)|Gone with the Wind]]'' aloud to pass the time, and Ponyboy recites [[Nothing Gold Can Stay (poem)|"Nothing Gold Can Stay"]] to Johnny. Dallas visits, taking them to get a meal at [[Dairy Queen]]. He brings a note from Sodapop that urges Ponyboy to come home, as Cherry is willing to testify on their behalf. Johnny favors turning themselves in, but Dallas disagrees.

Returning to the church, they discover that it is on fire with children trapped inside. They rescue them but suffer burns. Johnny breaks his back when part of the structure collapses on him. Ponyboy reunites with his brothers in the hospital and returns home. Their heroic deed makes the front page of the local paper. The next morning, Ponyboy falls ill and Two-Bit volunteers to care for him. Two-Bit and Ponyboy [[Hitchhiking|hitchhike]] towards the hospital, but duck into the Dairy Queen upon seeing Socs driving nearby. One of the Socs, Randy, peacefully talks to Ponyboy about an upcoming rumble to settle the Greaser–Soc rivalry. Ponyboy and Two-Bit visit Johnny and Dallas in the hospital. Ponyboy asks Two-Bit to buy another copy of ''Gone with the Wind'' at the hospital gift shop. Johnny's mother comes, but he refuses her visit. She takes out her ire on Ponyboy and Two-Bit, who decries her as a bad mother.

Dallas encourages them to win the rumble for Johnny, and they do. Dallas then drives an injured Ponyboy to the hospital to see Johnny. Dallas tells him about the Greasers' victory, but Johnny is dismissive and dies after telling Ponyboy to "[[Nothing Gold Can Stay (poem)|stay gold]]". A distraught Dallas robs a store, is pursued by the police, and he commits [[suicide by cop]]. In court, the judge exonerates Ponyboy for Bob's death as self-defense and places him in Darry's custody. Cherry sees Ponyboy and gives him an encouraging smile, but walks away in order to maintain her reputation. Ponyboy's English teacher offers him a passing grade if he writes a quality essay, but Ponyboy is uninspired. He finds a letter from Johnny explaining how saving the children was worth him sacrificing his own life and advising Ponyboy to "never change". Johnny's letter inspires Ponyboy's essay, which begins, "When I stepped out into the bright sunlight from the darkness of the movie house, I had only two things on my mind: Paul Newman and a ride home.", the book's opening line and the opening line of the movie’s narration.

==Production==
===Development===
[[Francis Ford Coppola]] had not intended to make a film about teen angst until Jo Ellen Misakian, a school librarian from [[Lone Star Elementary School]] in [[Fresno, California]], wrote to Coppola on behalf of her seventh and eighth grade students about adapting ''The Outsiders''.<ref name="variety cast" /><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lettersofnote.com/2013/03/the-outsiders.html|title=Letters of Note|publisher=Letters of Note|access-date=March 6, 2013|archive-date=March 8, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130308145831/http://www.lettersofnote.com/2013/03/the-outsiders.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

<blockquote> "We are all so impressed with the book, The Outsiders by SE Hinton, that a petition has been circulated asking that it be made into a movie. We have chosen you to send it to."<ref name=Hoad>{{cite web |last= Hoad |first= Phil |title= 'Tom Cruise was an intense kid': How Francis Ford Coppola made The Outsiders |work= The Guardian |date= 2021-11-01 |url= https://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/nov/01/how-we-made-the-outsiders-francis-ford-coppola-and-c-thomas-howell |access-date= 2021-11-01}}</ref></blockquote>

Approximately 15 pages of children's signatures were attached to the letter written in different colors. Moved by the letter, Coppola read the book and was impressed by the relationships between the greaser kids. It brought back memories of when he had been a drama counselor working with children at a summer camp in his youth.<ref name=Hoad/>

===Filming===
[[File: The Outsiders House.jpg|thumb|[[The Outsiders House Museum]], used for filming in the movie, located at 731 Curtis Brothers Lane in Tulsa, Oklahoma, now showcases props from the film.]]
The film was shot on location in [[Tulsa, Oklahoma]].<ref>{{cite web |title="The Outsiders" film, shot in Tulsa, page 1 |url=http://tulsatvmemories.com/outside1.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100107130941/http://tulsatvmemories.com/outside1.html |archive-date=January 7, 2010 |website=tulsatvmemories.com}}</ref> Filming took place from March 29 to May 15, 1982. A newspaper used to show a story about the three greasers saving the kids in ''The Outsiders'' includes a real story from 1982 regarding the death of a man hit by a train in Boston.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://masscases.com/cases/app/18/18massappct417.html |title=COMMONWEALTH vs. WILLIAM M. JOYCE (and companion cases) |publisher=Massachusetts Trial Court Law Libraries |access-date=August 31, 2017 |archive-date=November 13, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171113093716/http://masscases.com/cases/app/18/18massappct417.html |url-status=live}}</ref> Coppola's craving for realism almost led to disaster during the church-burning scene. He pressed for "more fire", and the small, controlled blaze accidentally triggered a much larger, uncontrolled fire, which a downpour doused.<ref>{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ALT9c80ZIncC&q=%22the+outsiders%22+coppola+tulsa+northside&pg=RA1-PA208 |title=G. Phillips, ''Godfather: the intimate Coppola'', p. 208 |isbn=0813129060 |access-date=October 16, 2016 |archive-date=June 12, 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20200612232828/https://books.google.com/books?id=ALT9c80ZIncC&pg=RA1-PA208&lpg=RA1-PA204&dq=%22the+outsiders%22+coppola+tulsa+northside&lr=&as_brr=3&ie=ISO-8859-1&output=html |url-status=live |last1=Phillips |first1=Gene D. |publisher=University Press of Kentucky}}</ref>

The original length of the film was 133 minutes. Warner Bros. felt that the film was a mistake and was too long.<ref name=Hoad/> As a result, Coppola cut it down to 91 minutes for the theatrical release.

The pranks that went on during the filming have become legendary, initiated mainly by [[Matt Dillon]], [[Rob Lowe]], [[Tom Cruise]], and [[Patrick Swayze]].<ref name="variety cast" /> The targets were often [[C. Thomas Howell]] and [[Diane Lane]].<ref name="variety cast" /> Ralph Macchio was not involved because he was so focused on getting his role right.<ref name="variety cast" /> The author of the original novel, S. E. Hinton, was involved during the filming as she and Coppola wrote the screenplay together; she appears as a nurse during a hospital scene in the film.<ref name="variety cast" /> She also later stated that she served as an informal "den mother" to many of the actors, as she was "close to all of them".<ref name="variety cast" />

==Reception==
===Box office===
In the United States and Canada, the film grossed $5.1 million from 829 theaters on its opening weekend, ranking second behind ''[[Spring Break (film)|Spring Break]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/1983W12/ |title=Domestic 1983 Weekend 12 (March 25–27, 1983) |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=March 13, 2026}}</ref> ''The Outsiders'' grossed $25.8 million worldwide.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0086066/ |title=The Outsiders (1983) |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=March 13, 2026}}</ref>

===Critical response===
Review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]] has a rating of 71% based on 52 reviews, with an average score of 6.3/10. The site's consensus reads, "The cracks continue to show in Coppola's directorial style, but ''The Outsiders'' remains a blustery, weird, and fun adaptation of the classic novel."<ref name="RT">{{cite web| title= The Outsiders|url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/outsiders/|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=January 28, 2025|archive-date=December 29, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201229043203/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/outsiders|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] rated the film two and a half out of four stars, citing problems with Coppola's vision, "the characters wind up like pictures, framed and hanging on the screen."<ref>{{cite web |last= Ebert |first= Roger |url= http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-outsiders-1983 |title=The Outsiders Movie Review & Film Summary (1983) |publisher= RogerEbert.com |date=March 25, 1983 |access-date= November 5, 2016 |archive-date=December 14, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161214143655/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-outsiders-1983 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a score of 45 out of 100, based on 18 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-outsiders|title=The Outsiders|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=January 28, 2020|archive-date=July 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200704202504/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-outsiders|url-status=live}}.</ref>

The film's casting directors, Janet Hirshenson and [[Jane Jenkins]], wrote in a 2007 book that the film's realistic portrayal of poor teenagers "created a new kind of filmmaking, especially about teenagers — a more naturalistic look at how young people talk, act, and experience the world. This movie was one of the few Hollywood offerings to deal realistically with kids from the wrong side of the tracks, and to portray honestly children whose parents had abused, neglected, or otherwise failed them."<ref>{{cite book | first1=Janet | last1=Hirshenson | first2=Jane | last2=Jenkins | title=A Star Is Found: Our Adventures Casting Some of Hollywood's Biggest Movies | url=https://archive.org/details/starisfoundourad00hirs | url-access=registration | page=[https://archive.org/details/starisfoundourad00hirs/page/86 86] | quote=the outsiders beach blanket bingo. | publisher=Houghton Mifflin Harcourt | date=November 5, 2007 | isbn=9780151012343 | access-date=November 5, 2016}}</ref>

Stéphane Delorme, in his book on Coppola, wrote: "''The Outsiders'' is a wonder. And wonder is also the subject of the film. 'Stay Gold', says the song over the title credits. [...] The artificiality of the rural setting, which is as fake as in ''[[The Night of the Hunter (film)|The Night of the Hunter]]'', places us in the distant, mythical past. It takes only dye to turn these blond heads into golden heads, and thus to go from nostalgia for one's youth in the 1960s to a general regret for a golden age."<ref>{{cite book |last=Delorme |first=Stéphane |title=Francis Ford Coppola |publisher=[[Cahiers du Cinéma]] |year=2010 |isbn=978-2-8664-2569-2 |edition= |pages=50}}</ref>

===Accolades===
''The Outsiders'' was nominated for four [[Young Artist Awards]], given annually since 1978 by the Young Artist Foundation. [[C. Thomas Howell]] won "Best Young Motion Picture Actor in a Feature Film". [[Diane Lane]] was nominated for "Best Young Supporting Actress in a Motion Picture". The film was nominated for "Best Family Feature Motion Picture".<ref>{{cite web|title=Young Artist Awards - 1984|url=https://www.imdb.com/event/ev0000716/1984|website=IMDb|ref=6|access-date=July 1, 2018|archive-date=May 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526150217/https://www.imdb.com/event/ev0000716/1984/1|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Francis Ford Coppola]] was nominated for the Golden Prize at the [[13th Moscow International Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Francis Ford Coppola Bio |url=http://www.mtv.com/artists/francis-ford-coppola/biography/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141119211627/http://www.mtv.com/artists/francis-ford-coppola/biography/ |archive-date=November 19, 2014 |access-date=September 16, 2014 |website=MTV |ref=7}}</ref>

==Aftermath and legacy==

=== "The Complete Novel" re-release ===
On September 20, 2005, Coppola re-released the film on DVD as a two-disc set called ''The Outsiders: The Complete Novel'', including 22 minutes of additional footage and an updated soundtrack. Coppola re-inserted some [[deleted scene]]s to make the film more faithful to the book. This brought it up to a 114-minute running time. At the beginning of the film, he added scenes where Ponyboy gets stalked and jumped, the gang talks about going to the movies, Sodapop and Ponyboy talk in their room, and Dallas, Ponyboy, and Johnny bum around before going to the movies. At the end, Coppola added the scenes taking place in court, Mr. Syme talking to Ponyboy, Sodapop, and Darry in the park.

Coppola's father, Carmine Coppola, had written a soaring, romantic score for the original release, which at the time Coppola felt may have been the wrong choice, but he was not prepared to say that to him.<ref name=Hoad/> By the time he came to recut the film for the "Complete Novel" re-release, his father had died, which allowed Coppola the opportunity to balance Carmine Coppola's music with music popular in the 1960s as well as new music composed by [[Michael Seifert (producer)|Michael Seifert]] and Dave Padrutt.

The film was re-rated by the [[MPAA]] as PG-13 for "violence, teen drinking and smoking, and some sexual references".<ref>{{cite news |last=Smith |first=Dinitia |date=September 12, 2005 |title=Inside an outsider |pages=42 |work=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=888&dat=20050912&id=YelRAAAAIBAJ&sjid=e3IDAAAAIBAJ&pg=4486,2824454&hl=en |url-status=live |access-date=May 21, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526150216/https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=888&dat=20050912&id=YelRAAAAIBAJ&sjid=e3IDAAAAIBAJ&pg=4486%2C2824454&hl=en |archive-date=May 26, 2021 |via=[[St. Petersburg Times]]}}</ref>

Disc 2 of the DVD includes some unique features, featuring behind-the-scenes interviews with the cast and crew, readings from the novel, additional deleted scenes, the original theatrical trailer, and an [[NBC News]] ''[[Today (American TV program)|Today]]'' segment from 1983 talking about how ''The Outsiders'' has inspired teenagers across the world.

The director also removed three scenes that were in the theatrical version to improve pacing. Those scenes were Ponyboy and Johnny looking at their reflections in the lake and talking about their hair, attempting to catch a rabbit, and playing poker. They can be found on the second disc as additional scenes, along with other deleted scenes that were filmed, but not put into the movie. In addition, Swayze, Macchio, Lane, and Howell gathered at Coppola's estate to watch the re-release, and their [[Audio commentary|commentary]] is included on the DVD. Dillon and Lowe provided separate commentary.

A Blu-ray edition of ''The Outsiders: The Complete Novel'' was released in Region A on June 3, 2014.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Outsiders-Blu-ray/43529/|title=The Outsiders Blu-ray|work=Blu-ray.com|access-date=March 28, 2014|archive-date=March 28, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140328152631/http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Outsiders-Blu-ray/43529/|url-status=live}}</ref>

A manufacture-on-demand [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] containing both versions of ''The Outsiders'' including The Complete Novel, was released through [[Studio Distribution Services]] on November 9, 2021.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://twitter.com/WarnerArchive/status/1455233254848598034|access-date= November 2, 2021 |title=From the Warner Archive Collection, classics are coming to Blu-ray this month! 🎞|date= November 1, 2021|author= Warner Archive Collection|website= [[Twitter]]}}</ref>

=== Sequel TV series ===
{{main|The Outsiders (American TV series)}}
A television series based on the characters of the novel and film aired in 1990. It consists of a different cast playing the same characters. It picks up right after the events of the film's ending and lasted only one season.

=== Stage musical adaptation ===
{{main|The Outsiders (musical)}}
A stage musical based on both the novel and film held its world premiere at [[La Jolla Playhouse]] in February–March 2023. The production was directed by [[Danya Taymor]] from a libretto by [[Adam Rapp]], with songs by [[Jamestown Revival]] and music supervision, arrangements, and orchestrations by Justin Levine.<ref>{{cite web |date=March 8, 2022 |title=The Outsiders Musical to Make World Premiere at la Jolla Playhouse |url=https://www.theatermania.com/san-diego-theater/news/the-outsiders-musical-world-premiere-la-jolla_93457.html |website=Theater Mania}}</ref> It was announced on August 21, 2023, that the show would begin previews on [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]] at the [[Bernard B. Jacobs Theatre]] on March 16. It officially opened on April 11. It received 12 nominations at the [[77th Tony Awards]], winning four including [[Tony Award for Best Musical|Best Musical]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Paulson |first=Michael |date=2024-06-17 |title='The Outsiders,' a Broadway Adaptation of the Classic Novel, Wins the Tony for Best Musical |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/06/16/theater/the-outsiders-best-musical-tony.html |access-date=2024-07-05 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

With the help of friends, the Oklahoma Film and Music Office, the City Council, local businesses, and individuals who volunteered, the restoration started.<ref name=":22">{{cite web |last=O'Shansky |first=Joe |date=August 4, 2016 |title=The Curtis House in Crutchfield |url=http://www.thetulsavoice.com/August-A-2016/The-Curtis-House-in-Crutchfield/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220515172908/https://www.tulsapeople.com/the-curtis-house-in-crutchfield/article_ae42c4d4-ff60-5aec-9e67-d91e5cc4c25d.html |archive-date=May 15, 2022 |access-date=June 6, 2017 |website=TulsaPeople Magazine}}</ref><ref name="tulsaworld.com">{{cite web |last1=Graham |first1=Ginnnie |date=April 2, 2016 |title=Ginnie Graham: Going inside 'The Outsiders' house |url=http://www.tulsaworld.com/news/columnists/ginniegraham/ginnie-graham-going-inside-the-outsiders-house/article_ca377a70-0045-57a6-b87d-d85a96be4562.html |access-date=June 6, 2017 |website=Tulsa World}}</ref> After enough funds had been raised, the house went through extensive renovations to restore it and maintain its authenticity from the film.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Terrell |first1=Ron |date=January 12, 2017 |title='Outsiders' House: The Latest On The Renovations |url=http://www.fox23.com/news/renovations-continue-on-north-tulsa-home-seen-in-the-outsiders-film/483560436 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220413013615/https://www.fox23.com/news/renovations-continue-on-north-tulsa-home-seen-in-the-outsiders-film/483560436/ |archive-date=April 13, 2022 |access-date=June 6, 2017 |website=Fox23 News}}</ref>

=== Set photography book ''The Outsiders ‘Rare and Unseen’'' and ''The Outsiders on set'' ===
In October 2022, O'Connor with The Outsiders House Museum published the book ''The Outsiders 'Rare and Unseen{{'}}'', which contains 148 photos by [[David Burnett (photojournalist)|David Burnett]], who was the set's photographer. O'Connor said: "We originally got the first lot of photos and then [Burnett] said there may be more. They found the rougher photos, and for me, that's where the rubber meets the road because they [the actors]'re unpolished, their guard's down, they're not posing."<ref>{{cite web |date=2022-10-11 |title=New book shows unseen images of 'The Outsiders' |url=https://www.fox23.com/news/new-book-shows-unseen-images-outsiders/FAKXLEI6WFFC7HODKB6XWZ6I4M/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221013220844/https://www.fox23.com/news/new-book-shows-unseen-images-outsiders/FAKXLEI6WFFC7HODKB6XWZ6I4M/ |archive-date=2022-10-13 |access-date=2022-10-13 |website=FOX23 News}}</ref>

In May 2024, O'Connor with The Outsiders House Museum published the book ''The Outsiders on set,'' which contains 254 pages of photos by Nancy Moran a photographer who visited ''The Outsiders''<nowiki/>'s set while they were shooting. O'Connor explained that when he discovered that this additional slew of photos existed, he approached Moran, who he didn't know, and to his surprise she let him have the negatives so that he could develop them.<ref>{{cite web |last=World |first=Jimmie Tramel Tulsa |date=2024-05-09 |title=Photos in new book show 'Outsiders' actors before they became famous |url=https://tulsaworld.com/life-entertainment/local/movies-tv/photos-in-new-book-show-outsiders-actors-before-they-became-famous/article_92dd67b4-0bae-11ef-ac13-27e60b1e57f6.html |access-date=2024-05-12 |website=Tulsa World}}</ref>
