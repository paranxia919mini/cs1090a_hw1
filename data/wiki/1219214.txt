'''''Dracula 2000''''' (also known as '''''Wes Craven Presents: Dracula 2000''''' and internationally as '''''Dracula 2001''''')<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.vampyreverse.com/about/alternate.shtml|title=vampyreverse, the|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160923012714/http://www.vampyreverse.com/about/alternate.shtml|archive-date=September 23, 2016|df=mdy-all}}</ref> is a 2000 American [[supernatural horror film]] co-written and directed by [[Patrick Lussier]], produced by [[Joel Soisson]], and starring [[Gerard Butler]] in the title role along with [[Christopher Plummer]], [[Jonny Lee Miller]], [[Justine Waddell]], [[Omar Epps]], [[Vitamin C (singer)|Colleen Fitzpatrick]], [[Jeri Ryan]], and [[Jennifer Esposito]]. Its plot follows [[Count Dracula]], who, after having escaped captivity by [[Abraham Van Helsing]] over a century following the events of the novel ''[[Dracula]],'' arrives in [[New Orleans|New Orleans, Louisiana]] in the 21st century and seeks out Mary Heller, the modern-day daughter of Van Helsing. The screenplay incorporates a theological subplot regarding Dracula's origins, revealing him to be [[Judas Iscariot]].<ref name=Donaldson/>

Principal photography of ''Dracula 2000'' took place on location in New Orleans and [[Toronto]] in the summer of 2000. The film had a rushed post-production process, with reshoots and editing occurring in the fall of 2000, shortly before the studio's December release deadline. ''Dracula 2000'' was released theatrically in the United States and Canada on December 22, 2000. It received largely negative reviews from critics, and was a [[box-office bomb]], grossing $47.1 million against a $54 million budget.

''Dracula 2000'' was followed by two [[direct-to-video]] sequels, ''[[Dracula II: Ascension|Ascension]]'' in 2003 and ''[[Dracula III: Legacy|Legacy]]'' in 2005. Series director Lussier and Joel Soisson, who co-wrote all three films, created a plot for a fourth film and discussed releasing it theatrically, but the film was not produced.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/16438 |title=BD Horror News - Patrick Lussier Talks Fourth 'Dracula' Film|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120930044920/https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/16438/ |archive-date=September 30, 2012|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Plot==
In 2000 [[London]], wealthy antiquary Matthew Van Helsing runs an antique shop on the site of Carfax Abbey, a business inherited from his grandfather, the [[Dutch people|Dutch]] physician and polymath [[Abraham Van Helsing]]. Solina, Van Helsing’s American secretary, orchestrates a heist with her boyfriend, Marcus, and their crew—Trick, Nightshade, Dax, and Eddie—to infiltrate the shop's fortified high-tech vault in search of riches. Instead, they discover a sealed silver coffin. While attempting to open the coffin, Eddie and Dax are impaled by spikes, spraying the coffin with blood and alerting Van Helsing to their presence. Assuming the coffin must contain Van Helsing's valuables, the group escapes with it aboard a plane to [[New Orleans]], and Van Helsing pursues them with his apprentice, Simon Sheppard, in tow.

Aboard their plane, Nightshade unlocks the coffin, revealing the withered body of [[Count Dracula|Dracula]]. Having been awakened by the blood on his coffin, Dracula attacks and vampirizes Solina and her group. Rejuvenated, Dracula shares a telepathic link with Mary Heller, an English expatriate who sees him as he sees her. Following a [[Louisiana]] swamp crash, Dracula emerges to vampirize anchorwoman Valerie Sharpe after killing her cameraman, while local authorities take the other victims to a makeshift morgue. He then travels to New Orleans to find Mary. Haunted by dreams of an unknown Dracula, Mary clings to her resentment toward the father she blames for her late mother’s mental decline.

Upon arrival, Van Helsing and Simon thin the vampire ranks, but Dracula infiltrates a police station to claim Solina, leaving a trail of slaughtered officers in their wake. Van Helsing confesses he is the 19th-century original; having survived since Dracula's 1897 attacks,{{efn|These attacks correspond to the events of [[Bram Stoker]]’s 1897 novel, ''[[Dracula]]'', which serves as the canonical backstory for the film.}} he has cheated death for over a hundred years by harvesting the vampire's blood via leeches and hiding behind his own family tree. Despite a century of effort, Van Helsing cannot kill Dracula, the primordial vampire who reviles [[God]], [[silver]], and [[Christian culture|Christian cultures]]. Conceived after his prolongation began, Mary is Van Helsing’s contemporary daughter—a child born into a shared bloodline with the vampire himself. Petrified by his true identity, Van Helsing’s wife fled with Mary to escape both the monster and the man, fearing the latent vampirism taking root in her daughter's blood; however, Van Helsing secretly tracked their every move and grieved in silence after his wife's death.

Dracula is led to Mary's home by her friend Lucy Westerman, whom he seduces and vampirizes. There, he confronts and kills Van Helsing. On her return, Mary finds her father's carcass and is ambushed by Dracula and his brides: Solina, Valerie, and Lucy. Simon rushes Mary into a church to escape Dracula, but the vampire easily invades the sanctuary. Though they escape into a cemetery, Dracula intercepts them and abducts Mary, craving a born equal over a mere sired thrall.

On a rooftop, Dracula transforms Mary and reveals that he is [[Judas Iscariot]], the [[Apostles in the New Testament|Apostle]] who betrayed [[Jesus|Jesus Christ]] to the [[Sanhedrin]] for a bribe of [[thirty pieces of silver]]. After Jesus was crucified, Judas hanged himself in contrition, only to be cursed by God with eternal life as the world’s first vampire. Dracula blames Jesus for his damnation because he had [[Jesus predicts his betrayal|foreknowledge of his betrayal]] and failed to avert the prophecy. Seeking revenge, the fallen Apostle aims to spite Christ by causing the world to be overrun by vampires. Dracula's immortality is a choice; he refuses to seek the redemption required to end his eternal life. After Simon kills Valerie, he is subdued by Solina and Lucy and brought to Dracula, who offers his blood to Mary. Mary feigns biting Simon, then turns on the brides and stabs Dracula, proving her free will remains intact.

Matched in strength, Mary loops a crucifix cable around Dracula's neck and drags him off the roof. As the sun consumes him, Dracula looks to the crucifix as Judas, freeing Mary from her curse before finally perishing.

Returning to the [[United Kingdom]] to claim the Van Helsing estate, Mary assumes watch over Dracula's remains with Simon steadfastly at her side. Despite being able to stand in the sunlight without burning pain, Mary's eyes briefly flicker with an eerie glow, suggesting she has not fully returned to normal.

==Production==
===Development===
While finalizing [[post-production]] on ''[[Scream 3]]'', [[Bob Weinstein]] approached editor [[Patrick Lussier]] on a modernized take on [[Count Dracula|Dracula]] entitled ''Dracula 2000''. When Lussier asked Weinstein what the film was about, Weinstein responded "I don't know." [[Joel Soisson]], having written under Lussier for ''[[The Prophecy 3: The Ascent]]'', was brought on board to flesh out the film. The filmmakers quickly drafted a screenplay by January 2000, which envisioned a smaller scale story than Weinstein had hoped for.<ref name="Dracula 2000 EW">{{cite magazine|last=Ascher-Walsh|first=Rebecca|url=https://ew.com/article/2000/11/17/making-dracula-2000/|title=The Making of ''Dracula 2000''|date=November 17, 2000|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241223014447/https://ew.com/article/2000/11/17/making-dracula-2000/|archive-date=December 23, 2024}}</ref> Soisson's initial pitch, which included a vampire hunting priest, would ultimately be recycled for the sequel, ''[[Dracula II: Ascension]]''.<ref>{{cite web|author=The Arrow|url=https://www.joblo.com/interview-patrick-lussier-2/|title=Interview: Patrick Lussier|date=July 11, 2003|website=[[JoBlo.com]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241222054135/https://www.joblo.com/interview-patrick-lussier-2/|archive-date=December 22, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Condit|first=Jon|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/3331/lussier-patrick-dracula-trilogy/|title=Patrick Lussier (''Dracula'' Trilogy)|date=July 14, 2005|website=[[Dread Central]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250114130552/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/3331/lussier-patrick-dracula-trilogy/|archive-date=January 14, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> Inspired by the prior [[Hammer Horror]] entries, particularly ''[[Dracula A.D. 1972]]'', Lussier attempted to employ similar methods for his film.<ref>{{cite web|last=O'Brien|first=Jon|url=https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/dracula-2000-y2k-anniversary|title=''Dracula 2000'' Captured the Spirit of Y2K Better Than Any Other Movie|date=December 16, 2000|website=SYFY Wire|accessdate=June 22, 2023}}</ref> Lussier and Soisson also sought to differentiate their Dracula from other adaptations. The director took note of the character's scorn for [[Christianity|Christian]] iconography, ultimately linking him to [[Judas Iscariot]].<ref name=Donaldson>{{cite web|url=https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/dracula-2000-25-year-anniversary|work=[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]|title=The Dumbest Dracula Movie Wasted A Brilliant Idea|last=Donaldson|first=Kayleigh|date=December 22, 2025|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251223221150/https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/dracula-2000-25-year-anniversary|archive-date=December 23, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite AV media|title=''Making of'' Dracula 2000|publisher=Dimension Home Video|year=2001|medium=Documentary}}</ref>

[[Wes Craven]] served as an executive producer on the project. Craven's name was used heavily in marketing, as done on previous films such as ''[[Wishmaster (film)|Wishmaster]]'' and ''[[Don't Look Down (1998 film)|Don't Look Down]]''.<ref>{{cite web|last=Korngut|first=Josh|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/421808/they-is-the-ultimate-forgotten-wes-craven-presents-horror-movie/|title=''They'' Is The Ultimate Forgotten Wes Craven Presents Horror Movie|date=January 13, 2022|website=[[Dread Central]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241223020454/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/421808/they-is-the-ultimate-forgotten-wes-craven-presents-horror-movie/|archive-date=December 23, 2024}}</ref> [[Ehren Kruger]] performed a script polish, while [[Scott Derrickson]] and his writing partner [[Paul Harris Boardman]] were enlisted for a rewrite just two weeks before production.<ref name="This week in Hollywood">{{Cite news |last=Ascher-Walsh |first=Rebecca |date=June 16, 2000 |title=This week in Hollywood |language=en-US |newspaper=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |url=http://www.ew.com/article/2000/06/16/this-week-hollywood |url-status=dead  |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170104101930/http://ew.com/article/2000/06/16/this-week-hollywood-58/ |archive-date=2017-01-04}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Seibold|first=Witney|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/786251/scott-derrickson-reveals-one-of-the-most-upsetting-moments-of-his-filmmaking-career/|title=Scott Derrickson Reveals One Of The Most Upsetting Moments Of His Filmmaking Career|date=March 2, 2022|website=Slashfilm|accessdate=June 22, 2023}}</ref><ref name="The Devil Is Real. ===Casting===
During [[pre-production]] in [[Toronto]], Lussier had already [[blocking (stage)|blocked]] several scenes using assistants. [[Christopher Plummer]], [[Jennifer Esposito]], [[Colleen Ann Fitzpatrick|Colleen Fitzpatrick]], [[Omar Epps]], [[Justine Waddell]], [[Jonny Lee Miller]], and [[Jeri Ryan]] arrived on set, but no actor had been cast in the title role. [[Gerard Butler]] would be cast as Dracula only two days before filming commenced.<ref name="Dracula 2000 EW" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2000/film/news/craven-s-dracula-hires-a-butler-1117782921/|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|title=Craven’s ‘Dracula’ hires a Butler|last=Lyons|first=Charles|date=June 22, 2000|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260815233043/https://variety.com/2000/film/news/craven-s-dracula-hires-a-butler-1117782921/|archive-date=August 15, 2026}}</ref>

===Filming===
Filming took place in [[New Orleans]] and Toronto, beginning on June 26, 2000, and wrapping on September 6.<ref>{{cite web|last=Vejvoda|first=Tim|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2000/08/01/the-stax-report-script-review-of-dracula-2000|title=The Stax Report: Script Review of ''Dracula 2000''|date=August 1, 2000|website=IGN|accessdate=June 22, 2023}}</ref><ref name="Dracula 2000 EW" /> A rough cut of the film was assembled in only six days. [[Additional photography]] occurred on October 23 and spanned eight days.<ref name="Dracula 2000 EW" /> [[Nathan Fillion]] was added during the reshoots, filming his brief scenes on [[Halloween night]].<ref>{{cite AV media|last1=Lussier|first1=Patrick|last2=Soisson|first2=Joel|title=Dracula 2000 ''Audio Commentary''|date=2001|publisher=Dimension Home Video|medium=Audio Commentary}}</ref> In November, the [[Writers Guild of America]] ruled that Soisson would receive sole screenplay credit and share story credit with Lussier.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://directories.wga.org/project/820497/dracula-2000/|title=''Dracula 2000'' Writing Credits|date=November 1, 2000|website=WGA Directory|accessdate=June 22, 2023}}</ref> The final cut of the film was expected to be delivered on December 12, 2000, a mere ten days before its theatrical release. Speaking to ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', Craven compared ''Dracula 2000'''s rushed production to his time working on ''[[Scream 2]]'', which was released in December 1997 after being filmed between June and August of that year. Craven said "What's the benefit of making movies so fast? There's no benefit whatsoever. It's terribly difficult, and completely unnecessary."<ref name="Dracula 2000 EW" />

==Release==
''Dracula 2000'' premiered in the United States on December 22, 2000.<ref>{{cite news|last=Hart|first=Hugh|date=December 21, 2000|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/203705996/|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|title=The Resurrection of the Undead|page=6|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

===Home media===
''Dracula 2000'' was first released on home video in the United States by Dimension Home Video on July 17, 2001. The DVD edition included a variety of special features, such as deleted and extended scenes, a behind-the-scenes featurette, and auditions, including those featuring Gerard Butler. The release also offered an audio commentary with director Patrick Lussier and screenwriter Joel Soisson. The DVD was presented in a 2.35:1 widescreen aspect ratio with English and French 5.1 Dolby Digital audio options.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/review/2701|title=Dracula 2000|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231007180503/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/review/2701|archive-date=October 7, 2023|date=July 3, 2001|last=Arrington|first=Chuck|url-status=live|website=[[DVD Talk]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Dracula 2000 (2000) |url=http://www.dvdmg.com/dracula2000.shtml |work=DVD Movie Guide|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260410122244/http://www.dvdmg.com/dracula2000.shtml|archive-date=April 10, 2026|last=Jacobson|first=Colin|date=2001}}</ref> A VHS release accompanied the DVD, also distributed by Dimension Home Video on July 17, 2001. The VHS version included the film in its theatrical aspect ratio.

Dimension Films was sold by [[The Walt Disney Company]] in 2005, with its parent label [[Miramax]] then being sold by Disney in 2010. That same year, private equity firm [[Filmyard Holdings]] took control of Miramax and the pre-October 2005 library of Dimension.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/business/2010/jul/30/film-industry-privateequity|title=Disney sells Miramax to investment group for $660m|first=David|last=Teather|date=July 30, 2010|work=[[The Guardian]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221207161227/https://www.theguardian.com/business/2010/jul/30/film-industry-privateequity|archive-date=December 7, 2022}}</ref> Filmyard sublicensed the home media rights for several Dimension and Miramax films to [[Echo Bridge Entertainment]]. ==Reception==
===Box office===
On its opening weekend, ''Dracula 2000'' released alongside ''[[Cast Away]]'', ''[[The Family Man]]'', ''[[The Gift (2000 film)|The Gift]]'', ''[[Miss Congeniality (film)|Miss Congeniality]]'', ''[[O Brother, Where Art Thou?]]'', and ''[[Thirteen Days (film)|Thirteen Days]]''. The studio hoped the film would [[Counterprogramming (film distribution)|counterprogram]] against the "season's romantic comedies and dramas".<ref name="Dracula 2000 EW" />

The film opened at #7 in its first week at the box office with $8,636,567. In its second week, the film had a 56.5% drop-off, but hung onto the #8 spot. The film grossed $33,022,767 domestically and $14,030,858 overseas for a worldwide total of $47,053,625, failing to make back its $54 million budget.<ref name="mojo">{{cite web|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=dracula2000.htm|title=Dracula 2000|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120130175112/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=dracula2000.htm|archive-date=January 30, 2012|df=mdy-all}}</ref> ''Dracula 2000'' was the sixth-highest-grossing film for Miramax/Dimension Films in 2000, exceeding the box office takes of such expensive Dimension Films releases like ''[[Reindeer Games]]'', as well as Miramax's December opener for that year, ''[[All the Pretty Horses (film)|All the Pretty Horses]]''.<ref name=mojo/>

===Critical response===
The film received generally negative reviews from critics. [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reports a rating of 19% based on 70 reviews, with an average score of 3.59/10. The site's consensus states: "This retelling tries to offer a different spin on the origin of Dracula. Unfortunately, there's nothing here audiences haven't seen before".<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dracula_2000/ | title = ''Dracula 2000'' Movie Reviews, Pictures | access-date = March 3, 2025 | website = [[Rotten Tomatoes]] | url-status = live | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090327142353/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dracula_2000/ | archive-date = March 27, 2009 | df = mdy-all}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a 26 out of 100 rating, based on 14 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.metacritic.com/movie/dracula-2000 | title = Wes Craven Presents: Dracula 2000 (2000): Reviews | access-date = July 9, 2009 | publisher = [[Metacritic]] | url-status = live | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20100904045301/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/dracula-2000 | archive-date = September 4, 2010 | df = mdy-all}}</ref>

Berge Garabedian of [[JoBlo.com|JoBlo]] offered a positive review, calling it "A fun vampire movie", "a novel adaptation of an old time legend", and "[good] for pretty much anyone looking for some enjoyable bloody fun".<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.joblo.com/reviews.php?mode=joblo_movies&id=776 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20110606152614/http://www.joblo.com/reviews.php?mode=joblo_movies&id=776 |url-status=dead|title=JoBlo's movie review of Dracula 2000: Justine Waddell, Christopher Plummer, Gerard Butler |archive-date=June 6, 2011|website=[[JoBlo.com]]|date=December 20, 2000|last= Garabedian|first=Berge}}</ref> BeyondHollywood.com wrote: "''Dracula 2000'' is not the worst vampire movie I've seen, but it's definitely not the best either. There are some very good moments, most of them featuring the frail Van Helsing as he attempts to battle the fast and deadly vampires. Also, I appreciated the background given to Dracula's aversion to silver, crosses, and God, as well as Dracula's 'true' origins. Not bad work, but it could have been much better".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.beyondhollywood.com/dracula-2000-2000-movie-review/|title=Dracula 2000 (2000) Movie Review|date=May 16, 2003|website=BeyondHollywood.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090503154122/http://www.beyondhollywood.com/dracula-2000-2000-movie-review/|archive-date=May 3, 2009|df=mdy-all|access-date=July 1, 2009}}</ref>

[[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave the film a "C−" score,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,93618~1~~,00.html|title= Wes Craven Presents: ''Dracula 2000'' |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090703074608/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,93618~1~~,00.html |archive-date=July 3, 2009|last=Gleiberman|first=Owen|author-link=Owen Gleiberman|work=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date= January 3, 2001}}</ref> while [[James Berardinelli]] of ReelViews panned the film, writing: "Of all the indignities to have been visited upon Dracula during the past century (including being the [[General Mills monster-themed breakfast cereals|"inspiration" for a cereal]] and a ''[[Sesame Street]]'' [[Count von Count|character]], and being [[Dracula: Dead and Loving It|lampooned by Mel Brooks]]), none is more unsettling than what has happened to the world's most famous vampire in ''Dracula 2000''".<ref>{{cite web|author=Berardinelli, James|url=https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/d/dracula2000.html|title=''Dracula 2000''|author-link=James Berardinelli|work=ReelViews|date=2000|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260214025802/https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/d/dracula2000.html|archive-date=February 14, 2026}}</ref> Jane Crowther of the [[BBC]] gave the film 1/5 stars, saying it was "Destined to enter the so-bad-it's-good hall of fame" and "a prime example of that old remake adage; if you can't do it better or different – don't do it at all."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Crowther |first=Jane |date=May 18, 2001 |title=Dracula 2001 |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/05/18/dracula_2001_review.shtml|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260107140623/https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2001/05/18/dracula_2001_review.shtml|archive-date=January 7, 2026|url-status=live|website=[[BBC]]}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{main|Dracula 2000 (soundtrack)}}
The film's [[Rock music|rock]] and [[Metal music|metal]] soundtrack includes [[Powerman 5000]]'s song "[[Powerman 5000|Ultra Mega]]", [[Linkin Park]]'s song "[[One Step Closer (Linkin Park song)|One Step Closer]]", [[Pantera]]'s song "[[Dracula 2000 (soundtrack)|Avoid the Light]]", [[System of a Down]]'s cover of [[Berlin (band)|Berlin]]'s "[[The Metro (song)|The Metro]]", [[Slayer]]'s song "Bloodline" and [[Disturbed (band)|Disturbed]]'s song "A Welcome Burden".
