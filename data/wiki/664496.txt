'''''The Last Seduction''''' is a 1994 American [[neo-noir]] [[erotic thriller]] film directed by [[John Dahl]] and starring [[Linda Fiorentino]], [[Peter Berg]], and [[Bill Pullman]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1994/10/26/movies/film-review-the-last-seduction-a-femme-fatale-who-lives-up-to-the-description.html|last=Maslin|first=Janet|author-link=Janet Maslin|title=THE LAST SEDUCTION; A Femme Fatale Who Lives Up To the Description|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=October 26, 1994|access-date=November 22, 2025|url-access=registration|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141231071309/https://www.nytimes.com/1994/10/26/movies/film-review-the-last-seduction-a-femme-fatale-who-lives-up-to-the-description.html|archive-date=December 31, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> It was produced by [[ITC Entertainment]] and distributed by [[October Films]]. 

Fiorentino's performance garnered acclaim and generated talk of an [[Academy Award]] nomination, but she was deemed ineligible because the film was broadcast in July 1994 on [[HBO]] before its theatrical release on October 26. October Films and ITC Entertainment sued the [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]] but were unable to make Fiorentino eligible for a nomination.<ref name="CS">{{cite web |last=Bloomenthal |first=Andrew |date=September 10, 2015 |title=Seduced by Steve Barancik: The Last Seduction |url=https://creativescreenwriting.com/seduced-by-steve-barancik-the-last-seduction/ |access-date=September 10, 2015 |website=Creative Screenwriting}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title='Last Seduction' Sues For Oscar Eligibility |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |agency=[[Associated Press]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1994/12/21/arts/last-seduction-sues-for-oscar-eligibility.html |date=1994-12-21 |access-date=2022-10-09 |url-access=registration |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121226215559/https://www.nytimes.com/1994/12/21/arts/last-seduction-sues-for-oscar-eligibility.html |archive-date=December 26, 2012 |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
Bridget Gregory works as a telemarketing manager in New York City. Her husband, Clay, a physician in debt to a [[loan shark]], sells pharmaceutical cocaine to drug dealers for $700,000. At home, Clay slaps Bridget after she insults him. She flees their apartment with the money, headed to Chicago.

Bridget stops in Beston, a small town near [[Buffalo, New York|Buffalo]]. She has sex with Mike Swale, a local man back from a whirlwind marriage in Buffalo, which he refuses to talk about. Bridget changes her name to Wendy Kroy, and takes a job at the insurance company where Mike works. They begin a relationship, but Bridget refuses to tell Mike about her past.

When Mike tells her how to tell if a man is cheating on his wife by reading his [[credit report]]s, Bridget devises a plan based on selling murders to cheated wives. When Mike objects, they argue and he leaves. A [[private detective]] hired by Clay, Harlan, holds Bridget at gunpoint in her car. Bridget tricks him into removing his seatbelt, then purposely crashes her car, killing him. As Harlan was black, she takes advantage of local racism to persuade the police to close the case without investigation.

Bridget lies to Mike that she has traveled to Florida to kill Lance Collier, an abusive husband. Instead, she goes to Buffalo to meet Mike's ex-wife, Trish. Upon returning, Bridget shows Mike the money she stole from Clay, claiming it is her cut of the life insurance payout from the Florida job. She tells Mike they can start a life together with the money, but insists he must murder a tax lawyer in New York who is cheating elderly women out of their homes. When he refuses, Bridget forges a letter from Trish saying she is moving to Beston. Desperate to avoid Trish, Mike accepts the job.

Bridget swaps the name on Clay's door with the name of the attorney Mike believes he has been sent to kill. Mike enters the apartment and handcuffs Clay. Clay realizes what is happening when Mike mentions Bridget's alias and shows Mike a photo of himself and Bridget together. They form a plan to double-cross her. When Bridget arrives, Clay tries to make amends with her, but she empties a canister of chemical mace<ref name="LastSeductionMace">{{cite AV media |people=Dahl, John (Director) |date=October 26, 1994 |title=The Last Seduction |medium=Motion picture |publisher=October Films |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HLbq2SA_XHU&t=6045s |access-date=April 28, 2026 |time=1:40:45 |quote=Bridget Gregory (Linda Fiorentino) utilizes a canister of Chemical Mace to incapacitate and eventually kill Clay Gregory (Bill Pullman) by discharging the irritant directly into his airway.}}</ref> down his throat, killing him. She tells a stunned Mike to "rape" her. When he refuses, she tells him she knows that Trish is transgender and goads him with homophobic slurs. While Mike has rough sex with her, she dials 911 and allows them to listen, incriminating Mike. He is jailed for rape and murder, while Bridget escapes with Clay's money and multimillion-dollar life insurance payout. In a limousine, she burns the apartment label, the only evidence linking her to Clay's death.

==Production==
Screenwriter [[Steve Barancik]] said he believed the film was originally pitched to [[ITC Entertainment]] as a "standard [[Skinemax]]" low-budget film, even though the filmmakers had "an under-the-radar intention to make a good movie."<ref name=CS/> [[Linda Fiorentino]] said of accepting the role, "After I read that script, I was in Arizona and I got in a car and drove six hours to get to the meeting because I had never read anything so unique in terms of a female character. And I walked in the meeting with [[John Dahl]], the director, and I said, 'John, you are not allowed to hire anyone but me for this film.' And I wasn't kidding."<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Linda Fiorentino Finds Good Ways to Be Bad |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/fiorentino-finds-good-ways-to-be-bad |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |date=October 8, 1995 |access-date=October 9, 2022 |via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref>

ITC Entertainment executives were upset with a scene in which Fiorentino is dressed as a [[cheerleader]] and wears [[suspenders]] over her breasts. Barancik recalled, "Apparently, a guy from the company who was monitoring things and watching the dailies saw the suspenders over Linda's nipples and shouted out, 'Are we making an [[Art film|art movie]]?!' He shut down production and called the principals of the movie on the carpet, and they all had to pledge that they had no artistic pretensions."<ref name=CS/> The scene was cut, and the sexual role-playing theme was lost.<ref name=CS/>

[[Peter Berg]] said it was Fiorentino's idea to shoot the chain-link-fence sex scene in that way. "She said, 'John, get a camera,' and she climbed up on me against that fence and told John Dahl to shoot it, and that was the scene. She thought of it, she conceived it, she executed it. It was awesome."<ref>{{cite web |last=Vineyard |first=Jennifer |url=https://www.vulture.com/2014/12/linda-fiorentino-sex-scene-last-seduction-peter-berg.html |title=Peter Berg on His Wild The Last Seduction Sex Scenes With Linda Fiorentino |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |date=December 3, 2014 |access-date=5 March 2022 |url-access=limited |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150130054403/https://www.vulture.com/2014/12/linda-fiorentino-sex-scene-last-seduction-peter-berg.html |archive-date=January 30, 2015 |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
''The Last Seduction'' received critical acclaim. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 94% based on 50 reviews, with an average rating of 8/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "Like ''[[Body Heat]]'', ''The Last Seduction'' updates film noir techniques for a modern era, imbuing this erotic film with '90s snark."<ref>{{cite web |title=The Last Seduction |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_last_seduction |access-date=November 22, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], which assigns a [[weighted average]] score out of 100 to reviews from mainstream critics, the film received an average score of 85, based on 12 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web|title=The Last Seduction|website=[[Metacritic]]|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-last-seduction |access-date=July 6, 2021}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] gave the film four out of four stars, highlighting Fiorentino's ability to project her character with [[dry humor]] and a freedom from Hollywood conventions typically surrounding a female antagonist.<ref name="ebert"/> Ebert later ranked the film fifth on his year-end list of 1994's best films.<ref name="Ebert-1994"/> He wrote in the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'':

{{blockquote|John Dahl's ''The Last Seduction'' knows how much we enjoy seeing a character work boldly outside the rules. It gives us a diabolical, evil woman, and goes the distance with her. We keep waiting for the movie to lose its nerve, and it never does: This woman is bad from beginning to end, she never reforms, she never compromises, and the movie doesn't tack on one of those contrived conclusions where the morals squad comes in and tidies up.<ref name="ebert">{{cite news|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|date=November 18, 1994|url=http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-last-seduction-1994|title=The Last Seduction Movie Review (1994)|newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|access-date=June 28, 2017|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref>}}

Director [[Quentin Tarantino]] rated ''The Last Seduction'' at number 77 of his favorite films.<ref>{{cite web|last=Bose|first=Swapnil Dhruv|url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/every-film-recommended-by-quentin-tarantino/|title=A list of every film ever recommended by Quentin Tarantino|website=Far Out|date=July 6, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220706165636/https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/every-film-recommended-by-quentin-tarantino/|archive-date=July 6, 2022|url-status=live}}</ref>
