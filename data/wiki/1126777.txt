'''''Nightbreed''''' is a 1990 American [[dark fantasy]] film written and directed by [[Clive Barker]], based on his 1988 [[novella]] ''[[Cabal (novella)|Cabal]]''.  It stars [[Craig Sheffer]], [[Anne Bobby]], [[David Cronenberg]], [[Charles Haid]], [[Hugh Quarshie]], and [[Doug Bradley]]. The film follows an unstable mental patient named Aaron Boone who is falsely led to believe by his doctor that he is a serial killer. Tracked down by the police, his doctor, and his girlfriend Lori, Boone eventually finds refuge in an abandoned cemetery called Midian among a tribe of [[monster]]s and outcasts known as the "Nightbreed" who hide from humanity.

At the time of its release, the film was a commercial and critical failure. In several interviews, Barker protested that the film company tried to sell it as a standard [[slasher film]],<ref>{{cite journal|last=Timpone|first=Anthony|title=Barker Bites Back|journal=Fangoria Horror Spectacular|year=1990|issue=1|url=http://www.clivebarker.info/nightbreedb.html|access-date=22 March 2012}}</ref> and that the powers-that-be had no real working knowledge of ''Nightbreed''{{'}}s story.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Salisbury|first=Mark|title=Flesh And Fury|journal=Fear|date=October 1990|issue=22|url=http://www.clivebarker.info/nightbreed.html}}</ref> Since its initial theatrical release, ''Nightbreed'' has become a [[cult film]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Interview|url=http://www.clivebarker.info/morenightbreed.html|publisher=The Official Clive Barker Resource: Revelations|access-date=22 March 2012|first1=Amber|last1=Black|first2=Tim|last2=Trautmann|year=1996|quote=The movie has, and this is wonderful, you know, a lot of fans. And it's a cult movie and it's a real pleasure to me.}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Halley|first=Stefan|title=10 Films that have gone from Crap to Cult|url=http://www.eatsleeplivefilm.com/the-list-10-films-that-have-gone-from-crap-to-cult/|publisher=Eat Sleep Live Film|access-date=22 March 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121123024242/http://www.eatsleeplivefilm.com/the-list-10-films-that-have-gone-from-crap-to-cult/|archive-date=November 23, 2012|url-status=usurped}}</ref>

Over time, Barker expressed disappointment with the [[Final cut privilege|final cut]] approved by the studio and always longed for the recovery of the reels so the film might be re-edited. In 2014, original film elements for the cut material were re-obtained and were edited into a [[director's cut]], released through [[Scream Factory]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Films Still To Come...?|url=http://www.clivebarker.info/newfilmsd.html|publisher=The Official Clive Barker Resource: Revelations|access-date=22 March 2012|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050512074322/http://www.clivebarker.info/newfilmsd.html|archive-date=May 12, 2005|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

==Plot==
Aaron Boone has recurring nightmares of a place called Midian. His girlfriend Lori Winston encourages him to resume [[psychotherapy]] with Dr. Phillip Decker. Meanwhile, a serial killer has been massacring entire families. When Boone returns to Decker's office, he is horrified to learn that his dreams mirror these ritualistic killings. Decker tells Boone to turn himself in and start taking [[lithium (medication)|lithium]], but he actually gives Boone [[Lysergic acid diethylamide|LSD]].

Boone is hospitalized during the resulting [[Psychedelic experience|trip]]. Another patient is ranting about Midian and explains where it is. When the patient peels his face off, Boone escapes from the hospital during the commotion and heads to Midian. All he finds there is a graveyard where he falls asleep. At night, he is confronted by monstrous beings who argue over whether he belongs there. Boone claims he is a murderer, but they know he is not. One of them tries to eat Boone, but he escapes.

Outside the graveyard, Boone is confronted by the police who have been led there by Dr. Decker. He tricks the police into shooting Boone. In the morgue, Boone is revived by the bite he received in the graveyard. He returns to Midian and learns about the Nightbreed who live there. He is inducted into their society through the blood of their deity [[Baphomet]].

Lori follows Boone's path to Midian. She is followed there by Decker who is the serial killer. He framed Boone for the murders in an attempt to locate the Nightbreed. He tries to lure Boone out of Midian by using Lori, but Boone rescues her. The Nightbreed explain that they are just creatures from [[folklore]] who used to live in peace until humans tried to extinguish them. They banish Lori and Boone from Midian.

They check into a motel but find that Decker has been there and slaughtered people in the room next door. Boone is arrested for Decker's murders, and the doctor convinces the police to storm Midian. His ultimate goal is to destroy the Nightbreed.

While the police head to Midian, several Nightbreed break Boone out of jail and enlist his help. A chaotic battle ensues between the Nightbreed and the police. Decker confronts Boone and is killed. Boone seeks out Baphomet, and he explains that Boone has caused the end of Midian, which had been foretold. Baphomet charges Boone with finding a new home for the Nightbreed and renames him Cabal.

Determined to stay with Boone, Lori commits suicide and Boone resurrects her with a bite. He promises the Nightbreed that he will follow Baphomet's orders.

In the ruins of Midian, Ashberry stands in front of Decker's corpse and states that he wants vengeance on Baphomet and the Breed. When he presses Baphomet's blood to Decker's wound, Decker springs back to life with a scream as Ashberry repeatedly shouts "Hallelujah!"

=== Alternative endings ===
==== The Cabal Cut ====
In ''The Cabal Cut'', the resurrection of Decker plays as a [[post-credits scene]].

==== Director's Cut ====
In the alternative ending used in ''The Cabal Cut'' and ''Director's Cut'' of the film, Narcisse is killed earlier in the battle by Decker, so he is not present during the subsequent events. The Nightbreed await Boone in a barn whilst Boone says his goodbyes to Lori, as he must find a new home for the Nightbreed. Boone promises to return to her, but knowing that Boone will retain his youth and immortality as she grows old, Lori suddenly stabs herself, forcing Boone to resurrect her as a Nightbreed. They profess their love for one another and begin their journey.

Meanwhile, Captain Eigerman wanders the underground remains of the cemetery where he stumbles upon the transformed Ashberry, who longs for revenge after his burning by Baphomet. Eigerman shares this desire, but Ashberry rejects Eigerman's offer, kills him, and starts his hunt for the Nightbreed.

The surviving Nightbreed watch Boone and Lori in the distance. Rachel tells Babette that Boone will return soon, perhaps the next day, to lead them to a new haven. Boone and Lori now appear together as part of a prophecy in a Nightbreed painting.

==Themes and interpretations==

''Nightbreed'' has been characterized as containing themes related to [[queerness]] and the [[LGBT]] community. In 1997, author [[Harry M. Benshoff]] called it, "One of the first horror films to make an explicit connection between monsters and the activist politics of the queer community".<ref name="Benshoff 1997" />

Filmmaker [[Alejandro Jodorowsky]] called ''Nightbreed'' "the first truly gay horror fantasy epic", explaining how the unconsummated relationship [[Doctor–patient relationship|between doctor and patient]] is in his view the central theme.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20120708124853/http://www.everythingisundercontrol.org/nagtloper/write/dyinglight.php Between Death and the Devil: The Unofficial Richard Stanley Website - Dying Light: An obituary for the great British horror movie (originally appeared in British Horror Cinema, 2001)] everythingisundercontrol.org on [[Wayback Machine]]</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://shadowtheatre13.com/thethreemothers12.html |title=Lachrymae - The Trail of the Three Mothers |publisher=Shadowtheatre13.com |access-date=2014-07-28}}</ref> In 2015, Tyler Coates of ''[[Decider (website)|Decider]]'' called Jodorowsky's interpretation of the characters' relationship as being "only the tip of the iceberg and, I'd argue, a [[red herring]]"; Coates focuses instead on the presence of queer subtext "blatantly seen in the Nightbreed's culture", writing that, "Because normalcy is subjective and based solely on how the majority defines it, it's important to establish mini-societies and cultures with people like you."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://decider.com/2015/01/27/nightbreed-gay-horror-fantasy-epic/|title=Is Clive Barker's 'Nightbreed' A "Gay Horror Fantasy Epic"?|last=Coates|first=Tyler|date=January 27, 2015|website=[[Decider (website)|Decider]]|access-date=September 2, 2022}}</ref>

In 2019, Trace Thurman of ''[[Bloody Disgusting]]'' wrote that the Nightbreed as depicted in the film "represent queerness, or any fill-in-the-blank '[[Other (philosophy)|Other]]' you can think of."<ref name="Thurman 2019">{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3556029/horror-queers-nightbreed/|title=[Horror Queers] The Inherent Queerness of Clive Barker's 'Nightbreed'|last=Thurman|first=Trace|date=April 24, 2019|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=September 2, 2022}}</ref> Noting that, beyond writer-director [[Clive Barker]] being "one of the most famous queer horror artists of our time [...] The narrative itself is filled to the brim with queerness and serves as an allegory for intolerance. Can anyone watch ''Nightbreed'' and not automatically associate the titular creatures with queer people? They've been outcast by society and are deemed as dangerous by the 'normal' people. The climax of the film culminates in an assault on the Nightbreed's home base of Midian as the 'normies' would rather kill all of them as opposed to understand them."<ref name="Thurman 2019" /> That same year, Leigh Monson of ''Birth. Movies. Death.'' wrote that the director's cut of the film "places on display the full scope of a narrative that is a broad and potent allegory for the persecution of the queer community."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://birthmoviesdeath.com/2019/04/16/monsters-in-the-closet-nightbreed-the-directors-cut|title=Queer Underworld: Nightbreed: The Director's Cut|last=Monson|first=Leigh|date=April 16, 2019|website=Birth. Movies. Death.|access-date=September 2, 2022}}</ref>

In 2021, ''[[The A.V. Club]]''{{'}}s Charles Bramesco also examined the presence of queer themes in the film's director's cut, writing that some of its [[homoerotic]] subtext was initially removed when executives at [[20th Century Fox]] demanded that the film be trimmed during its post-production.<ref name="Bramesco 2021">{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/clive-barker-s-cut-of-nightbreed-was-too-opulently-homo-1846453905|title=Clive Barker's cut of Nightbreed was too opulently homoerotic for the studio|last=Bramesco|first=Charles|date=March 15, 2021|website=[[The A.V. Club]]|access-date=September 2, 2022}}</ref> Bramesco argued, "They were blind to the subtext of this community as a home for misfits, where the placeless Boone—who doesn't seem all that interested in sex with his torch-singing girlfriend—can find an accepting family. The director's cut allows us more time with the creatures and ups their number, emphasizing that they're only fearsome to those afraid of difference."<ref name="Bramesco 2021" />

==Production==
===Development===
Barker always loved monsters and felt that "there's a corner of all of us that envies their powers and would love to live forever, or to fly, or to change shape at will. So, when I came to make a movie about monsters, I wanted to create a world we'd feel strangely at home in".<ref name="Barker">{{cite news|last=Barker |first=Clive |title=Chains of Love |work=Fear |date=December 1988 }}</ref> He was interested in creating a "horror mythology from the ground up" and developing characters that would live on in sequels.<ref name="Nutman">{{cite AV media notes|last=Nutman |first=Philip |chapter=Introduction to ''Nightbreed'' |title=Nightbreed U.S. Video |year=1991|others=[[Clive Barker]]}}</ref> As he finished writing the novella ''[[Cabal (novella)|Cabal]]'', he realized that it would make a good film that he would direct himself.<ref name="Presskit">{{cite news|title=''Nightbreed'' Presskit |publisher=[[Morgan Creek Productions|Morgan Creek]] |year=1990 }}</ref> He originally envisioned a trilogy of films.<ref name="Salisbury2">{{cite news|last=Salisbury |first=Mark |title=Chains of Love |work=Fear |date=December 1988 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://outlawvern.com/2010/06/24/nightbreed/ |title=Nightbreed « The Life and Art of Vern |date=June 24, 2010 |publisher=Outlawvern.com |access-date=2014-07-28}}</ref> [[Mark Frost]] wrote the initial draft of the screenplay for Barker.<ref>Bushman, David. ''Conversations With Mark Frost: Twin Peaks, Hill Street Blues, and the Education of a Writer''. Fayetteville Mafia Press, 2020, p. 83.</ref>

''Nightbreed'' was the first of a planned three-picture deal Barker had with [[Morgan Creek Productions|Morgan Creek]], [[Joe Roth]]'s production company, that included an adaptation of ''Son of Celluloid'' and a sequel to ''Nightbreed''. The first compromise Barker made was to change the title of the film from ''Cabal'' to ''Nightbreed'' because Morgan Creek insisted on a more commercial title and thought that the original one did not mean anything.<ref name="Jones2">{{cite news|last=Jones |first=Alan |title=Clive Barker's ''Nightbreed'' |work=[[Cinefantastique]] |date=November 1989 }}</ref> He was given a budget of $11 million, which was a considerable increase from the $2 million he had to work with on ''[[Hellraiser]]''. His goal was to make the ''[[Star Wars]]'' of horror films. The monsters in the book are represented [[impressionist]]ically over two or three paragraphs and the challenge Barker faced was to visualize them in much greater detail for the film.<ref name="Nutman2">{{cite news|last=Nutman |first=Philip |title=Bring on the Monsters! |work=[[Fangoria]] |date=October 1989 }}</ref>

Originally several of the creatures were supposed to be [[stop motion]] puppets, in addition to [[pixilation]] for the Boone and Peloquin transformation scenes, but make-up gradually replaced the puppets, and barely any of the stop motion made it till the finished movie. Animator Rory Fellowes said "The fact is we were too late for stopmotion and too early for CG".<ref>[https://www.clivebarker.info/nbroryfellowes.html Nightbreed - Production Memories]</ref><ref>[https://www.clivebarker.info/nbscene3a.html Revelations - Nightbreed scene 3]</ref>

===Filming===
For the film, Barker used three soundstages at [[Pinewood Studios]] shooting some scenes on location at Wexham Park Hospital, Slough, Berkshire, UK over several nights and in [[Calgary]], Alberta, Canada.<ref name="Nutman"/> [[Bob Keen]] and his crew had two months to play around with ideas before doing any modeling work. They used computer-controlled [[animatronics]] but only where necessary.<ref name="Robb">{{cite news|last=Robb |first=Brian J |title=Games without Frontiers |work=Fear |date=May–June 1989 }}</ref> Towards the end of principal photography, Barker brought ''Star Wars'' concept artist [[Ralph McQuarrie]] in to paint mattes for the Necropolis sequences and design the history of the Breed in a symbolic way on an enormous mural across a 60-foot space on the set at Pinewood to be used in the opening credits.<ref name="Gilbert">{{cite news|last=Gilbert |first=John |title=The Breed: The Source of the Soul |work=Fear |date=October 1989 }}</ref>

During an interview in 2022 on ''The Ghost of Hollywood'', cinematographer [[Robin Vidgeon]] mentioned that he disliked working with [[David Cronenberg]], stating Cronenberg complained to Clive that he was being usurped.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Season Two |url=https://theghostofhollywood.com/season-two |access-date=2022-03-04 |website=The Ghost of Hollywood |language=en-US}}</ref>

Barker was contractually obligated to deliver an R-rated film and could not make it as gory as his previous picture ''Hellraiser''.<ref name="Nutman"/> Barker previewed the first cut of ''Nightbreed'' with a temporary soundtrack that did not go well, as people were confused by the characters' motives. He made some changes and the second test screening was much more successful. However, the ending with Decker's death was not well received and Barker changed it.<ref name="Jones3">{{cite news|last=Jones |first=Alan |title=''Nightbreed'': The Trials and Tribulations of Clive Barker |work=[[Starburst (magazine)|Starburst]] |date=September 1990 }}</ref> In late July 1989, the studio pushed the release date for ''Nightbreed'' from its original autumn 1989 date to early February 1990 instead. The press release cited "the complex demands of the film's ground-breaking post-production optical effects", but this also included McQuarrie's mural and [[matte painting]]s, and a week of additional shooting in late August that would see key parts of the narrative re-shot.<ref name="Gilbert"/> Barker shot extra scenes over three days in [[Los Angeles]] in late 1989 which included additional scenes with David Cronenberg which expanded and clarified his character. Barker's original version ran two-and-a-half hours and Fox asked for almost an hour to be cut prompting editor Richard Marden to leave the project in protest. ''Nightbreed'' was cut to two hours and then again to 102 minutes.<ref name="Jones3"/>

The score was composed by [[Danny Elfman]] and conducted by [[Shirley Walker]], who also wrote the additional cue "Charge of the Berserkers" for the film's climax and received an onscreen credit. Elfman said of his score: "Once again it was time for me to stretch out... Combining dark/fun/sweet/tribal all into one. The great joy in the score for me, other than working for Clive Barker, was being able to use the children's voices and a whole slew of ethnic drums and instruments together with an orchestra, in an attempt to bring a unique musical tone to the film".<ref>{{cite web|title=Danny Elfman Says...|url=http://elfman.cinemusic.net/filmography/nightbreed.html|work=Danny Elfman's Music For A Darkened People|access-date=21 March 2012}}</ref> Barker said "Danny is an extraordinary talent. The most uncompromised portion of that entire movie is the score".<ref name=LA2000Barker>{{cite web|title=Q&A session with Clive Barker & Peter Atkins|url=http://www.clivebarker.com/html/visions/confess/nonls/latimes/latimes2000.htm|work=5th Annual LA Times Festival of Books April 29th, 2000|publisher=Clivebarker.com|access-date=22 March 2012}}</ref>

In an article on Elfman, described as a rising composer in Hollywood who had just scored [[Tim Burton]]'s ''[[Batman (1989 film)|Batman]]'' and was about to score [[Warren Beatty]]'s ''[[Dick Tracy (1990 film)|Dick Tracy]]'', ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'}}s Ron Givens noted that ''Nightbreed'' wouldn't get as much attention as these two big-budget movies, yet Givens praised Elfman's "needle-sharp crescendos and creepy choral plainchants" and added: "Seldom has scary-movie music been so spiritual".<ref>{{cite web|last=Givens|first=Ron|title=The Elfman Cometh|url=https://ew.com/article/1990/02/23/danny-elfman-rise/|publisher=Entertainment Weekly|access-date=21 March 2012|date=February 23, 1990}}</ref>

==Release==
===Marketing===
According to Barker, the studio did not promote it well with posters that misinterpreted the content. When he saw the way they were selling ''Nightbreed'', he freaked out and said: "What you doing? This isn't the movie, and was given all kinds of excuses ... Well, there isn't time to change it, we have to release it now".<ref name="Salisbury">{{cite news|last=Salisbury |first=Mark |title=Flesh and Fury |work=Fear |date=October 1990 }}</ref> The head of marketing at Morgan Creek never watched all the way through because it "disgusted and distressed" him, according to Barker.<ref name="Salisbury"/> The studio did not understand it, it had no movie stars, it was violent,<ref name="Salisbury"/> and it had elements of fantasy and horror which they saw as a weakness while Barker saw it as a strength. They ended up marketing ''Nightbreed'' as a slasher film with television teasers that were confusing and did not represent it.<ref name="Timpone">{{cite news|last=Timpone |first=Anthony |title=Barker Bites Back |work=Fangoria Horror Spectacular |year=1990 }}</ref> The trailer was sent to the [[MPAA]] and it was rejected 12 times. They forbade any monster footage, and it was cut down to someone being terrorized with a razor, which constituted only five minutes of Barker's film.<ref name="Jones3"/> Looking back, Barker realized that Fox was better at promoting films like ''[[White Men Can't Jump]]'' but "not so good at selling the quirky stuff".<ref name="Ferrante">{{cite news|last=Ferrante |first=Anthony C |title=Barker Looks Back |work=Bloody Best of Fangoria |date=September 1993 }}</ref>

The studio argued that there was no point showing ''Nightbreed'' to critics because the people who see horror films do not read reviews. Therefore, the film had to be sold to the lowest common denominator.<ref name="Jones">{{cite news|last=Jones |first=Alan |title=How Fox Bungled ''Nightbreed'' |work=[[Cinefantastique]] |date=July 1990 }}</ref> They refused to preview the film for critics, a decision which angered Barker.<ref name="Timpone"/>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Nightbreed'' was released on February 16, 1990, in 1,488 theaters, grossing $3.7 million on its opening weekend. It went on to make $8.8 million in the United States and Canada and $7 million internationally for a worldwide total of $16 million on an $11 million budget.<ref name="boxoffice">{{cite web|title=Nightbreed (1990)|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=nightbreed.htm|publisher=Box Office Mojo|access-date=22 March 2012}}</ref><ref name=ww/>

===Critical response===
[[Rotten Tomatoes]], a [[review aggregator]], reports 57% of 35 surveyed critics gave the film a positive review; the average rating was 5.7 out of 10. The site's consensus states: "''Nightbreed'''s imaginative world-building and startling creature designs are no match for its clumsy, uneven plotting".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/nightbreed/|title=Nightbreed (1990)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=September 9, 2026}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 40 out of 100, based on 14 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Nightbreed'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/ |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=14 July 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Nightbreed Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/nightbreed/ |access-date=2025-07-14 |website=www.metacritic.com |language=en}}</ref> In his review for the ''[[Toronto Star]]'', Henry Mietkiewicz wrote "''Nightbreed'' might have been a monster movie milestone, if Clive Barker's directorial abilities had kept pace with his skill as a master of British horror fiction. Unfortunately, ''Nightbreed'' probably will be remembered as much for its haphazard plotting and underdeveloped characters as its delightfully daring concept".<ref name="Mietkiewicz">{{cite news |last = Mietkiewicz |first = Henry |title = ''Nightbreed''s storytelling sacrificed |work = [[The Toronto Star]] |date = February 20, 1990 }}</ref> Derek Malcolm wrote in his review for ''[[The Guardian]]'' that "it is neither direct nor subtle enough as a piece of film-making. It is difficult to suggest that evil is human and monsters have souls within the context of a mountain of special effects. The result is patchy in the extreme and not always capable of transcending a genre that has become less and less intriguing as less and less is left to the imagination".<ref name="Malcolm">{{cite news |last = Malcolm |first = Derek |title = The angel who fell to earth |work = [[The Guardian]] |date = September 27, 1990 }}</ref>

However, ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'}}s [[Ty Burr]] gave the film a "B" rating, writing: "From the film's Gothic sets, fantastic makeup, and nightmarish plot line, it's clear that Barker owes as much to [[Edgar Allan Poe|Poe]] and [[H.P. Lovecraft|Lovecraft]] as to classic Hollywood screamers like ''[[Island of Lost Souls (1932 film)|Island of Lost Souls]]''. But Barker's most perverse touch is that he makes these creatures the good guys (no wonder the PR flacks were bamboozled). Despite their grotesque appearance, they're a more colorful and engaging bunch than the emissaries of the ''normal'' world. Barker piles on more subversive subtext than his story can bear — it's a monster movie, after all — but his daft, [[Grand Guignol]] vision has real power. The quality that freaked out the studio, Barker's ambition, is precisely what makes ''Nightbreed'' so impressive".<ref>{{cite web |author=Ty Burr |url=https://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,318149,00.html |title=Nightbreed Review |publisher=EW.com |date=September 14, 1990 |access-date=2011-07-08 |archive-date=June 23, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130623210129/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,318149,00.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Merchandise==
===Comic books===
In 1990, to tie in with the film's box office release, [[Epic Comics]] produced a four-issue adaptation of the film, which included significant differences from the finished movie, more closely related to Clive Barker's original script. The comic book continued to run past the end of the film, ultimately stretching to twenty-five issues before it was cancelled.<ref>{{cite web |author=do web 4u |url=http://www.fullmooncomicbooks.com/night.htm |title=Night Breed Comics, Night Breed series, Clive Barker, Epic Comics, Horror series |publisher=Fullmooncomicbooks.com |access-date=2011-07-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100212125645/http://www.fullmooncomicbooks.com/night.htm |archive-date=February 12, 2010 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.comicvine.com/aaron-boone/29-55067/ |title=Aaron Boone (comic book character) |publisher=Comicvine.com |access-date=2011-07-08}}</ref> The comic book was initially written by [[Alan Grant (writer)|Alan Grant]] and [[John Wagner]] and drawn by [[Jim Baikie]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lambiek.net/artists/b/baikie_jim.htm|title=Jim Baikie}}</ref>

A two-part [[graphic novel]] was also produced, ''[[Hellraiser]] vs Nightbreed: Jihad'', which merges the two worlds created by Clive Barker and features the [[Cenobite (Hellraiser)|Cenobites]] as agents of order against their chaotic [[Archenemy|nemesis]] the Nightbreed, and the Jihad (Holy War) of extermination that the first wage on the latter.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.comicvine.com/hellraiser-nightbreed-jihad-/37-130472/ |title=Hellraiser Nightbreed: Jihad #1 - (comic book issue) |publisher=Comic Vine |date=June 6, 2008 |access-date=2011-07-08}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.milehighcomics.com/cgi-bin/backissue.cgi?action=list&title=39830031948 |title=Mile High Comics |publisher=Mile High Comics |access-date=2011-07-08}}</ref>

''Nightbreed'' returned in 1992, as a short story in the second issue of the four issue ''Epic'' anthology series.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.faqs.org/copyright/put-a-nightbreed-in-your-tank-clive-barkers-hellraiser/ |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130414202059/http://www.faqs.org/copyright/put-a-nightbreed-in-your-tank-clive-barkers-hellraiser/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=April 14, 2013 |title=Put a Nightbreed in your tank, Clive Barker's Hellraiser, Imajica - song, music - Copyright Info |publisher=Faqs.org |access-date=2011-07-08 }}</ref>

A 12 issue mini-series was published by BOOM! Studios in 2014-2015 following the storyline of the Director's Cut of the film.<ref>{{cite web|title = Clive Barker's Nightbreed returns in a brand new comic-book series|url = http://www.blastr.com/2014-5-2/clive-barkers-nightbreed-returns-brand-new-comic-book-series|website = Blastr|access-date = 2015-11-13|archive-date = November 17, 2015|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20151117023201/http://www.blastr.com/2014-5-2/clive-barkers-nightbreed-returns-brand-new-comic-book-series|url-status = dead}}</ref> It was collected in three volumes.

==Legacy==
===TV series===
Morgan Creek reportedly began developing a [[television series]] based on the original film in 2014.<ref>[https://www.empireonline.com/news/story.asp?NID=38217 Restored Nightbreed Cut Gets DVD Release. 'The Cabal Cut' is finally official - by Owen Williams] July 23, 2013, [[Empire Online]]</ref><ref>[https://www.empireonline.com/news/story.asp?NID=33574 Clive Barker Plotting Nightbreed For TV. Hellraiser headed for small screen too - by Owen Williams] 04 April 2012, [[Empire Online]]</ref> In the following year, Morgan Creek announced the sale of the domestic rights to its library of 78 films, but the production company plans to retain the TV rights to ''Nightbreed''.<ref>{{cite web|title=Morgan Creek to Put Film Library Up for Sale|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/morgan-creek-put-film-library-826980|website=The Hollywood Reporter|date=September 24, 2015|access-date=2015-11-10}}</ref> In June 2018, [[Syfy]], Morgan Creek and Barker teamed up to develop the series.<ref>{{cite web|title='Nightbreed' TV Series Reboot From Clive Barker & Morgan Creek In Works At Syfy|url=https://deadline.com/2018/06/nightbreed-tv-series-reboot-clive-barker-morgan-creek-syfy-1202415885/|date=22 June 2018|website=Deadline|access-date=22 June 2018}}</ref> It is being written by [[Josh Stolberg]] and directed by [[Michael Dougherty]] for SyFy.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2018/06/nightbreed-tv-series-reboot-clive-barker-morgan-creek-syfy-1202415885/|title='Nightbreed' TV Series Reboot From Clive Barker & Morgan Creek In Works At Syfy|last=Andreeva|first=Nellie|date=2018-06-22|website=Deadline Hollywood|language=en|access-date=2020-10-24}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/tv/news/1150320-exclusive-godzillas-michael-dougherty-to-direct-nightbreed-series|title=Exclusive: Godzilla's Michael Dougherty to Direct Nightbreed Series!|last=Hermans|first=Grant|date=2020-09-30|website=Coming Soon|language=en|access-date=2020-10-24}}</ref>

===''The Cabal Cut''===
In 2009, Mark Miller, co-head of Barker's production company, Seraphim Films, helped track down the missing footage that was cut out of the director's cut of ''Nightbreed''.<ref>{{cite web|last=Barton|first=Steve|title=''Nightbreed'' Uncut Report: Wondering What You Didn't See? We've Got the Answers!|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/36744/nightbreed-uncut-report-wondering-what-you-didnt-seen-weve-got-anwers|publisher=DreadCentral.com|access-date=12 April 2012|date=April 1, 2010}}</ref> Miller discovered, after talking to a production executive at the studio, that the footage was never actually lost but readily accessible. When asked, a studio executive said that there was not a big enough audience to warrant the studio spending money on a new, extended cut of the film.<ref name="Zahn">{{cite news|last=Zahn |first=James |title=''Nightbreed'': The Director's Cut? |publisher=[[Fangoria]] |date=June 1, 2009 |url=http://fangoria.com/home/news/16-dvd-a-blu-ray/2691-nightbreed-the-directors-cut.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090612042953/http://www.fangoria.com/home/news/16-dvd-a-blu-ray/2691-nightbreed-the-directors-cut.html|archive-date=June 12, 2009}}</ref> A VHS copy of Barker's 145-minute version of the film's mid-1989 workprint was discovered. It did not feature any of the re-shoots of Decker's murders.<ref name="Zahn2">{{cite news|last=Zahn |first=James |title= Update on the Director's Cut of ''Nightbreed'' |publisher=[[Fangoria]] |date=June 10, 2009 |url=http://fangoria.com/home/news/9-film-news/2829-update-on-the-directors-cut-of-nightbreed.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090619000234/http://fangoria.com/home/news/9-film-news/2829-update-on-the-directors-cut-of-nightbreed.html|archive-date=June 19, 2009 }}</ref> An extended 159-minute cut version, from another VHS found in July 2009, premiered on March 27, 2010, as part of the HorrorHound Weekend in [[Indianapolis]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Barton|first=Steve|title=See ''Nightbreed'' Uncut for the First Time Ever|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/35706/see-nightbreed-uncut-first-time-ever|publisher=DreadCentral.com|access-date=April 12, 2012|date=February 4, 2010}}</ref> This new version adds almost an hour that was cut from the theatrical release, including a song sung by Lori, and more animation. Barker said that he hopes to bring back Danny Elfman to add more music.<ref name="LA2000Barker"/>

In early 2012, Russell Cherrington, a senior lecturer in film and video production at the [[University of Derby]], created a composite cut of the film using the footage found on both VHS tapes and a DVD then recently released by [[Warner Archive Collection]], Warner's made-to-order DVD service. This version is the most complete version of Barker's film available and has been dubbed ''The Cabal Cut''. The cut runs 155 minutes long and was shown at that year's "Mad Monster Party" in [[North Carolina]] with actors [[Craig Sheffer]] and Anne Bobby attending.<ref>{{cite web|last=Snellings|first=April|title=Seraphim's Mark Miller on ''Nightbreed'': The Director's Cut|url=http://www.rue-morgue.com/2012/03/sinister-seven-seraphim%E2%80%99s-mark-miller-on-nightbreed-the-director%E2%80%99s-cut|publisher=[[Rue Morgue (magazine)|Rue Morgue]]|date=March 21, 2012|access-date=12 April 2012|author2=Ron McKenzie|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120324202117/http://www.rue-morgue.com/2012/03/sinister-seven-seraphim%E2%80%99s-mark-miller-on-nightbreed-the-director%E2%80%99s-cut/|archive-date=March 24, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The "Mad Monster Party" projection of the ''Cabal Cut'' led to a renewal of interest among fans, especially on the Internet.<ref name=TurekShock>{{cite web|last=Turek|first=Ryan|title=Join Occupy Midian and the Push for a ''Nightbreed: The Cabal Cut'' Release!|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/166685-join-occupy-midian-and-the-push-for-a-nightbreed-the-cabal-cut-release|publisher=Shocktillyoudrop.com|access-date=13 April 2012}}</ref> A new petition was created and social networks were used to raise awareness for the extended cut and to encourage producers to release it. This would be colloquially known as "Occupy Midian", a term coined by actress Anne Bobby.<ref>{{cite web|last=Leitao|first=Jose|title=''The Cabal Cut'' Viral Trailer hits YouTube!|date=April 12, 2012|url=http://www.clivebarkercast.com/2012/04/12/the-cabal-cut-viral-trailer-hits-youtube/|publisher=The Clive Barker Podcast|access-date=13 April 2012|quote=“Occupy Midian” was a name thought up by Anne Bobby during an interview with the Clive Barker Podcast. This group aims to work in close co-operation with the creative people currently restoring the film.}}</ref>

A Blu-Ray release of the "Cabal Cut" was announced on July 13, 2017, via the Clive Barker Podcast.<ref>{{cite web|title=CABAL CUT To Be Released On Blu-Ray!|url=http://www.clivebarkercast.com/2017/07/13/cabal-cut-to-be-released-on-blu-ray-exclusive-announcement/|website=Clive Barker Podcast|date=July 13, 2017|access-date=13 July 2017}}</ref> This release utilized then-restored footage from the Director's Cut and the pre-existing VHS copies, for a total length of 145 minutes. The release features new material and commentary tracks exclusive to that release. Handled by Morgan Creek and Seraphim Films, the Cabal Cut Blu-Ray was released in a limited quantity through Clive Barker's online store.

===Shout! Factory restoration and ''Director's Cut''===
In 2013, [[Shout! Factory]] released ''The Cabal Cut'' on DVD.<ref>[https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/68819/exclusive-michael-plumides-talks-nightbreed-cabal-cut-heading-home-video#axzz2xHrxNwLo Michael Plumides talks about ''Nightbreed: The Cabal Cut''] [[Dread Central]]</ref><ref name=dvd_cabalcut>{{cite web|last=Zimmerman|first=Samuel|title=[SDCC '13] Scream Factory releasing "Nightbreed: The Cabal Cut" & more!|url=http://www.fangoria.com/new/sdcc-13-scream-factory-releasing-nightbreed-the-cabal-cut-more/|publisher=Fangoria|access-date=21 July 2013|archive-date=July 23, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130723020515/http://www.fangoria.com/new/sdcc-13-scream-factory-releasing-nightbreed-the-cabal-cut-more/|url-status=dead}}</ref>  This version was later clarified to be a brand new director's cut that uses the footage and not the full ''Cabal Cut''.<ref>{{cite web|last=Turek|first=Ryan|title=The Upcoming Nightbreed Special Edition is Not The Cabal Cut|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/346207-the-upcoming-nightbreed-special-edition-is-not-the-cabal-cut/|publisher=ShockTillYouDrop.com|access-date=4 September 2014|date=2 February 2014}}</ref>

Shout! Factory released ''The Director's Cut'' Blu-ray in 2014. This version, overseen by Clive Barker, runs twenty minutes longer than the theatrical version and contains forty minutes of new and altered footage.<ref>{{cite web| title = Scream Factory Facebook – Final Specs For Nightbreed: The Directors Cut Revealed! | website=[[Facebook]]| url=https://www.facebook.com/ScreamFactoryDVD/posts/492672024203147 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/iarchive/facebook/103961936407493/492672024203147 |archive-date=2022-02-26 |url-access=limited}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Shout, with [[Morgan Creek Entertainment]], located the original film elements in the Warner archives to newly restore the sequences. Barker said, "when Scream Factory told me that they found the ''Nightbreed'' film footage, I was gob-smacked! This is the ultimate validation of choices made by myself and Mark Miller all the way back in 2008".<ref>{{cite web|last=Squires|first=John|title=Full Release Details for Scream Factory's Nightbreed Director's Cut Blu-ray|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/76924/full-release-details-scream-factorys-nightbreed-directors-cut-blu-ray-tv-series-developme#axzz3CP29L86J|publisher=DreadCentral.com|access-date=12 April 2012|date=February 4, 2010}}</ref> Shout later published a Special Edition Blu-ray / DVD combo pack and a 3-disc limited edition Blu-ray, containing a booklet and the theatrical cut. When the limited edition sold out during pre-orders, Shout! doubled their production run.<ref>{{cite web| title = Scream Factory Facebook – Nightbreed: Limited Edition – Now 10,000 Units | website=[[Facebook]]| url=https://www.facebook.com/ScreamFactoryDVD/photos/a.130215417115478.24939.103961936407493/469403496530000/}}</ref>
