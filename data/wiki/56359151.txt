'''''Mandy''''' is a 2018<!-- Per [[WP:FILMLEAD]]: "If the nationality is not singular, cover the different national interests later in the lead section."--> [[Surrealist cinema|surrealist]] [[Action film|action]] [[horror film]] directed by [[Panos Cosmatos]], from a screenplay he co-wrote with Aaron Stewart-Ahn, and based on a story by Cosmatos. A co-production between [[Belgium]] and the [[United States]], it stars [[Nicolas Cage]], [[Andrea Riseborough]], [[Linus Roache]], [[Ned Dennehy]], [[Olwen Fouéré]], [[Richard Brake]], and [[Bill Duke]]. The film follows Red Miller (Cage), a lumberjack seeking vengeance against cult leader Jeremiah Sand (Roache), as well as his devout followers, for the murder of the eponymous character and Red's girlfriend, Mandy Bloom (Riseborough).

''Mandy'' premiered at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] on January 19, 2018, and was theatrically released in the United States on September 13, 2018. The film was a box office failure, grossing $1.7 million against its $6 million production budget. Despite this, it received critical acclaim, with particular praise for its visual style and originality, Cage's performance, Cosmatos' direction, and the action sequences. The film is widely considered a modern [[cult classic]].

At the [[45th Saturn Awards]], the film won for [[Saturn Award for Best Independent Film|Best Independent Film]], while Nicolas Cage was nominated for [[Saturn Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]]. Benjamin Loeb was nominated for [[Independent Spirit Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] at the [[34th Independent Spirit Awards]]. ''Mandy'' was one of the last films to be scored by composer [[Jóhann Jóhannsson]], who died seven months before the film's release, and the film is dedicated to his memory.<ref>{{cite web|url = https://variety.com/2018/film/columns/johann-johannsson-cinema-soundscape-1202694619/|title = How Composer Jóhann Jóhannsson Helped Change the Genre Cinema Soundscape|last = Debruge|first = Peter|date = February 10, 2018|work = [[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|publisher = [[Penske Business Media]]|access-date = March 6, 2018|archive-date = February 11, 2018|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20180211020515/http://variety.com/2018/film/columns/johann-johannsson-cinema-soundscape-1202694619/|url-status = live}}</ref>

==Plot==
===The Shadow Mountains===
In 1983, in the Pacific Northwest, recovering alcoholic war veteran (former [[MACV-SOG]] unit) lumberjack Red Miller lives with his girlfriend, artist and author Mandy Bloom, who had an abusive childhood. Mandy works as a gas station cashier by day and at night creates elaborate [[fantasy art]], which Red greatly admires.

While walking to work, Mandy catches the attention of Jeremiah Sand, leader of the religious [[cult]] Children of the New Dawn.

===Children of the New Dawn===
Sand orders his disciple Brother Swan to kidnap Mandy. Swan summons the Black Skulls, a cannibalistic, demonic biker gang that regularly consumes [[Lysergic acid diethylamide|LSD]].

After being offered a cult member as a sacrifice, the gang breaks into their home and subdues Mandy and Red. Cultists Mother Marlene and Sister Lucy drug Mandy with LSD eye drops and the venom of a giant wasp before presenting her to Sand. The cult leader attempts to seduce Mandy with his [[psychedelic folk]] music, but she just laughs at Sand, infuriating him. In retaliation, Sand stabs the bound and gagged Red and burns Mandy alive before him. After Sand and his followers leave, Red frees himself, mourns Mandy's death, and, after consuming a bottle of vodka, wordlessly swears revenge.

The next morning, Red visits his friend Caruthers to retrieve his crossbow and bolts. Before Red leaves, Caruthers warns him of the Black Skulls.

===Mandy===
Red reinforces his determination by forging a battle axe. He shoots a biker with his crossbow and runs him down, but the biker crashes his car and Red is captured in the process. At their hideout, Red breaks free from his restraints and kills the rest of the bikers. As he investigates their hideout, he ingests some of their drugs, causing him to instantly and severely [[Hallucination|hallucinate]].

Following the images in his visions, Red finds The Chemist, the person who made the Black Skulls' LSD. After telepathically understanding what the cult did to Red and Mandy, the Chemist tells him where to find them. At their makeshift wooden church in a quarry, Red kills the cult members one by one in increasingly brutal fashion. In the tunnels beneath the church, Red finds Mother Marlene and decapitates her. He then confronts Sand, taunting him by throwing Marlene's severed head at him before crushing his skull. He sets Sand's body and the church on fire before driving away. As Red drives, he hallucinates Mandy smiling in the passenger seat of his car, while the landscape behind him now appears fantastical and otherworldly.

==Production==
On June 7, 2017, [[Nicolas Cage]] was announced as the star of the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2017/06/nicolas-cage-to-star-thriller-action-mandy-xyz-films-spectrevision-elijah-wood-1202108799/|title=Nicolas Cage To Star In Action Thriller 'Mandy' From SpectreVision, XYZ Films & Umedia|last=Lodderhose|first=Diana|date=June 7, 2017|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|access-date=April 18, 2019|archive-date=April 18, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190418051159/https://deadline.com/2017/06/nicolas-cage-to-star-thriller-action-mandy-xyz-films-spectrevision-elijah-wood-1202108799/|url-status=live}}</ref> Production used the [[Arri Alexa]] camera, coupled with the [[Panavision]] [[anamorphic format]], to achieve the film's old-school atmosphere.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2018/01/sundance-2018-cameras-movies-1201918085/4/|title=Sundance 2018: Here Are the Cameras Used to Shoot This Year's Narrative Films|first=Chris|last=O'Falt|work=[[IndieWire]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|access-date=August 24, 2018|date=January 18, 2018|archive-date=August 4, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180804170212/https://www.indiewire.com/2018/01/sundance-2018-cameras-movies-1201918085/4/|url-status=live}}</ref> The filming faced scheduling difficulties because Cage broke his ankle while working on another project, and cinematographer Benjamin Loeb joined the project only two weeks before principal photography began. Director Panos Cosmatos provided Loeb with various films as sources of visual inspiration to use for the film, including ''[[The Hitcher (1986 film)|The Hitcher]]'', ''[[Days of Thunder]]'', ''[[Revenge (1990 film)|Revenge]]'', ''[[Psycho III]]'', ''[[Fist of the North Star (1986 film)|Fist of the North Star]]'', and ''[[Cobra (1986 film)|Cobra]]''.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Mandy: Edge of Darkness |url=https://theasc.com/articles/mandy-edge-of-darkness |access-date=2025-09-15 |website=The American Society of Cinematographers |language=en}}</ref>

The forest scenes were shot in the [[High Fens]] in Belgium. The cabin that Red and Mandy live in was constructed by the crew on the site of an unused Boy Scout pavilion. The lairs of the Black Skulls and Children of the New Dawn made use of an abandoned house outside of [[Chaudfontaine]]. The chainsaw battle was shot in a quarry outside of [[Bévercé]].<ref name=":0" /> The weapon forged by Red in the film was based on the "F" from the logo for [[extreme metal]] band [[Celtic Frost]].<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Schager |first1=Nick |date=14 September 2018 |title=Inside Nicolas Cage's Most Insane, Hell-Raising Performance Yet |url=https://www.thedailybeast.com/inside-nicolas-cages-most-insane-hell-raising-performance-yet |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210302135715/https://www.thedailybeast.com/inside-nicolas-cages-most-insane-hell-raising-performance-yet |archive-date=2 March 2021 |access-date=26 May 2020 |newspaper=The Daily Beast}}</ref> The song "[[Starless]]" by the English [[progressive rock]] band [[King Crimson]] plays over the opening credits. It is taken from their 1974 album ''[[Red (King Crimson album)|Red]]'', which is also the name of Cage's character.<ref>{{cite web |date=11 October 2018 |title=Nicolas Cage's Slasher Freakout 'Mandy' Makes Prog Rock Kick Ass |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/nicolas-cages-slasher-freakout-mandy-makes-prog-rock-kick-ass/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220507113434/https://www.vice.com/en/article/evwk84/nicolas-cages-slasher-freakout-mandy-makes-prog-rock-kick-ass |archive-date=7 May 2022 |access-date=7 May 2022 |publisher=vice.com}}</ref>

[[Legion M]], an entertainment studio that allows fans to invest in and be part of the creation of films, was a production partner for ''Mandy'' and hosted a panel discussion featuring director [[Panos Cosmatos]] and others at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] in 2018. Cage made a surprise appearance at the event.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://boomhowdy.com/film/nic-cage-crashes-legion-m-sundance-panel-mandy/|title=Nic Cage Crashes the Legion M Sundance Panel for 'Mandy'|last=Canton|first=KC|date=January 21, 2018|work=Boom Howdy|access-date=April 18, 2019|language=en-US|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180702204901/https://boomhowdy.com/film/nic-cage-crashes-legion-m-sundance-panel-mandy/|archive-date=July 2, 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Music==
{{Main|Mandy (soundtrack)}}The soundtrack of ''Mandy'' was one of the last films scored by [[Jóhann Jóhannsson]] before his death. Some of the inspirations that Cosmatos provided for Jóhannsson were, [[Queen (band)|Queen]]'s [[Flash Gordon (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] for ''[[Flash Gordon (film)|Flash Gordon]]'' (1980) and  [[Van Halen]]'s album ''[[Fair Warning (Van Halen album)|Fair Warning]]'' (1981). However, Jóhannsson's score did not reference any of them; the score comprises electronic and live instrumentation that was being heavily edited and processed for a "dark, massive, industrial-metal sound" that matched the violent tone of the film.<ref name=":02">{{Cite web |last=Burlingame |first=Jon |date=2018-09-13 |title=The Music of 'Mandy': How Johann Johannsson Melded Horror With Heavy Metal |url=https://variety.com/2018/film/news/mandy-horror-movie-music-composer-johann-johannsson-heavy-metal-1202941577/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190215050426/https://variety.com/2018/film/news/mandy-horror-movie-music-composer-johann-johannsson-heavy-metal-1202941577/ |archive-date=2019-02-15 |access-date=2024-06-27 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> The score featured [[Stephen O'Malley]] from [[Sunn O)))]] playing guitars. [[Randall Dunn]] served as the music producer and discussed with Jóhannsson on the use of specific analogue synthesizers. The team found a studio in Italy where most of the synth recordings were done.<ref name=":02" />

Most of the film's score had been completed in time for the [[2018 Sundance Film Festival]] premiere. Following Jóhannsson's death on February 9, 2018, the remainder of his cues were curated and produced by [[Kreng (musician)|Pepijn Caudron]] and [[Yair Elazar Glotman]].<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Wray |first=Daniel Dylan |date=2018-09-14 |title=How Jóhann Jóhannsson's Final Film Score Was Made |url=https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/how-johann-johannssons-final-film-score-was-made-mandy-oral-history/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190723071458/https://pitchfork.com/thepitch/how-johann-johannssons-final-film-score-was-made-mandy-oral-history/ |archive-date=2019-07-23 |access-date=2024-06-27 |website=Pitchfork |language=en-US}}</ref> Caudron was initially hired as a music editor, but due to the slow post-production process, he also wrote a couple of cues mostly based on Jóhannsson's samples. According to Dunn, the music in the film and the soundtrack release were different. Dunn first heard the soundtrack and the film's music later and found that more material has been featured in the film while mixing. He felt the soundtrack to be a bonus material.<ref name=":1" /> The film was dedicated to Jóhannsson.<ref>{{cite web |last=Debruge |first=Peter |date=February 10, 2018 |title=How Composer Jóhann Jóhannsson Helped Change the Genre Cinema Soundscape |url=https://variety.com/2018/film/columns/johann-johannsson-cinema-soundscape-1202694619/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180211020515/http://variety.com/2018/film/columns/johann-johannsson-cinema-soundscape-1202694619/ |archive-date=February 11, 2018 |access-date=March 6, 2018 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
[[File:Nicolas Cage 2011 CC.jpg|thumb|upright|[[Nicolas Cage]]'s performance garnered critical praise.]]

On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], {{RT data|score}} of critics have given the film a positive review based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The website's critics consensus reads, "''Mandy''{{'}}s gonzo violence is fueled by a gripping performance by Nicolas Cage—and anchored with palpable emotion conveyed between his volcanic outbursts."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/mandy_2018|title=Mandy (2018)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-date=2018-09-08|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180908195124/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/mandy_2018|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 83 out of 100 based on review from 30 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/mandy |title=Mandy Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=April 18, 2019 |archive-date=October 31, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181031153056/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/mandy |url-status=live }}</ref>

Reviewing the film after its world premiere at the Sundance Film Festival, Nick Allen of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' praised it, writing that "for all of the endless feral performances that Cage has given, in movies good, bad and forgettable, Cosmatos' style-driven, '80s-tastic passion for weird worlds and characters takes full advantage of Cage’s greatness, and then some."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/sundance/sundance-2018-mandy|title=Sundance 2018: Mandy|last=Allen|first=Nick|date=January 20, 2018|website=RogerEbert.com|publisher=Ebert Digital LLC|access-date=June 28, 2018|archive-date=February 22, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180222225547/https://www.rogerebert.com/sundance/sundance-2018-mandy|url-status=live}}</ref>

In a five-star review for ''Dirty Movies'', Stephen Lee Naish called the film "a blood soaked revenge caper," praising Cosmatos for a "masterful approach" that "aligns him with Kubrick and Lynch in delivering perfectly believable and fully realized worlds and characters that operate within their own laws of physics."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.dmovies.org/2018/09/22/mandy/|title=Mandy - film review|date=September 22, 2018|work=DMovies|access-date=October 5, 2018|language=en-GB|archive-date=September 10, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190910142017/https://www.dmovies.org/2018/09/22/mandy/|url-status=live}}</ref> Meanwhile, film critic Christopher Stewardson said the film "is sure to become a cult favourite all of its own."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://ourculturemag.com/2018/10/13/review-mandy-2018|title=Review: Mandy (2018) - Our Culture Mag|last=Stewardson|first=Christopher|date=2018-10-13|work=Our Culture Mag|access-date=2018-10-13|language=en|archive-date=2018-10-14|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181014130114/https://ourculturemag.com/2018/10/13/review-mandy-2018/|url-status=live}}</ref> In December 2018, [[Esquire (magazine)|''Esquire'']] named ''Mandy'' the top film in its ''25 Best Movies of 2018 So Far''.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/g14465325/best-movies-of-2018/|title='Mandy' Is the Best Movie of 2018 (So Far)|date=2018-10-01|work=Esquire|access-date=2018-10-08|language=en-US|archive-date=2020-01-08|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200108221221/https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/g14465325/best-movies-of-2018/|url-status=live}}</ref>
