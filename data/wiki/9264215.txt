'''''Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story''''' is a 2007 American [[musical film|musical]] [[comedy film]] directed by [[Jake Kasdan]], and written by Kasdan and co-producer [[Judd Apatow]]. It stars [[John C. Reilly]], [[Kristen Wiig]], [[Tim Meadows]] and [[Jenna Fischer]]. A parody of the [[Biographical film|biopic]] genre, ''Walk Hard'' is the story of a fictional [[country music|country]], [[rock and roll]] and [[folk music]] star played by Reilly.

''Walk Hard'' primarily references the musical biopics ''[[Ray (film)|Ray]]'' (2004) and ''[[Walk the Line]]'' (2005); in addition to [[Ray Charles]] and [[Johnny Cash]], the "Dewey Cox" character includes elements of the lives and careers of other notable musicians including [[Roy Orbison]], [[Glen Campbell]], [[Bob Dylan]], [[Jerry Lee Lewis]], [[Donovan]], [[John Lennon]], [[James Brown]], [[Jim Morrison]], [[Conway Twitty]], [[Neil Diamond]], [[Hank Williams]], [[Sonny & Cher]] and [[Brian Wilson]]. The film portrays fictional versions of artists [[Buddy Holly]], [[the Big Bopper]], [[Elvis Presley]], and [[the Beatles]]; some artists appear as themselves, including [[Eddie Vedder]], [[Jewel (singer)|Jewel]] and [[Ghostface Killah]]. In addition, the film parodies or pays tribute to the musical styles of [[David Bowie]], [[Billy Joel]], [[Van Dyke Parks]], [[the Gun Club]], and seventies punk rock.

The film was released in North America on December 21, 2007 by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]]. It received positive critical reviews, and was a [[Box-office bomb|box office disappointment]], grossing $20.6 million against a $35 million budget. It has since become a [[cult film]].<ref name=siegel>{{cite web |last= Siegel |first= Alan |title= Dewey Cox Ain't Dead: An Oral History of 'Walk Hard' |url= https://www.theringer.com/movies/2019/5/29/18642513/walk-hard-dewey-cox-oral-history-john-c-reilly-music-biopic-jake-kasdan-judd-apatow |website= [[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]] |access-date= September 14, 2020 |date= May 29, 2019}}</ref>{{Not in body|reason=article body needs expand before lead|date=May 2026}} For his performance, Reilly was nominated for the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy]].<ref>{{cite web|url= https://abc7.com/archive/5935362/|title= Complete list of Golden Globe winners|website= [[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]]|accessdate= April 16, 2024}}</ref>

==Plot==
In Springberry, [[Alabama]], 1946, young Dewey Cox accidentally cuts his brother Nate in half with a [[machete]]. His father blames him for Nate's death and the trauma causes him to [[anosmia|lose his sense of smell]]. Dewey meets a [[blues]] guitarist who discovers his life experience instilled in him a natural affinity for playing blues.

In 1953, Dewey performs at a school [[talent show]] and drives the crowd wild with his song "Take My Hand", and his father kicks him out of the house, calling it the "[[Devil]]'s [[Rock and Roll|music]]". A 14-year-old Dewey leaves Springberry with his 12-year-old-girlfriend Edith; they soon marry and have a baby.

Working at an all-[[African American]] nightclub, Dewey replaces singer Bobby Shad onstage and impresses Jewish record executive L'Chaim. While recording a [[rockabilly]] rendition of "[[That's Amore]]", he is berated by an executive. A desperate Dewey performs "Walk Hard", a song inspired by a speech he gave Edith, which restores the executive's religious faith and rockets him to superstardom.

The song quickly becomes a hit and Dewey becomes caught up in the rock 'n' roll lifestyle. He soon performs his first concert as the following act to [[Elvis Presley]], [[Buddy Holly]], and [[The Big Bopper]]. Dewey is introduced to [[cannabis (drug)|marijuana]] by his reluctant drummer Sam and becomes unfaithful to Edith. Dewey's father informs him that his mother has died while dancing to Dewey's song and blames Dewey's music for her death.

Distraught, Dewey finds Sam using [[cocaine]] and partakes, resulting in a cocaine-fueled [[punk rock]] performance. [[Choir]]-girl Darlene enters Dewey's life, and he produces several sexually suggestive hit records amid their courtship. He weds Darlene while still married to Edith, which leads to both women leaving him, after which Dewey purchases drugs from an undercover cop. After he serves time in prison and in rehab, Darlene returns.

They move to [[Berkeley, California]] in 1966 during the [[1960s counterculture|counterculture]] movement. Dewey's new singing style is compared to that of [[Bob Dylan]], which he angrily denies despite his new songs and style clearly imitating Dylan’s. On a band visit to [[India]], Dewey takes [[LSD]] with [[the Beatles]], leading to a ''[[Yellow Submarine (film)|Yellow Submarine]]''-esque hallucination.

Dewey becomes consumed with creating his masterpiece ''Black Sheep'' (a homage to [[Brian Wilson]]'s ''[[Smile (The Beach Boys album)|Smile]]''). The band resents his insane musical style and abusive behavior and breaks up; Darlene, also unable to deal with him, leaves him for [[Glen Campbell]]. During another stint in rehab, Dewey is visited by the ghost of Nate, who ridicules his self-pity and tells him to start writing songs again.

In 1976, Dewey now hosts a [[CBS]] [[variety television show]] but is unable to compose a masterpiece for his brother. At Nate's urging, Dewey attempts to reconcile with his father. However, his father reveals that he has spent years training with a machete to murder Dewey and forces him to duel, but accidentally cuts himself in half. Before dying, he forgives Dewey for Nate's death and tells him to be a better father. Dewey breaks down and destroys almost everything in his home.

Dewey is approached by one of his sons and decides to reconnect with his dozens of children. In 1992, a divorced Darlene returns to him. Finally realizing what is most important, Dewey regains his sense of smell and remarries her.

In 2007, L'Chaim's son Dreidel informs Dewey of his popularity with young listeners through [[rapper]] Lil' Nutzzak's [[sampling (music)|sampling]] of "Walk Hard". Dewey learns he is to receive a lifetime achievement award. They want him to sing a song at the ceremony, but Dewey is reluctant, fearing his old temptations. However, with his family's support, he reunites with his band and is finally able to create one great masterpiece, summing up his entire life with his final song, "Beautiful Ride", while also deciding against trying a drug, [[viagra]] offered to him by Sam.

A title card reveals that Dewey died three minutes after this final performance, which then also reads "Dewford Randolph Cox, 1936–2007". A [[post-credits scene]] is a short black-and-white clip of "the actual Dewey Cox, April 16, 2002" (still played by Reilly).

==Production and development==
{{quote box|width=310px|quote=I just had this idea to do a fake [[biographical film|biopic]]—or a real biopic about a fake person—and follow a musician's career trajectory.|source= —Jake Kasdan, 2007<ref name="hiatt"/>}}

[[Jake Kasdan]] brought the idea to his friend and fellow director Judd Apatow. They then began writing the film together.<ref name=hiatt/> The [[tongue-in-cheek]] references in this fake biopic were drawn from various sources. Apatow and Kasdan noted that they watched various types of biopics for inspiration, including those of [[Jimi Hendrix]] and [[Marilyn Monroe]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=38323 |first=Heather |last=Newgen |work=[[ComingSoon.net]] |title=Apatow, Kasdan and Reilly Walk Hard |date=12 October 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071014233427/http://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=38323 |archive-date=October 14, 2007 |access-date=December 11, 2007 }}</ref> Despite the humorous approach, the film was crafted in the serious tone of films [[Oscar bait|earmarked]] for an [[Academy Award|Oscar]], adding to the irony.<ref>{{cite news |last=Breznican |first=Anthony |date=2007-09-11 |title='Walk Hard' riffs on greatest rockers |newspaper=[[USA Today]] }}</ref>

[[John C. Reilly]], who actually sings and plays guitar, was chosen to play the title role. "We took the clichés of movie biopics and just had fun with them," Reilly said.<ref name=hiatt/> The "deliberate miscasting" of celebrity cameos, such as [[Elvis Presley]] and [[the Beatles]], was intended to enhance the comedy.<ref>{{cite news |last=Breznican |first=Anthony |date=23 November 2007 |title='Walk Hard' takes a run at musical legends |work=[[USA Today]] }}</ref> The film's poster is a reference to the "young lion" photos of [[Jim Morrison]].<ref name=faraci>{{cite web |last=Faraci |first=Devin |date=2007-11-29 |url=http://www.chud.com/index.php?type=news&id=12728 |title=The Devin's Advocate: the Judd Apatow Backlash |work=CHUD.com |access-date=2007-12-13 }}</ref> The film was shot on the [[Genesis (camera)|Panavision Genesis]].<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Marcks |first=Ian |date=September 15, 2025 |title=ShotDeck Drop: Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story (2007) |url=https://theasc.com/articles/walk-hard-dewey-cox-cinematography |magazine=American Cinematographer}}</ref>

==Reception==
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 75% based on 138 reviews, with an average rating of 6.6/10. The site's consensus states: "A parody that pokes fun at rock stars and reductive biopics alike, this comedy sings in large part because of stellar performances and clever original music."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/walk_hard |title=Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story Movie Reviews, Pictures |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |date=December 21, 2007 |access-date=2024-10-22}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] the film has a score of 63 out of 100 based on reviews from 32 critics.<ref name="metacritic">{{cite web |title= Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story |url= https://www.metacritic.com/movie/walk-hard-the-dewey-cox-story |website= [[Metacritic]] }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] scored the film three out of four and wrote: "Instead of sending everything over the top at high energy, like ''[[Top Secret!]]'' or ''[[Airplane!]]'', they allow Reilly to more or less actually ''play'' the character, so that, against all expectations, some scenes actually approach real sentiment."<ref>{{cite news |date=December 21, 2007 |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/walk-hard-the-dewey-cox-story-2007 |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |access-date=19 September 2019 }}</ref> Peter Travers of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' magazine wrote: "The tricky thing about parody movies is that the jokes get old fast and they're hit-and-miss. ''Walk Hard'', a spoof of every musical biopic from ''Ray'' to ''Walk the Line'', is guilty on both counts. How lucky that when the jokes do hit, they kick major ass."<ref>{{cite magazine |date=13 December 2007 |last1=Travers |first1=Peter |author-link1=Peter Travers |title=Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-reviews/walk-hard-the-dewey-cox-story-250593/ |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191023000126/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-reviews/walk-hard-the-dewey-cox-story-250593/ |access-date=28 September 2024 |archive-date=2019-10-23 |url-status=live }}</ref> A 2022 review of the best comedy films of the 21st century placed this at sixth.<ref name="best">{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/greatest-comedies-of-the-21st-century-630244/walk-hard-1234599232/ |title=70 Greatest Comedies of the 21st Century |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=2022-10-06 |accessdate=2022-06-10 |lang=en-US |last=Fear |first=David}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{Main|Walk Hard: The Dewey Cox Story (soundtrack)}}
Singer-songwriters [[Dan Bern]] and [[Mike Viola]] (of the [[Candy Butchers]]) wrote most of the film's songs, including "There's a Change a Happenin'", "The Mulatto Song", "A Life Without You (Is No Life at All)", "Beautiful Ride" and "Hole in My Pants". Charlie Wadhams and [[Benji Hughes]] wrote the song "Let's Duet".<ref name=siegel /> [[Marshall Crenshaw]] wrote the title song, and [[Van Dyke Parks]] penned the [[Brian Wilson]]-esque 1960s-styled psychedelic jam "Black Sheep" (the recording session seems to be a specific parody of Wilson's ''[[Smile (The Beach Boys album)|Smile]]'' album sessions, on which Van Dyke Parks worked).<ref name="hiatt">{{cite magazine |last=Hiatt |first=Brian |date=2007-08-09 |title=The Next 'Spinal Tap'? |magazine=Rolling Stone |volume=1032 |page=20 }}</ref> Antonio Ortiz wrote "Take My Hand". A number of critics noted the unusually high quality of many of the individual songs on the soundtrack, how well they reflected the styles and times they were attempting to parody, and how well they stood on their own as quality compositions. The soundtrack was nominated for both a [[Grammy Award|Grammy]] and [[Golden Globe Award]] and was nominated and won the Sierra Award for Best Song in a Motion Picture from the Las Vegas Film Critics Society. John C. Reilly sang on all the tracks and played guitar on most of them.
