'''''Us''''' is a 2019 American [[psychological horror film]] written and directed by [[Jordan Peele]], and starring [[Lupita Nyong'o]], [[Winston Duke]], [[Elisabeth Moss]] and [[Tim Heidecker]]. The story follows Adelaide Wilson and her family, who are attacked by a group of menacing [[doppelgängers]], called the "Tethered".

The project was announced in February 2018. Peele produced the film alongside [[Jason Blum]] and [[Sean McKittrick]], having previously collaborated with them on ''[[Get Out]]'' and ''[[BlacKkKlansman]]''. Filming took place in California, including [[Los Angeles]], [[Pasadena, California|Pasadena]] and [[Santa Cruz, California|Santa Cruz]], from July to October 2018.

''Us'' premiered at [[South by Southwest]] on March 8, 2019, and was theatrically released in the United States on March 22, by [[Universal Pictures]]. The film was a commercial success, grossing $256 million against a budget of $20 million, and received widespread critical acclaim, having been included in various lists of the greatest horror films of the 21st century.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/greatest-horror-movies-of-the-21st-century-103994/us-2019-905314/|title=65 Greatest Horror Movies of the 21st Century|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date= October 27, 2020|access-date=June 15, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/25-best-horror-movies-of-the-21st-century-ranked/us-2019-2/|title=The 25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century, Ranked|magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date= July 23, 2025|access-date=June 15, 2026}}</ref>

==Plot==
In 1986, little Adelaide Thomas wanders away from her parents at the [[Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk]] and enters a [[funhouse]]. She encounters a [[doppelgänger]] of herself in the [[house of mirrors]]. Following the encounter, she suddenly stops speaking, and withdraws from her family.

Thirty-three years later, Adelaide, now a woman, reluctantly joins her husband, Gabe Wilson, and their children, Zora and Jason, on a vacation. They are joined by friends, Josh and Kitty Tyler, along with their twin daughters, Becca and Lindsey, at the beach. During their outing, they observe paramedics removing the body of a man holding a sign that matches one Adelaide had seen on the day she encountered her doppelgänger, as a small girl. Later, Jason spots an individual bearing a striking resemblance to the deceased, motionless with outstretched arms and bloodied hands.

That night, Adelaide relays her story to Gabe, when the lights suddenly go out. Jason notices a family of four in the driveway, who break into the house and corner the Wilsons. The intruders are the Wilsons' doppelgängers: Red (Adelaide's doppelganger, who she met all those years ago), Pluto (Jason's pyromaniac, facially scarred double), Umbrae (Zora's sadistic double) and Abraham (Gabe's animalistic double). Red is the only double who can speak, albeit in a raspy voice. She explains that they are called "the Tethered", as they share a soul with their counterparts and have come to "untether" themselves. The Wilsons are separated and terrorized by their doppelgängers. Jason discovers that Pluto mirrors his actions. After Gabe kills Abraham, the family escapes.

Meanwhile, the Tyler family is attacked and murdered in their home by their doppelgängers. The Wilsons arrive and are attacked as well, but they manage to overpower and kill the Tylers' doubles. They watch the news and learn that the Tethered have been murdering their equivalents across the city then joining hands to form a human chain (similar to the one from the [[Hands Across America]] demonstration) that surrounds the city. The Wilsons decide to drive along the coast and escape to Mexico. While they are leaving, Umbrae intercepts the car but Zora throws her off the car and kills her; Adelaide goes separately to comfort Umbrae as she dies.

Arriving at the boardwalk, the Wilsons find many townspeople [[slaughtered]]. The road is blocked by a burning car. Jason, realizing it is a trap set by Pluto, orders everyone out of the car. Before Pluto can ignite the family's car, Jason walks backwards, causing Pluto to do the same and thus walk directly into the fire, to Adelaide's sadness. While the Wilsons are distracted by Pluto burning to death, Red appears and snatches Jason away. Adelaide chases Red to the funhouse where they first met and finds a secret entrance that leads to an underground facility overrun by white [[rabbits]], where she finds Red in a classroom.

Red vaguely explains that the Tethered are duplicates created, presumably by the [[government]], to control the populace.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ramella |first1=Brynne |title=Us' Tethered Explained |url=https://screenrant.com/us-movie-government-experiment-tethered-underground-tunnels-explained/ |website=[[Screen Rant]] |date=November 3, 2020 |access-date=27 October 2023}}</ref> When the experiment failed, the Tethered were abandoned underground mindlessly mimicking the actions of their counterparts and surviving on raw rabbit meat. After the other doppelgängers realized Red was "different", when she snapped them out of their lethargy, she spent years organizing them to escape and take vengeance by murdering their counterparts. Red and Adelaide begin to fight, but Red evades and counters all of Adelaide's attacks. When Adelaide allows Red to attack, she impales Red with a fireplace poker, then strangles her and breaks her neck. She rescues Jason from a locker with Jason also rescuing one of the rabbits.

As she escapes in an [[ambulance]] with her family, Adelaide reflects back to the night she first met Red: it's revealed that she is the Tethered doppelgänger and that Red was the original Adelaide. The doppelgänger, having choked Adelaide unconscious, dragged and trapped her underground then returned to the surface to usurp her life. Jason looks at his mother who simply smiles, while the Tethered form a human chain that stretches across the [[countryside]].

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Jordan Peele (14532261920) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|alt=Jordan Peele at the 73rd Annual Peabody Awards in 2014.|Writer, director, and producer [[Jordan Peele]]]]
After being dismayed with the "genre confusion" over his previous film, ''[[Get Out]]'',<ref name="RS 2019" /> Peele opted to make his next film a "full-on" horror film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://twitter.com/JordanPeele/status/1107355349177257984|title='Us' is a horror movie.|last=Peele|first=Jordan|date=March 17, 2019|website=[[Twitter]]|language=en|access-date=April 6, 2019}}</ref> Peele has said that an inspiration for ''Us'' was ''[[The Twilight Zone (1959 TV series)|The Twilight Zone]]'' episode "[[Mirror Image (The Twilight Zone)|Mirror Image]]", which was centered on a young woman and her evil doppelgänger.<ref name="RS 2019">{{cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-features/director-jordan-peele-new-movie-cover-story-782743/ |title=The All-American Nightmares of Jordan Peele |last=Hiatt |first=Brian |date=January 29, 2019 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |access-date=February 1, 2019}}</ref>

Peele also revealed that the idea of the Tethered living underground came to him when he was a teenager taking the train home from studying at [[Sarah Lawrence College]]:
{{Blockquote|quote="You get out of the train and you have to go down through an underpass and come out the other side. There's no one else there, just this dark, American town. I'd come up and I'd look over to the other side, and I'd picture seeing the tail end of myself going down that same tunnel, to presumably emerge right near me 30 seconds later. And that’s where I love to start with a horror story: ‘What is this primal thing that’s affecting me in a way I don’t quite understand?’"|source=Jordan Peele<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/mar/09/jordan-peele-on-us-this-is-a-very-different-movie-from-get-out|title=Jordan Peele on Us: 'This is a very different movie from Get Out'|last=Rose|first=Steve|website=[[The Guardian]]|date=March 9, 2019 |access-date=November 9, 2020}}</ref>}}

===Casting===
On May 8, 2018, it was announced that [[Lupita Nyong'o]], [[Winston Duke]] and [[Elisabeth Moss]] were all in negotiations to star in the film, with Nyong'o and Duke portraying a black couple, and Moss portraying one half of a white couple.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/jordan-peeles-us-casts-winston-duke-lupita-nyong-o-elisabeth-moss-1110023|title=Winston Duke, Lupita Nyong'o, Elisabeth Moss Circling New Jordan Peele Movie 'Us' (Exclusive)|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=May 8, 2018 |access-date=November 9, 2020}}</ref> Nyong'o later confirmed her casting by posting the film's promotional poster on her [[Instagram]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.instagram.com/p/Bi14janDtcd/ |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/iarchive/s/instagram/Bi14janDtcd |archive-date=December 26, 2021 |url-access=registration|title=Lupita Nyong'o on Instagram|website=[[Instagram]]|access-date=November 9, 2020}}{{cbignore}}</ref> The rest of the cast, including [[Tim Heidecker]], [[Yahya Abdul-Mateen II]], [[Shahadi Wright Joseph]] and Evan Alex were all confirmed in July of that year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/jordan-peele-thriller-us-rounds-cast-1130708|title=Jordan Peele Thriller 'Us' Rounds Out Cast|last=Galuppo|first=Mia|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=July 30, 2018 |access-date=November 9, 2020}}</ref>

Peele saw the characters of the film as an "archetypal foursome", with Adelaide being the leader, Zora being the warrior, Gabe being jester/fool and Jason being the wiz/magician.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gB1aA1iAsYA| archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211110/gB1aA1iAsYA| archive-date=2021-11-10 | url-status=live|title=Us {{!}} Jordan Peele Gives Us a Glimpse Into Us|website=[[YouTube]]| date=July 24, 2020|access-date=November 9, 2020}}{{cbignore}}</ref> For her role, Nyong'o had to use a different voice for the character of Red. She said her performance was inspired by the condition [[spasmodic dysphonia]], a condition that causes a person's voice to go into periods of [[spasm]]. In order to perfect her voice, she "worked with an ear, nose, and throat doctor, a vocal therapist, and my dialect coach to try and make sure I could do it and do it safely. 'Cause I had two roles to play, I couldn't afford to damage my voice."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2019/03/lupita-nyongo-in-jordan-peeles-us-terrifying/585649/|title=The Kinetic Horror of Lupita Nyong'o's Us Performance|website=[[The Atlantic]]|last=Giorgis|first=Hannah|date=March 26, 2019 |access-date=November 9, 2020}}</ref>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] began on July 30, 2018, in [[Santa Cruz, California]], including their famous [[Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk|Boardwalk]]. Most of the film was shot in [[Los Angeles]], and the main house featured is located in [[Pasadena, California|Pasadena]]. The house had modifications and the team spent six weeks there.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/Jordan-Peele-filming-Santa-Cruz-nyongo-moss-duke-13227464.php|title=Jordan Peele filming movie in Santa Cruz with Lupita Nyong'o, Elisabeth Moss, Winston Duke|last1=Pereira|first1=Alyssa|last2=SFGATE|date=September 13, 2018|website=SFGate|access-date=February 28, 2019}}</ref> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on October 8, 2018.<ref name="ProductionStart">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2018/07/jordan-peeles-us-starts-production-rounds-out-cast-1202436630/|title=Jordan Peele's 'Us' Starts Production, Rounds Out Cast|last=D'Alessandro|first=Anthony|date=July 30, 2018|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=July 30, 2018}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{Main|Us (soundtrack)}}
The 1995 [[Luniz]] song "[[I Got 5 on It]]" is heavily featured in this film, first at the beginning, when the family is driving to the beach and then later on in the film when the "tethered" family breaks into the vacation home. The once-fun song transmogrifies into an eerily orchestral "Tethered Mix", slowing everything down, and fully indulging the ominous quality of the film. Due to the track's popularity upon the trailer's release, it was edited into the final cut of the film, appearing during the climax.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/i-got-5-on-it-luniz-jordan-peele-us/|title=Luniz 'I Got 5 on It': The Origin of the Secret Weapon in Jordan Peele's 'Us'|date=2019-03-28|website=TheWrap|language=en-US|access-date=2019-04-29}}</ref> [[Waxwork Records]] announced in August 2019 that they would release the soundtrack to the film on vinyl, which contained Abels's popular score.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/jordan-peele-us-soundtrack-vinyl-release-8527864/|title=Jordan Peele's 'Us' Soundtrack Released on Vinyl: Exclusive|location=Billboard|author=Saponara, Michael|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |date=23 August 2019|access-date=7 August 2020}}</ref> The [[N.W.A]] song "[[Fuck tha Police]]" is also heard when Kitty Tyler tells the voice assistant Ophelia to "call the police", moments before she is killed by her Tethered counterpart, Dahlia, and the song plays throughout when the Wilsons enter the Tyler family's home. The ending features the [[Minnie Riperton]] song "[[Les Fleur]]s".

===Critical response===
[[File:Lupita Nyong'o SXSW 2019 (47282558132).jpg|thumb|upright|[[Lupita Nyong'o]] received widespread critical acclaim for her dual role performance in the film.]]
{{RT data|prose}} The website's critical consensus reads, "With Jordan Peele's second inventive, ambitious horror film, we have seen how to beat the sophomore jinx, and it is ''Us''."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/us_2019|title=Us (2019)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted average]] score of 81 out of 100, based on 56 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/us |title=Us Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=March 26, 2019}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale, while those at [[PostTrak]] gave it an overall positive score of 80%, with 60% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name=opening/> ''Us'' was described by ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' as "spill-your-soda scary", compared to the "existentially terrifying" ''Get Out''.<ref name="RS 2019" />

Monica Castillo of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film four out of four, writing that: "''Us'' is another thrilling exploration of the past and oppression this country is still too afraid to bring up. Peele wants us to talk, and he's given audiences the material to think, to feel our way through some of the darker sides of the human condition and the American experience."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/us-2019|title=Us Movie Review & Film Summary (2019) {{!}} Roger Ebert| last=Castillo|first=Monica|website=www.rogerebert.com|language=en|access-date=March 9, 2019}}</ref> David Griffin of ''[[IGN]]'' gave the film 9.0/10, calling it "a very, very strange film. But that's OK because it wouldn't be a Jordan Peele joint if there wasn't a little risk involved. Peele has proven that he's not a one-hit wonder with this truly terrifying, poignant look at one American family that goes through hell at the hands of maniacal doppelgangers".<ref name="ign">{{cite web|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2019/03/09/us-review|title=Us Review|last=Griffin|first=David|work=[[IGN]]|date=March 9, 2019|access-date=March 9, 2019|language=en}}</ref> John DeFore of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' called ''Us'' "a fiercely scary movie whose meaning is up for grabs".<ref name="thr">{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/us-review-1193550|title='Us': Film Review - SXSW 2019|last=DeFore|first=John|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=March 8, 2019|access-date=March 21, 2019}}</ref>

[[Richard Brody]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' called the film a "colossal achievement," writing, "''Us'' is a horror film—though saying so is like offering a reminder that ''[[The Godfather]]'' is a gangster film or that ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey (film)|2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' is science fiction. Genre is irrelevant to the merits of a film, whether its conventions are followed or defied; what matters is that Peele cites the tropes and precedents of horror in order to deeply root his film in the terrain of pop culture—and then to pull up those roots."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/review-jordan-peeles-us-is-a-colossal-cinematic-achievement|title='Review: Jordan Peele's "Us" Is a Colossal Cinematic Achievement|last=Brody|first=Richard|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|date=March 23, 2019|access-date=March 23, 2019}}</ref>

In July 2025, ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' ranked it number 12 on its list of the "25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century."<ref>{{cite web |date=July 23, 2025 |title=25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century, Ranked|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/25-best-horror-movies-of-the-21st-century-ranked/sinners-2025/ |access-date=2025-08-02}}</ref>

Conversely, [[Stephanie Zacharek]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' thought Peele had too many ideas and not enough answers compared to ''Get Out'' and said, "Peele goes even deeper into the conflicted territory of class and race and privilege; he also ponders the traits that make us most human. But this time, he's got so many ideas he can barely corral them, let alone connect them. He overthinks himself into a corner, and we're stuck there with him."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://time.com/5552617/review-us-jordan-peele/|title=Review: Jordan Peele's Us Is Dazzling to Look At. But What Is It Trying to Say?|last=Zacharek|first=Stephanie|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]|date=March 15, 2019|access-date=March 21, 2019}}</ref>

[[Disability rights movement|Disability rights]] groups National Spasmodic Dysphonia Association and [[RespectAbility]] criticised Nyong'o for basing her performance on [[spasmodic dysphonia]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-03-29 |title=Lupita Nyong'o under fire from disability groups for 'evil' voice in Us |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/mar/29/horror-film-us-under-fire-from-disability-groups-over-lupita-nyongo-evil-voice-jordan-peele-film |access-date=2022-09-30 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref> She later apologized, stating that she "wasn’t interested in vilifying or demonising the condition," and "crafted Red with love and care."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-04-01 |title=Lupita Nyong'o apologises after Us 'evil' voice disability row |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/apr/01/lupita-nyongo-apologises-after-us-disability-voice-row |access-date=2022-09-30 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref>

==Themes and interpretations==
Critic Jim Vejvoda related the Tethered to "[[urban legend]]s" and "[[xenophobia|xenophobic]] paranoia about the [[Other (philosophy)|Other]]", also writing they resembled the [[Morlock]]s in [[H. G. Wells]]'s 1895 novel, ''[[The Time Machine]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://ca.ign.com/articles/2019/03/23/us-movie-spoilers-ending-explained-what-was-hands-across-america-jeremiah-1111-what-are-the-tethers |title=Us Movie Explained: Hands Across America, Jeremiah 11:11, And The Tethers |website=[[IGN]] |publisher=[[j2 Global]]|location=Los Angeles, California|first=Jim |last=Vejvoda |date=March 23, 2019 |access-date=March 23, 2019}}</ref> Journalist Noel Ransome viewed the film as being about "the effects of [[classism]] and marginalization", writing "the Tethered are effigies of this same situational classism. They're trapped—mentally and physically—and ignored".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/jordan-peeles-us-is-not-so-secretly-about-class/ |title=Jordan Peele's 'Us' Is Not-so-Secretly About Class |website=[[Vice.com]] |publisher=[[Vice Media]]|location=New York City|first=Noel |last=Ransome |date=March 25, 2019 |access-date=March 26, 2019 }}</ref> Joel Meares of Rotten Tomatoes also noted that the Tethered, referencing the "we're Americans" line, are representatives of the duality of American society, how some citizens can afford to live on top of the class system while others are stuck in poverty. He also noted the title ''Us'' could mean "U.S.", or United States.<ref name="RTtheory">{{cite web|url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/jordan-peeles-us-explained-the-big-twists-references-themes-and-allusions/ |title=Jordan Peele's Us Explained: The Big Twists, References, Themes, And Allusions |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|location=Beverly Hills, California|first=Joel|last=Meares|date=March 28, 2019 |access-date=March 29, 2019}}</ref>

[[Manohla Dargis]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' notes that the Wilsons are "introduced with an aerial sweep of greenery" similar to the opening of [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'', and sees that movie as the principal influence on ''Us''. Describing Peele as a "true cinephile", she also identifies allusions to other films, including ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]'', ''[[A Nightmare on Elm Street]]'', and ''[[The Goonies]]'', as well as one scene suggesting an influence by the Austrian film director [[Michael Haneke]]'s 1997 horror film ''[[Funny Games (1997 film)|Funny Games]]'' and subsequent [[Funny Games (2007 film)|U.S. remake]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Dargis |first=Manohla |date=March 20, 2019 |title= 'Us' Review: Jordan Peele's Creepy Latest Turns a Funhouse Mirror on Us |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2019/03/20/movies/us-movie-review.html |work =[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

The Tethered's red jump suits and single glove were an allusion to [[Michael Jackson]], as was the "[[Thriller (song)|Thriller]]" shirt seen on young Adelaide, and Peele has stated that Jackson was "the patron saint of duality".<ref name=RTtheory/><ref name="mj">{{cite web|url=https://mashable.com/article/jordan-peele-michael-jackson-us-movie/ |title=Jordan Peele explains the significance of the Michael Jackson imagery in 'Us' |website=[[Mashable]] |first=Rachel|last=Thompson|date=March 19, 2019 |access-date=March 29, 2019}}</ref> Peele referenced many other instances of 1980s culture, including allusions to ''[[The Lost Boys]]'' and Hands Across America, stating "Everything in this movie was deliberate, that is one thing I can guarantee you. Unless you didn't like something and that was a complete accident".<ref name="mj"/><ref>{{cite web | last=Ryan | first=Mike | title=Jordan Peele On Why He Hid Three Corey Feldman Easter Eggs In 'Us' | website=[[Uproxx]] | publisher=Uproxx Media Group|location=Culver City, California|date=March 19, 2019 | url=https://uproxx.com/movies/jordan-peele-interview-us-corey-feldman/ | access-date=April 1, 2019}}</ref>

The film contains numerous references to [[Jeremiah 11|Jeremiah 11:11]], which reads: "Therefore thus saith the Lord: 'I will bring on them a disaster they cannot escape. Although they cry out to me, I will not listen to them'" ([[New International Version|NIV]]). Critic Rosie Fletcher commented on the context, with [[Jeremiah]] warning [[Jerusalem]] was facing destruction due to [[false idol]]s, and expressed the opinion that the film's characters also "worshiped the wrong things", such as Ophelia, the [[virtual assistant]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/us-jeremiah-1111-jordan-peele-movie/ |title=Us: How Jeremiah 11:11 Fits in Jordan Peele Movie |website=[[Den of Geek]] |publisher=Dennis Publishing|location=London, England|first=Rosie |last=Fletcher |date=March 23, 2019 |access-date=March 23, 2019}}</ref>

Peele later explained in the film's digital release special features that a central theme of the film is [[First World privilege|American privilege]]:<ref name="SF Privilege">{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/jordan-peele-explains-how-privilege-influenced-us-in-an-1835225625 |title=Jordan Peele explains how privilege influenced Us in an exclusive clip |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |publisher=[[j2 Global]]|location=Los Angeles, California|first=Shannon |last=Miller |date=June 4, 2019 |access-date=June 7, 2019}}</ref>
{{Blockquote|quote="One of the central themes in ''Us'' is that we can do a good job collectively of ignoring the ramifications of [[social privilege|privilege]]. I think it's the idea that what we feel like we deserve comes, you know, at the expense of someone else's freedom or joy. You know, the biggest disservice we can do as a faction with a collective privilege like the United States is to presume that we deserve it, and that it isn't luck that has us born where we're born. For us to have our privilege, someone suffers. That's where the Tethered connection, I think, resonates the most, is that those who suffer and those who prosper are two sides of the same coin. You can never forget that. We need to fight for the less fortunate."|source=Jordan Peele<ref name="SF Privilege"/>}}
