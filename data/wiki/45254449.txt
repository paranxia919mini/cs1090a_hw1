'''''Tangerine''''' is a 2015 American [[independent film|independent]] [[comedy-drama]] film directed, produced, and edited by [[Sean Baker]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Chris Bergoch]]. It follows a [[transgender sex worker]] as she discovers that her boyfriend, who is also her pimp, has been cheating on her. The film stars [[Kitana Kiki Rodriguez]], [[Mya Taylor]], and [[James Ransone]].

''Tangerine'' was shot on three [[iPhone 5s]] smartphones.<ref name="nash">{{Cite news |last=Shawhan |first=Jason |date=2015-08-06 |title=Beyond using progressive filming techniques and casting, Tangerine is expressive and warm |url=http://www.nashvillescene.com/nashville/beyond-using-progressive-filming-techniques-and-casting-tangerine-is-expressive-and-warm/Content?oid=5381312 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304050330/http://www.nashvillescene.com/nashville/beyond-using-progressive-filming-techniques-and-casting-tangerine-is-expressive-and-warm/Content?oid=5381312 |archive-date=March 4, 2016 |access-date=2025-03-07 |work=[[Nashville Scene]] |language=en }}</ref> It premiered at the [[2015 Sundance Film Festival]] on January 23 and had a [[limited release]] through [[Magnolia Pictures]] on July 10. It grossed $935,984<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Tangerine | title=Tangerine (2015) | work=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] | publisher=Nash Information Services, LLC. | access-date=April 8, 2018}}</ref> on a budget of $100,000<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.wired.com/2015/07/tangerine-iphone/ |title=Tangerine Is Amazing—But Not Because of How They Shot It |magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]] |date=July 7, 2015 |first=Angela |last=Watercutter |access-date=August 19, 2015}}</ref> and received critical acclaim for its screenplay, direction, performances, and portrayal of [[transgender]] individuals.

== Plot ==
[[Transgender sex worker]] Sin-Dee Rella, who has just finished a 28-day jail sentence, meets her friend Alexandra, another trans sex worker, at a donut shop in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]] on Christmas Eve. Alexandra reveals that Sin-Dee's boyfriend and [[Procuring (prostitution)|pimp]] Chester has been cheating on her with another woman. Sin-Dee storms out to search the neighborhood for Chester and the woman.

Alexandra hands out flyers for her musical performance that evening and argues with a client who refuses to pay; their argument is broken up by the police. Razmik, an [[Armenian Americans|Armenian]] cab driver, picks up a prostitute, but ejects her from his cab when he discovers she is not [[transgender]]. He meets Alexandra and fellates her in a car wash, then goes home to eat Christmas Eve dinner with his family. Alexandra goes to the bar for her performance, but no customers have arrived.

Sin-Dee finds the woman she is looking for, Dinah, at a [[brothel]] in a motel. She hauls her onto a bus to find Chester. Dinah taunts her for believing she is Chester's only girlfriend. Sin-Dee realizes she is late for Alexandra's performance and drags Dinah there instead. She reconciles with Dinah as they smoke [[Methamphetamine|crystal meth]] in the bar bathroom, and Sin-Dee applies Dinah's make-up. Alexandra performs to a mostly empty bar.

Razmik leaves his family to attend Alexandra's performance, saying he has to work, but discovers he is too late and searches for Sin-Dee. Suspicious, his mother-in-law, Ashken, hails another Armenian cab driver, Karo. The driver knows about Razmik's interests, and while initially hesitant, Karo agrees to help her track down Razmik.

Sin-Dee, Alexandra, and Dinah go to the donut shop, where Sin-Dee confronts Chester. He insists Dinah means nothing to him and reveals that he and Sin-Dee are engaged. Razmik arrives to buy sex from Sin-Dee, but he is followed by his mother-in-law. Ashken at first thinks Razmik's secret is that he is smoking [[Cannabis (drug)|marijuana]], but Chester explains that he has sex with prostitutes. She calls Razmik's wife, who arrives with their infant daughter. An argument among the eight escalates until the shop owner kicks them out. Razmik's wife is upset, but she tells her mother to mind her own business. He and his family go back to their apartment. Dinah walks back to the brothel but is told there is no room for her.

Outside the donut shop, Chester tells Sin-Dee that he also slept with Alexandra. Hurt, Sin-Dee leaves and tries to pick up some clients; they throw urine in her face and drive away shouting [[Transphobia|transphobic]] slurs. Alexandra takes Sin-Dee to a laundromat to clean her clothes and wig, and gives Sin-Dee her wig to wear while they wait.

== Production ==
[[Mark Duplass]] offered to fund a [[micro-budget]] film by [[Sean Baker (filmmaker)|Sean Baker]] before Baker considered ''Tangerine''. After a two-year festival run for his previous film ''[[Starlet (film)|Starlet]]'', and inspired by the independent films he saw at the [[New Zealand Film Festival]], Baker called Duplass to see if the offer still stood and began working on his next film. ''Tangerine'' was executive produced by Duplass and his brother [[Jay Duplass|Jay]], and produced by Karrie Cox and Marcus Cox with Through Films, as well as Darren Dean and [[Shih-Ching Tsou]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Canfield|first1=David|title=Magnolia Acquires Duplass Brothers-Produced 'Tangerine' After Acclaimed Sundance Debut|url=https://www.indiewire.com/article/magnolia-acquires-duplass-brothers-produced-tangerine-after-acclaimed-sundance-debut-20150127|access-date=30 January 2015|publisher=[[IndieWire]]|date=27 January 2015}}</ref>

Baker and [[Chris Bergoch]] wrote the screenplay together from September to December 2013.<ref name="cweekly">{{Cite web |last=Stein |first=Sophia |date=2015-07-16 |title=Tangerine – From Wonderful Wizard Sean Baker - Cultural Daily |url=https://www.culturaldaily.com/tangerine-from-wonderful-wizard-sean-baker/ |access-date=2025-03-07 |website=www.culturaldaily.com |language=en-US}}</ref> They met transgender actresses [[Mya Taylor]] and [[Kitana Kiki Rodriguez]], who had no major acting experience, at the [[Los Angeles LGBT Center]] in 2013.<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite news|last1=Jacobs |first1=Matthew|title='Tangerine' May Have Had A Tiny Budget, But The Film's Heart Is Bigger Because Of It|url=https://www.huffingtonpost.com/entry/tangerine-movie-transgender_us_559bc990e4b05d7587e22881 |newspaper=[[HuffPost]]|date=July 9, 2015}}</ref>

[[Principal photography]] began on December 24, 2014, and wrapped on January 18, 2015, taking place in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]] locations such as [[West Hollywood]] clubs and [[Santa Monica Boulevard]]. After viewing iPhone experiments on [[Vimeo]], Baker and cinematographer Radium Cheung shot the film using three [[iPhone 5s]] smartphones.<ref name=":0">{{cite news|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/sean-bakers-tangerine-shot-on-iphone-5s/|title=Sundance Shocker: Sean Baker's Tangerine Was Shot Entirely on iPhone 5s|date=January 2015|publisher=Slash Film|last1=Sciretta|first1=Peter|access-date=29 January 2015}}</ref> The money saved on equipment was used to pay extras and book filming locations. They used FiLMIC Pro (a camera app that allows iPhone users to [[Focus puller|pull focus]], control the [[aperture]] and [[Color correction|color temperature]] of a shot, and capture video at higher [[bit rate]]s, among other features) and an [[Anamorphic format|anamorphic]] lens adapter from Moondog Labs to shoot in a widescreen format, as well as [[Tiffen (company)|Tiffen]]'s [[Steadicam]] Smoothee to ensure smooth camera movements.<ref name="HDVideo">{{cite web |last=Matsumoto |first=Neil |date=14 July 2015 |title=Down The Street |url=http://www.hdvideopro.com/film-and-tv/feature-films/down-the-street |access-date=8 August 2015 |work=HD Video Pro |publisher=Werner Publishing Corp.}}</ref>

Baker was able to get three Moondog Labs anamorphic adapters for free by contacting the company, as the adapters were still in their prototype stage on [[Kickstarter]], and he informed them that he was working with Mark Duplass on the project. He told them it would let the film crew "shoot the way [[Sergio Leone]] would shoot westerns".<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2015/07/06/movies/sean-baker-talks-tangerine-and-making-a-movie-with-an-iphone.html|title=Sean Baker Talks 'Tangerine,' and Making a Movie With an iPhone|last=Murphy|first=Mekado|date=2015-07-05|work=The New York Times|access-date=2019-08-07|language=en-US|issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

Baker edited the film himself, using [[Final Cut Pro]] for a preliminary cut and [[DaVinci Resolve]] for color grading.<ref name="HDVideo" />

==Reception ==
[[Review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reports that {{RT data|score}} of {{RT data|count}} critics gave the film a positive review, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The site's critics consensus reads: "''Tangerine'' shatters casting conventions and its filmmaking techniques are up-to-the-minute, but it's an old-fashioned comedy at heart—and a pretty wonderful one at that."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/tangerine_2015|title=Tangerine (2015)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a weighted average score of 86 out of 100 based on 35 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/tangerine |title=Tangerine Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=April 23, 2018}}</ref>

''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' described the film as "a singularly delightful girlfriend movie with an attitude".<ref>{{cite news|last1=Rooney|first1=David|title='Tangerine': Sundance Review|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/tangerine-sundance-review-767066|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=30 January 2015}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]''{{'}}s [[Justin Chang]] wrote that ''Tangerine'' is "an exuberantly raw and up-close portrait of one of Los Angeles' more distinctive sex-trade subcultures."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Chang |first1=Justin |title=Sundance Film Review: 'Tangerine'|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/reviews/sundance-film-review-tangerine-1201414093/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=30 January 2015}}</ref> ''A. V. Wire''{{'}}s Seth Malvín Romero said: "''Tangerine'' is a stylistic tour-de-force. An original, dazzling, and unforgettable portrayal of betrayal and friendship that easily bests any other film this year."<ref>{{cite web|last1=Romero|first1=Seth|title=A. V. Wire Film Review: 'Tangerine' |url=http://avwire.com/2015/08/05/film-review-tangerine/|access-date=5 August 2015}}</ref> In a positive review, ''[[The A.V. Club]]''{{'}}s [[Ignatiy Vishnevetsky]] wrote that "for all of ''Tangerine's'' movement [...] and all of its slapping and arguing, it's the movie's quietest, softest moments that register most strongly." He concluded: "Perhaps these moments feel so graceful because they are oases; they matter because of the harsh, unforgiving terrain that has to be crossed in order to reach them. This terrain is drawn garishly, vividly, and with a sense of fun."<ref>{{Cite web|title = Tangerine is the frenetic transgender iPhone Christmas movie of the year|url = https://www.avclub.com/review/tangerine-frenetic-transgender-i-phone-christmas-m-221937|website = www.avclub.com| date=July 9, 2015 |access-date = 2015-11-17}}</ref>
