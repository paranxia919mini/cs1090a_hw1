'''''The Brave Bulls''''' is a 1951 American drama film directed by [[Robert Rossen]], written by [[John Bright (screenwriter)|John Bright]] and starring [[Mel Ferrer]], [[Miroslava (actress)|Miroslava]], [[Anthony Quinn]], [[Eugene Iglesias]], José Torvay and Charlita. The film was released on April 18, 1951 by [[Columbia Pictures]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/69589/the-brave-bulls |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121022214507/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/69589/The-Brave-Bulls/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=October 22, 2012 |title=The Brave Bulls (1951) - Overview |publisher=Turner Classic Movies |date= |access-date=2015-10-09}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.afi.com/members/catalog/DetailView.aspx?s=&Movie=50039 |title=The Brave Bulls |publisher=Afi.com |date= |access-date=2015-10-09}}</ref>

Although the film features extensive footage of [[bullfighting]] activity, the [[Hays Code]] prohibited the inclusion of graphic depictions of the killing of the bulls.<ref name="nytreview">{{cite news |last=Crowther |first=Bosley |date=1951-04-19 |title=The Screen in Review |work=[[The New York Times]] |page=39}}</ref>

== Reception ==
In a contemporary review for ''[[The New York Times]]'', critic [[Bosley Crowther]] wrote: "Tom Lea's absorbing story of a bull-fighter's battle against fear, told with such flavor and ferocity in his popular novel, 'The Brave Bulls,' has been brought to the screen with all the richness and vitality of the book in Robert Rossen's brilliant translation, which arrived at the Rivoli yesterday. Here, at last, is a picture that captures in its raw and savage power the full fascination and repulsion of bull-fighting in the lives of men."<ref name="nytreview" />
