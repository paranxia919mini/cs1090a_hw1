'''''Downfall''''' ({{langx|de|'''Der Untergang'''}}) is a 2004 German [[historical drama|historical]] [[war film|war]] [[drama film]] written and produced by [[Bernd Eichinger]] and directed by [[Oliver Hirschbiegel]]. It depicts the final days of [[Adolf Hitler]] (portrayed by [[Bruno Ganz]]), during the [[Battle of Berlin]] in [[World War II]], when [[Nazi Germany]] is on the verge of total defeat at the hands of the [[Allies of World War II|Allies]]. The cast includes [[Alexandra Maria Lara]], [[Corinna Harfouch]], [[Ulrich Matthes]], [[Juliane Köhler]], [[Heino Ferch]], [[Christian Berkel]], [[Alexander Held]], [[Matthias Habich]], and [[Thomas Kretschmann]]. The film is a German-Austrian-Italian co-production.

Principal photography took place from September to November 2003, on location in [[Berlin]], [[Munich]], and [[Saint Petersburg]], [[Russia]]. As the film is set in and around the {{lang|de|[[Führerbunker]]}}, Hirschbiegel used eyewitness accounts, survivors' memoirs, and other historical sources during production to reconstruct the look and atmosphere of 1940s Berlin. The screenplay was based on the books ''[[Inside Hitler's Bunker]]'' by historian [[Joachim Fest]] and ''[[Until the Final Hour]]'' by [[Traudl Junge]], one of Hitler's secretaries, among other accounts of the period.

The film premiered at the [[Toronto International Film Festival|Toronto Film Festival]] on 14 September 2004. It was controversial with audiences for showing a human side of Hitler and members of the Third Reich.<ref name="DW">{{cite web|url=https://www.dw.com/en/controversial-hitler-film-opens-across-germany/a-1331328-1|title=Controversial Hitler Film Opens Across Germany|work=[[Deutsche Welle]]|date=17 September 2004|access-date=25 March 2019}}</ref> It later received a wide theatrical release in Germany under its production company [[Constantin Film]]. The film grossed over $92 million. ''Downfall'' was a critical and commercial success, with international acclaim for the cast's performances (especially Ganz's portrayal of Hitler), Hirschbiegel's direction, and Eichinger's screenplay. It was nominated for [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]] at the [[77th Academy Awards]].

==Plot==
<!-- WP:FILMPLOT recommends the plot summary to be between 400-700 words. -->
In November 1942, [[Adolf Hitler]] interviews young women for the position of personal secretary in the [[Wolf's Lair]] and chooses [[Traudl Junge]].

On 20 April 1945, Hitler's 56th birthday, Berlin comes under fire from [[Red Army]] artillery, beginning the [[Battle of Berlin]]. ''[[Reichsführer-SS]]'' [[Heinrich Himmler]], unable to persuade Hitler to flee Berlin, leaves to secretly negotiate with the [[Allies of World War II|Allies]]. ''[[Gruppenführer|SS-Gruppenführer]]'' [[Hermann Fegelein]], Himmler's liaison officer and brother-in-law to Hitler's companion [[Eva Braun]], is also unable to persuade Hitler to leave. SS doctor ''[[Obersturmbannführer]]'' [[Ernst-Günther Schenck]] remains in Berlin in defiance of [[Operation Clausewitz]], granted permission to safeguard the health of the civilian population. The father of [[Hitler Youth]] member Peter Kranz, who has been conscripted to fight, tries to persuade him to desert but Peter denounces his father.

At a meeting in the {{lang|de|[[Führerbunker]]}}, Hitler forbids the overwhelmed [[9th Army (Wehrmacht)|9th Army]] to retreat, ordering ''[[Obergruppenführer]]'' [[Felix Steiner]]'s [[Army Detachment Steiner|units]] and [[General der Panzertruppe|Lieutenant General]] [[Walther Wenck]]'s [[12th Army (Wehrmacht)|12th Army]] to counter-attack. Hitler makes a rare foray out of the bunker to award Peter, who has destroyed two enemy tanks, the [[Iron Cross]], hailing him as braver than his generals. Hitler then talks to [[Reich Ministry of Armaments and War Production|armaments minister]] [[Albert Speer]] about his [[Nero Decree|scorched-earth policy]]. Speer disagrees with the destruction of Germany's infrastructure, but Hitler believes the [[Untermensch|German people are weak]] and deserve death. Eva Braun holds a party in the [[Reich Chancellery]], which is broken up by artillery fire, and refuses Fegelein's pleas to leave Berlin with him.

[[File:Bundesarchiv Bild 183-V04744, Berlin, Garten der zerstörte Reichskanzlei.jpg|thumb|The real-life Fuhrerbunker as pictured in 1947]]

When General [[Helmuth Weidling]] learns he has been sentenced to death for allegedly moving his command post, he reports to the ''Führerbunker'' to clear himself. His action actually impresses Hitler, who promotes him commander of Berlin's defence. Weidling mutters that he would have rather the Führer carry out his death sentence instead.

When Hitler learns Steiner's weakened forces did not counter-attack as ordered, he launches into a furious tirade, denouncing his generals and for the first time openly acknowledges the war is lost. He promises to commit suicide rather than surrender or leave Berlin.

''[[SS-Brigadeführer]]'' [[Wilhelm Mohnke]] asks Propaganda Minister [[Joseph Goebbels]] to stop sending [[Volkssturm|inexperienced soldiers]] into the fighting, but Goebbels replies he has no sympathy and that the German people, who proved weaker than their enemies, deserve their fate. Hitler [[Göring telegram|receives a message]] from ''[[Reichsmarschall]]'' [[Hermann Göring]], requesting state leadership for himself in the absence of Hitler's freedom of action. Hitler orders Göring's arrest for treason. Speer makes a final visit to the ''Führerbunker'' and admits he has defied the scorched earth order. Hitler, emotionally overcome by the betrayal, dismisses Speer without punishment and lets him leave Berlin.

Hitler learns of Himmler's secret negotiations with the Allies and orders his execution. SS physician ''Obergruppenführer'' [[Ernst-Robert Grawitz]] is refused permission by Hitler to leave Berlin. Fearing Soviet reprisal, he kills himself and his family with hand grenades. Hitler discovers Fegelein's plans to desert and has him executed despite Braun's pleas.

As conditions worsen in Berlin, Hitler hopes Wenck's 12th Army will turn the tide. Hitler dictates his [[Last will and testament of Adolf Hitler|last will and testament]] to Junge before marrying Braun in an early morning ceremony. When Hitler learns that the 12th Army is unable to save Berlin, he plans his suicide, testing the [[Hydrogen cyanide|poison]] on his dog [[Blondi]]. After bidding farewell to his staff, and refusing a final plea by [[Magda Goebbels]] to escape, he [[Death of Adolf Hitler|commits suicide]] with Braun. The couple are cremated in the Chancellery garden.

General [[Hans Krebs (Wehrmacht general)|Hans Krebs]] fails to negotiate a ceasefire with Red Army [[Colonel General]] [[Vasily Chuikov]], who insists on Germany's [[German Instrument of Surrender|unconditional surrender]] and the full [[Soviet occupation zone|Soviet takeover of Berlin]]. Goebbels poisons [[Goebbels children|their six children]] with [[Cyanide poisoning|cyanide capsules]] before committing suicide. Krebs and [[Wilhelm Burgdorf]] also [[Mass suicides in 1945 Nazi Germany|commit suicide]] before Weidling announces the unconditional surrender of German forces in Berlin. Peter, sole survivor of his unit, discovers that his parents were executed. The remaining occupants of the bunker attempt to break out from the city. German diplomat SS-''[[Brigadeführer]]'' [[Walther Hewel]] commits suicide as well, despite Schenck's attempts to persuade him to live. Only Junge successfully escapes, joined by Peter. The two find an abandoned bicycle and escape Berlin.

On May 8, the German government, with [[Karl Dönitz]] as Chancellor and the capital moved to Flensburg, unconditionally surrendered to the Allied forces, marking the end of the war for Germany. The film concludes with documentary footage of an elderly Junge confessing that her youthful ignorance should have been no excuse for her involvement in the Nazi regime.

==Production==
===Development===
Producer and screenwriter [[Bernd Eichinger]] wanted to make a film about [[Adolf Hitler]] and the [[Nazi Party]] for twenty years but was, at first, discouraged after its enormity prevented him from doing so.<ref name="NYT"/> Eichinger was inspired to begin the filmmaking process after reading ''[[Inside Hitler's Bunker|Inside Hitler's Bunker: The Last Days of the Third Reich]]'' (2002) by historian [[Joachim Fest]].{{sfn|Vande Winkel|2007|p=187}}<ref name="Filmdownfall">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2005/mar/20/features.review1|title=Now the Germans have their say|last=Summers|first=Sue|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=20 March 2005|access-date=20 February 2019}}</ref><ref name="NYT">{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2004/09/15/world/europe/the-alltoohuman-hitler-on-your-big-screen.html|title=The All-Too-Human Hitler, on Your Big Screen|work=[[The New York Times]]|last=Landler|first=Mark|date=15 September 2004|access-date=14 November 2018}}</ref> Eichinger also based the film on the memoirs of [[Traudl Junge]], one of Hitler's secretaries, called ''[[Until the Final Hour|Until the Final Hour: Hitler's Last Secretary]]'' (2002).<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2005/02/14/back-in-the-bunker|title=Back in the Bunker|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|last=Denby|first=David|date=14 February 2005|access-date=3 January 2019}}</ref>{{sfn|Machtans|Ruehl|2012}} When writing the screenplay, he used the books ''[[Inside the Third Reich]]'' (1969), by [[Albert Speer]],<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://newrepublic.com/article/60957/pass-the-fault|title=Pass the Fault|magazine=[[The New Republic]]|last=Oren|first=Michael B.|date=4 July 2005|access-date=3 January 2019}}</ref> one of the highest-ranking Nazi officials to survive both the war and the Nuremberg trials; ''Hitler's Last Days: An Eye-Witness Account'' (1973), by [[Gerhard Boldt]];<ref name="hi story">{{cite journal|title=Whose Hi/story Is It? The U.S. Reception of Downfall|journal=New German Critique|volume=34|issue=3|pages=1–16|url=https://read.dukeupress.edu/new-german-critique/article/34/3%20(102)/1/32990/Whose-Hi-story-Is-It-The-U-S-Reception-of-Downfall|publisher=[[Duke University Press]]|last=Bathrik|first=David|date=1 November 2007|access-date=3 January 2019|doi=10.1215/0094033X-2007-008|doi-access=free}}</ref> ''Das Notlazarett unter der Reichskanzlei: Ein Arzt erlebt Hitlers Ende in Berlin'' (1995) by [[Ernst-Günther Schenck]]; and ''Soldat: Reflections of a German Soldier, 1936–1949'' (1992) by [[Siegfried Knappe]] as references.{{sfn|Niemi|2018}}

After completing the script for the film, Eichinger presented it to director [[Oliver Hirschbiegel]]. Though he was interested in exploring how the people of Germany "could have plumbed such depths", as a German, Hirschbiegel hesitated to take it as he "reacted to the idea of Nazism as a taboo". Hirschbiegel eventually agreed to helm the project, stating that he "noticed that it just wouldn't leave me in peace, and in my heart, before accepting the project, I knew that I had already opened myself up to it."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cineuropa.org/en/interview/51595/|title=The Downfall – Interview: Oliver Hirschbiegel • Director|publisher=Cineuropa|last=Trapani|first=Salvatore|date=5 February 2005|access-date=21 February 2019}}</ref>{{sfn|Niemi|2018}}

''Downfall'' is the first German film to broach the subject of Hitler straight-on since ''[[The Last Ten Days]]'' (1955).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.criterion.com/current/posts/458-opening-pandora-s-box|title=Opening Pandora's Box|date=2006|publisher=The Criterion Collection}}</ref>

===Casting===
When [[Bruno Ganz]], who was Hirschbiegel's first choice for the role, was offered the role of Hitler, being sent the screenplay and Fest's book, he was reluctant to accept the part, and many of his friends advised against it,<ref name="Spiegel"/><ref name="johnston"/> but he believed that the subject had "a fascinating side", and ultimately agreed to take the role.<ref name="Seeking">{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2005/mar/25/1|title=Desperately seeking Adolf|work=[[The Guardian]]|author1=Diver, Krysia |author2=Moss, Stephen|date=25 March 2003 |location=London |access-date=6 February 2009}}</ref> Ganz watched ''The Last Ten Days'', in which Albin Skoda played Hitler, which convinced Ganz that it was really possible to play the dictator.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/film-studies-in-the-bunker-cinema-s-long-struggle-with-the-image-of-hitler-8435.html | title=Film Studies: In the bunker: Cinema's long struggle with the image of | work=The Independent | date=27 March 2005 }}</ref> 
Ganz studied the [[Hitler and Mannerheim recording]] for four months to properly mimic Hitler's conversational voice and Austrian dialect. Ganz came to the conclusion that Hitler had [[Parkinson's disease]], noting his observation of Hitler's shaky body movements present in the newsreel ''[[Die Deutsche Wochenschau#Production|Die Deutsche Wochenschau]]'', and decided to visit a hospital to study patients with the disease.<ref name="Seeking"/> Ganz auditioned in the casting studio with makeup for half an hour and tested his voice for Hirschbiegel who was convinced by his performance.<ref name="Spiegel"/><ref name="aboutfilm"/>

[[Alexandra Maria Lara]] was cast as [[Traudl Junge]]; she was given Junge's book ''[[Until the Final Hour]]'' (2002), which she called her "personal treasure", to read during filming. Before she was cast, she had seen [[André Heller]]'s documentary film ''[[Blind Spot: Hitler's Secretary|Im toten Winkel]]'' which impressed her and influenced her perspective on Junge.<ref name="Filmreporter">{{cite web|url=https://filmreporter.de/stars/interview/223-Alexandra-Maria-Lara-ueber-ihr/2|title=Alexandra Maria Lara über ihr Gefühls-Chaos|last=Bonke|first=Johannes|publisher=Filmreporter.de|date=17 September 2004|language=de|access-date=23 February 2019|archive-date=24 February 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190224001635/https://filmreporter.de/stars/interview/223-Alexandra-Maria-Lara-ueber-ihr/2|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="planetinterview">{{cite web|url=http://www.planet-interview.de/interviews/alexandra-maria-lara/33863/|title=Das Böse kann niemals eindimensional sein.|last=Sarkar|first=David|publisher=Planet Interview|date=25 August 2004 |language=de |access-date=23 February 2019}}</ref>

===Filming and design===
[[File:Admiralteysky District, St Petersburg, Russia - panoramio (288).jpg|thumb|right|Filming took place near the [[Obvodny Canal]] in [[Saint Petersburg]] in a run-down industrial district to imitate the setting for Berlin.]]

Principal photography lasted twelve weeks from September to November 2003, under the [[working title]] ''Sunset''.<ref name="nytimes">{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2003/10/07/movies/a-war-torn-berlin-reborn-in-russia.html|title=A War-Torn Berlin Reborn in Russia|work=[[The New York Times]]|last=Varoli|first=John|date=7 October 2003|access-date=12 November 2018}}</ref>{{sfn|Niemi|2018}} The film is set mostly in and around the {{lang|de|[[Führerbunker]]}}; Hirschbiegel made an effort to accurately reconstruct the look and atmosphere of [[World War II]] through eyewitness accounts, survivors' [[memoirs]], and other historical sources. Hirschbiegel filmed in the cities of [[Berlin]], [[Munich]], and [[Saint Petersburg]], Russia, with a run-down industrial district along the [[Obvodny Canal]] used to portray the historical setting in Berlin.<ref name="nytimes"/><ref name="hitlerpic">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2003/film/news/hitler-pic-lands-in-russia-1117890770/|title=Hitler pic lands in Russia|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|last=Meza|first=Ed|date=12 August 2003|access-date=12 November 2018}}</ref> Hirschbiegel noted the depressing atmosphere surrounding the shoot, finding relief through listening to [[Johann Sebastian Bach]]'s music.<ref name="johnston"/> Alexandra Maria Lara also mentioned the depressing and intense atmosphere during filming. To lighten the mood, Lara's colleagues engaged in activities such as football, while Ganz tried to keep a happy mood by retiring during shooting breaks.<ref name="planetinterview"/>

The film was produced on a €13.5{{spaces}}million budget.<ref name="Spiegel"/> The bunker and Hitler's [[Wolf's Lair]] were constructed at [[Bavaria Studios]] in Munich by production designer Bernd Lepel.<ref name="aboutfilm">{{cite web|url=http://www.aboutfilm.com/features/downfall/feature.htm|title=Interviews: DOWNFALL|publisher=AboutFilm.Com|last=Cavagna|first=Carlo|access-date=14 November 2018}}</ref><ref name="variety"/> The damaged [[Reich Chancellery]] was depicted through the use of [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]]. Hirschbiegel decided to limit the use of CGI, props and sets so as not to make the set design look like that of a [[theatre]] production,<ref name="aboutfilm"/> explaining:
{{blockquote|quote=The only CGI shot that's been used in the film was the one with the [Reich Chancellery] because of course we could not reconstruct that{{snd}}that's the only thing. I'm very proud of that, because if you do a war movie, you cannot do that and build sets. You feel the cardboard. You feel that it's all made to entertain, and it takes away from that horror that war basically means.<ref name="aboutfilm"/>}}

==Themes==
According to Eichinger, the film's overlying idea was to make a film about Hitler and wartime Germany that was very close to historical truth, as part of a theme that would allow the German nation to save their own history and "experience their own trauma". To accomplish this, the film explores Hitler's decisions and motives during his final days through the perspective of the individuals who lived in the Führerbunker during those times.{{sfn|Mazierska|2011}} Eichinger chose not to include mention of the [[Holocaust]] because it was not the topic of the film. He also thought it was "impossible" to show the "misery" and "desperation" of the [[Nazi concentration camps|concentration camps]] cinematically.<ref name="DW"/><ref name="andreas"/>

===Portrayal===
During production, Hirschbiegel believed that Hitler would often charm people using his personality, only to manipulate and betray them.<ref name="johnston"/> Many of the people in the film, including Traudl Junge, are shown to be enthusiastic in interacting with Hitler instead of feeling threatened or anxious by his presence and authority. The production team sought to give Hitler a three-dimensional personality, with Hirschbiegel telling NBC: "We know from all accounts that he was a very charming man{{snd}}a man who managed to seduce a whole people into barbarism."<ref name="soft">{{cite web|url=https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna6019248 |title=Film showing Hitler's soft side stirs controversy |work=[[NBC News]]|last=Eckardt |first=Andy|date=16 September 2004 |access-date=12 December 2018}}</ref> He said Hitler was "like a shell", attracting people with self-pity, but inside the shell was only "an enormous will for destruction".<ref name="johnston"/>

The film explores the suicides and deaths of the Nazi Party as opposed to the people who choose life. Hitler's provision of cyanide pills to those in the bunker and the Goebbels' murder of their children are shown as selfish deeds while people such as [[Ernst-Günther Schenck|Schenck]], who chose to help the injured and escape death, are shown as rational and generous.{{sfn|Vande Winkel|2007}}{{sfn|Bangert|2014}} In the DVD commentary, Hirschbiegel said that the events in the film were "derived from the accounts, from descriptions of people" in the bunker.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.popmatters.com/downfall-2005-dvd-2496235231.html|title=Downfall (2004)|last=Fuchs|first=Cynthia|publisher=[[PopMatters]]|date=3 August 2005|access-date=26 February 2019}}</ref> The film also includes an introduction and closing with the real [[Traudl Junge|Junge]] in an interview from ''[[Blind Spot: Hitler's Secretary|Im toten Winkel]]'', where she admits feeling guilt for "not recognizing this monster in time".{{sfn|Bangert|2014}}

==Release==
''Downfall'' premiered at the [[Toronto International Film Festival|Toronto Film Festival]] on September 14, 2004.<ref name="hi story"/>{{sfn|Bendix|2007}} After first failing to find a distributor, the film was eventually released on September 16 in Germany by [[Constantin Film]].<ref name="Filmdownfall"/><ref name="theguardian_iankershaw"/> It premiered in the U.S. in Manhattan on February 18, 2005, under [[Newmarket Films]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2005/02/18/movies/the-last-days-of-hitler-raving-and-ravioli.html|title=The Last Days of Hitler: Raving and Ravioli|last=Scott|first=A. O.|work=[[The New York Times]]|date=18 February 2005|access-date=20 February 2019}}</ref> On its broadcast in the UK, Channel{{spaces}}4 marketed it with the strapline: "It's a happy ending. He dies."<ref name="Irish Times">{{cite news|title=Hitler: The Lost Files|url=http://www.irishtimes.com/news/hitler-the-lost-files-1.1288885|newspaper=[[The Irish Times]]|access-date=19 March 2017}}</ref>

===Box office and awards===
''Downfall'' sold nearly half a million tickets in Germany for its opening weekend and attracted 4.5 million viewers in the first three months.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.irishtimes.com/news/german-film-on-hitler-s-demise-a-box-office-hit-1.990608|title=German film on Hitler's demise a box office hit|newspaper=[[The Irish Times]]|date=20 September 2004|access-date=14 November 2018}}</ref>{{sfn|Bendix|2007}} The final North American gross was $5,509,040, while $86,671,870 was made with its foreign gross.<ref name="Downfall"/> The film made $93.6{{spaces}}million altogether.{{sfn|Niemi|2018}}

''Downfall'' was nominated for [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]] at the [[77th Academy Awards]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dw.com/en/hitler-film-wins-oscar-nomination/a-1470071|title=Hitler Film Wins Oscar Nomination|publisher=[[Deutsche Welle|DW]]|date=26 January 2005|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref> It won the 2005 [[BBC Four]] World Cinema competition.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/4652074.stm|title=Downfall wins BBC world film gong |publisher=[[BBC]] |date=26 January 2006 |access-date=20 July 2009}}</ref> The film was also ranked number 48 in ''[[Empire (film magazine)|Empire]]'' magazine's "The 100 Best Films Of World Cinema" in 2010.<ref>{{cite web |title=The 100 Best Films Of World Cinema – 48. Downfall |url=http://www.empireonline.com/features/100-greatest-world-cinema-films/default.asp?film=48 |work=Empire}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
The review aggregation website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] gives the film a score of 90% based on 137 reviews from critics, with a weighted average of 8.2/10. The website's consensus reads, "''Downfall'' is an illuminating, thoughtful and detailed account of Hitler's last days."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/downfall/ |title=Downfall (Der Untergang) (2004) |publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=22 November 2021}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film was awarded the "Must-See" badge, holding a weighted average of 82 out of 100 based on 35 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/downfall |title=Downfall Reviews |publisher=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=9 September 2019}}</ref>

Reviews for the film were often very positive,{{sfn|Vande Winkel|2007|p=212}} despite debate surrounding the film from critics and audiences upon its release {{See below|[[#Humanization concerns|Humanization concerns]]}}.<ref name="crowds"/><ref name="andreas">{{cite web|url=http://www.spiegel.de/kultur/kino/der-untergang-die-unerzaehlbare-geschichte-a-318031.html|title={{-'}}Der Untergang': Die unerzählbare Geschichte|last=Borcholte|first=Andreas|work=[[Der Spiegel]]|date=15 September 2004 |language=de |access-date=23 February 2019}}</ref> Ganz's portrayal of Hitler was singled out for praise;<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/News_Story/Critic_Review/Guardian_Film_of_the_week/0,4267,1449213,00.html|title=Downfall Review|work=[[The Guardian]]|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|date=1 April 2005|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/downfall/review/|title=Downfall Review|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|last=Newman|first=Kim|date=10 May 2017|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.colesmithey.com/reviews/2005/05/downfall.html |title=German Filmmakers do Justice to the Fall of Hitler's Empire|date=9 May 2005|publisher=Smart New Media|last=Smithey|first=Cole}}</ref> [[David Denby]] for ''[[The New Yorker]]'' said that Ganz "made the dictator into a plausible human being". With respect to German uneasiness about "humanizing" Hitler, Denby said that while "journalists in [Germany] wondered aloud whether the "human" treatment of Hitler might not inadvertently aid the [[neo-Nazi]] movement...in his many rants in [the film] Hitler says that the German people do not deserve to survive, that they have failed him by losing the war and must perish – not exactly the sentiments [...] that would spark a recruitment drive. This Hitler may be human, but he's as utterly degraded a human being as has ever been shown on the screen, a man whose every impulse leads to annihilation".<ref name="newyorker_david_denby">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2005/02/14/back-in-the-bunker|title=David Denby's comments on ''Der Untergang''|date=14 February 2005 |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |last=Denby |first=David |access-date=5 May 2015}}</ref> Addressing other critics like Denby, ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' critic [[Roger Ebert]] said the film did not provide an adequate portrayal of Hitler's actions, because he felt no film could, and that no response would be sufficient. Ebert said Hitler was, in reality, "the focus for a spontaneous uprising by many of [[Germans|the German people]], fueled by racism, xenophobia, grandiosity and fear".<ref name="chicagosuntimes_rogerebert">{{cite news|last=Ebert|first=Roger|date=11 March 2005|title=Downfall|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/downfall-2005}}</ref>

[[:de:Hermann Graml|Hermann Graml]], history professor and former [[Luftwaffenhelfer|Luftwaffe helper]], praised the film and said that he had not seen a film that was "so insistent and tormentingly alive". Graml said that Hitler's portrayal was presented correctly by showing Hitler's will "to destroy, and his way of denying reality".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.spiegel.de/kultur/kino/historiker-zu-der-untergang-faktisch-genau-dramaturgisch-lau-a-318336.html|title="Der Untergang": Faktisch genau, dramaturgisch lau|work=[[Der Spiegel]]|language=de|date=16 August 2004|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref> Julia Radke of the German website [[:de:Zukunft braucht Erinnerung|Future Needs Remembrance]] praised the film's acting and called it well crafted and a solid ''[[Kammerspielfilm]]'', though it could lose viewer interest due to a lack of concentration on the narrative perspective.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.zukunft-braucht-erinnerung.de/der-untergang-hirschbiegel/|title=Hirschbiegel: Der Untergang. Filmrezension.|work=[[:de:Zukunft braucht Erinnerung|Future Needs Remembrance]]|language=de|last=Radke|first=Julia|date=1 November 2004|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref> German author [[:de:Jens Jessen (Journalist)|Jens Jessen]] said that the film "could have been stupider" and called it a "chamber play that could not be staged undramatically". Jessen also said that it was not as spectacular as the pre-media coverage could have led one to believe, and it did not arouse the "morbid fascination" the magazine ''[[Der Spiegel]]'' was looking for.<ref name="diezietjessen">{{cite news|url=https://www.zeit.de/2004/36/Der_Untergang|title=Stilles Ende eines Irren unter Tage|work=[[Die Zeit]]|language=de|last=Jessen|first=Jens|author-link=:de:Jens Jessen (Journalist)|date=26 August 2004|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref>

Hitler biographer Sir [[Ian Kershaw]] wrote in ''[[The Guardian]]'' that the film had enormous emotive power, calling it a triumph and "a marvellous historical drama". Kershaw also said that he "found it hard to imagine that anyone (other than the usual neo-Nazi fringe) could possibly find Hitler a sympathetic figure" in his final days, noting how Hitler "could be kind and considerate to his secretaries, and with the next breath show cold ruthlessness, dispassionate brutality, in determining the deaths of millions". He also praised Ganz's performance, stating that compared to actors who played Hitler beforehand, such as [[Hitler: The Last Ten Days|Alec Guinness]] or [[The Bunker (1981 film)|Anthony Hopkins]], "this is the only one which to me is compelling. Part of this is the voice. Ganz has Hitler's voice to near perfection. It is chillingly authentic".<ref name="theguardian_iankershaw">{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2004/sep/17/germany|title=The human Hitler|work=[[The Guardian]]|last=Kershaw|first=Ian|author-link=Ian Kershaw|date=17 September 2004 |location=London |access-date=20 July 2009}}</ref> Wim Wenders, in a review for the German newspaper ''[[Die Zeit]]'', said the film was absent of a strong point of view for Hitler which made him harmless, and compared ''Downfall'' to ''[[Resident Evil: Apocalypse]]'', stating that in ''Resident Evil'' the viewer would know which character was evil.<ref name="Spiegel" /><ref name="crowds" />

Alex von Tunzelmann of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film a grade of A, saying: "''Downfall'' is an intelligent, thoroughly researched recreation of Hitler's last days, and a terrific movie."<ref>{{cite news|title=Downfall: opening the book on the final days of Adolf Hitler|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=2013-05-16|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/filmblog/2013/may/16/downfall-final-days-adolf-hitler|access-date=2025-04-11}}</ref>

Tom Fordy praised the film as an achievement.<ref name="Telegraph review">{{cite news |last=Fordy |first=Tom |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/0/downfall-hitler-death-truth-fact-fiction/ |title={{-'}}I never thought people would empathise with Hitler': The truth about the Führer's Downfall |newspaper=[[The Daily Telegraph|The Telegraph]] |url-access=subscription |date=13 September 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240913134312/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/0/downfall-hitler-death-truth-fact-fiction/ |archive-date=2024-09-13 |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Humanization concerns ===

Bad people do not walk around with claws like vicious monsters, even though it might be comforting to think so.<ref name="johnston">{{cite web |last1=Johnston |first1=Sheila |title=The dangers of portraying Hitler |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/film-news/11573042/The-dangers-of-portraying-Hitler.html |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20220112/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/film-news/11573042/The-dangers-of-portraying-Hitler.html |archive-date=12 January 2022 |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |website=[[The Daily Telegraph|The Telegraph]] |date=30 April 2015 |access-date=16 November 2018}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

}}
''Downfall'' was the subject of dispute by critics and audiences in Germany before and after its release, with many concerned regarding Hitler's portrayal in the film in spite of his actions and ideologies.<ref name="crowds">{{cite web|url=http://www.columbia.edu/~lnp3/mydocs/culture/Downfall.htm|title=A film depicting Adolf Hitler's human side is attracting crowds and stirring debate in Germany|publisher=[[Columbia University]]|access-date=15 November 2018}}</ref><ref name="theguardian_iankershaw"/>{{sfn|Vande Winkel|2007|p=208}} The portrayal sparked debate in Germany due to publicity from commentators, film magazines, and newspapers,<ref name="soft"/><ref name="irishtimes">{{Cite news|url=https://www.irishtimes.com/news/my-hitler-part-in-downfall-1.426739|title=My Hitler part in 'Downfall'|newspaper=[[The Irish Times]]|date=26 March 2005|access-date=14 November 2018}}</ref> leading the German tabloid ''[[Bild]]'' to ask the question, "Are we allowed to show the monster as a human being?"<ref name="soft"/>

Russian press visited the set, making the producers uneasy and occasionally defensive. Yana Bezhanskay, director of Globus Film, Constantin's Russian partner, raised her voice to Russian journalists and said: "This is an antifascist film and nowhere in it do you see Hitler praised."<ref name="nytimes"/>

Cristina Nord from ''[[Die Tageszeitung]]'' criticized the portrayal, and said that though it was important to make films about perpetrators, "seeing Hitler cry" had not informed her on the last days of the Third Reich.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/europe/3663044.stm|title='Human' Hitler disturbs Germans|work=[[BBC]]|last=Furlong|first=Ray|date=16 September 2004|access-date=26 March 2019}}</ref> Some have supported the film: [[Hans-Jürgen Syberberg]], director of ''[[Hitler: A Film from Germany]]'' (1977), felt the time was right to "paint a realistic portrait" of Hitler.<ref name="johnston"/><ref name="Filmdownfall"/> Ganz said that he was proud of the film; though he said people had accused him of "humanizing" Hitler.<ref name="irishtimes"/>

==Historical accuracy==
The basic premises of the film are true and largely accurate. While the majority of the characters in the film are based on actual people, the character Peter Kranz is not, though based on [[Alfred Zech]], a 12-year-old who saved a dozen German soldiers from a Russian attack in his home village of Goldenau (now [[Złotniki, Opole Voivodeship|Złotniki]], [[Poland]]).<ref name="Telegraph review" /> Author [[Giles MacDonogh]] criticized the portrayals of SS officers [[Wilhelm Mohnke]] and [[Ernst-Günther Schenck]] as being sympathetic,{{sfn|Eberle|Uhl|2005|p=xviii}} noting that Mohnke was accused of murdering a group of British [[Prisoner of war#World War II|prisoners of war]] in the [[Wormhoudt massacre]].{{refn|group=N|Mohnke was rumoured, but never proven, to have ordered the execution near [[Dunkirk]] in 1940.{{sfn|Weale|2012}} He strongly denied the accusations against him, and told historian Thomas Fischer that he never issued any orders to take or execute English prisoners.{{sfn|Fischer|2008|p=26}}}} Hirschbiegel has said in interviews that these allegations, as well as allegations that Schenck had performed unethical medical experiments, were unproven.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2005/apr/05/secondworldwar.filmnews|title=Bunker film 'is too kind to Nazis'|work=[[The Guardian]]|last=Higgins|first=Charlotte|date=5 April 2005|access-date=3 January 2019}}</ref>

When [[Rochus Misch]], Hitler's bodyguard, was asked about the film's historical accuracy in a 2005 interview, he stated that although it was factually accurate, the film was "Americanized", claiming that Hitler never screamed in the bunker and that the bunker was generally quiet,<ref name=":0" /> although Hitler's furious rant about Steiner's failure to attack was recorded by eyewitnesses.{{sfn|Fest|2004|pp=163–164}} According to Misch, Magda Goebbels insisted that her children stay in the bunker and made the decision that they should be killed when she and her husband committed suicide, as opposed to the couple making the decision together, as depicted in the film.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Hattemer-Higgins |first=Ida |date=2005-02-21 |title=Hitler's bodyguard |url=https://www.salon.com/2005/02/21/nazi_3/ |access-date=2025-06-27 |website=Salon.com}}</ref> Bunker nurse [[Erna Flegel]] commended the film, stating that although it got some minor details wrong, it was generally accurate.<ref>{{Cite web|location=England|url=http://www.theguardian.com/world/2005/may/02/germany.secondworldwar|title=Interview: Erna Flegel|first2=Erna|last2=Flegel|authorlink2=Erna Flegel|quote=It was good. They got a few small details wrong but generally it was right. I even recognised myself as a nurse.|date=2005|first1=Luke|last1=Harding|work=The Guardian |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130829050409/http://www.theguardian.com/world/2005/may/02/germany.secondworldwar |archive-date=29 August 2013 }}</ref>

''Downfall'' is well known for its rise in popularity due to many internet [[parody]] videos and [[internet meme|memes]] which use several scenes in the film: when Hitler phones ''[[General der Flieger]]'' [[Karl Koller (general)|Karl Koller]] about Berlin's April 20 bombardment; when Hitler discusses a [[counterattack]] against advancing Soviet forces with his generals; where Hitler becomes angry after hearing that Steiner's attack never happened, due to a lack of forces; when Hitler hears Luftwaffe commander [[Göring telegram|Hermann Göring's telegram]]; when Hitler is having dinner and discovers ''[[Reichsführer-SS]]'' [[Heinrich Himmler]] secretly made a [[Surrender (military)|surrender]] offer to the [[Allies of World War II|Western Allies]]; and where Hitler orders [[Otto Günsche]] to find ''[[SS-Gruppenführer]]'' [[Hermann Fegelein]]. 

In these videos, the original German audio is retained, but new subtitles are added so that Hitler and his subordinates seem to be reacting to an issue or setback in present-day politics, sports, entertainment, popular culture, or everyday life.<ref name="rise">{{cite news |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/magazine/8617454.stm |title=The rise, rise and rise of the Downfall Hitler Hirschbiegel spoke positively about these parodies in a 2010 interview with ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' magazine, saying that many of them were funny and a fitting extension of the film's purpose.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://nymag.com/daily/entertainment/2010/01/the_director_of_downfall_on_al.html|title=The Director of ''Downfall'' Speaks Out on All Those Angry YouTube Hitlers |last=Rosenblum|first=Emma|date=15 January 2010|work=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|access-date=16 January 2010}}</ref> Nevertheless, [[Constantin Film]] asked video sites to remove them.<ref name="rise"/> The producers initiated a removal of parody videos from YouTube in 2010.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2010/apr/21/constantin-films-intellectual-property-spoofs|title=Downfall filmmakers want YouTube to take down Hitler spoofs|author=Finlo Rohrer|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=21 April 2010 |location=London |access-date=21 April 2010}}</ref> This [[Streisand effect|prompted]] more posting of parody videos of Hitler complaining that the parodies were being taken down, and a resurgence of the videos on the site.<ref name="robtolhurst" />

One particular parody was the subject of ''[[BP Refinery v Tracey]]'', in Australia, where a [[BP]] employee named Scott Tracey was terminated from his job for a video satirising [[collective bargaining]] negotiations at the company he was working in. Tracey managed to successfully appeal his unfair dismissal to the Full Federal Court who decided that the video in question was not offensive, and had his job reinstated and received $200,000 in compensation.<ref name="bp">{{Cite news |last=Zhou |first=Naaman |date=15 November 2019 |title=BP worker fired over Downfall video appeals, saying Fair Work did not understand meme |work=[[The Guardian]] |url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2019/nov/15/bp-worker-fired-over-downfall-video-appeals-saying-fair-work-did-not-understand-meme |access-date=3 March 2023}}</ref>
