'''''The King''''' is a 2019 [[Epic film|epic]] [[historical drama]] film directed by [[David Michôd]], based on several plays from [[William Shakespeare]]'s [[Henriad]].<ref>{{cite web|last1=Crabtree|first1=Isabel|date=9 November 2019|title=The King Might Not Be Totally Historically Accurate, But Timotheé Chalamet's Bowl Cut Sure As Hell Is|url=https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/a29741942/the-king-netflix-timothee-chalamet-accuracy/|access-date=14 November 2019|website=Esquire}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Bunyan|first1=Michael|date=25 October 2019|title=The True Story Behind the Netflix Movie The King|url=https://time.com/5707035/the-king-netflix-true-story/|access-date=14 November 2019|magazine=Time}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=Nelson|first1=Alex|date=11 November 2019|title=Is The King a true story? How accurately Henry V and Agincourt are portrayed in the Netflix drama and Shakespeare plays|url=https://inews.co.uk/culture/film/the-king-netflix-true-story-henry-v-agincourt-accuracy-netflix-series-shakespeare-plays-828629|access-date=14 November 2019|website=inews}}</ref> The screenplay was written by Michôd and [[Joel Edgerton]], who both produced the film with [[Brad Pitt]], [[Dede Gardner]], [[Jeremy Kleiner]], and [[Liz Watts]]. ''The King'' includes an ensemble cast led by [[Timothée Chalamet]] as the Prince of Wales and later King [[Henry V of England]], alongside Joel Edgerton, [[Sean Harris]], [[Lily-Rose Depp]], [[Robert Pattinson]], and [[Ben Mendelsohn]].

The film focuses on the rise of Henry V as king after his father dies as he also must navigate palace politics, the war his father left behind, and the emotional strings of his past life. ''The King'' premiered at the [[76th Venice International Film Festival]] on 2 September 2019, and was released digitally via [[Netflix]] on 11 October 2019. The film received favourable reviews from film critics but, despite being presented as an adaptation of Shakespeare's Henriad rather than as a historical [[documentary film]], was criticised by historians for historical inaccuracies and for deviating from the original plays.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. -->
After a battle with the Scots, King [[Henry IV of England|Henry IV]] meets with those who fought. [[Henry Percy (Hotspur)|Hotspur]] is angry and insulting towards the king. 

[[Kingdom of England|England]]'s Prince [[Henry V of England|Henry]] ("[[Prince Hal|Hal]]") spends his days drinking, whoring, and jesting with his companion [[John Falstaff|Falstaff]] in [[Eastcheap]].

His father King Henry IV grows tired of Hal's debauchery and announces that Hal's younger brother Prince [[Thomas of Lancaster, 1st Duke of Clarence|Thomas]] will inherit the throne. King Henry IV sends Thomas to subdue Hotspur's rebellion. However, Hal arrives and challenges Hotspur to [[Duel|single combat]], which Hal wins, ending the battle without further conflict.

Thomas dies shortly after while campaigning against the [[Glyndŵr rebellion|rebels in Wales]]. When King Henry IV dies, Hal is anointed as King Henry V. He opts for peace and conciliation with his father's many adversaries, despite his actions being seen as weakness.

At King Henry V's coronation feast, envoys from the [[Louis, Duke of Guyenne|Dauphin of France]] present Hal with a [[tennis ball]] as an insulting coronation gift. However, King Henry V frames this as a positive reflection of his boyhood. His sister [[Philippa of England|Philippa]], now the Queen of [[Kalmar Union|Denmark]], cautions her brother that nobles in any royal court have their own interests in mind and will never fully reveal their true intentions.

King Henry V interrogates a captured assassin who claims to have been sent to kill him by King [[Charles VI of France|Charles VI]] of [[Kingdom of France|France]]. [[Southampton Plot|French agents]] approach the English nobles [[Richard of Conisburgh, 3rd Earl of Cambridge|Cambridge]] and [[Thomas Grey (conspirator)|Grey]]. The traitors plot against Hal and unsuccessfully attempt to win over the Chief Justice, [[William Gascoigne|Gascoigne]]. Gascoigne advises Hal that a show of strength is necessary to unite England, so Hal declares war on [[France in the Middle Ages|France]] and has Cambridge and Grey beheaded. He approaches Falstaff and appoints him as his chief military strategist, saying that Falstaff is the only man he truly trusts.

The English army sets sail for France. After completing the [[Siege of Harfleur]], they receive taunting messages from the Dauphin. The English advance parties stumble upon a vast French army gathering to face them. [[Thomas Beaufort, Duke of Exeter|Dorset]] advises Hal to retreat, but Falstaff proposes using infantry without armor to attack the armored French cavalry, who would get weighed down and stuck in the mud.

Hal challenges the Dauphin to single combat to minimize bloodshed, but the Dauphin refuses. Falstaff's plan succeeds, and the outnumbered English army defeats the French, although Falstaff is killed. The Dauphin, witnessing his men's retreat, restates Hal's challenge, but slips in the mud until Hal orders his soldiers to kill him. Hal commands the execution of all French prisoners to prevent regrouping, despite Falstaff's warning against this unchivalrous act unworthy of a king.

Hal meets King Charles VI, who agrees to adopt him as his heir and offers him his daughter, [[Catherine of Valois]]. Hal returns to England with his new wife for a triumphant celebration. In private, she challenges his reasons for invading France and denies the alleged French actions against him, suggesting that the assassin was a plot from within his own court. Suspicious, Hal confronts Gascoigne, who admits that he staged the insult and acts of aggression, believing his sole duty is to protect the King, even if it requires deceiving him. In a cold fury, Hal kills the Chief Justice. Hal asks Catherine to only speak truth to him as the citizens of England cheer him on outside the castle.

=== Music ===
{{Main|The King (soundtrack)}}
The film's original score was composed by [[Nicholas Britell]], who thought of approaching the film's music from the 21st century, instead of the medieval 15th century approach, saying "because of the timelessness of these issues, if felt like something that we could explore with the sound of different time periods, just to make you look at the early 1400's in a way that it felt like you hadn't seen it before". He felt that the "1400s looks like it was a foreign planet".<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Beachum |first1=Chris |last2=Laws |first2=Zach |date=2019-10-25 |title=Nicholas Britell ('The King' composer) on 'looking at the 1400's like it was a foreign planet' through music [EXCLUSIVE VIDEO INTERVIEW] |url=https://www.goldderby.com/article/2019/nicholas-britell-the-king-composer-netflix-video-interview/ |access-date=2022-11-29 |website=GoldDerby |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-11-14 |title=Nicholas Britell's Latest Score Is Fit for a King |url=https://www.ascap.com/news-events/articles/2019/11/britell-the-king-netflix |access-date=2022-11-29 |website=Ascap.com |publisher=American Society of Composers and Lyricists}}</ref> He experimented the film's music using bass clarinets run with tape filters, and sounds of metal while composing.<ref name=":12">{{Cite web |last=Tangcay |first=Jazz |date=2019-11-05 |title=Nicholas Britell Gets to the Emotional Core of Composing for 'The King' |url=https://variety.com/2019/artisans/news/nicholas-britell-the-king-composer-interview-1203393683/ |access-date=2022-11-29 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> It was Britell's most "dark and sombre" music reflecting the zone of the film.<ref name=":02">{{Cite web |last=Grobar |first=Matt |date=2019-11-04 |title='The King' Composer Nicholas Britell Crafts "Most Somber" Score Of His Career In First Collaboration With David Michôd |url=https://deadline.com/2019/11/the-king-composer-nicholas-britell-david-michod-netflix-interview-news-1202777020/ |access-date=2022-11-29 |website=Deadline |language=en-US}}</ref> [[Lakeshore Records]] released the album consisting of 15 tracks from Britell's score, on 1 November 2019 in digital and CD formats, while the vinyl edition was released three years later on 8 July 2022.<ref name=":12"/>

== Historical accuracy ==
Rather than a historical [[documentary film]], ''The King'' is a 21st century adaptation of [[William Shakespeare]]'s 16th-century [[Henriad]], which are fictionalised adaptations of 15th-century events; as ''[[The Independent]]'' opined, "as an adaptation of an adaptation, a few things are bound to have been lost in translation".<ref name="truestory">{{cite web |last1=Nelson |first1=Alex |title=Is The King a true story? |url=https://inews.co.uk/culture/film/the-king-netflix-true-story-henry-v-agincourt-accuracy-netflix-series-shakespeare-plays-359285 |website=inews.co.uk |language=en |date=11 November 2019}}</ref> After its release, ''The King'' became the subject of widespread commentary over its [[Historicity|historical accuracy]]. The film, based on the Henriad, a collection of [[Shakespearean history|Shakespearean history plays]] about the [[List of monarchs of England|monarchs of England]], includes several fictional characters (such as [[John Falstaff]]) and contains several historical dramatisations of the real events the movie was based on.<ref name="realstory">{{cite magazine |last1=Bunyan |first1=Rachael |title=The True Story Behind Netflix's The King |url=https://time.com/5707035/the-king-netflix-true-story/ |magazine=TIME |language=en |date=25 October 2019}}</ref>

Numerous articles were published on the historicity of ''The King'', analysing the historical accuracy of various elements of the movie. The following historical inaccuracies were mentioned:

* The film depicts Henry V as being reluctant to go to war with France, but being eventually persuaded by the Archbishop of Canterbury. In reality, Henry V was keen to go to war with the French, with his primary motivation being [[Expansionism|expansionist]] territorial claims in France.<ref name="timothee">{{cite web |title='The King' Might Not Be Totally Historically Accurate, But Timotheé Chalamet's Bowl Cut Sure As Hell Is |url=https://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/a29741942/the-king-netflix-timothee-chalamet-accuracy/ |website=Esquire |date=9 November 2019}}</ref>
* The character of [[John Falstaff]], as depicted in the film, is a fictional character created by Shakespeare. Many historians have suggested that the English knight [[John Fastolf|Sir John Fastolf]] served as a real-life inspiration for the character of Falstaff.<ref name="timothee" />
* The film depicts a rivalry between Henry V and his brother, Prince Thomas, who is depicted as dying in [[Wales]] prior to Henry's coronation, an event which pushes Henry to ascend to the throne. In reality, Thomas was [[killed in action]] at [[Battle of Baugé]] in the [[Duchy of Anjou]] eight years after Henry V's coronation.<ref> {{Cite ODNB |last=Harriss |date=September 2010 |first=G.L. |title=Thomas, duke of Clarence (1387–1421) |id=27198 |author-link=G. L. Harriss}}</ref>
* ''The King'' depicts the Battle of Agincourt as taking place on green and hilly terrain, when in reality it occurred on [[fallow]] fields on the French plains. The film also depicts the French as holding the heights during the battle when in reality it was the English who did so.<ref name="franceinfo">{{cite web|url=https://france3-regions.francetvinfo.fr/hauts-de-france/azincourt-comment-film-roi-netflix-pietine-allegrement-realite-historique-1744495.html|title=Azincourt: comment le film "Le Roi" (Netflix) piétine allègrement la réalité historique|work=franceinfo|date=4 November 2019|access-date=4 December 2020|language=fr}}</ref>
* The film implies that Henry V and Catherine of Valois married almost immediately after the Battle of Agincourt, when in reality their marriage occurred five years later on 2 June 1420.   
* The film depicts the Dauphin of France, [[Louis, Duke of Guyenne|Louis de Guyenne]], as being present at the Siege of Harfleur (summer 1415) and the Battle of Agincourt (October 1415, shown dying in the battle) when in reality, he was present at neither battle and died of dysentery in December 1415.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Famiglietti |first=R. C. |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=UkKyAAAAIAAJ&source=gbs_citations_module_r&cad=2 |title=Royal Intrigue: Crisis at the Court of Charles VI, 1392-1420 |date=1986 |publisher=AMS Press |isbn=978-0-404-61439-3 |language=en}}</ref>
* A crucial part of the English defence, the [[archer's stake|sharpened stakes]], or palings, which were set at an angle towards the French cavalry to protect the archers, was almost entirely ignored in the film although there was a brief shot of a small pile of palings awaiting deployment.<ref name="franceinfo"/>
* The film's costume design consists of numerous anachronisms and inaccuracies, consisting of armor and clothing from the entirety of the fifteenth century not present during the film's period, alongside entirely ahistorical designs.<ref name="franceinfo"/>
