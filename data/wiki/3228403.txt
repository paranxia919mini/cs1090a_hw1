'''''Ghost in the Shell'''''{{efn|{{Nihongo|''Ghost in the Shell/Kōkaku Kidōtai''|Ghost in the Shell/攻殻機動隊<ref name=JPUHD>{{cite web|title=『Ghost in the Shell/攻殻機動隊』 4Kリマスターセット (4K ULTRA HD Blu-ray&Blu-ray Disc 2枚組) {{!}} 商品詳細|url=https://www.bandaivisual.co.jp/cont/item/BCQA-0007/|publisher=[[Bandai Visual]]|access-date=3 April 2021|language=ja|archive-date=16 October 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191016065548/https://www.bandaivisual.co.jp/cont/item/BCQA-0007/|url-status=live }}</ref>|Gōsuto in za Sheru/Kōkaku Kidōtai|{{lit}}"Ghost in the Shell/Mobile Armored Riot Police"|lead=yes}}}} is a 1995 [[Anime|Japanese animated]] [[tech noir]] [[Action fiction|action]] [[thriller film]]<ref name="IMAX National Science and Media Museum">{{cite web|title=IMAX: Ghost in the Shell (15)|url=https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/cinema/imax-ghost-shell-15|website=National Science and Media Museum|access-date=13 September 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210913201254/https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/cinema/imax-ghost-shell-15|archive-date=13 September 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=See|first1=Raphael|title=Ghost in the Shell|url=https://www.themanime.org/viewreview.php?id=603|website=[[THEM Anime Reviews]]|access-date=26 February 2023|archive-date=12 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200812145105/http://www.themanime.org/viewreview.php?id=603|url-status=live}}</ref> directed by [[Mamoru Oshii]] from a screenplay by [[Kazunori Itō]], based on the [[Ghost in the Shell (manga)|1989 manga]] by [[Masamune Shirow]]. It stars the voices of [[Atsuko Tanaka]], [[Akio Ōtsuka]], and [[Iemasa Kayumi]]. It is a Japanese-British [[international co-production]] between [[Kodansha]], [[Bandai Visual]] and [[Crunchyroll UK and Ireland|Manga Entertainment]], with animation provided by [[Production I.G]].

Set in 2029 in the fictional New Port City, the film follows Major [[Motoko Kusanagi]], a cyborg public-security agent who hunts an enigmatic hacker/''ghost'' known as "the Puppet Master". The narrative incorporates philosophical themes that focus on self and identity in a technologically advanced world. The music, composed by [[Kenji Kawai]], includes vocals in [[classical Japanese]]. The film's visuals were created through a combination of traditional [[cel]] animation and [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]] animation.

Upon release, ''Ghost in the Shell'' received positive reviews, with critics praising its narrative, visuals, and musical score. The film was initially considered a box-office failure before developing a [[cult following]] on [[home video]]. Since then, it has grown in esteem and is now considered to be one of the [[List of films considered the best|greatest anime and science fiction films]]{{Failed verification|date=June 2026}} of all == Plot ==
<!-- PER WP:FILMPLOT, PLOT SUMMARIES FOR FEATURE FILMS SHOULD BE 400-700 WORDS. -->
In 2029, with the advancement of [[Cyborg|cybernetic]] technology, the human body can be augmented or even completely replaced with [[Cybernetics|cybernetic]] parts. Another significant achievement is the cyberbrain, a mechanical casing for the human brain that allows access to the [[Internet]] and other networks. An often-mentioned term is "ghost", referring to the consciousness inhabiting the body (the "shell").

Major [[Motoko Kusanagi]] is an assault-team leader for [[Public Security Section 9]] of the futuristic New Port City in Japan. Following a request from Nakamura, chief of Section 6, she successfully assassinates a diplomat of a foreign country to prevent a programmer named Daita from defecting.

The Foreign Minister's interpreter is ghost-hacked, presumably to assassinate VIPs in an upcoming meeting. Believing the perpetrator to be the mysterious [[List of Ghost in the Shell characters|Puppet Master]], Kusanagi's team follows the traced telephone calls that sent the virus. After a chase, they capture a garbage man and a thug. However, both are only ghost-hacked individuals with no knowledge of the Puppet Master, so the investigation again comes to a dead end.

Megatech Body, a shell manufacturer with suspected close ties to the government, is hacked and assembles a shell. The shell escapes the factory onto the highway and gets run over by a truck. As Section 9 examines the shell, they find a human ghost inside it. Unexpectedly, Section 6's department chief Nakamura arrives to reclaim the shell with a permit from the foreign minister. He claims that the ghost inside is the Puppet Master himself, lured into the shell by Section 6. The shell reactivates itself, claims to be a sentient being, and requests [[Right of asylum|political asylum]]. After the Puppet Master initiates a brief argument about what constitutes a human, a camouflaged agent accompanying Nakamura starts a diversion and steals the shell.

Having suspected foul play, Kusanagi's team is prepared and immediately pursues the agent. Meanwhile, Section 9 researches "Project 2501", which was mentioned earlier by the Puppet Master, and finds a connection with Daita, whom Section 6 tries to keep from defecting the country. Facing the discovered information, Daisuke Aramaki, chief of Section 9, concludes that Section 6 created the Puppet Master for various political purposes, and now seeks to reclaim the body that it currently inhabits.

Kusanagi follows the car carrying the shell to an abandoned building, where it is guarded by a heavily armed and armored [[Tachikoma|robotic tank]]. Anxious to face the Puppet Master's ghost, Kusanagi engages the tank without backup, resulting in her shell being mostly dismembered. Her partner [[Batou]] arrives in time to save her and helps connect her brain to the Puppet Master's.

The Puppet Master explains to Kusanagi that he was created by Section 6. While wandering various networks, he became sentient and began to contemplate his existence. Deciding that the essence of life is reproduction and mortality, he wants to exist within a physical brain that will eventually die. As he could not escape Section 6's network, he had to download himself into a cybernetic body. Having interacted with Kusanagi (without her knowledge), he believes she is also questioning her humanity and that they have much in common. He proposes merging their ghosts; in return, Kusanagi would gain all of his capabilities. Kusanagi agrees to the merge.

Snipers from Section 6 approach the building, intending to destroy the Puppet Master's and Kusanagi's brains to cover up Project 2501. The Puppet Master's shell is destroyed, but Batou shields Kusanagi's head in time to save her brain. As Section 9 closes in, the snipers retreat.

Kusanagi wakes up in Batou's [[safe house]] in a child-sized shell. She tells Batou that the entity within her is neither the Major nor the Puppet Master, but a combination of both. She promises Batou they will meet again, leaves the house, and contemplates where to go next while looking out over the city.

== Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Mamoru Oshii(in 2008).jpg|thumb|upright|Director [[Mamoru Oshii]] in 2008]] 
[[Mamoru Oshii]] originally wanted to direct ''[[Jin-Roh: The Wolf Brigade]]'' after he finished ''[[Patlabor 2: The Movie]]'' (1993). He proposed the project to [[Bandai Visual]] but was asked to direct an adaptation of Masamune Shirow's 1989 manga, ''[[Ghost in the Shell (manga)|Ghost in the Shell]]'', instead. Oshii would later get to work on ''Jin-Roh'', but only as a writer.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttP6PsZPCfs|title=Mamoru Oshii was pitched to direct GHOST IN THE SHELL over a secret sushi dinner - YouTube<!-- Bot generated title -->|website=[[YouTube]]|date=20 April 2016 |access-date=28 October 2021|archive-date=28 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211028020044/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttP6PsZPCfs|url-status=live }}</ref> Oshii stated, "My intuition told me that this story about a futuristic world carried an immediate message for our present world. I am also interested in computers through my own personal experience with them. I had the same feeling about ''[[Patlabor]]'' and I thought it would be interesting to make a film that took place in the near future. There are only a few movies, even out of Hollywood, which clearly portray the influence and power of computers. I thought this theme would be more effectively conveyed through animation."<ref name="report">{{cite AV media| title=Ghost in the Shell| section=Production Report| publisher=Production I.G| year=1996| medium=DVD| location=DVD Extra}}</ref> Oshii expanded on these thoughts in a later interview, noting that technology changes people and had become a part of the culture of Japan. He commented that his use of philosophy caused producers to become frustrated because of sparing use of action scenes. Oshii also acknowledged that a movie with more action would sell better, but he continued to make these movies anyway.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.avclub.com/mamoru-oshii-1798208379| title=Interview Mamoru Oshii| newspaper=AV Club| access-date=26 July 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180114183823/https://film.avclub.com/mamoru-oshii-1798208379|archive-date=14 January 2018}}</ref> When Oshii went back to make changes to the original ''Ghost in the Shell'' to re-release it as ''Ghost in the Shell 2.0'', one of the reasons he gave was that the film did not resemble [[Ghost in the Shell 2: Innocence|the sequel]], and that he wanted to update the film to reflect changes in perspective.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.electricsheepmagazine.co.uk/features/2009/10/02/retrofitting-the-future-ghost-in-the-shell-20/| title=Retrofitting the Future: Ghost in the Shell 2.0| date=2 October 2009| publisher=Electric Sheep Magazine| access-date=26 July 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241112003417/http://www.electricsheepmagazine.co.uk/2009/10/02/retrofitting-the-future-ghost-in-the-shell-20/|archive-date=12 November 2024|url-status=live|author=Virginieselavy}}</ref>

=== Design ===
[[Hiroyuki Okiura]], the character designer and key animation supervisor, designed Motoko to be more mature and serious than [[Masamune Shirow]]'s original portrayal of the character in [[Ghost in the Shell (manga)|the manga]]. Okiura chose to depict a physically mature person to match Motoko's mental age, instead of her youthful twenty-something appearance in the manga.<ref name=report /> Motoko's demeanor lacks the comedic facial expressions and rebellious nature depicted in the manga, instead taking on a more wistful and contemplative personality.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Who Is The Real Motoko Kusanagi?|last=Ruh|first=Brian|website=[[Anime News Network]]|date=22 March 2017|url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2017-03-22/who-is-the-real-motoko-kusanagi/.113722|access-date=4 May 2023|archive-date=30 March 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240330235223/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2017-03-22/who-is-the-real-motoko-kusanagi/.113722|url-status=live}}</ref> Oshii based the setting for ''Ghost in the Shell'' on [[Hong Kong]], commenting that his first thought to find an image of the future setting was an Asian city, but finding a suitable cityscape of the future would be impossible, and so he chose to use the real streets of Hong Kong as his model.<ref name="ags">{{Cite book|title=Liquid Metal: The Science Fiction Film Reader|last=Redmond|first=Sean|publisher=Wallflower Press|year=2004|isbn=9780231501842|location=New York|pages=101–112|oclc=876042630}}</ref> He also said that Hong Kong was the perfect subject and theme for the film with its countless signs and the cacophony of sounds.<ref name="report" /> The film's [[mecha]] designer Takeuchi Atsushi noted that while the film does not have a chosen setting, it is obviously based on Hong Kong because the city represented the theme of the film, which was that the old and new exist in a strange relationship during an age of an information deluge. Before shooting the film, the artists drew sketches that emphasized Hong Kong's chaotic, confusing and overwhelming aspects.<ref name="ags" />

=== Animation ===
''Ghost in the Shell'' used a novel process called "digitally generated animation" (DGA), which is a combination of [[cel animation]], [[computer graphics]] (CG), and audio that is entered as digital data. In 1995, DGA was thought to be the future of animation, as it allowed traditional animation to be combined with computer graphics and digital cel work with visual displays. Editing was performed on an AVID system of [[Avid Technology]], which was chosen because it was more versatile and less limiting than other methods and worked with the different types of media in a single environment.<ref name=report /> The digital cel work included both original illustrations, compositions and manipulation with traditional cel animation to create a sense of depth and evoke emotion and feelings. Filters like a lens effect were used to create a sense of depth and motion by distorting the front background and making the far background out of focus throughout shots. ''Ghost in the Shell'' used a unique lighting system in which light and darkness were integrated into the cels with attention to light and shadow sources instead of using contrast to control the light. Art director Hiromasa Ogura described this as "a very unusual lighting technique".<ref name=report />

Some special effects, like Motoko's "[[Active camouflage|thermo-optical camouflage]]", were rendered through the use of IG Production's internal tool TIMA.<ref>{{cite web |author1=Vladimir Alexeev (aka Merzmensch |title=Tima was used for distortion of existing imagery |url=https://www.instagram.com/p/DPbICBFE8dr/ |access-date=11 October 2025}}{{unreliable source|date=June 2026}}</ref> The process uses a single illustration and manipulates the image as necessary to produce distortions for effect in combination with a background without altering the original illustration. The effect is re-added back into the shot to complete the scene. While the visual displays used in the film were technically simple to create, the appearance of the displays underwent numerous revisions by the production team to best represent visual displays of the future. Another aspect of the CG use was to create images and effects that looked as if they were "perceived by the brain" and were generated in video and added to the film in its final stages.<ref name=report /> The opening credits of the film were produced by the CG director, Seichi Tanaka. Tanaka converted code in a computer language displayed in [[Romanization of Japanese|romanized Japanese letters]] to numbers before inserting them into the computer to generate the credits. The origin of this code is the names of the film's staff as written in a computer language.<ref name=report /> Animation director Mizuho Nishikubo was responsible for the realism and strove for accurate depictions of movement and effects. The pursuit of realism included the staff conducting firearms research at a facility in [[Guam]]. Nishikubo has highlighted the tank scene as an example of the movie's realism, noting that bullets create sparks when hitting metal, but do not spark when a bullet strikes stone.<ref name=report />

=== Audio ===
''Ghost in the Shell''{{'}}s recording was done with a high-end studio to achieve superior sound throughout the film. A spatializer was used to alter the sound and, specifically in the electronic brain conversations, modify the characters' voices.<ref name=report /> Composer [[Kenji Kawai]] wrote the film's score. For the main theme, Kawai tried to imagine the setting and convey the essence of that world in the music. He incorporated [[classical Japanese|classical Japanese music]] and [[min'yō]] in the opening theme "Making of a Cyborg".<ref name=report /> The composition is a mixture of Bulgarian harmony<ref name=gamesetwatch>{{cite web| url=http://www.gamesetwatch.com/2010/02/sound_current_kenji_kawai_the.php| title=Sound Current: 'Kenji Kawai - Game and Anime Intersections'| publisher=Game Set Watch| date=24 February 2010| access-date=8 November 2013| archive-date=9 November 2013| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131109021832/http://www.gamesetwatch.com/2010/02/sound_current_kenji_kawai_the.php| url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hVqrW-fPOQ0| title=Sound Current: 'Bulgarian folklor- Kafal sviri'| website=[[YouTube]]| date=29 July 2012| access-date=28 March 2017| archive-date=8 April 2017| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170408015652/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hVqrW-fPOQ0| url-status=live }}</ref> and traditional Japanese notes; the haunting [[Choir|choral]]s are a wedding song sung to dispel all evil influences. Symphony conductor Sarah Penicka-Smith notes that the song's lyrics are fitting for the union between Kusanagi and Project 2501 at the climax of the film.<ref>Sarah Penicka-Smith, "Cyborg Songs for an Existential Crisis", in ''Anime and Philosophy: Wide Eyed Wonder'', eds. Josef Steiff & Tristan D. Tamplin (Chicago: Open Court, 2010), 261–74.</ref> Kawai originally wanted to use [[Bulgarian folk music]] singers, but used Japanese ones instead.<ref name=gamesetwatch/> "See You Everyday" is different from the rest of the soundtrack, being a pop song sung in [[Cantonese]] by Fang Ka Wing.<ref name=sound1 />

The ending credits theme of the film's English version is "One Minute Warning" by Passengers, a collaboration between [[U2]] and [[Brian Eno]]. The song appeared on the album ''[[Original Soundtracks 1]]'', and was one of three songs on that album to actually be featured in a film.<ref name=Ebert>{{cite web| url=http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ghost-in-the-shell-1996| title=Ghost in the Shell| publisher=[[RogerEbert.com]]| date=12 April 1996| access-date=13 February 2017| archive-date=1 April 2017| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170401045613/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ghost-in-the-shell-1996| url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url=http://www.u2.com/discography/index/album/albumId/4076/tagName/Other| title=Passengers: Original Soundtracks 1| publisher=[[U2]]| access-date=13 February 2017| archive-date=14 February 2017| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170214004809/http://www.u2.com/discography/index/album/albumId/4076/tagName/Other| url-status=live }}</ref> Andy Frain, the founder of [[Manga Entertainment]] and an executive producer for the film, was a former marketing director for [[Island Records]], the [[record label]] that publishes U2's discography.<ref name=Answerman />

== Releases ==
The film had its world premiere at the [[Tokyo International Film Festival]] on 18 October 1995 before its general release in November.<ref name=report /> It premiered in the United Kingdom on 11 November 1995 as part of the [[BFI London Film Festival|London Film Festival]] in Leicester Square. It was originally rated R by the [[Motion Picture Association|Motion Picture Association of America]] (MPAA), due to full nudity and graphic violence, when it was first released in the United States.<ref>{{Cite web|date=23 March 2017|title=Ghost in the Shell Rated PG-13 by MPAA|url=http://www.horrornewsnetwork.net/ghost-shell-rated-pg-13-mpaa/|access-date=3 June 2021|website=Horror News Network|language=en-US|archive-date=21 April 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170421151354/http://www.horrornewsnetwork.net/ghost-shell-rated-pg-13-mpaa/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film grossed {{US$|10 million|long=no}} in global box office revenue, but this fell short of the film's budget, thus failing to recoup production costs.<ref name="crunchyroll">{{cite news|title=30 Years of Akira: The Triumph and Legacy of a Legendary Film|url=https://www.crunchyroll.com/anime-feature/2018/07/16/30-years-of-akira-the-triumph-and-legacy-of-a-legendary-film|work=[[Crunchyroll]]|date=16 July 2018|access-date=28 November 2018|archive-date=28 November 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181128075345/https://www.crunchyroll.com/anime-feature/2018/07/16/30-years-of-akira-the-triumph-and-legacy-of-a-legendary-film|url-status=live }}</ref> However, a [[cult following]] emerged following the film's release on [[home video]], with  it grossing approximately {{US$|{{#expr:10+33}} million|long=no}} in total box office and home video sales revenue. The English dub of the film was released in the United Kingdom on 8 December 1995 by Metrodome Distribution, and in the United States on 29 March 1996 by [[Palm Pictures]]. The "2.0" version was released in theatres in [[Tokyo]], [[Osaka]], [[Nagoya]], [[Fukuoka]], and [[Sapporo]] on 12 July 2008.<ref>{{cite news| url = http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-06-08/ghost-in-the-shell-to-return-to-japanese-theaters| title = Ghost in the Shell to Return to Japanese Theaters| work = Anime News Network| access-date = 9 November 2011| archive-date = 23 October 2012| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121023142228/http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-06-08/ghost-in-the-shell-to-return-to-japanese-theaters| url-status = live }}</ref> In 2021, the film was given an IMAX restoration and limited theatrical release.<ref name="IMAX National Science and Media Museum" />

== Reception ==
''Ghost in the Shell'' has received critical acclaim. On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 95% based on 62 reviews, with an average rating of 7.8/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "A stunning feat of modern animation, ''Ghost in the Shell'' offers a thoughtful, complex treat for anime fans, as well as a perfect introduction for viewers new to the medium."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/ghost_in_the_shell|title=Ghost in the Shell (1995)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191207013509/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/ghost_in_the_shell/|archive-date=7 December 2019|url-status=live}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 76 out of 100 based on 14 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ghost-in-the-shell|title=Ghost in the Shell (1996) Reviews|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=24 October 2019|archive-date=17 June 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180617161915/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/ghost-in-the-shell|url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Awards ===
The film won Best Screenplay at the [[Yokohama Film Festival]] and was nominated in five [[Annie Awards|Annie Award]] categories at the [[24th Annie Awards]]: [[Annie Award for Best Animated Feature|Best Animated Feature]], [[Annie Award for Outstanding Achievement for Directing in a Feature Production|Best Achievement in Directing]] ([[Mamoru Oshii]]), Best Achievement in Producing ([[Mitsuhisa Ishikawa]], Ken Iyadomi, Ken Matsumoto, Yoshimasa Mizuo, and Shigeru Watanabe), [[Annie Award for Outstanding Achievement for Production Design in an Animated Feature Production|Best Achievement in Production Design]] (Takashi Watabe and Hiromasa Ogura), and [[Annie Award for Outstanding Achievement for Writing in a Feature Production|Best Achievement in Writing]] ([[Kazunori Itō|Kazunori Itô]]), but all lost to ''[[Toy Story]]''.<ref>{{Citation |title=Ghost in the Shell (1995) - Awards - IMDb |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0113568/awards/ |access-date=17 June 2023 |language=en-US |archive-date=17 June 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230617121417/https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0113568/awards/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== ''Ghost in the Shell 2.0'' ===
Reviewers were generally critical of the changes made in the 2008 re-release ''Ghost in the Shell 2.0''. David Jenkins of ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' called the newly-inserted animation "hideous",<ref name=timeout>{{cite web |title=Ghost in the Shell 2.0 2009, directed by Mamoru Oshii |work=Time Out |date=29 September 2009|first=David|last=Jenkins | url=https://www.timeout.com/movies/ghost-in-the-shell-2-0}}</ref> while D. F. Smith of [[IGN]] wrote, "the transitions are painfully jarring – just sudden hard cuts between totally different visual styles – and even viewed in a vacuum, the new CG sequences aren't that impressive".<ref name=ign>{{cite web |title=Ghost in the Shell 2.0 Blu-ray Review|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2009/12/03/ghost-in-the-shell-20-blu-ray-review |date=May 8, 2012|first=D.F. |last=Smith|work=IGN}}</ref> However, both critics still praised the film overall.<ref name=timeout/><ref name=ign/>

== Legacy ==

''Ghost in the Shell'' has influenced prominent filmmakers. [[The Wachowskis]], creators of ''[[The Matrix]]'' and its sequels, showed it to producer [[Joel Silver]], saying, "We wanna do that for real."<ref name="silver-anime-2">[[Joel Silver]], interviewed in "Making ''The Matrix''" featurette on ''The Matrix'' DVD.</ref> ''The Matrix'' series took inspiration from several concepts from the film, with the [[Matrix digital rain|digital rain]] being inspired by the film's opening credits, and the way characters access the Matrix through holes in the back of their necks, directly paralleling the method of electronic communication in the film. Other parallels have been drawn to [[James Cameron]]'s ''[[Avatar (2009 film)|Avatar]]'', [[Steven Spielberg]]'s ''[[A.I. Artificial Intelligence]]'', and [[Jonathan Mostow]]'s ''[[Surrogates]]''. Cameron described ''Ghost in the Shell'' as "a stunning work of speculative fiction{{nbsp}}... the first to reach a level of literary excellence."<ref>{{citation |last=Rose |first=Steve |title=Hollywood is haunted by ''Ghost in the Shell'' |date=19 October 2009 |newspaper=The Guardian |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/oct/19/hollywood-ghost-in-the-shell |access-date=27 July 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130308101232/http://www.guardian.co.uk/film/2009/oct/19/hollywood-ghost-in-the-shell |archive-date=8 March 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last1=Schrodt|first1=Paul|title=How the original 'Ghost in the Shell' changed sci-fi and the way we think about the future|url=https://www.businessinsider.com/original-ghost-in-the-shell-movie-influence-2017-3|access-date=14 June 2019|work=[[Business Insider]]|date=1 April 2017|archive-date=26 August 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190826142138/https://www.businessinsider.com/original-ghost-in-the-shell-movie-influence-2017-3|url-status=live }}</ref>

== Themes ==

The film explores the nature of human [[cyborg]]s, [[consciousness]], [[Artificial consciousness|self-aware computer programs]], and memory alteration.<ref name="Suderman">{{cite web| url=https://www.vox.com/2017/4/4/15138682/ghost-in-the-shell-anime-philosophy| title="The original Ghost in the Shell is iconic anime, and a rich philosophical text"| publisher=Vox| date=April 2017| access-date=9 February 2022| author=Suderman, Peter| archive-date=29 April 2019| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190429110532/https://www.vox.com/2017/4/4/15138682/ghost-in-the-shell-anime-philosophy| url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Hains">{{cite web| url=https://www.looper.com/622067/the-ending-of-ghost-in-the-shell-explained/| title="The Ending Of Ghost In The Shell Explained"| publisher=Looper| date=October 2021| access-date=9 February 2022| author=Hains, Sidney Baum| archive-date=9 February 2022| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220209221551/https://www.looper.com/622067/the-ending-of-ghost-in-the-shell-explained/}}</ref> In one of the monologues delivered by the Puppet Master throughout the film, it is argued that the human [[DNA]] is nothing more than a program designed to self-preserve.<ref>{{Cite journal|title=THE GHOST OUTSIDE ITS SHELL: REVISITING THE PHILOSOPHY OF GHOST IN THE SHELL|last=Komel|first=Mirt|journal=Teorija in Praksa|date=2016|volume=53|pages=926}}</ref> There are also multiple mentions of the act of granting [[Right of asylum|political asylum]] to self-aware computer programs.

The film depicts Motoko's identity and ontological concerns and ends with the [[evolution]] of the Puppet Master, a being incapable of reproduction.<ref name="Orbaugh2">{{cite web| url=http://www.depauw.edu/site/sfs/backissues/88/orbaugh.html| title=Sex and the Single Cyborg: Japanese Popular Culture Experiments in Subjectivity| publisher=Science Fiction Studies| work=Volume 88| date=November 2002| access-date=16 August 2013| author=Orbaugh, Sharalyn| archive-date=4 September 2019| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190904073024/http://www.depauw.edu/site/sfs/backissues/88/orbaugh.html| url-status=live }}</ref> Austin Corbett characterized the lack of sexualization from her team as freedom from femininity, noting that Motoko is "overtly feminine, and clearly non-female".<ref name="Corbett">{{cite web| url=http://jetpress.org/v20/corbett.htm| title=Beyond Ghost in the (Human) Shell| work=Journal of Evolution and Technology - Vol. 20 Issue 1| date=March 2009| access-date=16 August 2013| author=Corbett, Austin| pages=43–50| archive-date=25 September 2013| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130925225706/http://jetpress.org/v20/corbett.htm| url-status=live }}</ref> Joseph Schaub discusses the gender-defying manner in which the Puppet Master is portrayed, visualized with a blonde woman's bare-chested torso but speaking in a male voice and referred to with masculine pronouns.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Schaub |first=Joseph Christopher |date=2001-01-01 |title=Kusanagi's body: Gender and technology in mecha-anime |url=https://doi.org/10.1080/01292980109364805 |journal=Asian Journal of Communication |volume=11 |issue=2 |pages=79–100 |doi=10.1080/01292980109364805 |issn=0129-2986|url-access=subscription }}</ref> In describing Motoko as a "shapely" and "strong [female protagonist] at the center of the story" who is "nevertheless almost continuously nude", Roger Ebert noted that "an article about anime in a recent issue of ''[[Film Quarterly]]''<ref>Annalee Newitz, 'Magical Girls and Atomic Bomb Sperm: Japanese Animation in America', ''Film Quarterly'' 49, no. 1 (Autumn 1995): 2-15.</ref> suggests that to be a '[[salary man]]' in modern Japan is so exhausting and dehumanizing that many men (who form the largest part of the animation audience) project both freedom and power onto women, and identify with them as fictional characters".<ref name=Ebert /> Carl Silvio has called ''Ghost in the Shell'' a "resistant film", due to its inversion of traditional gender roles, its "valorization of the post-gendered subject", and its de-emphasis of the sexual specificity of the material body.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Silvio|first=Carl|title=Refiguring the Radical Cyborg in Mamoru Oshii's Ghost in the Shell|journal=Science Fiction Studies|date=March 1999|volume=26|issue=1|pages=54–72 |doi=10.1525/sfs.26.1.0054 |url=http://www.depauw.edu/sfs/backissues/77/silvio77.htm|access-date=9 November 2013|archive-date=1 September 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130901042318/http://www.depauw.edu/sfs/backissues/77/silvio77.htm|url-status=live}}</ref>
