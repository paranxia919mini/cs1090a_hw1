'''''Dahomey''''' is a 2024 [[documentary film]] directed by [[Mati Diop]]. It is a dramatised account of 26 royal treasures from the [[Dahomey|Kingdom of Dahomey]] (in modern-day Benin), which were held in a museum in France. The film explores how the artifacts were returned from France to Benin, and the reactions of Beninese people.<ref name="lead:cine">{{Cite web |url= https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/454362 |title= 20 European films we're anticipating in 2024 |first= David |last= Katz |date= 9 January 2024 |access-date= 28 January 2024 |website= [[Cineuropa]] |language= en |archive-date= 31 January 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240131132042/https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/454362/ |url-status= live }}</ref> The film was an international co-production between companies in France, Senegal, and Benin.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://cineuropa.org/film/455750/ |title=Dahomey |first=Mati |last=Diop |date=9 February 2024 |access-date=9 February 2024 |website=[[Cineuropa]] |language=en |archive-date=15 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240215160525/https://cineuropa.org/film/455750/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film had its world premiere at the main competition of the [[74th Berlin International Film Festival]],<ref name="bda:acc2">{{cite web |date=1 February 2024 |title=Berlinale Documentary Award and Jury |url=https://www.berlinale.de/en/festival/awards-and-juries/berlinale-documentary-award.html |access-date=2 February 2024 |work=Berlinale |archive-date=28 January 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220128130040/https://www.berlinale.de/en/festival/awards-and-juries/berlinale-documentary-award.html |url-status=live }}</ref> where it won the festival's top prize, the [[Golden Bear]].<ref name="winner:hr2">{{Cite web |first=Scott |last=Roxborough |date=24 February 2024 |title=Mati Diop Doc 'Dahomey' Wins Berlin Golden Bear |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/2024-berlin-film-festival-winners-mati-diop-sebastian-stan-emily-watson-1235833970/ |access-date=24 February 2024 |website=Hollywood Reporter |language=en |archive-date=25 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240225034232/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/2024-berlin-film-festival-winners-mati-diop-sebastian-stan-emily-watson-1235833970/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Abbatescianni |first=Davide |date=22 January 2024 |title=The Berlinale unveils its Competition and Encounters titles |url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/455195 |access-date=28 January 2024 |website=[[Cineuropa]] |language=en |archive-date=15 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240215160521/https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/455195/ |url-status=live }}</ref> It was released theatrically in France on 11 September 2024, by [[Les Films du Losange]], to critical acclaim. ==Contents==
The documentary film blends facts and fiction to narrate the stories of 26 African artworks.<ref name="lead:cine"/> The royal artefacts from the [[Dahomey|Kingdom of Dahomey]] (1600–1904) were taken to France during [[French Dahomey|the region's colonial period]] (1872–1960). In the 21st century, they were put on display in the [[Musée du Quai Branly – Jacques Chirac]], a museum of non-European art located in Paris. Following a campaign for [[repatriation (cultural property)|repatriation]], the artefacts were returned to Benin.

Among the returned works were statues of two kings of Dahomey, [[Glele]] and [[Béhanzin]]. Their throne, which had been seized by French soldiers in 1892, was also given back.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://de.euronews.com/2021/11/09/schatze-aus-dahomey-frankreich-gibt-geraubte-kunst-an-benin-zuruck |title=Schätze aus Dahomey: Frankreich gibt geraubte Kunst an Benin zurück |trans-title=Treasures from Dahomey: France returns stolen art to Benin |date=9 November 2021 |access-date=28 January 2024 |website=[[Euronews]] |language=de |archive-date=28 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240128052121/https://de.euronews.com/2021/11/09/schatze-aus-dahomey-frankreich-gibt-geraubte-kunst-an-benin-zuruck |url-status=live }}</ref> The art pieces are now displayed in a museum in [[Abomey]], the old royal city, about 65 miles from the [[Gulf of Guinea]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://momaa.org/directory/musee-histsorique-d-abomey/ |title=Musee Histsorique d' Abomey |access-date=28 January 2024 |website=MoMAA |language=en |archive-date=19 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240119214640/https://momaa.org/directory/musee-histsorique-d-abomey/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film includes a discussion by students at the [[University of Abomey-Calavi]], presenting their views on the repatriation of cultural assets. Some of the students criticise the Paris museum for returning only 26 of the 7,000 worldwide [[ethnographic]] objects it holds.<ref name="DeadlineHollywood 18-02-2024">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2024/02/dahomey-review-mati-diop-audacious-doc-offers-a-provocative-view-of-modern-africa-berlin-film-festival-1235829786/#!|title='Dahomey' Review: Mati Diop's Audacious Doc Offers A Provocative View Of Modern Africa – Berlin Film Festival|website=Deadline|first=Stephanie|last=Bunbury|access-date=29 February 2024|date=18 February 2024|archive-date=29 February 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240229130322/https://deadline.com/2024/02/dahomey-review-mati-diop-audacious-doc-offers-a-provocative-view-of-modern-africa-berlin-film-festival-1235829786/#!|url-status=live}}</ref>

A prominent role in the film is given to the 26th art object to be repatriated, a statue that represents [[Ghezo|King Ghézo]], who ruled from 1818 to 1859, shown below. A [[voice-over]] by the Haitian writer [[Makenzy Orcel]] (who wrote this part of the script), playing the object, tells of the time it spent in storage at the Paris museum, its memories of Africa and thoughts of returning to its homeland.

==Production==
[[File:Film Crew of Dahowey at Berlinale 2024.jpg|thumb|Film crew of ''Dahomey'': Habib Ahandessi, Joséa Guedje, [[Mati Diop]] and Gildas Adannou at Berlinale 2024]]

The documentary was produced by Les Films du Bal in co-production with Fanta Sy and distributed by [[Les Films du Losange]]. The director was [[Mati Diop]], who also wrote the script, and the [[director of photography]] was Joséphine Drouin-Viallard. It was edited by Gabriel Gonzalez. The thoughts of the voiced statue were written by [[Makenzy Orcel]]. The music was composed by [[Wally Badarou]] and [[Dean Blunt]]. Corneille Houssou, [[Nicolas Becker (sound engineer)|Nicolas Becker]] and Cyril Holtz were the sound designers.<ref name="rev:variety"/> The film incorporates footage from the surveillance cameras at the [[Musée du Quai Branly – Jacques Chirac]] and the Palais de la Marina in [[Cotonou]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://en.unifrance.org/movie/56518/dahomey |title=Dahomey |date=18 December 2023 |access-date=28 January 2024 |website=[[Unifrance]] |language=en |archive-date=28 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240128062723/https://en.unifrance.org/movie/56518/dahomey |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception==
[[File:MKr354597 Mati Diop (Goldener Bär der Berlinale 2024).jpg|thumb|upright|Mati Diop with the [[Golden Bear]] award for the best film of the [[Berlin International Film Festival|Berlinale]] 2024]]

===Critical response=== 
On the review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]] website, the film has an approval rating of 99% based on 81 reviews, with an average rating of 8.2/10. The website's critics consensus reads: "With a rigorous yet fantastical approach, Mati Diop's ''Dahomey'' provocatively uncovers the restitution and repatriation of a stolen legacy, and serves as a powerful statement for decolonization."<ref name="score:rotten">{{cite Rotten Tomatoes|title=Dahomey|id=dahomey|type=m|access-date=13 December 2024}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a weighted average score of 85 out of 100 based on 23 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=dahomey|type=movie|title=Dahomey|access-date=13 December 2024}}</ref>

On the [[AlloCiné]], which lists 27 press reviews, the film obtained an average rating of 4.1/5.<ref name="rec:allocine">{{Cite web |url= https://www.allocine.fr/film/fichefilm_gen_cfilm=314794.html |title= Dahomey |trans-title= Dahomey |website= [[AlloCiné]] |date= 13 September 2024 |access-date= 13 December 2024 |language= fr |archive-date= 25 February 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240225022159/https://www.allocine.fr/film/fichefilm_gen_cfilm=314794.html |url-status= live }}</ref>

David Rooney reviewing the film for ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' described it as "Richly layered and resonant", and opined: "This directorial flourish liberates the looted treasures from being mere objects, with smart use of subjective camera by DP Joséphine Drouin-Viallard helping to make them come alive as characters."<ref name="rev:hr">{{Cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1235829050/ |title='Dahomey' Review: Mati Diop Tracks the Return of African Royal Treasures Plundered by French Colonists in Powerful Consideration of Reparations |first=David |last=Rooney |website=The Hollywood Reporter |date=18 February 2024 |access-date=18 February 2024 |language=en |archive-date=24 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240224203926/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1235829050/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

E. Nina Rothe, writing for the ''[[International Cinephile Society]]'', noted that the film "is important, with its message crucial to restitution providing the beginning of righting the wrongs of colonialism".<ref name="rev:ics">{{Cite web |url=https://icsfilm.org/reviews/berlinale-2024-review-dahomey-mati-diop/ |title=Berlinale 2024 review: Dahomey (Mati Diop) |first=E. Nina |last=Rothe |website=International Cinephile Society |date=22 February 2024 |access-date=22 February 2024 |language=en |archive-date=23 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240223231754/https://icsfilm.org/reviews/berlinale-2024-review-dahomey-mati-diop/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Wendy Ide wrote in ''[[ScreenDaily]]'' while reviewing the film at Berlinale, "In this agile, cerebral film, using a combination of deft fly-on-the-wall footage, a centrepiece debate among students at the University of Abomey-Calavi and an unexpected element of fantasy, the film feels like an important contribution to an ongoing conversation about the legacy of colonialism in Africa, and to the thorny topic of restitution and repatriation of cultural heritage to the country of its origin."<ref name="rev:sd">{{cite web |url=https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/dahomey-berlin-review/5190575.article |title='Dahomey': Berlin Review |first=Wendy |last=Ide |date=18 February 2024 |access-date=18 February 2024 |website=[[ScreenDaily]] |language=en |archive-date=2 March 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240302002542/https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/dahomey-berlin-review/5190575.article |url-status=live }}</ref>

Jessica Kiang, writing in ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' in her review of Berlinale, said: "French-Senegalese director Mati Diop fashions her superb, short but potent hybrid doc ''Dahomey'' as a slim lever that cracks open the sealed crate of colonial history, sending a hundred of its associated erasures and injustices tumbling into the light." Kiang concluded: "''Dahomey'' is a striking, stirring example of the poetry that can result when the dead and the dispossessed speak to and through the living."<ref name="rev:variety">{{Cite news|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/reviews/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1235911765/|title='Dahomey' Review: Mati Diop's Exquisitely Strange Documentary Meditation on the Return of Looted Artifacts to Benin|first=Jessica|last=Kiang|work=Variety|date=18 February 2024|access-date=18 February 2024|language=en|archive-date=18 February 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240218151747/https://variety.com/2024/film/reviews/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1235911765/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Stephanie Bunbury, in her review at Berlinale for [[Deadline Hollywood|''Deadline'']] said: "Open-ended, fecund with imagination and ideas, never hectoring or lecturing, not so much posing questions as asking what questions might be posed: Mati Diop's film is a marvelous provocation."<ref name="DeadlineHollywood 18-02-2024" />

Adam Solomons of ''[[IndieWire]]'', reviewing at Berlinale, graded the film B and criticised the runtime of the film, he opined: "''Dahomey'' might have worked better at a runtime of [closer to 30 minutes]: the student debate, though well staged, becomes a bit repetitive, and some of the shots of boxes being loaded and unloaded go at a snail's pace." Concluding Solomons praised the director [[Mati Diop]] and wrote: "''Dahomey'' is a bold and memorable history lesson. But with Diop's expressive talents as they are, it's fair to hope that she returns to the world of fiction next time."<ref name="rev:iw">{{Cite web |url= https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/movies/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1234954949/ |title= 'Dahomey' Review: Mati Diop Literally Gives Looted African Artifacts a Voice in Strange, Sensitive Doc |first= Adam |last= Solomons |website= IndieWire |date= 18 February 2024 |access-date= 19 February 2024 |language= en |archive-date= 18 February 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240218224143/https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/movies/dahomey-review-mati-diop-1234954949/ |url-status= live }}</ref>

Writing for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', Robert Daniels praised "inventive" Diop's "distinct approach to the seemingly straightforward topic", highlighting the film's "dreamlike score", and saying that ''Dahomey'' "fills and nourishes the viewer with urgent desires, providing space for the light that constitutes the souls of Black folk to shine brighter through repair. Diop is back, and she is just as searing and imperative as ever."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Daniels |first=Robert |title=Berlin Film Festival 2024: Dahomey, My Favorite Cake, A Traveler's Needs {{!}} Festivals & Awards {{!}} Roger Ebert |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/berlin-film-festival-2024-dahomey-my-favorite-cake-a-travelers-needs |access-date=22 February 2024 |website=RogerEbert.com |date=19 February 2024 |language=en |archive-date=21 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240221150915/https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/berlin-film-festival-2024-dahomey-my-favorite-cake-a-travelers-needs |url-status=live }}</ref>

Reviewing in ''Le Polyester'', Nicolas Bardot gave the film a 5/6 rating, and stated: "Mati Diop ambitiously mixes the political and the poetic. Her stories always project further than the facts apparent before our eyes." Concluding, Bardot opined: "In ''Dahomey'', it is not only the present that the past finds, but also the future."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://lepolyester.com/critique-dahomey/ |title=Berlinale {{!}} Critique: Dahomey |trans-title=Berlinale {{!}} Review: Dahomey |first=Nicolas |last=Bardot |work=Le Polyester |publisher=WordPress |date=18 February 2024 |access-date=25 February 2024 |language=fr |archive-date=24 February 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240224111434/https://lepolyester.com/critique-dahomey/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Nicholas Bell in Ion Cinema rated the film with three and half stars and opened his review stating, "The spirit of [[Ozymandias]], the classic poem from [[Percy Bysshe Shelley]], might rouse itself in one's mind during Mati Diop's short but passionate documentary ''Dahomey'' – "Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!"" Thus Bell opined, "[the film] is a depiction of a journey with so much more going on beneath the surface than an exchange of cultural artifacts." Concluding his review, Bell said: "Much like her 2019 narrative debut, ''[[Atlantics]]'', Diop proves to be exceptionally adept at coalescing textures and strands in remarkably dense ways, and ''Dahomey'' is an excellent point of entry in an ongoing conversation."<ref name="rev:ionc">{{Cite news |url= https://www.ioncinema.com/reviews/mati-diop-dahomey-review |title= Plunder Years: Diop Reflects on the Complex Realities of Reparation |first= Nicholas |last= Bell |work= Ion Cinema |date= 18 February 2024 |access-date= 25 February 2024 |language= en |archive-date= 25 February 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240225050004/https://www.ioncinema.com/reviews/mati-diop-dahomey-review |url-status= live }}</ref>

[[Peter Bradshaw]] reviewing for ''[[The Guardian]]'' rated the film with four stars out of five and wrote, "It is an invigorating and enlivening film, with obvious implications for the Elgin/Parthenon marbles in the British Museum."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2024/feb/23/dahomey-review-benin-bronze-berlin-film-festival-mati-diop |title=Dahomey review – interrogative reverie about looted African sculptures |first=Peter|last=Bradshaw |author-link=Peter Bradshaw |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=23 February 2024 |access-date=15 February 2024}}</ref>

Shubhra Gupta, reviewing for ''[[The Indian Express]]'', wrote that the film using a unique documentary approach laced with fantasy "powerfully challenges post-colonial notions of reparations and repair", and that the film is "A question that deserves our attention, and the answers that emerge from it..."<ref name="rev:ie">{{Cite web|url=https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/entertainment-others/from-hilde-with-love-dahomey-the-undead-memories-of-holocaust-colonialism-9170180/ |title= From Hilde, With Love to Dahomey: The undead memories of Holocaust, colonialism |first= Shubhra |last=Gupta |website= The Indian Express |date= 20 February 2024 |access-date= 27 February 2024|language=en}}</ref>

In June 2025, ''[[IndieWire]]'' ranked the film at number 48 on its list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)."<ref name="Best">{{cite web |date=June 16, 2025 |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)|url=https://www.indiewire.com/lists/best-movies-2020s/ |access-date=2025-06-23}}</ref>

=== Accolades ===
[[File:Mati Diop with Golden Bear of Berlinale 2024.tiff|thumb|[[Mati Diop]] with the Golden Bear at [[Berlin International Film Festival|Berlinale]] 2024]]
''Dahomey'' was selected to compete at the [[74th Berlin International Film Festival]], where it was awarded the [[Golden Bear]] award for the best film. It is the second African film to win the top prize at the festival, following [[Mark Dornford-May]]'s South African drama film ''[[U-Carmen eKhayelitsha]]'' in 2015. ''Dahomey'' was the second documentary in a row to take the Golden Bear, after [[Nicolas Philibert]]'s ''[[On the Adamant]]'' in 2023. During her acceptance speech, Diop called for people "to tear down the wall of silence together" and "to rebuild through restitution", which entails "bringing justice".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/457564 |title=Mati Diop's Dahomey bags the Berlinale Golden Bear |first=Davide |last=Abbatescianni |date=24 February 2024 |access-date=25 February 2024 |website=Cineuropa |language=en |archive-date=1 April 2024 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240401081821/https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/457564 |url-status=live }}</ref>

In August 2024, the film was selected for nomination to [[37th European Film Awards]] held at [[Lucerne Culture and Congress Centre|Kultur- und Kongresszentrum Luzern]] in [[Lucerne]] on 7 December 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |title=European Film Awards 2024: The European Film Academy announces this year's Documentary Film Selection |url=https://www.europeanfilmacademy.org/press/european-film-awards-2024-documentary-selection/ |access-date=24 August 2024 |website=[[European Film Academy]] |language=en-US |archive-date=23 August 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240823025019/https://www.europeanfilmacademy.org/press/european-film-awards-2024-documentary-selection/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Roxborough |first=Scott |date=21 August 2024 |title=European Film Awards Pick 2024 Documentary Films Shortlist |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/european-film-awards-2024-documentary-shortlist-1235980239/ |access-date=24 August 2024 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |language=en-US |archive-date=23 August 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240823025015/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/european-film-awards-2024-documentary-shortlist-1235980239/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film was ranked 15th among the top 25 European works of 2024 by the journalists at [[Cineuropa]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/471402 |title=Cineuropa's Best of 2024 |date=19 December 2024 |access-date=19 December 2024 |website=[[Cineuropa]] |language=en |archive-date=18 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241218231733/https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/471402/ |url-status=live }}</ref>
