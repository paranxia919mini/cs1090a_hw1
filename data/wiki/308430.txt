'''''The Faculty''''' is a 1998 American [[science fiction film|science fiction]] [[horror film]] directed and edited by [[Robert Rodriguez]] with a screenplay by [[Kevin Williamson (screenwriter)|Kevin Williamson]], about a group of high school students who begin to suspect that their teachers are being replaced by [[Extraterrestrial life|extraterrestrial]]s. It stars [[Jordana Brewster]] in her film debut, [[Clea DuVall]], [[Laura Harris]], [[Josh Hartnett]], [[Shawn Hatosy]], [[Famke Janssen]], [[Piper Laurie]], [[Bebe Neuwirth]], [[Robert Patrick]], [[Usher (musician)|Usher Raymond]], [[Jon Stewart]], and [[Elijah Wood]].

The film was theatrically released on December 25, 1998, by [[Miramax|Miramax Films]]. It grossed $63.2 million<ref name="JPBO">{{cite web|url=https://jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=3058|title=The Faculty (1998)|work=JP's Box Office|access-date=November 23, 2019|archive-date=July 26, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190726032850/http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=3058}}</ref> and has developed a [[cult following]] since its release.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.criminalelement.com/blogs/2015/04/under-the-radar-genre-movies-you-may-have-missed-the-faculty-1998-high-school-aliens-angie-barry|title=Under the Radar: Genre Movies You May Have Missed — ''The Faculty''|first=Angie|last=Barry|website=Criminalelement.com|date=April 13, 2015|access-date=August 28, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://screenrant.com/the-faculty-reasons-greatest-best-90s-horror-movie-film/|title = The Faculty: 10 Reasons It's the Greatest 90's Horror Movie|website = [[Screen Rant]]|date = December 7, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://screenrant.com/faculty-underrated-90s-horror-scifi-movie/ | title=The Faculty is an Overlooked '90s Horror Movie You Should See | website=[[Screen Rant]] | date=November 29, 2019 | access-date=July 22, 2022 | archive-date=July 22, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220722003925/https://screenrant.com/faculty-underrated-90s-horror-scifi-movie/ | url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://whatculture.com/film/10-underrated-90s-teen-movies?page=7 | title=10 Most Underrated 90s Teen Movies | date=August 27, 2014 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/the-faculty-revisiting-the-90s-pulp-horror-cult-classic | title=The Faculty: Revisiting the '90s Pulp-Horror Cult Classic | date=October 9, 2023 }}</ref>

==Plot==
One evening at Herrington High School in [[Ohio]], teachers and Principal Drake leave after discussing the school's budget. When Drake returns to retrieve her keys, she is attacked by the school's football coach, Joe Willis. Drama teacher Mrs. Olson emotionlessly stabs Drake with scissors as she flees the school.

The following morning, the students arrive, including Casey Connor, the dedicated but perpetually harassed photographer for the school newspaper. Casey is the unappreciated assistant to spiteful Delilah Profitt, the paper's editor-in-chief and head cheerleader. Delilah's mistreated boyfriend Stan Rosado is contemplating quitting the football team to pursue academics. Zeke Tyler is an intelligent yet rebellious student repeating his senior year. Zeke sells, among other illegal items, a powdery [[MDMA|ecstasy]]-like drug he distributes hidden in ballpoint pens. He is confronted by teacher Elizabeth Burke, who expresses concern for him over his illegal activities. Naive transfer student Marybeth Louise Hutchinson befriends self-styled outcast Stokely Mitchell, who has deliberately spread rumors that she is a lesbian though she has a crush on Stan. Marybeth develops a crush on Zeke which is reciprocated.

Casey finds a strange creature on the football field and takes it to science teacher Mr. Furlong, who believes it is a new species of [[cephalopod]]-specific parasite called a [[mesozoa]]n. Delilah and Casey hide in the teachers' lounge to find a story. They witness Coach Willis and Ms. Olson forcing one of the parasites into the ear of the school nurse. They also find the body of another teacher, Mrs. Brummel. Casey and Delilah flee, and Casey calls the police, but his claims are dismissed.

The next day, Casey tells Delilah, Stan, and Stokely he believes the teachers are being controlled by aliens. After Zeke and Marybeth tease them about their theory, Mr. Furlong attempts to infect them. Zeke injects his homemade drugs into Furlong's eye, killing him. Zeke takes the five to his house, where he experiments on a specimen retrieved by Casey. He discovers it needs water to survive and can be killed by his "drug", revealed to actually be raw, powdered caffeine (as caffeine acts to dehydrate the user, and the aliens are hyper-sensitive to hydration of both themselves and their host). Zeke makes everyone take his drug to prove they are uninfected. Delilah is revealed as infected and she destroys Zeke's lab and most of his drug supply before escaping.

Acting on Stokely's speculation that killing the alien queen will revert everyone to normal, the group returns to the school, where their football team is playing and infecting opposing players. Believing Principal Drake to be the queen, they isolate her in the gym and fatally shoot her. Stan confronts the coach and team to see if the plan worked, but becomes infected himself. Zeke and Casey retrieve more of Zeke's drugs from his car. Casey leads infected students away from Zeke, who encounters Miss Burke in the parking lot and incapacitates her.

At the gym, Marybeth reveals herself to be the alien queen; earlier on, she faked taking the drug. Casey and Stokely flee to the swimming pool, where Stokely is injured and becomes infected. Zeke and Casey hide in the locker room, where Marybeth reverts to her human disguise. She explains she is taking over Earth because her planet is dying. Marybeth transforms back into her true form and hurls Zeke across the room into the lockers, knocking him out. Casey seizes the drug and traps the queen behind retracting bleachers. Just as the queen infects him, he stabs the drug into her eye, killing her and halting his infection. Casey returns to the locker room and finds Stokely and Zeke alive.

One month later, everyone has returned to normal. Stan and Stokely, who has shed her goth girl image, are now dating. Zeke has taken Stan's place on the football team, while Miss Burke affectionately watches him practice. Delilah, no longer vindictive, is now dating Casey, who is considered a local hero as various news media reveal the attempted alien invasion is now public knowledge, even as the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] denies it.

==Production==
In 1990, David Wechter and Bruce Kimmel wrote their first draft of the script and sent it out, but there were no buyers. It was not until after the success of ''[[Scream (1996 film)|Scream]]'' (1996) that [[Miramax|Miramax Films]] bought the script and rushed it into production. [[Bob Weinstein]] and [[Harvey Weinstein]] brought in Kevin Williamson to do rewrites, keeping the basic story, but rewriting the dialogue and adding new characters to make it more "hip". Originally, Williamson was set to direct the film, but he chose not to so he could direct his self-penned script ''[[Teaching Mrs. Tingle]]'' (1999). The Weinsteins brought in Robert Rodriguez to direct the film instead.<ref>{{cite news |last=Goldstein |first=Patrick |date=January 1, 1999 |title=The Faculty |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=http://www.teako170.com/faculty.html |access-date=September 29, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.sfgate.com/entertainment/article/The-Real-Story-Behind-The-Faculty-How-Miramax-2952947.php|title=The Real Story Behind 'The Faculty'/How Miramax used a hot screenwriter to promote script developed by others|date=1999-01-10|newspaper=[[San Francisco Weekly]]|access-date=2026-05-25}}</ref>

''The Faculty'' takes place in the fictional town of Herrington, Ohio, but was shot in [[Austin, Texas|Austin]], [[San Marcos, Texas|San Marcos]], [[Dallas]], and [[Lockhart, Texas]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Faculty Film Locations |url=https://www.austintexas.org/film-commission/film-tourism-guide/the-faculty/ |access-date=April 25, 2022 |website=www.austintexas.org |language=en-us}}</ref> In a retrospective interview, Clea Duvall said the making of the film "was so much fun. It was mostly night shoots, so it was like we were in this alternate universe. Working all night long and making this fun sci-fi horror movie. I loved it."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zuckerman |first=Esther |date=August 24, 2016 |title=Clea DuVall on ''Can't Hardly Wait'', ''Buffy'', and how cramps almost cost her ''Carnivàle'' |url=https://www.avclub.com/clea-duvall-on-can-t-hardly-wait-buffy-and-how-cramps-1798251486 |website=[[The A.V. Club]]}}</ref>

===Home media and rights===
In the United States, the film's home video releases were initially handled by [[Buena Vista Home Entertainment]] (under the Dimension Home Video banner). The film was first released on [[VHS]] on May 14, 1999. On June 15, 1999, the film was released on [[DVD]] and [[LaserDisc]] in the United States.<ref name="ld">{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/search.php?search=The+Faculty&sort=title|title=LaserDisc Database - Search - The Faculty|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref> It also received a Hong Kong LaserDisc release in July 1999, which was handled by ERA Home Entertainment.<ref name="ld"/> The film's Australian [[DVD Region code|Region 4]] DVD release was handled by [[Village Roadshow]], who had an Australian distribution agreement with Miramax at the time.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.45cat.com/dvd/disc/9398710205893|title=DVD - The Faculty - Roadshow Entertainment - Australia|website=www.45cat.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web | title=THE FACULTY {{!}} Australian Classification | url=https://www.classification.gov.au/titles/faculty-2 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250913001215/https://www.classification.gov.au/titles/faculty-2 | access-date=2026-09-04 | archive-date=2025-09-13}}</ref>

In 2005, Dimension was sold by [[the Walt Disney Company]], with Disney later selling off the parent label Miramax in 2010. Miramax and the rights to the pre–October 2005 Dimension library were subsequently taken over by private equity firm [[Filmyard Holdings]] that same year.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/business/2010/jul/30/film-industry-privateequity|title=Disney sells Miramax to investment group for $660m|first=David|last=Teather|work=The Guardian |date=July 30, 2010}}</ref> Filmyard licensed the home video rights for high-profile Dimension/Miramax titles to [[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]], with lower-profile titles and niche horror titles being licensed to [[Echo Bridge Entertainment]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/echo-bridge-distribute-miramax-titles-101170/|title=Echo Bridge to Distribute Miramax Titles on DVD, Blu-Ray|first=Alex Ben|last=Block|date=February 17, 2011}}</ref> The film received its first U.S. [[Blu-ray]] release on September 11, 2012, through Echo Bridge.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Faculty-Blu-ray/44819/|title=The Faculty Blu-ray|via=www.blu-ray.com}}</ref> In 2014, Filmyard Holdings terminated their home video agreement with Echo Bridge, with the film receiving a U.S. Blu-ray reissue on October 7, 2014, from [[Lionsgate Home Entertainment]]. The Lionsgate Blu-ray included a digital download code for the film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Faculty-Blu-ray/109144/|title=The Faculty Blu-ray (Blu-ray + Digital HD)|via=www.blu-ray.com}}</ref> During Filmyard's ownership of Miramax, they had agreements with other local distributors, some of which existed during the Disney ownership era. International Blu-ray releases for ''The Faculty'' occurred in several countries: in Canada on October 6, 2009, by [[Alliance Films|Alliance]]; in Australia on September 1, 2011, by [[Reel Corporation|Reel]]; in the United Kingdom on October 3, 2011, by Lionsgate; in Germany on October 6, 2011, by [[Studio Canal]]; and in France on January 10, 2012, by Studio Canal. In 2011, Filmyard Holdings licensed the Miramax library and pre–October 2005 Dimension library to streamer [[Netflix]]. This deal included ''The Faculty'', which was available on the service for five years, eventually being removed on June 1, 2016.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/leaving-soon/miramax-deal-with-netflix-ends-on-june-1st-over-400-movies-leaving/|title=Miramax Deal With Netflix Ends on June 1st - Over 400 Movies Leaving|date=May 21, 2016|website=What's on Netflix}}</ref>

Filmyard Holdings sold Miramax to Qatari company [[beIN Media Group]] in March 2016.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2016/mar/02/bein-media-group-miramax-film-studio-acquisition|title=Iconic film studio Miramax sells to Doha-based beIN Media Group|first=Nigel M.|last=Smith|work=The Guardian |date=March 2, 2016}}</ref> In April 2020, [[ViacomCBS]] (now known as Paramount Skydance) acquired the rights to Miramax's library and Dimension's pre–October 2005 library after buying a 49% stake in Miramax from beIN.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/viacomcbs-closes-acquisition-miramax-stake-375-million-deal-1286478/|title=ViacomCBS Closes Acquisition of 49 Percent Miramax Stake in $375 Million Deal|first=Georg|last=Szalai|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=April 3, 2020}}</ref> ''The Faculty'' was among the 700 titles Paramount acquired in the deal,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://parkcircus.com/film/107546-The-Faculty|title=The Faculty|website=Park Circus}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.kaleidescape.com/marine-movie-store/movie/the-faculty-1998|title=The Faculty (1998) &#124; Kaleidescape Movie Store}}</ref> and it has been distributed by [[Paramount Pictures]] since April 2020. As part of the deal, Paramount entered into a first-look agreement with beIN/Miramax, which allows Paramount to release any future projects based on Miramax and pre–October 2005 Dimension properties.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/neal-mortiz-paramount-first-look-deal-1235871980/|title=Neal H. Moritz, Paramount Renew First Look Production Deal|first=Etan|last=Vlessing|date=April 11, 2024}}</ref>

In late 2020, [[Paramount Home Entertainment]] began reissuing many of the Dimension/Miramax titles they had acquired, and on September 22, 2020, they released another Blu-ray edition of ''The Faculty''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com.au/The-Faculty-Blu-ray/dp/B08R75GGWK|title=2020 Paramount Home Entertainment Blu-ray for The Faculty|via=amazon.com}}</ref> Paramount later sublicensed the home video rights for the film to [[Shout! Factory]]'s horror label Scream Factory, and on December 17, 2024, Scream Factory released a [[4K Ultra HD]] Blu-ray of the film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://shoutfactory.com/products/the-faculty-collectors-edition|title=The Faculty [Collector's Edition]|website=Shout! On March 4, 2021, ''The Faculty'' was made available on Paramount's then-new streaming service [[Paramount+]] as one of its inaugural launch titles.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.newsweek.com/paramount-plus-line-tv-shows-films-movies-1573726|title=All the Shows and Movies Streaming Now on Paramount+|first=Samuel Spencer Newsweek Is A. ==Reception==
=== Box office ===
''The Faculty'' was viewed on 2,365 screens on its opening weekend, debuting at No. 5 in the US and making $11,633,495.<ref name=":0">{{cite news |last=Welkos |first=Robert W. |date=December 29, 1998 |title='Patch Adams' Just What Holiday Ordered |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-dec-29-ca-58424-story.html|access-date=June 7, 2012 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121015141803/https://articles.latimes.com/1998/dec/29/entertainment/ca-58424 |archive-date=October 15, 2012}}</ref> Its eventual US gross was $40.3 million.<ref name=mojo>{{cite Box Office Mojo|id= 0133751 |title=The Faculty|accessdate= June 25, 2021}}</ref> Its debut below commercial expectations has been partly credited to the studio's decision to release the movie on Christmas Day, a time when audiences are likelier to watch traditional dramas or feel-good fare.<ref name=":0" />

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''The Faculty'' holds an approval rating of 57% based on 65 reviews, with an average rating of 6.00/10. The site's critics consensus called the film a "Rip-off of other sci-fi thrillers."<ref name=RT>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/faculty/|title=The Faculty (1998)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date=July 23, 2026}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 61 out of 100 based on 19 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://metacritic.com/movie/the-faculty|title=The Faculty Reviews|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=April 22, 2018}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{Cite web|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/|title=Home|website=Cinemascore}}</ref>

Positive reviews at the time praised Kevin Williamson's self-aware script and trademark [[Self-referential humor|meta humor]] that included references to iconic [[Science fiction film|science-fiction]] films.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Faculty |url=https://www.tvguide.com/movies/the-faculty/2000036009/ |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[TVGuide.com]] |language=en}}</ref> In a review for ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', Dennis Harvey wrote, "''The Faculty'' works hard at mixing a canny cocktail of cineastic in-jokes, affectionate teenploitation and high-octane suspense that's as enjoyable as it is impossible to take seriously."<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Harvey |first=Dennis |date=December 28, 1998 |title=The Faculty |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/reviews/the-faculty-3-1200456169/ |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Harvey added that Williamson and Robert Rodriguez combine to "make a complete lack of socially redeeming value seem so much fun that '''The Faculty''<nowiki/>' might well become a pulp classic".<ref name=":1" /> Charles Taylor of ''[[Salon.com|Salon]]'' called the film "subversive" and said "its honest, good-natured junkiness… feels like a relief".<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Taylor |first=Charles |date=January 15, 1999 |title=The Faculty |url=https://www.salon.com/ent/movies/reviews/1999/01/15reviewb.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19991008002707/https://www.salon.com/ent/movies/reviews/1999/01/15reviewb.html |archive-date=October 8, 1999 |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[Salon.com]]}}</ref> He appreciated the film's homages to genre movies including ''[[Carrie (1976 film)|Carrie]]'' (1976), ''[[Invasion of the Body Snatchers (1978 film)|Invasion of the Body Snatchers]]'' (1978) and ''[[Blue Velvet (film)|Blue Velvet]]'' (1986) while also citing the character of Stokely as the standout amongst the teen archetypes.<ref name=":2" />

Michael Sauter of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave the film a grade of B− and expressed a wish that the film had used the faculty characters more.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sauter |first=Michael |date=June 18, 1999 |title=The Faculty |url=https://ew.com/article/1999/06/18/faculty-2/ |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[EW.com]] |language=en}}</ref> In contrast, Tom Sinclair, also of ''EW'', gave the film a C+ and said Williamson has become "too invested in the earnestness of teen angst to portray it in the scathing parodistic terms a hip horror movie demands".<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Sinclair |first=Tom |date=January 8, 1999 |title=The Faculty |magazine=Entertainment Weekly |url=http://ew.com/article/1999/01/08/faculty-3/|access-date=April 27, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170317034525/http://ew.com/article/1999/01/08/faculty-3/ |archive-date=March 17, 2017}}</ref> ''[[The New York Times]]'' gave a mostly negative review but praised the cast.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Van Gelder |first=Lawrence |author-link=Lawrence Van Gelder |date=December 25, 1998 |title='The Faculty': No Chance of Tenure |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/122598faculty-film-review.html |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> ''[[The Austin Chronicle]]'' awarded the film 3.5 stars and said, "While it may suffer a bit from excess character clutter (nearly 10 major characters throughout), it's nonetheless a slam-bang, sci-fi actioner, relentlessly paced and edited, with a pounding soundtrack and some ingenious aliens courtesy of [[Bernie Wrightson]] and KNB Effects."<ref>{{Cite web |date=December 25, 1998 |title=Movie Review: The Faculty |url=https://www.austinchronicle.com/events/film/1998-12-25/the-faculty/ |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[Austin Chronicle]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

===Retrospective===
In subsequent years, various critics have written about ''The Faculty''{{'s}} impact.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=September 10, 2012 |title=The Faculty |url=https://www.timeout.com/movies/the-faculty |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]] |language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=August 1, 2008 |title=The Faculty |url=http://boxoffice.com/reviews/2008/08/the-faculty.php|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100205165432/http://boxoffice.com/reviews/2008/08/the-faculty.php |archive-date=February 5, 2010 |access-date=April 27, 2022 |website=[[Boxoffice]]}}</ref> Aliya Whiteley wrote, "It's all about examining the tropes of science fiction with a smart, funny angle. Half of the fun in this film is in identifying where you first came across a certain character's name or saw a particular special effect. For instance, there's a brilliant moment with a head on legs that I defy you to watch and not think of a [[The Thing (1982 film)|certain John Carpenter film]]." Whiteley concluded, "''The Faculty'' is very definitely a big mess of a movie. But if you love all things sci-fi, it's a good mess."<ref name="den">{{cite web |author=Whiteley, Aliya |date=October 11, 2013 |title=Revisiting Robert Rodriguez's ''The Faculty'' |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/the-faculty/27623/revisiting-robert-rodriguezs-the-faculty |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012140155/http://www.denofgeek.com/movies/the-faculty/27623/revisiting-robert-rodriguezs-the-faculty |url-status=dead |archive-date=October 12, 2013 |access-date=April 21, 2014 |publisher=[[Dennis Publishing]]}}</ref> Keith Phipps described the film as "a Kevin Williamson-scripted high-school variation on ''Invasion of the Body Snatchers''."<ref>{{cite web |url= https://avclub.com/article/robert-rodriguez-13753 |last=Phipps |first=Keith |date= February 14, 2002 |title= Robert Rodriguez: Interview |website= [[The A.V. Club]] |access-date= April 21, 2014}}</ref> Haleigh Foutch considered it one of the most iconic 1990s teen horror films: "Yet another win from 90s teen screenwriter in chief Kevin Williamson, ''The Faculty'' fused Williamson's knack for snappy teen drama with Robert Rodriguez's subversive camp to fantastic results. It's smart without ever taking itself too seriously and campy without ever losing its cool, drawing proudly from the tradition of classic alien invasion movies and casting them in the 90s teen tradition."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://collider.com/90s-teen-horror-movies-ranked/#scream|title=The Most Iconic 90s Teen Horror Movies, Ranked|date=October 20, 2019|website=Collider}}</ref>

Director [[Ryan Coogler]] cited ''The Faculty'' as one of the inspirations for his 2025 film ''[[Sinners (2025 film)|Sinners]]''. Coogler said, "I love ''The Faculty'' from Rodriguez. I love how he mashed up ''Invasion of the Body Snatchers'' and ''The Thing'', but set it in a 1990s high school. I love the confusing mishmash of movie and setting."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |date=April 8, 2025 |title=Ryan Coogler On Why He Pushed Back ''Black Panther 3'' To Make Vampire Horror Pic ''Sinners'' With Michael B. Jordan |url=https://deadline.com/2025/04/ryan-coogler-interview-black-panther-3-michael-b-jordan-sinners-1236363447/ |access-date=April 14, 2025 |website=Deadline}}</ref>

===Cast and director's reception===
In a series of interviews, several ''The Faculty'' cast members reflected on their experiences with the film and its surprising journey to cult status. Robert Rodriguez, the director, shared that he had been drawn to ''The Faculty'' because of his love for genre-mixed films, even though he knew this would limit the movie's initial audience. He admitted that while the film did not perform as expected at the time, it had gained a strong following over the years: "I've never heard people talk so enthusiastically about ''The Faculty'' until the last couple of years." Rodriguez also recalled his casting process, highlighting how actors like Elijah Wood and Josh Hartnett stood out. He believed the film’s original title, ''The Faculty'', did not resonate with younger audiences, suggesting titles like ''Alienated'' or ''The Others'', which the studio later used for another movie.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/robert-rodriguez-interview-hypnotic-the-faculty/ | title=Director Robert Rodriguez on his films Hypnotic and the Faculty | date=May 30, 2023 }}</ref>

Writer Kevin Williamson said he initially passed on the script for the film but eventually agreed to the project after producer Bob Weinstein repeatedly encouraged him to reconsider. Williamson later said that he ultimately enjoyed working on the film and cited his admiration for director Robert Rodriguez.<ref>{{Cite web | title=Kevin Williamson Talks Scream 7, Remembers James Van Der Beek | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/kevin-williamson-scream-7-james-van-der-beek-interview-1236528175/ | access-date=2026-09-04 | website=www.hollywoodreporter.com}}</ref>

Jordana Brewster reflected on the contrast between ''The Faculty'' and ''The Fast and the Furious'', noting that they expected ''The Faculty'' to be a massive hit due to the success of teen movies, such as ''Scream'', at the time. She recalled thinking it would make them huge stars: “We all thought we were gonna be these huge teen stars... but it didn’t quite work out that way.” Despite the film’s initial underperformance, she is now proud of its cult status, which she finds "cool".<ref>{{Cite web|last=Nemiroff|first=Perri|date=June 23, 2021|title=Jordana Brewster on the Sky High Expectations ''Scream'' Set for ''The Faculty'' and What Happened When the Film Didn't Meet Them|url=https://collider.com/scream-the-faculty-jordana-brewster-interview/|access-date=July 13, 2021|website=Collider}}</ref>

Clea DuVall also discussed the film's initial reception, noting that it did not have the impact they expected during its release, despite the popularity of Kevin Williamson's work at the time. However, she has been pleased to see ''The Faculty''{{'}}s lasting influence: "It's cool to see the legs that movie has had." She acknowledged that people still recognize her as Stokely, the character she portrayed, which continues to be a huge part of her legacy: "I feel like that is the number one thing that people come up to me and say or recognize me from still." DuVall mentioned that while she does not fully understand why the film did not perform better initially, she believes that broader cultural conversations may be partly responsible for its rediscovery.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.them.us/story/clea-duvall-high-school-tegan-and-sara-interview | title=Clea DuVall Went Back to 'High School' and Found Compassion for Herself | date=October 11, 2022 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.vulture.com/2012/10/american-horror-story-clea-duvall-interview.html | title=Clea DuVall on Playing American Horror Story's Closeted School Teacher | date=October 25, 2012 }}</ref>

Elijah Wood described his experience on ''The Faculty'' as one of his most memorable. He said, "For all around experience, I would say ''The Faculty'' because the crew was so amazing. [Robert Rodriguez] was amazing to work with. There aren't enough adjectives to describe how cool it was working with him." He went on to emphasize the fun and unique atmosphere on set, stating, "It was so fun getting a chance to work with Robert, who had a very different approach than any other director that I'd worked with." Wood also highlighted the close-knit environment, noting, "There was a lot of love on the set. We're all really close, so it was cool."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://news.yahoo.com/elijah-wood-best-movies-roles-lord-rings-macaulay-culkin-jamie-lee-curtis-204324873.html | title=Role Recall: Elijah Wood on learning to curse from Jamie Lee Curtis, hanging from a cliff with Macaulay Culkin, and taking the role of a lifetime in 'The Lord of the Rings' | date=February 7, 2020 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=Quint interviews ELIJAH WOOD! | url=https://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/1203 }}</ref>

===Analysis===
The film has been praised for its portrayal of teenage [[Alienation from human society|alienation]], especially within the high school environment and its system of [[clique]]s and [[Role|social roles]].{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|p=175}}{{sfnp|Sherman|2014|p=132}} In particular, the alien invasion through parasitized bodies has been recognized as functioning as a metaphor for those concepts, reflecting the fear of losing one's nascent [[individuality]] to a crowd.{{sfnp|Sherman|2014|p=132}}{{sfnp|West|2018|pp=111–112}} The alien queen encapsulates this by offering the heroes a world without alienation or differences, in which "everything that is both wonderful and awful about being a teenager is done away in favor of blind allegiance".{{sfnp|West|2018|p=111}}

Experts also noted in ''The Faculty'' a reinvention of the fear of [[female sexuality]] often found in the [[horror genre]].{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|pp=173, 175}}{{sfnp|Ferrimond|2017}} The character of Marybeth, the alien queen, is "a complex imbrication of woman, alien and power" that acts beyond the role of [[femme fatale]].{{sfnp|Ferrimond|2017}} She masquerades as a virginal, unassuming girl clad in floral dresses, but at the final battle she reveals her true sexual threat, becoming nude by her alien transformation and turning confident and flirtatious.{{sfnp|Ferrimond|2017}} Her role as a threatening, [[Castration|castrating]] agent is underlined by the sharp teeth of her species, which evoke a [[vagina dentata]], and their association to water, the archaic, womb-like female element.{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|p=178}} As the [[Archaic mother|monstrous mother]] of her race, she tries to seduce the heroes by offering them a symbolic return to the womb.{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|p=183}} As Sharon Packer and Jody Pennington put it:

<blockquote>The image on the screen is dual: we see the beautiful, young, naked Marybeth strolling around looking for Casey, and the shadow of the monstrous form in the walls. Marybeth delivers a speech which ties the elements of the movie together. It is about the "world" she came from and its promises of "[[paradise]]" for lost and lonely humans, trapped in high-school "[[hell]]".{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|p=182}}</blockquote>

The character of Miss Burke precedes Marybeth in the same line, revealing her hidden sexuality only after being infected and turned into a monster. The scene of her detached, tentacled head in particular echoes the [[Sigmund Freud|Freudian]] [[Medusa's Head|Medusa head]].{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|pp=179–180}}{{sfnp|Ferrimond|2017}} The [[monstrous feminine]] is therefore used in the film to reflect the teenage characters entering adult world, where they are forced to "come to terms with female sexuality and overcome their fear of its 'monstrous' aspects in order to become fully functioning adults".{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington|2014|p=183}} Casey, the male character closest to his softer, feminine side, is the final hero of the film.{{sfnp|Packer|Pennington |2014|pp=178–179}}
