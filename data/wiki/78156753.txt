{{Nihongo|'''''Demon Pond'''''|夜叉ヶ池|Yasha-ga-ike|lead=yes}} is a 1979 Japanese [[romantic fantasy]] film directed by [[Masahiro Shinoda]]. It is an adaptation of the 1913 play of the same name by [[Kyōka Izumi|Izumi Kyōka]], with an adapted screenplay written by Haruhiko Mimura and Tsutomu Tamura. The film stars [[Tsutomu Yamazaki]], [[Gō Katō|Go Kato]], and [[Kabuki]] actor [[Bandō Tamasaburō V|Tamasaburo Bando]].  

The film was largely well received by critics in Japan, and has since been well regarded in retrospective reviews as well.  

== Plot ==
The story is set in Japan in the 1930s.<ref name=":2">{{Cite book |last=Desser |first=David |title=Eros plus massacre: an introduction to the Japanese new wave cinema |date=1988 |publisher=[[Indiana University Press]] |isbn=978-0-253-20469-1 |series=A Midland book |location=Bloomington |pages=190-192|url=https://archive.org/details/erosplusmassacre00davi/}}</ref> Wandering through the countryside, a schoolteacher, Gakuen Yamasawa, arrives at a village suffering from severe [[drought]]. There, he discovers his long-lost friend, Akira, who is now married to Yuri, a rumored sorceress whose fate is intertwined with a bell that must be rung each day to appease a nearby lake's [[dragon]] god—otherwise, an apocalyptic [[flood]] will be unleashed upon the region. Desperate for water, the foolish villagers plan to sacrifice Yuri to the dragon. Meanwhile, a host of pond spirits, led by the lovesick Princess Shirayuki, debate whether they should save the humans from their doom, or allow the princess to see her lover, Kengamine.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Demon Pond |url=https://www.janusfilms.com/films/2123 |access-date=October 19, 2024 |website=Janus Films |archive-date=September 18, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240918093217/https://www.janusfilms.com/films/2123 |url-status=live }}</ref> The villagers break into Yuri's home, intending to sacrifice her on the back of an [[ox]]. Akira and Gakuen return and try to convince the mob to spare Yuri, but the village leaders, including the [[National Diet|Diet Member]], refuse to listen. During the confrontation, Yuri commits suicide by stabbing herself with a [[sickle]]. Akira decides not to ring the bell and unleashes the flood, which destroys the village. He then commits suicide with the sickle. Gakuen survives by tying himself to a pillar of the belfry, while the corrupt village leaders drown in the flood. Princess Shirayuki, finally able to freely visit her lover, rises from the water with the other pond spirits and disappears into the sky.

== Production ==
In 1978, a popular stage production of the ''Demon Pond'' play was shown in Japan. This stirred interest in creating a film version of the play.{{Citation needed|date=March 2026}} Tamasaburo Bando, at the time a world-famous Kabuki ''[[Oyama (Japanese theatre)|onnagata]]'' actor, was brought on to star in the film playing the [[dual role]] of Yuri and Yuki.<ref name=":2" /> As this was Bando's film debut, he was not used to the idiosyncrasies of film, particularly, the use of [[Close-up|closeups]]. He would frequently delay production and ask for [[Take|retakes]] as he did not like the way his face appeared in closeup shots.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=Dudley Andrew |type=DVD |language=en |publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]] |year=2024 |time=00:08:44 - 00:09:53 |people=[[Dudley Andrew]]}}</ref> The technique of using an actor for dual roles was previously used in ''[[Double Suicide (1969 film)|Double Suicide]]'', and is used to imply a relationship between the two characters even if a direct relationship is not explicitly made in the narrative.<ref name=":2" /><ref>{{Cite news |last=Wu |first=Ash |date=2025-04-24 |title=Masahiro Shinoda, Leading Light of Japan’s New Wave Cinema, Dies at 94 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/04/24/movies/masahiro-shinoda-dead.html |access-date=2026-03-08 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> Additionally, both characters are played differently whereas the princess is bold and theatrical, while the young Yuki is played in a naturalistic fashion typical of cinema.<ref name=":3">{{Cite news |last=Maslin |first=Janet |date=1982-07-21 |title=TAMASABURO STARS IN JAPAN'S 'DEMON POND' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1982/07/21/movies/tamasaburo-stars-in-japan-s-demon-pond.html |access-date=2026-03-08 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref><ref name=":2" />

The film is based on a kabuki stage production, and follows some elements of the format such as a five act format.<ref name=":4" /> However, the film has the married couple embrace in a kiss which is atypical in kabuki but common in film.<ref name=":2" /> 

Masao Kosugi was the [[cinematographer]] for the film, and had previously worked with Shinoda on ''[[Assassination (1964 film)|The Assassin]]''.<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal |date=1981 |editor-last=Shoemaker |editor-first=Greg |title=Demon Pond |journal=Japanese Fantasy Film Journal |volume=13 |pages=6-7}}</ref> The film's score, composed by [[Isao Tomita]], heavily utilizes [[Moog synthesizer|Moog synthesizers]], mixing [[Classical music|classical]] and [[electronic music]] genres. Scenes taking place on dry land are often accompanied by Tomita's arrangement of pieces by composer [[Claude Debussy]], whereas underwater scenes are scored by Tomita's arrangement of [[Modest Mussorgsky]]'s ''[[Pictures at an Exhibition]].''<ref>{{Cite web |title=Demon Pond (1979) |url=https://www.criterion.com/films/33384-demon-pond |access-date=October 19, 2024 |website=The Criterion Collection |archive-date=November 17, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241117185907/https://www.criterion.com/films/33384-demon-pond |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media |title=Dudley Andrew |type=DVD |language=en |publisher=The Criterion Collection |year=2024 |time=00:16:00 |people=Dudley Andrew}}</ref>

Nobuo Yajima was hired as the special effects director for the film. He made extensive use of [[Compositing|composite shots]] in the film, and utilized an [[optical printer]] to combine different shots. Blue screens and miniature sets were also used, particularly during the scene where the village floods. A [[water column]] coming from the pond was created by pouring water into a basin on a solid background, which was composited onto footage of the pond via an optical printer. The footage of water pouring was reversed to give the illusion that the water was rising out of the pond. [[Miniature effect|Miniature]] sets were created of the village which were flooded with liters of water being poured on the sets. [[Matte (filmmaking)|Matte]] effects were also used in shots where villagers are seen running from the deluge.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=A Deluge of Special Effects |type=DVD |language=fr |publisher=The Criterion Collection |year=2024 |orig-date=2022 |time=00:05:40 - 00:11:00 |via=Carlotta Films and Allerton Films |people=Fabian Mauro}}</ref>

== Release ==
[[Shochiku]] released ''Demon Pond'' in Japanese theaters on October 20, 1979.<ref name=":0" /> Upon release, the film did well at the box office.<ref name=":1">{{Cite AV media |title=Dudley Andrew |type=DVD |language=en |publisher=The Criterion Collection |year=2024 |time=00:09:54 |people=Dudley Andrew}}</ref> [[Janus Films]] handled the American distribution of the film and it was screened at the 1985 Janus Films Festival.<ref>{{Cite news |date=1985-09-12 |title=Janus Films |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/la-weekly-janus-films/79719320/ |access-date=2026-03-09 |work=LA Weekly |pages=143}}</ref>

Aside from one television broadcast on [[TV Asahi]], the film was not re-released for 42 years. It would eventually be restored on 2021 to commemorate the 100th anniversary of Shochiku as a film distributor. Director Shinoda and actor Bando supervised a [[4K resolution|4K restoration]] of the film, which aired on the Shochiku-operated channel Satellite Theater in March of that year. It was re-released theatrically in the following summer.<ref>{{Cite web |date=January 28, 2021 |title=篠田正浩監督×坂東玉三郎主演「夜叉ヶ池」4Kデジタルリマスター版で42年ぶりによみがえる |trans-title="Yasha-ga-ike," directed by Masahiro Shinoda and starring Tamasaburo Bando, is revived for the first time in 42 years in a 4K digitally remastered version. |url=https://eiga.com/news/20210128/14/ |access-date=October 19, 2024 |website=eiga.com |language=Japanese}}</ref>

The restored version made its premiere at [[Museum of Modern Art|MoMA]] as the opener of its ''Beyond Ozu: Hidden Gems of Shochiku Studios'' series on June 10, 2022.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Yashagaike (Demon Pond). 1979. Masahiro Shinoda |url=https://www.moma.org/calendar/events/7891 |access-date=October 19, 2024 |website=MoMA}}</ref> The restored version was screened at the [[Cannes Film Festival]] in 2021.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-07-07 |title=Demon Pond (Yashagaike) by Masahiro Shinoda in restored version: tradition turned on its head |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2021/demon-pond-yashagaike-by-masahiro-shinoda-in-restored-version-tradition-turned-on-its-head/ |access-date=2026-03-08 |website=Festival de Cannes |language=en-US}}</ref> [[The Criterion Collection]] released the film on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD]] on October of 2024, its first ever home video release in North America.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Demon Pond (1979) |url=https://www.criterion.com/films/33384-demon-pond |access-date=October 19, 2024 |website=The Criterion Collection |archive-date=November 17, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241117185907/https://www.criterion.com/films/33384-demon-pond |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Reception ==
Upon release, reviews in Japan praised Tamasaburo Bando's performance.<ref name=":1" /> In 1980, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote a critical review of the film saying “Occasional great images and self-evident ambitiousness provoke a continued extension of credit and patience throughout most of this exceedingly bizarre fantasy, but it ultimately goes unrewarded."<ref name=":0" /> 

Janet Maslin of ''[[The New York Times]]'', praised the film saying "The film has a primitive style at times, especially where its special effects are involved, and it also occasionally works harder than necessary to startle the audience. Most of the time, Mr. Shinoda casts a spell as powerful and persuasive as Tamasaburo's own."<ref name=":3" />

Reviewers outside of Japan also praised Tamasaburo Bando's performance.<ref name=":0" /> ''Variety'' and ''New York times'' reviewers said that the gender of Bando was not obvious to them in the film.<ref name=":3" /><ref name=":0" />

Jacob Oller writing for the ''[[The A.V. Club|A.V. Club]]'' was positive about the film saying "''Demon'' ''Pond'' evokes classic Japanese psychedelia like ''House'', with Bando as its haunting, hallucinatory centerpiece."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Cult Of Criterion: Demon Pond |url=https://www.avclub.com/cult-of-criterion-demon-pond |access-date=2026-03-08 |website=AV Club |language=en-US|date=October 29, 2024|last=Oller|first=Jacob}}</ref> It was described by the critic [[Michael Atkinson (writer)|Michael Atkinson]] as “a jolt of delicious weirdness.”<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |title=Demon Pond: Here Comes the Flood |url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8624-demon-pond-here-comes-the-flood |access-date=2026-03-08 |website=The Criterion Collection |language=en |archive-date=2025-10-09 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251009170935/https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8624-demon-pond-here-comes-the-flood |url-status=live |last=Atkinson|first=Michael}}</ref> In 2024, [[David M. Edelstein|David Mermelstein]] writing in ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'' praised the film.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mermelstein |first=David |date=2024-10-30 |title=‘Demon Pond’: A Japanese Film’s Eerie Depths |url=https://www.wsj.com/opinion/demon-pond-japanese-film-criterion-collection-2bb15e1a |access-date=2026-03-08 |website=The Wall Street Journal |language=en-US}}</ref>
