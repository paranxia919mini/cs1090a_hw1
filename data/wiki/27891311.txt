'''''Moneyball''''' is a 2011 American [[biographical film|biographical]] [[sports drama]] film directed by [[Bennett Miller]] and adapted by [[Steven Zaillian]] and [[Aaron Sorkin]]. It stars [[Brad Pitt]], [[Jonah Hill]], [[Philip Seymour Hoffman]], [[Robin Wright]] and [[Chris Pratt]]. The film is based on the 2003 book ''[[Moneyball: The Art of Winning an Unfair Game]]'' by [[Michael Lewis]], an account of the [[Oakland Athletics]] [[baseball]] team's [[2002 Oakland Athletics season|2002 season]] and their general manager [[Billy Beane]]'s attempts to assemble a competitive team with half (or even a third) as much money as his rivals. ''Moneyball'' follows Beane and his deputy [[Paul DePodesta#Moneyball|Peter Brand]] as they scout the major leagues for undervalued talent by taking a sophisticated [[sabermetric]] approach to [[Scout (sport)|scouting]] and analyzing players, triggering skepticism and resentment within the baseball community.

[[Columbia Pictures]] bought the rights to Lewis's book in 2004, hiring Chervin to write the screenplay. [[David Frankel]] was initially set to direct with Zaillian now writing the screenplay, but was soon replaced by [[Steven Soderbergh]], who planned to make the film in a semi-documentary style featuring interviews from real athletes, and having the real players and coaches on the team portray themselves. But before its July 2009 filming start, the film was put in [[Turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]] due to creative differences between Soderbergh and Sony over a last-minute script rewrite. Soderbergh exited, and Miller was hired to direct, with Pitt becoming a producer and Sorkin hired for rewrites. Filming began in July 2010 at various stadiums such as [[Dodger Stadium]] and [[Oakland Coliseum]].

The film premiered at the [[2011 Toronto International Film Festival]] and was released on September 23, 2011, to box office success and critical acclaim, particularly for its acting and screenplay. The film received [[List of accolades received by Moneyball (film)|numerous accolades]], including six [[Academy Awards]] nominations including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]], [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]] for Pitt and [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]] for Hill.

==Plot==
<!-- Per MOS:FILMPLOT, the plot summary should be 400-700 words. -->
The [[Oakland Athletics]] of [[Major League Baseball]] have [[History of the Oakland Athletics#Moneyball|difficulty fielding competitive teams]] due to low revenue and owners who are reluctant to spend money. [[General manager (baseball)|General manager]] [[Billy Beane]] drafts and develops cheap, young, and talented players,{{Efn|In Major League Baseball, players do not become [[Free agency (Major League Baseball)|free agents]] (that is, they cannot auction off their services to the highest bidder) for the first six years of their Major League careers.}} but the Athletics lose the [[2001 American League Division Series]] (ALDS) to the [[New York Yankees]], baseball's richest and most successful team.{{Efn|In 2001, the Yankees' payroll was $112.8 million, over three times larger than the Athletics' payroll of $33.8 million. The Yankees had the largest payroll in baseball, while the Athletics had the second-smallest.<ref>{{Cite web |title=2001 Major League Baseball Attendance & Team Age |url=https://www.baseball-reference.com/leagues/majors/2001-misc.shtml |access-date=2024-10-31 |website=Baseball-Reference.com |language=en}}</ref>}} For the [[2002 Oakland Athletics season|2002 season]], Beane is given a paltry $41 million budget.{{Efn|In 2002, the Yankees once again spent three times as much money as the Athletics.<ref>{{Cite web |title=2002 Major League Baseball Attendance & Team Age |url=https://www.baseball-reference.com/leagues/majors/2002-misc.shtml |access-date=2024-10-31 |website=Baseball-Reference.com |language=en}}</ref>}} Through [[Free agency (Major League Baseball)|free agency]], three richer teams poach three of Beane's best players: [[Jason Giambi]], [[Johnny Damon]], and [[Jason Isringhausen]]. Adding insult to injury, Giambi joins the Yankees.

Beane is skeptical about traditional baseball scouting methods after the [[New York Mets]] drafted him in the first round of the [[1980 Major League Baseball draft|1980 draft]]—prompting Beane to decline a [[Stanford Cardinal baseball|Stanford]] scholarship—only for Beane to have an unimpressive playing career. Beane tries to trade for the [[Cleveland Indians]]' [[Karim García]], but Cleveland refuses on the advice of team advisor Peter Brand, a [[Yale]] economics graduate who privately complains to Beane that Cleveland rarely takes his advice, and expresses a belief that baseball teams focus too much on individual players to have success. Intrigued, Beane asks whether Brand would have drafted him in 1980. After Brand reluctantly admits that he would not have drafted Beane until the ninth round, Beane hires Brand.

Beane and Brand study [[sabermetrics]], an unconventional scouting philosophy. Unable to afford more talented, expensive players, Beane and Brand focus on maximizing the team's [[on-base percentage]] (OBP) and compromise on skills like [[Stolen base|base stealing]], defense, and [[Batting average (baseball)|batting average]]. They acquire undervalued players like aging [[David Justice]], injured catcher [[Scott Hatteberg]], and [[Submarine (baseball)|submariner]] [[Chad Bradford]]. Beane fires head scout [[Grady Fuson]], who refuses to abandon his traditional scouting methods.

A poor start to the season prompts the media and the team to question Beane's philosophy. Manager [[Art Howe]], who is angling for a contract extension, disregards Brand's advice to put the players with the best OBP at the top of the [[Batting order (baseball)|batting order]]. Howe resists playing Hatteberg at first base, so Beane forces Hatteberg into the lineup by trading away Howe's favored first baseman [[Carlos Peña]]. Although [[Jeremy Giambi]] has good on-base skills, Beane decides that Giambi lacks the intangible qualities to succeed and trades him as well.{{efn|In his review of the film, critic [[Alan Sepinwall]] remarked that "I've been around the more analytically-minded corners of Sports Internet for so long that I remember the absolute *meltdown* that happened online when the Jeremy Giambi/John Mabry trade happened. I just looked up the Baseball Prime comments from that day. A highly representative quote: 'So much for Billy Beane, Genius.'"<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sepinwall |first=Alan |date=2023-07-16 |title=A ★★★★ review of Moneyball (2011) |url=https://letterboxd.com/alan_sepinwall/film/moneyball/ |access-date=2024-11-01 |website=letterboxd.com |language=en}}</ref> In 2012, Chris Jaffe remarked in ''[[The Hardball Times]]'' that "I don’t think any such trade could inflict such psychological shockwaves on that stat community as this one did. I just don’t think it’s possible. ... Now, stats vs. scouts has become more stats and scouts."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jaffe |first=Chris |date=2012-05-22 |title=10th anniversary: Giambi-Mabry trade |url=https://tht.fangraphs.com/tht-live/10th-anniversary-giambi-mabry-trade/ |access-date=2024-11-01 |website=The Hardball Times}}</ref>}} Beane persuades team owner [[Stephen Schott]] to trust in the plan. With Cleveland performing poorly, Beane devises a trade for the Indians' star reliever [[Ricardo Rincón]].

The Athletics' performance improves, placing them on the verge of an [[American League|AL]]-record-breaking [[List of Major League Baseball longest winning streaks|20th consecutive win]]. Although Beane rarely attends games, his daughter Casey persuades him to attend the next game against the [[Kansas City Royals]]. Oakland leads 11–0 when Beane arrives, but the Royals mount a furious comeback and tie the game. Hatteberg hits a [[walk-off home run]] to the Oakland fans' delight. Despite the celebration, Beane tells Brand he will not be satisfied until they have changed baseball by winning the [[World Series]].

The Athletics are the 2002 [[American League West]] champions but lose to the [[Minnesota Twins]] in the first round of [[2002 ALDS|division playoffs]]. A media analyst asserts that the Athletics lost because they lacked intangible qualities that cannot be measured with statistics. Later, [[Boston Red Sox]] owner [[John W. Henry]] offers Beane the largest contract for a general manager in history to take over the Red Sox organization. Beane discloses Henry's offer to Brand and says that their strategy failed. Brand shows Beane a video of batter [[Jeremy Brown]], who hits a home run, but does not realize it. Sensing the meaning of the video and what Brand is trying to say, Beane thanks Brand. Beane drives while listening to a burned [[CD]] of Casey singing "[[The Show (Lenka song)|The Show]]", prompting him to cry.

An epilogue reveals that Beane turned down the $12.5 million offer by the Red Sox, who used sabermetrics to win the [[2004 World Series]], while Beane has yet to win a World Series.

===Development and pre-production===
[[File:Aaron Sorkin (27566400913).jpg|thumb|upright|Sony Chairwoman [[Amy Pascal]] asked [[Aaron Sorkin]] (pictured) to rewrite the screenplay. He agreed on the condition the initial screenwriter [[Steven Zaillian]] gave his blessing. Sorkin and Zaillian shared screenplay credit.]]
In May 2004, [[Sony Pictures]] acquired the rights to the [[Michael Lewis]] book, and had hired [[Stan Chervin]] to write the screenplay.<ref name="movieweb">{{cite web |last1=B. |first1=Brian |title=Sony acquires Moneyball |date=May 5, 2004 |url=https://movieweb.com/sony-acquires-moneyball/ |work=[[MovieWeb]] |access-date=4 July 2020}}</ref> By October 2008, [[Brad Pitt]] was being courted to star in the film, now being written by [[Steven Zaillian]], and [[David Frankel]] was attached to direct.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Siegel |first1=Tatiana |title=Columbia pitches 'Moneyball' to Pitt |date=October 17, 2008 |url=https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/columbia-pitches-moneyball-to-pitt-1117994175/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=4 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170723223209/http://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/columbia-pitches-moneyball-to-pitt-1117994175/ |archive-date=July 23, 2017 |url-status=live}}</ref> Frankel and Pitt met with one another during the week of the [[66th Golden Globe Awards]] to discuss the project, but eventually Frankel would exit by February 2009, with [[Steven Soderbergh]] entering negotiations to direct.<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Sperling |first1=Nicole |title=Brad Pitt and Steven Soderbergh to team up on 'Moneyball' movie? |url=https://ew.com/article/2009/02/05/moneyball-3/ |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |access-date=4 July 2020 |date=February 5, 2009 |archive-date=September 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200904184905/https://ew.com/article/2009/02/05/moneyball-3/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Soderbergh confirmed his involvement in May that year, and in talking about the film, stated "I think we have a way in, making it visual and making it funny. I want it to be really funny and entertaining, and I want you to not realize how much information is being thrown at you because you're having fun. We've found a couple of ideas on how to bust the form a bit, in order for all that information to reach you in a way that's a little oblique".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://suicidegirls.com/interviews/Steven%20Soderbergh%3A%20The%20Girlfriend%20Experience |title=Steven Soderbergh: The Girlfriend Experience |website=[[SuicideGirls.com]] |date=21 May 2009 |access-date=May 21, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090525093205/http://suicidegirls.com/interviews/Steven%20Soderbergh:%20The%20Girlfriend%20Experience/ |archive-date=May 25, 2009 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Demetri Martin]] was cast to play [[Paul DePodesta]] in the film, with former Athletics players [[Scott Hatteberg]] and [[David Justice]] playing themselves, and interview segments featuring players [[Darryl Strawberry]] and [[Lenny Dykstra]] set to occur.<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Fleming |first1=Michael |title=Demetri Martin swings at 'Moneyball' |date=April 21, 2009 |url=https://variety.com/2009/film/markets-festivals/demetri-martin-swings-at-moneyball-1118002626/ |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=4 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150927214928/http://variety.com/2009/film/markets-festivals/demetri-martin-swings-at-moneyball-1118002626/ |archive-date=September 27, 2015 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Production under Soderbergh was set to begin in July 2009, and was to be shot on location at [[Oakland Coliseum]]. [[Art Howe]], former manager of the team, was also set to appear as himself.<ref>{{cite news |title=A's notebook: Lights, camera, action … for 'Moneyball |date=May 26, 2009 |url=https://www.eastbaytimes.com/2009/05/26/as-notebook-lights-camera-action-for-moneyball/ |work=[[East Bay Times]] |access-date=4 July 2020 |archive-date=September 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200904184921/https://www.eastbaytimes.com/2009/05/26/as-notebook-lights-camera-action-for-moneyball/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Five days before its July 8 filming start date, Sony cancelled the film and entered it into "limited [[Turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]]". The cited reason for the cancellation was that, upon a last-minute script revision by Soderbergh that added "an abundance of baseball details", studio executives felt the audience would feel alienated. It was also stated that the studio now felt the film was too "arty" for its $58 million budget. Soderbergh was said to be unwilling to compromise, leaving Sony chairwoman [[Amy Pascal]] "apoplectic". [[Paramount Pictures]] and [[Warner Bros.]] both turned down offers to pick up the project.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Brooks |first1=Xan |title=Soderbergh's Moneyball mothballed |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/jul/03/steven-soderbergh-brad-pitt-moneyball-mothballed |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=July 3, 2009 |access-date=4 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190107123213/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/jul/03/steven-soderbergh-brad-pitt-moneyball-mothballed |archive-date=January 7, 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref> Soderbergh revealed he exited the film in a September 2009 interview with ''[[The Orlando Sentinel]]'', saying, "There have been a couple of times in my career where I've been unceremoniously removed from projects. I don't waste a lot of energy on it. It doesn't get you anywhere. As soon as it became clear that there was no iteration of that movie that I was going to get to direct, I immediately started looking around for something else to do".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Fischer |first1=Russ |title=Soderbergh Finally Talks About Moneyball |date=September 2, 2009 |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/soderbergh-finally-talks-about-moneyball/ |access-date=4 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200705015113/https://www.slashfilm.com/soderbergh-finally-talks-about-moneyball/ |archive-date=July 5, 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref>

== Accuracy ==
David Haglund of ''[[Slate Magazine|Slate]]'' and [[Jonah Keri]] of ''[[Grantland]]'' criticized the film and book for excluding pitchers [[Tim Hudson]], [[Mark Mulder]], and [[Barry Zito]] and position players such as [[Eric Chavez]] and [[Miguel Tejada]]—all discovered via traditional scouting methods, and key contributors to the success of the 2002 Athletics.<ref>{{cite magazine |first=David |last=Haglund |url=http://www.slate.com/articles/sports/sports_nut/2011/09/more_moneyball_same_problems.html |date=2011-09-21 |title=More Moneyball, Same Problems |magazine=[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]] |access-date=2017-05-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170503025408/http://www.slate.com/articles/sports/sports_nut/2011/09/more_moneyball_same_problems.html |archive-date=May 3, 2017 |url-status=live}}</ref> The ''San Francisco Chronicle'' noted several inaccuracies in the film, including that players such as Giambi and Bradford had already joined the team before the time depicted in the film, and that the Giambi and Peña trades did not occur at the same time.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Slusser |first1=Susan |title='Moneyball' movie plays games with the truth |date=September 25, 2011 |url=https://www.sfgate.com/athletics/article/Moneyball-movie-plays-games-with-the-truth-2308642.php |newspaper=[[San Francisco Chronicle]] |access-date=26 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180123031958/http://www.sfgate.com/athletics/article/Moneyball-movie-plays-games-with-the-truth-2308642.php |archive-date=January 23, 2018 |url-status=live|author-link=Susan Slusser}}</ref>

Former Oakland A's manager Art Howe has spoken publicly about his disapproval of his portrayal in an interview on ''[[SiriusXM]]''. "It is very disappointing to know that you spent seven years in an organization and gave your heart and soul to it and helped them go to the postseason your last three years there and win over 100 games your last two seasons and this is the way evidently your boss [Beane] feels about you." Howe also said producers of Miller's version of the film didn't contact him to consult on his portrayal.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.foxsports.com/stories/mlb/howe-upset-with-moneyball-portrayal |title=Howe upset with 'Moneyball' portrayal |work=[[Fox Sports]] |date=September 27, 2011 |access-date=November 16, 2012}}</ref> Hatteberg also said that Howe was portrayed inaccurately, saying: "Art Howe was a huge supporter of mine. I never got the impression from him that I was not his first choice." He mentioned Howe and Beane had a "turbulent relationship".<ref>{{cite web |first=Mike |last=Ryan |url=http://blog.moviefone.com/2011/09/21/scott-hatteberg-moneyball-interview-facts-fiction/ |title=Real Life 'Moneyball' Major Leaguer Scott Hatteberg on the Facts and Fiction of the New Film |website=[[Moviefone]] |date=September 21, 2011 |access-date=November 18, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130601123614/http://blog.moviefone.com/2011/09/21/scott-hatteberg-moneyball-interview-facts-fiction |archive-date=June 1, 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Moneyball'' grossed $75.6&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada and $34.6&nbsp;million in other territories for a worldwide total of $110.2&nbsp;million, against a production budget of $50&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /> The film grossed $19.5&nbsp;million from 2,993 theaters in its opening weekend, finishing second at the box office behind the 3D re-release of ''[[The Lion King]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?yr=2011&wknd=38&p=.htm|title=Weekend Box Office: September 23–25, 2011 |work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=February 16, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170217063204/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?yr=2011&wknd=38&p=.htm|archive-date=February 17, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> In its second weekend it grossed $12&nbsp;million (a drop of only 38.3%), again finishing second.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?view=&yr=2011&wknd=39&p=.htm|title=Weekend Box Office: September 30 – October 1, 2011 |work=Box Office Mojo
|access-date=February 16, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170217062939/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?view=&yr=2011&wknd=39&p=.htm|archive-date=February 17, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Moneyball'' holds an approval rating of 94% based on 265 reviews, with an average rating of 8.00/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "Director Bennett Miller, along with Brad Pitt and Jonah Hill, take a niche subject and turn it into a sharp, funny, and touching portrait worthy of baseball lore."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/moneyball |title=Moneyball |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]] |access-date=January 8, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181213204655/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/moneyball |archive-date=December 13, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 87 out of 100, based on 42 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/moneyball |title=Moneyball |work=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=July 7, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120626072406/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/moneyball |archive-date=June 26, 2012 |url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cinemascore.com |title=Find CinemaScore |format=Type "Moneyball" in the search box |website=[[CinemaScore]] |access-date=March 2, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180102130540/https://www.cinemascore.com/ |archive-date=January 2, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref> The film appeared on 35 critics' top-ten lists for the best films of 2011, with two critics ranking it first and another ranking it second.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/feature/movie-critic-best-of-2011-top-ten-lists |title=2011 Film Critic Top Ten Lists [Updated Dec. 22] |first=Jason |last=Dietz |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |date=December 8, 2011 |access-date=December 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120626084253/http://www.metacritic.com/feature/movie-critic-best-of-2011-top-ten-lists |archive-date=June 26, 2012 |url-status=live}}</ref>
