'''''The Secret of NIMH''''' is a 1982 American [[independent film|independent]] animated [[Fantasy film|fantasy]] [[adventure film]] directed by [[Don Bluth]] in his directorial debut and based on [[Robert C. O'Brien (author)|Robert C. O'Brien]]'s [[children's novel]] ''[[Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH]]''. Featuring an [[ensemble cast]] consisting of [[Elizabeth Hartman]] in her final film role as its protagonist, [[Mrs. Brisby]], with [[Peter Strauss]], [[Arthur Malet]], [[Dom DeLuise]], [[John Carradine]], [[Derek Jacobi]], [[Paul Shenar]] and [[Hermione Baddeley]] in supporting roles, the film was produced by Bluth's production company, [[Don Bluth Productions]], in association with [[Aurora Productions, Hollywood|Aurora Productions]].

''The Secret of NIMH'' was released in the United States on July 2, 1982, by [[MGM/UA Entertainment Co.]] under the [[United Artists]] label. It was met with critical acclaim and won a [[Saturn Award]] for Best Animated Film of 1982. Though the film only made $14.7 million against a $7 million budget at the box office, it turned a solid profit through home video and overseas releases. It was followed in 1998 by a [[direct-to-video]] sequel, ''[[The Secret of NIMH 2: Timmy to the Rescue]]'', which was made without Bluth's involvement.

In 2015, a live-action/animated remake was reported to be in the works.<ref name="ignreboot">{{cite web |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2015/03/04/live-actioncg-rats-of-nimh-movie-in-the-works |title=Live-Action/CG Rats of NIMH Movie in the Works |website=IGN |author=Nicholson, Max |date=March 4, 2015 |access-date=October 19, 2015 |archive-date=October 15, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151015074326/http://www.ign.com/articles/2015/03/04/live-actioncg-rats-of-nimh-movie-in-the-works |url-status=live}}</ref> A television series adaptation was also revealed to be in development since 2021.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://deadline.com/2021/09/rats-of-nimh-animated-series-in-the-works-fox-1234828638/|title=Animated Series Based On Robert C. O'Brien's 'NIMH' In Works At Fox As Network Steps Up Adult Cartoon Drive|website=Deadline Hollywood|first=Peter|last=White|date=September 8, 2021}}</ref>

==Plot==
[[Mrs. Brisby]],{{Efn|Changed from "Mrs. Frisby" in the original novel, as explained in [[#Writing|"Writing"]] section.|name=Brisby}} a widowed field mouse, lives in a cinder block with her children on a farm owned by the Fitzgibbons family. She intends to move her family out of the field as plowing time approaches, but her son Timothy has fallen ill, making it impossible for him to travel.

Brisby visits Mr. Ages, a friend of her late husband Jonathan, who diagnoses the illness as [[pneumonia]], provides her with medicine, and warns that Timothy must stay inside for at least three weeks to recover. On her way home, Brisby befriends Jeremy, a clumsy but amiable crow, shortly before they narrowly escape the Fitzgibbons' cat, Dragon. The next morning, she discovers that the plowing has begun early. Although her neighbor, Auntie Shrew, helps disable the tractor used for the task, Brisby realizes she needs a better plan. Jeremy then takes her to meet the Great Owl, who at first rejects her, but upon learning she is Jonathan Brisby's widow, tells her to visit a colony of rats living beneath a rose bush on the farm and seek out Nicodemus, their wise and mystical leader.

Brisby enters the rose bush and encounters an aggressive guard rat named Brutus, who chases her away. She is led back in by Ages and is amazed to see the rats' use of electricity and other technology. Brisby meets Justin, the friendly [[captain of the guard]]; Jenner, a ruthless and power-hungry member opposed to Nicodemus; and finally Nicodemus himself. From Nicodemus, she learns that many years ago the rats, along with her husband and Ages, were part of a series of experiments at the [[National Institute of Mental Health]] (NIMH for short). The experiments gave them human-like intelligence, enabling them to escape, as well as extending their lifespans. They are unable to live as typical rats would, however, and need human technology to survive, which they can get only by stealing, placing them at great risk of discovery. To avoid being at the mercy of humankind again, the rats have collectively decided to leave the farm and live independently in an area they refer to as Thorn Valley. Nicodemus then gives Brisby a magical [[amulet]] that will activate when the wearer is courageous. Meanwhile, Jenner, who wishes for the rats to remain in the rose bush, plots to eliminate Nicodemus.

Out of respect for Jonathan, the rats agree to help her move her home. First, they need to drug Dragon so it can be done without interference. Only Brisby can do this, as the rats cannot fit through the hole leading into the house; Jonathan was killed by Dragon in a previous attempt, while Ages broke his leg in another. That night, she puts the drug into Dragon's dish, but the Fitzgibbons' son catches her. While trapped in a birdcage, she overhears a telephone conversation between the Fitzgibbonses' patriarch and the staff of NIMH and learns that the institute intends to exterminate the rats in the morning. Brisby then escapes from the cage and runs off to warn them.

As a thunderstorm approaches, the rats begin moving the Brisby home, with the children and Auntie Shrew inside, using a rope and pulley system. Jenner sabotages the assembly, causing it to fall apart and crush Nicodemus to death. Brisby soon arrives to warn the rats about NIMH's arrival, but Jenner attacks her and attempts to steal the amulet as his reluctant accomplice, Sullivan, alerts Justin, who comes to Brisby's aid. Jenner mortally wounds Sullivan and engages Justin in a sword fight, but is killed by a knife in the back from the dying Sullivan.

The Brisby home begins to sink into the muddy ground and Brisby and the rats are unable to raise it. All appears lost until Brisby's will to save her family suddenly gives power to the amulet, which she uses to lift the house and move it to safety. The next day, the rats, with Justin as their new leader, have departed for Thorn Valley as Timothy begins to recover, while Jeremy meets and falls in love with an equally clumsy female crow.

==Production==
===Background===
The film rights to the book ''[[Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH]]'' had reportedly been offered to [[Walt Disney Animation Studios|Walt Disney Productions]] in 1972, but they were turned down.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=41}}

''The Secret of NIMH'' was the first [[feature film]] to be [[Film director|directed]] by [[Don Bluth]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2022-08-21/don-bluth-career-secret-of-nimh-anastasia-disney | title=Don Bluth made animation history by leaving Disney. Looking back, he has no regrets | website=[[Los Angeles Times]] | date=August 21, 2022 }}</ref> On September 13, 1979, Bluth, fellow animators [[Gary Goldman]] and [[John Pomeroy]] and eight other animation staff left the feature animation department at Disney to set up their own independent studio, [[Don Bluth Productions]]. The studio worked, at first, out of Bluth's house and garage, but moved to a two-story, {{convert|5500|sqft|adj=on}} facility in [[Studio City, California]], several months later.<ref name="beck">{{cite book |last=Beck |first=Jerry |title=The Animated Movie Guide |url=https://archive.org/details/animatedmoviegui0000beck |url-access=registration |year=2005 |publisher=Chicago Review Press |isbn=1-55652-591-5 |pages=[https://archive.org/details/animatedmoviegui0000beck/page/243 243–4]}}</ref>

While they were still working at Disney, they produced the 27-minute short film ''[[Banjo the Woodpile Cat]]'' (1979) as a side project to gain other production skills that the company and their animation program were not addressing. Bluth asked [[Ron W. Miller]], [[Walt Disney]]'s son-in-law and the president and CEO of the company at the time, to view ''Banjo'', but Miller declined. As Goldman recalled, "that pulled the enthusiasm rug out from under us. We had hoped that the studio might like what we were doing and agree to buy the film and allow us to finish the short film in the studio, which would allow us to recoup what we had spent in terms of money and the many hours that we and the other members of the team had invested in the film".<ref name="Traditional Animation" />

Before they started making ''Banjo'', ''Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH'' was read by artist and story writer [[Ken Anderson (animator)|Ken Anderson]], who called it "a wonderful story".<ref name="Traditional Animation">{{cite web|url=https://www.traditionalanimation.com/2012/30th-anniversary-of-the-secret-of-nimh-gary-goldman-exclusive/|title=30th Anniversary of Secret of NIMH – Gary Goldman Exclusive!|last=Carpenter|first=Les|date=July 2, 2012|publisher=Traditional Animation|access-date=January 1, 2016|archive-date=January 12, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160112025631/http://www.traditionalanimation.com/2012/30th-anniversary-of-the-secret-of-nimh-gary-goldman-exclusive/|url-status=live}}</ref> He gave the book to Bluth for him to read and make a film out of after Bluth finished the animation direction of ''[[Pete's Dragon (1977 film)|Pete's Dragon]]'' (1977). Bluth later showed the novel to Disney animation director [[Wolfgang Reitherman]], who turned down Bluth's offers to make a film based on the book, explaining that Disney has already a mouse named [[Mickey Mouse]] and they had recently made a similar film about mice named ''[[The Rescuers]]'' (1977). However, Bluth also presented the novel to the other staff that would work for Don Bluth Productions later on and they all loved it. Two months later, former Disney executive James L. Stewart, who now had started [[Aurora Productions, Hollywood|Aurora Productions]], called Goldman and told him about Anderson's idea of making a film based on ''NIMH''.<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> At Bluth, Goldman and Pomeroy's request, Aurora Productions acquired the film rights and offered Don Bluth Productions a budget of US$5.7 million and 30 months to complete the film, tighter in both budget and schedule than most Disney animated features at the time.<ref name="counts" />

===Writing===
{{Quote box|width=275px|align=right|quote="The amulet was a device, or symbol, to represent the internal power of Mrs. Brisby.&nbsp;... In many ways, it was an extension of Mrs. Brisby&nbsp;... a visual extension of an internal (and harder to show in a film) power".|source= —Don Bluth, explaining the amulet's inclusion in the film{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=46}}}}
One of the earliest drafts of the film was written by [[Steven Barnes]], who received a creative consultant credit in the final product and was closer to the original novel.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=43}} The story would have focused more on the rats and their time at NIMH as it did in the book, which was reduced to a short [[Flashback (narrative)|flashback]] in later revisions to bring Mrs. Brisby and her plight into the forefront. It also included (from the book) a female rat named Isabella (described as "a young, cute, somewhat motor-mouthed rat with a crush on Justin"), who was ultimately left out, with much of her dialogue given to Nicodemus. A revised synopsis dated July 2, 1980, by an unattributed author would take the film closer to its completed form, which ended with the mysterious disappearance of the rats, leading the characters and audience to wonder if they ever really existed, or were just an elaborate illusion.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=44}}

Bluth himself would later make several changes to the story, most notably with the addition of mystical elements not present in the original novel.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|pages=44–46}} He explained that "regarding magic, we really believe that animation calls for some magic, to give it a special 'fantastic' quality".<ref name="remember3">{{cite web |url=https://www.adammcdaniel.com/DonBluth/Don_Bluth_Interview3.htm |author=McDaniel, Adam |title=About the Script |publisher=Remembering NIMH: An Interview with Don Bluth |access-date=October 31, 2015 |archive-date=January 12, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160112003703/http://adammcdaniel.com/DonBluth/Don_Bluth_Interview3.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> This was most apparent in the magic [[amulet]] given to Mrs. Brisby, which was meant to be a visual representation of her character's internal power, something harder to show on film. The object was also meant to introduce a [[Spirituality|spiritual]] aspect to the plot, with the director remarking: "The stone or amulet is just a method of letting the audience know that Mrs. Brisby has found 'Courage of the Heart'. Magic? Maybe. Spiritual? Yes".<ref name="remember3" /> In the same vein, Nicodemus was made into a [[Magician (fantasy)|wizard]] to "create more mystery" about himself and the rats' colony.

Among other changes, the antagonist Jenner was given much more prominence in the film, being only mentioned in the book as a traitor who leaves, to "add drama" to the narrative by giving it a more visible enemy. In the movie, Justin succeeds Nicodemus as the leader of the rats to give his character more of an [[Character arc|arc]] and to allow him an opportunity to "grow and change".{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=46}} Unlike the original work, Justin does not rescue Mrs. Brisby from the cage at the Fitzgibbons' house; instead, she helps her children without the rats' assistance by using the amulet, once again giving focus to her personal story. As Bluth put it, "''The Secret of NIMH'' is really a story about Mrs. Brisby and her need to save her children. If the rats save her children, then she hasn't grown in the film".{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=46}}

During the film's production, Aurora contacted [[Wham-O]], the manufacturers of [[Frisbee]] flying discs, with concerns about possible trademark infringements if the "Mrs. Frisby" name in O'Brien's original book was used in the film. Wham-O rejected Aurora's request for waiver to use the same-sounding name to their "Frisbee", in the film. Aurora informed Bluth & company that Mrs. Frisby's name would have to be altered. By then, the voice work had already been recorded for the film, so the name change to "Mrs. Brisby" necessitated a combination of re-recording some lines and, because John Carradine was unavailable for further recordings, careful sound editing had to be performed, taking the "B" sound of another word from Carradine's recorded lines, and replace the "F" sound with the "B" sound, altering the name from "Frisby" to "Brisby".{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=50}}

===Casting===
Goldman described the casting process as "exciting, fun, and sometimes strange".<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> He stated that focusing on the characteristics of each character, the voices and acting abilities were crucial, saying that using voices that added to a film's texture was part of the team's philosophy in the development of a film. Goldman found the most memorable casting decision to be [[Dom DeLuise]] for the character of Jeremy, which Goldman, Bluth and Pomeroy all considered when they watched the 1978 film ''[[The End (1978 film)|The End]]'' in their respective homes, independently of each other.<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> [[Elizabeth Hartman]] was cast as Mrs. Brisby, with Goldman calling her performance in ''[[A Patch of Blue]]'' "so believable and sincere that we all felt that she was right for the part". Pomeroy suggested [[Derek Jacobi]], who starred in the 1976 miniseries ''[[I, Claudius (TV series)|I, Claudius]]'', to play the part of Nicodemus.<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> [[Peter Strauss]], whom the team previously saw in another miniseries from 1976, ''[[Rich Man, Poor Man (miniseries)|Rich Man, Poor Man]]'', was cast as Justin.<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> [[Paul Shenar]] was assigned to play Jenner since the staff liked his "dark, powerful voice".<ref name="Traditional Animation" /> Shakespearean actor [[John Carradine]] was "perfect for the dark, ominous Great Owl", while [[Aldo Ray]] was assigned to voice Jenner's reluctant accomplice, Sullivan, whom Goldman said "also had a great distinctive voice".<ref name="Traditional Animation" />

===Animation===
[[File:TheSecretofNIMHscreen.jpg|thumb|Mrs. Brisby meeting Nicodemus. Backlighting techniques were used in this scene to give Nicodemus's eyes a bright glow. According to the commentary for the 2007 DVD release, Bluth wanted Nicodemus and the Great Owl to be seen as aspects of the same character, accounting for some similarities in their designs.]]
The production of ''The Secret of NIMH'' lasted from January 1980 to early June 1982.<ref name="Presskit">{{cite web |url=http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/press1.html |title=The Secret of NIMH: Press Information |date=June 3, 1982 |access-date=January 1, 2016 |via=Vanden-Eykel.com |archive-date=February 6, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120206010939/http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/press1.html |url-status=live}}</ref> The studio set out with the explicit goal in mind of returning feature animation to its "[[Golden age of American animation|golden era]]", concentrating on strong characters and story and experimenting with unusual and often more labor-intensive animation techniques.<ref name="counts">{{cite news |last=Counts |first=Kyle |date=February 1982 |title=Coming: The Secret of NIMH |work=[[Cinefantastique]] |url=http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/cfeb822.htm |access-date=October 28, 2015 |archive-date=March 3, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303234032/http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/cfeb822.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> Bluth believed that older techniques were being abandoned in favor of lower production costs and that the only way that animation could survive was to continue [[Traditional animation|traditional]] production methods.

Among the techniques experimented with on ''The Secret of NIMH'' were [[rotoscoping]], multiple passes on the camera to achieve transparent shadows, backlit animation (where animated [[matte (filmmaking)|mattes]] are shot with light shining through [[color gel]]s to produce glowing areas for artificial light and fire effects), and multiple color palettes for characters to fit in different lighting situations, from daylight, to night, to warm environments, to underwater. Mrs. Brisby had 46 different lighting situations; therefore there were 46 different color palettes, or lists of color, for her. Two modern, computerized versions of the [[multiplane camera]] were also manufactured for this production.<ref name="mandell">{{cite news |last=Mandell |first=Paul |date=June 1982 |title=Interview with Dorse Lanpher |journal=Fantastic Films |url=http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/ffmtext.htm |access-date=October 28, 2015 |via=Vanden-Eykel.com |archive-date=January 11, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160111023959/http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/ffmtext.htm |url-status=live}}</ref>

Objects like the bird cage that Mrs. Brisby escapes from, and the boats used by the rats, were made as physical models, painted and photographed, and eventually xeroxed onto animation cels which were then painted. Also Mrs. Brisby's home and several other locations were built as a physical model and photographed from different angles. These pictures were used as reference for the layout artists.<ref>[https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/the-process-of-nimh The Process of 'NIMH']</ref>

To achieve the film's detailed full animation while keeping to the tight budget, the studio strove to maximize its use of time and resources. The crew often worked long hours with no immediate financial reward (though they were offered a cut of the film's profits, a practice common for producers, directors and stars of live action films, but never before offered to artists on an animated feature).{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=39}} Producer Gary Goldman recalled working 110-hour weeks during the final six months of production. Around 100 in-house staff worked on the film, with the labor-intensive [[cel]] painting farmed out to 45 people working from home.<ref name="mcdaniel">{{cite web |last=McDaniel |first=Adam |publisher=Remembering NIMH: An Interview with Don Bluth |title=Reflections on the Film |url=https://www.adammcdaniel.com/DonBluth/Don_Bluth_Interview1.htm |access-date=October 28, 2015 |archive-date=January 12, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160112003638/http://adammcdaniel.com/DonBluth/Don_Bluth_Interview1.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> Many minor roles, including incidental and crowd voice work, were filled in by the in-house staff. The final cost of the film was $6.385 million. The producers, Bluth, Goldman and Pomeroy and the executive producers at Aurora mortgaged their homes collectively for $700,000 to complete the film, with the understanding that their investment would be the first to be repaid. The film was the sixth animated feature to be presented in the [[Dolby Stereo]] sound system.

In animating Justin and Jenner's sword fight, the animators referenced similar sequences in films such as ''[[The Adventures of Robin Hood]]'' (1938) and ''[[The Vikings (film)|The Vikings]]'' (1958).<ref name="mcdaniel" />

==Release==
[[Tim Hildebrandt]] spent two weeks painting ''The Secret of NIMH''<nowiki>'</nowiki>s promotional poster.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Dods|first=John|date=December 1982|title=The New Career of Tim Hildebrandt|magazine=[[Starlog (magazine)|Starlog]]|issue=65|page=58}}</ref>

The film's distributor, [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM/UA Entertainment Co.]], barely showed any interest in the film, leading Aurora to raise another $4,200,000 from private funding to pay for both the prints and the advertising campaign.<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=December 29, 1982 |title=Bluth Hits Distrib Re 'NIMH;' Preps Animated 'Canterbury' Pic |url=http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/var2982.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200205161541fw_/http://www.vanden-eykel.com/nimharchive/articles/var2982.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=2020-02-05 |magazine=Variety |via=Internet Archive}}</ref> The financiers had expected the film to open in wide release in 1,000 venues, but MGM opted for a limited opening weekend in 100 theaters, with its widest release in only 700. Although it faced competition with the blockbuster ''[[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial]]'' (directed by future Bluth partner [[Steven Spielberg]]),<ref name="beck2">{{cite news |last=Beck |first=Jerry |title=Don Bluth Goes Independent |date=June 1, 1996 |access-date=October 20, 2015 |work=[[Animation World Network]] |url=https://www.awn.com/animationworld/don-bluth-goes-independent |archive-date=March 14, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160314015512/http://www.awn.com/animationworld/don-bluth-goes-independent |url-status=live}}</ref> it performed better in those theaters alone in its opening week than ''[[Poltergeist (1982 film)|Poltergeist]]'', ''[[Rocky III]]'', ''[[Firefox (film)|Firefox]]'', and ''[[Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan]]''.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=55}} ''NIMH'' grossed nearly $14.7 million in North America,<ref name="boxoffice" /> though it was more successful on home video, cable, and foreign releases, ultimately turning a profit.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=55}}

===Home media===
''The Secret of NIMH'' debuted on [[Super 8 film]] and several home video formats in 1983, including [[VHS]],{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=56}} [[Betamax]], [[Capacitance Electronic Disc|CED Videodisc]], [[8 mm video format|Video8]]<ref name="nimhster">{{cite web |url=https://www.freewebs.com/nimhster/theatricalvhsdvd.htm |title=Theatrical/VHS/DVD |publisher=Nimhster's Secret of NIMH Fandom Website |access-date=December 30, 2014 |archive-date=January 1, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150101222949/http://www.freewebs.com/nimhster/theatricalvhsdvd.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> and [[LaserDisc]],<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/41565/ML100211/Secret-of-NIMH-The-%281982%29 |title=LaserDisc Database - Secret of NIMH, The [ML100211] |publisher=LaserDisc Database |access-date=December 30, 2014 |archive-date=March 6, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160306004606/http://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/41565/ML100211/Secret-of-NIMH-The-(1982) |url-status=live}}</ref> which were distributed by [[MGM Home Entertainment|MGM/UA Home Video]] in North America and [[Warner Home Video]] in Europe, Australia and Japan.<ref name="EU">{{cite web |url=https://www.pre-cert.co.uk/display.php?vId=UK10664 |title=Pre-Cert Video: The Secret of NIMH (1982) on Warner Home Video |access-date=December 30, 2014 |publisher=Pre-Cert Video |archive-date=September 24, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924081046/http://www.pre-cert.co.uk/display.php?vId=UK10664 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.pre-cert.co.uk/display.php?vId=AUS02078 |title=Pre-Cert Video: The Secret of NIMH (1982) on Warner Home Video |access-date=December 30, 2014 |publisher=Pre-Cert Video |archive-date=September 24, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924081044/http://www.pre-cert.co.uk/display.php?vId=AUS02078 |url-status=live}}</ref> A [[Video 2000]] version was also released exclusively in Europe.<ref name="EU" /> With a $79 purchase price in the United States, the VHS edition sold approximately 25,000 copies within the first few months.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=56}}

On September 6, 1990, the film was re-released on both VHS and LaserDisc in a new advertising campaign with lower retail prices. It was this new wide availability on video, as well as broadcasts on cable, that helped ''NIMH'' garner a [[cult following]] long after its theatrical debut.{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=56}} This was followed by another VHS release under the MGM/UA Family Entertainment label in 1994, along with a [[Philips]] [[CD-i]] [[Video CD|video disc]] version that same year, which was available exclusively through Warner Home Video worldwide.<ref name="nimhster" />

The film was released on [[DVD-Video|DVD]] for the first time on November 17, 1998, which was reprinted numerous times in the ensuing years, both as a stand-alone release or bundled with other animated films from MGM or [[20th Century Fox]].<ref name="Disney">{{cite web |url=https://www.dvdizzy.com/secretofnimh.html |title=The Secret of NIMH: Family Fun Edition DVD Review |author=Gutierrez, Albert |publisher=UltimateDisney.com |access-date=October 28, 2015 |archive-date=September 23, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923221218/http://www.dvdizzy.com/secretofnimh.html |url-status=live}}</ref> Don Bluth and Gary Goldman later oversaw a high-definition restoration of the film, which was released on June 19, 2007, in a 2-disc DVD set called the "Family Fun Edition". Improvements in the transfer over the 1998 DVD include color correction and dirt and dust removal and included special features such as audio commentary from both individuals and an interview featurette.<ref name="Disney" /> A [[Blu-ray]] version was released on March 29, 2011, which retained the special features of the "Family Fun Edition".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=5869 |title=More MGM Catalog Titles Heading to Blu-ray |publisher=Blu-ray.com |access-date=February 21, 2011 |archive-date=July 8, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110708073414/http://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=5869 |url-status=live}}</ref> The film was released on Blu-ray again by Eureka Entertainment, a Region B issue under the label's [[Masters of Cinema]] line.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Secret of NIMH |url=https://eurekavideo.co.uk/movie/the-secret-of-nimh/ |access-date=November 26, 2025 |website=Eureka}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
''The Secret of NIMH'' received positive reviews upon its release. It holds an approval rating of 93% on [[Rotten Tomatoes]] based on 70 reviews, with an average rating of 7.6/10. The consensus states: "''The Secret of NIMH'' seeks to resurrect the classical style of American animation and succeeds, telling a mature story with rapturous presentation."<ref name="RT">{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/secret_of_nimh/ |title=The Secret of NIMH (1982) |website=Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=November 11, 2021 |archive-date=October 25, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151025201113/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/secret_of_nimh |url-status=live}}</ref> The film also has a [[weighted average]] score of 76 out of 100 on [[Metacritic]] based on 15 reviews, which indicates "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Secret of NIMH Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-secret-of-nimh |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=2020-02-02 |archive-date=2020-11-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201113212253/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-secret-of-nimh |url-status=live}}</ref>

Critics [[Gene Siskel]] and [[Roger Ebert]] gave the movie two "yes" votes on a July 15, 1982, episode of their television program ''[[Sneak Previews]]'', stating that Don Bluth and his team had succeeded in their goal of matching the high-quality animation of Disney's heyday while also incorporating a deeper plot and greater variety of settings than other recent animated films.<ref>{{cite episode |host1=Ebert, Roger |host2=Siskel, Gene |url=https://siskelebert.org/?p=6633 |via=SiskelEbert.org |access-date=October 16, 2015 |title=Tron, The Secret of NIMH, The Devils Playground (1982) |series=Sneak Previews |season=5 |number=37 |archive-date=October 21, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201021134858/https://siskelebert.org/?p=6633 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0075583/episodes?year=1982 |website=IMDB |title=Sneak Previews (TV Series 1975–1996) – Episodes |access-date=October 19, 2015 |archive-date=May 1, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210501060013/https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0075583/episodes?year=1982 |url-status=live}}</ref> In his print review for the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'', Ebert gave the film three out of four stars, calling it "an artistic success", praising the quality of its animation and saying that it "contains that absolute rarity among feature-length animated cartoons, an interesting premise".

Ebert found that ''NIMH'' may not resonate as well on an emotional level with younger viewers, since "it has so many characters and involves them in so many different problems that there's nobody for the kids in the audience to strongly identify with".<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-secret-of-nimh-1982 |title=The Secret of NIMH Movie Review (1982) |work=Chicago Sun-Times |via=[[RogerEbert.com]] |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |access-date=December 20, 2014 |archive-date=December 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141223224913/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-secret-of-nimh-1982 |url-status=live}}</ref> Siskel, writing for the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'', found the film "charming" but stated that the narrative was "littered with too many unimportant characters" and that Dom DeLuise "insert[ed] too much of himself" into the character of Jeremy. Despite this, Siskel found the film, particularly the second half, to be a "genuine pleasure" and felt that even adults will be drawn into the story by the end, giving it three stars out of four.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/68361486/gene-siskels-review-of-the-secret-of/ |date=July 19, 1982 |title=Tempo: 'NIMH' charmer could have used more Disney |page=29 |work=Chicago Tribune |access-date=January 23, 2021 |author=Siskel, Gene |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |archive-date=January 29, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210129122655/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/68361486/gene-siskels-review-of-the-secret-of/ |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref>

[[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' noted the film's animation was "something of a technical and stylistic triumph" comparable to that of Disney's golden age, but he expressed dismay at the narrative, finding it too complicated and lacking in an "easily identifiable central character".<ref>{{cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |title='N.I.M.H' Shades of Disney's Golden Era |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1982/07/30/movies/nimh-shades-of-disney-s-golden-era.html |work=The New York Times |page=C12 |date=July 30, 1982 |access-date=January 23, 2021 |archive-date=January 22, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210122191110/https://www.nytimes.com/1982/07/30/movies/nimh-shades-of-disney-s-golden-era.html |url-status=live}}</ref> In his review for the 1990 VHS re-release, Jeff Unger of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave ''The Secret of NIMH'' an A grade, calling it "a wonderful adaptation" of the original book, adding that "Bluth and his animators, bless them, chose to revive an endangered art form – classically detailed animation. They drew their characters exquisitely and gave them individual personalities. The entire ensemble – artists, actors, animals, and musicians – created something unique: the world's first enjoyable rat race."<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://ew.com/article/1990/09/21/secret-nimh/ |date=September 21, 1990 |last=Unger |first=Jeff |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |title=The Secret of NIMH Review |access-date=December 23, 2014 |archive-date=December 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141223224209/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,318188,00.html |url-status=dead}}</ref> Similarly, [[Richard Corliss]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine called the film "something gorgeous to look at".<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://time.com/archive/6856843/cinema-bright-rats-bright-lights/ |magazine=Time |date=July 26, 1982 |author=Corliss, Richard |title=Cinema: Bright Rats, Bright Lights |access-date=December 23, 2014 |archive-date=December 24, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141224000856/http://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,922968,00.html |url-status=live}}</ref>
<!-- The Nostalgia Critic has not been referred to as a legitimate critic. Anyone wanting to argue this point can discuss it on the talk page. -->

John Nubbin reviewed ''The Secret of NIMH'' for ''[[Different Worlds]]'' magazine and stated that "''The Secret of NIMH'' is a delight to watch, and good news for all lovers of quality animation. Not only because it is a fine film, but also because it gives us hope for more of the same (or better) from this new, quality-pledging studio."<ref name="dw24">{{cite journal | last = Nubbin |first = John | title = The Sword of Hollywood | journal = [[Different Worlds]] | issue = 24 | pages =44–45 |date=September 1982}}</ref>

===Accolades===
''The Secret of NIMH'' won Best Animated Film of 1982 at the 10th annual [[Saturn Award]]s, where it also received a nomination for Best Fantasy Film, losing to ''[[The Dark Crystal]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://award.mtime.com/24/1983/ |title=The 10th Saturn Awards 1983 |website=MTime.com |access-date=December 20, 2014 |archive-date=December 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141223230015/http://award.mtime.com/24/1983/ |url-status=live}}</ref> In his acceptance speech, Bluth remarked: "Thanks. We didn't think anyone had noticed".{{sfn|Cawley|1991|page=56}} The film was also nominated for Best Family Feature for Animation, Musical or Fantasy at the [[4th Youth in Film Awards|4th annual Youth in Film Awards]], being beaten by ''E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial'',<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.youngartistawards.org/pastnoms4.htm |publisher=Young Artist Awards |access-date=December 23, 2014 |title=4th Annual Awards |archive-date=July 10, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150710220551/http://www.youngartistawards.org/pastnoms4.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> while the home video release received an Award of Excellence from the [[Film Advisory Board]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.filmadvisoryboard.com/aoe/OnVideo.php |title=Film Advisory Board, Inc. |access-date=December 30, 2014 |publisher=Film Advisory Board |archive-date=January 1, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150101215054/http://www.filmadvisoryboard.com/aoe/OnVideo.php |url-status=live}}</ref> In 2008, the [[American Film Institute]] nominated this film for its [[AFI's 10 Top 10|Top 10 Animation Films list]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://connect.afi.com/site/DocServer/10top10.pdf?docID=381&AddInterest=1781 |title=AFI's 10 Top 10 Nominees |format=PDF |access-date=August 19, 2021 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716071937/http://connect.afi.com/site/DocServer/10top10.pdf?docID=381&AddInterest=1781|archive-date=July 16, 2011}}</ref>

==Sequel==
{{Main|The Secret of NIMH 2: Timmy to the Rescue}}
A [[direct-to-video]] sequel directed by [[Dick Sebast]] and produced by [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Animation]] titled ''The Secret of NIMH 2: Timmy to the Rescue'' was released on December 22, 1998.<ref name="nimh2ew">{{cite magazine |author=Bernardin |first=Mark |date=December 18, 1998 |title=The Secret of NIMH 2: Timmy to the Rescue |url=https://www.ew.com/article/1998/12/18/secret-nimh-2-timmy-rescue |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160224141841/http://www.ew.com/article/1998/12/18/secret-nimh-2-timmy-rescue |archive-date=February 24, 2016 |access-date=October 19, 2015 |magazine=Entertainment Weekly}}</ref> Set several years after the events of the first film, the plot focuses on Mrs. Brisby's son Timothy as he struggles to live up to his father's prestigious reputation. Apart from [[Dom DeLuise]] and [[Arthur Malet]] reprising their roles as Jeremy and Mr. Ages, respectively, none of the original voice cast returned for the film. The film was made without Don Bluth's input or involvement and was panned by critics and fans upon release.<ref name="nimh2ew" /><ref>{{cite news |url=https://pqasb.pqarchiver.com/chicagotribune/access/37573203.html?dids=37573203%3A37573203&FMT=ABS&FMTS=ABS%3AFT&type=current&date=Dec+24%2C+1998&author=Harlene+Ellin.+Special+to+the+Tribune.&pub=Chicago+Tribune&desc=THE+MYSTERY+OF+%60NIMH+II%27%3A+WHY+DID+THEY+EVEN+BOTHER%3F&pqatl=google |last=Ellin |first=Harlene |title=The Mystery of 'NIMH II': Why Did They Even Bother? |date=December 24, 1998 |access-date=October 28, 2015 |url-access=registration |work=Chicago Tribune |publisher=[[Tribune Company]] |page=7.B |archive-date=March 22, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160322134350/http://pqasb.pqarchiver.com/chicagotribune/doc/418590137.html?FMT=ABS&FMTS=ABS%3AFT&type=current&date=Dec%2024%2C%201998&author=Harlene%20Ellin.%20Special%20to%20the%20Tribune.&pub=Chicago%20Tribune&edition=&startpage=&desc=THE%20MYSTERY%20OF%20%60NIMH%20II%27%3A%20WHY%20DID%20THEY%20EVEN%20BOTHER%3F |url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Live-action remake and television series==
In 2009, [[Paramount Pictures]] was working with [[Neil Burger]] on a remake of ''The Secret of NIMH''; nothing has materialized since.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Donahoo|first=Daniel|date=July 29, 2009|url=https://www.wired.com/2009/07/a-new-secret-of-nihm/|title=A New Secret of NIMH|magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]]|access-date=November 26, 2019|archive-date=December 22, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161222130205/https://www.wired.com/2009/07/a-new-secret-of-nihm/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Pedersen|first=Nicole|date=July 27, 2009|url=https://collider.com/paramount-and-director-neil-burger-set-to-reanimate-the-rats-of-nimh/|title=Paramount and Director Neil Burger Set to Reanimate THE RATS OF NIMH|website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|access-date=November 26, 2019|archive-date=January 5, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200105022957/https://collider.com/paramount-and-director-neil-burger-set-to-reanimate-the-rats-of-nimh/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2015, [[MGM]] re-acquired the rights to produce a new film based on the original novel ''Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH''. The film was to be produced by the team of Daniel Bobker and [[Ehren Kruger]], with screenplay by ''[[Ice Age (franchise)|Ice Age]]'' series writer [[Michael Berg (screenwriter)|Michael Berg]].<ref name="DL">{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2015/03/mrs-frisby-the-rats-of-nimh-ice-age-5-michael-berg-mgm-1201385967/ |author=Fleming, Mike Jr. |title=MGM Options 'Mrs. Frisby & The Rats Of Nimh', Sets 'Ice Age's Michael Berg To Hatch Family Franchise |date=March 4, 2015 |access-date=October 19, 2015 |archive-date=October 6, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151006200458/http://deadline.com/2015/03/mrs-frisby-the-rats-of-nimh-ice-age-5-michael-berg-mgm-1201385967/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The film was to be James Madigan's directorial debut.<ref name="Director">{{cite web |last1=Cecchini |first1=Mike |title=The Secret of NIMH Remake Gets Director |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/us/movies/secret-of-nimh/244316/the-secret-of-nimh-remake-gets-director |website=Den of Geek! |date=January 31, 2017 |access-date=9 March 2019 |archive-date=25 May 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180525100356/http://www.denofgeek.com/us/movies/secret-of-nimh/244316/the-secret-of-nimh-remake-gets-director |url-status=dead}}</ref> Planned as a [[computer-generated imagery|CGI]]/[[live-action]] hybrid in the style of films like ''[[The Smurfs (film)|The Smurfs]]'' and ''[[Alvin and the Chipmunks (film)|Alvin and the Chipmunks]]'', the film was to be "an origin story in which an imperiled mouse protagonist befriends a comical crew of lab rats as they turn hyper-intelligent. They escape a secret laboratory and become the great minds of vermin civilization, forced to outwit the humans hot on their tails".<ref name="ignreboot" /> The studio planned to turn the novel into a family franchise.<ref name="DL" />

The [[Russo brothers]] were attached as executive producers of the remake as of April 2019.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://collider.com/russo-brothers-producing-remakes-mgm-movies/|title=Russo Brothers to Oversee Remakes of MGM Classics, Including 'The Thomas Crown Affair'|website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|date=April 10, 2019|first=Matt|last=Goldberg|access-date=April 17, 2020|archive-date=June 14, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200614020527/https://collider.com/russo-brothers-producing-remakes-mgm-movies/|url-status=live}}</ref>

A television series based on the books was in development at [[Fox Broadcasting Company|Fox]] as an [[event series]] in September 2021.<ref>{{cite web |last1=White |first1=Peter |title=Animated Series Based On Robert C. O'Brien's 'NIMH' In Works At Fox As Network Steps Up Adult Cartoon Drive |url=https://deadline.com/2021/09/rats-of-nimh-animated-series-in-the-works-fox-1234828638/ |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=September 8, 2021}}</ref>
