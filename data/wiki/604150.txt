'''''The Doors''''' is a 1991 American [[biographical film|biographical]] [[Psychological drama|psychological]] [[musical film]] directed by [[Oliver Stone]], who also co-wrote the film with [[Randall Jahnson|J. Randall Jahnson]]. The film is based on the history of American rock band [[the Doors]] and their influence on music and [[counterculture]]. The film stars [[Val Kilmer]] as singer [[Jim Morrison]], [[Meg Ryan]] as Morrison's girlfriend [[Pamela Courson]], [[Kyle MacLachlan]] as keyboardist [[Ray Manzarek]], [[Frank Whaley]] as lead guitarist [[Robby Krieger]], [[Kevin Dillon (actor)|Kevin Dillon]] as drummer [[John Densmore]], [[Billy Idol]] as Cat, and [[Kathleen Quinlan]] as journalist [[Patricia Kennealy-Morrison|Patricia Kennealy]].

The film portrays Morrison as a larger-than-life icon of 1960s [[rock and roll]] and counterculture, including portrayals of Morrison's [[recreational drug use]], [[free love]], [[hippie]] lifestyle, [[alcoholism]], interest in [[hallucinogen|hallucinogenic drugs]] as [[entheogen]]s, and his growing [[fascination with death|obsession with death]], presented as threads which weave in and out of the film.

Produced by [[Bill Graham (promoter)|Bill Graham Films]], [[Carolco Pictures]], [[Imagine Entertainment]], Ixtlan, and [[StudioCanal|Le Studio Canal+]], and released by [[TriStar Pictures|Tri-Star Pictures]] on March 1, 1991, ''The Doors'' grossed $34.4 million in the United States and Canada on a $32 million production budget. The film received mixed reviews from critics; while Kilmer's performance, the supporting cast, the cinematography, the production design and Stone's directing were praised, criticism was centered on its historical inaccuracy and depiction of Morrison.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, the plot summary should be 400-700 words. -->
In 1949, young [[Jim Morrison]] and his family are traveling on a desert highway in [[New Mexico]] where they encounter an auto wreck and see an elderly Native American dying by the roadside. In 1965, Jim arrives in [[California]] and is assimilated into the [[Venice, Los Angeles|Venice Beach]] culture. During his tenure studying at [[University of California, Los Angeles|UCLA]], he meets [[Pamela Courson]] and they fall in love, becoming a couple. He also meets [[Ray Manzarek]] for the first time, as well as [[Robby Krieger]] and [[John Densmore]], who form [[the Doors]] with Morrison.

Jim convinces his bandmates to travel to [[Death Valley]] and experience the effects of [[psychedelic drug]]s. Returning to Los Angeles, they play several shows at the famous [[Whisky a Go Go]] nightclub and develop a rabid fanbase. Jim's onstage antics and lewd performance of the group's song "[[The End (The Doors song)|The End]]" upset the club's owners, and the band is ejected from the venue. After the show, they are approached by producer [[Paul A. Rothchild]] and [[Jac Holzman]] of [[Elektra Records]] and are offered a deal to record their [[The Doors (album)|first album]]. The Doors are soon invited to perform on ''[[The Ed Sullivan Show]]'', only to be told by one of the producers that they must change the lyric "girl we couldn't get much higher" in the song "[[Light My Fire]]", due to a reference to drugs. Despite this, Morrison performs the original lyric during the live broadcast and the band is not allowed to perform on the show again.

As the Doors' success continues, Jim becomes increasingly infatuated with his own image as "The Lizard King" and develops an addiction to alcohol and drugs. Jim becomes intimate with rock journalist [[Patricia Kennealy-Morrison|Patricia Kennealy]], who involves him in her [[witchcraft]] activities, participating in a mystical [[Handfasting (Neopaganism)|handfasting]] ceremony. Meanwhile, an elder spirit watches these events.

The rest of the band grows weary of Jim's missed recording sessions and absences at concerts. Jim arrives late and intoxicated at a Miami, Florida concert, becoming increasingly confrontational towards the audience and threatening to expose himself onstage. The incident is a low point for the band, resulting in criminal charges against Jim, cancellations of shows, breakdowns in Jim's personal relationships, and resentment from the other band members.

In 1970, following a lengthy trial, Jim is found guilty of [[indecent exposure]] and ordered to serve time in prison. However, he is allowed to remain free on bail, pending the results of an appeal. Patricia tells Jim that she is pregnant with his child, but Jim convinces her to have an abortion. Jim visits his bandmates for the final time, attending a birthday party hosted by Ray where he wishes the band luck in their future endeavors and gives each of them a copy of his poetry book ''[[An American Prayer]]''. As Jim plays in the front garden with the children, he sees that one of them is his childhood self and comments, "This is the strangest life I've ever known" (a lyric from the Doors song "Waiting for the Sun"), before passing out.

In 1971, Jim and Pam move to Paris, France, to escape the pressures of the L.A. lifestyle. On the evening of July 3, Pam finds Jim dead in the bathtub of their apartment. He is buried at [[Père Lachaise Cemetery]], beside other famous figures as [[Oscar Wilde]], [[Marcel Proust]] and [[Moliere]]. Pam also dies three years later at the [[27 Club|age of 27]].

== Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Oliver Stone 01.jpg|thumb|upright|After numerous attempts by other directors, Oliver Stone signed on to direct the film]]
Several film directors, including [[Quentin Tarantino]],<ref name="farout">{{cite magazine |first=Swapnil Dhruv |last=Bose |date=January 27, 2022 |title=Val Kilmer Explains How He Prepared to Play the Role of Jim Morrison |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/val-kilmer-prepared-to-play-jim-morrison/ |magazine=Far Out Magazine |access-date=March 24, 2023 |archive-date=August 22, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230822122702/https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/val-kilmer-prepared-to-play-jim-morrison/ |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Francis Ford Coppola]], [[Aaron Russo]], [[Jerry Weintraub]], [[Brian De Palma]], [[Jonathan Taplin]], [[Irving Azoff]], [[Martin Scorsese]] and [[William Friedkin]] had all flirted with or been approached about making a Doors biopic over the years.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=308}}<ref name="latimes.com">{{cite web | url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-01-07-ca-509-story.html | title=Jim Morrison: Back to the Sixties, Darkly : The storm still swirls around a generation's ultimate bad boy as Oliver Stone prepares to bring his story to the screen | website=[[Los Angeles Times]] | date=7 January 1990 | access-date=2025-03-08 | archive-date=2025-03-12 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250312072554/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-01-07-ca-509-story.html | url-status=live }}</ref> In 1985, [[Columbia Pictures]] acquired the rights from the Doors and the Morrison estate to make a film. Producer Sasha Harari wanted filmmaker Oliver Stone to write the screenplay but never heard back from his agent. After two unsatisfactory scripts were produced, Imagine Films replaced Columbia. Harari contacted Stone again and the director met with the surviving band members, telling them he wanted to keep a particularly wild scene from one of the early drafts. The group was offended by this and exercised their right of approval over the director, rejecting Stone.{{citation needed|date=April 2024}}

By 1989, [[Mario Kassar]] and [[Andrew Vajna]], who owned [[Carolco Pictures]], had acquired the rights to the project and wanted Stone to direct it.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=308}} The Doors had seen Stone's film ''[[Platoon (film)|Platoon]]'' (1986) and were impressed with what he had done,{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=310}} and Stone agreed to make the film after his next project, ''[[Evita (1996 film)#Oliver Stone: 1987–1994|Evita]]''. After spending years working on ''Evita'' and courting both [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] and [[Meryl Streep]] to play the titular role, the film fell apart over salary negotiations with Streep and Stone quickly moved into pre-production for ''The Doors''.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=310}} Stone was announced to direct the project in January 1990.<ref name="latimes.com"/>

Guitarist Robby Krieger had always opposed a Doors biopic until Stone signed on to direct.<ref name="Mitchell">{{cite news | last = Mitchell | first = Justin | title = Opening Up a Closed Door | newspaper = [[St. Petersburg Times]] | page =19 | date = December 28, 1990 }}</ref> Conversely, keyboardist Ray Manzarek had traditionally been the biggest advocate of immortalizing the band on film but opposed Stone's involvement.<ref name="Mitchell"/> He was not happy with the direction that Stone was going to take with the film and refused to give his approval. According to actor Kyle MacLachlan, "I know that he and Oliver weren't speaking. I think it was hard for Ray, he being the keeper of the Doors myth for so long".<ref name="MacInnis">{{cite news | last = MacInnis | first = Craig | title = The Myth is Huge, but the Truth is the Lure of the Eternal | work = [[Toronto Star]] | pages =H1 | date = March 2, 1991 }}</ref> According to Krieger, "when the Doors broke up Ray had his idea of how the band should be portrayed and John and I had ours".<ref name="Mitchell"/> Manzarek stated that he was not asked to consult on the film and wanted it to be about all four band members equally, rather than the focus being on Morrison.<ref name="Broeske">{{cite news | last = Broeske | first = P | title = Stormy Rider | work = Sunday Herald| date = March 10, 1991 }}</ref> Conversely, Stone stated that he repeatedly tried to get Manzarek involved, but "all he did was rave and shout. He went on for three hours about his point of view{{nbsp}}... I didn't want Ray to be dominant, but Ray thought he knew better than anybody else".{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=312}} Krieger claimed in his book ''Set the Night on Fire'' that Manzarek was also jealous of Stone because he wanted to direct the film himself.{{sfn|Krieger|2021|pp=287–288}}

=== Screenplay ===
Stone first heard the Doors in 1967, when he was a 21-year-old soldier in [[Vietnam]].<ref name="McDonnell">{{cite news | last = McDonnell | first = D. | title = Legendary Rocker Lives Again | newspaper = [[Herald Sun]] | page =27 | date = March 2, 1991 }}</ref> Before filming started, Stone and his producers had to negotiate with the three surviving band members and their label, [[Elektra Records]], as well as the parents of both Morrison and his girlfriend Pamela Courson. Morrison's parents would only allow themselves to be depicted in a dream-like flashback sequence at the beginning of the film. The Coursons wanted there to be no suggestion in any way that Pamela caused Morrison's death. Stone found the Coursons the most difficult to deal with because they wanted Pamela to be portrayed as "an angel".<ref name="McDonnell"/> While researching the film, Stone read through transcripts of interviews with over 100 people.<ref name="Economist">{{cite news | title = Oliver Stone and ''The Doors'' | newspaper = [[The Economist]] | date = March 16, 1991 }}</ref> Stone finally penned the film script in the summer of 1989, later stating that "''The Doors'' script was always problematic. Even when we shot, but the music helped fuse it together".{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=311}} Stone first picked the songs he wanted to use and then wrote "each piece of the movie as a mood to fit that song".{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=311}} The Coursons did not like Stone's script and tried to slow the production down by refusing to allow any of Morrison's later poetry to be used in the film. When Morrison died, Courson acquired the rights to Morrison's poetry; when she died, her parents got the rights.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=311}}

=== Casting ===
For nearly 10 years prior to production, the project went through [[development hell]] after being considered by many studios and directors. Several actors, including [[Tom Cruise]], [[Johnny Depp]], [[Timothy Hutton]], [[Steven Bauer]], [[Gregory Harrison]], [[Christopher Lambert]], [[Michael Ontkean]], [[Jason Patric]], [[John Travolta]] and [[Richard Gere]], were each considered for the role of Morrison when the project was still in development in over the years,<ref name="McDonnell2">{{cite news | last = McDonnell | first = D. | title = Rider on the Storm | newspaper = [[The Courier-Mail]] | date = March 16, 1991 }}</ref><ref name="latimes.com"/> with [[Bono]] of [[U2]] and [[Michael Hutchence]] of [[INXS]] also expressing interest in the role. Stone initially offered the role to [[Ian Astbury]] of [[the Cult]], who declined the role because he was not happy with the way Morrison was going to be represented in the film.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=308}}

When Stone began talking about the project in 1988, he had Val Kilmer in mind to play Morrison, after seeing him in the [[Ron Howard]] fantasy film ''[[Willow (1988 film)|Willow]]''.<ref name="McDonnell"/><ref name="Green">{{cite news | last = Green | first = Tom | title = Kilmer's Uncanny Portrait of Morrison Opens Career Doors | newspaper = [[USA Today]] | pages =4D | date = March 4, 1991 }}</ref> Kilmer had the same kind of singing voice as Morrison and, to convince Stone that he was right for the role, spent several thousand dollars of his own money and made his own eight-minute audition video, singing and looking like Morrison at various stages of his life.<ref name="Hall">{{cite news | last = Hall | first = Carla | title = Val Kilmer, Lighting the Fire | newspaper = [[Washington Post]] | pages =G1 | date = March 3, 1991 }}</ref> To prepare for the role, Kilmer lost weight and spent six months rehearsing Doors songs every day; the actor learned 50 songs, 15 of which are actually performed in the film. Kilmer also spent hundreds of hours with the Doors' producer [[Paul A. Rothchild]], who related "anecdotes, stories, tragic moments, humorous moments, how Jim thought{{nbsp}}... interpretation of Jim's lyrics".<ref name="Hall"/> Rothchild also took Kilmer into the studio and helped him with "some pronunciations, idiomatic things that Jim would do that made the song sound like Jim".<ref name="Hall"/> Kilmer also met with Krieger and Densmore but Manzarek refused to talk to him.<ref name="Hall"/> When the Doors heard Kilmer singing they could not tell whether the voice was Kilmer's or Morrison's.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=314}}

Stone auditioned approximately 60 actresses for the role of Pamela Courson.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=316}} The role required nudity and the script featured sex scenes, which generated a fair amount of controversy. Casting director Risa Bramon felt that [[Patricia Arquette]] auditioned very well and should have gotten the role.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=316}} [[Jennifer Tilly]] auditioned for the role and recalled the audition process in a 2025 [[Twitter]] post in tribute to Kilmer: "A long time ago, I was auditioning for the movie ''The Doors''. It was kind of a cattle call. They paired together potential Jims with potential Pamelas. And they were running behind so we were spilling out of the casting office, sitting on the porch, the lawn, and the driveway. All of a sudden, a ’60s convertible came screeching up, blaring Doors music at top volume. And a guy jumped out and strode inside: He had wild hair and he was barefoot, shirtless and wearing nothing but a pair of tight leather pants. We all looked at each other like… Who is this guy? We were more than a little shook by the sheer audacity of his entrance. Well, of course, it was Val Kilmer and from that minute on, nobody else stood a chance".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Knapp |first=JD |date=April 2, 2025 |title=Jennifer Tilly Shares Sweet Story Auditioning for 'The Doors' Alongside Val Kilmer: 'Nobody Else Stood a Chance' |url=https://www.thewrap.com/val-kilmer-the-doors-audition-jennifer-tilly-sweet-story/ |website=[[TheWrap]] |access-date=April 22, 2025 |archive-date=April 3, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250403031755/https://www.thewrap.com/val-kilmer-the-doors-audition-jennifer-tilly-sweet-story/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Tilly ended up playing a groupie called "Okie girl", but her scenes were deleted from the final cut.<ref>{{Cite web |date=April 2, 2025 |title=Jennifer Tilly pays heartfelt tribute to The Doors co-star Val Kilmer |url=https://nz.news.yahoo.com/jennifer-tilly-pays-heartfelt-tribute-150329064.html |website=[[Yahoo News]]}}</ref>

[[Meg Ryan]] was cast as Courson. To prepare for the role, she talked to the Coursons and people that knew Pamela.<ref name="McDonnell"/> Before doing the film, she was not familiar with Morrison and "liked a few songs", adding, "I had to reexamine all my beliefs about [the 1960s] in order to do this movie".{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=322}} In doing research, she also encountered several conflicting views of Pamela.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=316}}

Krieger acted as a [[technical advisor]] on the film, chiefly to show his cinematic alter ego, Frank Whaley, where to put his fingers on the guitar [[fretboard]] during the [[Miming in instrumental performance|mimed]] performance sequences.<ref name="Mitchell"/> Similarly, Densmore also acted as a consultant on the film, tutoring Kevin Dillon.<ref name="McDonnell2"/>

=== Filming ===
With a budget set at $32 million, ''The Doors'' was filmed over 13 weeks, predominantly in and around Los Angeles, California; Paris, France; [[New York City, New York]]; and the [[Mojave Desert]].<ref name="Broeske"/>{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=317}} Stone originally hired [[Paula Abdul]] to choreograph the film's concert scenes. She dropped out of the project because she did not understand Morrison's on-stage actions and was not familiar with the time period. Abdul recommended Bill and Jacqui Landrum, who watched hours of concert footage before working with Kilmer and got him to do dance exercises to loosen up his upper body and jumping routines to develop his stamina.<ref name="Thomas">{{cite news | last = Thomas | first = Karen | title = Helping Stage ''The Doors'' | newspaper = USA Today | pages =2D | date = March 12, 1991 }}</ref>

During the concert scenes, Kilmer did his own singing, performing over the Doors' master tapes without Morrison's lead vocals, avoiding [[lip-synching]].{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=318}} Kilmer shared his audition tape, his preparation and some behind-the-scenes footage of the film in the 2021 documentary ''[[Val (film)|Val]]''. Kilmer's endurance was put to the test during the concert sequences, which took several days to film, with Stone stating, "His voice would start to deteriorate after two or three takes. We had to take that into consideration."<ref name=Kilday>{{cite news |last=Kilday |first=Gregg |title=Love Me Two Times |newspaper=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=March 1, 1991 |url=https://ew.com/article/1991/03/01/becoming-jim-morrison/ |access-date= 2010-07-17 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100924002858/https://ew.com/ew/article/0,,313463,00.html |archive-date=September 24, 2010}}</ref> One sequence, filmed inside the Whisky a Go Go, proved to be more difficult than others due to all the smoke and sweat, a result of the body heat and intense camera lights. "The End" sequence took five days to shoot, after which Kilmer was completely exhausted.<ref name="Kilday"/> Kilmer and Ryan were picking glass out of their knees between takes while filming one of their characters' fight scenes, Kilmer recalled: "How we dealt with it was just with a lot of humor and sensitivity. We were very dependent on each other in that sensitive state which you have to live in when you're inside someone else's life."<ref name="Kilday"/>

Kilmer described the film saying; "Ultimately, the movie isn't about the '60s. It isn't about the Doors. It isn't even about Jim Morrison. It's about fame. That's the line that Oliver chose to hang it on. In my feeble way, I've been trying not to suffer those pains."<ref name="Kilday"/> Kilmer was so immersed into the role of Jim Morrison that he had to go to therapy after he finished filming in order to recover properly.<ref name="farout"/>

Controversy arose during filming when a memo linked to Kilmer circulated among cast and crew members, listing rules of how the actor was to be treated for the duration of principal photography.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=326}} These provisions forbade people to approach him on set without good reason, address him by his own name while he was in character or stare at him on set. An upset Stone contacted Kilmer's agent and the actor claimed it was a misunderstanding and that the memo was for his own people and not the film crew.{{sfn|Riordan|1996|p=326}}

=== Soundtrack ===
{{Main|The Doors (soundtrack)}}
The film contains over two dozen of the Doors' songs, although only half of these appear on the accompanying [[The Doors (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] album. In the film, original recordings of the band are combined with vocal performances by Kilmer himself,<ref>{{cite web |first=Kevin |last=Burwick |url=https://movieweb.com/the-doors-movie-1991-val-kilmer-rehearsal-video-jim-morrison/ |title=Val Kilmer Shares Rare Doors Movie Rehearsal Video |website= [[MovieWeb]] |date=June 14, 2017 |access-date=November 17, 2021}}</ref> although none of Kilmer's performances appear on the soundtrack album. In addition, two songs by [[the Velvet Underground]] ("[[Heroin (The Velvet Underground song)|Heroin]]" and "[[Venus in Furs (song)|Venus in Furs]]") are heard throughout the film, with the former appearing on the soundtrack.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/r84281 |title=The Doors |work=[[AllMusic]] |access-date=November 17, 2021 |archive-date=August 29, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110829150341/http://www.allmusic.com/album/r84281 |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Historical inaccuracies==
{{quote box|It's not Jim Morrison. It's Oliver Stone in leather pants.|source=—Ray Manzarek, dismissing the film's portrayal of Morrison.<ref>{{cite web |first=Tom |last=Meek |date=April 5, 2010 |title=Interview: Ray Manzarek of the Doors |url=https://fwiw.zone/2013/03/17/ray-manzarek/ |website=For What It's Worth |access-date=July 7, 2022}}</ref>}}
The film is based mostly on real people and actual events, with some segments reflecting Stone's vision and [[dramatization]] of those people and events.{{sfn|Krieger|2021|p=258}} For example, when Morrison is asked to change the lyric in "Light My Fire" for his appearance on ''The Ed Sullivan Show'', he is depicted as blatantly ignoring the request, defiantly shouting "Higher! Yeah!" into the television camera.{{sfn|Goldsmith|Willson|Fonseca|2016|p=205}} However, during the actual broadcast, Morrison simply sang the vocal with the same emphasis as on the record. Morrison later said the inclusion of "higher" in the live version was an accident, and that he had meant to change the lyric but was so nervous about performing on live television that he forgot. Conversely, Ray Manzarek said the Doors only pretended to agree to the changing of words and deliberately played the song as they always had, albeit without any added emphasis on the offending words.{{sfn|Manzarek|1998|pp=251–252}}

Several acts of violence portrayed in the film are disputed: Morrison is depicted as locking Pamela Courson in a closet and setting it on fire;<ref>{{cite magazine |first=Steven P. |last=Wheeler |title=A Conversation with Frank Lisciandro |magazine=ROKRITR.com |date=April 10, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |first=Patrick |last=Goldstein  |title=Ray Manzarek Slams 'The Doors' |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=March 7, 1991}}</ref> having an argument with Courson at a [[Thanksgiving]] celebration, where they both threaten each other with a knife; and angrily throwing a television set at Manzarek for licensing the use of "Light My Fire" in a [[Buick]] television commercial.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.crystal-ship.com/entrevues.php?cht=03 |title=Chat with Ray Manzarek |date=November 17, 1997 |quote=Jim burning up Pam in the closet never happened. Jim throwing a television set at Ray never happened! The car commercial on TV never happened. The big fight after Ray and Dorothy's wedding never happened etc.}}</ref> Even though Manzarek was frank about Morrison's tendency to go into senseless rages,{{sfn|Manzarek|1998|pp=180, 205-207, 305-308}} participants in the film agree that none of these specific incidents occurred. Stone acknowledges in the [[DVD]] director's commentary that the Thanksgiving scene never actually took place, nor the scene when the band members travel to a desert where Morrison encourages them to take psychedelic drugs.<ref name=dvd>{{cite AV media |title=The Doors |type=DVD Audio Commentary |people=[[Oliver Stone]] |publisher=[[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]] |year=1991}}</ref>

Dialogue that took place between Morrison and [[Patricia Kennealy]] is misrepresented in scenes between Morrison and Pam Courson, with Courson speaking Kennealy's words. Courson is also depicted as saying hostile things to Kennealy, when by Kennealy's reports the interactions between the two women were polite.<ref>{{cite magazine |first=Patricia |last=Kennealy |author-link=Patricia Kennealy |date=August 1991 |title=The Movie: A Personal Review |magazine=The Doors Quarterly Magazine |volume=24}}</ref> Kennealy is also portrayed as being the girl Morrison was with in the shower stall backstage before the December 9, 1967 [[New Haven]] concert (not 1968 as noted in the film), when in fact he was with a local teenage co-ed from [[Southern Connecticut State University]].{{sfn|Weidman|2011|p=267}} Additionally, the New Haven venue is presented in the film as an amphitheater with a large balcony and a packed audience, when in reality it was a rather decrepit, half-empty hockey rink with audience members sitting on folding wooden chairs. In the scene at a press conference set in New York City in 1967, when Kennealy is first introduced to Morrison, the singer is asked a question regarding "the dreadful reviews your new poetry book has got"; at that time, Morrison had not yet published any volumes of his poetry.{{citation needed|date=May 2021}}

John Densmore is portrayed as hating Morrison when the singer's personal and drug problems begin to dominate his behavior. However, Densmore states in his biography ''Riders on the Storm'' that he never directly confronted Morrison about his drunken behavior.{{sfn|Densmore|1991|p=268}}

In the concert sequences naked women are shown prancing around onstage, which Densmore said did not happen at any of the Doors' concerts.<ref>{{cite magazine |first=Jim |last=Clash |date=January 25, 2015 |title=Doors Drummer John Densmore On Oliver Stone, Cream's Ginger Baker (Part 3) |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimclash/2015/01/25/doors-drummer-john-densmore-on-oliver-stone-creams-ginger-baker-part-3/ |magazine=[[Forbes]] |access-date=July 9, 2022}}</ref> Krieger said that he did not take acid prior to the concert at the [[Dinner Key Auditorium]] in [[Miami]] on March 1, 1969, as depicted in the film.<ref>{{cite news |first=Steve |last=Knopper |date=April 15, 1991 |title=Ex-Doors Disagree on Oliver Stone Film |newspaper=[[Knight Ridder]]}}</ref>

The surviving Doors members were all, to one degree or another, unhappy with the final film but still praised Kilmer's performance. In a 1991 interview with Gary James, Manzarek criticized Stone for exaggerating Morrison's alcohol consumption: "Jim with a bottle all the time. It was ridiculous{{nbsp}}... It was not about Jim Morrison. It was about Jimbo Morrison, the drunk. God, where was the sensitive poet and the funny guy? The guy I knew was not on that screen."<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.classicbands.com/RayManzarekInterview.html |title=Interview with Ray Manzarek at Classic Bands |access-date=2025-04-16 |archive-date=2025-04-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250411151443/http://www.classicbands.com/RayManzarekInterview.html |url-status=live }}</ref> In the afterword of his book ''Riders on the Storm'', Densmore says that the movie is based on "the myth of Jim Morrison" and criticizes the film for portraying Morrison's ideas as "muddled through the haze of the drink [alcohol]".{{sfn|Densmore|1991|p=Afterword}} In a 1991 interview, Krieger said that Kilmer did a good job portraying Morrison and that "it was scary sometimes how much he was like Jim".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Robby Krieger (The Doors) interview 1991 {{!}} A never-published interview with Robby Krieger |url=https://www.thetapesarchive.com/robby-krieger/ |access-date=April 21, 2025 |website=The Tapes Archive |archive-date=February 3, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240203140108/https://www.thetapesarchive.com/robby-krieger/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In a 1994 interview, Krieger said that the film does not give the viewer "any kind of understanding of what made Jim Morrison tick". Krieger added, "They left a lot of stuff out. Some of it was overblown, but a lot of the stuff was very well done, I thought."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.classicbands.com/RobbyKriegerInterview.html |title=Interview With Robby Krieger |website=Classicbands.com |access-date=2014-05-19 |archive-date=2014-05-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140520002636/http://www.classicbands.com/RobbyKriegerInterview.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

In a 2015 interview with ''[[Forbes]]'', Densmore said that Manzarek wanted to direct the film at one point before Stone, and he was trying to tell Stone how to make the movie and was not allowed on set for a while. Densmore said that Manzarek did not want the film to show Morrison's darker side, to which Densmore recalled: "Well, that's ludicrous. Maybe Oliver focused quite a bit on that, but I love him for giving it a go. I think Val Kilmer should have been nominated for an Oscar. He gave me the creeps on the set - he was so close to Jim. Actors, they're weird. They transform their bodies." Densmore added that his only regret about the film is that it wasn't more about the times, the sixties. "Oliver chose the tortured artist, a valid storyline probably not dissimilar to stuff jangling around in Mr. Stone's head", he said.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Clash |first=Jim |date=January 25, 2015 |title=Doors Drummer John Densmore On Oliver Stone, Cream's Ginger Baker (Part 3) |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimclash/2015/01/25/doors-drummer-john-densmore-on-oliver-stone-creams-ginger-baker-part-3/ |website=[[Forbes]] |access-date=July 9, 2022 |archive-date=July 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220709074206/https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimclash/2015/01/25/doors-drummer-john-densmore-on-oliver-stone-creams-ginger-baker-part-3/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In Manzarek's biography of the Doors, ''Light My Fire'', he often criticizes Stone and his account of Morrison. For example, in Stone's re-creation of Morrison's student film at UCLA, Morrison watches a [[Normandy landings|D-Day]] sequence on television and shouts profanities in [[German language|German]], with a near-nude [[Germans|German]] exchange student dancing on top of the television sporting a [[swastika]] armband. According to Manzarek, the only similarity between Stone's version and Morrison's was that the girl in question was German.{{sfn|Manzarek|1998|pp=55-57}}<ref>{{cite magazine |first=Alex von |last=Tunzelmann |date=February 17, 2011 |title=Oliver Stone's The Doors: a flash in the pan that won't light anyone's fire |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2011/feb/17/reel-history-oliver-stone-the-doors |magazine=[[The Guardian]] |access-date=November 18, 2021}}</ref> Manzarek described Stone's version as a "grotesque exaggeration", and recalled that Morrison's film was a "much lighter, much friendlier, much funnier kind of thing".{{sfn|Manzarek|1998|pp=55-57}}

As the credits point out and as Stone emphasizes in his DVD commentary, some characters, names, and incidents in the film are fictitious or amalgamations of real people.<ref name=dvd/> In the [[1997 in film|1997]] [[documentary film|documentary]], ''The Road of Excess'', Stone states that Quinlan's character, Patricia Kennealy, is a composite, and in retrospect should have been given a fictitious name.<ref>{{cite AV media | first =Charles | last =Kiselyak | year =1997 | title =The Road of Excess | type =documentary}}</ref> Kennealy was hurt by her portrayal in the film and objected to a scene where Morrison states that he did not take their [[handfasting]] ceremony seriously. Kennealy is credited as a "[[Wiccan]] priestess" for her cameo role as the priestess who married the real Jim and Patricia, but the real priestess was a [[Celtic paganism|Celtic pagan]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Kennealy |first=Patricia |year=1998 |title=Blackmantle - A Book of The Keltiad |location=New York |publisher=HarperPrism |isbn=0-06-105610-3}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Kennealy |first=Patricia |author-link=Patricia Kennealy |year=1993 |title=Strange Days: My Life With And Without Jim Morrison |pages=[https://archive.org/details/strangedaysmylif00kenn/page/169 169–180] |publisher=[[Penguin Random House|Dutton/Penguin]] |location=New York |isbn=978-0-45226-981-1 |url=https://archive.org/details/strangedaysmylif00kenn/page/169}}</ref>

The former Doors said the depiction of Pamela Courson is not accurate at all, as their book ''The Doors'' describes this version of Courson as "a cartoon of a girlfriend".{{sfn|Fong-Torres|Doors|2006|p=232}} Courson's parents had inherited Morrison's poems when their daughter died, and Stone had to agree to restrictions about his portrayal of her in exchange for the rights to use the poetry. In particular, Stone agreed to avoid any suggestion that Courson may have been responsible for Morrison's death.<ref>{{cite news |first=Pat H. |last=Broeske |date=January 7, 1990 |title=Jim Morrison: Back to the Sixties Darkly |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref> However, Alain Ronay and Courson herself had both said that she was responsible. In ''Riders on the Storm'', Densmore says Courson said she felt terribly guilty because she had obtained drugs that she believed had either caused or contributed to Morrison's death.{{sfn|Densmore|1991|p={{page needed|date=November 2021}}}}

== Release and reception ==
''The Doors'' was released theatrically by [[TriStar Pictures|Tri-Star Pictures]] on March 1, 1991, and debuted at number 2 at the box office.<ref name="BOM" /> The film grossed $34.4 million in the United States and Canada.<ref name="BOM" />

In July 1991, ''The Doors'' was entered into the [[17th Moscow International Film Festival]].<ref name="Moscow1991">{{cite web |url=http://www.moscowfilmfestival.ru/miff34/eng/archives/?year=1991 |title=17th Moscow International Film Festival (1991) |access-date=2013-03-02 |work=MIFF |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140403102003/http://www.moscowfilmfestival.ru/miff34/eng/archives/?year=1991 |archive-date=2014-04-03}}</ref> In April 2019, a restored version of the film was selected to be shown in the Cannes Classics section at the [[2019 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Cannes Classics 2019 |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/press/press-articles/communique/articles/cannes-classics-2019-1 |website=Festival de Cannes |access-date=26 April 2019 |date=26 April 2019 |archive-date=26 April 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190426215446/https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/press/press-articles/communique/articles/cannes-classics-2019-1 |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Critical reception ===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has a 58% approval rating based on 64 reviews and an average rating of 5.90/10. The site's consensus states: "Val Kilmer delivers a powerhouse performance as one of rock's most incendiary figures, but unfortunately, Oliver Stone is unable to shed much light on the circus surrounding the star."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/doors/ | title=The Doors (1991) | work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] | publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]] | access-date=July 27, 2026 | archive-date=April 8, 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190408071038/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/doors | url-status=live }}</ref> [[Metacritic]] reports a 62 out of 100 rating, based on 19 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-doors | title=The Doors reviews | work=[[Metacritic]] | publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] | access-date=January 12, 2016 | archive-date=March 5, 2016 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305093649/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-doors | url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Cinemascore |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date=2018-12-20}}</ref>

In a contemporary review, [[Roger Ebert]] gave ''The Doors'' two-and-a-half out of four stars. Ebert wrote that the experience of watching the film was "not always very pleasant," but praised the acting performances, particularly Kilmer's.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Ebert |first1=Roger |author-link1=Roger Ebert |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-doors-1991 |title=The Doors |date=March 1, 1991 |website=RogerEbert.com |access-date=November 17, 2021 |archive-date=November 17, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211117164147/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-doors-1991 |url-status=live }}</ref> Contrarily, [[Peter Travers]] in ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' wrote a glowing review, rating it with four out of four stars.<ref>[http://m.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-doors-19910301 "The Doors" (review)] {{Webarchive|url=https://archive.today/20140718001758/http://m.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-doors-19910301 |date=2014-07-18 }} ''[[Rolling Stone (magazine)|Rolling Stone]]'' (March 1, 1991)</ref> In a 2010 piece for [[Q (magazine)|''Q'' magazine]], Keith Cameron stated that "few people emerged from seeing the film having raised their opinions of that band and especially its singer Jim Morrison." The problem, as critic Cameron put it, was not so much that "Stone dwelled upon Morrison the inebriate, the philanderer, or the pretentious Lizard King", but rather the "clichéd Hollywood devices for sucking the wonder from the pioneering band: actors with fake hair saying silly things{{nbsp}}..." and "a self-important director's turgid attempts to make grand statements about America."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Cameron |first=Keith |magazine=[[Q (magazine)|Q]] |date=October 2010 |title=Review Music DVDs. The Doors. When You're Strange |page=134}}</ref>

=== Industry reception ===
[[Nicolas Cage]] said Kilmer should have won the Oscar for ''The Doors''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ntim |first=Zac |date=April 2, 2025 |title=Nicolas Cage Pays Tribute To “Genius Actor” Val Kilmer: “He Should Have Won The Oscar For 'The Doors'” |url=https://deadline.com/2025/04/nicolas-cage-genius-actor-val-kilmer-oscar-the-doors-1236357100/ |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref>

Following Kilmer's death on April 1, 2025, the Doors issued a statement on social media saying; "We are sad to hear of the passing of Val Kilmer, a truly gifted actor whose powerful performance as Jim in ''The Doors'' film helped introduce the band's story and music to a new generation. Val brought incredible depth, intensity, and heart to every role he played, and his dedication to portraying Jim left a lasting impression. Thank you, Val - you'll always be a part of The Doors' story".<ref>{{Cite web |author=The Doors |date=April 2, 2025 |title=We are sad to hear of the passing of Val Kilmer, a truly gifted actor whose powerful performance as Jim in The Doors film helped introduce the band's story and music to a new generation. |url=https://www.instagram.com/thedoors/p/DH9BhL2NAMs/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250421092525/https://www.instagram.com/thedoors/p/DH9BhL2NAMs/ |archive-date=April 21, 2025 |access-date=April 21, 2025 |website=[[Instagram]]}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Drummer John Densmore called Kilmer "a gifted actor" who "miraculously played Jim [Morrison] in ''The Doors'' movie",<ref>{{Cite web |last=Densmore |first=John |date=April 2, 2025 |title=The gifted actor, Val Kilmer, who miraculously played Jim (Morrison) in The Doors movie, has broke on thru to the other side. RIP. |url=https://www.instagram.com/johndensmore/p/DH9CIlHxT_x/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250421064748/https://www.instagram.com/johndensmore/p/DH9CIlHxT_x/ |archive-date=April 21, 2025 |access-date=April 21, 2025 |website=[[Instagram]]}}{{cbignore}}</ref> and later wrote: "As an actor, Val's channeling of Jim (Morrison) was so close, he gave me the creeps on the set of Oliver Stone's biopic. He should have been nominated for an Oscar as Best Actor."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Densmore |first=John |date=April 5, 2025 |title=As an actor, Val's channeling of Jim (Morrison) was so close, he gave me the creeps on the set of Oliver Stone's biopic. He should have been nominated for an Oscar as Best Actor. |url=https://www.instagram.com/johndensmore/p/DIFcZnvszxD/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20250422003031/https://www.instagram.com/johndensmore/p/DIFcZnvszxD/ |archive-date=April 22, 2025 |access-date=April 22, 2025 |website=[[Instagram]]}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

== Home media and ''The Final Cut''==
The film's rights have been with French company [[StudioCanal]] since 1996, as their parent company [[Canal+ S.A.|Canal+]] had outbid [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]] for the rights to [[Carolco Pictures]]' library after it went bankrupt in 1995.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-01-17-fi-25405-story.html|title=New Carolco Library Bid Sends Fox Running|first=James|last=Bates|date=January 17, 1996|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=May 20, 2026}}</ref><ref>Copyright notice in the credits for ''The Doors'', 1991.</ref> The North American TV rights and digital distribution rights for Carolco's library (including ''The Doors'') belong with [[Paramount Pictures]] through its acquisition of [[Spelling Television|Spelling Entertainment]], which itself was given these specific rights from Carolco in 1992.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.paramountmovies.com/movies/the-doors|title=Watch The Doors &#124; DVD/Blu-ray or Streaming &#124; Paramount Movies|via=www.paramountmovies.com}}</ref> StudioCanal licensed out home video releases for Carolco titles to [[Artisan Entertainment]], and its later successor [[Lionsgate Home Entertainment]]. Artisan released ''The Doors'' on [[DVD]] in 1997 and again on August 14, 2001,<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=https://www.dvdsreleasedates.com/movies/2386/The-Doors-(1991).html|title=The Doors DVD Release Date|website=DVDs Release Dates|language=en|access-date=2019-05-23|archive-date=2020-09-29|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200929111942/https://www.dvdsreleasedates.com/movies/2386/The-Doors-(1991).html|url-status=live}}</ref> with it later released on [[Blu-ray]] on August 12, 2008.<ref name=":0" /> The film was released on [[4K resolution|4K]] [[Blu-ray]] on July 23, 2019, with a new version of the film dubbed ''The Final Cut''. Supervised by Stone, it features remastered sound and removes a scene where Morrison, following his trial, almost commits suicide.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/72244/thedoors4kultrahdbluray.html|title=The Doors: The Final Cut - 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray Ultra HD Review {{!}} High Def Digest|website=ultrahd.highdefdigest.com|access-date=2019-05-23|archive-date=2020-10-24|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201024235605/https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/72244/thedoors4kultrahdbluray.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Stone later noted that he had cut 3 minutes for ''The Final Cut'' as he initially felt the film was too long but shortly after realised he had made a mistake as it left unanswered questions. He has said he prefers the theatrical version and wanted to ensure that version is available on home media.<ref>{{cite tweet|url=https://twitter.com/TheOliverStone/status/1754348801865728289|title=Note from Director to #TheDoors fans|user=TheOliverStone|author-link=Oliver Stone|number=1754348801865728289|access-date=February 8, 2024}}</ref>
