{{Nihongo|'''''Godzilla Minus One'''''|ゴジラ{{ruby-ja|-1.0|マイナスワン}}|Gojira Mainasu Wan|lead=yes}} is a 2023 Japanese [[Epic film|epic]]{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Vulture"/><ref name="WashingtonPost"/><ref name="SCMP"/><ref name="Empire"/>}} ''[[kaiju]]'' film written, directed, and with visual effects by [[Takashi Yamazaki]]. It is the 37th film in the [[Godzilla (franchise)|''Godzilla'' franchise]], the 33rd film thereof produced by [[Toho]], and the fifth installment in the [[Godzilla (franchise)#Reiwa era (2016–present)|''Reiwa'' era]].{{efn|Japan's [[Reiwa era|''Reiwa'' era]] began on May 1, 2019,<ref name="CNN"/> however, Toho considers ''[[Shin Godzilla]]'' (2016) and the [[Polygon Pictures]] anime trilogy – ''[[Godzilla: Planet of the Monsters]]'' (2017), ''[[Godzilla: City on the Edge of Battle]]'', and ''[[Godzilla: The Planet Eater]]'' (both 2018) – as part of the ''Reiwa'' era.{{sfn|Skipper|2022|p=217}}}} Set in [[postwar Japan]], the film stars [[Ryunosuke Kamiki]] as a former ''[[kamikaze]]'' pilot suffering from [[post-traumatic stress disorder]] after encountering the giant monster [[Godzilla (Takashi Yamazaki)|Godzilla]]. The supporting cast includes [[Minami Hamabe]], [[Yuki Yamada (actor)|Yuki Yamada]], [[Munetaka Aoki]], [[Hidetaka Yoshioka]], [[Sakura Ando]], and [[Kuranosuke Sasaki]].

Following ''[[Shin Godzilla]]'' (2016), Toho was unable to produce another live-action ''Godzilla'' film until 2020, owing to a contract with [[Legendary Entertainment]]. Executive producer [[Minami Ichikawa]] appointed Yamazaki to create the movie upon the completion of ''[[The Great War of Archimedes]]'' (2019). Development began in March 2019, when Yamazaki devised the film's plot. [[Principal photography]] was postponed due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Japan|COVID-19 pandemic]], leaving Yamazaki three years to work on the script. He took inspiration from previous ''Godzilla'' movies and the works of [[Hayao Miyazaki]] and [[Steven Spielberg]]. In February 2022, [[Robot Communications]] publicized that Yamazaki would soon direct an untitled ''kaiju'' movie. Filming occurred in [[Chūbu region|Chūbu]] and [[Kantō region|Kantō]] from March to June 2022 on a reputed {{USD|10 million|long=no}} budget. [[Shirogumi]]'s [[Chōfu]] studio spent eight months creating the visual effects. The film was revealed to be an installment in the ''Godzilla'' series in November 2022, and its title was announced in July 2023.

''Godzilla Minus One'' premiered at the [[Shinjuku Toho Building]] on October 18, 2023, and was released in Japan on November 3, to celebrate the franchise's 70th anniversary.{{efn|name=anniversary|The franchise's 70th anniversary falls on November 3, 2024. However, ''Godzilla Minus One'' was released one year prior due to Toho's contract with [[Legendary Entertainment]] that forbids them from releasing their ''Godzilla'' films in the same year as Legendary's [[MonsterVerse]] films.<ref name="Polygon trailer"/> Legendary's ''[[Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire]]'' was released on March 29, 2024.<ref name="Wingard"/>}} [[Toho International]] later released the film in North America on December 1. Despite its mixed reception from Japanese critics, the film earned widespread critical acclaim internationally. Many Western critics praised it as one of the best films of 2023 and among the best in the ''Godzilla'' franchise. The film grossed {{USD|116 million|long=no}} worldwide, becoming the [[List of Japanese films of 2023#Highest-grossing films|third-highest-grossing Japanese film of 2023]] and surpassing ''Shin Godzilla'' as the most successful Japanese ''Godzilla'' film. It also attained [[List of accolades received by Godzilla Minus One|numerous accolades]], including a leading 12 nominations at the [[47th Japan Academy Film Prize]] (winning eight) and winning [[Academy Award for Best Visual Effects|Best Visual Effects]] at the [[96th Academy Awards]]. A sequel, ''[[Godzilla Minus Zero]]'', is set for release in November 2026.

==Plot==
<!-- Per MOS:FILMPLOT, summaries should be no more than 400-700 words. The current summary is 699-->
In 1945, near the end of [[World War II]], ''[[kamikaze]]'' pilot Kōichi Shikishima lands his [[Mitsubishi A6M Zero]] for repairs at the Japanese base on [[Odo Island]]. Lead mechanic Sōsaku Tachibana deduces that Kōichi is feigning technical issues to flee from his duty. That night, [[Godzilla (Takashi Yamazaki)|Godzilla]], a large dinosaur-like creature, attacks the garrison. Tachibana tells Kōichi to fire at the monster with his plane's [[Type 99 cannon|cannons]], but he panics, fails to shoot, and is knocked unconscious. When he awakes the following day, he finds that the only other survivor is Tachibana, who is furious at him for failing to act.

The war now over, Kōichi returns home to find that his parents were killed in the [[bombing of Tokyo]]. Plagued by [[Survivor guilt|survivor's guilt]], he begins supporting a woman, Noriko Ōishi, whose parents also died in the bombing, and an orphaned baby, Akiko, whom Noriko rescued. He finds employment aboard a [[minesweeper]] tasked with disposing of wartime [[naval mines]]. Meanwhile, Godzilla is mutated and empowered by the United States' [[Operation Crossroads|nuclear tests]] at [[Bikini Atoll]]; it sinks the [[USS Redfish (SS-395)|USS ''Redfish'']] and destroys several other ships en route to Japan. Owing to [[Cold War|tensions]] with the [[Soviet Union]], the U.S. offers no help save for a few decommissioned [[Imperial Japanese Navy]] (IJN) vessels approved by [[General (United States)|General]] [[Douglas MacArthur]]. The Japanese government, concerned about inducing panic so soon after the war's end, does not notify the public about the danger.

In May 1947, Kōichi and his minesweeper crew travel to the [[Ogasawara Islands]] and are tasked with stalling Godzilla's approach to Japan. They release a mine into Godzilla's mouth and detonate it, causing significant damage, but Godzilla quickly regenerates. The [[heavy cruiser]] ''[[Japanese cruiser Takao (1930)|Takao]]'' then engages Godzilla, but is destroyed when the monster unleashes its heat ray.<!--The English-subbed print of the film itself refers to it as the "heat ray" not the atomic breath--> After returning to Tokyo, Kōichi opens up to Noriko about his encounters with Godzilla. Days later, Godzilla makes landfall in Japan and attacks [[Ginza]], where Noriko works. She narrowly survives the initial attack and reunites with Kōichi. Enraged by tank fire, Godzilla obliterates much of the district with its heat ray, killing tens of thousands. Noriko pushes Kōichi to safety, but is caught in the blast herself. A devastated Kōichi mourns her along with his fellow crewmates, vowing revenge.

Former naval engineer Kenji Noda, a crewmate, becomes frustrated by the government's inaction. He devises a plan to destroy Godzilla by luring it out to [[Sagami Bay]] before surrounding it with [[Freon]] tanks and rupturing them, sinking the monster, and letting the resultant water pressure crush it. Should the plan fail, balloons will be inflated under Godzilla to force it back up, killing it through [[explosive decompression]]. To enact his plan, Noda has recruited navy veterans to crew disarmed IJN [[destroyer]]s. Kōichi recruits Tachibana to repair a broken-down [[Kyushu J7W Shinden|Kyushu J7W ''Shinden'']] fighter. He plans to kill Godzilla in a suicide attack by flying into its mouth and detonating explosive charges on board. He leaves Akiko in the care of his neighbor, Sumiko, before Godzilla resurfaces.

As Kōichi lures Godzilla to the trap set by two destroyers, Sumiko receives a telegram intended for Kōichi. Godzilla survives the initial plunge and then breaks free before being forced back up, sustaining serious but nonfatal injuries from the resultant [[Decompression sickness|decompression]]-induced [[barotrauma]]. With the help of a fleet of [[tugboat]]s organized by Mizushima, another crewmate from the minesweeper, the ships haul Godzilla to the surface. Enraged, Godzilla prepares to destroy all the vessels with its heat ray, but Kōichi crashes the plane into Godzilla's mouth and destroys its head, causing the energy of the heat ray to tear its body apart. Having worried about his suicidal inclinations, the crew celebrates as Kōichi ejects before the explosion and parachutes to safety, using an [[ejection seat]] that Tachibana installed in the ''Shinden''; despite agreeing to work on the ''Shinden'' on the understanding that Kōichi would kill himself to atone for his failure on Odo Island, Tachibana eventually implored him to let go of his guilt and continue living.

Upon returning home, Sumiko gives Kōichi the telegram, which leads him to a hospital where he reunites with Noriko, who survived the destruction but has a black bruise creeping up her neck.{{efn|Director [[Takashi Yamazaki]] confirmed at Godzilla Fest Osaka in April 2024 that the black mark on Noriko's neck was caused by Godzilla's "cells".<ref name="Sponichi-Secret"/>}} Meanwhile, a chunk of Godzilla's flesh begins to regenerate as it sinks deeper into the ocean.

==Production==
===Background and development===
[[File:Yamazaki Takashi from "Godzilla Minus One" at Red Carpet of the Tokyo International Film Festival 2023 (53348355520) (cropped) (2).jpg|thumb|upright|alt=A photo of Takashi Yamazaki|Director, writer and [[visual effects supervisor]] [[Takashi Yamazaki]] in 2023.]]
[[Toho]] first approached then-emerging filmmaker [[Takashi Yamazaki]] around 2005, while he was making ''[[Always: Sunset on Third Street]]'', with an offer to direct a ''[[Godzilla (franchise)|Godzilla]]'' film. Yamazaki declined at the time, concluding that his [[Godzilla]] would need to be entirely made with [[computer-generated imagery]] (CGI), which was not yet feasible with the then-limited CGI technology available in Japan. Two years later, Yamazaki incorporated a dream-like sequence featuring Godzilla into ''[[Always: Sunset on Third Street 2]]'' (2007). ''Godzilla Minus One'' producer [[Kazuaki Kishida]] later explained that Toho kept Yamazaki in mind for a future ''Godzilla'' project, viewing it "not as a question of if but when to do it".{{sfn|Ryfle|Godziszewski|2025|p=409}}

Following the critical and commercial success of ''[[Shin Godzilla]]'' (2016) in Japan, Toho received numerous proposals for live-action follow-ups. According to producers [[Kenji Yamada (producer)|Kenji Yamada]] and Kishida, studio executives rejected these ideas, believing the next film needed to be entirely new and exceptional.{{sfn|Ryfle|Godziszewski|2025|p=409}}<ref name="RealSound-Dec"/> In 2017, ''Shin Godzilla'' co-director [[Shinji Higuchi]] stated that the company would not be able to produce another ''Godzilla'' film until after 2020. This is attributed to their contract with [[Legendary Entertainment|Legendary Pictures]]—who were producing the [[MonsterVerse]]—forbidding them from releasing their potential ''Godzilla'' films in the same year as Legendary's films.<ref name="Squires"/> The ensuing year, Toho executive Keiji Ōta imparted that ''Shin Godzilla'' would not receive a sequel and expressed interest in a potential [[shared universe]] ''Godzilla'' series akin to the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]].<ref name="Stephens"/>

In 2019, Toho began a "Godzilla Room" division, devoted to planning new ''Godzilla'' projects.<ref name="gqjapan"/> Following the completion of his film ''[[The Great War of Archimedes]]'' that same year, producer [[Minami Ichikawa]] appointed Yamazaki to make Toho's next ''Godzilla'' film.<ref name="gqjapan"/><ref name="Pressconference"/> Development officially began when Yamazaki submitted the film's plot in March 2019.<ref>{{Cite web |title=『ゴジラ‐1.0』全世界大ヒットの裏側 |trans-title=The story behind the worldwide box office success of ''Godzilla Minus One'' |url=https://www.saiyo-info.net/toho/godzilla/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250708085038/https://www.saiyo-info.net/toho/godzilla/ |archive-date=July 8, 2025 |access-date=April 21, 2026 |publisher=[[Toho]] |language=ja}}</ref> ''Godzilla Minus One'' ultimately became Yamazaki's third time working on a production utilizing Godzilla, as he also created the [[motion simulator]] attraction ''[[Godzilla the Ride: Giant Monsters Ultimate Battle]]'' (2021) for [[Seibu-en]] during pre-production.<ref name="RealSound-Dec"/>

On February 18, 2022, [[Robot Communications]] announced the film under the working title {{Nihongo|''Blockbuster Monster Movie''|超大作怪獣映画|Chōtaisaku Kaijū Eiga}}, via a casting call on its official website.<ref name="Robot"/><ref name="HuffPost"/> Robot stated Yamazaki would direct and that Toho would present the film.<ref name="Robot"/> The next day, ''[[HuffPost]]'' writer Kenji Ando mentioned that fans on social media were conjecturing that it would be a remake of the [[Godzilla (1954 film)|original ''Godzilla'' film]], and cited Yamazaki's comments from an interview regarding his depiction of Godzilla in ''Always: Sunset on Third Street 2'': "You can't have Godzilla unless it's [set in] the [[Shōwa era|''Shōwa'' era]]".<ref name="HuffPost"/>

Yamazaki's unnamed ''[[kaiju]]'' project was declared to be a ''Godzilla'' film on November 3, 2022, at an event hosted by Toho celebrating the franchise's 68th anniversary, known as "Godzilla Day". The company also reported that the film had completed filming and had entered post-production with a targeted release date of November 3, 2023. Yamazaki was named the film's writer and [[visual effects supervisor]].<ref name="King"/> On December 13, 2022, Toho's head of planning, Hisashi Usui, implied that the movie is connected to ''Godzilla'' (1954).<ref name="NikkanSports-Dec2022"/>

===Writing and influences===
The script initially took a year to develop based on an outline from March 2019. However, the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Japan|COVID-19 pandemic]] forced the crew to postpone filming for a few years,<ref name="gqjapan"/><ref name="Natalie-Interview"/> resulting in work on the screenplay taking roughly three years.<ref name="Pressconference"/> The worldwide anxiety and perceived government unreliability during the pandemic became one of his major inspirations for the story<ref name="Aera1"/><ref name="Pulliam-Moore"/>{{sfn|Kodansha|2023|pp=32–33}} and Yamazaki hoped these events would be reflected clearly in the finished film.<ref name="Aera1"/> He steered clear of setting the movie in modern Japan and having to draw inspiration from the [[2011 Fukushima nuclear disaster]] as he believed it would become too similar to ''Shin Godzilla'' (2016).<ref name="Natalie-Interview"/><ref name="Aera1"/><ref name="JP-IGN"/> Instead, Yamazaki decided to set ''Godzilla Minus One'' in postwar Japan, having already depicted that period in a handful of his previous films.<ref name="Natalie-Interview"/> He also took this opportunity to include the heavy cruiser ''Takao'', ''Shinden'' fighter, destroyers ''Yukikaze'' and ''[[Japanese destroyer Hibiki (1932)|Hibiki]]'' because he was a fan of military history and had never depicted them before.{{sfn|Kodansha|2023|pp=32–33}}

Yamazaki was inspired by [[Shusuke Kaneko]]'s ''[[Godzilla, Mothra and King Ghidorah: Giant Monsters All-Out Attack]]'' (2001)—which he has cited as one of his favorite ''Godzilla'' films<ref name="scifijapan2"/>—while writing the screenplay for ''Godzilla Minus One''.<ref name="Kubota"/> He reflected in a discussion with Kaneko: "I had forgotten the contents of ''GMK'' for a while, but it seems like I self-consciously thought about it when writing the scenario for [''Godzilla Minus One'']. Without realizing it, I was under considerable influence".<ref name="Kubota"/> ''Godzilla Minus One'' was also heavily influenced by the 1954 film,<ref name="G54"/><ref name="Aera1"/><ref name="Reyes"/> ''Shin Godzilla'' (2016),<ref name="Reyes"/> [[Steven Spielberg]]'s ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]'' (1975)<ref name="Natalie-Interview"/><ref name="AVClub"/><ref name="IGN"/> and ''[[War of the Worlds (2005 film)|War of the Worlds]]'' (2005),<ref name="AVClub"/> the films of [[Hayao Miyazaki]],<ref name="CinemaToday-Nov" /> and [[Bong Joon Ho]]'s ''[[The Host (2006 film)|The Host]]'' (2006).<ref>{{Cite web |last=村上幸将 |title=山崎貴監督、オスカー獲得「ゴジラ－１.０」はポン・ジュノ監督「漢江の怪物」に学んだと明かす - シネマ : 日刊スポーツ |url=https://www.nikkansports.com/entertainment/news/202503260001443.html |access-date=2025-03-26 |website=nikkansports.com |language=ja |archive-date=March 26, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250326122458/https://www.nikkansports.com/entertainment/news/202503260001443.html |url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[Godzilla (2014 film)|Godzilla]]'' (2014) director [[Gareth Edwards (filmmaker)|Gareth Edwards]] identified Spielberg's films ''[[Close Encounters of the Third Kind]]'' (1977) and ''[[Jurassic Park (film)|Jurassic Park]]'' (1993), and [[Christopher Nolan]]'s ''[[Dunkirk (2017 film)|Dunkirk]]'' (2017) as other evident influences on the film.<ref name="CinemaToday-Nov"/>

A [[novelization]] of the film, written by Yamazaki, was published in Japan by [[Shueisha]] on November 8, 2023.<ref name="Novel"/> The novel features a scene set on Odo Island that Yamazaki proposed for the film but was left unfilmed since Toho refused to allow him to do [[Pick-up (filmmaking)|pick-ups]].<ref name="Weintraub"/>

===Casting===
[[File:Ando Sakura at Opening Ceremony of the Tokyo International Film Festival 2017 (28423646269).jpg|thumb|upright|left|alt=A photograph of Sakura Ando in 2017|[[Sakura Ando]] (pictured in 2017) performed her scenes for the film and [[Hirokazu Kore-eda]]'s ''[[Monster (2023 Japanese film)|Monster]]'' simultaneously.]]
Yamazaki sought to cast talented individuals who were able to give convincing performances of people living during the Shōwa period and could make Godzilla's presence in the film seem more realistic.{{sfn|Kodansha|2023|pp=32–33}}{{Sfn|''Figure King''|2023|pp=18–21}} His casting decisions were influenced little by the previous ''Godzilla'' films, since this movie was about the lives of ordinary Japanese in the 1940s rather than politicians, bureaucrats, scientists, and the [[Japan Self-Defense Forces|Self-Defense Forces]]. The reason for this was that Yamazaki wanted audiences to empathize with and connect with the characters despite the post-war setting.{{Sfn|Toho Stella|2023|loc=Production Notes}}

During preproduction, producer Minami Ichikawa offered [[Ryunosuke Kamiki]] and [[Minami Hamabe]] to play the film's leads, Kōichi Shikishima and Noriko Ōishi, prior to them playing similar roles in the [[NHK]] drama series ''[[Ranman (TV series)|Ranman]]'' (2023).<ref name="Pressconference"/> According to Yamazaki, the media criticized this casting, believing it would be too similar to their roles in ''Ranman'', when it was disclosed the two would star in the film at a press conference on September 4, 2023.{{sfn|''Figure King''|2023|pp=18–21}} Yamazaki also revealed that he cast [[Kuranosuke Sasaki]] as Captain Seiji Akitsu because of his performances in ''[[Asadora]]'' such as ''[[Hiyokko]]'' (2017), which had a major influence on him.{{sfn|''Figure King''|2023|pp=18–21}} One of the producers approached [[Sakura Ando]] about playing the role of Shikishima's neighbor before the COVID-19 outbreak, but she had to wait several years to play the role. However, when she finally got the chance to act, the producer suggested that she choose between playing in the film or [[Hirokazu Kore-eda]]'s ''[[Monster (2023 Japanese film)|Monster]]'' since they were being filmed simultaneously. Refusing to make an appearance in just one of the films, Ando said she "fought for it and in the end [...] got to be in both".<ref name="Ando"/>

Ōishi and Shikishima's adopted daughter Akiko was originally planned to be a boy. After meeting two-year-old [[Child actor|child actress]] Sae Nagatani, however, Yamazaki decided to change this in order for her to play the role. When questioned how he managed to get Nagatani to cry for some scenes, the director responded "I found a genius".<ref name="Akiko"/>

===Creature design===
{{Further|Godzilla (Takashi Yamazaki)#Design}}
The design of Godzilla in ''Minus One'' is a variation of the one in ''Godzilla the Ride''.<ref name="Aera1"/>{{rp|3}}<ref name="gqjapan2"/> Inspired by the ''Godzilla, Mothra and King Ghidorah: Giant Monsters All-Out Attack'' design, Yamazaki initially envisioned his design having "half-moon shaped eyes", but modeling head Kosuke Taguchi gave it "almond-shaped" ones instead, with the final design having "golden, almond-shaped eyes".<ref name="gqjapan2"/> Yamazaki also elaborated that since the crew created this Godzilla in digital form: "it allowed for much more detail than what was possible with any type of handcrafted version. So we were able to increase the resolution of the scales, for example, and make them feel really, really sharp and give it this aggressive texture. And in terms of the bottom half of Godzilla, we made it feel very heavy and dense in a way that made the viewer feel like this mountain and triangular silhouette was walking and moving through a space."<ref name="IndieWire"/>

Yamazaki attempted to make this Godzilla the most horrifying version yet.<ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="Roeloffs"/> The crew designed Godzilla to be ferocious, violent, and dynamic, with a static, god-like aspect. Its dorsal fins were made more "spiky and ferocious" than the incarnation in ''Godzilla the Ride'', as if its regenerative energy had become disorderly. Yamazaki stated that the team also tried to make Godzilla the "deadliest in history" adding that it is "discerning today, experiencing the freshness and fear felt by audiences at the time".<ref name="gqjapan2"/>

===Filming===
[[File:震電（実物大模型）.jpg|thumb|alt=A photo of the Kyushu J7W Shinden replica inside a museum|The replica of the [[Kyushu J7W Shinden|Kyushu J7W ''Shinden'']] used in the film on display at the [[Tachiarai Peace Memorial Museum]]]]
[[Principal photography]] took place on location in the [[Chūbu region|Chūbu]] (in the [[Aichi Prefecture|Aichi]] and [[Nagano Prefecture|Nagano]] prefectures) and [[Kantō region|Kantō]] regions of [[Honshu]], starting on March 13, 2022, and [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapping]] on June 20.<ref name="Robot"/><ref name="HuffPost"/> Robot declared in February that, due to the film's post-war Japan setting, there would be restrictions on the extras' costume sizes, hairstyles, and hair colors (with hair dyeing being prohibited) for extras participating in filming.<ref name="Robot"/> The maritime sequences were filmed at [[Lake Hamana]] and in the [[Enshū Sea]]. Between April and June 2022, several community businesses near the [[Tenryū River]] helped the crew modify and maintain boats to shoot navy scenes in Enshū.<ref name="Ohira"/> Other shooting locations included the City Hall in [[Okaya, Nagano]],<ref name="FNN"/> the Tsukuba Naval Air Group Memorial Museum in [[Kasama, Ibaraki|Kasama]], the Former Kashima Naval Air Base Site in [[Miho, Ibaraki|Miho]], and the Shimodate General Sports Park in [[Chikusei]].<ref name="Ibaraki"/>

Scenes featuring the Kyushu J7W ''Shinden'' were partly realized through the construction of a 1:1 scale replica of the aircraft,<ref name="nishinippon"/> of which only a single example exists and is located outside Japan in the collection of the [[Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center]] in [[Chantilly, Virginia]].<ref name="IndieWire"/> Yamazaki noted that "Initially, the budget didn't allow for any portion of the [airplane] to be built" but "thinking outside the box, having a plan B, we were able to find a museum that was willing to purchase the prop after the film was made, which offset the production budget it would have taken to produce the plane in the first place".<ref name="IndieWire"/> Thus, following the completion of shooting, the replica was transported to and put on display at the [[Tachiarai Peace Memorial Museum]] in [[Chikuzen, Fukuoka]] in July 2022.<ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="Asahi-Shinden"/> Toho donated the replica under anonymity, only revealing their involvement in the construction of the model after ''Godzilla Minus One'' released.<ref name="nishinippon"/>

===Post-production===

====Visual effects====
All 610 of the film's visual effect shots were handled by a crew of 35 artists{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Nikkei"/><ref name="Weintraub"/><ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="Schilling-March"/><ref name="THR"/><ref name="IGN-Interview"/><ref name="Aera2"/>}} at [[Shirogumi]]'s [[Chōfu]] studio,<ref name="cgworld"/>{{Sfn|TV Shinshu|2023|loc=14:40}} under the supervision of Yamazaki and direction of [[Kiyoko Shibuya]].{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="Schilling-March"/><ref name="latimes"/><ref name="aframe"/><ref name="Donohoo"/><ref name="Godzilla.com2"/>}} According to the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', between a quarter and a third of the film's budget was spent on visual effects.<ref name="latimes"/>

Eight months were spent on creating the visual effects.<ref name="Weintraub"/> A [[TV Shinshu]] special about Yamazaki released in October 2023 indicated that the team began creating the effects for the film in July 2022.{{Sfn|TV Shinshu|2023|loc=14:40}} Shirogumi indicated by opening a recruitment call for visual effects designers and compositors in August 2022, that post-production had begun and visual effects were taking place from that same month until January 2023;<ref name="Shirogumi"/> they later changed the dates to between November 2022 and February 2023.<ref name="Shirogumi2"/> Their website named the 3D animation software [[Houdini (software)|Houdini]] and [[Autodesk Maya|Maya]] for design and [[Nuke (software)|Nuke]] for compositing.<ref name="Shirogumi"/><ref name="Shirogumi2"/> Yamazaki had made a 3D [[maquette]] design on [[ZBrush]], with Taguchi augmenting the design by adding his own elements, including the insertion of polygons and rendering displacement maps using [[Redshift (renderer)|Redshift]]. Then, the team [[Retopology|retopologized]] the maquette design and finalized the displacement maps with [[Autodesk Mudbox|Mudbox]].<ref name="Maxon"/> After the visual effects were finished, post-production concluded in late May 2023.<ref name="Natalie-Sep"/>

The ocean sequences were originally not intended to be as sizable in the film until compositor [[Tatsuji Nojima]], who composes computer-generated water at home as a hobby, presented Yamazaki with some of his water simulations, inspiring the director to rewrite its climax and include more scenes set at sea.<ref name="IndieWire"/> The team strived to create these sequences, especially Godzilla's destruction scene.<ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="cgworld"/> Yamazaki reflected that "It put a huge strain on all of our rendering engines, so we created so much data in the process that when we added it all up it was easily over a petabyte. In the end, we erased the data from the scene where it was done, and made it while opening the hard disk."<ref name="IndieWire"/>

Yamazaki informed Shinji Higuchi that the film's destruction sequences and on-screen deaths were inspired by the Shibuya sequence from ''[[Gamera 3: Revenge of Iris]]'', in which Higuchi directed the effects.<ref name="Mibu"/> Some of the characters present on-screen during Godzilla's rampage were created using Houdini; around 60 extras were 3D scanned to be replaced by a digital duplicate.<ref name="cgworld"/> Yamazaki also paid homage to previous ''Godzilla'' films by not using any "muscle simulation" for the monster<ref name="aframe"/> and employed miniatures to depict the post-war Tokyo townscape, which is a traditional Japanese special effect (or ''[[tokusatsu]]'') technique.<ref name="NTV"/>

====Music and sound effects====
{{Main|Godzilla Minus One (soundtrack){{!}}''Godzilla Minus One'' (soundtrack)}}

Yamazaki's frequent collaborator [[Naoki Satō]] scored the film, drawing inspiration from [[Studio Ghibli]]'s anime movies for the poignant scenes and the music of [[Akira Ifukube]] to accentuate the ''kaiju'' sequences.<ref name="WaxworkRecords"/> {{Ill|Rambling Records|ja|ランブリング・レコーズ}} released ''[[Godzilla Minus One (soundtrack)|Godzilla Minus One Original Soundtrack]]'', on CD in Japan on October 28, 2023, with a limited edition [[Phonograph record|vinyl]] following on November 24.<ref name="Oct2023Natalie"/> On January 19, 2024, Toho announced that [[Waxwork Records]] would release the score on vinyl overseas, with pre-ordering starting that same day.<ref name="Jan2024Godzilla"/>

Natsuko Inoue handled the sound effects. She felt it was her mission to recreate the original Godzilla's roar using a modern sound system. Having tried many methods to keep the sound intact, Inoue decided that it wasn't strong enough, so she opted to record outdoors and use the echoes to enhance it. She decided to play the roar at the [[Zozo Marine Stadium|ZOZO Marine Stadium]] to create the fresh sound effect she desired, believing it was the only stadium that could meet the requirements they needed as it had huge speakers, no ceiling, was spacious, and was slightly sloped. Reminiscing on enhancing the roar at the stadium, Inoue said "I'll never forget the emotion I felt when I played it from the biggest speaker behind the electronic bulletin board"; Yamazaki recalled, "I felt a shiver in the pit of my stomach when I thought that people who actually saw Godzilla would hear this sound."<ref name="Roar"/> After the crew played the sound at the stadium, they received several complaints from nearby residents.<ref name="aframe"/>

Producer Gō Abe stated that sound effects from the {{Ill|Ichibata Dehani 50 series|ja|一畑電気鉄道デハニ50形電車}} were utilized for the scene where Godzilla attacks a [[63 series]] train, as the crew sought to enhance the postwar setting through practical sounds.<ref name="sanin-chuo"/>

====Black-and-white edition====
During post-production, colorist Masahiro Ishiyama was assigned to create a black-and-white version of the film, titled {{nihongo|''Godzilla Minus One/Minus Color''|ゴジラ{{ruby-ja|-1.0|マイナスワン}}／{{ruby-ja|C|マイナスカラー}}|Gojira Mainasu Wan/Mainasu Karā}}.<ref name="IGN-Interview"/> Yamazaki proposed that Godzilla's atomic breath should remain in color for the black-and-white edition, similar to how [[Akira Kurosawa]]'s black-and-white [[crime film]] ''[[High and Low (1963 film)|High and Low]]'' (1963) features colored smoke in one scene. However, this concept was rejected by the rest of the crew.<ref name="IGN-Interview"/> In regards to the ''Minus Color'' version, the director said in a statement: "Rather than just making it [[monochrome]], it is a cut-by-cut. I had them make adjustments while making full use of various mattes as if they were creating a new movie."<ref name="BD"/><ref name="Fangoria"/> This version was also the last credit for producer [[Shūji Abe]],<ref name="AbeEiga"/><ref name="AbeMWP"/> who died on December 11, 2023;<ref name="Abe"/> Yamazaki and the visual effects team paid tribute to Abe in their [[Oscar speech|acceptance speech]] at the [[96th Academy Awards]].<ref name="abcnews"/>

Toho released ''Minus Color'' in Japanese theaters on January 12, 2024,<ref name="BD"/><ref name="Fangoria"/> and in the United States on January 26, where it played until February 1.<ref name="Godzilla.com2"/><ref name="Deadline1"/>

==Thematic analysis==
''Godzilla Minus One'' addresses many themes derived from its post-war setting, including [[Anti-nuclear movement|anti-nuclear]],<ref name="Sakigake"/> [[Anti-war film|anti-war]],<ref name="Sakigake"/> trauma,<ref name="Escapist"/> hope,<ref name="Escapist"/> guilt,<ref name="Slankard"/> and redemption.<ref name="Slankard"/> According to Yamazaki, Godzilla symbolizes the Japanese perspective of [[nuclear holocaust]] during that period, akin to the original 1954 ''Godzilla'' film.{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Aera1"/><ref name="G54"/><ref name="IndieWire"/><ref name="Sakigake"/><ref name="AP-Nov"/>}} He also remarked in regards to the monster's portrayal: "There is a concept in Japan called {{nihongo foot|''Tatari-gami'';|タタリ神|extra2={{Lit.|cursing gods}}|group=lower-alpha|ref_name=Tatari-gami}} there are good gods, and there are bad gods. Godzilla is half-monster, but it's also half-god".<ref name="AP-Nov"/>

Esther Zuckerman, writing for ''[[The New York Times]]'', noticed that the film is similar to Christopher Nolan's ''[[Oppenheimer (film)|Oppenheimer]]'' and Hayao Miyazaki's ''[[The Boy and the Heron]]'', which were also released in 2023 and set during and after World War II. She felt that—although the film and ''The Boy and the Heron'' never directly address the [[atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki]]—they provided the Japanese point of view of this historical event which viewers of ''Oppenheimer'' would subsequently want to see.<ref name="Zuckerman"/> The thematic similitudes shared between ''Minus One'' and ''Oppenheimer'' were deemed "striking" by Yamazaki. He conveyed how both were released amidst increasing global tensions: "I think the threat of nuclear warfare is almost at its highest right now in recent years than in any other year that most of us have been alive".<ref name="MovieMaker"/>

''[[The Austin Chronicle]]'' pointed out that the film's theme of having the protagonist decide whether to persist in living or accept death parallels Akira Kurosawa's ''[[Ikiru]]'' (1952).<ref name="AustinChronicle"/> The ''[[South China Morning Post]]''{{'}}s James Marsh asserted that the characters in ''Minus One'' unanimously condemn their government for persuading many to take their lives during World War II; regardless, some reviewers accused the movie of "pushing a pro-military agenda".<ref name="SCMP"/>

==Release==
===Marketing and promotion===
On June 12, 2023, the film's [[Twitter]] account began a daily countdown for all of Toho's live-action ''Godzilla'' films, starting with ''Shin Godzilla (2016)''.<ref name="Irikura"/> On July 11, Toho divulged the title of its secret ''Godzilla'' film, ''Godzilla Minus One'', alongside a poster featuring a visual of Godzilla primarily created by Yamazaki, teaser trailer, the U.S. release date, and a statement from the director.<ref name="Murakami"/> In the ensuing days, merchandise and a full-body shot of Godzilla were unveiled.<ref name="Oricon"/><ref name="Dawe"/> A series of pre-release products and an exhibit promoting the film were at a display in Yamazaki's hometown of [[Matsumoto, Nagano|Matsumoto]] in Nagano, from July 15 to October 29.<ref name="Oricon"/><ref name="Exhibition"/>

Toho released the trailer for ''Godzilla Minus One'' alongside the poster and details on the central cast and staff members on September 4.<ref name="Natalie"/> During a press conference on that same day, the director clarified that its title has multiple meanings, explicitly referring to how Godzilla's destruction changed Japan's position from a "post-war zero situation" to a "minus". When explaining other possible reasons for the title, Yamazaki said that the film takes place before the 1954 film and that it emphasizes the theme of loss throughout.<ref name="Pressconference"/> On September 14, 15 shots and a visual of Godzilla from the film were released, followed by ticket sales and flyers for its Japanese release.<ref name="Sep2023Eiga"/> That same day, ''SciFi Japan'' reported that ''Godzilla Minus One'' had remained the top trending film on social media sites in Japan and the U.S., with the trailer accumulating over 9 million views on [[YouTube]].<ref name="Sep2024SciFiJapan"/> In September 25, the mayor of [[Hamamatsu]] announced that they would promote the movie by making the nearby Lake Hamana—where some scenes were shot—a tourist attraction starting the next month.<ref name="Ohira"/> Television stations across Japan began airing a special featuring behind-the-scenes footage and interviews with Yamazaki, Kamiki, and Hamabe in late October.<ref name="BTS"/>

The premiere took place at [[Toho Cinemas]]' theater inside the [[Shinjuku Toho Building]] on October 18, 2023.<ref name="Eiga.com-Premiere"/><ref name="Suzuki"/> That same day, Yamazaki and the headliners attended the [[red carpet]] along [[Godzilla Street]] in [[Kabukichō]], [[Shinjuku]]. This carpet was 50.1 meters long, proclaimed as the fictitious height of the film's titular monster.<ref name="Eiga.com-Premiere"/><ref name="Suzuki"/> In addition, the "Godzilla Attack Truck" debuted there, and would later travel around Japan to promote the movie.<ref name="Eiga.com-Premiere"/> On October 23, Yamazaki, Kamiki, and Hamabe attended the red carpet at the opening of the [[36th Tokyo International Film Festival]],<ref name="RedCarpetTIFF"/> and [[soft drink]] manufacture [[Cheerio (company)|Cheerio]] began presales for a new ''[[Chūhai]]'' drink called the "Godzilla Energy {{Sic|Chu-hi|nolink=y}}".<ref name="Chu-hi"/>

===Box office===
''Godzilla Minus One'' dethroned ''Shin Godzilla'' (2016) as the highest-grossing Japanese ''Godzilla'' film ever on December 29, 2023.<ref name="Klein"/><ref name="Bolt"/> In January 2024, Toho's CEO, [[Hiroyasu Matsuoka]], stated that it exceeded the company's expectations at the worldwide box office and helped their yearly theatrical income exceed {{JPY|100 billion}} ({{USD|680 million|long=no}}) for the first time. He also felt the film "benefited from less competition on release due to the [[2023 Hollywood labor disputes|strike in Hollywood]]".<ref name="JapaneseBoxOffice"/> According to [[Box Office Mojo]], the film earned a worldwide total of {{USD|113.6 million|long=no}} with {{USD|57.1 million|long=no}} in the United States and Canada, and {{USD|56.9 million|long=no}} in other territories. However, the site ceased updating the film's Japanese box office after its 25th weekend (April 20-21, 2024), by which it had registered that the film had grossed {{USD|48.2 million|long=no}} there.<ref name="BOM-Japan"/>{{efn|{{Ill|Kogyo Tsushinsha|ja|興行通信社}} documented that ''Godzilla Minus One'' was still playing in Japanese theaters as of June 23, 2024.<ref name="KogyoTsushinsha"/>}} In July 2024, ''[[The Japan Times]]'' reported that ''Godzilla Minus One'' grossed {{USD|116 million|long=no}} internationally.<ref name="Schilling-July"/>

====Japan====
''Godzilla Minus One'' was the closing film at the 36th Tokyo International Film Festival on November 1, where it was shown with English subtitles.<ref name="TIFF"/> To celebrate the franchise's 70th anniversary,{{efn|name=anniversary}} it was released nationwide in Japan on November 3, the same date as the first ''Godzilla'' film's [[wide release]] in 1954.<ref name="NikkanSports-Dec2022"/><ref name="Roeloffs"/> In September, Toho stated in a press release that the film would be shown in over 500 theaters nationwide—including in [[IMAX]], [[Dolby Cinema]], [[4DX]], [[MX4D]], and [[ScreenX]] formats—making it one of their largest domestic distributions to date.<ref name="Natalie"/><ref name="scifijapan1"/> It grossed more than {{JPY|1 billion}} (approximately {{USD|6.6 million|long=no}}) and sold 650,000 tickets during its initial three days.<ref name="Inoue"/> This included {{USD|1.2 million|long=no}} from 49 IMAX theaters, making it the largest opening for a live-action Japanese film in the format.<ref name="Azeem"/>

The film remained the [[List of 2023 box office number-one films in Japan|number one]] film for three consecutive weekends,<ref name="gross2"/> but was overtaken by ''[[Fly Me to the Saitama#Sequel|Tonde Saitama ~Biwako Yori Ai o Komete~]]'' in its fourth weekend.<ref name="Hazra"/> In January 2024, the {{Ill|Motion Picture Producers Association of Japan|ja|日本映画製作者連盟}} reported that ''Godzilla Minus One'' was the [[List of Japanese films of 2023#Highest-grossing films|fifth-highest-grossing Japanese film of 2023]].<ref name="Eiren"/> It eventually became the highest-grossing live-action film to debut in Japan during 2023<ref name="Mar2024CinemaToday"/> and the 94th [[List of highest-grossing films in Japan|highest-grossing movie ever at the Japanese box office]].<ref name="KogyoTsushinsha"/>

The film was met with a mixed critical reception in its native Japan.<ref name="Gendai"/><ref name="Anais"/><ref name="Ishii"/> Tokyo-based film critic and journalist [[Mark Schilling]] wrote that Japanese critics frequently rebuke Yamazaki's body of work, partly because "most are left-leaning" and view some of them, including the [[War film|war drama]] film ''[[The Eternal Zero (film)|The Eternal Zero]]'' (2013), as "nationalistic if not outright [[jingoistic]]".<ref name="Schilling-March"/> Schilling further stated that ''Godzilla Minus One'' had an "element of soft nationalism",<ref name="Schilling-July"/> as well as quoting essayist and film historian [[Inuhiko Yomota]] who said that it was a "dangerous movie".<ref name="Schilling-March"/>

Schilling appreciated the film's post-war setting for allowing the characters to defeat Godzilla using technology from that time, rather than "digitally generated miracles".<ref name="Schilling-July"/> Daisuke Satō of ''[[IGN#Regional websites|IGN Japan]]'' and Kazuo Ozaki cited Yamazaki's direction and visual effects as incomparable to his previous movies.<ref name="JP-IGN"/><ref name="Ozaki"/> Satō believed that the characters besides Kamiki's had "stereotypical Shōwa values", and subsequently perceived that Noriko and Sumiko were depicted in a sexist manner.<ref name="JP-IGN"/> The dialogue was "eye-roll-inducing stuff" for Matt Schley of ''The Japan Times''.<ref name="Schley"/>

''{{Ill|Cinema Today|ja|シネマトゥデイ}}'' later named ''Godzilla Minus One'' the best movie released in Japan during 2023 on their "Top 20 Movies" list, noting how it "has shown the world the potential of Japanese cinema".<ref name="Top20"/> Contrarily, the Japanese film magazine ''{{Ill|Eiga Geijutsu|ja|映画芸術}}'' ranked it the third worst on their list of "The Ten Best and Ten Worst Japanese Films of 2023".<ref name="Natalie-Jan"/>

====International reviews====
{{Metacritic breakdown|positive=33|mixed=1|negative=0}}
Internationally, ''Godzilla Minus One'' garnered widespread critical acclaim{{efn-lr|name=acclaim|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Roeloffs"/><ref name="Schilling-March"/><ref name="Godzilla.com2"/><ref name="THR Critics"/><ref name="BBC"/><ref name="CBR"/><ref name="Deadline2"/><ref name="Hypergalactic"/>}} and was listed among the top films of 2023.{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="CBR"/><ref name="50Best"/><ref name="CriticsTopTen"/><ref name="Quad-CityTimes"/><ref name="30Best"/><ref name="TimeOut"/><ref name="RT2023Best"/><ref name="Whittaker"/>}} According to ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'', American critics favored the film over recent Hollywood productions, lauding its drama, low-budgeted visual effects, and usage of "''kaiju'' as a metaphor for social critique".<ref name="THR Critics"/> {{Rotten Tomatoes prose|score=99|count=213|average=8.5|consensus=With engaging human stories anchoring the action, ''Godzilla Minus One'' is one kaiju movie that remains truly compelling between the scenes of mass destruction.|ref=yes |access-date=December 22, 2023}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 81 out of 100, based on 34 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Godzilla Minus One'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/godzilla-minus-one |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=December 22, 2023 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> American audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, and polls by [[PostTrak]] gave it a 92% overall positive score, with 83% recommending it.<ref name="opening2"/>

[[James Berardinelli]] and the ''[[Daily Express]]'', among others, praised ''Minus One'' as one of the best, if not ''the'' best, in the ''Godzilla'' franchise.{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="BBC"/><ref name="CBR"/><ref name="ReelViews"/><ref name="Express"/><ref name="Chosun"/>}} A few critics also felt it achieves contemporizing the original 1954 film.<ref name="Roeloffs"/> ''Deadline Hollywood'' described it as "a Godzilla for the ages, a Godzilla [[art film]]".<ref name="DeadlineInternationalFilms"/> ''[[Screen International]]'' and ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' found Yamazaki's depiction of Godzilla in the film to be frightening.<ref name="Grierson"/><ref name="Semlyen"/> ''IGN'' said that it was "more swell" albeit not as terrifying as ''Shin Godzilla''.<ref name="IGNReview"/> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', ''[[The Washington Post]]'', and ''Deadline'' concurred that one of the film's highlights was its largely emotionally-driven storyline.<ref name="WashingtonPost"/><ref name="DeadlineInternationalFilms"/><ref name="Variety"/> ''The Washington Post'' compared it favorably to ''[[Top Gun: Maverick]]'' (2022), commenting that these were reminders of the importance of movies that combine "concise and creative action with emotionally resonant characters".<ref name="WashingtonPost"/> ''Screen International'' and ''The Washington Post'' also wrote that Satō's score was "booming".<ref name="WashingtonPost"/><ref name="Grierson"/>

Many reviewers complimented the characters.{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Roeloffs"/><ref name="Anais"/><ref name="THR Critics"/><ref name="ReelViews"/><ref name="Express"/><ref name="DeadlineInternationalFilms"/><ref name="WashingtonPost"/><ref name="Variety"/><ref name="Slate"/>}} ''Deadline Hollywood'' and the ''Daily Express'' agreed that they were fully developed.<ref name="Express"/><ref name="DeadlineInternationalFilms"/> According to [[Dana Stevens (critic)|Dana Stevens]], "Kamiki's anguished, vulnerable performance is one crucial part of what makes this protagonist so memorable".<ref name="Slate"/> In contrast, ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' was critical of the supporting cast, singling out the performances of Hidetaka Yoshioka and Munetaka Aoki.<ref name="RogerEbert.com"/>

===Industry response===
Industry figures largely praised ''Godzilla Minus One''.<ref name="Donohoo"/><ref name="Pegg"/> Writing for ''Deadline Hollywood'', Pete Hammond stated that the film "astounded" Hollywood and the filmmakers' use of their low budget impressed the Visual Effects Governors.<ref name="Deadline2"/> Many Hollywood insiders were also stunned by Nagatani's performance as Akiko and questioned the crew "how did you find that child actor?", according to producer [[Kazuaki Kishida]].<ref name="Akiko"/>

[[Hideaki Anno]], co-director of ''Shin Godzilla'', felt that the film's technical prowess exceeded Yamazaki's prior efforts.<ref name="Anno"/> ''Godzilla'' (2014) director Gareth Edwards admitted to feeling "jealous", adding that "this is what a ''Godzilla'' movie should be".<ref name="Schilling-March"/><ref name="IGN"/> At the film's American premiere, actor [[Seth Green]] and ''[[Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019 film)|Godzilla: King of the Monsters]]'' (2019) director [[Michael Dougherty]] voiced their praise to Yamazaki and Kamiki.<ref name="Dougherty"/> Filmmakers [[Adam Wingard]] (director of ''[[Godzilla vs. Kong]]'' and ''Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire''), [[Joe Dante]], [[James Ponsoldt]], and [[Juel Taylor]] listed the film amongst their favorite films of 2023.<ref name="DirectorsPick"/> Several filmmakers, including [[John Landis]], spoke to Yamazaki and three other members of the visual effects crew while they were at the [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]] on January 13, 2024, expressing that they believe the movie is the best of 2023.<ref name="Jan2024CinemaToday"/> Yamazaki met Steven Spielberg, who was a significant influence on the film<ref name="IGN"/>—at the 2024 Oscar Nominees Luncheon. According to ''[[The A.V. Club]]'', Spielberg was "obsessed" with the film and told Yamazaki: "I saw it once in my home, and then I had to go see it again in IMAX, then Dolby Atmos".<ref name="AVClub"/> ''Oppenheimer'' director Christopher Nolan praised the film, saying it had much in common with Yamazaki's 2013 film ''The Eternal Zero'' and offered deep insights into its main characters. ===Controversies===
{{Quote box
| quote = I want to say in Toho's defense, the budget was less than {{USD|15 million|long=no}}, but that's a pretty high-budget film for Japanese production standards.
| author = — Takashi Yamazaki
| width = 25%
| source = ''[[MovieMaker]]'' (January 2024)<ref name="MovieMaker"/>
}}
Upon the film's release, its budget became a topic of wide discussion.<ref name="MovieMaker"/> ''[[Boston.com]]'' and [[LaSalle Ishii]] noted that, despite a relatively meager budget, many viewers felt the film delivered visual effects that were superior in quality compared to many of [[Marvel Studios]]' {{USD|200 million|long=no}} productions.<ref name="Slane"/><ref name="Dec2024NikkanGendai"/> It reportedly had a budget under 10% of that held by the previous ''Godzilla'' film, ''Godzilla vs. Kong'' (2021), produced by American studio Legendary Entertainment.{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Vulture"/><ref name="Nikkei"/><ref name="IGNReview"/><ref name="Slate"/>}} On November 14, 2023, Yamazaki denied that the movie cost {{JPY|1 billion}}, stating that its budget was higher.<ref name="Nov2023Sponichi"/> Starting that same month, multiple websites—including ''Variety'', ''IGN'', ''[[The Times]]'', ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'', and ''The Hollywood Reporter''—asserted that the film's budget was {{USD|15 million|long=no}}{{efn-lr|name=15million}} (roughly {{JPY|2.2 billion}}).<ref name="Roeloffs"/><ref name="Anais"/>{{rp|2}}<ref name="Dec2024NikkanGendai"/> However, Yamazaki also denied this figure, and specified that the film cost less.<ref name="MovieMaker"/> The ''[[Hindustan Times]]'' reported the budget to be {{USD|10 million|long=no}},<ref name="Upadhayay"/> and Yamazaki later confirmed that the budget was within {{USD|10–15 million|long=no}}.<ref name="Vulture"/> Subsequently, ''The A.V. Club'' cited it as {{USD|10–12 million|long=no}}, adding that the number is "on the higher end for the Japanese film industry".<ref name="AVClub"/> Hideo Kojima also claimed during an interview with Yamazaki that the film's budget was under {{JPY|1.5 billion}}, to which Yamazaki agreed.<ref name="Aera2"/> Regardless, neither Toho nor Yamazaki divulged the film's actual budget figure, with the latter refusing to out of fear that "everyone's gonna want me to make a movie for that number".<ref name="Nikkei"/><ref name="Deadline2"/>

Some Western journalists claimed that the film's low budget and low amount of animators reflected harsh working conditions in the Japanese film industry.<ref name="Schilling-March"/><ref name="Slane"/><ref name="Williamson"/> According to Yamazaki, the film's visual effects team was not mistreated. He added that they avoided working long hours on the film and installed a kitchen in the studio to make it "more comfortable and cozy". Moreover, he explained there are two categories of animation studios in Japan: "white" and "[[Black company (Japan)|black]]", with "black" studios being the exploiters; the name of the film's visual effects studio, Shirogumi, literally means "white team" in Japanese.<ref name="Vulture"/><ref name="Schilling-March"/>

Shortly after its Netflix premiere on June 1, 2024, the film provoked controversy in South Korea. On June 12, ''[[The Chosun Ilbo]]'' reported that some accused the film of "glorifying ''kamikaze'' pilots" with the scene where its protagonist, Kōichi Shikishima, rams his plane into Godzilla's mouth to kill the monster. South Korean viewers reportedly also attacked the depiction of Godzilla, believing its defeat to represent a "mental victory" for Japan because the monster symbolizes a [[nuclear weapon]]. Similar condemnations were previously made against writer-director Takashi Yamazaki for his 2013 film ''The Eternal Zero'', which also depicts a ''kamikaze'' pilot.<ref name="Chosun"/>

===Accolades===
{{Main|List of accolades received by Godzilla Minus One{{!}}List of accolades received by ''Godzilla Minus One''}}

''Godzilla Minus One'' won [[Academy Award for Best Visual Effects|Best Visual Effects]] at the 96th Academy Awards,{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Nikkei"/><ref name="abcnews"/><ref name="Godzilla.com1"/><ref name="McPherson3"/><ref name="Goodfellow"/><ref name="Fuster"/><ref name="Malhotra"/>}} becoming the first non-English language film in [[Academy Awards]] [[List of Academy Award records|history]] to do so.<ref name="Goodfellow"/> It was the first ''Godzilla'' film to be nominated for an Academy Award, as well as the first [[List of Japanese Academy Award winners and nominees|Japanese]] film ever to receive a nomination in the Best Visual Effects category.<ref name="Vulture"/><ref name="AVClub"/><ref name="aframe"/>{{efn|Excluding the Japanese-American co-production ''[[Tora! Tora! Tora!]]'' (1970),<ref name="Vulture"/> which featured visual effects created by the American studio [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]].{{sfn|Culhane|1981|p=44}}}} Of these personal records in Academy Award history, Yamazaki became the second director (after [[Stanley Kubrick]]),{{efn-lr|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="MovieMaker"/><ref name="latimes"/><ref name="aframe"/><ref name="Donohoo"/>}} Kiyoko Shibuya became the first woman of color, and Tatsuji Nojima became the first person of [[Generation Z]] (and currently the youngest), to win the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects.<ref name="Godzilla.com1"/>

At the [[Austin Film Critics Association Awards 2023|19th Austin Film Critics Association Awards]], ''Godzilla Minus One'' was selected as the sixth [[Austin Film Critics Association Award for Best Film|best film]] of 2023 and won Best International Film.<ref name="Whittaker"/> The film received nominations for three [[Asian Film Awards]] (winning two; Best Visual Effects and Best Sound),<ref name="Brzeski"/> four [[Blue Ribbon Awards]] (winning three; [[Blue Ribbon Award for Best Film|Best Film]], [[Blue Ribbon Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]] for Kamiki, and [[Blue Ribbon Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] for Hamabe),<ref name="Chunichi"/><ref name="Sponichi"/> four [[Critics' Choice Super Awards]] (winning two: Best Science Fiction/Fantasy Movie and Best Villain in a Movie for Godzilla),<ref name="Davis"/> and four from the [[Seattle Film Critics Society]] (winning three; Best International Film, Best Visual Effects, and Villain of the Year).<ref name="NextBestPicture"/> ''Godzilla Minus One'' won eight of its leading twelve nominations at the [[47th Japan Academy Film Prize]], including [[Japan Academy Film Prize for Picture of the Year|Best Picture]], [[Japan Academy Film Prize for Screenplay of the Year|Best Screenplay]], and [[Japan Academy Film Prize for Outstanding Performance by an Actress in a Supporting Role|Best Supporting Actress]] for Sakura Ando, making it the most-awarded film at that year's ceremony.<ref name="Mar2024CinemaToday"/><ref name="Japan-Academy-Prize"/>

===Cultural impact===
According to Toho, the film was a "global phenomenon", with fans making it "propel to the forefront of pop culture".<ref name="Godzilla.com2"/> In February 2024, Gavin J Blair of ''The Hollywood Reporter'' suggested that ''Godzilla Minus One'' is a significant contributor to the recent revival of [[Japanese popular culture]] in the West, alongside Miyazaki's ''The Boy and the Heron'', the first season of Netflix's [[One Piece (2023 TV series)|live action adaptation of ''One Piece'']], and the [[FX (TV channel)|FX]] television series ''[[Shōgun (2024 TV series)|Shōgun]]'' (2024).<ref name="Hypergalactic"/> ''Collider'' claimed that ''Godzilla Minus One'' "helped the ''Godzilla'' series become more popular than ever before" and ''Variety'' implied that the film had gained a legendary status by April 2024.<ref name="Highest-Grossing"/><ref name="Davis"/> Amidst the film's popularity, Yamazaki and the visual effects team were assigned to create another ''kaiju'' film, the [[Web film|web]] [[Short film|short]] ''Foodlosslla: What Should Humanity Do!?'' (2024).<ref name="advertimes"/> In May, Yamazaki was included in [[Gold House]]'s annual list of [[Gold House#A100|100 Most Impactful Asians]] as a result of ''Godzilla Minus One''<nowiki/>'s achievements.<ref name="GoldHouse"/>

As a tribute to ''Godzilla Minus One'', director Adam Wingard and visual effects supervisor Alessandro Ongaro recreated a shot of the ground bursting beneath Godzilla's foot for the Rome sequence in ''Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire'' (2024).<ref name="Wingard"/> The positive word-of-mouth Yamazaki's movie generated is also believed to have helped ''Godzilla x Kong'' achieve several box office milestones,<ref name="Fuster"/><ref name="Malhotra"/> including becoming the [[2024 in film|fifth-highest-grossing film of 2024]].<ref name="BOM2024"/>

In January and February 2024, Yamazaki claimed that no discussions of a sequel had transpired but expressed interest in directing one.<ref name="Empire"/><ref name="Klein2"/><ref name="Schilling-January"/> He elaborated his desire to proceed with following the same characters' lives thereon and have Godzilla battle an antagonizing ''kaiju''.<ref name="Empire"/><ref name="Bythrow"/> In an interview with ''{{Ill|Mono Magazine|ja|モノ・マガジン}}'', Yamazaki implied that a sequel would explore the curse Godzilla left behind on Japan, similar to that of the Tatari-gami in ''[[Princess Mononoke]]'' (1997).{{sfn|''Mono Magazine''|2023|p=17}} Hamabe remarked when interviewed by the same publication: "If there is a next series, I might be the one stepping on and crushing people"<!--次のシリーズがあったら、私が人間を踏み潰してるかもしれま-->.{{sfn|''Mono Magazine''|2023|p=13}} On another occasion, Ando told the ''Daily Express'' that she wants her character to encounter Godzilla in a sequel to the film, joking that she would like to be "jumping like this [[Spider-Man]]-type woman taking down Godzilla".<ref name="Ando"/>

The possibility of a sequel was largely discussed at a stage greeting held after a screening of the film's black-and-white version in Tokyo on January 12, 2024. Several cast members were in attendance, and Yamazaki attended remotely from the United States;<ref name="CinemaToday"/> the latter signified wanting the characters to return to the sea for the sequel.<ref name="Stagegreeting"/> Yamada proposed the idea of using Noriko as the key to locating Godzilla since, according to his interpretation, she possesses the monster's "cells".<ref name="Stagegreeting"/> During a February 2024 Q&A with ''Collider'', Yamazaki announced that he had begun developing a new movie, and denied that it would be a sequel to ''Minus One''.<ref name="Weintraub"/>

Producer Minami Ichikawa believed that Toho would take their time to produce the next live-action ''Godzilla'' film since they want "great ideas, an excellent script, a talented director, and the right cast to work on it carefully" because "Godzilla deserves to have that level of intentionality".<ref name="gqjapan"/> On November 1, 2024, it was disclosed that a new ''Godzilla'' movie had been greenlit, with Yamazaki returning to write, direct, and handle the visual effects; no mention was made whether it will be a sequel to ''Godzilla Minus One'' or a standalone film.<ref name="Gizmodo"/> At the [[23rd Visual Effects Society Awards]] on February 11, 2025, Yamazaki announced that storyboarding and scripting of the new ''Godzilla'' film is in progress, and he expects it to cost more than ''Godzilla Minus One''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Giardina |first=Carolyn |date=February 12, 2025 |title=''Godzilla Minus One'' Director Takashi Yamazaki; ''Here'' VFX Pros Weigh In on AI at VES Awards |url=https://variety.com/2025/artisans/news/godzilla-minus-one-director-takashi-yamazaki-ai-ves-awards-1236305429/ |access-date=February 12, 2025 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> On April 14, [[Bloomberg News]] reported that a sequel is in development.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kim |first=Sohee |date=April 14, 2025 |title='Godzilla' Studio Toho to Invest $830 Million in Global Push |url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-14/-godzilla-studio-toho-to-invest-830-million-in-global-push |url-access=subscription |access-date=April 14, 2025 |website=[[Bloomberg News]]}}</ref> In July, Toho International president Koji Ueda suggested the sequel could potentially be released in 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kim |first=Sohee |date=July 17, 2025 |title=Godzilla Conquered Japan. Now Its Owner Plots a Global Takeover |url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-17/godzilla-studio-toho-plans-more-films-sequels-anime-and-jujutsu-kaisen |url-access=registration |access-date=July 17, 2025 |website=[[Bloomberg L.P.|Bloomberg]] |archive-date=July 26, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250726063353/https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-17/godzilla-studio-toho-plans-more-films-sequels-anime-and-jujutsu-kaisen |url-status=live }}</ref> On November 3, 2025, Toho was announced the sequel would be titled ''[[Godzilla Minus Zero]]'', with news outlets subsequently reporting it would be released in 2026.<ref name=":22">{{Cite web |last=Brzeski |first=Patrick |date=2025-11-03 |title=Japan’s Toho Studios Reveals Title for Next Godzilla Movie |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/japan-toho-studios-title-godzilla-minus-zero-1236416477/ |access-date=2025-11-04 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=November 3, 2025 |title=Monsters, Mayhem, and More: Your Godzilla Minus One Watch List on Netflix |url=https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/godzilla-minus-one-kaiju-movies |access-date=November 4, 2025 |website=[[Netflix]]}}</ref>
