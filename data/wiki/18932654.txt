'''''Koyaanisqatsi'''''{{efn|{{IPAc-en|uslang|k|oʊ|,|j|ɑː|n|i|s|ˈ|k|ɑː|t|s|i}} {{respell|koh|YAH|nees|KAHT|see}}; {{IPA|hop|koˈjaːnisˈqat͡si|lang}}{{sfn|Hopi Dictionary Project|1998|pp=863-864}}}}<!--See talk page for pronunciation explanation--> is a 1982 American [[non-narrative film|non-narrative]] [[documentary film]] directed and produced by [[Godfrey Reggio]], featuring music by [[Philip Glass]] and cinematography by [[Ron Fricke]]. Described as an "essay in images and sound on the state of American civilization",<ref name="nytreview">{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1983/09/14/movies/film-koyaanisqatsi-essay-on-us.html |title=Film: 'Koyaanisqatsi,' Essay on U.S. |date=September 14, 1983 |page=C22 |work=[[The New York Times]] |access-date=February 20, 2025}}</ref> the film comprises a montage of [[stock footage]], [[slow motion]], and [[time-lapse photography|time-lapse]] visuals of natural and urban environments across the United States. Following its [[premiere]]s at the [[Telluride Film Festival|Telluride]] and [[New York Film Festival]]s in 1982, it began a [[limited theatrical release]] the next year. Produced on a budget of $2.5 million, the film grossed $3.2 million at the box office, and was one of the highest-grossing documentaries of the 1980s.

The title comes from the [[Hopi language|Hopi]] word {{lang|hop|[[wikt:koyaanisqatsi|koyaanisqatsi]]}}, meaning "life out of balance".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://philipglass.com/films/koyaanisqatsi/|title=Film: Koyaanisqatsi|access-date=January 26, 2017}}</ref> It is the first film in the [[Qatsi trilogy|''Qatsi'' trilogy]], which was followed by ''[[Powaqqatsi]]'' (1988) and ''[[Naqoyqatsi]]'' (2002).<ref>{{Cite web archived|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/481055/koyaanisqatsi#articles-reviews|url-status=dead|archive-date=October 20, 2012|title=TCM.com}}</ref> The trilogy depicts different aspects of the relationship between humans, nature and technology. ''Koyaanisqatsi'' is the best known of the trilogy and is considered a [[cult film]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.michigandaily.com/content/cult-koyaanisqatsi-blends-music-film|title=Cult ''Koyaanisqatsi'' blends music, film|work=The Michigan Daily|access-date=February 12, 2018|language=en}}</ref><ref>[https://siskelebert.org/?p=6069 Cult Movies on Videocassette, 1987 - Siskel and Ebert Movie Reviews]</ref>

==Synopsis==
<!-- "KOYAANISQATSI" / "ORGANIC" / "CLOUDS" / "RESOURCE"-->
''Koyaanisqatsi'' runs for 86 minutes and comprises a montage of visuals accompanied by [[minimal music]], without narration, dialogue, or [[intertitle]]s. The film is structured into 13 passages, which are demarcated by dramatic musical shifts in the [[film score|score]]. It opens with [[Cave painting|pictographs]] from the Great Gallery of [[Horseshoe Canyon (Utah)|Horseshoe Canyon]], followed by a [[close-up]] of a [[Launch vehicle|rocket]] launch, while a deep [[bass (voice type)|bass voice]] chants the film's title. The images [[dissolve (filmmaking)|dissolve]] into a sequence featuring landscapes of [[geological formation]]s and [[dune]]s in the [[desert]]s of the [[American Southwest]]. [[Time-lapse photography|Time-lapse imagery]] shows shadows quickly moving across landscapes, and bats flying out of a [[cave]]. Clouds move in time-lapse footage, which is followed by a [[waterfall]], then [[ocean wave]]s in [[slow-motion]]. This segues into footage of [[resource]]s: [[reservoir]]s, [[Cultigen|cultivated flowers]], [[surface mining]], [[Electric power transmission|electrical power infrastructure]], [[evaporation pond]]s, [[oil drilling]], [[blast furnace]]s, and concludes with a [[nuclear weapons testing|nuclear weapons test]] forming a [[mushroom cloud]] over the desert.

<!-- "VESSELS" -->
The following sequence begins with people [[sun bathing]] on a beach next to a power plant, followed by people touring a power plant. The next images show clouds reflected on the [[façade]] of a glass skyscraper, [[Boeing 747]] aircraft [[taxiing]] at an airport, cars driving on urban [[controlled-access highway|freeways]], [[military vehicle]]s and [[military aircraft|aircraft]], and [[air-to-ground weaponry]] being deployed. <!-- "PRUITT IGOE" / "PRUITT IGOE CODA" / "SLO MO PEOPLE" -->
The film continues with modern skyscrapers in [[New York City]], followed by areas of [[urban decay]]. [[Dilapidation|Dilapidated buildings]] and other abandoned infrastructure undergo [[demolition]] and form large [[fugitive dust|dust clouds]]. The sequence concludes with a [[coda (music)|coda]] featuring time-lapse footage of clouds and sunlight reflected in façades of glass skyscrapers in time-lapse, followed by people walking through busy city streets in slow-motion.

<!-- "THE GRID INTRO" / "THE GRID" -->
The next sequence begins with an [[introduction (music)|introduction]] that shows time-lapse footage of sunsets reflected in the façades of skyscrapers, [[wide shot]]s of cities at night with vehicle lights moving fast along streets and freeways, and the [[moon]] disappearing behind a building. The [[rhythm]] of the music increases while time-lapse footage in cities show vehicles driving, crowds of people walking, shopping centers, factories, [[food processing]] plants, and [[channel surfing]]. As the sequence comes to a close, the music and visuals are at their fastest, until the sequence ends and the music goes silent.

<!-- "MICROCHIP" / "PROPHECIES" -->
[[Close-up]] shots of [[microchips]] are interspersed with [[satellite photography]] of city grids. The final sequence features various individuals of all [[social classes]], some of whom [[fourth wall|look directly into the camera]], accompanied by [[Hopi mythology|Hopi prophecies]] sung in the soundtrack. As the film's title is chanted in a deep voice, a rocket is launched and explodes shortly after take-off; the camera follows its debris as it falls back towards Earth. The film concludes with another image of the pictograph seen in the opening shot.

==Production==
===Background===
In 1972, [[Godfrey Reggio]], of the Institute for Regional Education (IRE), was working on a media campaign in Albuquerque, New Mexico, which was sponsored by the [[American Civil Liberties Union]] (ACLU). The campaign involved invasions of privacy and the use of technology to control behavior. Instead of making public service announcements, which Reggio felt "had no visibility", advertising spots were purchased for television, radio, newspapers, and billboards.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=383}} Over thirty billboards were used for the campaign, and one design featured a close-up of the human eye, which Reggio described as a "horrifying image".{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=384}} To produce the television commercials the IRE hired cinematographer [[Ron Fricke]], who worked on the project for two years. The television advertisements aired during prime-time programming and became so popular that viewers would call the television stations to learn when the next advertisement would be aired.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=383}} Godfrey described the two-year campaign as "extraordinarily successful", and as a result, [[Methylphenidate|Ritalin (methylphenidate)]] was eliminated as a behavior-modifying drug in many New Mexico school districts.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=384}} But after the campaign ended, the ACLU eventually withdrew its sponsorship, and the IRE unsuccessfully attempted to raise millions of dollars at a fundraiser in Washington, D.C. The institute had only $40,000 left in its budget, and Reggio was unsure how to use the small amount of funds. Fricke insisted to Reggio that the money could be used to produce a film, which led to the production of ''Koyaanisqatsi''.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=63}}

===Filming===
[[File:Pruitt-Igoe-collapses.jpg|thumb|The demolition of the [[Pruitt–Igoe]] housing project in St. Louis, Missouri, was one of the early scenes filmed once production began in 1975.]]
Reggio and Fricke chose to shoot unscripted footage and edit it into an hour-long film.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=63}} Production began in 1975 in St. Louis, Missouri.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=385}} [[16 mm film]] was used due to budget constraints, despite the preference to shoot with [[35mm movie film|35 mm film]].{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=385}}{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=386}} Footage of the [[Pruitt–Igoe]] housing project was shot from a helicopter, and Fricke nearly passed out during filming, having never flown in a helicopter before.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=63}} Reggio later chose to shoot in [[Los Angeles]] and New York City. As there was no formal script, Fricke shot whatever he felt would "look good on film".{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=64}} While filming in New York City, Fricke developed an idea to shoot portraits of people. A grey paper backdrop was displayed in Times Square, and Fricke stood {{convert|10|ft|m|sigfig=1}} back with the camera. People walking by started posing for the camera, thinking it was a [[still camera]], and several shots from the setup ended up in the film. Reggio was not on location in Times Square when Fricke shot the footage and thought the idea of shooting portraits of people was "foolish". Upon viewing the footage, Reggio decided to devote an entire section of the film to portraits. The footage was processed with a special chemical to enhance the film's shadows and details, as all footage was shot only with existing lighting. The IRE's $40,000 was exhausted after the filming, and almost two cases of film had been used. The unedited footage was screened in Santa Fe, New Mexico, but Fricke said it was "boring as hell" and there were "not that many good shots".{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=64}} Fricke later moved to Los Angeles, and took a job as a waiter, unable to get a job in the film industry. While Fricke was working in Los Angeles, he edited the footage into a 20-minute reel, but "without regard for message or political content".{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=64}}

{{quote box|quote=I just shot anything that I thought would look good on film. Shooting [[homelessness|bums]], as well as buildings, didn't matter. It was all the same from my standpoint. I just shot the form of things.|source=—Ron Fricke{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=64}}|width=250px}}

The IRE was continuously receiving funding and wanted to continue the project in 1976, using 35&nbsp;mm film.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=64}} After quitting his waiting job, Fricke traveled with a camera crew to the [[Four Corners]], which was chosen for filming for its "alien look".{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=66}} Due to the limited budget, Fricke shot with a 16&nbsp;mm [[zoom lens]] onto 35&nbsp;mm film. To compensate for the lens size, a 2× extender was added, which turned it into a full 35&nbsp;mm zoom lens, allowing footage to be clearly captured onto 35&nbsp;mm film. The two-week shoot included [[aerial shot|aerial footage]] taken from an airplane using a [[hand-held camera]] and ground footage taken using a tripod. The first aerial footage was too "shaky", so additional footage was taken from a camera mounted onto the airplane. Fricke traveled back to New York City in 1977, during which [[New York City blackout of 1977|a major city-wide blackout]] occurred. Footage of the blackout was filmed in Harlem and the South Bronx, and the film was [[colorfulness|desaturated]] to match the appearance of the 16&nbsp;mm footage.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=66}}

Reggio and Fricke came across time-lapse footage in "some low-visibility commercial work". They felt such footage was "the language [they] were missing", and collectively decided to implement time-lapse as a major part of the film to create "an experience of acceleration". For the time-lapse footage, Fricke purchased a [[Mitchell Camera|Mitchell]] camera,{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=66}} and built a motor with an [[intervalometer]], which was used to precisely move the camera between frames. The system was powered by a [[VRLA battery|gel cell battery]] that lasted for twelve hours, which enabled Fricke to shoot without the use of a [[electric generator|generator]].{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=68}} Most time-lapse shots were filmed at a [[frame rate]] of 1½ frames per second. Fricke wanted the footage to "look normal" and not contain any "gimmicky" special effects.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=68}} The time-lapse shot overlooking the freeway in Los Angeles was filmed from the top of a building through a [[multiple exposure|double exposure]], with ten-second delay between frames. The first take was shot throughout the day for twelve hours, then the film was rewound and the same scene was shot at night for twenty minutes.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=68}}
The shot of the [[Boeing 747]] was filmed at [[Los Angeles International Airport]], and was the longest continuous shot in the film. Fricke and his [[focus puller]], Robert Hill, filmed at the airport every day for two weeks.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=70}} To keep the shot of the aircraft within the frame, the camera was slowly moved by increasing the voltage to the gear motors.{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=68}}

In addition to footage shot by Fricke, some of the footage of people and traffic in New York City was shot by cinematographer [[Hilary Harris]]. During post-production, Reggio was introduced to Harris' ''[[Organism (film)|Organism]]'' (1975), which predominately features time-lapse footage of New York City streets. Reggio was impressed with Harris' work and subsequently hired him to work on ''Koyaanisqatsi''.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=387}} Footage filmed by cinematographer [[Louis Schwartzberg]] was added into the cloud sequence, and additional stock footage was provided by [[MacGillivray Freeman Films]].{{sfn|Gold|1984|p=72}}

While Reggio was working on post-production at the [[Samuel Goldwyn Studio]] in 1981,<ref name="lonegiant">{{cite web |url=http://www.indiewire.com/article/interview_lone_giant_godfrey_reggios_naqoyqatsi/ |title=INTERVIEW: Lone Giant: Godfrey Reggio's 'Naqoyqatsi{{'-}}|work=[[IndieWire]]|date=October 18, 2002|access-date=April 26, 2011|first=Erin|last=Torneo}}</ref> he met film director [[Francis Ford Coppola]] through an associate from [[Zoetrope Studios]], Coppola's production company. Before shooting ''[[The Outsiders (film)|The Outsiders]]'' (1983) and ''[[Rumble Fish]]'' (1983), Coppola requested to see ''Koyaanisqatsi'', and Reggio arranged a private screening shortly after its completion.{{sfn|MacDonald|1992|p=399}} Coppola told Reggio that he was waiting for a film such as ''Koyaanisqatsi'' and that it was "important for people to see", so he added his name into the credits and helped present and distribute the film.{{sfn|Ramsey|1986|p=62}} Coppola also decided to introduce and end the film with footage of [[pictogram|pictographs]] from the Great Gallery at [[Horseshoe Canyon (Utah)|Horseshoe Canyon]] in Utah after visiting the site and becoming fascinated by the ancient sandstone murals.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-08-10-vw-2386-story.html|title=The Past Comes to Life in Horseshoe Canyon|first=Charles|last=Hillinger|date=August 10, 1986|access-date=April 26, 2011|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref>

==Music==
[[File:Philip Glass 003.jpg|thumb|Composer [[Philip Glass]] ''(pictured in 1993)'']]
The film's score was composed by [[Philip Glass]] and was performed by the [[Philip Glass Ensemble]], conducted by [[Michael Riesman]]. One piece written for the film, "Façades", was intended to be played over a montage of scenes from New York's [[Wall Street]]. It was ultimately not used in the film; Glass released it as part of his album ''[[Glassworks (Glass)|Glassworks]]'' in 1982.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://philipglass.com/compositions/facades/|title=Facades – Philip Glass|publisher=Philip Glass}}</ref>

The opening for "The Grid" begins with slow sustained notes on brass instruments. The music builds in speed and [[dynamics (music)|dynamics]] throughout the piece's 21 minutes. When the piece is at its fastest, it is characterized by a [[synthesizer]] playing the piece's bass line [[ostinato]].

Glass's music for the film is a highly recognizable example of the [[minimal music|minimalist]] school of composition, which is characterized by heavily repeated figures, simple structures, and a tonal (although not in the traditional [[common practice]] sense of the word) harmonic language. Glass was one of the first composers to employ minimalism in film scoring, paving the way for many future composers of that style.<ref>{{cite book |last=Eaton |first=Rebecca Marie Doran |date=May 2008 |title=Unheard Minimalisms: The Functions of the Minimalist Technique in Film Scores |url=https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/bitstream/handle/2152/3839/eatonr88741.pdf |location=Austin, TX |publisher=The University of Texas at Austin }}</ref> The choral pieces were sung for the film by the [[Western Wind Vocal Ensemble]], a NYC-based 6-singer choral group specializing in choral and vocal music from many different eras and places, co-founded by [[William Zukof]] and [[Elliot Z. Levine]].

The score is composed of nine [[movement (music)|movements]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.orsymphony.org/concerts-tickets/program-notes/2024-25/koyaanisqatsi-live-with-the-philip-glass-ensemble/ |title=Koyaanisqatsi: Live with the Philip Glass Ensemble{{snd}} Program Listing |date=November 14, 2024 |publisher=[[Oregon Symphony]] |access-date=February 12, 2025}}</ref> which are further divided into 13 discrete [[section (music)|sections]], including [[introduction (music)|intros]] and [[coda (music)|codas]].<ref name="greatnorthern"/>

An abbreviated version of the film's soundtrack was released in 1983 by [[Island Records]]. Despite the fact that the amount of music in the film was nearly as long as the film itself, this 1983 soundtrack release was only 46 minutes long, and only featured some of the film's pieces.

In 1998, Philip Glass re-recorded the film score for [[Nonesuch Records]] with a length of 73 minutes. This new recording includes new additional tracks that were cut from the film, as well as extended versions of tracks from the original album. This album was released as ''Koyaanisqatsi'', rather than a soundtrack of the film.

In 2009, a remastered album was released under Philip Glass' own music label, Orange Mountain Music, as ''Koyaanisqatsi: Complete Original Soundtrack''. This recording, with a length over 76 minutes, includes the original sound-effects and additional music that was used in the film.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Koyaanisqatsi - Philip Glass |url=https://philipglass.com/recordings/koyaanisqatsi-09/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250427215930/https://philipglass.com/recordings/koyaanisqatsi-09/ |archive-date=April 27, 2025}}</ref>

The music has become so popular that the Philip Glass Ensemble has toured the world, playing the music for ''Koyaanisqatsi'' live in front of the movie screen.

==Meaning==
[[File:Godfrey Reggio (1995) by Erling Mandelmann.jpg|thumb|Godfrey Reggio (pictured in 1995), director of ''Koyaanisqatsi'' and the other films in the Qatsi trilogy]]
Reggio stated that the ''Qatsi'' films are intended to simply create an experience and that "it is up [to] the viewer to take for himself/herself what it is that [the film] means." He also said that "these films have never been about the effect ''of'' technology, ''of'' industry ''on'' people. It's been that everyone: politics, education, things of the financial structure, the nation state structure, language, the culture, religion, all of that exists within the host of technology. So it's not the effect ''of'', it's that everything exists within [technology]. It's not that we ''use'' technology, we ''live'' technology. Technology has become as ubiquitous as the air we breathe ..."<ref name="eol"/>

Reggio initially wanted to avoid using a word in the film's title, and instead wanted to use a visual symbol. He intended to use the 1952 photograph of an audience wearing [[3D glasses]], taken by [[J. R. Eyerman]] for [[Life (magazine)|''Life'' magazine]]. As he was eventually forced to choose a word for the title, he "wanted a word that had no baggage culturally". He chose the word ''[[:wiktionary:koyaanisqatsi|koyaanisqatsi]]'', which means "life out of balance" in [[Hopi language|Hopi]], a [[Indigenous languages of the Americas|Native American language]] spoken by the [[Hopi|Hopi people]]. Reggio learned the word from his friend [[David Monongye]], a spiritual leader in the Hopi community.<ref name="greatnorthern">{{cite web|url=https://thegreatnorthernfestival.com/tgn-blog/life-out-of-balance |title=Life Out of Balance // An interview with Godfrey Reggio |first=Steve |last=Marsh |date=2002 |access-date=February 11, 2025 |publisher=The Great Northern}}</ref> Reggio explained the lack of dialogue by stating "it's not for lack of love of the language that these films have no words. It's because, from my point of view, our language is in a state of vast humiliation. It no longer describes the world in which we live."<ref name=eol>{{cite video |people=Carson, Greg (director) |date=2002 |title=Essence of Life |medium=DVD |publisher=[[MGM Home Entertainment]] }}</ref>

The word ''koyaanisqatsi'' literally translates to "chaotic life".{{sfn|Hopi Dictionary Project|1998|p=154}} It is a [[compound (linguistics)|compound]] word composed of the [[bound form]] ''koyaanis-'' ("corrupted" or "chaotic"){{sfn|Hopi Dictionary Project|1998|p=154}} and the word ''qatsi'' ("life" or "existence").{{sfn|Hopi Dictionary Project|1998|p=463}} The ''[[Hopi Dictionary]]'' additionally defines ''koyaanisqatsi'' as "life of moral corruption and turmoil", referring to the life of a group.{{sfn|Hopi Dictionary Project|1998|p=154}} The latter translation has been used in promotional materials for the film and is one of the five definitions listed before the closing credits.<ref name="afi"/>

In the score by [[Philip Glass]], the word "koyaanisqatsi" is chanted at the beginning and end of the film in an "otherworldly"<ref>{{cite book|last=Neveldine|first=Robert Burns|title=Bodies at Risk: Unsafe Limits in Romanticism and Postmodernism|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=UcJGLUMD-boC&pg=PA112|year=1998|publisher=SUNY Press|isbn=9780791436493|page=112}}</ref> dark, sepulchral ''[[basso profondo]]'' by singer Albert de Ruiter over a solemn, four-bar organ-passacaglia bassline. Three [[Hopi prophecies]] sung by a choral ensemble during the latter part of the "Prophecies" movement are translated just prior to the end credits.

During the end titles, the film gives [[Jacques Ellul]], [[Ivan Illich]], [[David Monongye]], [[Guy Debord]], and [[Leopold Kohr]] credit for inspiration. Moreover, amongst the consultants to the director are listed names including [[Jeffrey Lew]], T. A. Price, Belle Carpenter, Cybelle Carpenter, [[Langdon Winner]], and Barbara Pecarich.

==Releases==

===Theatrical distribution===
''Koyaanisqatsi'' premiered on September 25, 1982, at the [[Telluride Film Festival]], after a [[workprint]] had been screened at the [[Santa Fe Film Festival]] on April 27.<ref>{{cite web | title=New Mexico Filmmakers Play a Part in "Samsara" | publisher=[[Santa Fe Film Festival]] | date=November 2, 2012 | url=https://santafefilmfestival.com/index/new-mexico-filmmakers-play-a-part-in-samsara/ | access-date=March 12, 2024}}</ref><ref name="afi">{{cite web |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/MovieDetailsPrintView/69890|title=Koyaanisqatsi (1983) |work=[[AFI Catalog of Feature Films]] |access-date=January 11, 2024}}</ref> It was screened again at the [[New York Film Festival]] (NYFF) on October 4, which was promoted as the film's "world premiere", despite its previous screening in September.<ref>{{cite web | last=Robbins | first=Jim | title=Koyaanisqatsi | website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] | date=October 6, 1982 | url=https://variety.com/1982/film/reviews/koyaanisqatsi-1200425298/ | access-date=March 12, 2024}}</ref> While the NYFF was based in the [[Lincoln Center]], an exception was made to screen ''Koyaanisqatsi'' at the larger [[Radio City Music Hall]] due to the film's "spectacular visual and sound quality".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/424423676|title=The Film Festival Turns 20, Mixing Old and New|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|first=Annette|last=Insdorf|date=September 19, 1982|access-date=March 12, 2024|id={{ProQuest|424423676}} }}</ref>

[[Triumph Films]] offered to distribute the film, but Reggio turned down the offer as he wanted to work with a smaller company so he could be more involved with the release. He chose Island Alive as the distributor, a company newly formed in 1983 by [[Chris Blackwell]] of [[Island Records]],<ref>{{cite web|title=Our Founder: Chris Blackwell|url=http://www.islandoutpost.com/about_us/our_founder/|website=Island Outpost|access-date=September 4, 2011}}</ref> and ''Koyaanisqatsi'' was the company's first release. Select theaters distributed a pamphlet that defined the title and the Hopi prophecies sung in the film, as well as a copy of the soundtrack from Island Records. The first theatrical run featured four-track [[Dolby Stereo]] sound, while later runs featured [[monaural]] sound.

The film's initial limited release began in San Francisco at the [[Castro Theatre]] on April 27, 1983.<ref>{{cite web archived|url=http://www.joblo.com/releases.php?mode=theatrical&month=April&year=1983|title=Theatrical Release Dates – April 1983|work=[[JoBlo.com]]|access-date=September 4, 2011|archive-date=October 23, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> The producers spent $6,500 on marketing the initial release, which grossed $46,000 throughout its one-week run, and was the highest-grossing film in the San Francisco Bay Area that week. It was released in Los Angeles a month later where it grossed $300,000 at two theaters within 15 weeks. Additional releases in select cities throughout the United States continued in September 1983, beginning with a release in New York City on September 15. In mid-October, ''Koyaanisqatsi'' was released onto 40 to 50 screens throughout the country.<ref name="marketing">{{Cite magazine |first=Karen |last=Kreps |date=December 1983 |page=68 |title=The Marketing of Koyaanisqatsi |magazine=[[Boxoffice Pro|Boxoffice]]|url=http://www.boxoffice.com/the_vault/issue_page?issue_id=1983-12-1&page_no=72 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130118085639/http://www.boxoffice.com/the_vault/issue_page?issue_id=1983-12-1&page_no=72 |archive-date=January 18, 2013 |access-date=April 9, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> It was a surprise [[art film|arthouse]] hit as well as a popular presentation at colleges and universities.<ref>[https://harvardfilmarchive.org/programs/synaesthetic-cinema-minimalist-music-and-film Godfrey Reggio/IRE Collection - Harvard Film Archive]</ref><ref>[https://harvardfilmarchive.org/programs/godfrey-reggio Godfrey Reggio, Cinematic Seer - Harvard Film Archive]</ref><ref>[https://harvardfilmarchive.org/programs/synaesthetic-cinema-minimalist-music-and-film Synaesthetic Cinema: Minimalist Music and Film - Harvard Film Archive]</ref>

It ultimately grossed $3.2 million at the box office, and became the second-highest-grossing documentary of the 1980s, following ''[[Roger & Me]]'' (1989).<ref name="plantinga"/>

===Home media===
''Koyaanisqatsi'' was originally released on VHS and laserdisc by [[Michael Nesmith]]'s [[Pacific Arts Corporation|Pacific Arts Video]].<ref>[https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/01099/ID5304PA/Koyaanisqatsi:-Life-Out-of-Balance LaserDisc Database]</ref><ref>[https://siskelebert.org/?p=4009 At the Cassette Store, 1985 (incomplete) - Siskel and Ebert Movie Reviews]</ref> However, because of copyright issues, the film was out of print for most of the 1990s.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.spiritofbaraka.com/koyaanisqatsi |title=Koyaanisqatsi |work=Spirit of Baraka |date=May 21, 2007 |access-date=May 28, 2008}}</ref>

The rights to ''Koyaanisqatsi'' were passed through various multinational entertainment companies, which eventually prevented a home video release. IRE enforced their legal and contractual rights by creating a federal court lawsuit. IRE distributed a privately issued release of the film on DVD. The release was available to those who made a donation of at least $180 to IRE, and was distributed in a sleeve that was signed by Reggio.<ref>{{cite web archived |url-status=dead |url=http://www.koyaanisqatsi.org/howyoucanhelp/help_IRE.las |title=How You Can Help: Getting Involved |access-date=November 21, 2016 |work=Koyaanisqatsi.org |publisher=IRE |archive-date=November 9, 2000}}</ref>

[[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM)]] eventually received the rights to the film, and ''Koyaanisqatsi'' was released on DVD by [[MGM Home Entertainment]] on September 18, 2002, coinciding with the release of ''[[Naqoyqatsi]]'' (2002). Both films were available in a two-disc box set. Each DVD includes a documentary with interviews by Reggio and Glass and trailers for the [[Qatsi trilogy|''Qatsi'' trilogy]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s582reggio.html|title=Koyaanisqatsi & Powaqqatsi|first=Glenn|last=Erickson|work=DVD Savant|access-date=June 6, 2011}}</ref> Unlike the IRE release, which featured the film in the [[open matte]] format in which it was filmed, the MGM release was cropped into a widescreen aspect as it originally was presented in theaters.<ref>{{cite web|series=Comparison between the two versions|url=http://www.dvdbeaver.com/film/DVDCompare2/koyaanisqatsi.htm|access-date=June 6, 2011|title=Koyaanisqatsi DVD Comparison|work=DVD Beaver}}</ref>

On January 13, 2012, a Blu-ray version (screen ratio 16:9) was released in Germany.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.de/dp/B0063NT8UG|title=German Blu-ray release|work=Amazon.de|access-date=April 9, 2012}}</ref> The Blu-ray was also released in Australia by Umbrella Entertainment on March 22, 2012.<ref>{{cite web archived|url=http://www.umbrellaent.com.au/p-3302-koyaanisqatsi-blu-ray.aspx|title=Australian Blu-ray release|work=Umbrellaent.com.au|access-date=May 30, 2012|archive-date=June 18, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> In December 2012, [[The Criterion Collection|Criterion]] released a remastered DVD and Blu-ray of ''Koyaanisqatsi'', as part of a box set containing the ''Qatsi'' Trilogy. The release features 5.1 surround sound audio and a restored digital transfer of the film in 1.85:1 aspect ratio, approved by director Godfrey Reggio.<ref>{{cite web|title=Koyaanisqatsi (1983) <nowiki>|</nowiki> The Criterion Collection|url=https://www.criterion.com/films/28034-koyaanisqatsi|access-date=March 15, 2020|work=The Criterion Collection}}</ref>

==Reception==
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Koyaanisqatsi'' has an approval rating of 91% based on 23 reviews, with an average rating of 8.10/10. The website's critical consensus reads "''Koyaanisqatsi'' combines striking visuals and a brilliant score to produce a viewing experience that manages to be formally daring as well as purely entertaining."<ref name="rottent">{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/koyaanisqatsi_life_out_of_balance |title=Koyaanisqatsi – Life Out of Balance (1982) |access-date=November 25, 2019 |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a weighted average score of 72 out of 100, based on 10 critics, indicating "generally favourable reviews".<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/koyaanisqatsi?ftag=MCD-06-10aaa1c | title=Koyaanisqatsi | website=[[Metacritic]] }}</ref> In 1983, the film was entered into the [[33rd Berlin International Film Festival]].<ref name="Berlinale">{{cite web |url=http://www.berlinale.de/en/archiv/jahresarchive/1983/02_programm_1983/02_Programm_1983.html |title=Berlinale: 1983 Programme |access-date=November 17, 2010 |work=berlinale.de}}</ref> Czech pedagogue [[Rudolf Adler]], in his textbook for film educators, describes ''Koyaanisqatsi'' as "formulated with absolute precision and congenial expression."<ref>Adler, R., ''Audiovizuální a Filmová výchova ve vyučování'' (Prague: [[:cs:Gymnázium a Hudební škola hlavního města Prahy|Gymnázium a Hudební škola hlavního města Prahy]], 2018), [http://eurohudebka.cz/data/audiovizualni_vychova.pdf#page=62 p. 60].</ref>{{rp|60}} It has also been described as a [[postmodernist film]].<ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=wfd-c0blcb0C The Postmodern Presence - Google Books (pg.37)]</ref>

''Koyaanisqatsi'' is followed by the sequels ''[[Powaqqatsi]]'' and ''[[Naqoyqatsi]]'' and the shorts ''[[Anima Mundi (film)|Anima Mundi]]'' and ''Evidence''. ''Naqoyqatsi'' was completed after a lengthy delay caused by funding problems and premiered in the United States on October 18, 2002. The film's cinematographer Ron Fricke went on to direct ''[[Baraka (film)|Baraka]]'', a [[pure cinema]] movie that is often compared to ''Koyaanisqatsi''.

==Legacy==
*In 2000, the film was selected for preservation in the United States [[National Film Registry]] by the [[Library of Congress]] for being "culturally, aesthetically, or historically significant".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.koyaanisqatsi.org/aboutus/press.php|title=Welcome to Koyaanisqatsi|website=www.koyaanisqatsi.org|access-date=2020-04-27}}</ref><ref>[https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-film-preservation-board/film-registry/descriptions-and-essays/ Brief Descriptions and Expanded Essays of National Film Registry Titles|Library of Congress]</ref>
* The theme song of the Commodore 64 game ''[[Delta (video game)|Delta]]'' is based on the film's theme score.
* A clip of this film was featured in the 2016 film ''[[20th Century Women]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |first=Nick |last=Newman |date=October 7, 2016 |title=[NYFF Review] 20th Century Women |url=https://thefilmstage.com/nyff-review-20th-century-women/}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Twentieth Century Women (2016) |url=http://waltermetz.com/twentieth-century-women-2016/ |date=March 31, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Review: Mike Mills' '20th Century Women' |url=http://mubi.com/notebook/posts/review-mike-mills-20th-century-women |website=MUBI |date=December 27, 2016 }}</ref>
* The trailer for ''[[Grand Theft Auto IV]]'' resembles the style of the film, featuring different shots of the fictional Liberty City, with the track "Pruitt–Igoe" in the background. This track also appears in the final cutscene of the missions "Out of Commission" and "A Revenger's Tragedy" alongside the in-game radio station The Journey.<ref>[https://philipglass.com/glassnotes/pruit_igoe/ "Pruitt Igoe"]. Philip Glass.</ref>
* The 2009 film ''[[Watchmen (2009 film)|Watchmen]]'' accompanies the [[origin story]] of its character [[Doctor Manhattan]] with a montage of the pieces "Prophecies" and "Pruitt–Igoe", and features the same tracks in its trailer.<ref>{{Cite news |title=What Songs Appear on the 'Watchmen' Movie Soundtrack? |url=http://movies.about.com/od/watchmen/a/watchmen-songs.htm |archive-date=November 3, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161103024537/http://movies.about.com/od/watchmen/a/watchmen-songs.htm |url-status=dead |work=[[About.com]] |access-date=February 12, 2018}}</ref>
* The film inspired the album ''Music to Films'' by Dr. Atmo and [[Oliver Lieb]], released on the record label [[FAX +49-69/450464]]. The music is completely synchronized to the film.
* The [[noise rock]] band [[Cows (band)|Cows]] covered a version of the title track, "Koyaanisqatsi", on their 1987 debut album, ''[[Taint Pluribus Taint Unum]]''.
* The chanted "koyaanisqatsi" lyric from the film's title song was parodied in [[P. D. Q. Bach]]'s "Prelude to Einstein on the Fritz" (itself a pun on the title of the opera ''[[Einstein on the Beach]]'' composed by Philip Glass), replaced with the lyric "coy hotsy-totsy".<ref>{{cite news |last=Gann |first=Kyle |date=January 19, 1999 |title=Classical Trash |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/1999-01-19/music/classical-trash/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081222082629/http://www.villagevoice.com/1999-01-19/music/classical-trash/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=December 22, 2008 |work=[[The Village Voice]] |access-date=May 28, 2008}}</ref>
* This film also inspired RaMell Ross's 2018 Oscar-nominated documentary ''[[Hale County This Morning, This Evening]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=How The Qatsi Trilogy Gave RaMell Ross a New Way of Seeing |url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6197-how-the-qatsi-trilogy-gave-ramell-ross-a-new-way-of-seeing |publisher=The Criterion Collection}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Under the Influence: RaMell Ross on THE QATSI TRILOGY|date=February 19, 2019 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6FQ7F4zgFag |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/6FQ7F4zgFag |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live |via=www.youtube.com}}{{cbignore}}</ref>
* ''Koyaaniqatsi'' was cited as an inspiration for the viral [[Simcity 3000]] video ''Magnasanti''.<ref>[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NTJQTc-TqpU ''Magnasanti''].</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2013/designandviolence/sim-city-magnasanti-vincent-ocasla/|title=Magnasanti (Vincent Ocasla)|date=January 7, 2014 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/q-a-vincent-ocasla-the-22-year-old-who-designed-the-perfect-totalitarian-city/ |title=Q+A: Vincent Ocasla, the 22-Year-Old Who Designed the Perfect Totalitarian City |date=April 30, 2011}}</ref>
* A 1999 episode of ''[[The Simpsons]]'', "[[Hello Gutter, Hello Fadder]]", opens with a 26-hour time lapse of Homer's bedroom while he oversleeps, with music similar to Phillip Glass Koyaanisqatsi score in the background.
* The [[Arkansas Governor's School]], a month long public residential academic program offered by the [[Clinton Foundation]] to rising seniors in the state of Arkansas, screens ''Koyaanisqatsi'' to every incoming class to prompt discussion regarding philosophy, political science, and English.   
* A 2010 episode of ''[[The Simpsons]]'', "[[Stealing First Base]]", features a parody [[Itchy & Scratchy]] film titled ''Koyaanis-Scratchy: Death Out of Balance''.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Short |first1=Hogan |title=11 Films You Didn't Know Were Perfect to Watch on 4/20 |url=http://beatroute.ca/2017/04/17/11-films-didnt-know-perfect-watch-420/ |access-date=August 25, 2018 |work=BeatRoute Magazine |date=April 17, 2017 }}{{Dead link|date=July 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

* The [[Stranger Things (season 4)|fourth season]] of the [[Netflix]] original series ''[[Stranger Things]]'' features the track "Prophecies" in its seventh episode.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/every-song-in-volume-1-of-stranger-things-season-4|access-date=2022-06-05|title=Every Song in Volume 1 of 'Stranger Things' Season 4|first=Tara|last=Bitran|website=[[Netflix]] |date=2022-05-27}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.elitedaily.com/entertainment/stranger-things-4-soundtrack-song-list|access-date=2022-06-05|title=The Stranger Things 4 Soundtrack Brings All the '80s Nostalgia|first=Ani|last=Bundel|date=2022-05-28}}</ref>
