'''''Hocus Pocus''''' is a 1993 American [[fantasy comedy film]]<ref>{{cite web|last=Brenner|first=Paul|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/hocus-pocus-v22697|website=[[AllMovie]]|title=Hocus Pocus (1993) – Kenny Ortega|access-date=March 14, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190323170501/https://www.allmovie.com/movie/hocus-pocus-v22697|archive-date=March 23, 2019}}</ref> directed by [[Kenny Ortega]] from a screenplay by [[Mick Garris]] and Neil Cuthbert, and a story by [[David Kirschner]] and Garris. It follows a comically villainous trio of [[witches]] ([[Bette Midler]], [[Sarah Jessica Parker]], and [[Kathy Najimy]]) who are inadvertently resurrected by a teenage boy ([[Omri Katz]]) in [[Salem, Massachusetts]], on [[Halloween]] night.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/hocus-pocus-plot-story-recap-1993/|title=‘Hocus Pocus’ Recap: What to Remember Before Watching ‘Hocus Pocus 2’|website=The Wrap|date=September 30, 2022|first=Dessi|last=Gomez|access-date=August 31, 2026}}</ref>

==Plot==
<!---Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], feature film summaries are not to exceed 700 words. Current wordcount 687. Please add nothing without checking the wordcount first.---->

On October 31, 1693, in [[Salem, Massachusetts]], Thackery Binx discovers that his younger sister Emily has been kidnapped by the three [[witches]] Winifred, Mary and Sarah Sanderson. Binx confronts the Sanderson sisters at their cottage, but not in time to save Emily, whose life force they have drained to keep themselves young. The witches punish Binx for interfering by turning him into an [[immortality|immortal]] black cat, so he must live forever with his guilt. Binx's friend Elijah rallies the townsfolk to arrest the Sandersons; they are condemned to death, but Winifred casts a curse during the hanging, claiming that a [[virgin]] will light the Black-Flame Candle in the cottage on a day when the [[full moon]] and [[All Hallows' Eve]] coincide, resurrecting the sisters. Binx decides to try to thwart the witches' plans.

Three centuries later, Max Dennison, who has recently moved to Salem from [[Los Angeles]], reluctantly takes his younger sister Dani out [[trick-or-treating]]. They meet up with his new classmate Allison Watts, on whom he has a crush.  Max, Dani, and Allison visit the former Sanderson cottage, which is now an abandoned museum. Max, who believes the story of the witches is "hocus pocus", inadvertently resurrects the witches by lighting the Black-Flame Candle. The witches attempt to suck out Dani's soul, but Max intervenes. Binx arrives and reveals that he can talk; following Binx's instructions, Max steals Winifred's [[spellbook]], and the children flee to a cemetery, which the witches cannot step into as it is [[consecrated ground|hallowed ground]].  From a distance, Winifred raises her former fiancé, Billy Butcherson, from his grave and sends him after the children, forcing them to leave the cemetery. Winifred also reveals to her sisters that, unless they can suck the life out of at least one child, the three of them will turn to dust when the sun rises.

Max, Dani, and Allison hurry to a [[Halloween party]] that most of the town's adults are attending. Max tries to warn his parents and the other adults of the danger, but the witches take the stage and use a performance of "[[I Put a Spell on You]]" to enchant the partying adults to dance until they die. Binx and the three children then lure the witches into the local high school's [[Kiln|pottery kiln]] and burn them alive; the witches temporarily disintegrate, but the curse revives them after the children have gone.

Not realizing that the witches have survived, Max and Allison open the spellbook, hoping to reverse the curse on Binx. This action allows the witches to find the children, abduct Binx and Dani, and retrieve the spellbook. Sarah uses her [[siren (mythology)|singing]] to lure all of Salem's children to the Sanderson cottage; however, Max and Allison rescue Dani and Binx by briefly tricking the witches into believing that [[Daylight saving time|the sun has risen an hour early]].

Back at the cemetery, Billy catches up to the children, cuts open his stitched mouth with Max's knife, and reveals that he hates Winifred and is on their side. The witches snatch Dani back after attacking the children from the air, and Winifred attempts to use their last vial of potion to suck out Dani's life force. Binx attacks Winifred to make her drop the potion, resulting in her throwing him to the ground near Emily's grave. Max catches and drinks the potion to save Dani. Winifred takes Max on her broomstick to suck out his life force, and the two of them fight in mid-air. The rest of the party fend off Mary and Sarah, while Max and Winifred fall off the broom into the hallowed ground of the cemetery, causing Winifred to turn to stone. The sun rises, turning Mary and Sarah into dust, with Winifred's stone body also exploding into dust shortly after. With the witches vanquished, Billy gratefully returns to his grave to sleep while, at the same time, Binx dies in his cat form. Dani discovers the body of Binx and begins to weep, but his spirit, now free and returned to human form due to the curse being broken, thanks the children. Emily's spirit then appears to escort her brother to the afterlife.

In a [[mid-credits scene]], the enchanted adults are freed from the spell and leave the Halloween party, exhausted from dancing all night. Some bullies that were earlier trapped in the Sanderson museum still wait in a cage to be freed, while the living eye on the witches' spellbook opens and looks around, suggesting [[Hocus Pocus 2|the Sanderson Sisters may return.]]

== Production ==
=== Development ===
In the 1994 TV documentary ''Hocus Pocus: Begin the Magic'', and on the film's [[Blu-ray]] release, producer [[David Kirschner]] explains how he came up with the idea for the film one night. He and his young daughter were sitting outside and his neighbor's black cat strayed by. Kirschner invented a tale of how the Sanderson Sisters came about, stemming from the Winifred Brood.

''Hocus Pocus'' started life as a script by [[Mick Garris]], that was bought by [[Walt Disney Pictures]] in 1984.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/hocus-pocus-originally-darker-kids-movie/|title=Hocus Pocus Was Originally a Much Darker Kids Movie|website=Den of Geek|date=September 29, 2022|first=David|last=Crow|access-date=August 31, 2026}}</ref> The film's working title was ''Disney's Halloween House''; it was much darker and scarier, and its protagonists were all 12-year-olds.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/hocus-pocus-1993-disney-comedy-was-scarier-in-early-draft|title=The original ‘Hocus Pocus’ script conjured ‘much scarier’ vibes than Disney’s finished comedy|website=Syfy|date=October 27, 2022|first=Benjamin|last=Bullard|access-date=August 31, 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://ew.com/movies/hocus-pocus-cast-original-script-scarier/|title=''Hocus Pocus'' cast recalls 'much scarier' original draft before it became 'female ''Three Stooges'' '|website=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=October 30, 2022|first=Joey|last=Nolfi|access-date=August 31, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/hocus-pocus-original-script-scarier-1234777650/|title=Original ‘Hocus Pocus’ Script Was ‘Much Scarier’ Before Becoming ‘Three Stooges’ Witch Story|website=Indiewire|date=October 31, 2022|first=Samantha|last=Bergeson|access-date=August 31, 2026}}</ref> Garris and Kirschner pitched it to [[Steven Spielberg]]'s [[Amblin Entertainment]]; Spielberg saw Disney as a competitor to Amblin in the family film market at the time and refused to co-produce a film with his "rival."<ref name=":0" />

=== Writing ===
Various rewrites were made to the script to make the film more comedic and made two of its young protagonists into teenagers; however, production was stalled several times until 1992, when Bette Midler expressed interest in the script and the project immediately went forward.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=https://postmortempodcast.libsyn.com/25th-anniversary-of-hocus-pocus|title=Post Mortem with Mick Garris: 25th Anniversary of Hocus Pocus!|website=postmortempodcast.libsyn.com|language=en|access-date=October 8, 2018|archive-date=October 9, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181009013346/https://postmortempodcast.libsyn.com/25th-anniversary-of-hocus-pocus|url-status=live}}</ref> Midler, who plays the central antagonist of the film (originally written for [[Cloris Leachman]]),<ref name=":0" /> is quoted as saying that '''''Hocus Pocus''''' "was the most fun I'd had in my career up to that point".{{citation needed|date=October 2012}}

=== Filming ===
The film is set in [[Salem, Massachusetts]], but most of it was shot on sound stages in [[Burbank, California]]. However, its daytime scenes were filmed in Salem and [[Marblehead, Massachusetts]], during two weeks of filming with the principal cast.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/hocus-pocus-salem-history-excerpt-1235147056/|title=How Disney’s Sleeper Hit ‘Hocus Pocus’ Changed Salem Forever|website=Rolling Stone|date=October 31, 2024|first=Shannon|last=Carlin|access-date=August 29, 2026}}</ref>

[[Pioneer Village (Salem, Massachusetts)|Pioneer Village]], a recreation of early-colonial Salem, was used for the opening scenes set in 1693.<ref name="Slane"/> Other locations included [[Old Burial Hill (Marblehead, Massachusetts)|Old Burial Hill]] in Marblehead, where Max is accosted by Ice and Jay, the Old Town Hall in Salem, where the town Halloween party takes place, and Phillips Elementary School, where the witches are trapped in a kiln. The exterior of Max and Dani's house is a private residence on Ocean Avenue in Salem.<ref name="Slane">{{cite news| url=https://www.boston.com/travel/massachusetts-news/2018/10/19/hocus-pocus-movie-filming-locations-in-salem-marblehead/| title=A local's guide to 'Hocus Pocus', 25 years later| first=Kevin|last=Slane| publisher=www.boston.com| date=October 20, 2018| access-date=October 20, 2018| archive-date=October 20, 2018| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181020014431/https://www.boston.com/culture/travel/2018/10/19/hocus-pocus-movie-filming-locations-in-salem-marblehead| url-status=live}}</ref>

== Music ==
{{main|Hocus Pocus (soundtrack){{!}}''Hocus Pocus'' (soundtrack)}}

[[File:John Debney.JPG|thumb|190x190px|[[John Debney]] composed the film's score.]]

The [[Film score|musical score]] for ''Hocus Pocus'' was composed and conducted by [[John Debney]]. [[James Horner]] was originally slated to score the film, but became unavailable at the last minute, so Debney had to score the entire film in two weeks. Even though he did not score the film, Horner came back to write the theme for Sarah (sung by Sarah Jessica Parker, more commonly known as "Come Little Children") which is featured in Intrada's Complete Edition of the score.

Debney released a promotional score through the internet containing 19 tracks from the film. Bootlegs were subsequently released across the internet, primarily because the promotional release missed the entire opening sequence music.

== Release ==
The film was theatrically released by [[Walt Disney Pictures]] in the United States and Canada on July 16, 1993. The film received mixed reviews from film critics at the time of its release.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a24481750/hocus-pocus-25th-anniversary-feminist-reinterpretation/|title=We Didn't Deserve ''Hocus Pocus'' in 1993!|website=Elle|first=Amanda|last=Fitzsimons|date=October 31, 2018|access-date=August 28, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2022/09/29/hocus-pocus-movie-1993-review-hocus-pocus-2/7938625001/|title='Hocus Pocus' wasn't always beloved. === Home media ===
[[Walt Disney Home Video]] released the film on [[VHS]] and [[LaserDisc]] in the United States and Canada on January 5, 1994.<ref>{{cite book |title=Hocus Pocus [VHS] |asin=6302968143}}</ref> and later to [[DVD]] on June 4, 2002.<ref name="Numbers">{{cite web |title=Hocus Pocus - DVD Sales |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1993/0HOPO-DVD.php |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111015223453/http://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1993/0HOPO-DVD.php |archive-date=October 15, 2011 |access-date=November 3, 2011 |publisher=The Numbers}}</ref> Following the film's release on DVD, it has continued to show strong annual sales, raking in more than $1 million in DVD sales each October.<ref name="Numbers" /> In the mid-to-late 1990s, the film was rebroadcast annually all throughout October on [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] and [[Disney Channel]] before switching over to [[Freeform (TV channel)|ABC Family]]'s ''[[13 Nights of Halloween]]'' lineup in the 2010s. The film has continuously brought record viewing numbers to the lineup, including a 2009 broadcast watched by 2.5 million viewers.<ref>{{cite web |date=November 3, 2009 |title=ABC Family's 11th annual "13 Nights of Halloween 2009" Scares Up Event-Best Deliveries |url=https://tvbythenumbers.zap2it.com/2009/11/03/abc-familys-11th-annual-13-nights-of-halloween-2009-scares-up-event-best-deliveries/32465/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120225221637/http://tvbythenumbers.zap2it.com/2009/11/03/abc-familys-11th-annual-13-nights-of-halloween-2009-scares-up-event-best-deliveries/32465/ |archive-date=February 25, 2012 |access-date=October 30, 2011 |publisher=[[TV by the Numbers]]}}</ref> In 2011, an October 29 airing became the lineup's most watched program, with 2.8 million viewers.<ref name="TV by the Numbers">{{cite web |title=ABC Family's "13 Nights of Halloween 2011" Scares Up Record Crowd |url=https://tvbythenumbers.zap2it.com/2011/11/01/abc-family%E2%80%99s-13-nights-of-halloween-2011-scares-up-record-crowd/109316/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111106021806/http://tvbythenumbers.zap2it.com/2011/11/01/abc-family%E2%80%99s-13-nights-of-halloween-2011-scares-up-record-crowd/109316/ |archive-date=November 6, 2011 |access-date=November 3, 2011 |publisher=[[TV by the Numbers]] |df=mdy-all}}</ref> On September 4, 2012, the film was released on [[Blu-ray]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Upcoming Disney Catalog Releases for 2012 |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=8386 |access-date=March 16, 2012 |publisher=Blu-ray.com}}</ref> Disney re-released the film on Blu-ray and Digital HD on September 2, 2018, as part of the film's 25th anniversary. == Reception ==

=== Box office ===
''Hocus Pocus'' was released July 16, 1993, and came in fourth place on its opening weekend behind ''[[Jurassic Park (film)|Jurassic Park]]'', ''[[In the Line of Fire]]'' and ''[[The Firm (1993 film)|The Firm]]'', grossing $8.1&nbsp;million.<ref>{{cite news|last=Fox|first=David|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-07-20-ca-14806-story.html|title=Weekend Box Office : So Far, This Is Summer to Beat|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|date=July 20, 1993|access-date=July 17, 2019|archive-date=July 17, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190717051209/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-07-20-ca-14806-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It dropped from the top ten ranking after two weeks of release.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2153350657/weekend/|title=Hocus Pocus (1993) – Weekend Box Office Results|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> The film was released the same day as ''[[Free Willy]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/calendar/1993-07-16/|title=Upcoming Movies in Theaters|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> According to Kirschner, Disney chose to release ''Hocus Pocus'' in July to take advantage of children being off school for the summer.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pcPc21XI-mI&list=PLRxFhP-1mXPvH0KTW7l823uQc5B4kMlyh |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211213/pcPc21XI-mI |archive-date=2021-12-13 |url-status=live|title=Interview with David Kirschner|date=July 18, 2018|website=YouTube|access-date=February 26, 2020}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

In October 2020, amid the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], ''Hocus Pocus'' was re-released in 2,570 theaters. It made $1.9&nbsp;million over the weekend, finishing second behind ''[[Tenet|Tenet]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://variety.com/2020/film/news/hocus-pocus-box-office-rerelease-1234791512/ |title='Hocus Pocus' Leads Box Office Nearly Three Decades After Original Release |date=October 3, 2020 |access-date=October 4, 2020 |archive-date=October 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201004014803/https://variety.com/2020/film/news/hocus-pocus-box-office-rerelease-1234791512/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=October 4, 2020 |url=https://deadline.com/2020/10/tenet-hocus-pocus-weekend-box-office-cineworld-regal-shutdown-1234591012/ |title='Tenet' Reaches $45M+ Domestic, $307M WW Before Possible Cineworld & Partial Regal Shutdown – Sunday Final |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=October 4, 2020 |archive-date=October 5, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201005183208/https://deadline.com/2020/10/tenet-hocus-pocus-weekend-box-office-cineworld-regal-shutdown-1234591012/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The following two weekends it made $1.2&nbsp;million and $756,000, respectively.<ref>{{cite news |date= October 11, 2020 |last= Brueggemann |first= Tom |title= 'Tenet' No Longer #1 as 'The War With Grandpa' Wins Weekend Box Office With $3.6 Million |url= https://www.indiewire.com/2020/10/tenet-the-war-with-grandpa-wins-weekend-box-office-1234592050/ |website= [[IndieWire]] |access-date= October 11, 2020 |archive-date= October 12, 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201012093705/https://www.indiewire.com/2020/10/tenet-the-war-with-grandpa-wins-weekend-box-office-1234592050/ |url-status= live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=October 18, 2020 |url=https://deadline.com/2020/10/the-honest-thief-2-hearts-jacob-elordi-weekend-box-office-1234598767/ |title=Liam Neeson Action Pic 'Honest Thief' Nabbing $4M+ Total By Sunday; === Critical response ===
On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Hocus Pocus'' has an approval rating of 42% based on 65 reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "Harmlessly hokey yet never much more than mediocre, ''Hocus Pocus'' is a muddled family-friendly effort that fails to live up to the talents of its impressive cast."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1045250-hocus_pocus/|title=Hocus Pocus (1993)|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=November 15, 2025|archive-date=December 29, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161229101615/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1045250-hocus_pocus|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a weighted average score of 43 out of 100, based on 27 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=hocus-pocus|type=movie|title=Hocus Pocus Reviews|access-date=October 19, 2021}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Home – Cinemascore|url=https://www.cinemascore.com|access-date=July 13, 2020|website=CinemaScore|archive-date=January 2, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180102130540/https://www.cinemascore.com/|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Gene Siskel]], reviewing for ''[[The Chicago Tribune]]'', remarked that the film was a "dreadful witches' comedy with the only tolerable moment coming when Bette Midler presents a single song".<ref>{{cite news|last=Siskel|first=Gene|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1993-07-16-9307160021-story.html|title='Free Willy' Is For Movie Dieters – It's Lite|newspaper=Chicago Tribune|date=July 16, 1993|access-date=July 17, 2019|archive-date=July 17, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190717051210/https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1993-07-16-9307160021-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] in ''[[The Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film one star out of a possible four, writing that it was "a confusing cauldron in which there is great activity but little progress, and a lot of hysterical shrieking".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/hocus-pocus-1993|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|title=Hocus Pocus|date=July 16, 1993|website=Roger Ebert.com|access-date=October 25, 2019|archive-date=October 24, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191024222159/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/hocus-pocus-1993|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[The Miami Herald]]'' called it "a pretty lackluster affair", adding this comment: "Despite the triple-threat actress combo, ''Hocus Pocus'' won't be the ''[[Sister Act]]'' of 1993. There are a lot of gotta-sees this summer, and this isn't one of them."<ref>{{cite news|url=http://nl.newsbank.com/nl-search/we/Archives?p_product=MH&s_site=miami&p_multi=MH&p_theme=realcities&p_action=search&p_maxdocs=200&p_topdoc=1&p_text_direct-0=0EB4CDD0575B554D&p_field_direct-0=document_id&p_perpage=10&p_sort=YMD_date:D&s_trackval=GooglePM|title=Hocus Pocus starts strong, goes flat|date=July 16, 1993|access-date=August 9, 2010|url-access=registration|work=The Miami Herald|page=7G|archive-date=October 24, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121024073839/http://nl.newsbank.com/nl-search/we/Archives?p_product=MH&s_site=miami&p_multi=MH&p_theme=realcities&p_action=search&p_maxdocs=200&p_topdoc=1&p_text_direct-0=0EB4CDD0575B554D&p_field_direct-0=document_id&p_perpage=10&p_sort=YMD_date:D&s_trackval=GooglePM|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote that the film "has flashes of visual stylishness but virtually no grip on its story".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1993/07/16/movies/review-film-bette-midler-queen-witch-in-heavy-makeup.html|last=Maslin|first=Janet|title=Review/Film; Bette Midler, Queen Witch in Heavy Makeup|date=July 16, 1993|access-date=August 9, 2010|work=[[The New York Times]]|archive-date=July 27, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090727031558/http://www.nytimes.com/1993/07/16/movies/review-film-bette-midler-queen-witch-in-heavy-makeup.html|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Ty Burr]] of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave the film a C−, calling it "acceptable scary-silly kid fodder that adults will find only mildly insulting. Unless they're Bette Midler fans. In which case it's depressing as hell"; and stating that while Najimy and Parker "have their moments of ramshackle comic inspiration, and the passable special effects should keep younger campers transfixed [...] [T]he sight of the Divine Miss M. mugging her way through a cheesy supernatural kiddie comedy is, to say the least, dispiriting."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/article/1993/07/23/hocus-pocus-2/|last=Burr|first=Ty|title=Hocus Pocus Review|date=July 23, 1993|access-date=October 28, 2011|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|archive-date=October 21, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171021055831/http://ew.com/article/1993/07/23/hocus-pocus-2/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Kim Newman]] of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' gave the film two stars out of five, writing, "Trying to break expectations isn't always a wise idea and here Disney show how not to do it. With this supposed-family movie, they disappoint on nearly every level. The plot is weak, the action poor and it's got Bette Midler, simply dreadful."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/hocus-pocus-review/|last=Newman|first=Kim|title=Hocus Pocus Review|date=|access-date=October 9, 2022|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]}}</ref>

On September 15, 2015, the Hocus Pocus Villain Spelltacular was introduced at the [[Magic Kingdom]] as a part of [[Mickey's Not-So-Scary Halloween Party]]. The show introduces new actresses as the Sanderson Sisters who try to make a villain party and summon or attract various [[Disney villain]]s in the process.<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Snetiker|first1=Marc|title='Hocus Pocus' stage show will haunt Disney World this Halloween|url=https://www.ew.com/article/2015/05/27/hocus-pocus-stage-show-will-haunt-disney-world-this-halloween|access-date=September 16, 2015|magazine=Entertainment Weekly|date=May 27, 2015|archive-date=September 4, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150904214335/http://www.ew.com/article/2015/05/27/hocus-pocus-stage-show-will-haunt-disney-world-this-halloween|url-status=live}}</ref> In September 2016, entertainment critic Aaron Wallace published ''Hocus Pocus in Focus: The Thinking Fan's Guide to Disney's Halloween Classic,'' the first full-length book written about the film. The book includes a foreword by [[Thora Birch]] and afterword by [[Mick Garris]]. Billed as a "lighthearted but scholarly look at the film", the book analyzes the film's major themes, which it identifies as festivity, nostalgia, home, horror, virginity, feminism, Broadway-style musical moments, sibling rivalry, "Spielbergian" filmmaking style, Disney villain traditions, and more. Wallace also analyzes [[Walt Disney World]]'s Hocus Pocus Villain Spelltacular as part of the film's legacy and includes "the largest collection of ''Hocus Pocus'' fun facts and trivia ever assembled", complete with extensive endnote citations.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.laughingplace.com/w/news/2016/08/30/author-aaron-wallace-announces-first-ever-book-dedicated-disneys-hocus-pocus/|title=Author Aaron Wallace Announces First-Ever Book Dedicated to Disney's "Hocus Pocus" - LaughingPlace.com|first=Laughing Place Disney|last=Newsdesk|date=August 30, 2016|access-date=September 23, 2016|archive-date=September 24, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160924031012/http://www.laughingplace.com/w/news/2016/08/30/author-aaron-wallace-announces-first-ever-book-dedicated-disneys-hocus-pocus/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://attractionsmagazine.com/new-book-hocus-pocus-now-available/|title=New book about 'Hocus Pocus' now available|date=September 21, 2016|access-date=September 23, 2016|archive-date=September 24, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160924025249/http://attractionsmagazine.com/new-book-hocus-pocus-now-available/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.aaronwallaceonline.com/book/hocus-pocus-book-thinking-fan-disney/|title=Hocus Pocus in Focus: A Hocus Pocus Book for The Thinking Fan|work=Aaron Wallace Online: Books, Podcasts, and Reviews |access-date=September 23, 2016|archive-date=September 25, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160925085949/http://www.aaronwallaceonline.com/book/hocus-pocus-book-thinking-fan-disney/|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Possible musical adaptation ===
During an interview on ''The Big Seance Podcast'' in September 2021, creator David Kirschner revealed that a Broadway adaptation of the original film is in development.<ref>{{cite podcast|url=https://bigseancepodcast.com/195-hocus-pocus-and-the-sequel-with-creatorwriter-and-executive-producer-david-kirschner-big-seance|title=195. Hocus Pocus and the Sequel with Creator/Writer and Executive Producer David Kirschner|website=The Big Seance Podcast|date=September 2, 2021|first=Patrick|last=Keller|access-date=May 3, 2023}}</ref> During an interview leading up to the release of ''Hocus Pocus 2'' in September 2022, he stated that "I just want to pinch myself, and I'm just afraid that I'm going to be nine years old and on a [[Little League]] field again. But it's just so wonderful just to stand back and watch all of this ... I think you're going to be very pleased." Kenny Ortega revealed to ''[[Forbes]]'' in 2020 his interest in possibly returning to direct the production, saying "I think a ''Hocus Pocus'' musical would be great fun, really great fun."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-09-30 |last=Walsh |first=Savannah |title=A ''Hocus Pocus'' Musical May Soon Run Amok on Broadway |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/09/a-hocus-pocus-musical-may-soon-run-amok-on-broadway |website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |access-date=2022-10-14}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-09-15 |last=Thompson |first=Simon |title=Kenny Ortega Talks Disney's 'Hocus Pocus' And His Dream Of Turning It Into A Broadway Musical |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/simonthompson/2020/09/15/kenny-ortega-disney-hocus-pocus-broadway-musical-sequel-halloween/ |website=Forbes |access-date=2022-10-14}}</ref>
