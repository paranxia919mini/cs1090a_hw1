'''''Afternoons of Solitude''''' ({{langx|es|'''Tardes de soledad'''|links=no}}) is a 2024 [[documentary film]] directed by [[Albert Serra]] about the Peruvian bullfighter [[Andrés Roca Rey]].<ref>[https://www.filmlinc.org/films/afternoons-of-solitude/] ''Afternoons of Solitude'' [[Albert_Serra]] Filmlinc</ref> Though Roca is on screen throughout, the film contains neither commentary nor interviews, and reveals very little about Roca's personality, motives, or his life outside [[bullfighting]].<ref name="romney" />

The film world premiered at the main competition of the [[72nd San Sebastián International Film Festival]] on 23 September 2024, where it won the [[Golden Shell]]. It was theatrically released in Spain on 7 March 2025 by [[A Contracorriente Films]].

It earned [[European Film Awards|EFA Awards]] nominations for [[European Film Award for Best Film|Best Film]] and [[European Film Award for Best Documentary|Best Documentary]].

== Plot ==
The film depicts matador [[Andrés Roca Rey]] and his entourage throughout fourteen [[Bullfighting|corrida]]s. There are long extended fight sequences filmed very close up, punctuated by torero Roca and his entourage, before or after these sometimes bloody sequences, traveling by car and talking about the performance of Roca. There are also scenes of Roca in his hotel room praying and dressing or undressing from his flamboyant [[traje de luces]] (suit of lights) costume before or after these corridas.<ref>[https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/afternoons-of-solitude-san-sebastian-review/5197455.article] Review of ''Afternoons Of Solitude'', San Sebastian, by Jonathan Romney, 23 September 2024</ref>

== Production ==
The film is a Spanish-French-Portuguese co-production by Andergraun Films alongside Lacima Producciones, Idéale Audiences, and Rosa Filmes.<ref name="barraclough">{{Cite web|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/global/films-boutique-albert-serra-afternoons-of-solitude-1236151833/|title=Films Boutique Picks Up Albert Serra's 'Afternoons of Solitude' (EXCLUSIVE)|date=20 September 2024|first=Leo|last=Barraclough}}</ref> Production was extended by five years, three years for shooting footage and two for editing the material.<ref name="fotogramas" />

The color [[cinematography]], by Artur Tort Pujol, is often very closely focused on Roca and his face, isolating him in a way similar to [[Douglas Gordon]] and [[Philippe Parreno]]'s 2006 documentary of [[Zinedine Zidane]] called ''[[Zidane: A 21st Century Portrait]]''.{{citation needed|date=November 2025}} Extreme closeups sometimes isolate details in the ring: like the bull's hooves or its face during its death throes or the side of the [[picador]]'s horse. The film makes however no overt comment about bullfighting.

== Music ==
The use of music contains that of Marc Verdaguer and [[Camille Saint-Saëns]] and [[Jorma Kaukonen]] of the [[Jefferson Airplane]]'s "[[Embryonic Journey (instrumental)|Embryonic Journey]]" [[Flamenco]]-style instrumental song from their 1967 [[Surrealistic Pillow]] LP.<ref>[https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/afternoons-of-solitude-san-sebastian-review/5197455.article]  Review of ''Afternoons Of Solitude'', San Sebastian, by Jonathan Romney, 23 September 2024</ref>

== Reception ==
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|88||43|''Afternoons of Solitude'' is a stark, ritualistic immersion into bullfighting whose open-ended editing and sensory repetition pursue an unflinching purity, marking it as one of Albert Serra's most mature and unexpectedly accessible works.|ref=yes|access-date=14 March 2026}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 85 out of 100, based on 12 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Afternoons of Solitude'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/afternoons-of-solitude |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=14 March 2026 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

Luis Martínez of ''[[El Mundo (Spain)|El Mundo]]'' gave the film 4 stars, declaring it as "a monumental, precious, precise, brutal, grief-stricken, tragic, beautiful and, from any point of view, unique film".<ref>{{Cite web|first=Luis|last=Martínez|website=[[El Mundo (Spain)|El Mundo]]|url=https://www.elmundo.es/cultura/cine/2024/09/23/66f15e53fdddff513b8b4577.html|date=23 September 2024|title=La sangre y la vida: Albert Serra nos hunde en lo profundo del mito táurico en 'Tardes de soledad' (****)}}</ref>

Jonathan Romney of ''[[ScreenDaily]]'' described the film as "a deeply immersive work and an unashamedly repetitive one".<ref name="romney">{{Cite web|website=[[ScreenDaily]]|url=https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/afternoons-of-solitude-san-sebastian-review/5197455.article|first=Jonathan|last=Romney|date=23 September 2024|title='Afternoons Of Solitude': San Sebastian Review}}</ref>

''Afternoons of Solitude'' topped [[Cahiers du Cinéma's Annual Top 10 Lists|''Cahiers du Cinéma''{{'}}s annual top 10 films list]] for 2025.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Cahiers du cinéma’s Top 10 Films of 2025 Includes Afternoons of Solitude, One Battle After Another, and Nouvelle Vague|url=https://thefilmstage.com/cahiers-du-cinemas-top-10-films-of-2025-includes-afternoons-of-solitude-one-battle-after-another-and-nouvelle-vague/|date=2025-11-28|access-date=2025-11-28|language=en-US}}</ref>
