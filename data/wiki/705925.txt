'''''Far and Away''''' is a 1992 American [[epic film|epic]] [[Western (genre)|Western]] [[romance film|romantic]] [[adventure film|adventure]] [[drama (film and television)|drama film]] directed by [[Ron Howard]] from a screenplay by [[Bob Dolman]] and a story by Howard and Dolman. It stars [[Tom Cruise]] and [[Nicole Kidman]]. This was the last cinematography credit for [[Mikael Salomon]] before he moved on to a directing career.  The music score was by [[John Williams]]. It was screened out of competition at the [[1992 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/5/year/1992.html |title=Festival de Cannes: Far and Away |access-date=2009-08-17 |work=festival-cannes.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121005005934/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/5/year/1992.html |archive-date=2012-10-05 }}</ref>

Cruise and Kidman, who were married at the time, play Joseph Donnelly and Shannon Christie, [[Irish people|Irish]] [[Irish Americans|immigrants]] seeking their fortune in the 1890s [[United States|America]], eventually taking part in the [[Land Run of 1893]].<ref name="lawsuit" /><ref>{{cite magazine|title=Irish Risky|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=1992-05-22|url=https://ew.com/article/1992/05/22/tom-cruise-and-nicole-kidman-star-far-and-away/|access-date=2010-12-08|first=Melina|last=Gerosa|archive-date=2013-11-03|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131103233656/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,310539,00.html|url-status=live}}</ref> This was [[Cyril Cusack]]'s final film before his death the following year.

''Far and Away'' was released by [[Universal Pictures]] on May 22, 1992. The film received mixed reviews from critics and grossed $137.8 million against a $60 million budget.

==Plot==
In 1892 Ireland, impoverished [[tenant farmer]] Joseph Donnelly dreams of one day owning his own land. After his father Joseph Sr. dies, their family home is burned by their landlord Daniel Christie's men, due to unpaid rent. Joseph goes to Christie's home to kill him in revenge but his daughter Shannon stabs his leg with a pitchfork. Daniel's wife Nora and Shannon nurse Joseph back to health so he can be hanged for the attempted murder.

Headstrong Shannon, who resists being molded into a refined lady, dreams of going to America where land is being given away for free. When Joseph tries to flee the Christie home, Stephen Chase, the Christies' brutal estate manager, intercepts him. He challenges Joseph to a duel for the next morning. The next morning, Daniel tells Joseph that he knew nothing about the tenant evictions or unfair rents. As the duel is about to commence, Shannon arrives in a horse-drawn cart and convinces Joseph to run away with her to America, needing a servant and protector.

While sailing to America, Shannon and Joseph meet McGuire, who confirms Shannon's story about the [[Oklahoma Territory|Oklahoma]] land giveaway. He explains that Oklahoma is a thousand miles inland and the desired land is claimed in a race. Shannon reveals she has a collection of silver spoons that will pay their way to Oklahoma.

Upon arriving in [[Boston]], McGuire is shot dead in the street and Shannon loses her silver spoons that McGuire stole. An Irish boy introduces the couple to Mike Kelly, a ruthless Irish [[ward boss]] who arranges jobs and lodging. Joseph soon becomes a regular in [[bare-knuckle boxing]] matches at Kelly's club for extra cash. Over the following weeks, Joseph and Shannon struggle adapting to their new lives. Romantic tension flares between them, especially after Grace, a dancer at Kelly's club, flirts with Joseph, inciting Shannon's jealousy.

One night, Joseph discovers Shannon is dancing in Kelly's [[American burlesque|burlesque]] show for extra money. When he storms in to retrieve her, Kelly and the other Irishmen there beg him to fight an Italian contender, promising to split the two-hundred-dollar purse. It will be enough money to go to Oklahoma. Joseph is winning until he notices one of his backers groping Shannon. Distracted, Joseph is defeated by a [[sucker punch]]. As payback for the loss, Kelly and his men take all of the couple's money and toss Shannon and Joseph out into the winter streets.

Cold and starving, the pair break into a seemingly abandoned house. They share a romantic moment before the owners return, chasing them off and shooting Shannon in the back as they escape. Joseph, knowing the Christies are in Boston looking for Shannon, takes her to them. After leaving Shannon in their care, he heads west to the [[Ozarks|Ozarks Mountains]] and finds work laying train track. Months later, while en route to the next work site, Joseph notices a wagon train headed for Oklahoma and joins them.

Joseph arrives in Oklahoma just in time for the [[Land Run of 1893]] and finds that Shannon, Stephen and the Christies are also there. Stephen, after seeing Joseph talking to Shannon, threatens to kill him if he goes near her again. Joseph, riding a half-broke horse, outpaces everyone during the race. He catches up to Shannon and Stephen, who prematurely sneaked across the starting line. Before Joseph can plant his claim flag, a fight breaks out between him and Stephen, ending with Joseph being crushed by Stephen's horse. Shannon rushes to Joseph, rejecting Stephen. Joseph professes his love for Shannon and seems to die but is suddenly revived when she reciprocates his feelings. They drive his flag into the ground and claim their land together.

==Production==
[[Ron Howard]]'s grandfather, who participated in the [[Land Run of 1893]], 99-years prior to the film's release, was the inspiration for inclusion of the plotline in the movie.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Engel |first1=Joel |title=FILM: Sets Big and Small Challenge Movie Makers; 'Far and Away': Was the Race Going to Turn Into a Stampede? |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/06/07/archives/film-sets-big-and-small-challenge-movie-makers-far-and-away-was-the.html |work=The New York Times |date=7 June 1992}}</ref> The film was shot in Montana for business reasons, but the [[Oklahoma Historical Society]] was involved in its production.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://newsok.com/article/2395384|title=Oklahomans Become Extras in "Far and Away"|last=Davis|first=Sandi|work=[[The Oklahoman]]|date=1992-05-22|access-date=2016-08-31}}</ref> [[Imagine Entertainment|Imagine Production Co.]] toured the areas around Montana for a week. They visited different areas before selecting [[Billings, Montana]].  Howard, whose film ''[[Backdraft (film)|Backdraft]]'' was in the stages of being released in theatres at the time, arrived in Billings to begin the groundwork for the film. One site outside of town was a {{Convert | 12000 | acre | km2 | adj = on}} ranch, which was going to be used to film the Oklahoma Land Rush scene. Working titles for the film included ''The Irish Story''<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b7b4c76c3|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160924235706/http://www.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b7b4c76c3|url-status=dead|archive-date=September 24, 2016|title=Far and Away (1992)|work=[[British Film Institute]]|access-date=2016-08-31}}</ref> and ''An Irish Story''.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/1991/01/27/cruise-new-wife-to-star-in-an-irish-story/|title=Cruise, New Wife To Star In 'An Irish Story'|author=<!-- Staff -->|work=[[Orlando Sentinel]]|date=1991-01-27|access-date=2016-08-31}}</ref>

Principal photography began in Montana on May 28, 1991. After several weeks of preparation, the cast and crew filmed the Oklahoma Land Rush scene on July 7, 1991. Eight hundred riders and extras, nine hundred horses, mule, oxen and two hundred wagons were used on a quarter-mile wide set. Nine cameras were used to film the action sequences. During the filming of the scene, four people broke bones and one horse died.<ref name="lawsuit">{{cite news |date= 1992-06-14 |first= Jane |last= Galbraith |title= A look inside Hollywood and the movies. : LEGAL DEPT. : Lawsuit, Lawsuit on the Range |work= [[Los Angeles Times]] |url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-06-14-ca-969-story.html |access-date= 2020-02-22 |archive-date= August 6, 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240806185909/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-06-14-ca-969-story.html |url-status= live }}</ref>  Joseph Donnelly ([[Tom Cruise]])'s boxing match was filmed at the [[Billings station|Billings Depot]]. Local area residents were used as extras for the sequence.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://billingsgazette.com/special-section/gazette-125/covering-celebs-gazette-follows-famous-folks-in-town/article_c14cc102-5543-11df-be4b-001cc4c002e0.html|title=Covering celebs Gazette follows famous folks in town|last=Healy|first=Donna|work=[[Billings Gazette]]|date=2010-05-02|access-date=2016-08-31}}</ref> [[American Humane Society]] reported that "The production company not only met American Humane's Guidelines, but went that extra mile to ensure both the physical and mental wellness of the animals."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Far And Away |url=https://www.americanhumane.org/production/far-and-away/ |access-date=2026-01-11 |website=American Humane Society |language=en-US}}</ref>
After filming wrapped in Billings, the cast and crew traveled to [[Dublin]], Ireland, to complete filming. [[Ardmore Studios]] in [[Wicklow]] was used to film interior sequences,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.independent.ie/regionals/braypeople/entertainment/kevins-starring-role-in-wicklow-film-industry-ardmore-chief-a-key-player-in-irish-film-business-27601063.html|title=Kevin's starring role in Wicklow film industry Ardmore chief a key player in Irish film business|author=<!-- Staff -->|work=[[The Irish Independent]]|date=2011-08-15|access-date=2016-08-31}}</ref> and the streets of [[Boston]] were filmed in Dublin.

It was the first film shot in [[Super Panavision 70#Panavision System 65/Super 70|Panavision Super 70]]<ref name=dv>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|date=May 21, 1992|title=Panavision redefines the wide-body look|page=17|last=Everett|first=Todd}}</ref> and the first film to be shot in [[70 mm film|70 mm]] in a decade since ''[[Tron]]'' (1982).<ref name=nat>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|date=May 21, 1992|title=Uni/Imagine throw dice 'Far and Away'|page=17|last=Natale|first=Richard}}</ref><ref name=AFI/> The [[Arriflex 765]] camera was also used, as the camera was capable of 100 [[Frame rate|frames per second]] which was used for [[Slow motion|slow-motion]] shots during the Oklahoma land rush scene.<ref name=dv/>

==Release==
''Far and Away'' was released on May 22, 1992, in 1,583 theaters, 163 of which were in 70 mm.<ref name=AFI>{{cite web|url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/59238-FAR-ANDAWAY?sid=3eb970ad-d84c-46ea-86ed-80ce5c3a7d57&sr=0.4158961&cp=1&pos=1|title=Far and Away|website=[[American Film Institute]]|date=1992|access-date=March 9, 2020}}</ref><ref name="BOM"/><ref name=nat/>

===Home media===
The film was originally released on [[VHS]] and [[LaserDisc]] with it then released in the United States on [[DVD]] in May 1998 by [[Universal Pictures Home Entertainment]] with subtitles in English, Spanish, and French.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/far-and-away|title=Far and Away|date=May 28, 1998|access-date=March 9, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/dvd/Far-and-Away-DVD/2509/|title=Far and Away DVD|website=Blu-ray.com|date=1998|access-date=March 9, 2020}}</ref> It was first released as a [[Blu-ray]] disc and HD download package on March 4, 2014, with one extra feature, a [[theatrical trailer]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Far-and-Away-Blu-ray/37218/|title=Far and Away Blu-ray|website=Blu-ray.com|date=March 4, 2014|access-date=March 9, 2020}}</ref>

===Extended TV cut===
For its airing on [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] in March 1995, the network reinstated 35 minutes of deleted scenes to fill two two-hour blocks over two nights.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://bootlegcomparisons.com/2019/03/20/far-and-away-theatrical-blu-ray-vs-abc-television-broadcast/|title=Far and Away: Theatrical Blu-ray Vs. ABC Television Broadcast|last=christophernguyen726|date=2019-03-20|website=Bootleg Comparisons |access-date=2019-04-09}}</ref> The 170 minute version has never been officially released on home media.

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film, which cost $60 million to make, earned $13 million in its first weekend at the box office<ref name="BOM" /><ref>{{cite news|title= 'Lethal Weapon,' 'Sister Act' Pack a Sales Punch : Box office: The two films dominate weekend ticket action. But 'Far and Away,' starring Tom Cruise, lags behind.|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=1992-06-01|url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-06-01-ca-375-story.html|access-date=2011-01-12|first=David J.|last=Fox}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title= 'Lethal Weapon 3' Destroying Records|work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=1992-05-19|url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-05-19-ca-386-story.html|access-date=2010-12-08|first=David J.|last=Fox}}</ref> and stumbled at the box office, making only $58 million in the United States and Canada.<ref name="BOM" /><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|last=Putzer|first=Gerald|date=January 3, 1993|title=Sequels are B.O. Winners|url=https://variety.com/1993/film/news/sequels-are-b-o-winners-102555/|access-date=October 12, 2019|archive-date=October 12, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191012193804/https://variety.com/1993/film/news/sequels-are-b-o-winners-102555/|url-status=live}}</ref> It was the third highest-grossing film in Ireland with a gross of £0.8 million.<ref name=gross>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Screen International]]|date=26 March 1993|page=34|title=Statistics}}</ref> It grossed $79 million internationally for a worldwide total of $137 million.<ref name="BOM" />

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]] the film has an approval rating of 51% based on reviews from 39 critics. The site's critics' consensus reads: "Handsome and simplistic, ''Far and Away'' has the beauty of an American epic without the breadth."<ref>{{Cite web |title= Far and Away (1992) |url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/far_and_away/ |website= [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher= [[Fandango Media]] |access-date= March 21, 2025 }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] it has a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]] score of 49 out of 100 based on reviews from 19 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Far and Away Reviews|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/far-and-away|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date= February 22, 2020 }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a grade "A" on a scale of A to F.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= FAR AND AWAY (1992) A |work= [[CinemaScore]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] praised the film's cinematography while criticizing its script: {{blockquote|''Far and Away'' is a movie that joins astonishing visual splendor with a story so simple-minded it seems intended for adolescents... It's depressing that such a lavish and expensive production, starring an important actor like Tom Cruise, could be devoted to such a shallow story.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=May 22, 1992 |title=Far and Away |url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19920522/REVIEWS/2052203018 |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun Times]] |publisher=[[Chicago Public Media]] |issn=1553-8478 |oclc=51500916 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100524134148/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19920522/REVIEWS/205220301 |url-status= dead |archive-date=May 24, 2010 |via=[[RogerEbert.com]] |access-date=December 8, 2010}}</ref>}}

[[Todd McCarthy]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it "handsomely mounted and amiably performed but leisurely and without much dramatic urgency."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=McCarthy |first=Todd |author-link=Todd McCarthy |date=May 11, 1992 |title=Far and Away |url=https://variety.com/1992/film/reviews/far-and-away-2-1200429747/ |magazine= [[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |issn=0042-2738 |oclc=60626328 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131106170143/https://variety.com/1992/film/reviews/far-and-away-2-1200429747/ |archive-date=November 6, 2013 |access-date=September 1, 2019}}</ref> [[Hal Hinson]] of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' wrote: "''Far and Away''... is the director's attempt to step into the cinematic shoes of directors [[John Ford]] and [[David Lean]]. And, certainly, he's stepped into something with this sprawling, old-fashioned melodrama."<ref>{{cite news |last=Hinson |first=Hal |author-link=Hal Hinson |date=May 22, 1992 |title=Far and Away |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/farandawaypg13hinson_a0a776.htm |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |publisher=[[Graham Holdings|The Washington Post Company]] {{small |now Nash Holdings}} |issn=0190-8286 |oclc=2269358 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121111171652/https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/farandawaypg13hinson_a0a776.htm |archive-date=November 11, 2012 |access-date= September 1, 2019}}</ref>
