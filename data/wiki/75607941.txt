'''''Emilia Pérez''''' ({{IPA|es-419|eˈmilja ˈpeɾes|lang}}) is a 2024 Spanish-language French musical [[crime film]] written and directed by [[Jacques Audiard]]. It is based on Audiard's [[Libretto|opera libretto]] of the same name, which he loosely adapted from a chapter of the 2018 novel ''[[Écoute (novel)|Écoute]]'' by French writer Boris Razon. The film follows a Mexican cartel leader ([[Karla Sofía Gascón]]) who aims to disappear and [[Gender transition|transition into a woman]] with the help of a lawyer ([[Zoe Saldaña]]). [[Selena Gomez]], [[Adriana Paz]], [[Mark Ivanir]], and [[Édgar Ramírez]] also appear in starring roles.

''Emilia Pérez'' had its world premiere on 18 May 2024 at the [[2024 Cannes Film Festival|77th Cannes Film Festival]], where it won the [[Jury Prize (Cannes Film Festival)|Jury Prize]] and the [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] award for its female ensemble. It was theatrically released by Pathé in France on 21 August 2024. The film received generally positive reviews from critics in the United States and Europe, who praised the direction, music, performances and themes, but received backlash on multiple fronts.<ref>{{cite news |date=28 January 2025 |title=Why 'Emilia Pérez' is loved by Hollywood and hated by everyone else |url=https://www.economist.com/culture/2025/01/28/why-emilia-perez-is-loved-by-hollywood-and-hated-by-everyone-else |access-date=3 March 2025 |newspaper=[[The Economist]] |issn=0013-0613}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Marquez Guajardo |first=Alejandra |date=14 February 2025 |title='Emilia Pérez' was nominated for 13 Oscars. Why do so many people hate it? |url=https://theconversation.com/emilia-perez-was-nominated-for-13-oscars-why-do-so-many-people-hate-it-248297 |access-date=3 March 2025 |website=[[The Conversation (website)|The Conversation]] |language=en-US}}</ref> In Mexico, the film was panned by both audiences and critics, who criticized its cultural misrepresentation, songwriting, use of stereotypes, and Spanish dialogue.<ref name="outrage">{{cite news |last1=Graham |first1=Thomas |title=Awards success of cartel boss musical ''Emilia Pérez'' prompts outrage in Mexico |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/jan/08/emilia-perez-film-mexico-misrepresentation |access-date=23 January 2025 |newspaper=The Guardian |date=8 January 2025}}</ref> [[LGBTQ]] commentators were also critical of its [[Media portrayals of transgender people|depiction of transgender people]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/awards/story/2025-01-23/emilia-perez-netflix-oscars-explainer|title='Emilia Pérez': All the buzz and backlash around the Oscars' top nominee, explained|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|first=Tracy|last=Brown|date=23 January 2025|access-date=26 January 2025|url-status=dead|archive-date=25 January 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250125125630/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/awards/story/2025-01-23/emilia-perez-netflix-oscars-explainer}}</ref><ref name="GLAAD">{{cite web|url=https://glaad.org/emilia-perez-is-not-good-trans-representation/|title='Emilia Pérez' is Not Good Trans Representation|website=glaad.org|date=15 November 2024|access-date=28 January 2025}}</ref>

Despite the polarized response, ''Emilia Pérez'' earned [[List of accolades received by Emilia Pérez|numerous accolades]]. At the [[97th Academy Awards]], the film led with 13 nominations and won two: [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] for Saldaña and [[Academy Award for Best Original Song|Best Original Song]] for "[[El Mal]]". At the [[82nd Golden Globe Awards]], the film won four awards, including [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy|Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy]] and [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Non-English Language|Best Motion Picture – Non-English Language]], from 10 nominations. It also received 11 nominations at the [[78th British Academy Film Awards]], including [[BAFTA Award for Best Film|Best Film]], winning two.

==Plot==
Rita Mora Castro, a struggling attorney in [[Mexico City]], leads the defense in a murder case involving a prominent media figure's wife. Against her own conscience, Rita proceeds to argue that the woman died by suicide ("El alegato"). After winning the case, Rita receives an anonymous call with a mysterious but lucrative offer. Reflecting on her discontent, she agrees to a meeting ("Todo y nada"). Her client, revealed to be cartel kingpin Juan "Manitas" Del Monte, expresses the desire to covertly undergo [[gender-affirming surgery]] and begin a new and authentic life ("El encuentro") as a woman.

After consulting with doctors in [[Bangkok]] ("La vaginoplastia") and [[Tel Aviv]] ("Lady"), Rita finds a surgeon who agrees to perform the procedure after hearing Manitas's recollections of [[gender dysphoria]] during childhood ("Deseo"). Following the procedure, Manitas's wife, Jessi, and their children are relocated to Switzerland. Jessi pleads to go to the United States to be with her sister instead, but she is told that Switzerland was chosen for their safety. Rita is paid an exorbitant sum for her services. Meanwhile, Manitas fakes their death and begins a new life as Emilia Pérez.

Four years later in London, Rita encounters Emilia, who wants to reunite with her children ("Por casualidad"). Rita arranges for Jessi and the children to return to Mexico City to live with Emilia, introducing her as a distant, wealthy cousin of Manitas who has volunteered to help raise the children. Jessi does not recognize Emilia and opposes the arrangement, ultimately agreeing to return to Mexico only to reunite with Gustavo Brun, a past lover with whom she had an affair during the later years of her marriage ("Bienvenida").

Adjusting to their new life in Mexico, Rita and Emilia have a chance encounter with the [[Madres buscadoras|mother of a missing child]]. Emilia reflects on her criminal past ("Mis siete hermanos y yo"). Later, as she is putting her son to bed, he says he still recognizes her scent ("Papá"). Remorseful, Emilia uses her connections with incarcerated cartel members to create a nonprofit that identifies the bodies of cartel victims ("Para") in order to return them to their families. Rita and Emilia collaborate in building the nonprofit and recruiting donors, some of whom, Rita notes, are dangerous and corrupt ("[[El Mal]]"). Epifanía, a woman whose abusive husband's remains were identified by the nonprofit, meets with Emilia to confirm his death. The two subsequently begin a relationship ("El amor").

Meanwhile, Jessi continues rekindling her relationship with Gustavo ("[[Mi camino]]"). She tells Emilia that the two plan to marry and move the family to a new home. When Emilia refers to the children as "mine" and becomes physically aggressive, Jessi flees with the children. After Emilia cuts off Jessi's allowance and threatens Gustavo into leaving Mexico, Jessi and Gustavo kidnap Emilia and demand ransom from Rita. She receives a package with a few of Emilia's severed fingers. Arriving at the designated location, Rita attempts to negotiate with Gustavo, but a shootout ensues with the security team Rita has hired to rescue Emilia.

Emilia finally reveals her true identity to Jessi, recounting intimate details of their first meeting and their wedding day ("Perdóname"). Gustavo and a confused Jessi load Emilia into the trunk of Gustavo's car and drive off. As Jessi slowly realizes what has happened, she becomes guilt-ridden and orders Gustavo to pull over, holding him at gunpoint. As the two struggle for the gun, the car veers off the road, killing Gustavo, Jessi, and Emilia.

Rita, devastated over Emilia's death, tells Emilia and Jessi's children what has happened and offers to be their guardian. Epifanía marches in the street singing Emilia's eulogy and celebrating her fight for truth and freedom ("Las damas que pasan").

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Clement Ducol and Camille Dalmais the 2024 Toronto International Film Festival 2.jpg|thumb|Composers [[Clément Ducol]] and [[Camille (French singer)|Camille Dalmais]] at the [[2024 Toronto International Film Festival]]]]
In January 2022, ''[[Télérama]]'' published a series of five-episode articles covering in detail [[Jacques Audiard]]'s [[pre-production]] process.{{sfn|Rigoulet|2022}} Audiard developed the screenplay from what was originally intended to be an [[Libretto|opera libretto]] in four acts.<ref name="Vulture">{{cite web|url=https://www.vulture.com/2023/01/emilia-perez-selena-gomez-musical.html|title=Selena Gomez to Return to Her Musical Roots for Jacques Audiard's Upcoming Film|last=Guy|first=Zoe|date=23 January 2023|website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]|access-date=20 December 2023|archive-date=20 December 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231220104558/https://www.vulture.com/2023/01/emilia-perez-selena-gomez-musical.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The title character was inspired by a chapter from Boris Razon's 2018 novel ''[[Écoute (novel)|Écoute]]''.<ref>{{cite news|last=Lévesque|first=François|date=28 October 2024|title=''Emilia Pérez'' par ses glorieuses héroïnes|url=https://www.ledevoir.com/culture/cinema/822501/emilia-perez-glorieuses-heroines|newspaper=[[Le Devoir]]|language=fr|location=Los Angeles|access-date=30 January 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.pathefilms.ch/sites/pathefilms/cache/file/97AE9F37-CC44-4467-B3D22CCEFD7A6417.pdf|title=Emilia Pérez|website=www.pathefilms.ch|access-date=25 July 2024}}</ref> ''Emilia Pérez'' marks the first time Audiard has written a film alone. Previous co-writer [[Thomas Bidegain]] serves as a creative collaborator.{{sfn|Keslassy 2|2023}}

[[Clément Ducol]] and French singer [[Camille (French singer)|Camille]] composed the film's original songs followed by the original score.<ref>{{cite web |last=Fleming |first=Ryan |date=11 February 2025 |title='Emilia Pérez' Songwriters Clément Ducol & Camille On Crafting A "Catalyst For Narrative And Emotions" Through Music |url=https://deadline.com/2025/02/emilia-perez-songwriters-clement-ducol-camille-interview-1236285934/ |access-date=11 November 2025 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood|Deadline]]}}</ref> Camille wrote the lyrics to the songs in Spanish with the assistance of Mexican translator Karla Aviles,<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Flores |first=Griselda |date=4 November 2024 |title=How the 'Unbelievable' Story of 'Emilia Pérez' Became 'Believable' Through Compelling Musical Numbers |url=https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/emilia-perez-netflix-fillm-music-clement-ducol-camille-1235819470/ |access-date=11 November 2025 |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]}}</ref> and performed on the [[Demo (music)|demo]].<ref>{{cite web |title=A Q&A with Golden Globe Winning Songwriter and Composer Camille |url=https://theawfc.com/spotlight/camille/ |access-date=11 November 2025 |publisher=[[Alliance for Women Film Composers]]}}</ref> [[Damien Jalet]] choreographed the musical sequences.<ref name="Vulture"/> [[Anthony Vaccarello]], of fashion house [[Yves Saint Laurent (fashion house)|Yves Saint Laurent]], created the costumes.{{sfn|Keslassy|2024}}

===Filming===
Production was initially to begin in autumn 2022 but was delayed six months due to varying scheduling conflicts with the cast members.{{sfn|Keslassy 1|2023}}<ref>{{cite web|last=Kay|first=Jeremy|date=12 May 2022|title=Jacques Audiard lining up trans musical 'Emilia Perez'|url=https://www.screendaily.com/news/jacques-audiard-lining-up-trans-musical-emilia-perez/5170480.article|website=[[Screen Daily]]|access-date=20 December 2023|archive-date=13 May 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220513202943/https://www.screendaily.com/news/jacques-audiard-lining-up-trans-musical-emilia-perez/5170480.article|url-status=live}}</ref> It was originally set to take place on location in Mexico but was moved to the Studios de Bry-Sur-Marne near [[Paris]] instead,<ref>{{cite news |date=18 September 2024 |title=Cinéma : les studios de Bry-sur-Marne, futur Hollywood européen |url=https://www.lesechos.fr/weekend/business-story/cinema-les-studios-de-bry-sur-marne-futur-hollywood-europeen-2119554 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240922091311/https://www.lesechos.fr/weekend/business-story/cinema-les-studios-de-bry-sur-marne-futur-hollywood-europeen-2119554 |archive-date=22 September 2024 |access-date=31 January 2025 |newspaper=Les Echos |language=fr |url-status=live}}</ref> in accordance with Audiard's wishes.{{sfn|Keslassy 1|2023}} The interior scenes included a reconstruction of an "authentic Mexican backdrop".{{sfn|Keslassy 2|2023}} Audiard stated that the studio setting would afford him the ability to "produce more form" and give him "more freedom for the parts that are sung and choreographed".<ref name="Vulture"/>

[[Principal photography]] began in May 2023 in the [[Île-de-France]] region, before [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapping]] on 5 July 2023.<ref name="ScriptOclap">{{cite web|url=https://scriptoclap.fr/oeuvres/emilia-perez/|title=Emilia Perez|website=ScriptOclap|language=fr|access-date=20 December 2023|archive-date=20 December 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231220124737/https://scriptoclap.fr/oeuvres/emilia-perez/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Poret |first=Marie-Manon |date=18 April 2023 |title=En tournage : « Emilia Perez », la comédie musicale de Jacques Audiard avec Selena Gomez |url=https://www.troiscouleurs.fr/cinema/cinema-news/en-tournage-emilia-perez-la-comedie-musicale-de-jacques-audiard-avec-selena-gomez/ |access-date=23 December 2025 |website=[[Trois couleurs (magazine)|TroisCouleurs]] |language=fr-FR}}</ref>{{sfn|Richford|2023}}{{sfn|Ravindran|Frater|Keslassy|2023}} The film is produced by [[Pascal Caucheteux]] through his company [[Why Not Productions]], and also by Audiard and Valérie Schermann through their company Page 114;{{sfn|Keslassy 2|2023}} in co-production with [[Pathé]], [[France 2 Cinéma]], and Vaccarello's [[Saint Laurent Productions]], a division of Yves Saint Laurent.{{sfn|Keslassy|2024}}

[[Karla Sofía Gascón]] wished to portray Emilia Pérez both in the pre-surgery and post-surgery stages.<ref>{{cite web|last=Machado|first=Yolanda|url=https://ew.com:443/emilia-perez-karla-sofia-gascon-insisted-playing-before-after-transition-roles-8745314|title=Why Karla Sofia Gascón insisted on playing Emilia Pérez before and after her transition|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=15 November 2024|access-date=12 January 2025}}</ref>

==== Statements by Claudia Sheinbaum ====
In January 2025, during her daily morning press conference, Las Mañaneras, the President of Mexico, [[Claudia Sheinbaum]], defended freedom of expression and rejected censorship. She also emphasized the need for Mexico to be recognized for its history, culture, and traditions, not for negative stereotypes of drug trafficking that she believes the film could reinforce. She noted that tourism continues to grow in Mexico, indicating that the country's image is not damaged, and highlighted the pride of the Mexican people and the importance of promoting their cultural richness. She considered that the French film, by addressing the issues of drug trafficking and disappearances, could be validating prejudices and a distorted European view of Mexico, something that does not align with the country's reality.<ref>{{cite web |last=Moreno |first=Por Lenys |date=28 January 2025 |title=President Claudia Sheinbaum commented on 'Emilia Pérez' urging to promote Mexico “as who we are” |url=https://www.metroworldnews.com/en/2025/01/28/president-claudia-sheinbaum-commented-on-emilia-perez-urging-to-promote-mexico-as-who-we-are/ |access-date=27 December 2025 |website=Metro World News |language=en}}</ref>

== Controversy ==

=== Film crew comments ===

==== Jacques Audiard ====
Several statements by the film's director, Jacques Audiard, generated controversy. In an interview he implied that he had not studied the Mexican context in depth: "No, I didn't study that much. What I had to understand I already knew a little", although these statements, originally in French, were translated at the time by someone else.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://abcnoticias.mx/show/2024/12/26/director-de-emilia-perez-admite-que-no-investigo-nada-sobre-mexico-235576.html|title=Director de ''Emilia Pérez'' admite que no investigó nada sobre México|access-date=12 January 2025|date=26 December 2024|website=ABC Noticias|language=es-MX}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.unotv.com/entretenimiento/jacques-audiard-director-de-emilia-perez-admite-que-no-investigo-sobre-mexico/|title=Jacques Audiard, director de "Emilia Pérez", admite que no investigó sobre México|website=Uno TV|date=27 December 2024 |language=es-MX}}</ref>

In another interview, Jacques Audiard stated that "Spanish is a language of emerging countries, of modest countries, of poor people and migrants". Audiard later claimed that his comments were taken out of context and that he actually has a great appreciation for the Spanish language: "What has been said is the opposite of what I think".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cadenaser.com/nacional/2025/02/05/el-director-de-emilia-perez-se-pronuncia-sobre-sus-polemicas-palabras-sobre-el-espanol-lo-que-se-ha-dicho-es-lo-contrario-de-lo-que-pienso-cadena-ser/|access-date=22 February 2025|title=El director de 'Emilia Pérez' se pronuncia sobre sus polémicas palabras sobre el español: "Lo que se ha dicho es lo contrario de lo que pienso"|language=es|website=Cadena Ser|date=5 February 2025}}</ref> Audiard also said that they wanted bigger-name stars instead of Mexicans in order to be able to finance the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/02/28/world/americas/emilia-perez-mexico-oscars.html|access-date=1 March 2025|title=Why 'Emilia Pérez,' a Film About Mexico, Flopped in Mexico|language=en|website=[[The New York Times]]|last1=Wagner |first1=James |date=28 February 2025}}</ref>

At the [[2024 Cannes Film Festival]], stars Karla Sofía Gascón, Selena Gomez, Adriana Paz, and Zoe Saldaña collectively won the [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress|Best Actress Award]], while director Jacques Audiard won the [[Jury Prize (Cannes Film Festival)|Jury Prize]], and Clément Ducol and Camille won the [[Cannes Soundtrack Award|Soundtrack Award]]. The film was also nominated for the [[Palme d'Or]] and the [[Queer Palm]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2024/05/cannes-film-festival-winners-2024-full-list-1235940952/ |title=Cannes Film Festival: 'Anora' Wins Palme d'Or; 'All We Imagine as Light' Takes Grand Prize; 'Emilia Pérez' Jury Prize & Best Actress Ensemble – Full List |last=Tartaglione |first=Nancy |date=25 May 2024 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=25 May 2024 |archive-date=26 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240526070500/https://deadline.com/2024/05/cannes-film-festival-winners-2024-full-list-1235940952/ |url-status=live}}</ref> It was also named one of the [[American Film Institute Awards#2024|Top 10 Films of 2024]] by the [[American Film Institute]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Feinberg |first=Scott |date=5 December 2024 |title=AFI Awards: 'Emilia Pérez' in, 'Gladiator II' Out of Film Top 10, TV List Includes 'Shōgun' But Not 'Monsters' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/afi-awards-2025-emilia-perez-shogun-1236078712/ |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=5 December 2024 |archive-date=6 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241206021233/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/afi-awards-2025-emilia-perez-shogun-1236078712/ |url-status=live}}</ref>
