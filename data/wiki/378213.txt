'''''Scanners''''' is a 1981 Canadian [[Science fiction film|science-fiction]] [[horror film]] written and directed by [[David Cronenberg]]. The film stars include [[Stephen Lack]], [[Jennifer O'Neill]], [[Michael Ironside]], [[Lawrence Dane]] and [[Patrick McGoohan]]. In the film, "scanners" are [[psychic]]s with [[telepathy|telepathic]] and [[psychokinesis|telekinetic]] powers. ConSec, a purveyor of weaponry and security systems, searches out scanners to use them for its own purposes. The film's plot concerns the attempt by Darryl Revok (Ironside), a renegade scanner, to wage a war against ConSec. Another scanner, Cameron Vale (Lack), is dispatched by ConSec to stop Revok.

''Scanners'' premiered in January 1981 to lukewarm reviews from critics but became one of the first films produced in Canada to successfully compete with American films at the international box office.<ref name="boxoffice"/><ref name="ebert"/><ref name="canby"/><ref>{{Cite web|title=Scanners|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/89175/scanners|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120425002533/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/89175/Scanners/|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 25, 2012|access-date=2020-12-28|website=Turner Classic Movies|language=en}}</ref> It brought Cronenberg and his controversial style of [[body horror]] to the attention of mainstream film audiences for the first time and has since been reevaluated as a [[Cult film|cult classic]].<ref name="denofgeek">{{Cite web |date=November 23, 2012|title=Looking back at David Cronenberg's Scanners |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/looking-back-at-david-cronenbergs-scanners/ |website=Den of Geek |access-date=2020-12-28 }}</ref><ref name="avclub">{{Cite web|title=Exploding head aside, Scanners is one of Cronenberg's most conventional films|url=https://www.avclub.com/exploding-head-aside-scanners-is-one-of-cronenberg-s-m-1798180913 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |date=July 16, 2014 |access-date=2020-12-28}}</ref> It is particularly well known for a scene that depicts Revok psychically causing a rival scanner's head to graphically explode.

==Plot==
Cameron Vale, a vagrant who apparently suffers from [[auditory hallucination]] in the form of hearing voices in his head, causes a rude woman in a mall food court to have a [[seizure]] by what appears to be purely [[psychic]] means. Vale is then hunted down by unknown assailants, who capture him and deliver him to Dr. Paul Ruth, a [[psychologist]] who works for the [[private military company]] ConSec. 

Ruth explains to Vale that Vale is one of 237 persons known as ''scanners''. These scanners possess supernatural powers, namely [[telepathy]] and a form of [[telekinesis]] capable of harming another human being, just as Vale did to the woman in the subway. Ruth tells Vale that the voices Vale hears are not hallucinations, but that Vale is in fact "reading" or ''scanning'' the [[Stream of consciousness (psychology)|streams of consciousness]] of the strangers around him, and that it is Vale's inability to recognize and control his scanning power that is causing his apparent mental illness. 

Ruth injects Vale with a drug called ephemerol which temporarily inhibits scanning abilities. Ruth then teaches him to control his power so that he is not overcome by the unfiltered "voices" of other minds. Some deeper connection between scanners, Ruth's work, and the ConSec corporation is indicated.

ConSec holds a marketing demonstration of its scanning program in which a ConSec operative with scanning ability will read the thoughts of a normal (non-scanning) audience member. An attendee named Darryl Revok volunteers to be scanned. 

Revok, however, is in fact himself a powerful scanner. Instead of being scanned, Revok wreaks havoc by counter-scanning the ConSec operative. He focuses his formidable abilities against the operative, causing the latter's head to explode. It is revealed that Revok has been driven insane by his uncontrollable scanning, and that he is seeking other scanners in a scheme to achieve domination over non-scanners. Revok aims to kill any scanners who refuse to join him. 

Following the disastrous event, ConSec head of security Braedon Keller tells Ruth that the company's scanner program should be shut down. Ruth refuses, as he believes that scanners represent the next stage of [[human evolution]] and understands that a powerful, unknown scanner murdered the operative in the demonstration. Ruth then turns to Cameron Vale. Ruth argues that the assassination shows how dangerous Revok truly is, and convinces Vale to infiltrate Revok's group so that Revok can be neutralized and his plan thwarted. Vale begins to track down known scanners. 

Unbeknownst to Ruth, Keller—a non-scanner—is working for Darryl Revok. Keller informs Revok of the doctor's plan to infiltrate his group. Revok has Vale surveilled and sends assassins to murder him as Vale visits an unaffiliated scanner, an artist named Benjamin Pierce. Revok's assassins murder Pierce, but Vale survives and is able to read Pierce's dying thoughts. Vale learns of a group of scanners who oppose Revok, led by a woman named Kim Obrist. Vale tracks down Obrist and attends a meeting of the group, but Revok's assassins strike again; only Vale and Obrist survive. In the aftermath of the attack, Vale and Obrist learn of a [[Pharmaceutical industry|pharmaceutical company]] named Biocarbon Amalgamate which has some apparent connection to the ephemerol drug. 

Vale infiltrates the Biocarbon Amalgamate factory, where he discovers that it is run by Darryl Revok and that it manufactures ephemerol. Vale learns that Revok is distributing the drug with a ConSec [[computer program]] codenamed "Ripe". Vale and Obrist visit Ruth at the ConSec headquarters to update him on their discoveries. The doctor admits that he himself founded Biocarbon Amalgamate but that he no longer has a direct connection to it, and claims ignorance both of Revok's involvement and of the Ripe program. Ruth encourages Vale to investigate further by [[Hacker|hacking]] into the ConSec computer network with his scanning ability. Ruth explains that a [[cybernetic]] structure is analogous to the human [[Neural network (biology)|neural network]], which should allow Vale to access the ConSec telepathically. 

Vale takes leave of Ruth while Obrist is held captive by Keller but uses her powers to escape him. On Revok's orders, Keller kills Ruth and attempts to have ConSec guards murder Vale and Obrist, however the two scanners use their telepathic powers to neutralize the guards and are able to flee ConSec. From a [[telephone booth]], Vale succeeds at scanning into the ConSec network, and he "downloads" information about ephemerol shipments into his mind. 

Keller is killed when the computer explodes during his attempt to intercept Vale. Vale and Obrist visit Dr. Frane, someone who is receiving the drug. Obrist encounters a pregnant woman and realizes the woman's fetus has scanned her. Vale confronts Dr. Frane and learns that Revok's plan is to prescribe ephemerol to pregnant women, turning their children into scanners. Revok's group captures Vale and Obrist and takes them to the Biocarbon Amalgamate plant.

Revok reveals to Vale that they are both sons of Dr. Ruth, who developed ephemerol as a sedative for pregnant women. Ruth learned about the drug's [[side effect]] during his wife's pregnancies, and he made them the most powerful scanners in the world by administering a prototype dosage prior to abandoning them. Revok plans to create and lead a new generation of scanners to take over the world, but Vale refuses to join him. Vale accuses Revok of acting like his father, which enrages Revok. The brothers engage in a telepathic duel, which incinerates Vale's body. However, when Obrist encounters Revok, she discovers that Vale somehow has managed to swap minds with Revok, the latter dying in the former's body during the duel.

==Production==
===Financing===
''Scanners'' was based on David Cronenberg's scripts ''The Sensitives'' and ''Telepathy 2000'', which he planned to pitch to [[Roger Corman]] before beginning work on ''[[The Brood]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Kurl|first=Daniel|date=January 15, 2020|title=The Pain Begins! You Can't Breathe! You Explode! David Cronenberg's 'Scanners' Turns 39!|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3600821/pain-begins-cant-breathe-explode-david-cronenbergs-scanners-turns-39/|access-date=2020-12-28|website=Bloody Disgusting!|language=en-US}}</ref> Corman was shown the script, but did nothing with it.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=86}} Cronenberg has called ''Scanners'' one of his most difficult films to make; most Canadian film productions of the 1970s and the early 1980s were funded through a 100-percent [[Capital Cost Allowance]] [[tax shield]] for investors passed by Prime Minister [[Pierre Trudeau]] in 1974, and the film was rushed into production without a finished script or constructed sets to claim the subsidies.<ref name="avclub"/><ref>{{Cite web| title= Canada's Tax Shelter Era| publisher= National Canadian Film Day| website= canadianfilmday.ca| url= https://canadianfilmday.ca/canadas-tax-shelter-era/| access-date= 2020-12-28| language= en-US| archive-date= February 5, 2021| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210205205459/https://canadianfilmday.ca/canadas-tax-shelter-era/| url-status= dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=David Cronenberg: Scanners (1981)|url=https://3brothersfilm.com/blog/2018/7/27/david-cronenberg-scanners-1980| access-date= December 28, 2020|website=3BrothersFilm.com |date=July 27, 2018 |language=en-US}}</ref>

Victor Snolicki, Dick Schouten, and [[Pierre David (film producer)|Pierre David]] of Vision 4, a company taking advantage of Canada's tax shelter policies, aided Cronenberg in the film's financing.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=75}} Vision 4 dissolved after Schouten's death and reorganized into Filmplan International.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=85}}

===Filming===
The film's first draft was not a script, but instead a series of ideas.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=58}} The film was given two weeks of pre-production while a script was not yet written.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=86}} According to Cronenberg, he would spend mornings prior to filming writing scenes.<ref name="avclub"/> The film was initially titled ''The Sensitives'', but it was altered as Cronenberg felt "it was too wimpy" while ''Scanners'' "was very strong".{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=59}} Cronenberg stated that the drug aspect of the film might have been influenced by ''[[Blue Sunshine (film)|Blue Sunshine]]''.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=61}} Star [[Jennifer O'Neill]] was given a script with all of the violence edited out and cried after seeing the uncensored script.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=87}}

The film was shot in Montreal from October 30 to December 23, 1979, on a budget of $4.1 million ({{Inflation|CA|4,100,000|1979|fmt=eq}}).{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=322-323}} Cronenberg stated that "the first day was the most disastrous shooting day I've ever had" as "there was nothing to shoot" and a distracted truck driver watching the film crew hit a car, killing two women inside it.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=86-87}}

===Effects===
Make-up artist [[Dick Smith (make-up artist)|Dick Smith]] (''[[The Exorcist]]'', ''[[Amadeus (film)|Amadeus]]'') provided prosthetics for the climactic scanner duel and the iconic exploding head effect.<ref name="canby"/><ref>{{cite web |date=January 1, 1981 |author=Variety Staff |title=Scanners |url=https://variety.com/review/VE1117794658 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] }}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url= http://cinemafantastique.net/film1253,Scanners.html |title= Scanners| work= Cinemafantastique.net }}</ref><ref>{{cite web| last= Kinnear| first= Simon |date= August 15, 2011| title= 50 Best Movie Special Effects| url= http://www.totalfilm.com/features/50-best-movie-special-effects/scanners-1980 | website= TotalFilm.com |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120126130559/http://www.totalfilm.com/features/50-best-movie-special-effects/scanners-1980 |archive-date=January 26, 2012 |access-date= January 24, 2012}}</ref> [[Chris Walas]], working at [[Lucasfilm]] at the time and later providing effects work for ''[[The Fly (1986 film)|The Fly]]'' and ''[[Naked Lunch (film)|Naked Lunch]]'', also worked on the exploding head effect.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=90}} Cronenberg later said in 2006 that ''Scanners'' was his most difficult film to shoot due to its special effects and complex story.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=61}}

[[File:Scanners head explode screenshot.jpg|thumb|right|Scene of the explosion of a ConSec scanner's head]]

The head explosion scene was produced by trial and error, with the producers eventually deciding on a gelatin-encased plaster skull packed with "leftover burgers" as well as "latex scraps, some wax, and just bits and bobs and a lot of stringy stuff that we figured would fly through the air a little better". When other explosive techniques failed to give the desired effect, special effects supervisor Gary Zeller told the crew to roll cameras and get inside their trucks with doors and windows closed; he then crouched down behind the dummy and shot it in the back of the head with a shotgun.<ref name="Wickman2014">{{cite web |url=https://slate.com/culture/2014/07/scanners-exploding-head-scene-how-they-shot-it-as-revealed-on-the-new-criterion-collection-dvd-video.html |date=July 15, 2014 |title=How They Blew Up That Head in ''Scanners'' |last=Wickman |first=Forrest |website=[[Slate (website)|Slate]] |publisher=The Slate Group}}</ref>

The exploding head scene was filmed four times, but Cronenberg accepted the first shot and did not remain to watch the three others, opting to instead take a nap in his Winnebago. The scene depicting the exploding head was trimmed down to allow for a R-rating from the [[MPAA]]. Cronenberg originally intended for the scene to be the film's opening, but placed it later in the film after test screenings.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=90}}

==Release==
The film was distributed by [[New World Pictures]] in Canada, Les Films Mutuels in Quebec, and [[Embassy Pictures|Avco Embassy Pictures]] in the United States. ''Scanners'' was released in the United States on January 14, and in Canada on January 16, 1981.{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=322-323}} 

A novelization by Leon Whiteson, ''David Cronenberg's Scanners'', was also released in 1981.<ref name="Browning2007">{{cite book |last=Browning |first=Mark |title=David Cronenberg: Author Or Film-maker? |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=prEUHH2jcWAC&pg=PA91 |year=2007 |publisher=Intellect Books |isbn=978-1-84150-173-4|pages=91–}}</ref> The film was released on [[VHS]] in 1982.{{sfn|Mathijs|2008|p=97}}

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film grossed $2,758,147 from 387 theatres in its opening weekend.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|page=3|date=January 21, 1981|title='Crazy,' '9 To 5,' 'Which Way' Glow; 'Scanners' Strong}}</ref> It grossed domestically a total of $14,225,876 at the box office.<ref name="boxoffice">{{cite web | title = ''Scanners'' | publisher = Box Office Mojo | url = https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=scanners.htm | access-date = January 8, 2009 }}</ref> Cronenberg stated that it was his first film to be [[Lists of box office number-one films|number one]] at the box office.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=58}}

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 68% based on {{nowrap|44 reviews}}, with an average rating of 6.7/10. The site's critical consensus reads, "''Scanners'' is a dark sci-fi story with special effects that'll make your head explode."<ref name="tomatoes">{{cite web |title=Scanners (1981) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1018315_scanners? |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=August 11, 2023}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] it has a weighted average score of 60 out of 100 based on reviews from 8 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=Scanners |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/scanners |website=[[Metacritic]] }}</ref>
Film professor Charles Derry, in his overview of the horror genre ''Dark Dreams'', cited ''Scanners'' as "an especially important masterwork" and calling it the ''[[Psycho (1960 film)|Psycho]]'' of its day.<ref name="americanhorrors">{{Cite book | last = Derry | first = Charles | chapter = More Dark Dreams: Some Notes on the Recent Horror Film | editor-last = Waller | editor-first = Gregory | title = American Horrors: Essays on the Modern American Horror Film | page = [https://archive.org/details/americanhorrorse0000unse/page/173 173] | publisher = University of Illinois Press | place = Chicago | year = 1987 | isbn = 0-252-01448-0 | chapter-url = https://archive.org/details/americanhorrorse0000unse/page/173 }}</ref> In a contemporary review for ''[[Ares (magazine)|Ares]]'' magazine, Christopher John commented that "''Scanners'' is top-notch entertainment. It is haunting, exciting, shocking and literate—an unusual combination to discover in a film these days."<ref name="Ares">{{cite journal | last=John | first=Christopher | title=Film & Television | journal=[[Ares (magazine)|Ares]] | publisher=[[Simulations Publications, Inc.]] | date=May 1981 | issue=8 | page=31}}</ref>

Some reviews were less positive. Film critic [[Roger Ebert]] gave ''Scanners'' two out of four stars and wrote, {{-"}}''Scanners'' is so lockstep that we are basically reduced to watching the special effects, which are good but curiously abstract, because we don't much care about the people they're happening around".<ref name="ebert">{{cite news | last = Ebert | first = Roger | author-link = Roger Ebert | title = ''Scanners'' | work = [[Chicago Sun-Times]] | date = January 1, 1981 | url = http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19810101/REVIEWS/101010364/1023 | access-date = January 8, 2009 | archive-date = June 6, 2011 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20110606043757/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F19810101%2FREVIEWS%2F101010364%2F1023 | url-status = dead }}</ref> In his review for ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Vincent Canby]] wrote, "Had Mr. Cronenberg settled simply for horror, as [[John Carpenter]] did in his classic ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' (though not in his not-so-classic ''[[The Fog]]''), ''Scanners'' might have been a [[Grand Guignol]] treat. Instead he insists on turning the film into a mystery, and mystery demands eventual explanations that, when they come in ''Scanners'', underline the movie's essential foolishness".<ref name="canby">{{cite news | last = Canby | first = Vincent | title = ''Scanners'' | work = [[The New York Times]] | date = January 14, 1981 | url = https://www.nytimes.com/1981/01/14/movies/horror-movie.html | access-date = January 8, 2021 |author-link=Vincent Canby}}</ref> [[John Simon (critic)|John Simon]] of ''[[National Review]]'' described ''Scanners'' as trash.<ref>{{cite book |title=Reverse Angle: A Decade of American Film|last=Simon|first=John |publisher=Crown Publishers Inc. |year=1982 |page=xiv|author-link=John Simon (critic)}}</ref>

A reassessment of ''Scanners'' in the 2012 issue of ''[[CineAction]]'' looks at the film in light of Cronenberg's use of allegory and parables in much of his work. The argument is made that Cronenberg uses iconic imagery that refers directly and indirectly to the thirty-something Scanners as 1960s [[New Left|political radicals]], [[counterculture]] hippies, and as nascent [[Yuppie|Young Urban Professionals]]. As a result, the film can be seen "as an oblique reflection on what might happen when the counterculture becomes the dominant culture".<ref>{{cite news | last = Pepe | first = Michael | title = ''Lefties and Hippies and Yuppies, Oh My! David Cronenberg's Scanners Revisited'' | work = [[CineAction]] | issue = 88 | year = 2012 }}</ref> [[Kim Newman]] noted in an essay for [[The Criterion Collection]] that at the same time the film rejects the conservative values of the 1980s and the nostalgia for the 1950s present in contemporary science-fiction films such as ''[[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial]]'' and ''[[Back to the Future]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Newman |first=Kim |author-link=Kim Newman |title=Scanners: Mind and Matter |url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3225-scanners-mind-and-matter |website=The Criterion Collection |access-date=2021-04-20 }}</ref> The film's fictional drug ephemerol also mirrors the real-life [[thalidomide scandal]], in which the popular [[West Germany|West German]] medication [[thalidomide]] caused severe [[birth defect]]s in children born to mothers prescribed the drug for [[morning sickness]] in Western Europe and Canada.<ref name="denofgeek"/>

===Accolades===
Although ''Scanners'' was not nominated for any major awards, it did receive some recognition. The [[Academy of Science Fiction, Fantasy and Horror Films]] gave the film its [[Saturn Awards|Saturn Award]] in 1981 for "[[Saturn Award for Best International Film|Best International Film]]", and, in addition, the "[[Saturn Award for Best Make-up|Best Make-Up]]" award went to Dick Smith in a tie with ''[[Altered States]]''. The film had also been nominated for "[[Saturn Award for Best Special Effects|Best Special Effects]]".

''Scanners'' also won "Best International Fantasy Film" from [[Fantasporto]] in 1983, and was nominated for eight [[Genie Awards]] in 1982, but did not win any.<ref>[https://allmovie.com/work/scanners-43063/awards Allmovie Awards]</ref>

==Legacy==
''Scanners'' spawned sequels and a series of spin-offs; a [[remake]] was announced in 2007, but {{as of|2022|lc=y}} had not gone into production.<ref name="remake confirmed"/> Cronenberg was not involved in the sequels as he was both uninterested and would not make money off the characters or story he wrote.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=92}}

===Canceled remake===
In February 2007, [[Darren Lynn Bousman]] (director of ''[[Saw II]]'', ''[[Saw III]]'', and ''[[Saw IV]]'') was announced as director of a remake of the film, to be released by [[The Weinstein Company]] and [[Dimension Films]]. [[David S. Goyer]] was assigned to script the film. The film was planned for release on October 17, 2008, but the date came and went without further announcements and all of the parties involved have since moved on to other projects<ref name="remake confirmed">{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/markets-festivals/scanners-moves-to-new-dimension-2-1117960254/|title='Scanners' moves to new dimension|last=Fleming|first=Michael|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=February 27, 2007|access-date=2015-12-08}}</ref> because Cronenberg did not give his approval.<ref>{{cite news|last=Beggs|first=Scott|title=Mind-Blowing: David Cronenberg Is Producing A 'Scanners' Series For HBO|url=https://uproxx.com/tv/david-cronenberg-scanners-series-hbo/|quote="The first attempt came in 2007, when Darren Lynn Bousman was meant to reboot it, but it fell through because David Cronenberg didn’t give his blessing (and they wouldn’t more [sic] forward without it)."|date=September 21, 2022|work=Uproxx|access-date=October 21, 2025}}</ref>
