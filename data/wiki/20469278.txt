'''''Drag Me to Hell''''' is a 2009 American [[supernatural horror film]] directed and co-written by [[Sam Raimi]] with [[Ivan Raimi]], starring [[Alison Lohman]], [[Justin Long]], [[Lorna Raver]], [[Dileep Rao]], [[David Paymer]], and [[Adriana Barraza]]. The story focuses on a [[loan officer]] who, in an effort to prove to her boss that she can make the "hard decisions" at work, chooses not to extend an elderly woman's [[mortgage]]. The old woman places a retaliatory [[curse]] on her that, after three days of escalating torment, will plunge her into the depths of [[Hell]] to burn for eternity.

''Drag Me to Hell'' was developed by Sam and his brother, Ivan, who wrote the script for a film titled ''The Curse'' in the 1990s as a dark, cautionary fable. Conceived as a "cruel morality tale", the script was intentionally designed to punish a well-intentioned protagonist for making a single selfish mistake, capturing the visceral, slapstick-horror tone of Raimi’s earlier ''[[Evil Dead (franchise)|Evil Dead]]'' films. However, they shelved the project to focus on other films, eventually completing the script in 2007.  Following the massive commercial success of his [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' film trilogy]] (2002–2007), Sam Raimi wanted to return to his low-budget, independent horror roots. He partnered with [[Ghost House Pictures]] to produce the film independently from major studio constraints. It was shot in [[Tarzana, California]] in 2008 with a budget of $30 million.

''Drag Me to Hell'' premiered at the [[South by Southwest|SXSW]] on March 15, 2009, and was theatrically released in the United States on May 29, 2009, by [[Universal Pictures]]. The film received positive reviews from critics and grossed $90.8 million worldwide. It won the [[Saturn Award for Best Horror Film]] at the [[36th Saturn Awards]].

==Plot==
In 1969 [[Pasadena, California|Pasadena]], a [[Hispanic]] couple seek help from young medium Shaun San Dena, claiming their son Juan is hearing evil voices after having stolen a silver necklace from a [[Romani people|Romani]] woman's wagon, despite his attempts to return it. San Dena prepares a [[séance]], but an unseen force attacks them and drags Juan to [[Hell]]. San Dena vows to fight the demon again one day.

Forty years later, in present-day [[Los Angeles]], bank [[loan officer]] Christine Brown vies for a promotion to [[Branch manager|assistant branch manager]] with her co-worker, Stu Rubin. Her boss, Jim Jacks, advises her to demonstrate tough decision-making. When Sylvia Ganush, an elderly and disheveled Romani woman, asks for a third extension on her mortgage due to economic problems resulting from an illness, Christine, against her better judgement, denies her request, and Sylvia desperately begs not to have her house repossessed. As security guards escort her out, she accuses Christine of publicly shaming her. Later, in the parking garage, Sylvia attacks her as she is leaving. After a struggle, Sylvia rips a button from Christine's coat and places a curse on it. After she returns it, she warns that Christine will soon come begging to her.

Christine and her boyfriend Clay Dalton, a college professor, visit fortune teller Rham Jas, who explains that a dark spirit has latched onto her. At home, the entity tortures Christine; at work, she suffers a violent nosebleed while hallucinating about Sylvia. As she leaves, Stu covertly takes a file from her desk. Christine visits Sylvia's granddaughter Ilenka, intending to apologize to Sylvia, but Ilenka reveals Sylvia has recently died. She inadvertently embarrasses the attendees at her funeral, and Ilenka warns her that she deserves her eventual fate. Jas explains to Christine that she has been inflicted with the curse of the [[Lamia]] or "Black Goat", an ancient and powerful demon that torments its victims for three days before literally dragging them to Hell. Following his suggestion of an appeasatory sacrifice, Christine reluctantly kills her pet kitten before meeting Clay's wealthy parents Trudy and Leonard at their house for dinner, during which grotesque hallucinations torment her further.

Jas offers to introduce a furious Christine to San Dena for $10,000, which a sympathetic Clay pays on her behalf. Revealing that she has been awaiting vengeance against the Lamia, San Dena and her assistant prepare a séance, attempting to trap the spirit in a goat and kill the animal to destroy the demon, but the entity possesses her, the goat and her assistant, the last of whom vomits up the corpse of Christine's deceased cat. After successfully banishing the Lamia, she dies soon afterwards. Jas seals the button in an envelope and tells Christine that she can remove the curse by giving the button to someone else, thus transferring the curse to them.

At a 24-hour diner, Christine telephones Stu, accusing him of stealing her file and demanding he meet her there, intending to present the cursed button to him. When he arrives, he successfully implores her to pity him and abstain from notifying Jacks about his transgressions. Consulting Jas, Christine learns she can formally offer the curse to the deceased, so she exhumes Sylvia's corpse and forces the envelope into her mouth. A massive downpour nearly traps her in the grave, but she successfully manages to escape.

Returning home, she ventures to [[Union Station (Los Angeles)|Union Station]], where Clay intends to propose to her. Jacks also informs Christine that she has received the promotion, Stu having been dismissed for stealing her file. At the station, Clay presents her with an envelope he had discovered in his car which contains her cursed coat button; before exhuming Sylvia, she had mistaken it for a similar envelope containing a rare coin and had given to Clay earlier. Horrified, she stumbles backwards onto the tracks; as a train speeds towards her, demonic hands emerge from the ground and drag her into Hell below. A horrified Clay, having watched from above, holds the button.

==Themes and interpretations==
''Drag Me to Hell'' has been noted for its relevance to the [[subprime mortgage crisis]] and more broadly, the [[Great Recession]], which were ongoing at the time of the film's release.<ref name="Brown 2009">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/may/21/sam-raimi-returns-to-horror|title=Recession-era movie Drag Me to Hell marks revenge of the repossessed|last=Brown|first=Mark|date=May 21, 2009|website=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=September 27, 2023|archive-date=November 17, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231117162220/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/may/21/sam-raimi-returns-to-horror|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://slate.com/culture/2009/06/drag-me-to-hell-the-first-great-mortgage-crisis-parable.html|title=Drag Me to Hell: The First Great Mortgage-Crisis Parable?|last=Weiner|first=Jonah|date=June 9, 2009|website=[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]|access-date=September 27, 2023|archive-date=September 27, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230927224126/https://slate.com/culture/2009/06/drag-me-to-hell-the-first-great-mortgage-crisis-parable.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://consequence.net/2017/01/in-2009-sam-raimis-drag-me-to-hell-exploited-shame-to-gross-gut-wrenching-effect/|title=In 2009, Sam Raimi's Drag Me to Hell Exploited Shame to Gross, Gut-Wrenching Effect|last=Colburn|first=Randall|date=January 27, 2017|website=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]]|access-date=September 27, 2023|archive-date=September 27, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230927224125/https://consequence.net/2017/01/in-2009-sam-raimis-drag-me-to-hell-exploited-shame-to-gross-gut-wrenching-effect/|url-status=live}}</ref> Director [[Sam Raimi]] reportedly considered this a coincidence, stating, "We just wanted to tell the story of a person who wants to be a good person but who makes a sinful choice out of greed, for their own benefit, and pays the price for it."<ref name="Brown 2009" />

==Production==

===Background===
The original story for ''Drag Me to Hell'' was written ten years before the film went into production and was written by [[Sam Raimi]] and his brother [[Ivan Raimi]]. The film went into production under the name ''The Curse''.<ref name="prod">{{cite web | title = Drag Me to Hell Production Notes | url = http://www.dragmetohell.net/assets/production/production_notes.html | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090519202048/http://www.dragmetohell.net/assets/production/production_notes.html | archive-date = May 19, 2009 | work = Drag Me To Hell Official Website | publisher = [[Universal Pictures]] | location = Los Angeles, California | year = 2009 | access-date = 2009-06-27}}</ref> The Raimis wrote the script as a [[morality tale]], desiring to write a story about a character who wants to be a good person, but makes a sinful choice out of greed for her own betterment and pays the price for it.<ref name="festival-cannes">{{cite web | title = ''Drag Me to Hell'' : Cannes Press Conference (8:00) | url = http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10041.html | work = [[Cannes Film Festival]] | format = Video | year = 2009 | access-date = 2009-06-27 | archive-date = October 2, 2012 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121002064946/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10041.html | url-status = dead }}</ref> The Raimis tried to make the character of Christine the main focal point in the film, and tried to have Christine in almost all the scenes in the film.<ref name="prod" /> Elements of the film's story are drawn from the [[British cinema|British]] [[horror film]] ''[[Night of the Demon]]'' such as the similar-shaped demons and the three-day curse theme in the film.<ref>{{cite web | title = Movie Review: Drag Me to Hell : Mountain Xpress | url = http://www.mountainx.com/movies/review/drag_me_to_hell | first = Ken | last = Hanke | date = 2009-06-03 | work = [[Mountain Xpress]] | access-date = 2009-06-27 | archive-date = March 28, 2014 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140328065118/http://www.mountainx.com/movies/review/drag_me_to_hell | url-status = live }}</ref><ref name="variety" /> The most significant parallel is that both stories involve the passing of a cursed object, which has to be passed to someone else, or its possessor will be devoured by one or more demons. Unlike his past horror films, Raimi wanted the film to be rated PG-13 and not strictly driven by gore, stating, "I didn't want to do exactly the same thing I had done before."<ref name="prod" /><ref name="15cannes">{{cite web | title = ''Drag Me to Hell'' : Cannes Press Conference (15:30) | url = http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10041.html | work = [[Cannes Film Festival]] | format = Video | year = 2009 | access-date = 2009-06-27 | archive-date = October 2, 2012 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121002064946/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10041.html | url-status = dead }}</ref>

After finishing the script, Raimi desired to make the picture after the first draft of the script was completed, but other projects such as the [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' film series]] became a nearly decade-long endeavor, pushing opportunities to continue work on ''Drag Me to Hell'' to late 2007.<ref name="prod" /> Raimi offered director [[Edgar Wright]] the opportunity to direct ''Drag Me to Hell'' which Wright turned down as he was filming ''[[Hot Fuzz]]'' and felt that "If I did it, it would just feel like karaoke."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.theskinny.co.uk/latest/305032-hero_worship_sam_raimi|work=[[The Skinny (magazine)|The Skinny]]|title=Hero Worship: Sam Raimi|author=Wright, Edgar|author-link=Edgar Wright|access-date=July 8, 2013|date=July 8, 2013|archive-date=July 12, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130712160143/http://www.theskinny.co.uk/latest/305032-hero_worship_sam_raimi|url-status=live}}</ref> After the previous three ''Spider-Man'' films, Raimi came back to the script of ''Drag Me to Hell'', wanting to make a simpler and lower-budget film.<ref name="CannesInterview">{{cite web | title = ''Drag Me to Hell'' : Cannes Interview | url = http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10040.html | work = [[Cannes Film Festival]] | format = Video | year = 2009 | access-date = 2009-06-27 | archive-date = October 2, 2012 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121002065003/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/10040.html | url-status = dead }}</ref> In 2007, Sam Raimi's friend and [[Film producer|producer]] [[Robert Tapert]] of [[Ghost House Pictures]] had the company sign on to finance the film.<ref name="prod" /> [[Universal Pictures|Universal Studios]] agreed to distribute domestically.<ref name="prod" />

===Casting===
After completing the script and having the project greenlit, Raimi started casting the film.<ref name="prod" /> [[Elliot Page]]<!--per wp:GENDERID and consistent with Juno and Freeheld articles--> was originally cast for the main role of Christine, but dropped out of the project due to [[Screen Actors Guild#2008–09 Screen Actors Guild labor dispute|SAG strike]]-related scheduling issues.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.movieweb.com/news/NElPVqonXe3xpl | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20110611043502/http://www.movieweb.com/news/ellen-page-quits-sam-raimis-drag-me-to-hell | archive-date = June 11, 2011 | title = Ellen Page Quits Sam Raimi's ''Drag Me to Hell'' | work = MovieWeb | date = February 29, 2008 | quote = We were racing to start production so that we could accommodate [Elliot's] schedule. But like so many other productions trying to start before the potential SAG strike date, this one needed more time and we had to push back the start of production.}}</ref> The main role eventually went to Lohman, who did not enjoy [[horror film]]s, but enjoyed doing the stunts during filming.<ref name="prod" /> Stage actress [[Lorna Raver]] auditioned for the role of Mrs. Ganush. Raver was not aware of the specific nature of her character until being cast, stating that all she had read was "about a little old lady coming into the bank because they're closing down her house. It was only later that I saw the whole script and said, 'Oh my!'".<ref name="prod" /> To prepare for this role, Raver met with a Hungarian [[dialect coach]] and asked to have portions of the script translated into [[Hungarian language|Hungarian]].<ref name="prod" /> Raimi would later ask Raver to use some of the Hungarian words in the scenes of Ganush's attacking Christine.<ref name="prod" /> [[Dileep Rao]], who plays Rham Jas, made producer [[Grant Curtis]] mildly hesitant in casting him, stating that during his audition "he was a little bit younger than he read in the script. But as we were looking at his reading, Sam said, 'There's no minimum age requirement on wisdom.' Dileep has that wisdom and presence on screen, and that's what made him right. Once he got on camera, he brought that shoulder for Alison to lean on."<ref name="prod" /> Many of the actors playing secondary characters in ''Drag Me to Hell'' have appeared previously in Raimi's films, including Joanne Baron, Tom Carey, [[Molly Cheek]], Aimee Miles, John Paxton, [[Ted Raimi]], Bill E. Rogers, [[Chelcie Ross]], and [[Octavia Spencer]].<ref name="prod" />

===Filming===
Raimi said he set out to create "a horror film with lots of wild moments and lots of suspense and big shocks that'll hopefully make audiences jump. But I also wanted to have a lot of dark humor sprinkled throughout. I spent the last decade doing ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' and you come to rely on a lot of people doing things for you and a lot of help, but it's refreshing and wonderful to be reminded that, as with most filmmakers, the best way to do it is yourself, with a tight team doing the main jobs."<ref name="PostMag">{{cite web | last = Blair | first = Iain | title = Director's Chair: Sam Raimi Drag me to Hell | url = http://www.postmagazine.com/ME2/dirmod.asp?tier=4&mid=8F3A7027421841978F18BE895F87F791&sid=&nm=&id=9A2AE6F97AE946859A3C884BDE603E22&type=Publishing&mod=Publications%3A%3AArticle | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090802031958/http://www.postmagazine.com/ME2/dirmod.asp?sid=&nm=&type=Publishing&mod=Publications%3A%3AArticle&mid=8F3A7027421841978F18BE895F87F791&tier=4&id=9A2AE6F97AE946859A3C884BDE603E22 | archive-date=August 2, 2009 | work = Post Magazine | date = July 1, 2009 | access-date  = July 9, 2013}}</ref>

Production for ''Drag Me to Hell'' began on location in [[Tarzana, Los Angeles|Tarzana, California]].<ref name="prod" /> The production team included director of photography [[Peter Deming]], production designer Steve Saklad and visual effects supervisor Bruce Jones. The film was produced by [[Grant Curtis]] and [[Rob Tapert]]. Tapert and Raimi are longtime collaborators, having attended college together in Michigan.<ref name="PostMag" />

===Editing===
''Drag Me to Hell'' was edited by [[Bob Murawski]], who has collaborated with Raimi on several films including the ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' series, ''[[The Gift (2000 film)|The Gift]]'', and ''[[Army of Darkness]]''.<ref name="PostMag" /> Raimi has said of working with Murawski on ''Drag Me to Hell'', "He'd come (down to the set) to see how things were going and to let me know if he'd just cut something that wasn't working the way he'd wanted it to, or to suggest a pick-up shot I should get for a piece he felt we needed in a sequence I hadn't realized I needed. He's very detail-oriented... So we're very close collaborators." Raimi finds editing with Murawski to be "relaxing", adding, "I love it. For me, it's so relaxing, unlike pre-production, which is fraught with anxiety and fear about how we're going to do things, and production, which is so rushed and a sleepless time and you're just racing to finish every shot and worrying about focus and so on. So post is soothing and I can watch the film come together, so it's a time of discovery for me as Bob and I fit all the pieces together. I see new possibilities in post, as Bob puts the film together, sometimes in a way I never imagined..."<ref name="PostMag" /> The film was edited by Murawski on an Avid computer system in a West Los Angeles facility. The color grading was completed at Company 3 with colorist Stephen Nakamura. Nakamura used [[DaVinci Resolve]]. It was CO3's first start-to-finish feature in 4K resolution.<ref name="PostMag" /> "For us, post is a very creative time where it's not just about this factory producing the blueprinted product. It's really a very creative, experimental time where we try and take everything that's been written and then shot to the next level," said Raimi.<ref name="PostMag" /> The final sound mix was completed at the Dub Stage in Burbank with mixers Marti Humphrey and Chris Jacobson.<ref name="PostMag" />

===Visual effects===
The visual effects in ''Drag Me to Hell'' were created in many different ways, including [[Chroma key|greenscreen]], [[puppet]]s, prosthetics and [[computer-generated imagery|CGI]].<ref name="prod" /> Bruce Jones was the visual effects supervisor on the film. Of Jones, Raimi commented, "He brought a great can-do approach to the film... He's got a great team of artists and technicians with him, and he's got great instincts."<ref name="PostMag" />

There were hundreds of visual effects in the film, and different effects houses were used. According to Raimi, the [[Bay Area]]'s Tippett Studio was a big player. "We also had work done by Amalgamated Pixels, Ghost VFX, KNB Effects, Home Digital, Cinesoup and IE Effects," said Raimi. According to Raimi, "Bob (Murawski) and I kept adding visual effects as post proceeded. In this film, the supernatural, the unseen, is almost another character, so sequences were developed—even in post—that would suggest the presence of the supernatural, and we kept on adding. The same with the sound effects, so it was a very ongoing, very live process in post."<ref name="PostMag" />

Director of photography [[Peter Deming]] tried to use realistic lighting in the film. Said Deming, "Normally, you'd put all corrected bulbs in, but we went with what was there, including the shots in the street. We used the streetlight look and mixed that with interior lighting. There were a lot of odd color sources that we chose to leave the way they would be naturally. It's a heightened sense of realism."<ref name="prod" /> One of the earliest projects the visual effects teams did was the scene in which Mrs. Ganush attacks Christine in her car. To film the action, which included close-ups of Christine jamming her foot on the pedal, hitting the brake, and shifting gears, the team created a puzzle car which allowed the front engine compartment and back trunk—as well as all four sides and doors—to come away from the car. The roof came off in two directions.<ref name="prod" />

In 2024, ''The Independent'' reported that the maggots sprayed into Allison Lohman's mouth were live.<ref>{{cite news |last=Parkel |first=Inga |date=June 20, 2024 |title=Drag Me to Hell star reacts to 'disgusting' behind-the-scenes footage of live maggots sprayed in her mouth |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/drag-me-to-hell-maggots-allison-lohman-b2566115.html |work=[[The Independent]] |location= |publisher= |access-date=August 2, 2026}}</ref> However, the [[American Humane Society]]'s page for the film states that the "[p]roduction provided documentation that the flies, worms and maggots [...] were all fake."<ref>{{cite web |last= |first= |date= |title=Drag Me To Hell: Detailed Animal Action Report |url=https://www.americanhumane.org/production/drag-me-to-hell/ |website= |location= |publisher=[[American Humane Society]] |access-date=August 2, 2026}}</ref>

The [[film score]] was composed by [[Christopher Young]]. Young has collaborated with director Raimi previously on his films ''[[The Gift (2000 film)|The Gift]]'' and ''[[Spider-Man 3]]''. The soundtrack was released on June 2, 2009,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fangoria.com/musick/musick-news/3602-drag-me-to-hell-soundtrack-hits-stores.html|work=[[Fangoria]]|access-date=November 24, 2009|date=August 17, 2009|author=Zimmerman, Samuel|title=Drag Me to Hell soundtrack hits stores|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100103041404/http://fangoria.com/musick/musick-news/3602-drag-me-to-hell-soundtrack-hits-stores.html|archive-date=January 3, 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> and later in a [[Phonograph record|vinyl]] edition by [[Waxwork Records]] in 2018.<ref>{{cite web |author=Squires, John |date=24 October 2018 |title=Waxwork Brings the Complete 'Drag Me to Hell' Soundtrack to Vinyl This Friday! |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/music/3529303/waxwork-brings-complete-drag-hell-soundtrack-vinyl-friday/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201109021454/https://bloody-disgusting.com/music/3529303/waxwork-brings-complete-drag-hell-soundtrack-vinyl-friday/ |archive-date=November 9, 2020 |access-date=5 August 2020 |location=Bloody Disgusting}}</ref> Sam Raimi stated that emphasis was on using the soundtrack to create a world that didn't exist, a world of the "supernatural".<ref name="PostMag" /> The score contains elements of Young's previous work on ''[[Flowers in the Attic (1987 film)|Flowers in the Attic]]''. This is particularly apparent in the utilization of the ethereal childlike soprano vocals that feature prominently throughout the soundtrack.

==Release==
''Drag Me to Hell'' was first shown to the public as a "Work in Progress" print at the [[South by Southwest]] festival on March 15, 2009.<ref>{{cite web | title = Film Festival Schedule - Drag Me to Hell (Work in Progress) | url = http://sxsw.com/film/screenings/schedule/?a=show&s=F16660# | work = SXSW | location = Austin, Texas | year = 2009 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090314041225/http://sxsw.com/film/screenings/schedule/?a=show&s=F16660 | archive-date = March 14, 2009 | access-date = July 9, 2013}}</ref> The film debuted in its full form at the [[2009 Cannes Film Festival]], where it was shown out of competition on May 20, 2009, as a midnight screening. It was released on May 29, 2009.<ref>{{cite web | title = Drag Me To Hell Premiere - 2009 Cannes Film Festival | url = http://www.zimbio.com/photos/Sam+Raimi/Drag+Hell+Premiere+2009+Cannes+Film+Festival/U5dIkLyJE3K | work = zimbio.com | date = May 20, 2009 | access-date = 2018-10-17 | archive-date = October 18, 2018 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20181018122340/http://www.zimbio.com/photos/Sam+Raimi/Drag+Hell+Premiere+2009+Cannes+Film+Festival/U5dIkLyJE3K | url-status = deviated }}</ref><ref>{{cite news | title = Recession-era movie Drag Me to Hell marks revenge of the repossessed | url = https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/may/21/sam-raimi-returns-to-horror | work = [[The Guardian]] | date = May 21, 2009 | access-date = 2009-06-27 | location = London | first = Mark | last = Brown | archive-date = March 5, 2016 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20160305233032/http://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/may/21/sam-raimi-returns-to-horror | url-status = live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2009-05-19 |title=Out of Competition: “Drag Me to Hell” is a Midnight Screening |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2009/out-of-competition-drag-me-to-hell-is-a-midnight-screening/ |access-date=2026-04-21 |website=Festival de Cannes |language=en-US}}</ref>

===Home media===
''Drag Me to Hell'' was released on [[DVD]] and [[Blu-ray Disc]] in the US on October 13, 2009. For home video, an unrated edition was made available in addition to the theatrical version, the unrated version containing some additional moments of gory violence.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/drag-me-to-hell-v427518/releases|title=Drag Me to Hell (2009) - Releases|access-date=July 9, 2013|work=[[Allmovie]]|archive-date=May 18, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130518031523/http://www.allmovie.com/movie/drag-me-to-hell-v427518/releases|url-status=live}}</ref> In its first two weeks the DVD sold 459,217 copies generating $7.98 million in sales.<ref name="test">{{cite web |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movies/2009/DRAGM-DVD.php |title=Drag Me To Hell - DVD Sales |publisher=The-numbers.com |access-date=July 9, 2013 |archive-date=December 14, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131214031113/http://www.the-numbers.com/movies/2009/DRAGM-DVD.php |url-status=live }}</ref> It has since accumulated $13.9 million in DVD sales in the United States.

On February 13, 2018, [[Scream Factory]] released a two-disc Collector's Edition of ''Drag Me to Hell'', which included both the theatrical and unrated versions of the film remastered from the 2K digital intermediate, archival interviews and featurettes and all-new interviews with [[Alison Lohman]], [[Lorna Raver]] and [[Christopher Young]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Drag Me to Hell (Collector's Edition) Review - A Supernatural Horror Classic |url=https://fearflicks.click/products/cult-horrors-and-b-movies/drag-me-to-hell-collector-s-edition-review-a-supernatural-horror-classic |access-date=2024-09-19 |website=FEAR FLICKS |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240919062731/https://fearflicks.click/products/cult-horrors-and-b-movies/drag-me-to-hell-collector-s-edition-review-a-supernatural-horror-classic|archive-date=September 19, 2024}}</ref> They would also later issue an [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] edition on October 29, 2024, including all previous bonus features and a new director and editor-approved 4K remaster of both versions of the film, based on a scan of a preservation film negative.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Drag-Me-to-Hell-4K-Blu-ray/367059/ |title=Drag Me to Hell 4K Blu-ray (Collector's Edition) |access-date=2024-12-15 |via=www.blu-ray.com}}</ref>

==Reception==

===Box office===
The film was released in the United States on May 29, 2009. The film opened at #4 with $15.8 million from 2,900 screens at 2,508 theaters, an average of $6,310 per theater ($5,457 average per screen). In its second weekend, it dropped 56%, falling to #7, with $7 million, for an average of $2,805 per theater ($2,514 average per screen), and bringing the 10-day gross to $28,233,230.<ref name="the-numbers.com">{{cite web | url = https://www.the-numbers.com/movies/2009/DRAGM.php | title = The Numbers - Box Office Data for Drag Me to Hell | access-date = 2009-07-11 | publisher = [[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] | archive-date = December 15, 2013 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20131215171514/http://www.the-numbers.com/movies/2009/DRAGM.php | url-status = live }}</ref> Even though its two-week initial performance was described as "disappointing",<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://ew.com/article/2009/06/07/drag-me-to-hell-why-didnt-it-do-better/ | title='Drag Me to Hell': Why didn't it do better? | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] | access-date=May 1, 2023 | archive-date=May 1, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230501134419/https://ew.com/article/2009/06/07/drag-me-to-hell-why-didnt-it-do-better/ | url-status=live |last=Gleiberman|first=Owen|date=June 7, 2009}}</ref> ''Drag Me to Hell'' closed on August 6, 2009, with a final gross in the United States and Canada of $42.1 million, and an additional $48.7 million internationally for a total of $90.8 million worldwide.<ref name="boxofficemojo">{{cite web | url = https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=dragmetohell.htm | title = Drag Me to Hell (2009) | access-date = November 23, 2010 | publisher = [[Box Office Mojo]] | archive-date = May 14, 2010 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20100514233535/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=dragmetohell.htm | url-status = live }}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 92% based on 270 reviews, and an average rating of 7.6/10. The site's critical consensus states, "Sam Raimi returns to top form with ''Drag Me to Hell'', a frightening, hilarious, delightfully [[Camp (style)|campy]] thrill ride."<ref name="rottentomatoes">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/drag_me_to_hell/|title=Drag Me to Hell|access-date=October 22, 2019|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=IGN Entertainment, Inc|archive-date=October 14, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191014015129/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/drag_me_to_hell/|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 83 out of 100, based on 32 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="metacritic">{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/drag-me-to-hell/|title=Drag Me to Hell|work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=CNET Networks, Inc|access-date=June 27, 2009|archive-date=May 28, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090528222259/http://www.metacritic.com/film/titles/dragmetohell|url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/|title=Find CinemaScore|format=Type "Drag Me" in the search box|publisher=[[CinemaScore]]|access-date=April 2, 2021|archive-date=January 2, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180102130540/https://www.cinemascore.com/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Owen Gleiberman of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' gave the film an "A" rating, stating that "Raimi has made the most crazy, fun, and terrifying horror movie in years."<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Gleiberman|first=Owen|title=Drag Me to Hell > Movie Review > Entertainment Weekly|url=https://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,20281263,00.html|date=May 27, 2009|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=June 28, 2009|archive-date=June 27, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090627024602/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,20281263,00.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Betsy Sharkey of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' praised the film, writing that it "should not be dismissed as yet another horror flick just for teens. The filmmakers have given us a 10-story winding staircase of psychological tension that is making very small circles near the end."<ref>{{cite web|last=Sharkey|first=Betsy|title=Review: 'Drag Me to Hell'|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/la-et-drag29-2009may29,0,4792297.story|date=May 29, 2009|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=June 28, 2009|archive-date=June 12, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090612231929/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/la-et-drag29-2009may29,0,4792297.story|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Michael Phillips (critic)|Michael Phillips]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' described the film as a "hellaciously effective B-movie [that] comes with a handy moral tucked inside its scares, laughs and Raimi's specialty, the scare/laugh hybrid."<ref>{{cite web|last=Phillips|first=Michael|title='Drag Me to Hell' stars Alison Lohman, Justin Long, David Paymer|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/features/lifestyle/chi-tc-mov-drag-me-to-hell-0527-may29,0,4474885.story|date=May 29, 2009|work=[[Chicago Tribune]]|access-date=June 28, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090611155449/http://www.chicagotribune.com/features/lifestyle/chi-tc-mov-drag-me-to-hell-0527-may29,0,4474885.story|archive-date=June 11, 2009}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun Times]]'' gave the film 3 out of 4 stars, and stated that the film "is a sometimes funny and often startling horror movie. That is what it wants to be, and that is what it is."<ref>{{cite web | last = Ebert | first = Roger | title = 'Drag Me to Hell' Review | url = https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/drag-me-to-hell-2009 | date = 2009-06-03 | work = [[Chicago Sun Times]] | access-date = 2009-06-28 | archive-date = April 6, 2022 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20220406152602/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/drag-me-to-hell-2009 | url-status = live |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> In a positive review, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' said of the film: "Scant and barren of subtext, the pic is single-mindedly devoted to pushing the audience's buttons... Still, there's no denying it delivers far more than competing PG-13 thrillers."<ref name="variety">{{cite web | last = Debruge | first = Peter | title = Drag Me to Hell Review | url = https://www.variety.com/review/VE1117940322.html?categoryid=31&cs=1 | date = 2009-05-20 | work = [[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] | access-date = 2009-06-28 | archive-date = May 25, 2009 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090525193642/http://www.variety.com/review/VE1117940322.html?categoryid=31&cs=1 | url-status = live }}</ref> [[Bloody Disgusting]] gave the film four and a half stars out of five, with the review calling it "quite simply the most perfect horror film I've seen in a long, long while... [It's] a blast and moved quickly from start to finish [and] is well on its way to becoming an immediate classic."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3169864/b-d-s-first-thoughts-on-sam-raimi-s-drag-me-to-hell/|title=Drag Me to Hell|publisher=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=April 21, 2026|archive-date=June 9, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100609140704/http://www.bloody-disgusting.com/film/1667/review|url-status=live|date=January 28, 2009|last=Miska|first=Brad}}</ref> The film was then ranked thirteenth in Bloody Disgusting's list of the 'Top 20 Horror Films of the Decade'.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/18415|title=00's Retrospect: Bloody Disgusting's Top 20 Films of the Decade...Part 2|date=December 17, 2009|publisher=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|access-date=2010-01-03|archive-date=December 19, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091219195634/http://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/18415|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Rex Reed]] of ''[[The New York Observer]]'' thought that the plot wasn't believable enough,<ref>{{cite web | last = Reed | first = Rex | title = What Has Happened to Sam Raimi? | url = http://www.observer.com/2009/movies/what-has-happened-sam-raimi | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090528055620/http://www.observer.com/2009/movies/what-has-happened-sam-raimi | url-status = dead | archive-date = 2009-05-28 | date = 2009-05-29 | work =[[The New York Observer]] | access-date = 2009-06-28}}</ref> and Peter Howell of ''[[The Toronto Star]]'' disliked Lohman's performance and thought the film was "just not very funny".<ref>{{cite news | last = Howell | first = Peter | title = Drag Me to Hell: What a drag | url = https://www.thestar.com/news/drag-me-to-hell-what-a-drag/article_a3624c5e-31d9-5fcf-914a-98c39e467aac.html | date = 2009-05-29 | work = [[The Toronto Star]] | access-date = April 21, 2026 | archive-date = June 1, 2009 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090601093950/http://www.thestar.com/entertainment/article/642230 | url-status = live }}</ref>

Some reviews considered the film a [[comedy horror]] in the style that Raimi is known for. The film "blends horror and humor so well that viewers don't know whether to laugh or scream", noted ''[[TV Guide]]'', which also hailed it as "a popcorn film that aims to entertain—nothing more, nothing less—and it achieves that goal admirably. Few films, horror or otherwise, can boast such a claim, making Raimi's self-described 'spook-a-blast' an excellent example of a film where ambition and execution come together in perfect harmony."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.tvguide.com/movies/drag-me-to-hell/2000309645/ | title=Drag Me to Hell | access-date=May 1, 2023 | archive-date=May 1, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230501134417/https://www.tvguide.com/movies/drag-me-to-hell/review/2000309645/ | url-status=live }}</ref> Vic Holtreman of ''[[Screen Rant]]'' stated that the film blends comedy and horror in a similar fashion to the way ''[[Army of Darkness]]'' does.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://screenrant.com/drag-me-to-hell-reviews-vic-10582/ |title=Drag Me To Hell Review |publisher=Screen Rant |date=2009-05-28 |access-date=2010-03-22 |archive-date=October 23, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161023150734/http://screenrant.com/drag-me-to-hell-reviews-vic-10582/ |url-status=live |last=Holtreman|first=Vic}}</ref> According to a reviewer at [[UGO Networks]], the film is primarily a comedy rather than a horror, and this is consistent with Raimi's directing style, which has not included any "true horror" films.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ugo.com/movies/drag-me-to-hell-review-should-have-just-been-evil-dead |title=Drag Me To Hell Review - Should Have Just Been Evil Dead |publisher=UGO.com |date=2009-05-21 |access-date=2010-03-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100323191725/http://www.ugo.com/movies/drag-me-to-hell-review-should-have-just-been-evil-dead |archive-date=March 23, 2010 |url-status=dead }}</ref>
