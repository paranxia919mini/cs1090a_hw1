'''''Brandos Costumes''''' (1974) is a [[Cinema of Portugal|Portuguese film]] directed by [[Alberto Seixas Santos]] which was a part of the [[Novo Cinema]] movement – influenced by the cinematographic [[Italian neorealism|neo-realism]] and specially by the [[Nouvelle Vague]]. It was released in 1975, when the political regime portrayed in the film (the [[Estado Novo (Portugal)|Estado Novo]]) had already been destroyed.<ref>{{cite book |last=de España |first=Rafael |year=2013 |chapter=Portugal |editor-last=Aitken |editor-first=Ian |title=The Concise Routledge Encyclopedia of the Documentary Film |publisher=Routledge |page=737 |isbn=9780415596428}}</ref>

The film was released in ''Cinema Londres'', in Lisbon, on September 18, 1975.

== Synopsis ==
A portrait of the everyday life of a typical middle-class family in parallel with the fall of the [[Estado Novo (Portugal)|Estado Novo]], the 48-year dictatorship led by [[António de Oliveira Salazar|Salazar]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Luhr |first=William |year=1987 |title=World Cinema Since 1945 |publisher=Ungar |page=496 |isbn=9780804430784}}</ref> The daughters' conflicts and frustrations with their parents, their grandmother and their maid find an obvious echo in the country's collective events. The [[Carnation Revolution]] is about to explode.

== Historical context ==
As a rupturing film, ''Brandos Costumes'' is less identifiable by the presence of avant-garde aesthetics or an agile plot with a daring structure - not like [[Belarmino]], by [[Fernando Lopes (filmmaker)|Fernando Lopes]] or O Cerco, by [[António da Cunha Telles]] - than by its ideological [[Left-wing politics|left-wing]] posture, taking a portrait of the social classes, and by its social and political sense of critic.

Some characteristics of the new generation films, revolted with the state of things and motivated to denounce the social injustices, are clearly present in ''Brandos Costumes''. The theatrical tone of the representation of this work let it be integrated in the tradition that [[Manoel de Oliveira]] (O Passado e o Presente - 1971) explores.{{citation needed|date=June 2022}}
