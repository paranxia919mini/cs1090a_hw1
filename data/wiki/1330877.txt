'''''A Dirty Shame''''' is a 2004 American [[Satire (film and television)|satirical]] [[sex comedy]] film written and directed by [[John Waters]] and starring [[Tracey Ullman]], [[Johnny Knoxville]], [[Selma Blair]], and [[Chris Isaak]]. It follows a community in suburban Baltimore divided between people with highly conservative attitudes towards sexuality, and those who have been turned into sex addicts after experiencing concussions.

After premiering at the [[2004 Toronto International Film Festival]], ''A Dirty Shame'' was released in the United States on September 17, 2004. The film received mixed reviews from critics. Due to its sexual themes and content, it received an [[NC-17]] rating from the [[Motion Picture Association of America]], which limited the scope of its release and marketing, and it grossed $1.5 million domestically. Due to its poor box office performance, the film has stood as Waters' last directorial effort<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2013/nov/03/interview-john-waters-pink-flamingos-liverpool | title=John Waters: 'Doing the show is an effective anti-Alzheimer's exercise' | work=[[The Guardian]] | date=November 2, 2013 | access-date=January 28, 2015}}</ref> to date.

==Plot==
The people of [[Harford Road]] are firmly divided into two camps: the neuters, the [[puritan]]ical residents who despise anything even remotely carnal; and the perverts, a group of [[Sexual addiction|sex addicts]] whose unique [[Sexual fetishism|fetishes]] have all been brought to the fore by accidental [[concussion]]s. Repressed Sylvia Stickles finds herself firmly entrenched in the former camp.

Sylvia's [[Promiscuity|promiscuous]] daughter Caprice, a go-go dancer at a local biker dive-bar, the Holiday House, performs under the stage name "Ursula Udders" because of her very large N-cup breast implants and has a penchant for [[indecent exposure]].

One day, after leaving Caprice locked up over the garage, under [[house arrest]] "for her own good", Sylvia is smacked on the head by a passing car and meets Ray-Ray Perkins, a local mechanic and self-styled "sex saint" who opens her mind to a whole new world of sensual pleasure, as he and his followers search for the ultimate sex act.

Eventually, through a series of bizarre head knockings, everyone in the Harford Road area of Baltimore becomes a sex addict, as Ray-Ray shoots [[semen]] out of his head and becomes the messiah of "Let's Go Sexin'!"

==Production==
[[John Waters]] decided to make the film after discovering several sexual slang terms and branches existed on the internet, explaining the groups and terminology found in the film.<ref>DVD Features{{Full citation needed|date=August 2026}}</ref> The film was shot entirely on-location in [[Baltimore]] on [[Maryland Route 147|Harford Road]] which is prominently featured in the film. In a featurette on the DVD, veteran stage actress Suzanne Shepherd recalled that when she was cast as Big Ethel, she was completely unfamiliar with Waters' work and she had no idea what to expect when she showed up for the first script review. Horrified by what she was reading, she became so distraught that she began to cry. She tried to quit the project but Waters and her cast mates managed to persuade her to stay.<ref>{{cite AV media |title=All the Dirt on A Dirty Shame |date=2005 |type=DVD featurette |publisher=New Line Home Video}}</ref> [[Paul Giamatti]] was originally slated to play Vaughn Stickles, but dropped out before filming began.<ref>{{cite web |last= Rooney|first= David|date= September 18, 2003|title= Waters, New Line get 'Dirty'|url= https://variety.com/2003/film/people-news/waters-new-line-get-dirty-1117892676/|access-date= August 24, 2020|website= [[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>

The film received an [[NC-17]] rating from the [[Motion Picture Association of America]] (MPAA). This caused the film's distribution to be severely limited, as most major theater chains don't show NC-17 rated films while media outlets are reluctant to carry advertising for these movies.<ref>{{cite news|url = https://www.today.com/popculture/fox-searchlight-release-nc-17-film-wbna3949436|title = Fox Searchlight to release NC-17 film|work = [[Today (American TV program)|Today]]|date = January 13, 2004|access-date = April 24, 2020}}</ref> When Waters asked what he would need to cut for them to give his film an [[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system|R rating]], he was told that the ratings board "stopped taking notes."<ref name=":0">{{Cite video | people = John Waters | url = |title=This Film Is Not Yet Rated | medium = Movie | publisher = Independent Film Channel (IFC) | date = 2006 |title-link=This Film Is Not Yet Rated}}</ref> After Waters unsuccessfully attempted to appeal the rating, the film was released with the NC-17 classification.<ref>{{cite book|last = Waters|first = John|title = Mr. Know-it-All: The Tarnished Wisdom of a Filth Elder|publisher = [[Farrar, Straus and Giroux]]|location = New York|date = 2019|pages = 115–116|isbn = 978-0-374-21496-8}}</ref>

==Reception==
''A Dirty Shame'' received mixed reviews from critics, with its explicitness both lauded and lampooned. On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 55% based on 113 reviews, with an average rating of 5.7/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "John Waters casts his provocative eye towards libido in a sex satire that won't leave audiences feeling too ''Dirty'', but this sophomoric farce doesn't strike a nerve like his best work – a real ''Shame''."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dirty_shame |title=A Dirty Shame |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=June 23, 2026}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 56 out of 100, based on 34 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/a-dirty-shame |title=A Dirty Shame Reviews |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=March 13, 2018}}</ref>

One of the more positive reviews came from Kevin Thomas of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', who wrote

<blockquote>A gross-out pioneer, Waters has always had more on his mind than delirious, sex-crazed silliness. By allowing people to speak freely about their sexual urges and practices with a bluntness that is jaw-droppingly hilarious, Waters has drawn deeply upon comedy's liberating power. The more the sex addicts talk about their hang-ups the more comically harmless they seem, and thus it's all the more absurd for the puritanical to try to punish them for their various pursuits of pleasure.

Waters has always harnessed poor taste to lampoon attempts to limit freedom of expression. This raucously gritty and high-spirited film could scarcely be bluer in terms of the language, but from Waters it comes as a gust of fresh air.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2004-sep-24-et-dirty24-story.html |title=John Waters returns to his 'Dirty' roots |first=Kevin |last=Thomas |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=September 24, 2004 |access-date=March 13, 2018}}</ref></blockquote>

Also enthusiastic was [[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'', who wrote:

<blockquote>''A Dirty Shame'' is Waters unleashed, and wicked, kinky fun for anyone except the twits who rated it NC-17...You may even shed a tear when Sylvia bonds with her daughter by confessing, "I'm a cunnilingus bottom." OK, the jokes are hit-and-miss and the plot is nonexistent, but the Waters spirit stays consistently and sweetly twisted. When the cast takes to the streets singing, "Let's go sexin'", you want to cheer them on.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/a-dirty-shame-20040914 |title=A Dirty Shame |first=Peter |last=Travers |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=September 14, 2004 |access-date=March 13, 2018}}</ref></blockquote>

On the other end of the spectrum was [[Roger Ebert]], who gave the film one star out of a possible four, writing:

<blockquote>There is in show biz something known as "a bad laugh". That's the laugh you don't want to get, because it indicates not amusement but incredulity, nervousness or disapproval. John Waters' ''A Dirty Shame'' is the only comedy I can think of that gets more bad laughs than good ones...We go to a Waters film expecting bad taste, but we also expect to laugh, and ''A Dirty Shame'' is monotonous, repetitive and sometimes wildly wrong in what it hopes is funny.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/a-dirty-shame-2004 |title=A Dirty Shame |first=Roger |last=Ebert |author-link=Roger Ebert |website=Chicago Sun-Times |date=September 24, 2004 |access-date=March 13, 2018|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref></blockquote>

===Box office===
''A Dirty Shame'' opened on September 17, 2004, on one Baltimore screen and grossed $29,384 for the weekend. The next weekend, it expanded to 133 venues, where it grossed $448,914 ($3,375 per screen). It grossed a total of $1,339,668 in the United States and Canada.<ref name="mojo">{{Cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0365125/|title=A Dirty Shame (2004)|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=January 13, 2023}}</ref> Overseas, the film grossed an additional $574,498 (as of July 14, 2005), making its global box office total come to $1.9 million.<ref name="mojo" />

==Home media==
{{More citations needed section|date = September 2018}}
The film was released on DVD on June 14, 2005.<ref>{{cite web|first=Erik|last=Gruenwedel|url=http://www.hive4media.com/news/html/breaking_article.cfm?article_id=7370|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050407024605/http://www.hive4media.com/news/html/breaking_article.cfm?article_id=7370|title='Dirty Shame' A Parody of Uptight Culture|website=[[hive4media.com]]|archive-date=April 7, 2005|date=April 5, 2005|access-date=September 28, 2019|url-status=usurped}}</ref> It was issued in its original NC-17 version and in an edited, R-rated cut sold through [[Blockbuster Inc.|Blockbuster]], [[Wal-Mart]] and [[Best Buy]] as well as [[Target Corporation|Target]] dubbed "''The Neuter Version''". The R-rated version is heavily censored and removes most of the profanities including altered dialogue such as "sex toy". Additionally, a scene in which two characters stand naked in their doorway is replaced by one in which the characters are clothed. In an interview, Waters stated that this version is "essentially for brainless people and 'really weird collectors'".<ref name=":0" />

[[Netflix]] claimed to stream the NC-17 version but for a time, it was the censored version. It has since replaced it with the uncensored cut. A heavily censored version aired on [[Logo (TV channel)|LOGO]], a cable channel for gay interest. Despite several internet sites and even the spine of the DVD stating that it is "The Neuter Version", the Australian release of the film is the NC-17 version<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ezydvd.com.au/DVD/dirty-shame-a/dp/799492/799492|title=Buy Dirty Shame, A on DVD-Video from EzyDVD.com.au|publisher=[[EzyDVD]]|access-date=April 17, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140419012056/http://www.ezydvd.com.au/DVD/dirty-shame-a/dp/799492/799492|archive-date=April 19, 2014|url-status=dead|df=mdy-all}}</ref> with all profanity and nudity intact. It is rated [[Australian Classification Board|R18+]] for "sexual references and adult themes".
