'''''The Brain That Wouldn't Die''''' (also known as '''''The Head That Wouldn't Die''''') is a 1962 American [[Science fiction film|science fiction]] [[horror film]] directed by Joseph Green and  starring [[Jason Evers]] and [[Virginia Leith]]. It was written by Green and Rex Carlton.<ref>{{cite book|editor=Young, R.G.|title=The Encyclopedia of Fantastic Film: Ali Baba to Zombies|year=2000|publisher=Hal Leonard Corporation|isbn=1-557-83269-2|page=72}}</ref> The film was completed in 1959 under the working title '''''The Black Door''''' but was not theatrically released until May 3, 1962, as a [[double feature]] with ''[[Invasion of the Star Creatures]]''.<ref name=afi>{{cite book|last=American Film Institute|title=The American Film Institute Catalog of Motion Pictures Produced in the United States|volume=1|year=1997|publisher=University of California Press|isbn=0-520-20970-2|page=120}}</ref><ref name=mcgee>{{cite book|last=McGee|first=Mark T. |title=Fast and Furious: The Story of American International Pictures|year=1984|publisher=McFarland|isbn=0-899-50091-9|page=232}}</ref>

The film focuses upon a [[Mad scientist|mad doctor]] who develops a means of keeping human body parts alive. He keeps his fiancée's severed head alive for days, along with a lumbering, malformed brute (one of his earlier failed experiments) imprisoned in a closet.

The specific plot device of a mad doctor who discovers a way to keep a human head alive had been used in fiction earlier (such as ''[[Professor Dowell's Head]]'' from 1925), as well as other variants on this theme. It shares several key plot devices with the West German horror film ''[[The Head (1959 film)|The Head]]'' (1959).

The film was in the [[List of films in the public domain in the United States|public domain in the United States]] from the day of its release due to a flawed copyright notice.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/69565/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/#articles-reviews?articleId=151934 |title=Turner Classic Movies |access-date=June 6, 2021 |archive-date=June 6, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210606212444/https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/69565/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/#articles-reviews?articleId=151934 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

== Plot ==
[[File:The Brain That Wouldn't Die (1962) by Joseph Green.webm | thumb|thumbtime=16|left|{{center|The entire film}}]]
Dr. Bill Cortner saves a patient who had been pronounced dead, but the senior surgeon, Bill's father, condemns his son's unorthodox methods and theories of [[Organ transplantation|transplanting]].

While driving to his family's country house, Bill and his beautiful fiancée Jan Compton become involved in a car accident that decapitates her. Bill recovers her severed head and rushes to his country house basement laboratory. He and his disabled assistant Kurt revive the head in a liquid-filled tray. But Jan's new existence is agony, and she begs Bill to let her die. He ignores her pleas, and she grows to resent him.

Bill decides to commit murder to obtain a body for Jan. He hunts for a suitable specimen at a [[burlesque]] nightclub, on the streets, and at a beauty contest. Jan begins communicating [[Telepathy|telepathically]] with a hideous mutant, an experiment gone wrong, locked in a laboratory cell. When Kurt leaves a hatch in the cell door unlocked, the monster grabs and tears off Kurt's arm. Kurt dies from his injuries.

Bill lures an old girlfriend, figure model Doris Powell, to his house, promising to study her scarred face for plastic surgery. He drugs her and carries her to the laboratory. Jan protests Bill's plan to transplant her head onto Doris's body. He tapes Jan's mouth shut.

When Bill goes to quiet the monster, it grabs Bill through the hatch and breaks the door from its hinges. Their struggles set the laboratory ablaze. The monster, a seven-foot giant with a horribly deformed head, bites a chunk from Bill's right cheek. Bill dies, and the monster carries the unconscious Doris to safety. As the lab goes up in flames, Jan says "I told you to let me die". The screen goes black, followed by Jan's maniacal cackle, welcoming her long-awaited death.

== Production ==
The film was shot independently around [[Tarrytown, New York]], in 1959 under the working title ''The Black Door''.<ref name=afi/> Producer Rex Carlton suggested calling the film ''I Was a Teenage Brain Surgeon'' in the style of the similar titles of the time.<ref>p. 207  Senn, Bryan ''"Twice the Thrills! Twice the Chills!": Horror and Science Fiction Double Features, 1955-1974'' McFarland 28 February 2019</ref> The title was later changed to ''The Head That Wouldn't Die''.<ref name="gary">Gary A. Smith, ''The American International Pictures Video Guide'', McFarland, 2009. p. 33.</ref> Some prints of the film use both the opening title ''The Brain That Wouldn't Die'' and the closing title ''The Head That Wouldn't Die''.

The monster in the closet was played, in his first cinematic role, by [[Eddie Carmel]], a well-known [[Mandatory Palestine]]-born circus performer, who worked under the name "The [[Jewish]] Giant". He was the subject of a photograph by [[Diane Arbus]], titled "The Jewish Giant at Home with His Parents in the Bronx, N.Y., 1970".<ref name="SoundPortraits">{{cite web
 |url         = http://www.soundportraits.org/on-air/the_jewish_giant/

The main theme, titled "The Web", was composed by [[Abe Baker]] and Tony Restaino and was noted for creating a sinister mood.<ref name=mcgee/>

== Release ==
[[File:Bluff View Drive-in Ad - 14 September 1962, Brownwood, TX.png|thumb|140px|Advertisement from 1962 for ''The Brain That Wouldn't Die'' and co-feature, ''[[Invasion of the Star Creatures]]'']]
The movie was picked up for release by AIP and released in 1962 on a double bill with ''[[Invasion of the Star Creatures]]''. AIP cut it to 71 minutes for theatrical release.<ref name="gary"/>

=== ''Mystery Science Theater 3000'' episode ===
The film was featured in episode 513 of ''[[Mystery Science Theater 3000]]'', first airing on [[Comedy Central]] on October 30, 1993.<ref>{{cite web |last=sampo |title=Episode guide: 513- The Brain that Wouldn't Die |url=https://www.mst3kinfo.com/?p=1164 |website=Satellite News |date=June 23, 2016 |access-date=November 3, 2025}}</ref>

This film was the first movie watched by [[Mike Nelson (character)|Mike Nelson]] ([[Michael J. Nelson]]) in ''Mystery Science Theater 3000'', after he replaced Joel Robinson ([[Joel Hodgson]]) on the series. The theme song was altered for this change, and the "doorway sequence" into the theater was also updated.<ref name="auto">{{cite book|author=Beaulieu, Trace|display-authors=etal|title=The Mystery Science Theater 3000 Amazing Colossal Episode Guide|date=1996|publisher=Bantam Books|location=New York|isbn=9780553377835|pages=98–99|edition=1st}}</ref>

In a poll of Bring Back ''MST3K'' [[Kickstarter]] backers, which raised money for an eleventh season of the show, ''The Brain that Wouldn't Die'' was ranked #23.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/the-top-100-best-mst3k-episodes/|title=The Top 100 Best MST3K Episodes|date=April 15, 2017|website=Den of Geek|access-date=June 6, 2021|archive-date=November 15, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191115195150/https://www.denofgeek.com/us/tv/mst3k/260278/the-top-100-best-mst3k-episodes|url-status=dead}}</ref> Writer Jim Vorel ranked the episode considerably lower, at #131{{efn|Ranking based on 197 episodes {{as of|2018|lc=y|post=.}} }} in his ranking of ''MST3K'' episodes from the first twelve seasons, saying, "It’s a dark, fairly ugly movie with extremely cheap sets, but Mike’s presence puts the crew into an upbeat, energetic state that contrasts nicely with it."<ref>{{cite web |last=Vorel |first=Jim |title=Ranking Every MST3K Episode, From Worst to Best |url=https://www.pastemagazine.com/comedy/best-mst3k-episodes/ranking-every-mst3k-episode-from-worst-to-best | website=[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]] |date=October 25, 2017 |access-date= November 7, 2025}}</ref>

== Reception and legacy ==
''[[Boxoffice Pro|Boxoffice]]'' wrote: "Unrelievedly concerned with the by-now familiar characterization of the mad scientist bent on preserving his fantastic schemes for the ostensible good of future mankind, this Rex Carlton production is on a par with predecessor attractions, director Joseph Green's screenplay providing little unanticipated developments from fade-in to fade-out."<ref>{{Cite journal |date=30 July 1962 |title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die |volume=81 |issue=15 |pages=a11, a12. |id={{ProQuest|1670965142}} |magazine=[[Boxoffice Pro|Boxoffice]]}}</ref>

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 38% based on {{nowrap|13 reviews}}, with a [[weighted average]] rating of 4.63/10.<ref name="rottomatoes">{{cite web|title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die (1962) – Rotten Tomatoes|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/brain_that_wouldnt_die|website=Rotten Tomatoes.com|publisher=Rotten Tomatoes|access-date=July 9, 2019|archive-date=October 9, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221009062656/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/brain-that-wouldnt-die|url-status=live}}</ref>

Author and film critic [[Leonard Maltin]] awarded the film 1.5 out of four stars, calling it "poorly produced".<ref name="MaltinGreen2010">{{cite book|author1=Leonard Maltin|author2=Spencer Green|author3=Rob Edelman|title=Leonard Maltin's Classic Movie Guide|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=hLtaAAAAYAAJ|date=January 2010|publisher=Plume|isbn=978-0-452-29577-3|page=80|access-date=January 25, 2018|archive-date=October 9, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221009062656/https://books.google.com/books?id=hLtaAAAAYAAJ|url-status=live}}</ref>

On his website ''Fantastic Movie Musings and Ramblings'', Dave Sindelar gave the film a mostly negative review, noting that, although it managed to work up a certain amount of tension and featured some good gore effects, it was ruined by its lack of likable and intelligent characters and its "inability to decide just how it wants to be taken".<ref name="sindelar05">{{cite web|last1=Sindelar|first1=Dave|title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die (1962)|url=https://fantasticmoviemusings.com/2016/10/15/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die-1962/|website=Fantastic Movie Musings.com|date=October 15, 2016 |publisher=Dave Sindelar|access-date=January 25, 2018|archive-date=January 26, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180126033327/https://fantasticmoviemusings.com/2016/10/15/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die-1962/|url-status=live}}</ref>

''[[TV Guide]]'' awarded the film two out of four stars, calling it "one of the most genuinely bizarre 'brain' movies".<ref name="tvguiderev">{{cite web|title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die – Movie Reviews and Movie Ratings|url=http://www.tvguide.com/movies/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/review/119243/|website=TV Guilde.com|publisher=TV Guide|access-date=January 25, 2018|archive-date=January 26, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180126012954/http://www.tvguide.com/movies/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/review/119243/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Adaptations ===
The movie also inspired the musical stage production ''The Brain That Wouldn't Die! In 3D!!!'' by Tom Sivak and Elizabeth Gelman, that premiered at the [[New York Musical Theatre Festival]] in October 2011.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.playbill.com/news/article/154092-Stephen-Buntrock-and-Kathy-Voytko-to-Battle-The-Brain-That-Wouldnt-Die-In-3-D-at-NYMF|title=Stephen Buntrock and Kathy Voytko to Battle The Brain That Wouldn't Die! In 3-D!!! at NYMF|last=Hetrick|first=Adam|date=August 30, 2011|publisher=playbill.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110914055234/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/154092-Stephen-Buntrock-and-Kathy-Voytko-to-Battle-The-Brain-That-Wouldnt-Die-In-3-D-at-NYMF|archive-date=September 14, 2011}}</ref>

In 2015, Pug Bujeaud's musical theatrical production ''The HEAD! That Wouldn't Die'' was mounted in [[Olympia, Washington]] by Theater Artists Olympia. Lyrics and music were written by the ensemble cast and the TAO collective.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.northwestmilitary.com/music-and-culture/stage-and-visual-reviews/2015/10/the-head-that-wouldnt-die-redux/|title=Theater Artists Olympia revised their huge B-movie hit|last=Clayton|first=Alec|date=October 8, 2015|publisher=northwestmilitary.com|access-date=October 16, 2015|archive-date=January 11, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160111012958/http://www.northwestmilitary.com/music-and-culture/stage-and-visual-reviews/2015/10/the-head-that-wouldnt-die-redux/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Soon thereafter, Hollywood screenwriter Bruce Bernhard adapted the script as a staged musical comedy, creating a completely new score for it with songwriter Chris Cassone. The official world premiere for ''The Brain That Wouldn’t Die!…the Musical'' was at the Footlight Players Theatre in [[Charleston, South Carolina]] on October 13, 2016.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://patch.com/new-york/portchester/brain-wouldnt-die-musical|title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die Musical|date=June 21, 2017|website=Port Chester, NY Patch|access-date=June 6, 2021|archive-date=June 6, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210606173250/https://patch.com/new-york/portchester/brain-wouldnt-die-musical|url-status=live}}</ref>

A [[The Brain That Wouldn't Die (2020 film)|satirical feature film adaptation of the same title]] was filmed on location in [[Portland, Oregon]]. The film premiered on June 21, 2020, as part of the Portland Horror Film Festival.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://portlandhorrorfilmfestival.com/film/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/|title=The Brain That Wouldn't Die|first=Gwen|last=Callahan|date=June 2, 2020|access-date=June 24, 2020|archive-date=June 26, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200626162143/https://portlandhorrorfilmfestival.com/film/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://hollywoodtheatre.org//events/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die-2020-shorts-7/|title=THE BRAIN THAT WOULDN'T DIE (2020) + SHORTS 7|via=hollywoodtheatre.org|access-date=November 18, 2020|archive-date=August 19, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200819122350/https://hollywoodtheatre.org/events/the-brain-that-wouldnt-die-2020-shorts-7/|url-status=live}}</ref>
