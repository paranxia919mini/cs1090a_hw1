'''''The Prowler''''' is a 1981 American [[slasher film]] directed by [[Joseph Zito]], written by Neal Barbera and [[Glenn Leopold]], and starring [[Vicky Dawson]], [[Christopher Goutman]], [[Lawrence Tierney]], Cindy Weintraub, and [[Farley Granger]]. The film follows a group of college students in a coastal New Jersey town who are stalked and murdered during a graduation party by an apparent [[World War II]] veteran who killed his ex-girlfriend Rosemary Chatham in 1945.

Filmed in late 1980 in [[Cape May, New Jersey]], ''The Prowler'' was independently distributed by Sandhurst Releasing Corporation. It was not a major commercial success, ranking 135th overall that year at the U.S. box office, and grossing less than $1 million. In some international territories, the film was released under the alternate title ''Rosemary's Killer'' in a version that truncated many of its graphic murder sequences.

Though it has received mixed reviews from critics, ''The Prowler'' developed a [[cult following]] in the years after its release, with praise aimed at its hard-edged violence—showcasing special effects by [[Tom Savini]]—as well as its atmosphere. It has been named one of the greatest slasher films of all time by several publications, including ''[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]]''<ref name=complex>{{cite web|url=https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/best-slasher-movies-of-all-time/the-prowler|work=[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]]|title=The Best Slasher Films of All Time|date=October 23, 2017|last=Barone|first=Matt|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221216210112/https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/best-slasher-movies-of-all-time/|archive-date=December 16, 2022|url-status=live}}</ref> and ''[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]'' magazine,<ref name=paste>{{cite web|url=https://www.pastemagazine.com/articles/2018/08/the-50-best-slasher-movies-of-all-time.html|work=[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]|title=The Best Slasher Movies of All Time|date=August 8, 2018|last=Vorel|first=Jim|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190712181827/https://www.pastemagazine.com/articles/2018/08/the-50-best-slasher-movies-of-all-time.html|archive-date=July 12, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> and is noted as a classic of the slasher film subgenre.<ref name=Paul>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3690830/bayonets-pitchforks-golden-age-slasher-prowler-turns-40/|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|url-status=live|archive-date=May 28, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230528101155/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3690830/bayonets-pitchforks-golden-age-slasher-prowler-turns-40/|title=Bayonets and Pitchforks: Golden Age Slasher 'The Prowler' Turns 40|date=November 8, 2021|last=Paul|first=Zachary}}</ref> The film has also been compared by a number of critics to another slasher film of the same year with a similar plot, ''[[My Bloody Valentine (film)|My Bloody Valentine]]''.{{sfn|Albright|2012|p=239}}

== Plot ==
On March 12, 1944 in [[Avalon, California|Avalon Bay]], during [[World War II]], a woman named Rosemary Chatham [[Dear John letter|writes a letter to her boyfriend, breaking up with him]]. On June 28, 1945, Rosemary is attending a graduation dance with her new boyfriend Roy. The couple leave the dance to a lakeside gazebo. There, they are attacked by a mysterious prowler wearing an army combat uniform, who impales them both with a [[pitchfork]] and leaves behind a red rose.

On June 28, 1980, college senior Pam MacDonald is making last-minute arrangements for that night's graduation ball, the first to be held since the 1945 murders. Helping Pam are her friends Lisa, Sherry and Sherry's love interest Carl. Later while visiting her boyfriend and the town's deputy, Mark London, Pam overhears a report of a prowler. She expresses her concern about Mark's safety because Sheriff George Fraser is leaving town for a fishing trip. Mark reassures her and promises to meet her at the dance. Pam then heads back to the dorm to get ready and eventually leaves with her friends. Sherry stays behind to shower and receives a surprise visit from Carl. While undressing, he is attacked and killed with a [[bayonet]]. The killer then impales Sherry with a pitchfork in the shower.

At the dance, Pam spots Mark and motions him over. As he walks towards her, Lisa asks him to dance. Mark agrees, which upsets Pam. Mark then walks over to Pam and the two share a brief but tense exchange. A moment later, Lisa walks up and bumps Mark, which causes him to spill his drink on her dress. She returns to the dorm to change and is chased by the same prowler, but escapes. She runs outside into elderly wheelchair user Major Chatham who grabs her arm. Pam escapes his grasp and soon reunites with Mark. She tells him about the prowler, so he investigates. After checking the dorm, Mark only finds boot prints and wheelchair tracks outside. The two then go investigate the Major's home. Pam realizes that his daughter was Rosemary and that her killer was never caught. Convinced the prowler from earlier is the same killer, Mark and Pam head back to the dance and warn the chaperone, Allison, about the possible danger.

Meanwhile, Lisa goes out to a nearby swimming pool to cool off and encounters the killer, who slits her throat with the bayonet. Paul, Lisa's boyfriend, is arrested by Mark for disturbing the peace. Allison goes to find Lisa but is stabbed and killed also. Mark and Pam go to investigate the cemetery and discover an open grave with Lisa's body in it. Mark tries to call the lodge that Sheriff Fraser went to, but is ignored by the desk clerk. Mark next calls the state police for help. He informs Pam that the state police told him that the reported prowler had been caught three hours earlier and could not have killed Lisa.

Pam suspects that Lisa's killer is the same killer who murdered Rosemary and Roy in 1945. They go to investigate Major Chatham's house again. Mark is attacked as the prowler chases Pam through the house. Otto, a slow-witted employee of the local convenience store and an armed member of a small posse patrolling the neighborhood for the prowler, appears and shoots the prowler with a rifle, but he quickly recovers and shoots Otto dead with a sawed-off [[shotgun]] before again attacking Pam by reloading the shotgun. While pinned to the ground, Pam manages to unmask the prowler, revealed to be Sheriff Fraser. Pam wrests the shotgun from his hands, then eventually puts it under his chin and pulls the trigger, killing Fraser.

The next day, Mark returns Pam to her dorm and she goes up alone. Discovering Sherry and Carl's bodies in the shower, she screams as Carl briefly seems to come to life and grab her arm before returning to his dead state.

==Themes==
Critic [[Stephen Deusner]] of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' has interpreted ''The Prowler'' as a "a sly, strange statement about the stakes of war".<ref name=wp/> Deusner cites the film as transgressive in the genre due to its portrayal of a war veteran as its villain: "Not every movie could get away with casting a spurned veteran as its villain, especially not a [[World War II|WWII]] vet. Even in 1981, that generation was lionized for the austere morality of that conflict, set in sharp relief by the more controversial [[Vietnam War]]".<ref name=wp/> Scholar James Kendrick notes ''The Prowler'' as thematically linked to such [[slasher film]]s as ''[[The Burning (1981 film)|The Burning]]'' (1981), in which [[psychological trauma]] plays an integral role in the acts of murder committed, and where a present event provides the traumatized, maddened villain an "opportunity to take revenge on the guilty parties or their symbolic substitutes".{{sfn|Kendrick|2017|p=319}}

==Production==
===Development===
''The Prowler'' was co-written by [[Glenn Leopold]] and Neal Barbera, son of [[Joseph Barbera]].{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=131}} Director [[Joseph Zito]] read the screenplay and was drawn to its "misty quality": "It had this strange, dreamlike mood in it. It wasn't trying to be real, it was trying to be [[Surrealist cinema|surreal]] in a way".{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=131}}

===Casting===
[[Farley Granger]] was cast in the film through his connection to the wife of an executive producer, with whom he was enrolled in an acting course.{{sfn|Zito|Savini|2002|loc=11:04}}

===Filming===
[[File:Emlen-physick-estate.jpg|thumb|right|upright=1|The [[Emlen Physick Estate]] served as a filming location]]
Initially, Zito had wanted to shoot ''The Prowler'' in [[Avalon, California]], where it is set,{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=131}} but later decided to shoot the film in [[Cape May, New Jersey]] instead, which he felt had a "ghost town quality".{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=131}} The film was shot over a period of six weeks, which each consisted of six days' work,{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=132}} beginning in October 1980.<ref name=focus>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/36845134/courierpost/|work=[[Courier-Post]]|date=November 4, 1980|page=12|last=Curran|first=Karen|title=Cameras focus in Cape May|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Contemporaneous newspaper reports cite a budget of between $400,000 and $500,000,<ref name=focus/><ref name=McCarthy/> though Zito has stated that the film ultimately cost $1 million to produce.{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=132}}{{sfn|Zito|Savini|2002|loc=2:58}} During production, the film had the [[working title]] ''Graduation''.<ref name=cmch>{{cite news|work=[[Cape May County Herald]]|date=March 17, 1982|title=Cape Eyed By Movie Makers|page=17|url=http://vdls.cmclibrary.org/?a=d&d=CMCH19820317.1.17&e=-------en-20--1--txt-txIN-------}}</ref><ref name=McCarthy>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/press-of-atlantic-city/186229155/|work=[[Press of Atlantic City]]|last=McCarthy|first=T.J.|title=Everybody Is A Star – Including Me|date=October 10, 1980|p=18|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

The Inn at Cape May served as the building that appears in the dance sequences,<ref>{{cite web|work=Press of Atlantic City|url=https://www.pressofatlanticcity.com/life/these-horror-films-have-ties-to-south-jersey/article_d08a8182-0bc5-56e9-91e3-19e3145b99db.html|title=These horror films have ties to South Jersey|last=Reil|first=Maxwell|date=21 October 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180609184514/http://www.pressofatlanticcity.com/life/these-horror-films-have-ties-to-south-jersey/article_d08a8182-0bc5-56e9-91e3-19e3145b99db.html|archive-date=June 9, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> while the [[Emlen Physick Estate]] served as the Chatham house, where the film's last act takes place.<ref>{{cite web|work=Icona Resorts|url=https://icona.com/blog/jersey-shore-hotels-history/|title=A South Jersey History Tour|date=January 7, 2019|archive-url=https://archive.today/20191007093112/https://icona.com/blog/jersey-shore-hotels-history/|archive-date=October 7, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> The dormitory sequences were filmed inside the historic [[The Chalfonte Hotel|Chalfonte Hotel]].<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]]|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-philadelphia-inquirer/125385408/|date=November 3, 1980|page=2-C|title=Quiet Cape May gets starring role in horror movie|last=Lucas|first=Charlotte-Anne|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> The Franklin Street Civic Center served as a school gynmnasium featured in the film.<ref name=McCarthy/>

===Special effects===
[[Tom Savini]] designed the special effects for the film's elaborate murder sequences.{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=143}} Because the film's death sequences were so special effects-intensive, the shooting schedule was crafted to prioritize the filming of them specifically, with whole days dedicated to one death sequence.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3524516/butcher-block-brutal-slasher-prowler-showcase-tom-savinis-best-work/|title=Brutal Slasher 'The Prowler' is a Showcase of Tom Savini's Best Work|last=Navarro|first=Meagan|date=October 10, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190616125246/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3524516/butcher-block-brutal-slasher-prowler-showcase-tom-savinis-best-work/|archive-date=June 16, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Music==
[[Richard Einhorn]] composed the film's original score.<ref name=Edwards>{{cite web|title=Cinema Fearité presents The Prowler (Joseph Zito 1981)|work=Film Fracture|last=Edwards|first=James Jay|date=October 13, 2011|url=https://www.filmfracture.com/cinema-fearite-presents-the-prowler-joseph-zito-1981/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250512090850/https://www.filmfracture.com/cinema-fearite-presents-the-prowler-joseph-zito-1981/|archive-date=May 12, 2025}}</ref> James Jay Edwards, writing for the film website ''Film Fracture'' in 2011, praised Einhorn's score, describing it as "a horror masterpiece. Combining modern (for the time) electronic music with classical sensibilities, the score is always noticeable but never distracting, driving the action instead of being pulled along by it. While not as iconic as some of its contemporaries (like [[John Carpenter]]’s eerie piano piece from ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' or [[Harry Manfredini]]’s ki-ki-ki ha-ha-ha from ''[[Friday the 13th (1980 film)|Friday the 13th]]''), Einhorn’s compositions are more musical and moody, creating tension where the visuals lack it."<ref name=Edwards/> In a 2019 retrospective review for ''[[Exclaim!]]'', Brock Thiessen wrote of the score: "At times, Einhorn weaves in some beautifully romantic pieces, especially during the album's first suite, but then at others, he veers off into much more abstract territory, mixing orchestral cues and free-forming electronic soundscapes. In a way, it's like what you'd get from a purposely experimental [[Ennio Morricone|Morricone]] score."<ref name=Thiessen/>

In January 2019, [[Waxwork Records]] released the film's score on [[LP record|vinyl]] as a double LP.<ref name=Thiessen>{{cite web|url=https://exclaim.ca/music/article/richard_einhorn-the_prowler|work=[[Exclaim!]]|last=Thiessen|first=Brock|title=The Prowler|date=January 27, 2019|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220928224512/https://exclaim.ca/music/article/richard_einhorn-the_prowler|archive-date=September 28, 2022}}</ref> It was later issued on CD through Howlin' Wolf Records.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/original-score-cult-slasher-prowler-173346842.html|via=[[Yahoo]]|work=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]]|title=Original score to cult slasher The Prowler has a date with vinyl|last=Roffman|first=Michael|date=January 9, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191007083947/https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/original-score-cult-slasher-prowler-173346842.html|archive-date=October 7, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Release ==
===Censorship===
''The Prowler'' was released under the alternate title ''Rosemary's Killer''{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=4}} in [[Australia]] and [[Europe]] in a cut that excises much of Tom Savini's gore effects.{{sfn|Milne|Willemin|Hardy|1986|p=370}}<ref>{{cite web|work=Movie-Censorship.com|title=Prowler, The (aka Rosemary's Killer)|url=https://www.movie-censorship.com/report.php?ID=873236|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230528094201/https://www.movie-censorship.com/report.php?ID=873236|archive-date=May 28, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> The German version omits all of the gore scenes (including the revelation of the killer's identity) and replaced the soundtrack with bird sounds for daytime scenes, cricket sounds for the night scenes, and [[Richard Einhorn]]'s score with synthesizer music by an uncredited musician. This version goes by the title ''Die Forke des Todes'' (''The Pitchfork of Death'').<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.joblo.com/horror-movies/reviews/the-prowler|work=[[JoBlo.com]]|title=''The Prowler''|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161024021611/https://www.joblo.com/horror-movies/reviews/the-prowler|archive-date=October 24, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Encyclopedia of Horror'' (1986) reports that "Savini's particularly graphic special effects resulted in most of the murders being trimmed in the British release print".{{sfn|Milne|Willemin|Hardy|1986|p=370}}

=== Home media ===
VCII released the film on [[VHS]] in 1981.{{sfn|Boyreau|2009|pages=92–93}} [[Blue Underground]] released the fully uncut version of ''The Prowler'' on [[DVD]] in 2002<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/4374/prowler-the/|work=[[DVD Talk]]|title=''The Prowler''|date=August 13, 2002|last=Long|first=Mike|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171225071115/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/4374/prowler-the/|archive-date=December 25, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> and on [[Blu-ray]] in 2010.<ref name=hd/> The extras include a trailer, a still and poster gallery, behind the scenes gore footage with Tom Savini, and an audio commentary with Joseph Zito and Savini.<ref name=slant/>

==Reception==
===Box office===
Initially, [[Embassy Pictures|Avco Embassy Pictures]], who had previously released the slasher ''[[Prom Night (1980 film)|Prom Night]]'' (1980), expressed interest in distributing ''The Prowler''.{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=133}} The film was ultimately distributed independently in the United States by Sandhurst Distributing Corporation.{{sfn|Rockoff|2011|p=133}} It opened regionally in [[Louisville, Kentucky]] and [[Dayton, Ohio]], on June 26, 1981,<ref name=swemm>{{cite news|work=[[Courier Journal]]|date=June 27, 1981|title='The Dragon': scary; The scenery: good; The mystery: missing|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-courier-journal/121334179/|last=Swem|first=Gregg|page=7|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-cincinnati-enquirer/125385490/|work=[[The Cincinnati Enquirer]]|date=June 26, 1981|title=Walk-In Theatres|page=E-10|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> followed by a release in [[Kansas City, Missouri]], on September 4, 1981.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-kansas-city-star/125384334/|work=[[The Kansas City Star]]|title=''The Prowler''|date=September 4, 1981|page=20|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> It premiered in [[Los Angeles]] on October 9, 1981.<ref name=afi/> Overall, it ranked 135th at the U.S. box office for the year of 1981, earning less than $1 million during its theatrical run.{{sfn|Nowell|2010|p=234}}

=== Critical response ===
Linda Gross of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' panned the film for its violent content, adding that "director Joseph Zito prowls around aimlessly without creating an authentic sense of locale. Instead, he delivers a spaced-out nightmare in which the characters act like zombies".<ref>{{cite news|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|last=Gross|first=Linda|date=October 14, 1981|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/28735093/the_los_angeles_times/|page=95|via=Newspapers.com|title='The Prowler': Murder Once Again}}</ref> [[Stephen Deusner]] of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' described the film as "bloody, terrifying, [and] often sadistic... ''The Prowler'' is a [[war film|war movie]] re-imagined as a slasher flick. And its message is clear: Take your military heroes for granted and they will come back and kill you".<ref name=wp/> Deusner also noted that the film is "gorgeously shot", but conceded that it "is never entirely satisfying. There are too many missed opportunities to transcend the genre’s schlock, too many passages where nothing happens, too many scares that fall flat".<ref name=wp>{{cite news|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|title=The Goriest Generation: 'The Prowler', on DVD|last=Deusner|first=Stephen M.|date=July 26, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160313041038/https://www.washingtonpost.com/express/wp/2010/07/26/the-prowler-dvd-review/|archive-date=March 13, 2016|url-status=live|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/express/wp/2010/07/26/the-prowler-dvd-review/|author-link=Stephen Deusner}}</ref>

In a review for ''[[Slant Magazine]]'', Chris Cabin compared the film to both [[John Carpenter]]'s ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' (1978) and [[William Lustig]]'s ''[[Maniac (1980 film)|Maniac]]'' (1980), writing: "These films remain popular due mostly to the films that are made proficiently as far as gore theatrics, tone, and mood are concerned, and in these terms ''The Prowler'' is certainly deserving of its modest fan base. What the film lacks in narrative drive, coherence, and performance, it makes up with thoughtful lighting, strong cinematography from Raoul Lomas and an uncredited João Fernandes, and, of course, Savini’s lovingly overblown and impossible splatter effects".<ref name=slant>{{Cite web|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/the-prowler/1786/|title=Blu-ray Review: The Prowler|last=Cabin|first=Chris|date=July 30, 2010|website=[[Slant Magazine]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190215050511/https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/the-prowler/1786/|archive-date=February 15, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Film scholar [[John Kenneth Muir]] similarly praised the film, writing that it transcends many of its peers because of its "blunt-faced approach to violence" and Zito's "directorial virtuosity".{{sfn|Muir|2011|pages=201–202}}

''The Encyclopedia of Horror'' notes that like ''[[My Bloody Valentine (film)|My Bloody Valentine]]'' the film moves away from the genre's usual [[Midwestern United States|Midwestern]] setting, but that it does little with the new location, nor with its potentially interesting returning G.I. motif. Like ''Valentine'', the film is judged as being certainly polished, atmospheric, and suspenseful, though hardly original.{{sfn|Milne|Willemin|Hardy|1986|p=370}} [[Leonard Maltin]] gave the film a negative 1 <small><sup>1/2</sup></small> out of a possible 4 stars, criticizing the film's plot calling it "illogical".{{sfn|Maltin|2013|p=1129}} The ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' film guide noted the film as "cravenly conformist in every department".<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|location=London|title=''The Prowler'' (1981)|url=https://www.timeout.com/london/film/the-prowler-1981|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191007072441/https://www.timeout.com/london/film/the-prowler-1981|archive-date=October 7, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[AllMovie]] called it a "run-of-the-mill entry in the early '80s slasher film cycle" that "benefits from an unexpected amount of technical gloss, but has little else to offer".<ref>{{cite web|url = https://allmovie.com/movie/the-prowler-v39549/review|title = ''The Prowler'' (1981)|last = Guarisco|first = Donald|publisher = |date = |website = [[AllMovie]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141117000351/https://allmovie.com/movie/the-prowler-v39549/review|archive-date=November 17, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[IGN]]'', in their published review of the film in 2004, noted: "While not a great film by any stretch of the imagination, ''The Prowler'' just enough things right to earn a recommendation...&nbsp;If you're a fan of early slasher cinema, this one's worth a look—primarily for its stalk sequences (which are relatively well-executed, particularly the one in a swimming pool) and for the FX work of Tom Savini."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[IGN]]|title=The Horror Greek Speaks: The Prowler|date=March 15, 2004|author=''IGN'' Staff|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/03/16/the-horror-geek-speaks-the-prowler|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251205015948/https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/03/16/the-horror-geek-speaks-the-prowler|archive-date=December 5, 2025}}</ref>

In a 2016 retrospective, Dave Wilson of [[Dread Central]] noted the film's violent special effects and "sombre atmosphere" as strengths, though conceded that the film's characterization was bland.<ref name=dread>{{cite web|work=[[Dread Central]]|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/202200/retrospective-prowler-1981-35-years-later/|title=Retrospective: ''The Prowler'' (1981) – 35 Years Later|date=December 8, 2016|last=Wilson|first=Dave J.|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161209151601/https://dreadcentral.com/news/202200/retrospective-prowler-1981-35-years-later/|archive-date=December 9, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|75||8|consensus=|access-date=June 28, 2025|ref=y}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 45 out of 100, based on 5 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Prowler'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-prowler |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=June 28, 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

==Legacy==
In the years since its release, ''The Prowler'' has developed a [[cult following]]{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=143}}<ref name=hd>{{cite web|url=https://bluray.highdefdigest.com/3047/prowler.html|work=High-Def Digest|title=The Prowler Blu-ray Review|date=July 26, 2010|last=Duarte|first=M. Enois|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230528175613/https://bluray.highdefdigest.com/3047/prowler.html|archive-date=May 28, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://consequence.net/2019/01/the-prowler-vinyl-waxwork/|work=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence of Sound]]|title=Original score to cult slasher The Prowler has a date with vinyl|date=January 9, 2019|last=Roffman|first=Michael|url-status=live|archive-date=May 28, 2023|archive-url=https://archive.today/20230528175011/https://consequence.net/2019/01/the-prowler-vinyl-waxwork/}}</ref> and been cited as a classic of the slasher sub-genre from its "golden age" in the early 1980s.<ref name=Paul/> Describing the film for a revival screening, the [[Coolidge Corner Theatre]] dubbed the film "one of the cruelest 'body-count' movies of the 1980s."<ref>{{cite web|publisher=[[Coolidge Corner Theatre]]|title=The Prowler (1981)|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251205020511/https://coolidge.org/films/prowler-1981|archive-date=December 5, 2025|url-status=live|url=https://coolidge.org/films/prowler-1981}}</ref>

In 2017, ''[[Complex (magazine)|Complex]]'' magazine named it the 24th-best slasher film of all time.<ref name=complex/> The following year, ''[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]'' included it in their list of "The 50 Best Slasher Movies of All Time",<ref name=paste/> while the film's killer was ranked the 11th-greatest slasher villain of all time by ''[[LA Weekly]]''.<ref name=laweekly>{{cite web|work=[[LA Weekly]]|title=A Killer List: The Greatest Movie Slashers of All Time|date=October 22, 2018|last=Byrnes|first=Chad|url=https://www.laweekly.com/a-killer-list-the-greatest-movie-slashers-of-all-time/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190703130852/https://www.laweekly.com/a-killer-list-the-greatest-movie-slashers-of-all-time/|archive-date=July 3, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>
