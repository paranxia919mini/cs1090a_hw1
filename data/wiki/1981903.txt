'''''Rififi''''' ({{langx|fr|Du rififi chez les hommes}}{{efn|1=The title ''Du Rififi chez les hommes'' {{IPA|fr|dy ʁi.fi.fi ʃe lez‿ɔm|}} does not directly translate into English. A rough translation would be "Some rumble amongst men". ''Rififi'' is a slang word deriving from ''[[wikt:fr:rif|rif]]'', the French military term for "combat zone" during [[World War I]], ultimately from the [[Middle French]] ''rufe'', "fire" and [[Latin]] ''rūfus'', "red".<ref name="la times2" /><ref>''Le Nouveau Petit Robert, dictionnaire alphabétique et analogique de la langue française'' {{ISBN|2-85036-668-4}} and ''Le Petit Larousse illustré'' {{ISBN|2-03-530206-4}}.</ref>}}) is a 1955 French [[crime film]] adaptation of [[Auguste Le Breton]]'s novel of the same name. Directed by American [[Hollywood blacklist|blacklisted]] filmmaker [[Jules Dassin]], the film stars [[Jean Servais]] as the aging gangster Tony "le Stéphanois", [[Carl Möhner]] as Jo "le Suédois", [[Robert Manuel (actor)|Robert Manuel]] as Mario Farrati, and Jules Dassin as César "le Milanais". The foursome band together to commit an almost impossible theft, the burglary of an exclusive jewelry shop in the [[Rue de la Paix]]. The centerpiece of the film is an intricate half-hour heist scene depicting the crime in detail, shot in near silence, without dialogue or music. The fictional burglary has been mimicked by criminals in actual crimes around the world.<ref name=newspaperarchive/><ref name="Powrie71"/>

After he was blacklisted from [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]], Dassin found work in France, where he was asked to direct ''Rififi''. Despite his distaste for parts of the original novel, Dassin agreed to direct the film. He shot ''Rififi'' while working with a low budget, without a star cast, and with the production staff working for low wages.<ref name="ebert"/>

Upon the initial release of the film, it received positive reactions from audiences and critics in France, the United States, and the United Kingdom. The film earned Dassin the award for [[Best Director Award (Cannes Film Festival)|Best Director]] at the [[1955 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web |title=Festival de Cannes: Rififi |url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/3723/year/1955.html |publisher=festival-cannes.com |access-date=31 January 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110822151047/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/3723/year/1955.html|archive-date=22 August 2011}}</ref> ''Rififi'' was nominated by the [[National Board of Review]] for Best Foreign Film. ''Rififi'' was re-released theatrically in both 2000 and 2015<ref>{{cite web |author=Rialto Pictures |title=RIFIFI – Trailer |url=http://vimeo.com/135593615 |publisher=Vimeo, LLC |date=6 August 2015 |access-date=16 August 2015}}</ref> and is still highly acclaimed by modern film critics as one of the greatest works in French [[film noir]].<ref name="Truffaut209"/>

==Plot==
Tony "le [[Saint-Étienne#Population|Stéphanois]]", a criminal who has served a five-year prison term for a jewel heist, is out on the street and down on his luck. His friend Jo approaches him about a smash-and-grab proposed by mutual friend Mario in which the trio would cut the glass on a Parisian jeweler's front window in broad daylight and snatch some gems. Tony declines. He then learns that his old girlfriend, Mado, took up in his absence with gangster Parisian nightclub owner Pierre Grutter. Finding Mado working at Grutter's, Tony invites her back to his rundown flat. She is obviously well-kept, and Tony savagely beats her for being so deeply involved with Grutter. Tony changes his mind about the heist; he now accepts on the condition that rather than merely robbing the window, they will take on the more difficult but more lucrative task of robbing the store's safe. Mario suggests they employ the services of Italian compatriot César, a [[safecracker]]. The four devise and rehearse an ingenious plan to break into the store and disable its sophisticated alarm system.

The caper begins with the group chiseling through a cement ceiling from an upstairs flat on a Sunday night. The suspenseful break-in is completed, and the criminals appear to escape without leaving any trace of their identities. However, without the others' knowledge, César pocketed a diamond ring as a bauble for his lover Viviane, a chanteuse at Grutter's club. The heist makes headline news, and the four men arrange to [[Fence (criminal)|fence]] the loot with a London contact. Meanwhile, Grutter has seen Mado and her injuries, and she breaks off their relationship. Infuriated at Tony's interference in his life, Grutter gives heroin to his drug-addicted brother Rémy and tells him to murder Tony. But then, the other Grutter brother, Louis, shows them the diamond César gave to Viviane and they realize that César, Mario, and Tony were responsible for the jewel theft. Grutter forces César to confess. Forsaking a [[French franc|FF]]10&nbsp;million police reward, Grutter decides to steal the jewels from Tony's gang, with Rémy brutally murdering Mario and his wife Ida when they refuse to reveal where the loot is hidden. Tony retrieves it from the couple's apartment and anonymously pays for a splendid funeral for them. He then goes looking for Grutter and stumbles onto the captive César, who confesses to having squealed. Citing "the rules", Tony ruefully kills him.

Meanwhile, seeking to force their adversaries' hand, Grutter's thugs kidnap Jo's five-year-old son Tonio and hold him for [[ransom]]. The London fence arrives with the payoff, after which Tony leaves to single-handedly rescue the child by force, advising Jo it is the only way they will see him alive.  With Mado's help, he tracks Tonio down at Grutter's country house and kills Rémy and Louis while rescuing him. On the way back to Paris, Tony learns Jo has cracked under the pressure and agreed to meet Grutter at his house with the money. When Jo arrives Grutter tells him Tony has already snatched the child and kills him. Seconds too late to save his friend, Tony is mortally wounded by Grutter but kills him as Grutter tries to flee with the loot. Bleeding profusely, Tony drives maniacally back to Paris and delivers Tonio home safely before dying at the wheel as police and bystanders close in on him and a suitcase filled with FF120&nbsp;million in cash. Jo's wife, Louise, takes her child from the car and leaves the suitcase and the body to the police.

==Cast==

* [[Carl Möhner]] as Jo "le Suédois": A young [[Swedish people|Swedish]] gangster for whom Tony took the five-year rap. Jo invites Tony in on the heist. "Le Suédois" translates to "the Swede" in French.

* [[Jules Dassin]] as César "le Milanais": An expert safecracker hired by Tony with a weakness for women. Dassin played the role under the pseudonym of Perlo Vita.<ref name=ebert/>
* [[Magali Noël]] as Viviane: a night-club singer who gets involved with César "le Milanais"; she sings the film's title song.
* [[Claude Sylvain]] as Ida: Mario Ferrati's wife
* Marcel Lupovici as Pierre Grutter: Leader of the Grutter gang and owner of the night-club L'Âge d'Or.
* [[Robert Hossein]] as Rémy Grutter: A member of the Grutter gang, addicted to heroin.
* Pierre Grasset as Louis Grutter: A member of the Grutter gang.
* [[Marie Sabouret]] as Mado: The former lover of Tony "le Stéphanois" and the current lover of Pierre Grutter.
* [[Dominique Collignon-Maurin|Dominique Maurin]] as Tonio, the young son of Jo "le Suédois".
* [[Janine Darcey]] as Louise, Jo's wife and the mother of Tonio.

==Production==

===Development===
The film ''Rififi'' was originally to be directed by [[Jean-Pierre Melville]], a later luminary of the [[heist film]] genre. Melville gave his blessing to American director [[Jules Dassin]] when the latter asked for his permission to take the helm.<ref name="production notes" /> It was Dassin's first film in five years.<ref name="Powrie76">Powrie 2006, p. 76.</ref> He had been [[Hollywood blacklist|blacklisted in Hollywood]] after fellow director [[Edward Dmytryk]] named him as a [[Communist]] to the [[House Committee on Un-American Activities]] in April 1951.<ref name="production notes">{{cite AV media notes |type=Supplemental slideshow on DVD |title=Rififi |orig-year=1955 |publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]] |location=New York City |year=2001 |id={{ISBN|0-7800-2396-X}}}}</ref><ref name="Powrie76" /> Subsequently, Dassin attempted to rebuild his career in Europe. Several such film projects were stopped through long-distance efforts by the US government.<ref name="wga">{{cite web |last=Berg |first=Sandra |url=http://www.wga.org/writtenby/writtenbysub.aspx?id=2247 |title=When Noir Turned Black |access-date=24 December 2008 |work=Written by |date=November 2006 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927015754/http://www.wga.org/writtenby/writtenbysub.aspx?id=2247 |archive-date=27 September 2007}}</ref> Dassin attempted a film ''L'Ennemi public numero un'', which was halted after stars [[Fernandel]] and [[Zsa Zsa Gabor]] withdrew under American pressure.<ref name="Powrie76" /> An attempt to film an adaptation of [[Giovanni Verga]]'s ''[[Mastro-don Gesualdo]]'' in [[Rome]] was halted by the US Embassy.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin received an offer from an agent in Paris, where he met producer [[Henri Bérard]] who had acquired the rights to [[Auguste Le Breton]]'s popular [[crime novel]] ''Du Rififi chez les hommes''.<ref name="production notes"/> Bérard chose Dassin due to the major success in France of Dassin's previous film ''[[The Naked City]]''.<ref name="production notes"/>

Using his native [[English language|English]], Dassin wrote the screenplay to ''Rififi'' in six days with the help of screenwriter [[René Wheeler]], who subsequently took the material and translated it into French.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin hated the novel; he was repelled by the story's [[racist]] theme in which the rival gangsters were dark [[Arab]]s and North Africans pitted against light-skinned Europeans. As well, the book portrayed disquieting events such as [[necrophilia]]—scenes that Dassin did not know how to bring to the big screen.<ref name="production notes"/><ref name="makingRififi" /><ref name="nytimes"/> Bérard suggested making the rival gang Americans; he had assumed that Dassin would approve of this idea, but Dassin was against it – for he did not wish to be accused of taking oblique revenge on screen. Dassin downplayed the rival gangsters' ethnicity in his screenplay, simply choosing the Germanic "Grutter" as a surname.<ref name="production notes"/> The greatest change from the book was the heist scene, which spanned but ten pages of the 250-page novel. Dassin focused his screenplay on it to get past other events he did not know what to do with.<ref name="makingRififi"/> As produced, the scene takes a quarter of the film's running time and is shot with only natural sound, with no spoken words or music.<ref name="production notes"/>

===Filming===
Working with a budget of $200,000, Dassin could not afford top stars for the film.<ref name="ebert"/><ref name="nytimes"/> To carry the lead role, Dassin selected [[Jean Servais]], an actor whose career had slumped due to alcoholism.<ref name="production notes"/> For Italian gangster Mario Ferrati, Dassin cast [[Robert Manuel (actor)|Robert Manuel]] after seeing him perform a comic role as a member of [[Comédie-Française]].<ref name="production notes"/> After a suggestion made by the wife of the film's producer, Dassin cast [[Carl Möhner]] as Jo the Swede.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin would use Möhner again in his next film ''[[He Who Must Die]]''.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin himself played the role of the Italian safecracker César the Milanese.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin explained in an interview that he "had cast a very good actor in Italy, whose name escapes me, but he never got the contract!...So I had to put on the mustache and do the part myself".<ref name="production notes"/>

[[File:RififiDassin.jpg|right|thumb|alt=A screen capture from the film, showing a distraught César tied up to a pole staring at Tony le Stéphanois, who has his back to the camera.|Tony telling the tied-up César "I liked you, Macaroni. But you know the rules" before shooting him for betrayal. This scene was included to echo how Dassin felt after being betrayed by his contemporaries in America.<ref name="Powrie77"/>]]

''Rififi'' was filmed during winter in Paris and used real locations rather than studio sets.<ref name="ebert"/><ref name="Powrie77">Powrie 2006, p. 77.</ref> Due to the low budget, the locations were scouted by Dassin himself.<ref name="ebert"/> Dassin's fee for writing, directing, and acting was US$8,000.<ref name="nytimes"/> Dassin's [[production designer]], to whom he referred as "one of the greatest men in the history of cinema", was [[Alexandre Trauner]]. Out of friendship for Dassin, Trauner did the film for very little money.<ref name="production notes"/> Dassin argued with his producer Henri Bérard on two points: Dassin refused to shoot scenes when there was sunlight, claiming that he "just wanted grey";<ref name="makingRififi">{{cite AV media notes |title=Rififi |orig-year=1955 |type=Jules Dassin Interview |publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]] |location=New York City |year=2001 |id={{ISBN|0-7800-2396-X}}}}</ref> and there were to be no fistfights in the film. Such fight scenes had been important to the popular success in France of the [[Lemmy Caution]] film series.<ref name="makingRififi" />

''Rififi''{{'}}s heist scene was based on an actual burglary that took place in 1899 along [[Marseille]]'s cours St-Louis. A gang broke into the first-floor offices of a travel agency, cutting a hole in the floor and, using an umbrella to catch the debris, made off with the contents of the jeweler's shop below.<ref name="Powrie73">Powrie 2006, p. 73.</ref> The scene where Tony regretfully chooses to kill César for his betrayal of the thieves' [[code of silence]] was filmed as an allusion to how Dassin and others felt after finding their contemporaries willing to name names in front of the House Un-American Activities Committee.<ref name="Powrie77">Powrie 2006, p. 77.</ref> This act was not in the original novel.<ref name="makingRififi"/>

===Music and title===
[[File:Georges Auric 1940.jpg|thumbnail|left|Composer Georges Auric (pictured) and director Jules Dassin initially disagreed on whether to have music during the heist scene.]]
[[Georges Auric]] was hired as the composer for the film. Dassin and Auric originally could not agree about scoring the half-hour caper scene. After Dassin told Auric he did not want music, Auric claimed he would "protect [him]. I'm going to write the music for the scene anyways, because you need to be protected". After filming was finished, Dassin showed the film to Auric once with music and once without. Afterward, Auric agreed the scene should be unscored.<ref name="production notes"/>

==Release==
''Rififi'' debuted in France on 13 April 1955.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.allmovie.com/movie/rififi-v41398|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120726011702/http://www.allmovie.com/movie/rififi-v41398|archive-date=26 July 2012|access-date=22 August 2017|last=Bozzola|first=Lucia|publisher=AllMovie|title=Rififi}}</ref> The film was banned in some countries due to its heist scene, referred to by the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' reviewer as a "master class in breaking and entering as well as filmmaking".<ref name="la times">{{cite news |url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2008-apr-01-me-dassin1-story.html
|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]
|date=1 April 2008 |title=Blacklisted Director Jules Dassin Dies at 96 |first=Claudia |last=Luther}}</ref>
The Mexican interior ministry [[Banned films|banned]] the film because of a series of burglaries mimicking its heist scene.<ref name="newspaperarchive">{{Cite news |date=18 August 1956 |title=BAN FRENCH FILM MEXICO |url=http://www.newspaperarchive.com/LandingPage.aspx?type=nlp&search=rififi%2520ban&img=%5C%5Cna0001%5C583111%5C6562874.html |url-access=subscription |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080228193111/http://www.newspaperarchive.com/LandingPage.aspx?type=nlp&search=rififi%2520ban&img=%5C%5Cna0001%5C583111%5C6562874.html |archive-date=February 28, 2008 |work=The Lethbridge Herald |via=www.newspaperarchive.com}}</ref> ''Rififi'' was banned in [[Finland]] in 1955 and released in severely cut form in 1959 with an additional tax because of its content.<ref name="Tornudd">Törnudd 1986, p. 152.</ref><ref>{{Cite book|title=Taistelu elokuvasensuurista : valtiollisen elokuvatarkastuksen historia 1946-2006|last=Sedergren, Jari.|date=2006|publisher=Suomalaisen Kirjallisuuden Seura|isbn=9517468121|location=Helsinki|oclc=85017263}}</ref> In answer to critics who saw the film as an educational process that taught people how to commit burglary, Dassin claimed the film showed how difficult it was to actually carry out a crime.<ref name="wga"/>

Otherwise, his name would be removed from the film as the writer and director.<ref name="Levy343">Levy 2003, p. 343.</ref><ref name="Levy344">Levy 2003, p. 344.</ref> Dassin refused and the film was released by [[United Artists]] who set up a dummy corporation as the distributing company.<ref name="Levy344"/><ref name="Foerstel1998">Foerstel 1998, p. 165.</ref> The film was distributed successfully in America with Dassin listed in the credits; in this way he was the first to break the [[Hollywood blacklist]].<ref name="production notes"/><ref name="Foerstel1998" /> ''Rififi'' was released in the United States first with subtitles and then later with an [[English-language|English]] [[Dubbing (filmmaking)|dub]] under the title ''Rififi...Means Trouble!''.<ref name="production notes"/> The film was challenged on release by [[National Legion of Decency|The Roman Catholic Legion of Decency]]; as a result, the film suffered three brief cuts, and opened with a title card quoting the [[Book of Proverbs]] stating "When the wicked are multiplied, crime shall be multiplied: but the just shall see their downfall". After this change, the film passed with a B rating.<ref name="production notes"/> In 2005, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' announced that Stone Village Pictures had acquired the remake rights to ''Rififi'', the producers intending to place the film in a modern setting with [[Al Pacino]] taking the lead role.<ref name="variety">{{Cite web |url=https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/pacino-in-hest-mode-with-rififi-1117927741/ |title=Pacino in hest ''[sic]'' mode with 'Rififi' |access-date=8 January 2009 |last=Fleming |first=Michael |date=18 August 2005 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
Upon its original release, film critic and future director [[François Truffaut]] praised the film, stating that "Out of the worst crime novel I ever read, Jules Dassin has made the best crime film I've ever seen" and "Everything in ''Le Rififi'' is intelligent: screenplay, dialogue, sets, music, choice of actors. Jean Servais, Robert Manuel, and Jules Dassin are perfect."<ref name="Truffaut209">Truffaut 1994, p. 209.</ref> French critic [[André Bazin]] said that ''Rififi'' brought the genre a "sincerity and humanity that break with the conventions of a crime film, and manage to touch our hearts".<ref name="Powrie71">Powrie 2006, p. 71.</ref> In the February 1956 issue of the French film magazine ''[[Cahiers du cinéma]]'', the film was listed as number thirteen in the top twenty films of 1955.<ref name="Hillier285">Hillier 1985, p. 285.</ref> The film was well received by British critics who noted the film's violence on its initial release. ''[[The Daily Mirror]]'' referred to the film as "brilliant and brutal" while the ''[[Daily Herald (UK newspaper)|Daily Herald]]'' made note that ''Rififi'' would "make American attempts at screen brutality look like a tea party in cathedral city".<ref name="Powrie71" /> The American release of the film also received acclaim. Bosley Crowther of ''[[The New York Times]]'' referred to the film as "perhaps the keenest crime film that ever came from France, including "''[[Pépé le Moko]]''" and some of the best of [[Louis Jouvet]] and [[Jean Gabin]]."<ref name="nytimes1956">{{Cite web |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9806EFDF133EE63ABC4E53DFB066838D649EDE |title=Rififi (1955) |access-date=7 January 2009 |last=Crowther |first=Bosley |date=6 June 1956 |work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> The [[National Board of Review of Motion Pictures|National Board of Review]] nominated the film as the Best Foreign Film in 1956.<ref name="allmovieAwards">{{cite web |url=http://www.allmovie.com/work/rififi-41398/awards|title=Rififi: Awards |access-date=13 November 2010|publisher=[[AllMovie]]|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20101113043258/http://www.allmovie.com/work/rififi-41398/awards|archivedate=13 November 2010}}</ref>

''Rififi'' was re-released for a limited run within America on 21 July 2000 in a new 35&nbsp;mm print containing new, more explicit [[subtitle (captioning)|subtitle]]s that were enhanced in collaboration with Dassin.<ref name="metacritic" /><ref name="amgreview">{{cite web |url=http://www.allmovie.com/work/rififi-41398/review|title=Rififi: Review | access-date=3 June 2024|publisher=[[AllMovie]] | author=Bozzola, Lucia|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20101113043308/http://www.allmovie.com/work/rififi-41398/review|archivedate=13 November 2010}}</ref> The film was received very well by American critics on its re-release. On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 92% of 53 critics' reviews are positive. The website's consensus reads: "''Rififi'' depicts the perfect heist in more ways than one, telling its story so effectively that it essentially provided the template for an entire genre to follow."<ref name="rottentomatoes">{{cite web|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/du-rififi-chez-les-hommes-rififi/ |title=Rififi - Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=11 September 2012 |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=Flixster |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120903001342/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/du-rififi-chez-les-hommes-rififi/ |archive-date= 3 September 2012 }}</ref> At [[Metacritic]], which assigns a [[Standard score|normalized]] rating out of 100 to reviews from mainstream critics, the film has received an [[weighted mean|average]] score of 97, based on 13 reviews.<ref name="metacritic"/>
Kenneth Turan of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote the film was the "benchmark all succeeding heist films have been measured against... It's a film whose influence is hard to overstate, one that proves for not the last time that it's easier to break into a safe than fathom the mysteries of the human heart."<ref name="la times2"/> Lucia Bozzola of the online database [[Allmovie]] gave the film the highest possible rating of five stars, calling it "The pinnacle of heist movies" and "not only one of the best French noirs, but one of the top movies in the genre."<ref name="amgreview" /> In 2002, critic [[Roger Ebert]] added the film to his list of "Great Movies" stating "echoes of [''Rififi''] can be found from [[Stanley Kubrick|Kubrick]]'s ''[[The Killing (film)|The Killing]]'' to [[Quentin Tarantino|Tarantino]]'s ''[[Reservoir Dogs]]''. They both owe something to [[John Huston]]'s ''[[The Asphalt Jungle]]'' (1950), which has the general idea but not the attention to detail."<ref name="ebert"/>
''Rififi'' placed at number 90 on ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'''s list of The 100 Best Films Of World Cinema.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.empireonline.com/features/100-greatest-world-cinema-films/default.asp?film=90|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|title=The 100 Best Films Of World Cinema|access-date=22 July 2010|date=2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111202084443/http://www.empireonline.com/features/100-greatest-world-cinema-films/default.asp?film=90|archive-date=2 December 2011}}</ref>

Among negative reviews of the film, [[Dave Kehr]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' felt that "the film turns moralistic and sour in the last half, when the thieves fall out."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/rififi/Film?oid=1055475|title=Rififi Capsule|access-date=17 April 2010|author=Kehr, David|work=[[Chicago Reader]]|date=26 October 1985|archive-date=20 October 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201020040200/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/rififi/Film?oid=1055475|url-status=dead}}</ref>
The critic and director [[Jean-Luc Godard]] regarded the film negatively in comparison to other French crime films of the era, noting in 1986 that "today it can't hold a candle to ''[[Touchez pas au grisbi]]'' which paved the way for it, let alone ''[[Bob le flambeur]]'' which it paved the way for."<ref name="Godard">Godard 1986, p. 127.</ref>
