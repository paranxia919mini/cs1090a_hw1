{{nihongo|'''''Drive My Car'''''|ドライブ・マイ・カー|Doraibu Mai Kā|lead=yes}} is a 2021 Japanese [[Drama (film and television)|drama]] film<ref name="NYT"/> directed by [[Ryusuke Hamaguchi]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Takamasa Oe]]. Based on [[Haruki Murakami]]'s 2014 [[Men Without Women (Murakami short story collection)|short story of the same name]],<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Brzeski|first=Patrick|date=9 July 2021|title=Japan's Ryusuke Hamaguchi on Adapting Murakami for 'Drive My Car' and Vehicles as Confession Booths|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/cannes-2021-japan-ryusuke-hamaguchi-murakami-1234978601/|access-date=17 January 2022|website=The Hollywood Reporter|language=en-US}}</ref> it stars [[Hidetoshi Nishijima (actor)|Hidetoshi Nishijima]] as a theatre director who directs a multilingual production of ''[[Uncle Vanya]]'' while dealing with the death of his wife.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-60647486 |title=Drive My Car director Hamaguchi: Oscar buzz is beyond imagination |work=BBC News |date=17 March 2022 |access-date=18 March 2022}}</ref> [[Reika Kirishima]], [[Tōko Miura]], [[Park Yu-rim]], Jin Dae-yeon, Sonia Yuan, Ahn Hwitae, Perry Dizon, Satoko Abe, and [[Masaki Okada]] also star.

''Drive My Car'' had its world premiere at the [[2021 Cannes Film Festival]], where it competed for the [[Palme d'Or]] and won three awards, including [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Screenplay|Best Screenplay]]. The film received widespread critical acclaim with praise towards Hamaguchi's direction and screenplay; many declared it the best film of 2021.<ref>{{Cite web|date=19 December 2021|title=LA film critics pick 'Drive My Car' as year's best|url=https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/la-film-critics-pick-drive-my-car-as-years-best/|access-date=17 January 2022|website=The Seattle Times|language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=3 December 2021|title=New York film critics name 'Drive My Car' best film of 2021|url=https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/new-york-film-critics-name-drive-my-car-best-film-of-2021/|access-date=17 January 2022|website=The Seattle Times|language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=9 January 2022|title='Drive My Car' Is Named Best Film of 2021 by the National Society of Film Critics|url=https://collider.com/drive-my-car-best-film-2021-national-society-of-film-critics/|access-date=17 January 2022|website=Collider|language=en-US}}</ref> It was nominated for four awards at the [[94th Academy Awards]], winning [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]], and received [[List of accolades received by Drive My Car (film)|numerous other accolades]]. ''Drive My Car'' was the [[List of Academy Award winners and nominees from Japan#Best Picture|first Japanese film]] to receive a [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] nomination. It has since been called one of the [[best films]] of the 2020s and of the 21st century.<ref name= "21st Century">{{cite web | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-movies-21st-century/drive-my-car-2021/ | title=Hollywood Reporter Critics Pick the 50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far) | website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] | date=6 April 2023 }}</ref>

==Plot==
Stage actor and director Yūsuke Kafuku lives in [[Tokyo]] with his wife Oto, a screenwriter who conceives her stories after sex and narrates them to him. He learns his lines by listening to tapes recorded by Oto, while driving his red Aero performance variant of [[Saab 900|Saab 900 Turbo]]. After Yūsuke performs in ''[[Waiting for Godot]]'', Oto introduces him to young television star Kōji Takatsuki. When a theater festival Yūsuke was slated to judge in is delayed, he returns home, where he finds his wife having sex with Kōji, but does not disturb them. After a car crash, Yūsuke discovers he has [[glaucoma]] in one eye and must take eyedrops to prevent blindness. One day, Oto asks to have a serious conversation with Yūsuke. After he spends the day driving alone, Yūsuke returns home to find Oto dead from a brain hemorrhage. After the funeral, Yūsuke breaks down while performing the title role in ''[[Uncle Vanya]]''.

Two years later, Yūsuke accepts a residency in [[Hiroshima]] to direct a multilingual production of ''Uncle Vanya''. The theatre festival requires that he be chauffeured in his own car for insurance reasons, and despite his initial reluctance, he eventually bonds with his reserved young driver, Misaki Watari. With the help of [[dramaturge]] Gong Yoon-su, Yūsuke casts a diverse group of actors to perform in their native languages. He is impressed by Lee Yoo-na, a mute actress who communicates in [[Korean Sign Language]], and he also unexpectedly casts Kōji as Uncle Vanya.

After a rehearsal, Kōji invites Yūsuke for a drink, where the young actor pushes against harsh assessments of his character but admits to his unrequited love for Oto. He scolds someone for taking a photo of him. Yoon-su invites Yūsuke and Misaki to dinner with his wife, who is revealed to be Yoo-na. Driving home, Misaki tells Yūsuke about driving her abusive mother for long hours at a young age. They later visit a garbage facility, where Misaki explains that she drove garbage trucks after leaving her hometown when a landslide destroyed her home and killed her mother.

Drinking with Kōji again, Yūsuke reveals that he thinks he can no longer play Vanya himself, and suggests that Kōji's lack of self-control is a personal weakness but a strength as an actor. After Kōji slips away to confront a man taking photos of him, Misaki drives them home. Yūsuke reveals that he and Oto lost their young daughter, who would now be Misaki's age; Oto's gift for telling stories after sex was a bond that helped them both cope. Though he knew of his wife's affairs, Yūsuke believes she still loved him, and Kōji shares one of Oto's stories that Yūsuke never heard in its entirety.

The police interrupt a rehearsal and arrest Kōji, as the man he attacked has died from his injuries. Given two days to consider whether to take over as Vanya or else cancel the production, Yūsuke asks Misaki to take him to her childhood home in [[Hokkaido]]. Yūsuke shares his guilt for not coming home to face the discussion Oto wanted to have, which might have allowed him to save her life. Misaki reveals that she escaped the landslide but chose not to pull her mother from the wreckage, receiving a scar on her cheek she has chosen not to have treated. They visit the snowy remains of Misaki's childhood home and hug as they both confront their shared grief.

Yūsuke assumes the role of Vanya and gives an impassioned performance before a live audience, including Misaki. Yoo-na meaningfully delivers Sonya's final lines: "We shall hear the angels, we shall see the whole sky all in diamonds, we shall see how all earthly evil, all our sufferings, are drowned in the mercy that will fill the whole world. And our life will grow peaceful, tender, sweet as a caress… You've had no joy in your life; but wait, Uncle Vanya, wait… We shall rest." Actors and audience alike are moved by the performance. 

Some time later, Misaki is living in [[South Korea]]. Buying groceries, she returns to Yūsuke's red Saab, in which a dog rests. She takes off her [[surgical mask]], revealing that her scar is now barely visible, and drives away.

==Production==
The film is directed by [[Ryusuke Hamaguchi]]. The film was originally set in [[Busan]], South Korea, but was changed to [[Hiroshima]] due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.asahi-family.com/entertainment/29010|title= 今夏のカンヌで日本映画史上初の脚本賞ほか4冠に輝いた、村上春樹原作「ドライブ・マイ・カー」8/20(金)公開|access-date= 20 November 2021|work= Asahi Family|date= 19 August 2021}}</ref>

===Writing===
Hamaguchi was the co-writer of the filmscript with [[Takamasa Oe]]. It is primarily based on "Drive My Car" by [[Haruki Murakami]] from his 2014 short story collection, ''[[Men Without Women (Murakami short story collection)|Men Without Women]]''.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://deadline.com/2021/06/murakami-drive-my-car-cannes-ryusuke-hamaguchi-match-factory-1234766973/|title=The Match Factory Boards Murakami Adaptation 'Drive My Car', The Next Film From Cannes & Berlin Director Ryusuke Hamaguchi|work=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|first=Andreas|last=Wiseman|date=1 June 2021|access-date=3 June 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://variety.com/2021/film/asia/japan-hamaguchi-ryusuke-plots-his-next-film-1234929875/|title=Japan's Silver Bear-Winner Hamaguchi Ryusuke Plots His Next Film Moves|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|first=Mark|last=Schilling|date=14 March 2021|access-date=3 June 2021}}</ref> The script also features elements from Murakami's stories "Scheherazade" and "Kino" (both also part of ''Men Without Women'').<ref name=":0" /> For the film version, the co-authors were reported by ''The New York Times'' to have "greatly expanded on the (short) story's central dynamic, which turns on a sexist widowed actor and the much-younger female driver who motors him around in his cherished Saab."<ref>{{cite news|title='Drive My Car' Review: A Director Takes Your Heart for a Spin |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/11/24/movies/drive-my-car-review.html |date=November 24, 2021}}</ref>

===Cinematography===
Cinematographer Hidetoshi Shinomiya was assigned to do the filming for the project.<ref name="rogerebert.com">{{Cite web |last=Aguilar |first=Carlos |date=November 24, 2021 |title=Drive My Car movie review & film summary (2021) |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/drive-my-car-movie-review-2021 |access-date= |website=RogerEbert.com |language=en}}</ref>

===Set design===
The original story features a yellow [[Saab 900]] convertible, but it was changed in the film to a red Saab 900 Turbo to visually complement the Hiroshima landscape.<ref>{{Cite web|author=嶋田知加子|url=https://www.fnn.jp/articles/-/226701|title=映画「ドライブ・マイ・カー」は広島ロケの大作 カンヌ脚本賞受賞作を女子アナ＆シネマ通が語りつくす|date=20 August 2021|website=[[Fuji News Network]]|publisher=[[Shinhiroshima Telecasting]]|language=ja|accessdate=8 February 2022|archive-url=https://archive.today/20210821111214/https://www.fnn.jp/articles/-/226701|archive-date=21 August 2021}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
The original score for ''Drive My Car'' was composed by musician [[Eiko Ishibashi]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Hadfield |first=James |date=18 March 2022 |title=Eiko Ishibashi's score sets the scene in Oscar-contender 'Drive My Car' |url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2022/03/18/music/eiko-ishibashi-drive-my-car/ |publisher=[[The Japan Times]]}}</ref> In an interview with ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', director Hamaguchi said; "Typically, I don't use a lot of music in my films, but hearing the music Ishibashi made was the first time I thought this could work for the film."<ref>{{cite web |last=Amorosi |first=A.D |date=11 March 2022 |title='Drive My Car' Director and Soundtrack Composer on Crafting Film's Emotional Score: 'Our Film Language Was Very Similar' |url=https://variety.com/2022/film/spotlight/drive-my-car-soundtrack-eiko-ishibashi-1235202019/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> The soundtrack consists of 12 tracks.<ref>{{cite web |title=Drive My Car OST on Musicbrainz |url=https://musicbrainz.org/release/37f9fe4c-80d6-4799-8d74-1270b60f1d89 |website=musicbrainz.org}}</ref>

Hamaguchi wished to incorporate [[the Beatles]]' song "[[Drive My Car (song)|Drive My Car]]", which the film and story are named after.  However, it was too difficult to get permission for its usage. He instead included a string [[quartet]] piece by [[Beethoven]], which is directly referenced in Murakami's original story.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wong |first=Silvia |date=9 July 2021 |title=Berlin prize-winner Ryusuke Hamaguchi talks Cannes Competition title 'Drive My Car' |url=https://www.screendaily.com/features/berlin-prize-winner-ryusuke-hamaguchi-talks-cannes-competition-title-drive-my-car/5161390.article |access-date=8 February 2022 |website=[[Screen Daily]]}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[Pitchfork (website)|Pitchfork]]'', Quinn Moreland wrote that the soundtrack "possesses a cool remove, mirroring the film's glacial profundity with organic nuance and contemplative improvisation."<ref name=Pitchfork/> Vanessa Ague of ''[[The Quietus]]'' wrote; "Ishibashi creates a narrative within the theme and variations, tracing a musical path that stands on its own."<ref name=Quietus/> Writing for [[PopMatters]], Jay Honeycomb wrote; "Ishibashi's music washes over you when it comes, allowing the seeds planted by Hamaguchi to germinate and grow without drowning you in sentimentality."<ref>{{cite web |last=Honeycomb |first=Jay |date=18 April 2022 |title=Composer Eiko Ishibashi Veers Outside Convention with 'Drive My Car' |url=https://www.popmatters.com/eiko-ishibashi-drive-car-interview |website=[[PopMatters]]}}</ref>

==Release and reception==
''Drive My Car'' had its world premiere at the [[2021 Cannes Film Festival]] in competition for the [[Palme d'Or]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Sharf|first=Zack|date=3 June 2021|title=Cannes Film Festival 2021 Lineup: Sean Baker, Wes Anderson, and More Compete for Palme d'Or|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2021/06/2021-cannes-film-festival-lineup-1234641655/|access-date=3 June 2021|work=[[IndieWire]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=DRIVE MY CAR|url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/festival/films/drive-my-car|access-date=3 October 2021|website=Festival de Cannes 2021|date=12 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref> It was released in United Kingdom on 19 November 2021 and on 24 November 2021 in the United States.

In 2025, the film was showcased in the section 'Decisive Moments in Asian Cinema' at the [[30th Busan International Film Festival]], as part of the special "Asian Cinema 100", being the signature work of the director [[Ryusuke Hamaguchi]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://m.entertain.naver.com/movie/article/001/0015555593 |title=BIFF, 특별전 '아시아영화의 결정적 순간들'서 거장 작품 조명|trans-title=BIFF highlights masterful works in special exhibition "Decisive Moments in Asian Cinema."|author=Park Sung-jae |work=Yonhap News|publisher=Naver|date= August 8, 2025|access-date=August 8, 2025 |language=ko}}</ref>

===Home media===
The DVD and Blu-ray versions of the film were released in the United States on July 19, 2022, as part of [[The Criterion Collection]].<ref>{{Citation |last1=Hamaguchi |title=Drive My Car |date=2022-07-19 |url=https://www.amazon.com/Drive-Car-Criterion-Collection-Blu-ray/dp/B09YCV1GKR/ref=sr_1_1?crid=Y2PMTQUZXDI3&keywords=drive+my+car&qid=1651334086&s=movies-tv&sprefix=drive+my+car,movies-tv,80&sr=1-1 |publisher=The Criterion Collection |access-date= |last2=Ryusuke}}</ref>

=== Box office ===
{{As of|2022|4|8}}, ''Drive My Car'' has grossed $2.3 million in the United States and Canada, and $12.3 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $14.7 million.<ref name=BOM/>

In the United States, the film had grossed $944,000 at the time of its Oscar nominations on February 8, 2022. Between then and March 20, it grossed $1.15 million (a 122% increase), for a running total of $2.1 million.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://deadline.com/2022/03/oscar-best-picture-nomiees-box-office-boost-streaming-viewership-1234985202/ |title= Oscar's Halo Effect On Best Picture Nominees Dims — Not Just At Box Office But On Streaming: A Scorecard |date=March 24, 2022|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=March 24, 2022}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 97% based on 217 reviews, with an average rating of 8.7/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Drive My Car''{{'}}s imposing runtime holds a rich, patiently engrossing drama that reckons with self-acceptance and regret."<ref name=Rotten>{{cite Rotten Tomatoes|title=Drive My Car|type=m|id=drive_my_car |access-date=28 July 2024}}</ref> According to [[Metacritic]], which assigned a weighted average score of 91 out of 100 based on 42 critics, the film received "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=drive-my-car |type=movie |title=Drive My Car Reviews |access-date= 19 December 2021}}</ref>

The film received a positive review from [[Manohla Dargis]] in ''[[The New York Times]]'', where she wrote, "''Drive My Car'' sneaks up on you, lulling you in with visuals that are as straightforward as the narrative is complex."<ref name="NYT">{{cite news |last1=Dargis |first1=Manohla |title='Drive My Car' Review: A Director Takes Your Heart for a Spin |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/11/24/movies/drive-my-car-review.html |access-date=21 February 2022 |work=The New York Times |date=24 November 2021}}</ref> Writing for ''[[The Guardian]]'', [[Peter Bradshaw]] gave the film five stars out of five and called it an "engrossing and exalting experience".<ref name="Guardian">{{cite news |last1=Bradshaw |first1=Peter |title=Drive My Car review – mysterious Murakami tale of erotic and creative secrets |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/jul/14/drive-my-car-review-hamaguchi-murakami |access-date=3 January 2022 |work=The Guardian |date=14 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref>

Carlos Aguilar found the cinematography of the film to be exceptional, stating that: "Bountiful in subtle imagery from cinematographer Hidetoshi Shinomiya, the film mines majestic visual symbolism from seemingly ordinary occurrences. Take for example a shot of Yūsuke and Misaki's hand through the car's sunroof holding cigarettes as to not let the smoke permeate their sacred mode of transportation—an unspoken communion of respect."<ref name="rogerebert.com"/>

Justin Chang described it as "a masterpiece" that is "Perfectly paced, intricately structured and entirely absorbing". He credits "the elusive magic" that transpires between the film's two primary actors, [[Hidetoshi Nishijima (actor)|Hidetoshi Nishijima]] and [[Tōko Miura]], and "how acting can achieve the force of real life, and how real life requires a measure of acting."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-11-29 |title=Review: In 'Drive My Car,' Ryûsuke Hamaguchi delivers a haunting masterpiece of art and life |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2021-11-29/drive-my-car-review-ryusuke-hamaguchi |access-date=2023-01-17 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref>

Metacritic reported that ''Drive My Car'' appeared on over 89 film critics' top-ten lists for 2021, the most of any foreign-language film that year, and ranked first or second on 23 lists.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Best of 2021: Film Critic Top Ten Lists|url=https://www.metacritic.com/feature/film-critics-pick-10-best-movies-of-2021|access-date=20 February 2022|website=Metacritic|language=en|archive-date=18 January 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220118201057/https://www.metacritic.com/feature/film-critics-pick-10-best-movies-of-2021|url-status=dead}}</ref>

In 2023, it ranked number 19 on ''The Hollywood Reporter''{{'}}s list of the "50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far)."<ref name= "21st Century"/> ''[[Comic Book Resources]]'' ranked it at number 2 on its list of the "10 Best Indie Films of the 2020s (So Far)," calling it "one of those films where audiences are happy to watch a heartbreaking story because the experience is extraordinary."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.cbr.com/best-indie-movies-2020s/#drive-my-car|title=10 Best Indie Films of the 2020s (So Far)|website=[[Comic Book Resources]]|date=24 July 2023 }}</ref> It ranked number 5 on ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'}}s list of "The 20 Best Drama Movies of the 2020s So Far," writing that "Hamaguchi crafted a timeless examination of loss; while it’s initially a tough premise to sit through, ''Drive My Car'' becomes rewarding for those that invest in its message about the necessity of healing. The film reminds the viewer that moving forward after the death of a loved one is a difficult, but necessary task."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://collider.com/best-drama-movies-2020s/|title=The 10 Best Drama Movies of the 2020s So Far, Ranked|website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|date=24 August 2023 }}</ref> In June 2025, ''[[IndieWire]]'' ranked the film at number 9 on its list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)."<ref>{{cite web |date=June 16, 2025 |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)|url=https://www.indiewire.com/lists/best-movies-2020s/ |access-date=2025-06-23}}</ref> In July 2025, the film ranked number 89 on the "Readers' Choice" edition of ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century" and number 28 on ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century."<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-movies-of-the-21st-century-1235200512/ | title=100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=July 1, 2025 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |date=2 July 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref>

=== Accolades ===
{{Main|List of accolades received by Drive My Car (film){{!}}List of accolades received by ''Drive My Car'' (film)}}
[[File:Ryusuke Hamaguchi (HKAFF2018) (cropped).png|thumb|upright=0.78|[[Ryusuke Hamaguchi]] was nominated for the [[Academy Award for Best Director]].]]
The film was selected to compete for the [[Palme d'Or]] at the [[2021 Cannes Film Festival]] where it won three awards including [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Screenplay|Best Screenplay]].<ref name="Cannes">{{cite web|date=18 July 2021|title=Cannes Film Festival 2021: Full Winners List|url=https://www.tatlerasia.com/culture/entertainment/hk-cannes-2021-list-winners|access-date=18 July 2021|work=Asia Tatler}}</ref> Hamaguchi and Oe became the first Japanese individuals to win the Best Screenplay Award at Cannes.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Sato|first=Misuzo|date=18 July 2021|title='Drive My Car' wins Cannes Best Screenplay for Hamaguchi, Oe|url=https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14397906|url-status=live|access-date=9 October 2021|website=The Asahi Shimbun|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210718083652/https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/14397906 |archive-date=18 July 2021 }}</ref> At the [[79th Golden Globe Awards]], the film won [[Golden Globe Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=10 January 2022 |title=Japan's 'Drive My Car' wins Golden Globe for best non-English film |work=[[The Japan Times]] |url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2022/01/10/films/drive-my-car-golden-globes/ |access-date=8 February 2022}}</ref>

It was picked as the Japanese entry for the [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]] at the [[94th Academy Awards]], making the December 2021 shortlist.<ref name="Japan">{{cite web |url=https://www.sankei.com/article/20211011-FNXSUHMR6RKG5GHRKOIFXUP6VQ/ |title="Drive My Car" exhibited at the Academy Awards selection for Japan |work=Sankei |date=11 October 2020 |access-date=11 October 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2021/awards/news/2022-oscars-shortlist-beyonce-spiderman-jonny-greenwood-documentary-1235141206/ |title=Oscars Shortlists Include Beyoncé, 'Spider-Man' and Two Jonny Greenwood Scores as France's 'Titane' Is Snubbed |work=Variety |first=Clayton |last=Davis |date=21 December 2021 |access-date=21 December 2021}}</ref> It was nominated for four Academy Awards, including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] for Hamaguchi, [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]] for Hamaguchi and co-screenwriter Takamasa Oe, and [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]], winning the latter award.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last1=Lang |first1=Brent |last2=Moreau |first2=Jordan |last3=Grantham-Philips |first3=Wyatte |date=8 February 2022 |title=Oscar Nominations 2022: 'Power of the Dog' Leads With 12 Nods, 'Dune' Follows With 10 (Full List) |url=https://variety.com/2022/film/news/2022-oscar-nominations-list-nominees-1235172582/ |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=8 February 2022}}</ref><ref name=dalton>{{citation |last=Dalton |first=Andrew |title='Drive My Car' wins Oscar award for best international film | newspaper=[[Associated Press]] |date=March 27, 2022 |url=https://apnews.com/article/2022-oscars-best-international-film-winner-9757085ceae7bd78963f8815de1f32ad}}</ref>  It was the [[List of Academy Award winners and nominees from Japan#Best Picture|first Japanese film]] to receive a Best Picture nomination,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tartaglione |first=Nancy |date=8 February 2022 |title=Oscars: 'Drive My Car' Makes History As First Japanese Film Nominated For Best Picture |url=https://deadline.com/2022/02/drive-my-car-oscar-nominations-records-japan-1234928470/ |access-date=8 February 2022 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> and Hamaguchi became the [[List of Academy Award winners and nominees from Japan#Best Director|third Japanese director]] nominated for Best Director since [[Hiroshi Teshigahara]] in [[38th Academy Awards|1965]] and [[Akira Kurosawa]] in [[58th Academy Awards|1985]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=8 February 2022 |title=米アカデミー賞候補に濱口監督「ドライブ・マイ・カー」 |language=ja |work=[[The Nikkei]] |url=https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOGN032N80T00C22A2000000/ |access-date=8 February 2022}}</ref>

It became one of only seven films (and the first non-English-language film) to win Best Picture from all three major U.S. critics groups ([[Los Angeles Film Critics Association|LAFCA]], [[New York Film Critics Circle|NYFCC]], [[National Society of Film Critics|NSFC]]), the other six being ''[[Goodfellas]]'', ''[[Schindler's List]]'', ''[[L.A. Confidential (film)|L.A. Confidential]]'', ''[[The Social Network]]'', ''[[The Hurt Locker]]'', and ''[[Tár]]''.
