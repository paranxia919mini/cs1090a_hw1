'''''Grey Gardens''''' is a 1975 American [[documentary film]] by [[Albert and David Maysles]]. The film depicts the everyday lives of two [[Recluse|reclusive]], [[upper class|upper-class]] women, a mother and daughter both named Edith Beale, who lived in poverty at [[Grey Gardens (estate)|Grey Gardens]], a derelict mansion at 3 West End Road in the wealthy [[Georgica Pond]] neighborhood of [[East Hampton (town), New York|East Hampton]], New York. The film was screened at the [[1976 Cannes Film Festival]] but was not entered into the main competition.<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web |url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/2151/year/1976.html |title=Festival de Cannes: Grey Gardens |access-date=2009-05-10|work=festival-cannes.com}}</ref>

[[Ellen Hovde]] and [[Muffie Meyer]] also directed, and [[Susan Froemke]] was the associate producer. The film was edited by Hovde, Meyer and Froemke.<ref>{{Official website|http://mayslesfilms.com/film/grey-gardens/}}</ref>

In 2010, the film was selected by the [[Library of Congress]] for preservation in the United States [[National Film Registry]] as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant",<ref>{{Cite web|title=Hollywood Blockbusters, Independent Films and Shorts Selected for 2010 National Film Registry|url=https://www.loc.gov/item/prn-10-273/2010-national-film-registry-announced/2010-12-28/|access-date=2020-10-13|website=Library of Congress}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Complete National Film Registry Listing |url=https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-film-preservation-board/film-registry/complete-national-film-registry-listing/|access-date=2020-10-13|website=Library of Congress}}</ref> and in the 2014 ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' poll film critics voted ''Grey Gardens'' the tenth-best documentary film of all time.<ref name="BBC-doc">{{cite news |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-28602506 |title=Silent film tops documentary poll |accessdate=1 August 2014 |work=BBC News|date=August 2014 }}</ref> In November 2012, it topped the list of ''100 greatest documentary  films of all time'' by [[PBS]] through public voting.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |title=The 25 Greatest Documentaries of All Time |url=http://archive.pov.org/blog/news/2012/12/the-25-greatest-documentaries-of-all-time/ |access-date=2023-05-11 |website=POV {{!}} American Documentary |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Background==
[[File:Eh-village-map.gif|thumb|Map of [[East Hampton (village), New York|East Hampton]]; the Beales lived at Georgica Pond in the southwest of the [[Administrative divisions of New York#Village|village]].]]
[[Edith Ewing Bouvier Beale]] (1895–1977), known as "Big Edie", and her daughter [[Edith Bouvier Beale]] (1917–2002), known as "Little Edie", were the aunt and the first cousin, respectively, of former US First Lady [[Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis]]. The two women lived together at the [[Grey Gardens (estate)|Grey Gardens estate]] for decades with limited funds in increasing squalor and isolation.<ref>{{cite news|title=The top houses from the movies| url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/property/propertypicturegalleries/9239346/The-top-houses-from-the-movies.html| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120502235333/http://www.telegraph.co.uk/property/propertypicturegalleries/9239346/The-top-houses-from-the-movies.html| url-status=dead| archive-date=May 2, 2012| newspaper=Daily Telegraph | location=London|date=May 2, 2012}}</ref>

The house was designed in 1897 by Joseph Greenleaf Thorpe and purchased in 1923 by "Big Edie" and her husband [[Phelan Beale]]. After Phelan left his wife, "Big Edie" and "Little Edie" lived there for more than 50 years. The house was called Grey Gardens because of the color of the dunes, the concrete garden walls, and the sea mist.<ref>{{citation | url=http://www.easthamptonhistory.org/events/greygardens08-info2.html| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090422050405/http://www.easthamptonhistory.org/events/greygardens08-info2.html| archive-date=2009-04-22| access-date=October 13, 2013|title=A Return to Grey Gardens}}</ref>

Throughout the fall of 1971 and into 1972, their living conditions—their house was infested by fleas, inhabited by numerous cats and raccoons, deprived of running water, and filled with garbage and decay—were exposed as the result of an article in the ''[[National Enquirer]]'' and a cover story in ''[[New York (magazine)|New York Magazine]]''<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Sheehy| first=Gail| title=The Secret Of Grey Gardens | magazine =New York Magazine | url =https://nymag.com/news/features/56102/}} Retrieved on 20 May 2014.</ref> after a series of inspections (which the Beales called "raids") by the [[Suffolk County, New York|Suffolk County]] Health Department. With the Beale women facing eviction and the razing of their house, in the summer of 1972 Jacqueline Onassis and her sister [[Lee Radziwill]] provided the necessary funds to stabilize and repair the dilapidated house so that it would meet village codes.<ref>{{cite news 
|title='Grey Gardens' Heroines Trapped in House |date=May 16, 1976 |publisher=[[Asbury Park Press]] |quote=Jacqueline Bouvier Kennedy Onassis and Lee Radziwill, her sister, poured $32,000 into repairs, after which, says Mrs. Beale, Mrs. Onassis told her "We can't go on." The sanitarians were mollified; the Beale ladies, outraged by the invasion from the outside, allowed to stay. |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref>

Albert and David Maysles became interested in their story and received permission to film a documentary about the women, which was released in 1976 to wide critical acclaim. Their [[direct cinema]] technique left the women to tell their own stories.<ref name="Webb">{{cite web |author=Webb |first=Ralf |date=September 2018 |title=It's All In The Film: Direct Cinema, 'Grey Gardens' and 'That Summer' |url=https://www.thewhitereview.org/feature/film-direct-cinema-grey-gardens-summer/ |website=The White Review}}</ref>

==Production==
[[Albert and David Maysles]] came into contact with the Beales in 1972 after [[Lee Radziwill]] suggested they make a documentary on her childhood in [[East Hampton (town), New York|East Hampton]] and took them with her on a trip to Grey Gardens. The initial film was funded by Radziwill but was eventually shelved and the footage was lost. The Maysles brothers returned in 1974 without Radziwill's support to film ''Grey Gardens''.<ref name="Webb"/>

==Aftermath==
"Big Edie" died in 1977, and "Little Edie" sold the house in 1979 for $220,000 ({{Inflation|US|220000|1979|r=-3|fmt=eq}}) to [[Sally Quinn]] and her husband, longtime ''[[Washington Post]]'' editor [[Ben Bradlee]],<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2014/12/15/let-go | title=Let it Go | first=Joan | last=Acocella | magazine=[[The New Yorker]] | date=December 15, 2014}}</ref> who promised to restore the dilapidated structure (the sale agreement forbade razing the house). The couple restored the house and grounds.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Bray |first=Sarah |date=2015-05-14 |title=This is What Grey Gardens Looks Like Now |url=https://www.townandcountrymag.com/society/tradition/news/g1281/what-grey-gardens-looks-like-now/ |magazine=Town & Country |language=en-US}}</ref> "Little Edie" died in [[Florida]] in 2002 at the age of 84.<ref name="Martin">{{cite news |author=Martin |first=Douglas |date=January 25, 2002 |title=Edith Bouvier Beale, 84, 'Little Edie,' Dies |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2002/01/25/nyregion/edith-bouvier-beale-84-little-edie-dies.html |work=New York Times}}</ref>

[[Jerry Torre]], the teenage handyman shown in the documentary (nicknamed "[[The Marble Faun]]" by "Little Edie"), was sought by the filmmakers for years afterward, and was found by chance in 2005 driving a [[New York City]] taxicab.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Green| first=Adam| title=The Marble Faun | magazine=[[The New Yorker]] | date =March 6, 2006 | url =https://www.newyorker.com/archive/2006/03/06/060306ta_talk_green}}</ref> A 2011 documentary, ''The Marble Faun of Grey Gardens'', by Jason Hay and Steve Pelizza, showed that he was then a sculptor at the [[Art Students League of New York]].<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.politico.com/states/new-york/albany/story/2012/06/a-beautiful-youth-dark-days-and-redemption-for-the-gardener-of-grey-gardens-067223 |last=Smith |first=Melissa |publisher=[[Politico]] |title=A beautiful youth, dark days, and redemption for the gardener of 'Grey Gardens' |date=2012-06-12 |access-date=2016-07-31}}</ref>

[[Lois Wright]], one of the two birthday party guests in the film, hosted a public television show for 30 years in East Hampton from the early 1980s to December 2018.<ref>{{cite news| url=http://visit.easthamptonstar.com/Archive/3/PUBLIC-ACCESS-LTV-Celebrates-20th-Anniversary-journey-dinky-den-near-dump-state-art-studio| title=Public Access LTV Celebrates 20th Anniversary| first=Mayra| last=Tubatan| newspaper=[[The East Hampton Star]]| date=February 5, 2014| access-date=September 27, 2014| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141020032049/http://visit.easthamptonstar.com/Archive/3/PUBLIC-ACCESS-LTV-Celebrates-20th-Anniversary-journey-dinky-den-near-dump-state-art-studio| archive-date=October 20, 2014| url-status=dead}}</ref> She wrote a book about her experiences at the house with the Beales.<ref>{{Cite book | isbn=978-0977746217 | last=Wright | first=Lois | title=My Life at Grey Gardens: 13 Months and Beyond, a True and Factual Book | url=http://www.loiswright.net/ | year=2007| publisher=Wright }}</ref>

In 2006, Maysles made available previously unreleased footage for a special two-disc edition for [[the Criterion Collection]]. It included a new feature titled ''[[The Beales of Grey Gardens]]'', which also received a [[Limited release|limited theatrical release]].

Previously lost footage shot in 1972, using 16mm film, featuring [[Lee Radziwill]] visiting with the Beales, was released in 2017 as ''[[That Summer (2017 film)|That Summer]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2018/film/reviews/that-summer-review-1202740165/|title=That Summer Review|last=Weissberg|first=Jay|date=March 30, 2018|website=Variety}}</ref>

Quinn and Bradlee resided in the restored Grey Gardens for 35 years until Bradlee's death in 2014, after which Quinn found the home "too sad" to occupy alone. For the next several years, the property was available to rent until Quinn sold it in 2017. Prior to the sale, Quinn was forced to sell the remaining furniture originally belonging to the Beales, citing a lack of space. As of 2017, fashion designer [[Liz Lange]] is the owner of Grey Gardens. Lange and her husband have extensively remodeled the house, including lifting the house on stilts to create a basement in the existing crawlspace. The gardens surrounding the property have also been remodeled.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Dangremond |first1=Sam |title=Grey Gardens Sells for $15.5 Million |url=https://www.townandcountrymag.com/leisure/real-estate/news/a9532/grey-gardens-house-for-sale/ |website=Town & Country |access-date=12 January 2020 |date=20 December 2017}}</ref>

==Controversy==
Ethical questions have been raised about ''Grey Gardens'' since its release. In 1976, Walter Goodman wrote in ''[[The New York Times]]'' that "the film presents [the Beales] as a pair of grotesques," and asked "why were they put on exhibition this way?"<ref name="nyt-goodman">{{cite news| url=https://www.nytimes.com/1976/02/22/archives/grey-gardens-cinema-verite-or-sideshow-cin-ma-verit-or-sideshow.html | title='Grey Gardens': Cinéma Verité or Sideshow? | first=Walter | last=Goodman | newspaper=[[The New York Times]] | date=February 22, 1976 | access-date=March 16, 2021}}</ref> In 2014, in conversation with Alex Simon for ''The Hollywood Interview'', Albert Maysles was asked specifically about the issue of exploitation: "''Grey Gardens'' was very controversial when it was initially released, with some circles feeling you and David were exploiting these two women who seemed to be mentally ill." Maysles replied that "as someone with a background in psychology, I knew better than to claim [the Beales] were mentally ill. Their behavior was just their way of asserting themselves. And what could be a better way to assert themselves than a film about them asserting themselves? Nothing more, nothing less. It’s just them. They were always in control."<ref>{{cite web |last1=Simon |first1=Alex |title=Albert Maysles: Gimme Some Truth |url=http://thehollywoodinterview.blogspot.com/2014/04/albert-maysles-hollywood-interview.html |website=The Hollywood Interview |access-date=16 March 2021 |date=10 April 2014}}</ref> Nevertheless, the matter has continued to be the subject of debate, with some commentators coming down on the side of exploitation, others on the side of empathy.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.metro.us/review-grey-gardens-is-more-empathetic-than-exploitive/ | title=Review: 'Grey Gardens' is more empathetic than exploitive | first=Matt | last=Prigge| newspaper=[[Metro (Philadelphia newspaper)|Metro]]| date=March 6, 2015| access-date=March 16, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last1=Davidson |first1=David |date=1981 |title=Direct Cinema and Modernism: The Long Journey to GREY GARDENS |url=http://www.jstor.org/stable/20687549 |journal=Journal of the University Film Association |volume=33 |issue=1 |pages=3–13 |jstor=20687549 |access-date=16 March 2021}}</ref>{{refn|{{cite magazine |last=Coyne |first=Kelly Marie |date={{date|April 18, 2018}} |title=Gothic Camp: The Beales of East Hampton |url=https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/gothic-camp-the-beales-of-east-hampton/ |magazine=LA Review of Books |pages= |access-date=2025-11-25 |author-link=Kelly Marie Coyne }}}} In at least one case, it was concluded that both exploitation and empathy were to be found in the film.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://variety.com/2018/film/columns/that-summer-grey-gardens-edith-bouvier-beale-1202820552/ | title=In 'That Summer,' the Stars of 'Grey Gardens' Look More Unhinged Than Ever | first=Owen | last=Gleiberman| newspaper=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]| date=24 May 2018| access-date=16 March 2021}}</ref>

In his article, Goodman also pointed out that the Beales were represented in negotiations with the Maysles by their family lawyer and that they were "paid for their cooperation and are due to participate in any profits."<ref name="nyt-goodman"/> Adam White, writing for ''[[The Daily Telegraph|The Telegraph]]'' in 2018, reported that "The Beales' reasoning for allowing the cameras in was also practical: they were in dire need of money."<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/0/tragedy-grey-gardens-landmark-documentary-actually-exploitation/ | title=The tragedy of Grey Gardens: was the landmark documentary actually an exploitation movie? | first=Adam| last=White| newspaper=[[The Daily Telegraph|The Telegraph]]| date=June 1, 2018| access-date=March 16, 2021}}</ref> In the context of documentary making, paying one's subjects has long been a source of ethical debate in terms of how it impacts the final work.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Leeman |first1=Lisa |title=Money Changes Everything--or Does It?: Considering Whether Documentaries Should Pay for Play |url=https://www.documentary.org/feature/money-changes-everything-or-does-it-considering-whether-documentaries-should-pay-play |website=International Documentary Association |access-date=16 March 2021 |date=21 January 2004}}</ref>

==Adaptations==

===Musical theatre===
{{Main|Grey Gardens (musical)}}
The documentary, and the women's story, were adapted as a full-length musical, ''Grey Gardens'', with book by [[Doug Wright]], music by [[Scott Frankel]] and lyrics by [[Michael Korie]]. Starring [[Christine Ebersole]] and [[Mary Louise Wilson]], the show premiered at [[Playwrights Horizons]] in New York City in February 2006.<ref>Jones, Kenneth. {{Deprecated archive|url=https://archive.today/20120906091718/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/102449-Grey-Gardens-Takes-Root-on-Broadway-Beginning-Oct-3|title="'Grey Gardens' Takes Root on Broadway Beginning Oct. 3"}}, playbill.com, October 3, 2006.</ref> The musical re-opened on Broadway in November 2006 at the [[Walter Kerr Theatre]], and was included in more than 25 "Best of 2006" lists in newspapers and magazines.<ref>[http://www.psclassics.com/cd_greygardens.html "Grey Gardens: The Musical"], PS Classics.</ref><!--{{cn|date=December 2014}}--> The production won a [[Tony Award]] for Best Costume Design, and Ebersole and Wilson each won Tony Awards for their performances.<ref>Jones, Kenneth. [http://www.playbill.com/news/article/112998-Will-Grey-Gardens-Grow-in-London-Creative-Team-Has-Hope Will Grey Gardens Grow in London? Creative Team Has Hope] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121013094952/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/112998-Will-Grey-Gardens-Grow-in-London-Creative-Team-Has-Hope |date=October 13, 2012 }} playbill.com, November 21, 2007.</ref> The Broadway production closed on July 29, 2007.<ref>[http://www.ibdb.com/production.php?id=426592 "Grey Gardens"], Internet Broadway Database, accessed December 6, 2014.</ref> It was the first musical on Broadway ever to be adapted from a documentary.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Gans |first1=Andrew |last2=Jones |first2=Kenneth |date=July 2, 2007 |title=Grey Gardens Will Close on Broadway July 29 |url=http://www.playbill.com/news/article/grey-gardens-will-close-on-broadway-july-29-141935 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150929150918/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/grey-gardens-will-close-on-broadway-july-29-141935 |archive-date=September 29, 2015 |website=Playbill}}</ref>

A new Australian adaptation of the women's story, ''House of Rot: Grey Gardens,'' by Dino Dimitriadis and Victoria Falconer was created in 2025. 'Weaving original compositions with new interpretations of known songs both contemporary and classic.' The first production at The [[Malthouse Theatre]] in 2026 will star [[Paul Capsis]] and Adam Noviello as Big Edie and Little Edie respectively.

===Television film===
{{Main|Grey Gardens (2009 film)}}
''Grey Gardens'', an [[HBO]] film, stars [[Jessica Lange]] and [[Drew Barrymore]] as the Edies, with [[Jeanne Tripplehorn]] as [[Jacqueline Kennedy]], and [[Daniel Baldwin]] as [[Julius Krug]]. Directed and co-written (with [[Patricia Rozema]]) by filmmaker [[Michael Sucsy]], filming began on October 22, 2007, in [[Toronto]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://variety.com/2006/film/markets-festivals/thesps-tend-to-gardens-1117938667/ |title=Thesps tend to 'Gardens' |author= Fleming, Michael |date=February 21, 2006 |work=Variety}}</ref> It flashes back and forth between Little Edie's life as a young woman and the actual filming/premiere of the 1975 documentary. First aired on HBO on April 18, 2009, the film won three [[Primetime Emmys]]<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.emmys.com/shows/grey-gardens |title=Grey Gardens|author= Emmys}}</ref> and two [[Golden Globes]].<ref>{{cite web|url= http://www.goldenglobes.org/browse/film/26244|title= Grey Gardens|author= Golden Globes|url-status= dead|archive-url= https://archive.today/20120801064817/http://www.goldenglobes.org/browse/film/26244|archive-date= August 1, 2012|df= mdy-all}}</ref>
