'''''The Monkey''''' is a 2025 American [[Supernatural film|supernatural]] [[comedy horror]] film<ref name="EntertainmentWeekly">{{cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/the-monkey-horror-comedy-people-die-insane-ways-exclusive-8762143|title=''The Monkey'' is like a comedy, except 'people die in insane ways': Inside the Stephen King adaptation (exclusive)|first=Nick|last=Romano|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=December 19, 2024|access-date=December 27, 2024 | quote=It's a perfect example of the tone the director wanted to strike, that mix of horror and comedy.}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Parthimos |first=Stephen |date=2025-06-07 |title=The Monkey Review: This monkey has an especially morbid sense of humour |url=https://everythingmoviereviews.com/2025/06/07/the-monkey-review-this-monkey-has-an-especially-morbid-sense-of-humour/ |access-date=2026-03-24 |website=Everything Movie Reviews |language=en}}</ref> written and directed by [[Osgood Perkins]]. Based on [[Stephen King]]'s [[The Monkey|1980 short story]], the film stars [[Theo James]] in a [[dual role]] as twin brothers whose lives are turned upside down by a cursed [[Cymbal-banging monkey toy|toy monkey]] that causes random horrific deaths around them. [[Tatiana Maslany]], [[Christian Convery]], [[Colin O'Brien (actor)|Colin O'Brien]], [[Rohan Campbell]], [[Sarah Levy (actress)|Sarah Levy]], [[Adam Scott]], and [[Elijah Wood]] also star.

''The Monkey'' was theatrically released in the United States by [[Neon (company)|Neon]] on February 21, 2025. The film received generally positive reviews from critics and has grossed $68.9 million worldwide against a budget of $10–11 million.<ref name=budget/>

==Plot==
<!--Per [[WP: FILMPLOT]], film plots should be between 400 and 700 words--><!-- Only mention the deaths that are important to the story. -->
In 1999, Captain Petey Shelburn attempts to return a [[Wind-up_toy|wind-up]] drum-playing toy monkey at an antiques shop. Before he can do so, the monkey plays its drum, triggering a [[chain reaction]] that ends with a [[Harpoon cannon|harpoon gun]] disemboweling the shop owner. Shortly afterwards, Petey disappears, leaving his wife Lois to raise their twin sons Hal and Bill. The twins later discover the monkey among their father's belongings and wind its key. That evening, while they are at a ''[[Teppanyaki|hibachi]]'' restaurant, their babysitter Annie is accidentally decapitated by the chef.

One day, Hal chooses to wind the monkey's key in the hopes it will kill Bill, by whom he is constantly bullied. Instead, Lois suffers an [[aneurysm]] and dies in front of Bill. Hal disposes of the monkey before he and Bill move to [[Casco, Maine]], to live with their aunt Ida and uncle Chip. When the monkey mysteriously reappears at their new home, Bill winds its key, causing Chip to be trampled to death in a horse [[stampede]]. The twins seal the monkey in its box and throw it down a nearby well, hoping it will remain hidden.

Twenty-five years later, Hal, still paranoid and traumatized by his past, is estranged from his family, including his son Petey. His ex-wife, now remarried, plans to have her new husband adopt Petey, effectively cutting Hal out of Petey's life. After Ida dies in a freak accident, Bill contacts Hal for the first time in nine years, voicing his suspicion that the monkey has returned and insisting Hal drive to Ida's house to retrieve it. Hal believes it when he witnesses a woman exploding upon jumping into an electrified motel pool. While inspecting Ida's house, her real estate agent Barbara reveals to Hal that over the week following Ida's death, a series of bizarre fatal accidents have occurred in the town. A shotgun later falls out of a closet, killing Barbara.

Hal and Petey soon learn that Bill lives in town and has the monkey, having hired a local named Ricky to retrieve it. Bill, who knows about Hal's attempt to use the monkey to kill him, has been turning the monkey's key in retaliation, though the monkey has only been killing random people. Hal implores Bill to stop, fearing for his son's safety. As whoever turns the key is spared, Bill says he will stop if Petey takes over the task of perpetually turning the key in his stead; if Hal refuses, Bill threatens to keep winding the key until Hal is killed, no matter who dies in the process.

Ricky, obsessed with the monkey, forces Petey at gunpoint to retrieve it for him at Bill's house. Bill instructs Petey to wind the key, and a swarm of wasps kills Ricky. When Hal enters the house, Bill, furious at the sight of his brother still alive, attempts and fails to force the monkey to drum without winding the key in a desperate attempt to kill Hal. In retaliation, the monkey drums uncontrollably, triggering widespread death and destruction throughout the entire town. When Hal confronts Bill, the latter finally gives up, and the twins apologize to each other and reconcile over their shared grief for their mother. Shortly afterwards, the monkey beats its drum one last time, and Bill is suddenly decapitated by a [[bowling ball]] bearing Lois' name.

Driving through the now-devastated town, Hal and Petey accept their fates as the monkey's owners and vow to prevent the key from ever being wound again. A pale black-eyed man riding a horse—implied to represent [[Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse#Pale Horse|the Pale Horseman]]—passes by and acknowledges them. Hal, determined to reconnect with his son, suggests they go dancing, as it is something Lois had loved, and Petey accepts.<!-- Do not include the ending deaths with the cheer squad. == Production ==
[[File:Musical Jolly Chimp1.jpg|thumb|upright|The original story featured a [[cymbal-banging monkey toy]], but the cymbals were replaced with a drum for the film.]]
Director [[Frank Darabont]] originally held the film rights to [[Stephen King]]'s short story "[[The Monkey]]" and planned to begin working on a film adaptation upon completing ''[[The Mist (film)|The Mist]]'' (2007), itself an adaptation of [[The Mist (novella)|one of]] King's novellas. The project never materialized.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Finke |first=Nikki |date=2007-04-18 |title=Why Frank Darabont Told George Lucas 'You're Insane' Over ''Indiana Jones 4'' |url=https://deadline.com/2007/04/why-frank-darabont-told-george-lucas-youre-insane-over-indiana-jones-4-1928/ |access-date=2025-02-06 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref>

Going into the 2023 [[Cannes Film Market]], financer [[Black Bear Pictures]] announced that a film adaptation of "The Monkey" was in development and up for sale to distributors.<ref name="Range" /> [[Oz Perkins|Osgood Perkins]] was hired to write and direct, and [[James Wan]] would produce under his [[Atomic Monster]] banner. [[Theo James]] was cast in the lead role.<ref name="CFM" /> In March 2024, [[Tatiana Maslany]], [[Elijah Wood]], [[Christian Convery]], [[Colin O'Brien (actor)|Colin O'Brien]], [[Rohan Campbell]], and [[Sarah Levy (actress)|Sarah Levy]] were revealed to have joined the cast.<ref name="FilmingWrap" />

== Marketing==
The film's official trailer released on January 16, 2025, and amassed over 43 million views online within 24 hours. After 72 hours, it had over 100 million views;<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Shayo |first=Lukas |date=January 26, 2025 |title=New Stephen King Horror Movie Sets Massive Trailer Viewing Record Ahead of Release |url=https://screenrant.com/the-monkey-2025-movie-trailer-most-viewed-independent-horror-record/ |access-date=January 29, 2025 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref> according to Neon, this made it "the most watched independent horror film trailer ever".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Carter |first=Justin |date=January 26, 2025 |title=''The Monkey''{{'}}s Newest Gory Trailer Is a Record-Breaking Hit |url=https://gizmodo.com/the-monkey-trailer-neon-record-2000555005 |access-date=January 29, 2025 |website=[[Gizmodo]]}}</ref> In late January, Neon attempted to air a trailer for the film on four major television networks, and all four rejected the studio, citing the film's "excessive violence". Neon released screenshots of the email discussions with the networks, although identifying information was redacted.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mahler |first=Matt |date=January 29, 2025 |title=Leaked Emails Show That the New Stephen King Movie Is Too Violent to Get Ads |url=https://movieweb.com/the-monkey-movie-ads-emails-neon-tv-spot/ |access-date=January 30, 2025 |website=[[MovieWeb]]}}</ref>

==Release==
In May 2024, [[Neon (company)|Neon]] won a bidding war between multiple U.S. distributors for domestic rights at the [[Marché du Film]] and set a theatrical release date for 2025.<ref name="Neon"/> The film was released in the United States on February 21, 2025.<ref name="Date" /> ''The Monkey'' marks the second collaboration between Neon and Perkins after ''[[Longlegs]]'' (2024).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/video/the-monkey-teaser-theo-james-osgood-perkins-stephen-king/ |title=''The Monkey'' Teaser: Theo James Stars In Stephen King Adaptation Reteaming ''Longlegs'' Helmer Osgood Perkins with Neon |date=August 12, 2024 |first=Matt |last=Grobar |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=August 12, 2024 |archive-date=August 12, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240812205522/https://deadline.com/video/the-monkey-teaser-theo-james-osgood-perkins-stephen-king/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Prior to its official February release, the film was screened early in January at [[Grauman's Egyptian Theatre]] as part of [[Beyond Fest]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=January 24, 2025 |title=''The Monkey'', Osgood Perkins' Next Pic, Racks Up 109M Views in Trailer Traffic, a Record for Independent Horror Film |url=https://deadline.com/2025/01/the-monkey-trailer-traffic-osgood-perkins-1236266610/ |access-date=January 29, 2025 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> The film was later added onto the [[Hulu]] service on August 7, 2025 as part of Neon's output deal with the former.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sharf |first=Zack |date=2025-08-01 |title=20 Best Movies New to Streaming in August: ''Final Destination Bloodlines'', ''The Monkey'', ''Legend of Ochi'' and More |url=https://variety.com/lists/best-movies-streaming-august-2025/ |access-date=2025-08-07 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
{{As of|2025|04|17|df=US}}, ''The Monkey'' has grossed $39.7{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $29.1{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $68.9{{nbsp}}million<ref name="NUM" /><ref name="BOM" /> against a budget of $10–11 million.<ref name=budget/>

In the United States and Canada, ''The Monkey'' was released alongside ''[[The Unbreakable Boy]]'' and was projected to gross around $17{{nbsp}}million from 3,200 theaters in its opening weekend.<ref>{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=February 19, 2025 |title='Captain America: Brave New World' To Hold No. 1 With $30M+ As 'The Monkey' Looks To Swing $17M Opening – Box Office Preview|url=https://deadline.com/2025/02/the-monkey-captain-america-box-office-preview-1236294668/|access-date=February 19, 2025 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> The film made $5.9{{nbsp}}million on its first day, including an estimated $1.9{{nbsp}}million from early screenings. It debuted to $14{{nbsp}}million, finishing second at the box office behind holdover ''[[Captain America: Brave New World]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2025W08/|title=Domestic 2025 Weekend 8|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=February 25, 2025}}</ref> Men accounted for 58% of the audience during its opening, with those 25–35 years old comprising 35% and premium large format screens contributing 12%.<ref name="opening">{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=February 23, 2025 |title=NEON Sees 2nd Biggest Opening With ''The Monkey'' At $14M+ In Midst Of ''Anora'' Awards Season Tear – Box Office |url=https://deadline.com/2025/02/box-office-the-monkey-captain-america-1236297207/|access-date=February 23, 2025 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> In its second weekend, the film made $6.3{{nbsp}}million, a 54%{{nbsp}}drop and above-average hold for horror entries.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2025W09/|title=Domestic 2025 Weekend 9|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=March 3, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2025/03/box-office-last-breath-captain-america-monkey-1236305223/|title=''Captain America: Brave New World'' At $15M Leads Lowest Weekend YTD, ''Last Breath'' Gasping $7.8M – Box Office Sunday Update|date=March 3, 2025|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=March 3, 2025}}</ref> In its third, it fell 39% and made $3.9{{nbsp}}million.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2025W10/|title=Domestic 2025 Weekend 10|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=March 12, 2025}}</ref> The film dropped out of the box office top ten in its sixth weekend.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2025W13/|title=Domestic 2025 Weekend 13|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=April 4, 2025}}</ref>

===Critical response===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|77|6.7|294|Cruelly clever with some unforgettably gory set pieces, ''The Monkey'' reaffirms director Osgood Perkins' horror bona fides while revealing he also has a surprising – albeit sick – sense of humor.|ref=yes|access-date=April 15, 2025}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 62 out of 100, based on 48 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Monkey'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-monkey |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=February 23, 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "C+" on an A+ to F scale, while those surveyed by [[PostTrak]] gave it an average 2.5 out of 5 stars, with 49% saying they would "definitely recommend" it.<ref name=opening/>

Brian Tallerico of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film three out of four stars and wrote, "Perkins has always been a formally confident filmmaker, but ''The Monkey'' contains some of his most striking imagery, shot through with malice by Nico Aguilar, and perfectly assembled by editors Graham Fortin & [[Greg Ng]] (who also cut ''Longlegs'')."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-monkey-movie-review|title=The Monkey|first=Brian|last=Tallerico|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|date=February 2025|access-date=5 February 2025}}</ref> [[Peter Travers]] of [[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]] said the film "loses steam in its final section", but added, "King and Perkins are still a dream team of fright masters when it comes to revealing the psychological dread lurking under the macabre monkeyshines that keep us up nights."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Travers |first=Peter |date=February 21, 2025 |title=Review: 'The Monkey' doesn't disappoint even if it never quite reaches horror heights |url=https://abcnews.go.com/GMA/Culture/review-monkey-doesnt-disappoint-reaches-horror-heights/story?id=119013103 |access-date=2025-02-22 |publisher=[[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]]}}</ref> ''[[The Atlantic]]''{{'}}s David Sims wrote, "In the hands of another director, the tone could wobble too wildly. Perkins is a specialist in making childhood trauma feel grounded and relatable, however, and that holds true for the loopy scares of his latest movie."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sims |first=David |date=2025-02-21 |title=A Movie That Has Fun With the Inevitability of Death |url=https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2025/02/the-monkey-movie-review/681787/ |access-date=2025-02-22 |website=[[The Atlantic]]}}</ref>

Siddhant Adlakha from ''[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]'' gave the film a less positive review, describing it as "tonally haphazard" and criticizing it for a "lack of dramatic coherence". He argued that the film's irony and snark undermine any intended commentary about parenthood or death, comparing it unfavorably to ''[[Deadpool (film)|Deadpool]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Adlakha |first=Siddhant |date=2025-02-03 |title='The Monkey' Review: A Glib Thriller That Botches Stephen King's Horror Classic |url=https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/the-monkey-review-horror-oz-perkins |access-date=2025-02-06 |website=[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]}}</ref> ''[[The i Paper]]''{{'}}s Christina Newland gave the film two out of five stars, writing, "For a movie that professes to be bizarre, shocking and violent – and which shares a director with ''Longlegs'', the scariest horror film of last year – ''The Monkey'' is surprisingly lacking in any good ideas.
