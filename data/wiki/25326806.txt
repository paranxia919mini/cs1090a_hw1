'''''It Is Not the Homosexual Who Is Perverse, But the Society in Which He Lives '''''({{langx|de|Nicht der Homosexuelle ist pervers, sondern die Situation, in der er lebt}}) is a 1971 [[West Germany|West German]] [[avant-garde film]] directed by [[Rosa von Praunheim]].

The film was an emancipation call for [[homosexuals]] to organize and fight for [[LGBT equality|their freedom]]. Triggering the modern [[LGBT movements|gay rights movement]] in West Germany and Switzerland, and also founding great resonance internationally. The film became a media scandal because [[LGBT conservatism|conservative homosexuals]] and heterosexuals alike rejected the demands for equality in all areas of life and the call for public solidarity.<ref name="DeutscheWelle">{{Cite web|url=http://www.dw.com/en/germanys-most-famous-gay-rights-activist-filmmaker-rosa-von-praunheim-at-75/a-41514818|title=Germany's most famous gay rights activist: Rosa von Praunheim|access-date=2019-02-21|work=[[Deutsche Welle]]|archive-date=2021-07-23|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210723132748/https://www.dw.com/en/germanys-most-famous-gay-rights-activist-filmmaker-rosa-von-praunheim-at-75/a-41514818|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film had its world premiere at the Young Filmmakers Forum section of [[21st Berlin International Film Festival|1971 Berlin International Film Festival]].

==Plot==
A young man named Daniel moves to [[Berlin]] and meets Clemens. They fall in love, move in together and try to imitate a typical [[bourgeois]] marriage. After four months, they separate. Daniel moves into the villa of a rich older man. A little later, his boyfriend cheats on him with another man. Daniel realizes he was only an object of lust.

Daniel starts working in a [[Gay bar|gay café]], dresses in the latest fashion and quickly adapts to the ideals of the [[gay subculture]]. He likes to spend his free time at the [[Lido (swimming pool)|lido]] and shows off his body in a skimpy bathing suit. At night, he goes to trendy bars and becomes more and more addicted to constantly changing partners and fleeting [[Hookup culture|sex adventures]]. After a while, he also discovers the charms of [[Cruising for sex|cruising]] in parks and public toilets, where he also notices older [[Violence against LGBT people|homosexuals being beaten up]].

During a night out, Daniel ends up in a [[drag queen]] bar, looking for his latest hook-up. Here he meets Paul, who takes him to his apartment shared with several gay roommates. 

The residents discuss the challenges and problems of gay life with him and make it clear that he leads a superficial life. His task as an emancipated [[gay man]], they say, is to publicly acknowledge his gayness and be an activist, instead of just chasing fashion trends and fast, mostly anonymous sex. The group suggests that he get involved socially and politically with them, create networks and fight together with others for a fairer society in which homosexuals can develop freely and without [[Sexual orientation discrimination|discrimination]].

The plot deals with gay emancipation, homosexual culture, and other provocative themes for its time.<ref name="Goethe-Institut">{{Cite web|url=https://www.goethe.de/prj/ger/en/ihr/22294995.html|title=QUEER HISTORY - The Last Hundred Queer Years in Germany|access-date=2022-04-28|work=[[Goethe-Institut]]|archive-date=2024-03-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240302085228/https://www.goethe.de/prj/ger/en/ihr/22294995.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Release ==
The film had its world premiere at the Young Filmmakers Forum section of [[21st Berlin International Film Festival|1971 Berlin International Film Festival]]. The US premiere took place in 1972 at the [[Museum of Modern Art]] in [[New York City]], the British premiere in the same year at the [[National Film Theatre]] in [[London]].<ref>{{cite book|title=The Ambivalence of Gay Liberation|periodical=|publisher=The Ambivalence of Gay Liberation: Male Homosexual Politics in 1970s West Germany, Craig Griffiths, [[Oxford University Press]], 2021, p. 80|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ReAeEAAAQBAJ&dq=praunheim+new+york+museum+broadcasting&pg=PA80|url-status=|format=|access-date=2022-04-28|archive-url=|archive-date=|last=|date=25 February 2021|language=|pages=|isbn=978-0-19-263977-6|quote=}}</ref>

==Reception==
The film was attacked both by German conservatives, progressives and gay activists. Despite not being a very good film, according to [[Samuel Clowes Huneke]], it was revolutionary by conceiving homosexuality as a political identity.{{sfn|Huneke|2022|pp=125–126}} The film also spawned dozens of homosexual activism groups, to the left of older [[homophile]] organizations.{{sfn|Huneke|2022|pp=126–127}}

"Rosa von Praunheim is one of the world's most prolific gay filmmakers. His 1970 [1971] film ''It's Not the Homosexual Who is Perverse, But the Society in Which He Lives'' (''Nicht der Homosexuelle ist pervers, sondern die Situation, in der er lebt'') established him as the public trailblazer for the modern gay rights movement." ([[Academy of Arts, Berlin|Academy of Arts]])<ref name="RosavonPraunheim">{{Cite web|url=https://www.adk.de/en/projects/2018/mikesch-praunheim-schroeter/exhibition/rosa-von-praunheim.htm|title=Rosa von Praunheim|access-date=2022-04-28|work=[[Academy of Arts, Berlin|Academy of Arts]], 2018|archive-date=2022-03-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220305180747/https://www.adk.de/en/projects/2018/mikesch-praunheim-schroeter/exhibition/rosa-von-praunheim.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> "It is a personal liberation for Holger Mischwitzky [Rosa von Praunheim] - and a wake-up call for all homosexual men. [...] With this film, Rosa von Praunheim became the icon of the gay and lesbian movement in Germany almost overnight." ([[Deutsche Welle]])<ref name="GayLiberation">{{Cite web|url=https://learngerman.dw.com/de/filmemacher-und-paradiesvogel-rosa-von-praunheim-wird-75/a-41510287
|title=Rosa von Praunheim wird 75|access-date=2022-04-28 |work= [[Deutsche Welle]], 2017}}</ref> "Rosa von Praunheim's film made an epoch." ([[Frankfurter Rundschau]])<ref name="FR">{{Cite web|url=https://www.fr.de/kultur/gesellschaft/schwulendemo-das-bis-dahin-selbstverstaendlichste-wird-in-frage-gestellt-91507908.html|title=Schwulendemo: Das bis dahin Selbstverständlichste wird in Frage gestellt|date=28 April 2022|access-date=2022-04-28|work=[[Frankfurter Rundschau]]}}</ref>  "Praunheim became the spokesperson of politically active gays. Nearly all programs concerned about gay issues sought his presence." ([[Schwules Museum|Gay Museum]])<ref name="SchwulesMuseum">{{Cite web|url=https://www.schwulesmuseum.de/ausstellung/rosa-geht-in-rente-hommage-zu-rosa-von-praunheims-65-geburtstag/?lang=en|title=Rosa is retiring – Tribute to the 65th birthday of Rosa von Praunheim|access-date=2022-04-28|work=[[Schwules Museum|Gay Museum]], 2007}}</ref> The American film critic Joe Hoeffner wrote in an article about the twelve most important queer films: "Many films have been called revolutionary, but ''It Is Not the Homosexual…'' truly earns that description. The breakout film by director and activist Rosa von Praunheim (aka Holger Mischwitzky) became a foundational text of the German gay rights movement, and its call for liberation reverberated through the history of queer cinema.“<ref name="Joe Hoeffner">{{cite web|title=12 Films that Chart the History of New Queer Cinema|url=https://collider.com/best-new-queer-cinema-movies-history-paris-is-burning-the-watermelon-woman/|work=Joe Hoeffner, collider.com|date=20 August 2022|access-date=2022-08-31|archive-date=2022-09-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220902001226/https://collider.com/best-new-queer-cinema-movies-history-paris-is-burning-the-watermelon-woman/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The American [[LGBT]] activist and [[History of film|film historian]] [[Vito Russo]] wrote in a public letter to Rosa von Praunheim on the occasion of the film's upcoming US premiere: "[…] Your film deserves to be shown here in America. The gay and straight community alike here must be made aware that these things are being discussed and presented elsewhere in the world. It is only through constant exposure and expression that we can ever hope to overcome what has for so many years, for too many years, been an irrational taboo. When people spend their lives fighting for a cause in which they believe, it gives them immeasurable comfort to know that there are others fighting for freedom of expression both in their lives and in their art." (Signed February 18, 1972, kept in the [[Museum of Modern Art|MoMA Archive]])
