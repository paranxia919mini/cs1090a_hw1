'''''Happyend''''' (stylized in [[all caps]])<ref>{{cite web |title=映画 HAPPYEND (2024) |url=https://www.allcinema.net/cinema/396028 |publisher=allcinema |access-date=23 April 2025}}</ref> is a 2024 [[Dystopian film|dystopian]] [[Coming-of-age story|coming-of-age]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama]] film written and directed by [[Neo Sora]]. Set in a near-future [[Tokyo]], it stars Kurihara Hayato and Hidaka Yukito as two high school best friends whose friendship is tested before graduation. [[Ayumu Nakajima]], [[Makiko Watanabe]], and [[Shirō Sano]] appear in supporting roles. 

The film premiered at the [[81st Venice International Film Festival]] on September 2, 2024, in the [[Orizzonti]] section. It was released in Japan on October 4, 2024, by Bitters End, and was released in the United States on September 12, 2025, by [[Film Movement]].

==Premise==
Set in near-future Tokyo, two best friends are about to graduate high school, while threats of an earthquake loom. One night, they pull a prank on their principal, leading to a surveillance system being installed. They respond in contrasting ways.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/magnify-venice-happyend-neo-soras-1236081423/|title=Magnify Boards Neo Sora's Venice-Bound Japanese Dystopian Film 'Happyend' (EXCLUSIVE)|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|first=Elsa|last=Keslassy|date=July 23, 2024|access-date=August 6, 2024|archive-date=September 3, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240903144108/https://variety.com/2024/film/news/magnify-venice-happyend-neo-soras-1236081423/|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Themes ==
''Happyend'' explores how political unrest and personal relationships intersect. The film centers on a friendship that fractures as one character becomes politically active while the other withdraws. Director Neo Sora contrasts small, personal moments with broader social shifts, touching on themes such as surveillance, youth disillusionment, and state control. The story also engages with questions of Japanese identity, [[xenophobia]], and historical amnesia, particularly regarding [[Japanese colonial empire|Japan's colonial past]]. Underground music scenes and resistance to [[Artificial intelligence|AI]]-generated culture reflect the characters’ pushback against commercialization and conformity. The film also addresses the limited political agency of marginalized groups, including Japan's [[Koreans in Japan|Zainichi Korean]] community.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ramachandran |first=Naman |date=2025-03-15 |title=Neo Sora Talks Political Divide in 'Happyend': 'Japan Hasn't Really Reflected on its Colonial Past' |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/news/neo-sora-happyend-interview-1236338227/ |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|98|3=53|4=Balancing sharp social satire with tender adolescent drama, ''Happyend'' is a crisp and heartfelt story that quietly captures the enormous emotional stakes of youth on the cusp of adulthood.|ref=yes|access-date=4 January 2026}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 75 out of 100, based on 10 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Happyend'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/happyend |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=16 October 2024 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]{{'}}s'' David Rooney praised ''Happyend'' for its "expert tonal balance between the bittersweet, elegiac qualities of the end-of-school drama" and its portrayal of an educational institution "that becomes like a prison," reflecting broader political anxieties. He noted the film's "light yet lingering touch to larger fears affecting all of us" while remaining grounded in the personal. Rooney also highlighted Bill Kirstein's cinematography for "finding poetry in the stark urban landscapes," and noted that [[Lia Ouyang Rusli|Lia Ouyang Rusli's]] score complemented the visuals. He described the mostly newcomer cast as "naturals."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rooney |first=David |date=2024-09-02 |title='Happyend' Review: High School Becomes a Microcosm of Surveillance-State Oppression in Affecting Near-Future Drama |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/happyend-review-neo-sora-japan-1235988818/ |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref> Writing for ''[[Screen International|Screen Daily]]'', Jonathan Romney described the film as "a crisp and understated piece" and highlighted Bill Kirstein's cinematography for its use of "nocturnal cityscapes" and "sterile empty spaces" within school settings. He also noted the young cast's "organic energy," though remarked they were "unconvincingly a little heavy on the pop star looks."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Romney |first=Jonathan |title='Happyend': Venice Review |url=https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/happyend-venice-review/5196808.article |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=[[Screen Daily]] |language=en}}</ref>

''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]''<nowiki/>'s Marshall Shaffer rated the film 2 out of 4 stars, writing that the director "struggles to balance the immediacy of adolescent angst with the long-range outlook of using the students’ experience as a canary in the coal mine for society at large." The review noted that ''Happyend'' "never quite satisfies as a microcosmic analysis of Japan" due to limited depiction of the world outside the school. While describing the film as a "confident narrative feature debut" with insight into generational and national direction, Shaffer notes that it lacks "connective tissue between the small details and Sora's bigger-picture views."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Shaffer |first=Marshall |date=2024-09-24 |title='Happyend' Review: It's Only a Teenage Wasteland |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/happyend-review-neo-sora/ |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=Slant Magazine |language=en-US}}</ref> Whang Yee Ling of The Straits Times gave it 3 out of 5 stars, describing it as "unassuming but emotionally textured."<ref>{{Cite news |date=2025-05-07 |title=At The Movies: K-occult thriller Holy Night: Demon Hunters packs a punch, a bittersweet Happyend |url=https://www.straitstimes.com/life/entertainment/at-the-movies-k-occult-thriller-holy-night-demon-hunters-packs-a-punch-a-bittersweet-happyend |access-date=2025-05-18 |work=The Straits Times |language=en |issn=0585-3923}}</ref> The Guardian voted the film as one of the best to be released in the U.K. in 2025, calling it "beguiling debut feature set in an oppressive near-future Japan."<ref name="Guardian 2025-12-10">{{cite news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/ng-interactive/2025/dec/08/the-50-best-films-of-2025-in-the-uk |title=The 50 best films of 2025 in the UK: 50 to 21 |work=Guardian |publisher=Guardian |date= 2025-12-10 }}</ref>
