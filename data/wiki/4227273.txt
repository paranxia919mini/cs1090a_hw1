'''''Elvira: Mistress of the Dark''''' is a 1988 American [[comedy horror]] film directed by [[James Signorelli]], starring [[Cassandra Peterson]] as eccentric [[horror host]] Elvira. The film's screenplay, written by Peterson, [[John Paragon]], and Sam Egan,<ref>{{cite news|title= 'Elvira' Matches Films She Introduces on TV |work= Los Angeles Times|date=1988-09-30|url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-09-30-ca-3005-story.html|access-date=2010-08-24 | first=Kevin | last=Thomas}}</ref> follows Elvira inheriting a house nestled in the heart of an overtly prudish community.

Production of the film began following Peterson's success playing Elvira on the television program ''[[Elvira's Movie Macabre]]'' (1981-1986). She enlisted the aid of numerous writers and actors who she had previously worked with in the comedy troupe [[The Groundlings]]. Filming took place from January to March 1988.

''Elvira: Mistress of the Dark'' was released on September 30, 1988. It received mixed reviews, with critics variously praising or deriding its writing and humor. Peterson was nominated for a [[Saturn Award for Best Actress]] as well as a [[Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Actress]]. The film was a [[box office bomb]] upon release, grossing $5.5 million against a budget of $7.5 million. It has since attained a cult following, and remastered versions have been released on home media. A second film, ''[[Elvira's Haunted Hills]]'', was released in 2001, with Peterson reprising her role.

==Plot==
[[Female body shape|Buxom]] Los Angeles TV [[horror host]]ess Elvira, Mistress of the Dark, quits her job after the station's new owner sexually harasses her. She plans to open an act at the [[Flamingo Las Vegas|Flamingo Hotel]] in Las Vegas, but needs $50,000 for the project. Upon learning she is a [[beneficiary]] of her deceased great-aunt Morgana Talbot, she travels to Fallwell, Massachusetts, to claim the inheritance, which includes a mansion, a recipe book, and Morgana's pet [[poodle]], Algonquin, which Elvira renames Gonk. Unbeknownst to Elvira, the recipe book is actually a [[spellbook]], and Gonk is an [[Familiar|animal familiar]] with the ability to shapeshift.

In Fallwell, Elvira's worldly attitude and revealing clothes set the conservative town council, led by Chastity Pariah, against her, but theater operator Bob Redding befriends her. The town's teenagers quickly accept her, to the chagrin of their parents, who consider her a bad influence. Bowling alley owner Patty is interested in Bob, and at Elvira's late-night horror film festival at Bob's theater, she succeeds in humiliating Elvira. Elvira struggles to sell the house so she can depart for Las Vegas.

Meanwhile, Elvira is unaware that her harsh but seemingly harmless uncle, Vincent Talbot, is actually a [[warlock]] who is obsessed with obtaining Morgana's spellbook. He offers Elvira $50 for the book. When he visits Morgana's house to buy it from Elvira, Gonk hides it much to Vincent's dismay. He plans to kill Elvira and conquer the world, and has been fueling the townspeople's hostility.

Elvira tries to impress Bob with a home-cooked dinner, but mistakenly uses the spellbook as a cookbook and summons a creature that attacks them. Elvira later discovers a letter that reveals the magical prowess of her mother, great-aunt, and uncle, as well as herself. She also finds a magical ring that can be used for protection.

When Elvira tries to re-conjure the creature against the Morality Club at their picnic for revenge, she prepares the brew incorrectly and it instead has an [[aphrodisiac]] effect; the adults begin behaving sexually, dancing and removing their clothing while Elvira observes nearby. She is accosted by Vincent, who again offers to buy the book for a much higher price of $500, which Elvira refuses this time. When Patty confronts Elvira, the resulting fistfight ends up humiliating Patty by revealing that her bra is stuffed.

Vincent leads the townspeople in arresting Elvira for [[witchcraft]], which is still illegal in the state. They decide to [[Death by burning|burn her at the stake]]. The teenagers try to free her from jail but instead accidentally lock themselves into a different cell. Bob tries to recover the spellbook from the mansion, but is tied up by Vincent and his goons, who take the book. Gonk transforms into a rat and successfully frees Bob by gnawing through his bonds.

Elvira is tied to a stake and the fire is lit, but she uses Morgana's ring to summon a rainstorm that quenches the flames; she then escapes with Bob. At the mansion, Elvira and Vincent engage in a magical battle that sets fire to the house. Elvira successfully banishes Vincent to the [[underworld]], while the house and all of the magical artifacts are destroyed.

The next day, Elvira prepares to leave town. The townspeople apologize for their behavior, and they ask Elvira to stay. She kisses Bob, but as she is homeless, she insists that she must leave. As his sole living relative, Elvira has inherited Vincent's estate, which allows her to open her show in Las Vegas, where she performs a lavishly produced musical number.

And he could!}}</ref> and he was convinced that he got the part due to a genuinely stunned, naive reaction that he had to one of Peterson's off-color remarks.<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=Daniel Greene: As I chatted with them and they told me about the role of Bob, Cassandra said something to me that was rather outrageous, and I think the expression on my face, my look of shock and innocence... [[Kurt Fuller]], who was cast as Fallwell's realtor, Harold Glotter, was at the time working as an actual realtor to support himself.<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=It is interesting that I played Harold Glotter, realtor, and I was Kurt Fuller, realtor. I think I still had my toe in real estate. I used ''none'' of my real estate experience for the role!}}</ref> He was so convinced that he had bombed his audition that he called his agent right after to declare that he was quitting acting. Later that day, his agent called to say that he had been offered the role.<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=I call my agent and he says, "How'd it go?" I go, "I quit, I'm out of show business. It was ''terrible''! They didn't laugh, everything I did bombed." An hour later, he called back and said, "Well, they offered you the job, but I told them you quit show business."}}</ref>

The role of Randy was narrowed down to two actors: [[Kris Kamm]] and [[Brad Pitt]]. Kamm won the part because Peterson felt Pitt was so handsome that Elvira would ignore Bob and fawn over him.<ref name="TooMacabre"/><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Irvin |first=Sam |date=2018 |title=Elvira Reveals All |url=https://screemag.com/backissues.php |magazine=Screem |issue=36 |location= |publisher=Screem Publishing |access-date= |quote=I kept notes on everybody{{snd}} and the comment I wrote next to Brad's name was "Yum-Yum." He was so freakin' cute, and I thought, "If he's in the movie, Elvira would hit on him instead of the older hunk that she was supposed to swoon over."}}</ref>

===Filming===
The film was shot over a span of eight weeks,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://archive.org/details/pdfy-heG9yIIZBnZGKOLN/page/n23/mode/2up |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark Press Kit |last= |first=|date=1988 |website=Internet Archive |publisher=New World Pictures |access-date=2021-02-24 |quote=Filming took place over eight weeks on various sound stages and locations in the Southern California area.}}</ref> between January and March 1988.<ref>{{cite AV media |people=Cassandra Peterson|date=2017 |title=Audio Commentary with Cassandra Peterson, Edie McClurg, John Paragon |trans-title= |medium=Blu-Ray |language=English |format= |time= 01:33:50|location= |publisher=Arrow Video |quote=The whole thing took eight weeks to shoot. We started on January 20, 1988 and it went through March 18, 1988. }}</ref> The first scenes were shot at a bowling alley in [[La Crescenta-Montrose, California|Montrose, California]],<ref>{{cite web |url=https://archive.org/details/pdfy-heG9yIIZBnZGKOLN/page/n23/mode/2up |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark Press Kit |last= |first=|date=1988 |website=Internet Archive |publisher=New World Pictures |access-date=2021-02-24 |quote=Locations included the Montrose Lanes bowling alley in Montrose, California, which served as Patti's Tidy Bowl Lanes.}}</ref> and Peterson worried about beginning the production with her character's big monologue,<ref name="TooMacabre"/> but much bigger stresses were soon to follow.

One of the most problematic issues was Peterson's costar, Binnie,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://archive.org/details/pdfy-heG9yIIZBnZGKOLN/page/n51/mode/2up |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark Press Kit |last= |first=|date=1988 |website=Internet Archive |publisher=New World Pictures |access-date=2021-02-24 |quote=This newest and, literally, brightest Hollywood star is Binnie, the usually white male miniature poodle of many talents who is cast as Gonk (short for Algonquin), one-third of Elvira's inheritance.}}</ref> a temperamental poodle<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=Cassandra Peterson: Gonk was a really mean little bastard! I mean, I ''love'' dogs, I ''love'' animals, and every time I'm doing a scene with him, I wasn't really sure if he was just gonna bite my hand off if I pet him. I was always holding him and trying to smile, and inside I'm going, "I hope he doesn't bite me in the face."}}</ref> that did not seem to like anyone except his trainer.<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=Cassandra Peterson: He loved his trainer. He only had eyes for her. Everybody else, he's just like, "I hate you!" He just loved her.}}</ref> Peterson would not allow them to use a permanent dye on the dog's fur, so instead a vegetable dye mixture that had to be touched up and reapplied daily was used.<ref>{{Cite AV media notes |title=Too Macabre: The Making of Elvira |title-link= |others= |year=2018  |publisher=Arrow Video |id= |location= |quote=Cassandra Peterson: I insisted that the dye be vegetable dye because it's not good to dye animals with hair color. They had to redo it every morning because half of it had faded away, so they loved me for that.}}</ref> The dog had trouble following instructions, resulting in missed cues and costly retakes. The dog also bit Kurt Fuller's ankle too hard, leaving permanent scars on the actor, and certain scenes had to dubbed over to mask the trainer's commands.<ref name="TooMacabre"/>

==Music==
The original [[Film score|musical score]] was composed by [[James B. Campbell]]. Although there were several well-known songs in the film, licensing the original recordings was cost-prohibitive, so they were covered by stock singers.<ref>{{cite AV media |people=Patterson Lundquist |date=2017 |title=Audio Commentary  |time=40:47 |location= |publisher=Arrow Video |quote=All of the music in this film, the vocals were re-recorded. It was cheaper.}}</ref> The goofy ditty "Chicken Fried Steak", which is faintly heard at the gas station, was an original composition written and performed by [[Gary Austin]], the founder of The Groundlings.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://twitter.com/therealelvira/status/1254446450483707904?lang=en |title=Tweet|last= |first=|date=2020-04-26|website=Twitter|publisher=The Real Elvira|access-date=2021-02-25 |quote=The song was made for the movie. Written and performed by Gary Austin, who was the founder of the @groundlings.}}</ref>

There was no soundtrack album and the majority of songs have never been issued, but "Here I Am" and two different versions of Lori Chako's "Once Bitten, Twice Shy" eventually surfaced on the digital compilation ''Elvira's Gravest Hits''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.discogs.com/Elvira-Elviras-Gravest-Hits/master/988002 |title=Elvira – Elvira's Gravest Hits |author=<!--Not stated--> |date= |website=Discogs |publisher= |access-date=2021-02-24 |quote= |archive-date=2021-02-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210218045217/https://www.discogs.com/Elvira-Elviras-Gravest-Hits/master/988002 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical reception===
<!--{{As of|September 2025}}-->{{Rt prose|58||26|A gothic, campy, raunchy comedy elevated by Cassandra Peterson's iconic persona yet driven off course by one-note jokes, this isn't the best — or worst — introduction to ''Elvira, Mistress of the Dark''.}}<ref>{{cite web|website=Rotten Tomatoes|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark (1988)|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/elvira_mistress_of_the_dark/|access-date=September 5, 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 43 out of 100, based on 5 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Elvira: Mistress of the Dark'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date= |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark |access-date=September 5, 2025|publisher=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref>

[[Caryn James]] in ''[[The New York Times]]'' rated the film 2/5 in 1988 saying that "there are only a couple of fresh and funny moments".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1988/09/30/movies/review-film-elvira-a-friendly-witch.html?mtrref=www.google.com&gwh=6C21D9EEFE8675BBCD19131A1C2D5BE1&gwt=pay|title=Review/Film; 'Elvira,' a Friendly Witch|work=The New York Times|date=30 September 1988|access-date=November 9, 2021|last1=James|first1=Caryn|archive-date=9 November 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211109234422/https://www.nytimes.com/1988/09/30/movies/review-film-elvira-a-friendly-witch.html?mtrref=www.google.com&gwh=6C21D9EEFE8675BBCD19131A1C2D5BE1&gwt=pay|url-status=live}}</ref> Writing for ''[[The Washington Post]]'' in 1999, Richard Harrington said the film "is stupid fun, a distaff, gothic version of ''[[Pee-wee's Playhouse]]''. (...) [Elvira is] fun, a [[Transylvania]] [[Valley girl|Valley Girl]] grown up into the Queen of [[B movie|the Bs]], but after 96 minutes you may start thinking more fondly about those '50s and '60s camp classics she's usually interspersed with."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/elviramistressofthedarkpg13harrington_a0aa98.htm|title='Elvira, Mistress of the Dark'|last=Harrington|first=Richard|date=|newspaper=The Washington Post|access-date=November 9, 2021|archive-date=October 27, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221027053209/https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/elviramistressofthedarkpg13harrington_a0aa98.htm|url-status=live}}</ref>

Anthony Arrigo wrote in [[Dread Central]] in 2020 that "Elvira managed to make a seemingly [[oxymoron]]ic character into a household name, built off her bountiful looks, quick wit, and indefatigable charisma."<ref name=DC>{{cite news|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/reviews/336295/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark-blu-ray-review-the-peaks-of-horror-comedy/|title=ELVIRA: MISTRESS OF THE DARK Blu-ray Review – The Peaks Of Horror Comedy|last=|first=|date=|website=Dread Central|access-date=November 9, 2021}}</ref> In ''[[Nashville Scene]]'' in 2021, Jason Shawhan said it's "campy, witty and always eager to push the bawdy limits of a PG-13 rating".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nashvillescene.com/arts_culture/film_tv/hindi-horror-killer-klowns-elvira-and-more-now-available-to-stream/article_d715c275-dea8-568c-ad2c-dff3d9f13412.html|title=Hindi Horror, Killer Klowns, Elvira and More, Now Available to Stream|last=|first=|date=|website=Nashville Scene|access-date=November 9, 2021}}</ref> On ''[[Common Sense Media]]'', Kat Halstead wrote in 2024 that "It may fall into the 'so bad it's good' category (...) but this cult horror comedy is smarter than it first appears."<ref>{{cite news|last1=|first1=|title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark|url=https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark|access-date=November 22, 2024|publisher=Common Sense Media|date=}}</ref>

Peterson later said that New World Pictures' bankruptcy during the film's release severely curtailed its theatrical rollout, claiming it went from a planned 2,500 or 3,000 theaters to 150.<ref>{{cite web |last=O'Neal |first=Sean |title=Elvira, Mistress Of The Dark (and merchandising) |url=https://www.avclub.com/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark-and-merchandising-1798218122
 |website=The A.V. Club |date=October 28, 2009 |access-date=March 3, 2026}}</ref> Box Office Mojo noted the film's widest release was 627 theaters.<ref>{{cite web |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2823915009/weekend/

==Legacy==
===1990s–2000s: Proposed sequel, and ''Elvira's Haunted Hills'' ===
Peterson quickly sold a script for a [[sequel]], but it became tied up in red tape when [[Carolco Pictures]] went bankrupt.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=|first=|date=November 2001|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark|url= |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=10 |issue=5 |location= |publisher=|access-date= |quote=I wrote the script for another Elvira movie right after ''Elvira, Mistress of the Dark'' was released. I sold it to a company. They went bankrupt and took the script down with them.}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine  |last=Schiff |first=Laura |date=January 1998|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark|magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=6 |issue=7 |location= |publisher= |url=https://archive.org/details/Femme_Fatales_v06n07/page/n27/mode/2up |access-date=2021-02-25 |quote=I went to make a second movie very soon after the first one, and I got a deal with Carolco Pictures. I wrote the movie and they bought it and then they went bankrupt. And my movie got stuck in bankruptcy... I couldn't get the script back from Carolco.}}</ref> She followed through with plans to star in a sitcom, but 1993's ''The Elvira Show'' did not secure a spot on the TV schedule. Soon after, she announced the forthcoming ''Elvira vs. the Vampire Women'',<ref>{{cite magazine |last=|first=|date=Summer 1994|title=Attractions Fatale|url=https://archive.org/details/Femme_Fatales_v03n01/page/n3/mode/2up |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=3 |issue=1 |location= |publisher=|access-date= |quote=Undaunted, Peterson is shopping the pilot to other networks while seeking finance for her next film project, tentatively titled ELVIRA VS. THE VAMPIRE  WOMEN.}}</ref> but a contract dispute with [[Roger Corman]] prevented the film from being produced.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=|first=|date=November 2001|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark|url= |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=10 |issue=5 |location= |publisher=|access-date= |quote=So I was back to square one and decided to go into negotiations with Roger Corman on ''ELVIRA AND THE VAMPIRE WOMEN''. As much as I love Roger, I would've ended up making $1.50 an hour with the contract he wanted me to sign. He would have retained the rights and made all of the money from it.}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Schiff|first=Laura|date=January 1998|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=6 |issue=7 |location= |publisher= |url=https://archive.org/details/Femme_Fatales_v06n07/page/n27/mode/2up |access-date=2021-02-25 |quote=I talked to Roger Corman about making the sequel. We talked about it and talked about it over the years. I was always having meetings with him, and yes, ''great'', he wanted to do it, and it just kind of never happened. The amount of money was prohibitive for him.}}</ref>

The script for ''[[Elvira's Haunted Hills]]'' was written in the late 1990s, but after spending three years trying to get Hollywood to produce the project, she and then-husband Mark Pierson decided to finance it themselves.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=|first=|date=November 2001|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark|url= |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=10 |issue=5 |location= |publisher=|access-date= |quote=After finishing the script, the team of Peterson and Pierson shopped it around to companies for over three years before finally coming to the conclusion that they would have to make the movie themselves or surrender the project altogether.}}</ref> The shoot in Romania was grueling, and they had difficulty securing distribution. In the same vein as ''[[Young Frankenstein]]'', ''Haunted Hills'' spoofs the 1960s Roger Corman/[[Edgar Allan Poe]] films. Other than the Elvira character, there's no direct connection between the films, although it is sometimes referred to as a [[prequel]] since it is set more than a century earlier.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=|first=|date=November 2001|title=Elvira, Mistress of the Dark|url= |magazine=Femme Fatales |volume=10 |issue=5 |location= |publisher=|access-date= |quote=The first thing she fixed on was that HAUNTED HILLS should be a prequel, not a sequel: "I decided I wanted it set in Carpathia in the year 1851, so it's already funny from the opening scene that Elvira looks exactly the same as she does today".}}</ref>

===2010s–present: Netflix, Shudder, and Arrow 4K blu-ray ===
Prior to ''Mistress of the Dark'', there were already plans to feature Elvira in an animated series,<ref>{{cite AV media |people=Eric Gardner |date=2018 |title=Too Macabre - The Making of Elvira: Mistress of the Dark |time=42:30 |publisher=Arrow Video  |quote=We had aspirations to do a Saturday morning animated series, so we wanted to make sure she didn't care any too-risquee baggage with her.}}</ref> but this has yet to materialize. In 2019, Peterson pitched the idea to [[Netflix]] and [[Shudder (streaming service)|Shudder]], which both passed.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://comicbook.com/horror/news/elvira-cassandra-peterson-animation-project-mistress-of-the-dark-sequel-movie/ |title=Elvira Actress Clarifies Reports of Streaming Project, Teases New Movie on the Way |author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2019-09-13 |website= |publisher= |access-date=2021-02-25 |quote="I pitched my animation project to Netflix and Shudder and they both passed on it, because they're really not into doing animation", Peterson confirmed.}}</ref> Around the same time, the character made her inaugural [[Scooby-Doo]] appearance in ''[[Scooby-Doo! Return to Zombie Island|Return to Zombie Island]]''. Elvira returned the following year with an increased role in ''[[Happy Halloween, Scooby-Doo!]]'', in which she spends the bulk of in a flat-top variation of the Macabremobile and dispatches another monster with a stiletto to the forehead.

Peterson's ''Scooby'' experience was so positive that, after years of trying to get a third film produced, she announced in 2020 that the next Elvira film will likely be an animated feature.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://movieweb.com/elvira-mistress-of-the-dark-2-animated-movie/ |title=Elvira: Mistress of the Dark 2 May Become an Animated Movie |author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2020-10-22 |website= |publisher= |access-date=2021-02-25 |quote="I haven't done anything in animation until this Scooby-Doo thing as the character and the character lends itself so well to comic books and animation, she really is kind of a cartoon character anyway. I think that's the way it's going to go.}}</ref> Although no specifics have been released, a detailed story treatment has been completed.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://toofab.com/2020/10/22/elvira-movie-animated-not-live-action/ |title=Why The Next Elvira Movie Will Probably Be Animated (Exclusive) |author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2020-10-22 |website= |publisher= |access-date=2021-02-25 |quote="I have written a treatment and I just collaborated with someone, another writer, on a little more detailed treatment. I am honestly thinking that it is gonna go the way of animation now. I think it may have gone past the point of me wanting to do a live movie as Elvira", she told TooFab.}}</ref>

[[Arrow Films|Arrow Video]] has delivered an accurate reproduction of the [[35 mm movie film|35 mm]] experience."<ref name=DC/> Arrow's [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] had a commercial release on November 12, 2024.<ref>{{cite news|date=September 3, 2024
