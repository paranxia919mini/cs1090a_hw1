'''''Boxing Gym''''' is a 2010 American documentary film edited, produced, and directed by [[Frederick Wiseman]]. The film premiered at the [[2010 Cannes Film Festival|63rd Cannes Film Festival]] on May 20, 2010.<ref name="Nelson">{{cite web|last=Nelson|first=Rob|title=Boxing Gym|url=https://variety.com/2010/film/markets-festivals/boxing-gym-1117942880/|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|accessdate=August 23, 2019|date=May 20, 2010}}</ref>

==Critical response==
''Boxing Gym'' received critical acclaim from critics. [[Manohla Dargis]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' designated it an "NYT Critics Pick", and stated that "It's... easy to get swept up by the beats in the film because Mr. Wiseman, among the most celebrated direct-cinema practitioners, eschews voiceovers, talking-head interviews, extraneous footage and the customary and sometimes superfluous like."<ref>{{cite web|last=Dargis|first=Manohla|author-link=Manohla Dargis|title=Sweat, Blood and Philosophy to the Rhythms of the Sweet Science|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2010/10/22/movies/22box.html|website=[[The New York Times]]|accessdate=August 23, 2019|date=October 21, 2010}}</ref> Rob Nelson of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' also praised the film, noting that while it is shorter than most of Wiseman's works, "the viewer learns an enormous amount from [''Boxing Gym'',] but not at all in the conventional documentary manner", and instead through "images that put the audience in close contact with the boxers’ routines". Eric Kohn of ''[[IndieWire]]'' remarked that Wiseman's filmmaking method resulted in "an ethnographic snapshot of restless people in their natural habitat."<ref>{{cite web|last=Kohn|first=Eric|title=REVIEW &#124; Sweat Ballet: Frederick Wiseman's "Boxing Gym"|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2010/10/review-sweat-ballet-frederick-wisemans-boxing-gym-244653/|website=[[IndieWire]]|publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation|Penske Business Media, LLC]]|accessdate=23 August 2019|date=October 18, 2010}}</ref>
