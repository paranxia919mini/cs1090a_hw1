'''''Everything Everywhere All at Once'''''{{efn|Commonly abbreviated as '''''EEAaO'''''}} is a 2022 American<!--PLEASE SEE TALK PAGE BEFORE ADDING A LIST OF GENRES--> [[Independent film|independent]] [[Absurdist fiction|absurdist]] [[comedy-drama]] film<!-- "independent" is not a genre and therefore does not ignore/contradict the previous notice; please stop removing --> written and directed by [[Daniels (directors)|Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert]],{{efn|Known collectively as Daniels}} who produced it with [[Russo brothers|Anthony and Joe Russo]] and [[Jonathan Wang]]. The film [[Hybrid genre|incorporates media from several genres]], including [[Surreal humour|surreal comedy]], [[science fiction]], [[fantasy film|fantasy]], [[martial arts films]], and [[migrant literature|immigrant narrative]].<ref name="impressive"/><ref name="eastereggs"/><ref name="NYTReview"/> [[Michelle Yeoh]] stars as [[Evelyn Quan Wang]], a [[Chinese Americans|Chinese-American]] immigrant who, while being [[Income tax audit|audited by the IRS]], discovers that she must connect with [[Parallel universes in fiction|parallel universe]] versions of herself to prevent a powerful being from destroying the [[multiverse]]. The film also stars [[Stephanie Hsu]], [[Ke Huy Quan]], [[James Hong]], and [[Jamie Lee Curtis]].

Kwan and Scheinert began work on the project in 2010, and began writing the film's script in 2016. Production was announced in 2018, and principal photography ran from January to March 2020. The works of [[Cinema of Hong Kong|Hong Kong film]] director [[Wong Kar-wai]], as well as the children's book ''[[Sylvester and the Magic Pebble]]'' and the video game ''[[Everything (video game)|Everything]],'' served as inspiration for several scenes. Also inspired by contemporary scholars such as philosopher [[Mary-Jane Rubenstein]],<ref name="f225">{{cite web |date=2024-03-06 |title=Everything Everywhere All at Wes: Daniel Kwan and Mary-Jane Rubenstein in Conversation |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=thd44IPj7AI |access-date=2024-06-20 |website=YouTube}}</ref> the film explores [[Philosophy|philosophical]] themes such as [[existentialism]], [[nihilism]], [[surrealism]], and [[absurdism]], as well as themes such as [[Neurodiversity|neurodivergence]], [[Major depression|depression]], [[Transgenerational trauma|generational trauma]], and [[Asian Americans|Asian American]] identity. [[Everything Everywhere All at Once (soundtrack)|Its soundtrack]] features compositions by [[Son Lux]], and collaborations with [[Mitski]], [[David Byrne]], [[André 3000]], [[Nine Days|John Hampson]], and [[Randy Newman]].

''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' premiered at [[2022 South by Southwest Film Festival|South by Southwest]] on March 11, 2022, and began a limited theatrical release in the United States on March 25, before a wide release by [[A24]] on April 8. It grossed $143 million against a $14–25 million budget. The film garnered widespread acclaim from critics and received [[List of accolades received by Everything Everywhere All at Once|numerous accolades]]. At the [[95th Academy Awards]], it won seven out of the eleven awards it was nominated for: [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] (Yeoh), [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]] (Quan), [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] (Curtis), [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] and [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]] for Kwan and Scheinert, and [[Academy Award for Best Film Editing|Best Editing]]. The film also won two [[80th Golden Globes|Golden Globe Awards]], five [[28th Critics' Choice Awards|Critics' Choice Awards]] (including [[Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]]), a [[76th British Academy Film Awards|BAFTA Award]], and a record four [[29th Screen Actors Guild Awards|SAG Awards]] (including [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture|Best Ensemble]]).

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. The current word count is 700 (using this website https://wordcounter.net/). -->

[[Evelyn Quan Wang]] is a middle-aged [[Chinese people|Chinese]] immigrant who runs a laundromat with her husband Waymond in [[Simi Valley, California]]. Two decades earlier, they eloped to the [[United States]] and had a daughter, Joy. In the present day, Evelyn struggles to manage the failing laundromat while navigating an impending [[Internal Revenue Service]] (IRS) audit. As pressures mount from both her personal life and the demands of assimilation in the United States, Evelyn finds herself overwhelmed by the expectations placed upon her. Waymond is attempting to serve her with [[divorce papers]] in an effort to spark a discussion about their marriage; her rigorous father (referred to as ''Gong Gong'', [[Cantonese]] for ''grandfather'')<ref name=":4"/> is increasingly irritable with Evelyn and her life; and she has a strained relationship with Joy, who is battling depression<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ruiz |first1=Rebecca |title='Everything Everywhere All At Once' has the best take on mental health you never expected |url=https://mashable.com/article/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-meaning |website=Mashable |date=March 13, 2023 |publisher=Ziff Davis |access-date=11 April 2023}}</ref> and has a non-Chinese girlfriend, Becky, whom Evelyn is reluctant to accept.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=费青雅 |date=2023 |title= |script-title=zh:存在主义视域下《瞬息全宇宙》的解读 |trans-title=The Interpretation of Everything Everywhere All at Once from the Perspective of Existentialism |url=https://doi.org/10.19395/j.cnki.1674-246x.2023.03.022 |journal=视听 |issue=3 |pages=62–65 |doi=10.19395/j.cnki.1674-246x.2023.03.022 |access-date=2025-04-29 |via=[[CNKI]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last=田苗 |date=2023 |title= |script-title=zh:荒诞与反抗——探析《瞬息全宇宙》的存在主义危机 |trans-title=Absurdity and Rebellion: An Analysis of the Existential Crisis in ''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' |url=https://doi.org/10.16129/j.cnki.mysdx.2023.06.023 |journal=美与时代（下） |publication-place=[[Zhengzhou University]] |issue=6 |pages=119–121 |doi=10.16129/j.cnki.mysdx.2023.06.023 |access-date=2025-04-29}}</ref>

At a tense meeting with IRS Revenue Agent Deirdre Beaubeirdre, Waymond's body is taken over by Alpha-Waymond, a version of Waymond from the "Alphaverse." Alpha-Waymond explains to Evelyn that many [[Parallel universes in fiction|parallel universe]]s exist (the "[[multiverse]]") because every life choice creates a new alternative universe. In the Alphaverse, the now-deceased Alpha-Evelyn developed "verse-jumping" technology, which enables people to access the skills, memories, and bodies of their parallel selves by performing bizarre, statistically unlikely actions. The multiverse is threatened by Jobu Tupaki, the Alpha-Joy whose mind was splintered after Alpha-Evelyn pushed her to verse-jump beyond her endurance. Jobu experiences all universes at once and can verse-jump and manipulate reality at will. Jobu has created a [[black hole]]-like "[[Everything bagel|Everything Bagel]]"<ref name="WSJReview">{{cite web |url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-jamie-lee-curtis-michelle-yeoh-daniel-kwan-daniel-scheinert-the-daniels-stephanie-hsu-ke-huy-quan-james-hong-11648158899 |title='Everything Everywhere All at Once' Review: A Maximal Take on the Absurd |last=Nicholson |first=Amy |date=March 24, 2022 |website=[[The Wall Street Journal]] |access-date=April 17, 2022 |archive-date=June 15, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220615144524/https://www.wsj.com/articles/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-jamie-lee-curtis-michelle-yeoh-daniel-kwan-daniel-scheinert-the-daniels-stephanie-hsu-ke-huy-quan-james-hong-11648158899 |url-status=live}}</ref> that forms a [[Torus|toroid]] [[Gravitational singularity|singularity]]<ref name="Lee 2022"/> that could destroy the multiverse.

Evelyn is provided verse-jumping technology to fight Jobu's minions, who are converging on the IRS building. She uncovers other universes in which she made different choices and flourished, such as becoming a [[kung fu]] master and film star. She also learns that Waymond intends to file for divorce. Alpha-Waymond believes that Evelyn, as the greatest "failure" of all Evelyns in the multiverse, possesses the untapped potential needed to defeat Jobu. Gong Gong is taken over by Alpha-Gong Gong, who instructs Evelyn to kill Joy to prevent Jobu from using her to access Evelyn's universe. Evelyn refuses and decides to face Jobu by acquiring powers through repeated verse-jumping. Alpha-Gong Gong, convinced that Evelyn's mind has been compromised like Jobu's, sends soldiers after Evelyn. While they fight, Jobu locates and kills Alpha-Waymond in the Alphaverse. As Jobu confronts Evelyn in her universe, Evelyn's mind begins to splinter, causing her to collapse.

Evelyn uncontrollably verse-jumps alongside Jobu across many bizarre and diverse universes. Jobu discloses she does not intend to fight, but that instead, she has been searching for an Evelyn who can see, as she does, that nothing matters. She teleports Evelyn to the Everything Bagel, divulging that she wants to use it to allow herself and Evelyn to truly die. Upon looking into the Bagel, Evelyn is initially persuaded, and behaves cruelly and [[Nihilism|nihilistically]] in her other universes, hurting those around her.

Just as Evelyn enters the Bagel with Jobu, she pauses to listen to Waymond's pleas in her universe for everybody to stop fighting and to instead practice kindness, even when life is senseless. Evelyn has an [[Existentialism|existentialist]] epiphany and decides to follow Waymond's [[Absurdism|absurdist]]<ref>{{cite web |last1=Perry |first1=Lauren |date=July 15, 2022 |title=Everything, Everywhere, Nihilism, and Absurdism, All At Once |url=https://movieweb.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-nihilism-absurdism/ |access-date=15 March 2023 |website=[[MovieWeb]]}}</ref> and [[humanitarian]] advice, utilizing her multiverse powers to fight with [[empathy]]<ref>{{cite web |last=Smith |first=Barrett Edwards |title=Fighting With Kindness: Is Empathy the Biggest New Movie Superpower? |url=https://gamerant.com/empathy-biggest-new-movie-superpower/ |website=Game Rant |date=February 4, 2023 |publisher=Valnet Inc. |access-date=April 4, 2023}}</ref> and bring happiness to those around her. In doing so, she repairs her damage in the other universes and neutralizes Alpha-Gong Gong and Jobu's fighters. In her home universe, Evelyn reconciles with Waymond, accepts Joy and Becky's relationship and divulges it to Gong Gong, while Waymond convinces Deirdre to let them redo their taxes. Jobu decides to enter the Bagel alone as, simultaneously in Evelyn's universe, Joy pleads with Evelyn to let her go. Evelyn tells Joy that even when nothing makes sense and even though she could be anywhere else in the multiverse, she will always want to be with Joy. Evelyn and the others save Jobu from the Bagel, and Evelyn and Joy embrace.

Sometime later, with the family's relationships improved, they return to the IRS building to refile their taxes. As Deirdre talks, Evelyn's attention is momentarily drawn to her alternative selves, before she grounds herself back in her home universe.

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Swiss Army Man Interview with Daniel Scheinert and Daniel Kwan (cropped).jpg|thumb|Writer-directors [[Daniels (directors)|Daniel Scheinert and Daniel Kwan]]]]

Co-directors Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert, known as the [[Daniels (directors)|Daniels]], began researching the concept of [[Parallel universes in fiction|the multiverse]] as early as 2010, after being exposed to the concept of [[modal realism]] in the [[Ross McElwee]] documentary ''[[Sherman's March (1986 film)|Sherman's March]]'' (1986).<ref name="Lee 2022">{{cite web |url=https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-onces-influences-explained.html |title=Daniels Unpack the Everything Bagel of Influences Behind ''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' |last=Lee |first=Chris |date=April 13, 2022 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |access-date=April 17, 2022 |archive-date=April 16, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220416174415/https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-onces-influences-explained.html |url-status=live}}</ref> Kwan described the release of the animated film ''[[Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse]]'' (2018), which also deals with a multiversal concept, as "a little upsetting because we were like, 'Oh shit, everyone's going to beat us to this thing we've been working on'."<ref name="Lee 2022"/> He also stated, "Watching the [[Rick and Morty season 2|second season]] of ''[[Rick and Morty]]'' (2013–present) was really painful. I was like, 'They've already done all the ideas we thought were original!' It was a really frustrating experience. So I stopped watching ''Rick and Morty'' while we were writing this project".<ref name="Lee 2022"/>

Script writing began at the end of 2016 according to Kwan and Scheinert.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kwan |first=Daniel M. |last2=Scheinert |first2=Daniel |date=April 8, 2022 |title=Miracle Work: A Note from the Daniels |url=https://a24films.com/notes/2022/04/miracle-work-a-note-from-daniels |access-date=July 12, 2026 |website=a24films.com |language=en}}</ref> In early drafts of the screenplay, the directors planned for the main character to be a professor and have undiagnosed [[attention deficit hyperactivity disorder]] (ADHD); through his research for the project, Kwan learned that he had undiagnosed ADHD.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pasternack |first=Alex |date=April 7, 2022 |title={{'}}''Everything Everywhere All at Once''{{'}} is a mesmerizing ode to our chaos |url=https://www.fastcompany.com/90738552/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-multiverse-internet |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220411213804/https://www.fastcompany.com/90738552/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-multiverse-internet |archive-date=April 11, 2022 |access-date=April 11, 2022 |website=[[Fast Company]]}}</ref>

Scenes in which Evelyn trains in martial arts and becomes an action film star were visually and contextually inspired by the films of [[Wong Kar-wai]]. Chris Lee of ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'' writes that they "conjur[e] a mood of exquisite romantic yearning that will be instantly recognizable … as touchstones" of Wong's works. <!-- why is this not in Critical response? -->The universe in which Evelyn and Joy are rocks was influenced by the children's book ''[[Sylvester and the Magic Pebble]]'' (1969) and the video game ''[[Everything (video game)|Everything]]'' (2017).<ref name="Lee 2022"/>

Kwan has said the idea of the everything bagel "started as just a [[throwaway joke]]", a play on a type of American bagel called an "[[everything bagel]]", which is baked with a large variety of toppings. Scheinert said they spent time attempting to develop the religion of bagel followers, but encountered complications: "Jobu Tupaki's a nihilist; should there be [[dogma]]? Should there be a [[Holy book|book]]? What should their practices be as a religion? The bagel stuck because it became such a useful, simple symbol that we could point to as filmmakers. And you don't have to explain it much beyond the joke."<ref name="Lee 2022"/><ref name="WSJReview"/>

===Casting===
[[File:Ke Huy Quan (43865682592).jpg|thumb|upright|[[Ke Huy Quan]], pictured in 2018, retired from acting in 2002 and returned shortly before being cast in this film.]]

The script was written for [[Jackie Chan]] until Kwan and Scheinert reconceived the protagonist as a woman, feeling it would make the husband–wife dynamic in the story more relatable.<ref name="Bergeson 2022">{{cite web |last=Bergeson |first=Samantha |date=March 15, 2022 |title=Michelle Yeoh's Role in 'Everything Everywhere All at Once' Was Originally Written for Jackie Chan |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2022/03/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-michelle-yeoh-jackie-chan-1234708097/|website=[[IndieWire]]|url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220424171436/https://www.indiewire.com/2022/03/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-michelle-yeoh-jackie-chan-1234708097/ |archive-date=April 24, 2022 |access-date=April 11, 2022}}</ref>

The new script's lead character was initially named Michelle Wang, after the film's lead actress [[Michelle Yeoh]], who said, "If you ask the Daniels, when they started on this draft, they focused on, 'Well, we are doing this for Michelle Yeoh.'"<ref>{{cite web|last=Smart|first=Jack|date=April 13, 2022|title=Michelle Yeoh on the roller coaster ride of Everything Everywhere All at Once|url=https://www.avclub.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-michelle-yeoh-interv-1848785883|website=[[The A.V. Club]]|publisher=[[G/O Media]]|access-date=April 17, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220417195646/https://www.avclub.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-michelle-yeoh-interv-1848785883|archive-date=April 17, 2022}}</ref> The character's name was eventually changed to Evelyn. With her resemblance to the version of Evelyn as a martial artist and film star,<ref name="Radulovic 2022">{{cite web|last=Radulovic|first=Petrana|date=April 11, 2022|title=Michelle Yeoh's personal guide to Everything Everywhere All at Once's vast multiverse|url=https://www.polygon.com/23020865/michelle-yeoh-interview-everything-everywhere-all-at-once|website=Polygon|access-date=April 17, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220417195646/https://www.polygon.com/23020865/michelle-yeoh-interview-everything-everywhere-all-at-once|archive-date=April 17, 2022}}</ref> Yeoh opposed naming the character Michelle. "Evelyn deserves her own story to be told. This is a very ordinary mother [and] housewife who is trying her best to be a good mother to her daughter, a good daughter to her father, a wife that's trying to keep the family together […] I don't like to integrate me, Michelle Yeoh, into the characters that I play, because they all deserve their own journey and their stories to be told".<ref name="Radulovic 2022"/>

It was announced in August 2018 that Yeoh and [[Awkwafina]] had been cast to star in what was described as an "interdimensional action film" from Kwan and Scheinert, with [[Russo brothers|Anthony and Joe Russo]] attached to produce.<ref>{{cite web|last=Galuppo|first=Mia|date=August 30, 2018|title=Michelle Yeoh, Awkwafina in Talks for Film From 'Swiss Army Man' Directors|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/michelle-yeoh-awkwafina-reunite-film-swiss-army-man-directors-1138633|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=August 30, 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210411074205/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/michelle-yeoh-awkwafina-reunite-film-swiss-army-man-directors-1138633|archive-date=April 11, 2021}}</ref> Awkwafina left the project in January 2020 due to scheduling conflicts, and was replaced with [[Stephanie Hsu]]. James Hong, [[Ke Huy Quan]], and Jamie Lee Curtis joined the cast. It marked Quan's return to film acting, from which he had retired after ''[[Second Time Around (film)|Second Time Around]]'' (2002) due to a lack of casting opportunities.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Collis |first=Clark |date=February 24, 2022 |title=Whatever happened to Short Round? They were curious to learn what Quan had been doing, and learned that he was the right age to portray Waymond. Coincidentally, Quan had returned to acting, inspired by the success of ''[[Crazy Rich Asians (film)|Crazy Rich Asians]]'' (2018), soon before he was approached for the role.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wong |first=Stevie |date=December 2, 2022 |title='Everything Everywhere All At Once' Star Ke Huy Quan On Getting A Second Chance To Pursue His Dream: "I'm Always Grateful" |url=https://deadline.com/2022/12/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-ke-huy-quan-interview-1235182150/ |access-date=January 11, 2023 |website=Deadline Hollywood|archive-date=December 12, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221212175446/https://deadline.com/2022/12/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-ke-huy-quan-interview-1235182150/ |url-status=live}}</ref> This is a result of a [[domino effect]] where the director [[Jon M. Chu]] was inspired to create ''Crazy Rich Asians'' because of [[John Cho|#StarringJohnCho]], a movement about Asian representation in leading roles in Hollywood.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2019-02-21 |title=Jon M. Kwan talked about [[Whitewashing in film|whitewashing]] with studios, "The casting was probably one of the hardest things of the whole process. They're like, 'Oh, should we try a white actor?'… or 'Should we find other types of people that might actually bring in the money?'" He continued, "We had to have some really hard conversations with people to basically put our foot down and say, 'No, this was written for a Chinese family.'" Scheinert said that while it took a little longer, they ultimately ended up with an ideal cast.<ref>{{cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EYf31UGTuwY&t=293s |title=Oscar-nominated duo "The Daniels" on the success of "Everything Everywhere All At Once" |date=2023-03-04 |last=CBS Mornings |time=4:53}}</ref>

For Deirdre's appearance, Kwan discovered a picture of a real IRS agent he found online, which Curtis liked and wanted to emulate. Curtis wanted the character to be as "real" as possible and used her real belly for the film, as opposed to a prosthetic.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Collis |first=Clark |date=March 10, 2022 |title=Jamie Lee Curtis on why she wanted to look as 'real' as possible in Everything Everywhere All at Once |url=https://ew.com/movies/jamie-lee-curtis-explains-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-look/ |access-date=March 20, 2023 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]}}</ref>

===Visual effects===
Visual effects post-production for the film was done in-house, after the Daniels' negative experience with a dedicated post-production studio for their 2016 film ''[[Swiss Army Man]]''.<ref name="vfx">{{Cite web |last=Hemphill |first=Jim |date=April 15, 2022 |title=Less Marvel, More 'Ghostbusters': Behind the Handmade Visual Effects of 'Everything Everywhere All at Once' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-visual-effects-1234716610/|website=IndieWire|url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230313072323/https://www.indiewire.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-visual-effects-1234716610/ |archive-date=March 13, 2023 |access-date=March 13, 2023}}</ref> Instead, the filmmakers assembled a small team of eight artists headed by Zak Stoltz, who produced visual effects using [[Adobe After Effects]] and [[Adobe Premiere Pro]], and used [[Resilio Sync]] to share the large amounts of data once the pandemic hit.<ref name="vfx"/><ref name="tangcay-2023">{{Cite news |last=Tangcay |first=Jazz |date=2023-02-22 |title='Hollywood 2.0': How the Rise of AI Tools Like Runway Are Changing Filmmaking |url=https://variety.com/2023/artisans/news/artificial-intelligence-runway-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-1235532322/ |access-date=2025-06-05 |work=Variety}}</ref>

For the rock universe scene, [[Runway (company)|Runway AI]] was used to automate the process of [[rotoscoping]] shots filmed on a green screen, creating [[Matte (filmmaking)|mattes]] that then allowed the rocks to be combined with other imagery.<ref name="tangcay-2023" />

==Thematic analysis==
''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' incorporates elements from a number of genres and film mediums, including [[Surreal humour|surreal comedy]], [[Science fiction film|science fiction]], [[Fantasy film|fantasy]], [[martial arts film]]s and [[animation]].<ref name="impressive"/><ref name="eastereggs">{{Cite web |last=Izay |first=Ryan |date=April 9, 2022 |title=Everything Everywhere All At Once Easter Eggs & References |url=https://screenrant.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-easter-eggs-references/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220509183344/https://screenrant.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-easter-eggs-references/amp/ |archive-date=May 9, 2022 |access-date=May 9, 2022 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref><ref name="NYTReview">{{cite news|last=Scott|first=A. O.|author-link=A. O. Scott|date=March 24, 2022|title='Everything Everywhere All at Once' Review: It's Messy, and Glorious|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/24/movies/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-review.html|newspaper=The New York Times|url-access=subscription|access-date=April 18, 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220418130656/https://www.nytimes.com/2022/03/24/movies/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-review.html|archive-date=April 18, 2022}}</ref> [[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' described the film as a "swirl of genre anarchy", explaining that "while the hectic action sequences and flights of science-fiction mumbo-jumbo are a big part of the fun (and the marketing), they aren't really the point. [It is] a bittersweet domestic drama, a marital comedy, a story of immigrant striving and a hurt-filled [[ballad]] of mother–daughter love".<ref name="NYTReview"/> Laura Zornosa from ''The New York Times'' elaborates its addressing of [[Transgenerational trauma|intergenerational trauma]] through healing relationships. Additionally, [[Emily St. James]] wrote on [[Vox Media|''Vox'']] that the film is part of a rising subgenre of "millennial parental apology fantasy", imagining worlds in which parents and children reconcile.<ref>{{cite web|last=St. James|first=Emily|author-link=Emily St. James|date=April 25, 2022|title=Hollywood's hot new trend: Parents who say they're sorry|url=https://www.vox.com/culture/23025832/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-parental-apology-fantasy-turning-red|website=[[Vox (website)|Vox]]|access-date=March 3, 2023|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230225122208/https://www.vox.com/culture/23025832/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-parental-apology-fantasy-turning-red|archive-date=February 25, 2023}}</ref>

According to Charles Bramesco of ''[[The Guardian]]'', "The bagel of doom and its tightening grip on Evelyn's daughter lend themselves to the climactic declaration that there's nothing worse than submitting to the nihilism so trendy with the next generation. Our lone hope of recourse is to embrace all the love and beauty surrounding us, if only we're present enough to see it."<ref name="Bramesco 2022"/> This nihilism is also incorporated into the film's exploration of [[Asian American]] identity. [[Anne Anlin Cheng]] wrote in ''[[The Washington Post]],'' "It's not only that the multiverse acts as a metaphor for the [[Asians in New York City|immigrant Asian]] experience, or a convenient parable for the dislocations and personality splits suffered by hyphenated (that is, 'Asian-American') citizens, including [[LGBTQ culture in New York City|LGBT culture]]. It also becomes a rather heady vehicle for confronting and negotiating Asian-pessimism", a term she uses in reference to [[Afro-pessimism (United States)|Afro-pessimism]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Cheng|first=Anne Anlin|date=May 4, 2022|title='Everything Everywhere All at Once' is a deeply Asian American film|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/outlook/2022/05/04/everything-everywhere-asian-american-pessimism/|newspaper=[[Washington Post]]|access-date=June 6, 2022}}</ref>

''[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]]''{{'}}s Clint Worthington wrote that "for all its [[dadaist]] [[absurdism]] and blink-if-you-miss-it pace, Daniels weaves the chaotic possibilities in the multiverse into a cohesive story about the travails of the road not traveled, and the need to carve out your own meaning in a meaningless universe."<ref name="Worthington 2022">{{cite web |url=https://consequence.net/2022/03/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-review-michelle-yeoh/ |title=Everything Everywhere All At Once Is A Lot, and That's a Good Thing: Review |last=Worthington |first=Clint |date=March 24, 2022 |website=[[Consequence (publication)|Consequence]] |access-date=April 17, 2022 |archive-date=April 18, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220418013211/https://consequence.net/2022/03/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-review-michelle-yeoh/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Describing Jobu Tupaki's [[modus operandi]], Worthington notes "the living contradiction that is the everything bagel: if you put everything on a bagel, what more is left? And if you've experienced everything that the multiverse can offer, what's the point of any of it?"<ref name="Worthington 2022"/> Kwan said that the everything bagel concept "did two things. It allowed us to talk about nihilism without being too eye roll-y. And it creates a [[MacGuffin]]: a doomsday device. If, in the first half of the movie, people think that the bagel is here to destroy the world, and in the second half you realize it's a depressed person trying to destroy themselves, it just takes everything about action movies and turns it into something more personal."<ref name="Lee 2022"/> The writer George Gillett argues that the movie is "a coming-of-age film for the internet generation", with the multiverse resembling virtual environments which viewers increasingly exist within.<ref name="Gillett 2022">{{cite web |url=https://thelead.uk/everything-everywhere-wildly-surreal-film-captured-our-own-unreality |title=Everything Everywhere: the wildly surreal film that captured our own unreality |last=Gillett |first=George |date=March 16, 2023 |website=The Lead |access-date=March 29, 2023 |archive-date=March 20, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230320162416/https://thelead.uk/everything-everywhere-wildly-surreal-film-captured-our-own-unreality |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Music==

The musical score was composed by [[Son Lux]], whose members are [[Ryan Lott]], [[Ian Chang]], and [[Rafiq Bhatia]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://filmmusicreporter.com/2022/01/03/son-lux-scoring-daniel-kwans-daniel-scheinerts-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/ |title=Son Lux Scoring Daniel Kwan's & Daniel Scheinert's ''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' |date=January 3, 2022 |website=Film Music Reporter |access-date=January 6, 2022 |archive-date=January 4, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220104011808/http://filmmusicreporter.com/2022/01/03/son-lux-scoring-daniel-kwans-daniel-scheinerts-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Daniels asked them to approach the score individually, and not as a band. The film garnered widespread critical acclaim. {{RT prose|{{RT data|score}}|{{RT data|average}}|{{RT data|count}}|Led by an outstanding Michelle Yeoh, ''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' lives up to its title with an expertly calibrated assault on the senses.|ref=yes|access-date={{RT data|date}}}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 81 out of 100, based on 55 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/everything-everywhere-all-at-once |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=2024-10-30 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[PostTrak]] gave it an 89% positive score, with 77% saying that they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="WideWknd1"/>

Critics have noted that ''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' explores themes of immigrant identity, intergenerational conflict, and cultural assimilation. The film portrays the experiences of a Chinese American immigrant family, highlighting tensions between traditional values and life in the United States, as well as communication barriers shaped by language and cultural differences.<ref>{{cite web |title=Everything Everywhere All at Once: Motifs |url=https://www.sparknotes.com/film/everything-everywhere-all-at-once/motifs |website=SparkNotes |access-date=21 April 2026}}</ref>

Scholars Afarin Rajaei and Karen McKenna-Quayle argue that the film examines intergenerational trauma, conflicted identities, and the challenges faced by immigrant families navigating multiple cultural frameworks.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Rajaei |first=Afarin |last2=McKenna-Quayle |first2=Karen |title=A Multiverse of Perspectives: Cinema Therapy, Cultural Identity, and Family Healing With Everything Everywhere All at Once |journal=Family Relations |year=2025 |doi=10.1177/10664807251384187}}</ref>

===Legacy===
On January 1, 2024, ''Rolling Stone'' ranked the film at number 49 on its list of "The 150 Best Sci-Fi Movies of All Time", writing "As characters leap from universe to universe (including one where humanity evolved to have… hot dog fingers?), and borrow skills from their counterparts, the Daniels never lose sight of the frayed emotions that are still tying the three main characters together, which in turn kept the metaphysical machinations feeling clear and easy to follow."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-sci-fi-movies-1234893930/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-2022-2-1234930661/|title=The 150 Greatest Science Fiction Movies of All Time|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=January 2024}}</ref>

''[[IndieWire]]'' ranked it at number 8 on its list of the "50 Best Action Movies of the 21st Century" and number 5 on its list of the "62 Best Science Fiction Films of the 21st Century".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/feature/best-action-movies-1201839320/|title=The 55 Best Action Movies of the 21st Century|date=September 27, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/feature/best-sci-fi-movies-science-fiction-film-1201817994/|title=The 62 Best Sci-Fi Movies of the 21st Century, from 'Melancholia' and 'M3GAN' to 'Asteroid City'|date=November 22, 2023}}</ref> In 2025, it ranked number 77 on ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century".<ref name="Times">{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=June 23, 2025}}</ref>

''[[No Country for Old Men]]'', ''[[Slumdog Millionaire]]'', and ''[[Argo (2012 film)|Argo]]'' (all five films ended up winning [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] at their respective Academy Award ceremonies).<ref>{{cite web |last=Carr |first=Mary Kate |date=March 6, 2023 |title=Everything Everywhere All At Once completes guild sweep with WGA win ahead of Oscars |url=https://www.avclub.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-guild-sweep-wga-1850192095 |website=The A.V. Club |publisher=G/O Media |access-date=April 4, 2023}}</ref>

''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' made Academy Awards history in several categories. Yeoh was the first Asian Best Actress, the second [[woman of color]] after [[Halle Berry]] in 2002, and the first [[Malaysians|Malaysian]] to win any Academy Award. [[Stephanie Hsu]]'s nomination in the Best Supporting Actress category, alongside [[Hong Chau]]'s nomination for ''[[The Whale (2022 film)|The Whale]]'', marked the first time two actresses of Asian ethnicity were nominated in that category in the same year. Quan was the first Vietnam-born actor to win an Academy Award. Jamie Lee Curtis also won the first Oscar of her career, for Best Supporting Actress, helping the film win three out of four acting categories.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Pulver |first=Andrew |date=2023-03-13 |title=Jamie Lee Curtis wins first Oscar for best supporting actress in Everything Everywhere All at Once |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2023/mar/13/jamie-lee-curtis-wins-best-supporting-actress-oscar-for-everything-everywhere-all-at-once |access-date=2024-02-02 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> It is also the first science-fiction film to win Best Picture,<ref>Multiple sources:
*{{cite news |title=''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' wins Best Picture at Oscars |url=https://abcnews.go.com/GMA/Culture/video/wins-best-picture-oscars-97819726 |access-date=March 15, 2023 |agency=[[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]] |date=March 13, 2023}}
*{{cite news |last1=Deggans |first1=Eric |date=March 13, 2023 |title=Breaking Down Last Night's Oscars |work=[[NPR]] |url=https://www.npr.org/2023/03/13/1163164128/breaking-down-last-nights-oscars |access-date=March 15, 2023}}
*{{cite news |last1=Ettenhofer |first1=Valerie |date=March 13, 2023 |title=Jimmy Kimmel Really Beat a Dead Horse at the Oscars with Too Many Will Smith Jokes |website=/Film|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1225658/every-time-jimmy-kimmel-mocked-will-smith-at-the-2023-oscars/ |access-date=March 15, 2023}}
*{{cite magazine |last1=Teo |first1=Sharlene |title=The Oscar Success of Everything Everywhere All At Once is the Ultimate Validation of Asian Narratives |url=https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/entertainment/a43328105/oscars-success-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/ |access-date=April 1, 2023 |magazine=[[Harper's Bazaar]] |date=March 16, 2023}}</ref> and the first science-fiction film to win five of the [[List of Big Five Academy Award winners and nominees#Above-the-line awards|top six Academy Awards]]. While no film has ever won in all four acting categories, it was the third to win three out of four, after ''[[A Streetcar Named Desire (1951 film)|A Streetcar Named Desire]]'' (1951) and ''[[Network (1976 film)|Network]]'' (1976),<ref>{{cite news|last=Fuster|first=Jeremy|date=March 12, 2023|title=''Everything Everywhere All at Once'' Becomes Just 3rd Film With 3 Acting Oscar Wins|url=https://www.thewrap.com/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-oscars-record-acting/|website=TheWrap|access-date=March 15, 2023}}</ref> and the first to also win Best Picture.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Eng |first=Joyce |date=2023-03-12 |title=A Best Picture champ hasn't won multiple acting Oscars in a long time, but 'Everything Everywhere All at Once' can end that dry spell |url=https://www.goldderby.com/article/2023/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-oscar-predictions-picture-acting/ |access-date=2023-03-13 |website=GoldDerby|archive-date=March 12, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230312213821/https://www.goldderby.com/article/2023/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-oscar-predictions-picture-acting/ |url-status=live}}</ref> It is the first film since 2013's ''[[Gravity (2013 film)|Gravity]]'' to win seven Academy Awards, and the most awarded Best Picture winner since 2008's ''[[Slumdog Millionaire]]''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Blauvelt |first=Christian |date=March 12, 2023 |title=2023 Oscars Complete Winners List: With 7, 'Everything Everywhere' Wins Most Oscars Since 'Gravity' |publisher=[[IndieWire]] |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Reul |first=Katie |date=March 12, 2023 |title='Everything Everywhere All at Once' Wins Seven Oscars, Is Most-Awarded Best Picture The reaction to the film's Best Picture win was not without criticism, including [[Justin Chang]] lamenting in the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' that [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences|the academy]] "chose the wrong best picture", and compared its win to that of ''[[CODA (2021 film)|CODA]]'' at the previous ceremony:{{blockquote|"The more I’ve thought about ''Everything Everywhere'', for all its undeniable representational significance, the more traditional a best picture winner it seems.
