'''''We're No Angels''''' is a 1989 American [[comedy film]] directed by [[Neil Jordan]]. A remake of the [[We're No Angels (1955 film)|1955 film ''We're No Angels'']], it stars [[Robert De Niro]], [[Sean Penn]] and [[Demi Moore]]. It received mixed reviews and grossed $10.5 million on a $20 million budget.

==Plot==
{{More plot|section|date=April 2025}}<!--Plot is less than 200 words; per [[MOS:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words-->

In 1935, [[Great Depression]]-era convicts Ned and Jim, jailed on unspecified charges and abused by a ruthless warden, become escaped convicts when a vicious killer named Bobby escapes the electric chair.

The two escape to a small [[upstate New York]] town near the Canada–US border, where they are mistaken for a pair of [[priest]]s who are expected at the local monastery. They want to flee but cannot because misunderstandings and the warden's search party for Bobby make a trip across the bridge to Canada almost impossible.

Ned and Jim continue to masquerade as priests, trusted and welcomed by Father Levesque. An opportunity presents itself in the form a procession to the church's sister church across the border. Each participating priest must bring someone who needs help, so they decide on the deaf and mute daughter of Molly, a local laundress and prostitute.

Bobby is killed by police during the procession. Ned saves Molly's daughter from drowning, after which she is able to speak. Jim is befriended by a young monk and decides to stay in the monastery to become an actual priest. Ned heads to Canada with Molly and her daughter.

==Production==
The project seemed like it could not fail: per A.J. Black, Robert De Niro and Sean Penn were "a bankable duo who Paramount would have been unconcerned about throwing a significant budget toward, produced by an impresario with proven hits at his back, written by a celebrated wordsmith and helmed by an acclaimed young director breaking into the studio system."

Screenwriter [[David Mamet]] took the initial premise from the 1955 [[We're No Angels (1955 film)|movie of the same name]], but he created a new scenario.<ref name="Hansen">{{cite web |last1=Hansen |first1=John |date=June 10, 2019 |title='We're No Angels' (1989) playfully pillories the pious |url=https://reviewsfrommycouch.com/2019/06/movies/were-no-angels-1989-review/ |access-date=12 October 2019 |website=Reviews from My Couch}}</ref> Though the story is set in upstate New York, it was filmed in [[Mission, British Columbia]].<ref name=Ebert/> The set design involved the creation of an entire riverside town, with a budget of CDN $2.5 million for the set alone being the largest for a film produced in Canada to that date.<ref name="Black">{{cite web |last1=Black |first1=A.J. |date=May 10, 2019 |title=We're No Angels (1989) – The Filmography of Neil Jordan |url=https://ajblackwriter.com/2019/05/10/were-no-angels-1989-the-filmography-of-neil-jordan/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210416231015/https://ajblackwriter.com/2019/05/10/were-no-angels-1989-the-filmography-of-neil-jordan/ |archive-date=2021-04-16 |access-date=12 October 2019 |website=ajblackwriter.com}}</ref>

==Reception==
The film garnered mixed reviews. [[Rotten Tomatoes]] sampled 21 reviewers, and judged 48% of the reviews to be positive, with an average score of 5.2/10.<ref name="RottenTomatoes">{{cite web|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1023286-were_no_angels/|title=We're No Angels|publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=11 January 2024}}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a B− grade on scale of A to F.<ref>{{cite web |title=Hollywood's Star Vehicles Sputter at the Christmas Box Office : Movies: 'Blaze,' 'We're No Angels' and 'Family Business' opened with high holiday hopes. Despite their six bankable male leads, the films have fallen flat. |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-12-20-ca-578-story.html |website=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=20 December 1989 |quote=In addition to mixed reviews from critics, those three movies were given Cinemascore audience grades in the B- range}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' liked the film, saying, "Robert De Niro and Sean Penn have two of the best faces in the movies – screwed up, sideways faces with a lot of mischief in the eyes. ''We're No Angels'' is a movie made for those faces, and one of the pleasures of watching the film is to see them looking sidelong at each other as they try to figure a way out of the complicated mess they're in."<ref name=Ebert>{{cite web |last1=Ebert |first1=Roger |title=We're No Angels movie review (December 15, 1989) |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/were-no-angels-1989 |website=Rogerebert.com |publisher=Ebert Digital LLC |access-date=12 October 2019}}</ref>

In a retrospective review from 2019, critic John Hansen wrote, "''We're No Angels'' was probably hurt by being unappealing on the surface to both religious and non-religious moviegoers, with both groups assuming the film isn't for them. But it's actually for anyone who enjoys a smartly crafted comedy."<ref name="Hansen" />

===Box office===

The film debuted at #8 at the United States box office.<ref>{{cite news |last=Cerone |first=Daniel |date=December 19, 1989 |title=Weekend Box Office |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-12-19-ca-454-story.html |url-status=live |access-date=2012-06-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121015132000/http://articles.latimes.com/1989-12-19/entertainment/ca-454_1_weekend-box-office |archive-date=2012-10-15}}</ref> It had similar success on home video.<ref>{{cite news |last=Hunt |first=Dennis |date=August 2, 1990 |title=VIDEO RENTALS : 'Internal Affairs' Has Appeal |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-08-02-ca-1494-story.html |access-date=2012-06-13}}</ref>
