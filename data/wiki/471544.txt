'''''Blade''''' is a 1998 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Blade (character)|the Marvel Comics character]]. The first installment in the [[Blade (franchise)|''Blade'' franchise]], it was directed by [[Stephen Norrington]] and written by [[David S. Goyer]]. [[Wesley Snipes]] stars as Blade, a human with vampire strengths but not their weaknesses, who battles other vampires. The supporting cast includes [[Stephen Dorff]], [[Kris Kristofferson]] and [[N'Bushe Wright]].

''Blade'' was released by [[New Line Cinema]] in the United States on August 21, 1998, and was a commercial success, grossing $131 million on a budget of $45 million. It is also hailed as one of Snipes's signature roles.<ref>{{cite web |last=Harp |first=Justin |title=Marvel is rebooting Blade without Wesley Snipes |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a28457878/marvel-blade-reboot-mahershala-ali/ |website=Digital Spy |date=21 July 2019 |quote=Instead of Snipes reprising his iconic role... |access-date=August 8, 2020 |archive-date=October 1, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201001150406/https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a28457878/marvel-blade-reboot-mahershala-ali/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

''Blade'' was noted as a dark superhero film for its time,<ref name="Litchenfeld289">{{cite book | last= Lichtenfeld | first= Eric | title= Action Speaks Louder: Violence, Spectacle, and the American Action |publisher= [[Wesleyan University Press]] |year= 2007 |isbn= 978-0-8195-6801-4 |page= 289}}</ref> as well as being Marvel's first successful film.  It was followed by two sequels, ''[[Blade II]]'' (2002) and ''[[Blade: Trinity]]'' (2004). Snipes reprised his role as Blade in the 2024 film ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]''.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries are 400 to 700 words only. -->

In 1967, a pregnant woman is attacked by a vampire, causing her to go into [[premature labor]]. Doctors can save her baby, but the woman dies. Thirty years later, the child has become the vampire hunter [[Blade (New Line franchise character)|Blade]], who is known as the "Daywalker", a human-vampire hybrid that possesses the supernatural abilities of the vampires without any of their weaknesses, except for the requirement to consume human blood. Blade raids a [[rave|rave club]] owned by the vampire [[Deacon Frost]]; a badly injured vampire is taken by police to the hospital, where he feeds upon [[hematologist]] Karen Jenson and escapes. Blade takes Karen to a safe house where she is treated by his old friend [[Abraham Whistler]]. Whistler explains that he and Blade have been waging a secret war against vampires using weapons based on their elemental weaknesses, such as sunlight, silver, and garlic. As Karen is now "marked" by the bite of a vampire, both he and Blade tell her to leave the city. At a meeting of pure-blood vampire elders, Frost, the leader of a faction of younger vampires, is rebuked for trying to incite war between vampires and humans. As Frost and his kind are not natural-born vampires, they are considered socially inferior. 

Meanwhile, returning to her apartment, Karen is attacked by police officer Krieger, who is a [[familiar]], a human thrall loyal to vampires. Blade subdues Krieger, but thanks to Karen, Krieger escapes. Blade tracks him down and beats him into revealing Frost's interest in an archive of ancient vampire knowledge. Krieger informs Frost of what happened and is killed for his failure. Frost also has Elder Dragonetti stripped of his fangs and torched alive by the sunrise, then strips the other Elders of their authority and imprisons them. Meanwhile, Blade visits the archivist Pearl, a morbidly obese vampire, and, with a UV light, tortures him into revealing that Frost wants to command a ritual where he would use twelve pure-blood vampires to awaken the "blood god" La Magra, and Blade's blood is the key. Later, at the hideout, Blade injects himself with a special serum that suppresses his urge to drink blood. However, the serum is beginning to lose its effectiveness due to overuse. While experimenting with the anticoagulant [[EDTA#Medicine|EDTA]] as a possible replacement, Karen discovers that it explodes when combined with vampire blood. She manages to synthesize a vaccine that can cure the infected, but learns that it will not work on Blade. Karen is confident she can eventually cure his bloodlust, but it would take years he doesn't have.

After Blade rejects Frost's offer for a truce, Frost and his men attack the hideout, where they bite Whistler and abduct Karen. When Blade returns, he helps Whistler commit suicide. When Blade attempts to rescue Karen from Frost's penthouse, he is shocked to find his still-alive mother, Vanessa Brooks, who reveals that she came back the night she was attacked and was brought in by Frost, who appears and reveals himself as the vampire who bit her. Blade, whose real name is revealed to be Eric Brooks, is then subdued and taken to the Temple of Eternal Night, where Frost plans to perform the summoning ritual for La Magra. Karen is thrown into a pit to be devoured by her colleague Webb, who has transformed into a decomposing [[zombie]]-like creature. Karen injures him and escapes. Blade is drained of his blood, but Karen allows him to drink hers, enabling him to recover. Frost completes the ritual and obtains the powers of La Magra. Blade confronts Frost after killing all of his minions, including his mother, but initially finds him too powerful to defeat. Reasoning that Frost's physical body is still vulnerable, Blade gives him an overdose of EDTA; this triggers an adverse reaction that causes Frost to turn into a grotesque blob before exploding. Karen offers to help Blade cure himself; instead, he asks her to create an improved version of the serum so he can continue his crusade against vampires. In a brief [[epilogue]], Blade confronts an old vampire enemy in [[Moscow]].

==Production==

===Background===

The character [[Blade (character)|Blade]] was created in 1973 for Marvel Comics by the writer [[Marv Wolfman]] and artist [[Gene Colan]] as a supporting character in the 1970s comic ''[[The Tomb of Dracula]]''. The comic Blade used [[teakwood]] knives and was much more the everyman in his behavior and attitude. Though courageous, he displayed flaws as well, such as an inability to get along with certain other supporting cast members and a hatred of vampires that bordered on fanaticism.

The character was not originally a "daywalker" but a human being immune to being turned into a vampire. Lacking the superhuman speed and strength of his undead quarry, he relied solely on his wits and skill until he was bitten by the character [[Morbius]] as seen in [[Peter Parker: Spider-Man#Volume Two (1999–2003)|''Peter Parker: Spider-Man'']] #8, first published in August 1999. The film portrayal of Blade was updated for a 1990s audience, and the comics character was subsequently modified to match. Goyer replaced the daggers Blade used in the comics with a sword and gave him a more [[samurai]]-like aesthetic.<ref name="Otto" /> The film's version of Deacon Frost also differs greatly from his comic counterpart. He was older with white hair and a literal church deacon, but the film retains Frost's upstart ambitions.<ref name="Otto">{{cite web |last=Otto |first=Jeff |date=1 March 2004 |title=An Interview with David S. Goyer |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/03/01/an-interview-with-david-s-goyer |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121021080447/https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/03/01/an-interview-with-david-s-goyer |archive-date=October 21, 2012 |website=[[IGN]]}}</ref><!-- page 23 "There is nothing Baroque about him" --><ref name="Kutzera" />

===Development===

When [[New World Pictures]] bought the rights to [[Marvel Comics]], they were set to make a [[Mexico]]-set western starring [[Richard Roundtree]] as the vampire hunter. [[Marvel Studios]] then started to develop the film in early 1992, when rapper [[LL Cool J]] was interested in playing the lead role.<ref>{{cite news|author=Staff|url=https://variety.com/1992/film/news/marvel-characters-holding-attraction-for-filmmakers-101955/|date=1992-12-08|title=Marvel characters holding attraction for filmmakers|work=Variety |access-date=2024-10-12 |archive-date=March 5, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305034341/https://variety.com/1992/film/news/marvel-characters-holding-attraction-for-filmmakers-101955/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was eventually set up at [[New Line Cinema]], with [[David S. Goyer]] writing the script. When Goyer heard a film was in development, he went in to [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] with director [[Ernest Dickerson]].<ref name="Kutzera" /> [[David Fincher]] was also attached to the project and even worked on the script with Goyer.<ref>{{Cite web |date=December 8, 2004 |first=Brian |last=B |url=https://movieweb.com/director-david-goyer-talks-blade-trinity-and-batman-begins/ |title=Director David Goyer talks Blade: Trinity and Batman Begins! |website=MovieWeb}}</ref><ref name="ew-oral" /> New Line Cinema originally wanted to do ''Blade'' as "something that was almost a spoof" before the writer convinced them otherwise.<ref name="BloodScreen"/> At one point, the studio even asked if Blade could be white.<ref name="ew-oral">{{cite magazine |date=July 16, 2018 |author=Leah Greenblatt |title='Blade' oral history: Wesley Snipes and the cast look back at a modern cult classic |url=https://ew.com/comic-con/2018/07/16/blade-oral-history-wesley-snipes/ |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |access-date=November 24, 2020 |archive-date=May 14, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190514114748/https://ew.com/movies/2018/07/16/blade-oral-history-wesley-snipes/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Goyer wanted to take the character seriously, and ground them in a sense of reality with vampirism as a biological disease.<ref>{{cite web |author=Desmond Pfeiffer |url=https://archive.org/details/cinefantastique_1970-2002/Cinefantastique%20Vol%2029%20No%206-7%20%28Nov%201997%29/page/n6/mode/1up |title=Cinefantastique Magazine: 1970-2002 : Cinefantastique |date= November 25, 1970|access-date=2022-08-04}}</ref> He even pitched a trilogy of movies<!-- page 18 continues page 21 --> "almost [[Wagnerian]] in scope".<ref name="Kutzera" /><!-- "I'm going to pitch you the Star Wars of black vampire films" --><ref name="ew-oral" /> He also wanted to demystify the vampires and treat them as serious villains with a greater sense of realism instead of the doomed romantic characters shown in Anne Rice's [[Interview with the Vampire (film)|''Interview with the Vampire'']]. Goyer's early drafts predated but took a similar post-modern approach as the films ''[[From Dusk till Dawn]]'' and ''[[Vampire in Brooklyn]]''.<ref name="Kutzera" />
After failing to get a [[Black Panther (character)|Black Panther]] film into production,<ref name="blackpanther">{{cite web |date=January 30, 2018 |author=Ryan Parker |author2=Aaron Couch |title=Wesley Snipes Reveals Untold Story Behind His 'Black Panther' Film |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/black-panther-wesley-snipes-reveals-untold-story-behind-90s-film-1078868 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=February 23, 2019 |archive-date=January 30, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180130185406/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/black-panther-wesley-snipes-reveals-untold-story-behind-90s-film-1078868 |url-status=live}}</ref> in 1996, [[Wesley Snipes]] signed on to star as [[Blade (New Line franchise character)|Blade]].<ref>{{cite news |author=Gary Levin |url=https://variety.com/1996/scene/vpage/perelman-takes-marvel-to-bankruptcy-court-1117436186/ |title=Perelman takes Marvel to bankruptcy court |work=Variety |date=1996-12-29 |access-date=2014-10-12 |archive-date=March 3, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200303134112/https://variety.com/1996/scene/vpage/perelman-takes-marvel-to-bankruptcy-court-1117436186/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Snipes also produced through his company Amen Ra Films, and worked with the writers to invest the story with elements of classic tragedy - ”like ''[[King Lear|Lear]], like ''[[Hamlet]]"- to heighten the characters' emotional drama.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-aug-19-ca-14361-story.html|title= Blade to Snipes' Heat |last=Harrison |first=Eric |date=August 19, 1998 |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] }}</ref> ''Blade'' had a $45&nbsp;million budget.<ref name="BloodScreen">{{cite news | last = Allstetter | first= Rob | date = August 1997 | title = Special Report: Blood on the Big Screen | work = [[Wizard (magazine)|Wizard]] | issue = 72 | pages = 122–3}}</ref>

===Casting===

When Goyer first pitched the idea of doing a ''Blade'' film, [[Michael De Luca]], head of New Line Cinema, suggested [[Denzel Washington]], Wesley Snipes, and [[Laurence Fishburne]], but to Goyer, Snipes was always the perfect choice for [[Blade (New Line franchise character)|Blade]].<ref name="ew-oral" /> The finalized script was sent to Snipes and no other actor was seriously considered.<!-- page 27 --><ref name="Kutzera" />

[[Patrick McGoohan]] was the first choice to play [[Abraham Whistler|Whistler]], as Stephen Norrington was a fan of ''[[The Prisoner]]'' (1967). [[Jon Voight]] was also considered for the role.<ref name="Kutzera">{{cite magazine |author=Dale Kutzera |title=LONE WOLF VAMPIRE SLAYER |url=https://archive.org/details/cinefantastique_1970-2002/Cinefantastique%20Vol%2029%20No%2010%20%28Feb%201998%29/page/n26/mode/1up |website=Cinefantastique Magazine |page=27 |via=[[Internet Archive]] |date=1998 |quote=the only three people we were thinking about for Whistler were Patrick Mac-goohan, Jon Voight, and Kris Kristofferson.}}</ref><ref name="Allstetter">{{cite web |date=March 19, 2002 |author=Rob Allstetter |url=http://www.comicscontinuum.com/stories/0203/19/bladeindex.htm |title=Comics Continuum |publisher=Comics Continuum |access-date=2019-01-08 |archive-date=2012-07-23 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120723143748/http://www.comicscontinuum.com/stories/0203/19/bladeindex.htm|url-status=live |quote=Initially, with Whistler, we were thinking Patrick McGoohan, and then we started thinking Jon Voigt and then Kris Kristofferson}}</ref>

===Filming===

[[Principal photography]] commenced on February 5, 1997, and concluded on June 6, 1997,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mukhopadhyay |first=Arka |date=2022-03-29 |title=Where Was Blade Filmed? |url=https://thecinemaholic.com/where-was-blade-filmed/ |access-date=2025-11-10 |website=The Cinemaholic |language=en-US}}</ref> and was in large part done in Los Angeles, with some scenes being shot in [[Death Valley]]. All sets were constructed, and all on-set filming occurred, in what was formerly the Redken Shampoo factory in Canoga Park.<ref>{{cite news |author=Bashirah Muttalib |title=As more pics shoot in Calif., coffers swell |url=https://variety.com/1998/voices/columns/as-more-pics-shoot-in-calif-coffers-swell-1117481188/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=8 October 1998 |access-date=1 April 2020 |archive-date=April 14, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160414132304/http://variety.com/1998/voices/columns/as-more-pics-shoot-in-calif-coffers-swell-1117481188/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The effects for the film were done by [[Flat Earth Productions]].<ref>{{cite news |author=Marc Graser |title=Flat Earth founder forms new company |url=https://variety.com/2001/digital/news/flat-earth-founder-forms-new-company-1117853484/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=1 October 2001 |access-date=1 April 2020 |archive-date=March 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170309163820/http://variety.com/2001/digital/news/flat-earth-founder-forms-new-company-1117853484/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Post-production===

The first cut of the film was 140 minutes long. It had a disastrous test screening with audiences. Heavy edits and re-shoots were implemented, which delayed the release date for more than half a year. The most significant change was the addition of the final sword fight between Blade and Deacon Frost, which did not exist in the original cut. In the original ending, Frost turned into La Magra and became a large swirling mass of blood instead of keeping his form. This was scrapped because the filmmakers could not get the special effects to look right. It can be seen as a special feature on the DVD. [[Stan Lee]] originally had a cameo that was ultimately cut from the film. He played one of the cops who came into the blood club during the aftermath and discovered Quinn's body on fire. The scene where Karen and Deacon are talking about the cure for vampirism initially ran slightly longer, and answered the question of how the vampires would feed if everybody were turned into vampires. They would keep some humans alive in giant blood bags to harvest them. The bags can still be seen in a doorway during the scene, and later played an integral part of the plot in ''[[Blade: Trinity]]''.<ref>{{cite web |date=April 30, 1998 |url=http://www.aintitcool.com/node/928 |title=BLADE test screenings |author=staff |work=[[Ain't It Cool News]] |access-date=February 15, 2015 |archive-date=February 16, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150216002309/http://www.aintitcool.com/node/928 |url-status=live}}</ref> Marvel was not going to give [[Marv Wolfman]] and [[Gene Colan]] credit for the characters they had created, but Goyer insisted. He asked New Line, and they accepted, but representatives of Marv Wolfman said only he should get credit, and not Gene Colan. Goyer insisted that both be credited.<ref name="Otto" />

==Reception==

===Box office===

In North America, ''Blade'' dislodged ''[[Saving Private Ryan]]'', which had led the previous four weeks at the box office, to finish at number one with $17.1 million across 2,322 screens.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-aug-25-ca-16176-story.html |title=Snipes Helps Power Blade to No.1 |last=Welkos |first=Robert W.|work=Los Angeles Times|date=August 25, 1998}}</ref> The film would top the box office for two weekends until it was overtaken by ''[[There's Something About Mary]]''.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/111352494/mary-shows-its-something-reaching/ |title='Mary' shows it's something reaching No. 1 after 8 weeks |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221023145427/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/111352494/mary-shows-its-something-reaching/ |date=September 10, 1998 |access-date=October 23, 2022 |archive-date=October 23, 2022 |page=2 |publisher=[[The Star Press]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref> The film also opened in number one in both [[Spain]], with  $1.5&nbsp;million (US) in 200 theaters,  and [[Australia]], with $1&nbsp;million from 132 cinemas.<ref>{{cite news |author=Don Groves |title=Germans embrace Ryan at the B.O. |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/germans-embrace-ryan-at-the-b-o-1117481371/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=13 October 1998 |access-date=13 December 2008 |archive-date=June 7, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190607043942/https://variety.com/1998/film/news/germans-embrace-ryan-at-the-b-o-1117481371/ |url-status=live}}</ref> In the [[Flemish Region]] of Belgium, the film earned $323,000 from 20 cinemas, and the Netherlands earned the film $246,000 from 44 cinemas.<ref>{{cite news |author=Don Groves |title=Antz swarming o'seas |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/antz-swarming-o-seas-1117488039/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=3 November 1998 |access-date=13 December 2008 |archive-date=June 8, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190608040157/https://variety.com/1998/film/news/antz-swarming-o-seas-1117488039/ |url-status=live}}</ref> France made $1.9&nbsp;million in five days from 241 cinemas, but the film was less successful in Hong Kong (with $182,000 from 22 cinemas) and South Africa ($159,000 from 64 cinemas). The United Kingdom was more successful, taking in $5.7&nbsp;million over 10 days,<ref>{{cite news |author=Don Groves |title=Antz, Exorcist impressive o'seas |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/antz-exorcist-impressive-o-seas-1117488220/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=9 November 1998 |access-date=13 December 2008 |archive-date=June 7, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190607012449/https://variety.com/1998/film/news/antz-exorcist-impressive-o-seas-1117488220/ |url-status=live}}</ref> as was Brazil, making $855,000 in four days from 133 cinemas.<ref>{{cite news |author=Don Groves |title=Mary, Whisperer top $100 mil mark o'seas |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/mary-whisperer-top-100-mil-mark-o-seas-1117488803/ |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=24 November 1998 |access-date=13 December 2008 |archive-date=June 7, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190607035841/https://variety.com/1998/film/news/mary-whisperer-top-100-mil-mark-o-seas-1117488803/ |url-status=live}}</ref> ''Blade'' remained atop the North American box office in its second weekend, earning $11.1 million.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-aug-31-ca-18102-story.html|title='Blade' Still a Cut Above as Ticket Sales Heat Up|date=August 31, 1998|work=Los Angeles Times|access-date=November 16, 2025|last=Natale|first=Richard}}</ref> ''Blade'' finished its box office run with $70 million domestically and $61 million worldwide for a total of $131.2&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM"/>

Despite the success of the film, Marvel shared only a flat fee of $25,000.<ref>{{cite web |date=28 September 2012 |last=Howe |first=Sean |title=The Amazing Merrill Lynch Deal That Made The Avengers Possible |url=https://slate.com/business/2012/09/marvel-comics-and-the-movies-the-business-story-behind-the-avengers.html |website=Slate Magazine |access-date=May 9, 2019 |archive-date=April 28, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190428122228/https://slate.com/business/2012/09/marvel-comics-and-the-movies-the-business-story-behind-the-avengers.html |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 59% based on 114 reviews, with an average rating of 6/10. The site's critics' consensus states: "Though some may find the plot a bit lacking, ''Blade''{{'}}s action is fierce, plentiful, and appropriately stylish for a comic book adaptation."<ref>{{cite web |title= Blade |url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1083484_blade |website= [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=February 25, 2025|archive-date= September 24, 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20200924011654/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1083484_blade |url-status= live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 47 out of 100, based on 25 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Blade'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/blade |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=17 September 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250120134704/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/blade/ |archive-date=January 20, 2025 |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url= https://cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= Cinemascore |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 |access-date= 2019-02-04}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three stars out of four, writing: "''Blade'' ... is a movie that relishes high visual style. It uses the extreme camera angles, the bizarre costumes and sets, the exaggerated shadows, and the confident cutting between long shots and extreme closeups. It slams ahead in pure visceral imagery".<ref>{{cite news |date= August 21, 1998 |author= Roger Ebert |author-link= Roger Ebert |title= Blade |publisher= [[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url= https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/blade-1998 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20130620104856/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/blade-1998 |archive-date= June 20, 2013 |url-status= live |access-date= May 9, 2019}}</ref> [[James Berardinelli]] gave the film 2½ stars out of 4, writing: "''Blade'' has the capacity to dazzle, but it also will leave many viewers dissatisfied".<ref>{{cite web | author = James Berardinelli | author-link = James Berardinelli | title = Blade - Reelviews Movie Reviews - James Berardinelli | publisher = Reelviews.net | url = http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/b/blade.html | url-status = live | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20190509211809/http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/b/blade.html | archive-date = May 9, 2019 | access-date = May 9, 2019}}</ref> Berardinelli also wrote: "''Blade'' opens brilliantly, with a series of fast-paced, visually-engaging scenes that display the seedy underbelly of vampire society and introduce the implacable title character in true superhero fashion. For about its first hour, the movie offers violent, visceral, rapid-fire entertainment that concentrates as much on developing a distinctive atmosphere as on advancing a minimalist storyline. Unfortunately [...] it keeps going and going, eventually wearing out its welcome".<ref>{{cite web |author= James Berardinelli |author-link= James Berardinelli |title= Blade - A Film Review by James Berardinelli, for Playboy Magazine |publisher= Playboy magazine |url= http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/b/blade1.html |url-status= live |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20190509212137/http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/b/blade1.html |archive-date= May 9, 2019 |access-date= May 9, 2019}}</ref> Dennis Harvey of [[Variety (magazine)|''Variety'']] wrote: "Though slick and diverting in some aspects, increasingly silly pic has trouble meshing disparate elements -- horror, superhero fantasy, straight-up action -- into a workable whole".<ref name="Harvey">{{cite web |date=20 August 1998 |author=Dennis Harvey |title=Blade |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/reviews/blade-1200454648 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> John Krewson of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' was critical of the story and the dialogue, but praised the "creative cinematography and non-stop, decently choreographed gratuitous violence".<ref>{{cite web |date= 2002-10-04 |author= John Krewson |title= Blade |url= https://www.avclub.com/blade-1798196353 |website= [[The A.V. Club]] |access-date= February 20, 2020 |archive-date= November 23, 2019 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20191123162247/https://film.avclub.com/blade-1798196353 |url-status= live}}</ref>

Critics such as A. Asbjørn Jøn have noted not only the important place of ''Blade'' in the wider vampire genre, but also possible intertextual links between the Whistler character and a character named Whistler in ''A Dozen Black Roses'' (1996) by [[Nancy A. Collins]], as they possess "striking similarities in role, dramatic focus, visual appearance, and sharing the name".<ref>{{Cite journal|url = https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283318599|title = Vampire Evolution|last = Jøn|first = A. Asbjørn|date = 2003|journal = METAphor|access-date = 25 November 2015|page = 23|archive-date = January 12, 2021|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210112222202/https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283318599_Vampire_Evolution|url-status = live}}</ref>

===Accolades===

The film was nominated for [[Saturn Award for Best Horror Film|Best Horror Film]] and [[Saturn Award for Best Make-up|Best Make-up]] at the [[Saturn Awards]].<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Invasion of the Saturn Winners |url=https://www.wired.com/1999/06/invasion-of-the-saturn-winners/ |magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]] |access-date=20 October 2023 |date=June 18, 1999}}</ref> [[Stephen Dorff]] won [[MTV Movie Award for Best Villain|Best Villain]] at the [[MTV Movie & TV Awards|MTV Movie Awards]] while [[Wesley Snipes]] was nominated for [[MTV Movie Award for Best Fight|Best Fight]].<ref>{{cite web | url = http://www.mtv.com/ontv/movieawards/1999/ | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20080423094847/http://www.mtv.com/ontv/movieawards/1999/ | url-status = dead | archive-date = April 23, 2008 | title = 1999 MTV Movie Awards | work = [[MTV Movie Awards]] | access-date = October 26, 2010}}</ref>

==Sequels==
{{main|Blade II|Blade: Trinity|Blade: The Series}}
The success of the film led to two sequels, ''Blade II'' in 2002 and ''Blade Trinity'' in 2004, as well as a television series.

==Legacy==
{{Anchor|Future}}
In August 2014, Snipes spoke about his desire to return to the franchise: "I'd be open to it. I think we've got some stones left unturned and there's some latitude left for us to build on and I'd love to get back in the suit again and do some things I've learned how to do now that I didn't know how to do then".<ref>{{cite news |date= August 28, 2014 |title= Wesley Snipes back for fourth 'Blade' |url= http://www.torontosun.com/2014/08/28/wesley-snipes-back-for-fourth-blade |newspaper= Toronto Sun |access-date= August 30, 2014 |archive-date= September 9, 2016 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20160909190036/http://www.torontosun.com/2014/08/28/wesley-snipes-back-for-fourth-blade |url-status= live}}</ref>

During their 2019 [[San Diego Comic-Con]] presentation, [[Marvel Studios]] announced a ''Blade'' reboot set in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU), with [[Mahershala Ali]] starring as Blade.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://comicbook.com/marvel/2019/07/20/marvel-studios-blade-sdcc-panel-announce/ |title= Marvel Studios Announces Blade Reboot During SDCC Presentation |first= Charlie |last= Ridgely |date= July 20, 2019 |website= ComicBook.com |access-date= July 20, 2019 |archive-date= October 8, 2019 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20191008034157/https://comicbook.com/marvel/2019/07/20/marvel-studios-blade-sdcc-panel-announce/ |url-status= live}}</ref> Some fans of Snipes were disappointed, but Snipes expressed his support for Ali saying he would "do great".<ref>{{cite web |author=Princess Weekes |date=November 12, 2021 |title=Wesley Snipes Shows Support for Mahershala Ali as Blade |url=https://www.themarysue.com/wesley-snipes-shows-support-for-mahershala-ali-as-blade/ |website=The Mary Sue}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date= July 24, 2019 |url= https://www.wfaa.com/article/entertainment/entertainment-tonight/wesley-snipes-responds-to-marvel-rebooting-blade-its-all-good/603-1e968ec6-5582-44c5-8161-ee0867cbba01 |title= Wesley Snipes Responds to Marvel Rebooting 'Blade': 'It's All Good' |first= Zach |last= Seemayer |access-date= April 22, 2022 |archive-date= April 22, 2022 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20220422172442/https://www.wfaa.com/article/entertainment/entertainment-tonight/wesley-snipes-responds-to-marvel-rebooting-blade-its-all-good/603-1e968ec6-5582-44c5-8161-ee0867cbba01 |url-status= dead }}</ref>

In 2021, Marvel published ''The Darkhold: Blade'' [[One-shot (comics)|one-shot]] written by [[Daniel Kibblesmith]], presenting an alternate ending to the film, where Deacon Frost succeeded in his plans to use his power attained as avatar of La Magra to turn billions of humans around the world into vampires.<ref>{{cite web |date = November 2, 2021 |author=Jon Olsen |url=https://www.cbr.com/blade-marvel-darkhold-movie-what-if/ |title = Blade: The Movie Just Got a Perfect What if Story in Marvel's Darkhold |work=CBR.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date = November 7, 2021 |author=SHAUN CORLEY |url=https://screenrant.com/darkhold-blade-1-deacon-frost-la-magra/ |title = Marvel Reveals a World Where Blade Lost in His First Movie |website = [[Screen Rant]]}}</ref>

In 2024, Snipes reprised his role as Blade in the MCU film ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]''.<ref>{{cite web |date=4 August 2024 |last=Zee |first=Michaela |title=Wesley Snipes Breaks Two Guinness World Records After Blade Return in 'Deadpool & Wolverine' |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/wesley-snipes-blade-breaks-two-guinness-world-records-1236094977/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] }}</ref>
