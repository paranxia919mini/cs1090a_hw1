'''''Communion''''' is a 1989 American [[science fiction]] film directed by [[Philippe Mora]] and adapted by [[Whitley Strieber]] from [[Communion (Strieber book)|his 1987 book]].<ref name="The Advocate">{{cite news| last  = Dreher| first = Rod| title = Film describes author's link to 'visitors'| newspaper = The Advocate| location = Baton Rouge, Louisiana| date   = November 20, 1989| page   = 8-A| url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=news/0EB4758227497B54| access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite news| last  = Garner| first = Jack| title = CAPSULE REVIEWS OF '89 FILMS: Communion| newspaper = [[USA Today]]| location = Arlington, Virginia| date   = December 21, 1989| url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=news/12820ECA2DFCD3C0| access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref>

Starring [[Christopher Walken]] and [[Frances Sternhagen]], it tells a story of a family that experiences an [[close encounter|extraterrestrial phenomenon]] while on vacation at a remote home in the wilderness during which the father Whitley Strieber is abducted and all of their lives change.<ref name="The Advocate" /> According to Strieber, the story is a real-life account of his own encounter with "visitors",<ref>{{cite news| title = 'Communion,' viewers have close encounter| newspaper = [[Austin American-Statesman]]| location = Austin, Texas| date   = December 3, 1989| page = 10 |url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=news/0EAD89BFD78F7C65| access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref> with Walken playing the role of Strieber.<ref>{{cite news| title = AT THE MOVIES| newspaper = The Morning Call| location = Allentown, Pennsylvania| date   = December 1, 1989| page = D10 |url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=news/12AEEF5B61E58938 | access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref>

==Plot==
In 1985, New York–based author [[Whitley Strieber]] lives with his wife [[Anne Strieber|Anne]] and son Andrew in [[Manhattan]] and seems to be successful. However, he is awakened at night by paranoid dreams that someone else is in the room.

On a trip to the family cabin in the woods, the intruder alarm is triggered and Strieber sees a face watching him from the doorway. Bright light fills the cabin windows and wakes Andrew and two other family friends Alex and Sarah but Anne remains asleep.

Disturbed by this, the group returns to New York and life seemingly returns to normal, but Strieber finds that his work and personal life are becoming affected by recurring nightmares and visions of strange alien beings including [[Grey aliens|greys]], blue doctors and [[Insectoid aliens|bugs]]. This upsets Andrew and puts strain on his marriage.

After an incident at their cabin in which Strieber is so convinced that there are alien beings inside the home that he pulls his gun out and almost shoots Anne, worried that Andrew is beginning to have the same visions, he is finally convinced to see psychiatrist Dr. Janet Duffy specializing in [[hypnotic regression]] therapy.

The therapy confirms that Strieber has possibly been [[Alien abduction|abducted by unknown beings]] and experiments have been performed on him; however, he is still skeptical about it and reluctantly attends a group therapy session of fellow 'abductees'.

Eventually Strieber realizes he has to confront his visions, real or not, and returns to the cabin where most of the incidents seem to occur. He interacts with the alien beings and realizes he has been in contact with them his whole life and it was passed on from his father Karl and he will, in turn, pass it on to Andrew.

Making up with his family, Strieber comes to accept the alien visitors as part of his life and in the last scene he sits in his office and embraces the face of a 'grey' alien.

==Production==
''Communion'' was written by [[Whitley Strieber]] adapting his [[Communion (Strieber book)|book of the same name]], itself chronicling Strieber's alleged encounters with [[Extraterrestrial life|extraterrestrials]].<ref name= "CommunionProd"/> Strieber opted to work with director and friend [[Philippe Mora]] on adapting the story to film with the understanding Strieber would handle the writing without interference while Mora would handle direction without interference.<ref name= "CommunionProd"/> Strieber chose to have the film produced independently as he feared having a major studio adapt his experiences would turn it into a special effects-heavy horror film rather than a character piece.<ref name= "CommunionProd"/> During production it was reported that on set some members of the crew would crack jokes at Strieber's expense about the autobiographical aspects of the story.<ref name= "CommunionProd"/>  Strieber said of these instances:
<blockquote>Some people who work on movies are nice people; some people who work on movies are jerks. We had the usual mix on this crew.<ref name= "CommunionProd"/> </blockquote>

The filmmakers later arranged special screenings of the film in several cities, including [[New York City, New York|New York]], [[San Diego, California|San Diego]], [[San Antonio, Texas|San Antonio]] and [[St. Louis, Missouri|St. Louis]], for alleged abductees.<ref>{{cite news | last  = Broeske| first = Pat H.| title = Studios and producers often arrange special screenings for special interest-groups| newspaper = Las Vegas Review-Journal| location = Las Vegas, Nevada| date   = November 26, 1989| page   = 62| url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=image/v2%3A1508AFD0E83DBED6%40EANX-NB-16E45894AC998785%402447857-16E4557FB7F0D7CA%4061-16E4557FB7F0D7CA%40| access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref>

==Reception==
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds a 46% approval rating based on 12 reviews, with an average rating of 4.6/10.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Communion |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/communion |access-date=April 2, 2022 |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref>

Some critics praised [[Christopher Walken]]'s performance as the highlight of the film. ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' called his performance "terrific" and added: "Walken dazzles, giving us an intelligent, talented man caught in a nightmare and fearing for his sanity."<ref>{{cite web |date=November 10, 1989 |author1=[[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Thomas, Kevin]] |title=MOVIE REVIEW: Walken Has Purported Close Encounter in 'Communion' |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-11-10-ca-984-story.html |website=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref>

Strieber himself dismissed the film in an interview, noting the interpretation of events was too literal compared to his book. "[The film] probably overstates the case in terms of the way the beings look," he said. "They were very solid, but my glimpses of them were very brief; you see more of them in the movie than I think you should, and there was too much light on them."<ref>{{cite news | last  = Baron| first = David| title = Writer's Close Encounter Lures Moviegoers| newspaper = The Times-Picayune| location = New Orleans, Louisiana | date   = November 26, 1989| page   = F1| url    = https://infoweb.newsbank.com/apps/news/document-view?p=WORLDNEWS&t=&sort=YMD_date%3AD&hide_duplicates=2&maxresults=60&f=advanced&val-base-0=communion%20whitley%20strieber&fld-base-0=alltext&bln-base-1=and&val-base-1=1989&fld-base-1=YMD_date&docref=news/0FADA197073BA286| access-date = August 6, 2025}}</ref>

==Music==
The score was composed by [[Eric Clapton]] and [[Alan Clark (keyboardist)|Alan Clark]], though no official [[soundtrack album]] was ever released. In 2010, the main theme and end credit theme were released by film composer and former [[Oingo Boingo]] guitarist [[Steve Bartek]].
