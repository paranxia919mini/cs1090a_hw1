'''''The Muppet Movie''''' is a 1979 [[Musical film|musical]] [[Road movie|road]] [[comedy film]], directed by [[James Frawley]] and produced by [[Jim Henson]], and the first theatrical film to feature [[the Muppets]]. A co-production between the United Kingdom and the United States, the film was written by ''[[The Muppet Show]]'' writers [[Jerry Juhl]] and [[Jack Burns]]. Produced during the third season of ''The Muppet Show'', the film tells the [[origin story]] of the Muppets, as [[Kermit the Frog]] embarks on a cross-country trip to [[Los Angeles]], encountering several of the Muppets—who all share the same ambition of finding success in professional show business—along the way while being pursued by Doc Hopper, a greedy [[restaurateur]] with intentions of recruiting Kermit to promote his [[frog legs]] business.

The film stars Muppet performers Henson, [[Frank Oz]], [[Jerry Nelson]], [[Richard Hunt (puppeteer)|Richard Hunt]], and [[Dave Goelz]], as well as [[Charles Durning]] and [[Austin Pendleton]], and it features cameo appearances by [[Bob Hope]], [[Dom DeLuise]], [[James Coburn]], [[Cloris Leachman]], [[Richard Pryor]], [[Telly Savalas]], [[Edgar Bergen]] (in his final film appearance before his death), [[Orson Welles]], [[Madeline Kahn]], [[Milton Berle]], [[Carol Kane]], [[Steve Martin]], [[Mel Brooks]], [[Elliott Gould]], and [[Paul Williams (songwriter)|Paul Williams]]. Notable for its [[surreal humour]], [[meta-reference]]s and prolific use of [[Cameo appearance|cameo]]s, ''The Muppet Movie'' was distributed by [[Associated Film Distribution]] in the United Kingdom on May 31, 1979, and in the United States on June 22, 1979.

The film received critical praise, including two [[Academy Award]] nominations for [[Paul Williams (songwriter)|Paul Williams]] and [[Kenneth Ascher]]'s [[Film score|musical score]] and their song "[[Rainbow Connection]]". The film grossed $65.2 million worldwide against an $8 million budget. ''The Muppet Movie'' was followed by seven feature films starring the Muppets as well as several more television series and media. In 2009, the film was deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" by the [[Library of Congress]] and selected for preservation in the [[National Film Registry]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be set between 400 to 700 words only. As of June 1, 2024, the word count for this plot summary is 696 words. -->
The story opens with [[the Muppets]] sitting down for a private screening of a movie that acts as an origin story of how they all met.

[[Kermit the Frog]] lives a simple life in a [[Florida]] swamp. After he plays [[American Banjo Museum#Jim Henson|his banjo]] and sings "[[Rainbow Connection]]", he is approached by Bernie, a [[talent agent]] who encourages him to pursue a career in show business. Inspired, Kermit sets off on a cross-country trip to [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]].

He meets [[Fozzie Bear]], who is working as a hapless [[Stand-up comedy|stand-up comedian]], and invites him on his journey. The two set out in Fozzie's 1951 [[Studebaker Champion|Studebaker]] but are pursued by entrepreneur Doc Hopper and his assistant Max, who try to convince Kermit to be the new spokesfrog of Hopper's struggling [[Deep frying|French-fried]] [[frog legs]] restaurant franchise. Horrified, Kermit refuses, and he and Fozzie drive away. Unwilling to accept Kermit's refusal, Hopper follows the pair and resorts to increasingly forceful means of persuasion. In an old church, Kermit and Fozzie meet the rock band [[Dr. Teeth and the Electric Mayhem]], and their manager [[Scooter (Muppet)|Scooter]], who help them disguise their car. Driving on, they meet and are joined by [[Gonzo (Muppet)|Gonzo]] and his girlfriend [[Camilla the Chicken]], who are also interested in becoming movie stars. They trade in their failing vehicle at a used car lot, where they meet [[Sweetums]]. They invite Sweetums to come with them, but he runs away. The others drive away, only for Sweetums to emerge and reveal that he had only gone to pack his things.

The group meets [[Miss Piggy]] at a county fair and she and Kermit become love-stricken with each other. When they meet for dinner that night, Hopper and Max abduct Miss Piggy as bait to lure Kermit. When they capture Kermit, Hopper orders mad scientist Professor Krassman to give Kermit an electric lobotomy so that he would willingly perform in Hopper's advertisements, but Miss Piggy knocks out Hopper's henchmen and causes Krassman to have his brain fried by his own device. Immediately after the fight, however, Miss Piggy receives a job offer and abandons a devastated Kermit.

Joined by [[Rowlf the Dog]], the Muppets pick up Miss Piggy – who is hitchhiking on the side of the road – and continue their journey; but their car breaks down in the desert. Sitting at a campfire, the group realizes that they will likely miss the auditions the next day. Kermit wanders off, ashamed for bringing his friends on a fruitless journey, but [[Internal monologue|personal reflection]] restores his commitment. He returns to camp, where he discovers that the Electric Mayhem have come to their rescue, [[fourth wall|having learned of their plight by reading ahead in the film's script]]. The Mayhem offer to drive the group the rest of the way in their bus.

The group is warned by a reformed Max that Hopper has hired an assassin, Snake Walker, to kill Kermit. Kermit decides to face his aggressor and proposes a Western-style showdown in a nearby [[ghost town]]. There, they find inventor [[Dr. Bunsen Honeydew]] and his assistant [[Beaker (Muppet)|Beaker]]. Kermit and his friends are saved when one of Dr. Honeydew's inventions, "insta-grow" pills, enlarges Mayhem drummer [[Animal (Muppet)|Animal]], who frightens away Hopper and his henchmen.

Once the Muppets reach the Hollywood studio, they meet executive Lew Lord, who signs them to a "standard 'rich and famous' contract". The first take in their attempt to perform the script goes awry when Gonzo crashes into the prop rainbow, which knocks the scenery down and an explosion blows a hole in the roof of the studio, but a real rainbow shines through the hole and illuminates all the Muppets. Joined by characters from other Henson productions – including ''[[Sesame Street]]'', ''[[Emmet Otter's Jug-Band Christmas]]'', and ''[[The Land of Gorch]]'' – the Muppets sing the final verse in a reprise of "Rainbow Connection". As the film ends, Sweetums tears through the screen, elated that he caught up with them.

==Production==
===Development===
After the popularity of the Muppets had grown following the success of ''[[The Muppet Show]]'', [[Lew Grade]] agreed to finance the production of the film and signed the deal with his company [[ITC Entertainment]],{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=72}} which helped Jim Henson finance the film.<ref>{{Cite book |last=St. Pierre |first=Stephanie |title=The Story of Jim Henson, Creator of the Muppets |publisher=[[Dell Publishing]] |year=1991 |isbn=0673817474 |pages=71}}</ref> After the film's production greenlit, [[Jim Henson]] and selected team members traveled to [[California]] various times to develop the script, music, and infrastructure in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]] to shoot the film.<ref name=":6">{{Cite web |last=Bennett |first=Tara |date=July 25, 2019 |title=The Muppet Movie: An Oral History |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2019/07/25/the-muppet-movie-an-oral-history |access-date=December 19, 2022 |website=[[IGN]]}}</ref> [[James Frawley]] was hired to direct the movie due to the film's challenges.<ref name=":6" />

During Frawley and Henson's first meeting in [[London]], they were concerned that the Muppets would not blend well in [[real life]].<ref name="Frawley" /><ref name=":5">{{Cite news |date=August 31, 1979 |title=Henson's new adventure: 'The Muppet Movie' |pages=21 |work=[[Havre Daily News]] |url=https://www.newspaperarchive.com/us/montana/havre/havre-daily-news/1979/08-31/page-21/ |access-date=December 18, 2022}}</ref> Along with [[Frank Oz]] and the team, they filmed and tested how the characters would appear in real-world locations during the first few days of June 1978 in a UK meadow.<ref name=":6" /><ref name="Frawley">{{cite news|last1=Roessner|first1=Beth|title=First 'Muppets' director recalls original|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/movies/2014/03/21/first-muppets-director-jim-frawley/6715775/|access-date=September 5, 2014|work=USA Today|date=March 22, 2014}}</ref><ref name=":5" /><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=July 4, 2013 |title=7/5/1978 – 'Camera rolls on Muppet Movie.' |url=https://www.henson.com/jimsredbook/2013/07/751978/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140815233640/https://www.henson.com/jimsredbook/2013/07/751978/ |archive-date=August 15, 2014 |access-date=December 18, 2022 |website=Jim Henson's Red Book}}</ref>{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=73}} During filming tests, a cow was ambled near Fozzie for an unexpected rear look at a comparison between fake and natural fur hair.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=73}} The camera tests attracted the investment of [[ITC Entertainment]].<ref name=":6" /> On July 3, 1978, Henson flew to [[Los Angeles]] again to begin shooting for the film.<ref name=":1" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began on July 5, 1978, and continued for 87 days during summer and fall of that year.<ref name=":22">''The Muppet Show Fan Club'' newsletter (vol. 2, no. 1)</ref>{{Sfn|Jay Jones|2013|p=283}} Each minute took a day to be filmed.<ref name=":22"/> Filming locations included [[Albuquerque, New Mexico]] as well as various parts of Los Angeles and [[Northern California]], including the [[San Fernando Valley]].<ref name=":6" /><ref name="Frawley" /><ref name="NMex">{{cite book|title=100 years of filmmaking in New Mexico 1898–1998|publisher=New Mexico Dept. of Tourism|year=1998|page=118}}</ref> The Western street scenes were filmed on the former Columbia Ranch, then known as The Burbank Studios.<ref>{{Cite journal |date=July 1979 |title=American Cinematographer 'Behind the scenes of "The Muppet Movie"' |journal=American Cinematographer |volume=n/a |issue=n/a |pages=35}}</ref> Additionally, the interior shots were filmed at [[Radford Studio Center|CBS-MTM Studios]].<ref name=":6" /> All the sets were elevated by five feet to allow the puppeteers to perform.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |last=Thomas |first=Bob |date=August 22, 1978 |title=Kermit, Miss Piggy, Gonzo all star in 'Muppet Movie' |pages=21 |work=[[Lima News]] |url=https://www.newspaperarchive.com/us/ohio/lima/lima-news/1978/08-22/page-21/ |access-date=December 18, 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last1=Colker |first1=David |last2=Viertel |first2=Jack |date=March 1979 |title=On the Set with the Muppets |pages=18 |work=[[Take One (Canadian magazine)|Take One]] |url=https://archive.org/details/sim_take-one_1979-03_7_4/page/18/mode/2up?q=the+muppet+movie&view=theater |access-date=January 14, 2023}}</ref> According to Henson, the principal photography of the film was slower than in television.<ref name=":0" /> No effects were added after filming concluded.<ref name="Frawley" />

[[Austin Pendleton]] recalled that the film was shot on "a very unhappy set, because Jim [Frawley] was very unhappy directing that movie. And I noticed that was the only time the Muppet people used an outside person to direct a Muppet movie. They never did that again. After that, it was either Jim Henson or Frank Oz. And I would have liked to have been in one of those, because those sets were very harmonious. But this was not."<ref>{{cite web |last=Rabin |first=Nathan |date=July 29, 2009 |title=Austin Pendleton &#124; Film |url=https://www.avclub.com/austin-pendleton-1798217219 |access-date=December 21, 2012 |publisher=The A.V. Club}}</ref>

Several shots required Muppets standing and acting in a full-body shot. To perform Kermit sitting on a log, Henson squeezed into a specially designed metal container complete with an air hose (to breathe), a rubber sleeve which came out of the top to perform Kermit and a monitor to see his performance, and placed himself under the water, log, and the Kermit puppet.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=73}}<ref>{{cite news|last=Swansburg|first=John|title=Muppet Man|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/12/08/books/review/jim-henson-the-biography-by-brian-jay-jones.html?_r=0|access-date=10 April 2014|newspaper=The New York Times|date=6 December 2013}}</ref> He was also assisted in this operation by [[Kathryn Mullen]] and [[Steve Whitmire]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hennes |first=Joe |date=August 1, 2013 |title=Mokey Fraggle Speaks: The Kathy Mullen Interview, part 1 |url=https://toughpigs.com/kathy-mullen-1/ |access-date=November 23, 2023 |website=toughpigs.com}}</ref> During breaks, cups of ice tea were given to Henson through the rubber sleeve since he could not easily leave the tank.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=73}} Rescuers had to stand by the tank to pull Henson out if the tank leaked or the air supply had difficulties.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=73}} The scene took five days to be filmed.{{Sfn|Jay Jones|2013|p=235}}

In order for Kermit to ride a bicycle in a full-body shot, a Kermit puppet with legs was posed onto the seat and his legs and arms were attached to the pedals and handlebars. An overhead crane with a marionette system held the bicycle through strong strings invisible to the camera, guiding the bicycle forward.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Abernathy |first=Kristen |date=24 February 2021 |title=How The Muppet Movie Makes Kermit Ride A Bicycle |url=https://screenrant.com/kermit-bicycle-muppet-movie-effect-how/ |access-date=3 February 2023 |website=Screen Rant}}</ref> The crane and system were out of the camera's frame of vision.<ref name="Frawley" /> Specially made, remote-controlled puppets were placed on the set and controlled by puppeteers out of the frame. A dancing Kermit and [[Fozzie Bear]] were operated by Henson and Oz in front of a [[Chroma key|blue screen]], and they were composited onto a separate reel of the stage.{{Citation needed|date=December 2022}} For scenes involving Fozzie driving a [[Studebaker Champion|Studebaker]], cables, TV monitors, puppeteers, and its Muppets were filled in.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|pp=73–74}} A [[dwarfism|dwarf]] would sit in the trunk and control via remote control. A television monitor showed what was ahead.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=74}}<ref name=":22"/>

The closing reprise of "Rainbow Connection" featured a crowd of more than 250 Muppet characters—virtually every Muppet that had been created up to that point in time.<ref name=":22"/><ref name=":5" /><ref name=":3">{{Cite web |last=Falk |first=Karen |title=Ask Henson - Archives - Questions 50 and 97 |url=https://www.henson.com/ask/ask_archives_body.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20011106163355/http://www.henson.com/ask/ask_archives_body.html |archive-date=November 6, 2001 |access-date=December 23, 2022 |website=Ask Henson... Archives}}</ref> According to Henson Archivist Karen Falk, 137 puppeteers were enlisted from the Puppeteers of America (along with the regular Muppets performers) to perform every Muppet extant.<ref name=":22"/><ref name=":3" /> Prior to the day-long filming of the shot, Henson gave the enthusiastic participants a lesson in the art of cinematic puppetry. The scene involved 150 puppeteers performing in a pit that was {{convert|6|ft}} deep and {{convert|17|ft}} wide.<ref name=":22"/><ref name=":3" /> In September 1978, [[Edgar Bergen]], Henson's idol who appeared in a cameo role, died shortly after completing his scenes. Henson was asked by his family to say a few words with Kermit for his memorial service. Henson agreed, and he attended Bergen's memorial service for its speech.{{Sfn|Durrett|1994|p=75}} Henson dedicated the film to his memory.

===Music===
{{main|The Muppet Movie (soundtrack){{!}}''The Muppet Movie'' (soundtrack)}}

The film's music and lyrics were written by [[Paul Williams (songwriter)|Paul Williams]] and [[Kenneth Ascher]]. Regarding the music's composition, Williams said; "Jim Henson gave you more [creative] freedom than anybody I've ever worked with in my life. I said, 'You want to hear the songs as we're writing them?' He said, 'No. I'll hear them in the studio. I know I'm gonna love them.' You just don't get that kind of freedom on a project these days."<ref name="songfacts">{{cite web| url = http://www.songfacts.com/detail.php?id=3146 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170730233140/http://www.songfacts.com/detail.php%3Fid%3D3146| title = Rainbow Connection by The Muppets | archive-date=July 30, 2017 | website = songfacts.com | access-date = December 7, 2009 | url-status=dead}}</ref> "Never Before, Never Again" was originally sung by [[Johnny Mathis]], but was changed to Miss Piggy when Jim Henson thought it would be funnier if she sang it to herself.<ref name=":3" /> Mathis would later sing the song in the television special ''[[The Muppets Go Hollywood]]''.<ref name=":3" />

"Movin' Right Along", "Never Before, Never Again", and "I Hope That Somethin' Better Comes Along" were shortened in the film, compared to their soundtrack versions, for continuity purposes.{{Citation needed|date=December 2022}} The latter, a duet between Rowlf and Kermit, contained references that the studio considered too mature for children, although the song appeared complete in the British theatrical and home video debut versions.{{Citation needed|date=December 2022}}

===Marketing===
In May 1979, [[CBS]] aired ''[[The Muppets Go Hollywood]]'', a one-hour television special that promoted the then-upcoming release of ''The Muppet Movie''. In April, the film had been promoted when the Muppets hosted ''[[The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson]]''.{{Sfn|Jay Jones|2013|p=291}} Additionally, a book adaptation of ''The Muppet Movie'', adapted by Steven Crist, was published by Peacock Press/[[Bantam Books]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Crist |first=Steven |title=The Muppet Movie |date=1979 |publisher=Peacock Press/[[Bantam Books]] |isbn=978-0810913295}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |date=1986 |title=Something About the Author |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=jMVkAAAAMAAJ |publisher=[[Gale (publisher)|Gale]] |volume=43 |page=118 |isbn=9780810322530 |access-date=May 26, 2020}}</ref>

In [[Austin, Texas]], then-CBS affiliate [[KTBC-TV]] and American International Traveler sponsored a contest where adults who paid to see the film in the area were eligible to win a free trip to [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]].<ref name="Muppet Movie Austin"/>

===Home media===
''The Muppet Movie'' was the first film by [[ITC Films]] to be released on [[home video]] when [[Magnetic Video]] issued it in May 1980, having acquired the video rights to ITC's films. It was reissued in 1982 and 1984 by [[CBS/Fox Video]]. On January 29, 1993, [[Buena Vista Home Video]] re-released the film under their [[Jim Henson Video]] label on VHS and [[LaserDisc]], pricing at $24.99.<ref>{{cite news |last=Spain |first=Tom |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1993/01/14/the-great-muppet-deal/65bbc843-4113-47dd-809a-9b44a6ab1693/ |title=The Great Muppet Deal |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=January 25, 1993 |access-date=August 2, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://variety.com/1992/digital/news/henson-video-line-debuts-jan-29-with-eight-titles-100461/ |title=Henson Video line debuts Jan. 29 with eight titles |magazine=Variety |date=November 10, 1992 |access-date=March 31, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=McCormick |first=Moira |date=January 9, 1993 |title=Greening Of The Kids Mkt.; More Barney; New Discovery |pages=62 |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |url=https://worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/90s/1993/BB-1993-01-09.pdf |access-date=December 24, 2022}}</ref> The movie was reissued again on VHS by [[Columbia TriStar Home Video]] and Jim Henson Home Entertainment on June 1, 1999, followed by a DVD release on June 5, 2001. 

After [[The Walt Disney Company]]'s acquisition of the Muppets in 2004, the film rights to ''The Muppet Movie'' were acquired by Disney; the film was reissued under the [[Walt Disney Pictures]] banner in home media formats and was re-released by [[Buena Vista Home Entertainment]] on DVD on November 29, 2005, as part of the ''Kermit's 50th Anniversary Edition'' line. Disney released the film as the ''Nearly 35th Anniversary Edition'' on [[Blu-ray Disc]] and DVD on August 13, 2013. The film now streams in [[4K Ultra HD]] on [[Disney+]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Truitt|first=Brian|title=Kermit, Fozzie entertain in 'Muppet Movie' camera test|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/movies/2013/08/09/the-muppet-movie-blu-ray-exclusive-clip/2635835/|access-date=12 August 2013|newspaper=USA Today|date=9 August 2013}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
In its first six days at the Leicester Square Theatre, it grossed $31,884.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=June 13, 1979|page=39|title='Muppet' Stout 32G, West End; 'Heaven' Nice 16 1/2G, 'Men' 33G}}</ref> The film would later earn over $65 million in the United States and Canada,{{Sfn|Jay Jones|2013|p=296}} returning $32 million in box office rentals.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=Big Rental Films of 1979 |magazine=Variety |date=January 9, 1980 |page=29}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Harmetz |first=Aljean |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1981/02/24/business/universal-to-market-films-from-afd.html |title=Universal To Market Films from A.F.D. |work=The New York Times |page=D6 |date=February 24, 1981 |access-date=March 31, 2021}}</ref> For 32 years, ''The Muppet Movie'' was the [[List of highest-grossing puppet films|highest-grossing puppet film]] until the release of ''[[The Muppets (2011 film)|The Muppets]]'' in 2011.

The film's successful theatrical release encouraged [[Lew Grade]] into furthering his own film distribution company, which later backfired with the massive box office failures of ''[[Can't Stop the Music]]'' (from [[EMI Films|EMI]]) and ''[[Raise the Titanic (film)|Raise the Titanic]]'' (from [[ITC Films|ITC]]), both released by [[Associated Film Distribution]] just a year later.<ref name="grade">{{cite book |last=Grade |first=Lew |title=Still Dancing: My Story |publisher=William Collins & Sons |year=1987 |page=252 |isbn=0-002-17780-3}}</ref>

===Critical reception===
''The Muppet Movie'' currently holds an 89% approval rating on [[Rotten Tomatoes]], based on 102 reviews. The site's consensus says "''The Muppet Movie'', the big-screen debut of Jim Henson's plush creations, is smart, lighthearted, and fun for all ages."<ref name="RT">{{cite web |title=The Muppet Movie |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/muppet_movie/ |access-date=April 6, 2026 |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 74 out of 100 based on 7 reviews, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Muppet Movie Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-muppet-movie |access-date=December 22, 2022 |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]}}</ref>

The film received highly positive reviews in the United States. [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film three-and-a-half out of four stars. In his favorable review, he was fascinated that "''The Muppet Movie'' not only stars the Muppets but, for the first time, shows us their feet."<ref name="ebert">{{cite news|author=Ebert, Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-muppet-movie-1979|title=The Muppet Movie|work=Chicago Sun-Times|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]|date=November 14, 1979|access-date=February 1, 2012}}</ref> [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' offered equal praise, stating that the film "demonstrates once again that there's always room in movies for unbridled amiability when it is governed by intelligence and wit."<ref>{{cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |title=The Screen: Muppets Go to Hollywood |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1979/06/22/archives/the-screen-muppets-go-to-hollywoodroadiest-road-picture.html |access-date=9 September 2013|newspaper=The New York Times|date=22 June 1979}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' gave the film three-and-a-half stars out of four and called it "surely one of the summer's most entertaining films," which "does a fairly nice job of trying to be all things to all people. Which is not an easy job."<ref>Siskel, Gene (August 3, 1979). [https://www.newspapers.com/clip/37537856/ "The Muppets plant feet firmly on the big screen"]. ''[[Chicago Tribune]]''. ==Legacy==
In 2009, it was selected for preservation in the [[National Film Registry]] by the [[Library of Congress]] for being "culturally, historically or aesthetically significant."<ref name=":4">{{Cite news |date=December 30, 2009 |title=25 new titles added to National Film Registry |website=Yahoo! News |url=https://news.yahoo.com/s/ap/20091230/ap_en_mo/us_classic_films_glance |url-status=dead |access-date=December 30, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100106004526/http://news.yahoo.com/s/ap/20091230/ap_en_mo/us_classic_films_glance |archive-date=January 6, 2010}}</ref> In 2020, "Rainbow Connection" was deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" by the [[Library of Congress]] and selected for preservation in the [[National Recording Registry]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Schuessler |first=Jennifer |date=March 24, 2021 |title=Janet Jackson and Kermit the Frog Added to National Recording Registry |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/24/arts/music/national-recording-registry-janet-jackson.html |access-date=March 24, 2021}}</ref> One of the two 1951 Studebaker Commander 2-door sedans used in the film is now on display at the [[Studebaker National Museum]] in [[South Bend, Indiana]]. The car was used for the driving scenes. The bottom of the front seat was removed, and the puppeteers lay on the floor to move the muppets. The car was driven by an operator seated in the trunk using remote controls. A camera hidden in the "bullet" at the front of the car provided a closed-circuit video feed to a monitor in the trunk for the operator. In July 2025 the car completed an 18-month restoration. {{Citation needed|date=December 2022}}
