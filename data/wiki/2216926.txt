'''Salli Elise Richardson-Whitfield''' (born '''Salli Elise Richardson''', November 23, 1967<ref>Mallegg, Kristin B., ed. (2009). ''[https://archive.org/details/isbn_9781414433363/page/1024/mode/2up?q=%22Salli+Elise+Richardson%22 Who's Who Among African Americans]''. Farmington Hills, MI : Gale. p.&nbsp;1025. {{ISBN|9781414433363}}.</ref>) is an American actress, television director and producer. as an actress, Richardson is known for her role as Angela in the film ''[[A Low Down Dirty Shame]]'' (1994) and for her role as Dr. [[Allison Blake]] on the [[Syfy]] comedy-drama series ''[[Eureka (2006 TV series)|Eureka]]'' (2006–2012).

She is also known for her voice acting as Elisa Maza on the Disney animated series ''[[Gargoyles (TV series)|Gargoyles]]'' (1994–1996),<ref name="autogenerated1">{{cite news |last=Dretzka |first=Gary |date=August 8, 1995 |url=https://www.proquest.com/docview/283973964 |title=What's Dramatic, Fun and Keeping These Actors Busy? Sounds Like Gargoyles |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |page=7 |access-date=April 18, 2015 |archive-date=November 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171107004906/https://pqasb.pqarchiver.com/chicagotribune/doc/283973964.html |id={{ProQuest|283973964}} |url-status=live }}</ref> and as Viveca Foster on the [[CBS]] series ''[[Family Law (American TV series)|Family Law]]'' (1999–2002). Richardson also has appeared in a number of other films such as ''[[The Great White Hype]]'' (1996), ''[[Antwone Fisher (film)|Antwone Fisher]]'' (2002), ''[[Anacondas: The Hunt for the Blood Orchid]]'' (2004) and ''[[I Am Legend (film)|I Am Legend]]'' (2007). She had leading roles in the independent films ''[[Pastor Brown]]'' (2009), ''[[Black Dynamite]]'' (2009) and ''[[I Will Follow (film)|I Will Follow]]'' (2010). 

In the 2010s, Richardson started working as a television director and executive producer, receiving five [[Primetime Emmy Awards]] nominations. In 2024, she became the first black woman to be nominated for [[Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series|Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series]] for ''[[Winning Time: The Rise of the Lakers Dynasty]]'', and in 2026, she became the first black woman to receive two Outstanding Directing for a Drama series nominations for her works on ''[[The Gilded Age (TV series)|The Gilded Age]]'' and ''[[Task (TV series)|Task]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thelagosreview.ng/salli-richardson-whitfield-makes-emmy-history-with-2-drama-directing-nominations/|title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield makes Emmy history with 2 drama directing nominations|first=Toni|last=Kan|date=July 9, 2026}}</ref>

==Early life==
Richardson was born in [[Chicago]], Illinois.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.encyclopedia.com/topic/Salli_Richardson.aspx |title=Salli Richardson Facts, information, pictures &#124; Encyclopedia.com articles about Salli Richardson |publisher=Encyclopedia.com |access-date=May 29, 2014}}</ref> Her mother is of African American ancestry, and her father was of English and Italian descent.<ref>{{cite journal |author=Whitfield, Salli |display-authors=et al. |date=July 1997 |title=Black America and Tiger's Dilemma |journal=Ebony |pages=28–34, 138ff (esp. 29, 34) |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=pi1bXMlgu24C&q=%22SALII+RICHARDSON+Continued%22&pg=PA34 |access-date=March 12, 2019}}</ref> She played tennis in high school at the [[University of Chicago Laboratory Schools]], and launched her acting career in the Kuumba Workshop theater there; she graduated in 1985.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Salli Richardson {{!}} Encyclopedia.com|url=https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/film-and-television-biographies/salli-richardson|access-date=July 5, 2021|website=encyclopedia.com}}</ref>

==Career==
===Acting===
Richardson "began her acting career in the theater before transitioning to roles in television and film".<ref name="tvg">{{cite web |url=https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/salli-richardson-whitfield/bio/268386 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718074215/http://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/salli-richardson-whitfield/bio/268386 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 18, 2011 |title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield Biography |work=[[TV Guide]] |access-date=May 29, 2014}}</ref> In film, she played small roles in ''[[Prelude to a Kiss (film)|Prelude to a Kiss]]'', ''[[Mo' Money]]'', ''[[Posse (1993 film)|Posse]]'', and later had major roles in ''[[Sioux City (film)|Sioux City]]'' and ''[[A Low Down Dirty Shame]]''. From 1994 to 1996 she voiced the character Elisa Maza on the animated series ''[[Gargoyles (TV series)|Gargoyles]]''.<ref name="autogenerated1" /> She had many guest-starring roles in numerous television shows, such as ''[[Star Trek: Deep Space Nine]]'', ''[[New York Undercover]]'', ''[[The Pretender (TV series)|The Pretender]]'', ''[[Stargate SG-1]]'', ''[[NYPD Blue]]'', ''[[House (TV series)|House]]'', ''[[Bones (TV series)|Bones]]'', ''[[Criminal Minds]]'', ''[[NCIS (TV series)|NCIS]]'' and ''[[Castle (TV series)|Castle]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/salli-richardson-whitfield/credits/268386|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100715012824/http://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/salli-richardson-whitfield/credits/268386|url-status=dead|archive-date=July 15, 2010|title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield &#124; TV Guide|website=TV Guide}}</ref>
[[File:Salli Richardson.jpg|thumb|Richardson in 2008]]
Richardson starred opposite [[Dixie Carter]] and [[Kathleen Quinlan]] in the [[CBS]] legal drama series, ''[[Family Law (American TV series)|Family Law]]'', from 1999 to 2002. She had recurring roles as Nancy Adams on ''[[Rude Awakening (TV series)|Rude Awakening]]'', and as Laura on ''[[CSI: Miami]]''. She starred opposite [[Denzel Washington]] in the 2002 drama film ''[[Antwone Fisher (film)|Antwone Fisher]]''.<ref name=rt>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/celebrity/salli_richardson_whitfield|title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield |publisher=Rotten Tomatoes}}</ref> She also appeared in the 2004 horror film ''[[Anacondas: The Hunt for the Blood Orchid]]'', and starred opposite [[Will Smith]] in 2007's post-apocalyptic film ''[[I Am Legend (film)|I Am Legend]]''.<ref name=rt/> She later had a leading role in the independent dramas ''[[Pastor Brown]]'' (2009), and in ''[[I Will Follow (film)|I Will Follow]]'' (2010) directed by [[Ava DuVernay]].<ref name=rt/><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/i-will-follow-film-review-166540|title=I Will Follow: Film Review|website=The Hollywood Reporter|date=March 10, 2011}}</ref> She also starred in ''The Sin Seer'' with [[Isaiah Washington]], set for 2015 release.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.thewrap.com/richard-brooks-salli-richardson-whitfield-join-isaiah-washington-sin-seer/ |title=Richard Brooks, Salli Richardson-Whitfield Join Isaiah Washington in 'The Sin Seer' |publisher=TheWrap |access-date=May 29, 2014 |date=May 8, 2014}}</ref>

From 2006 to 2012, Richardson starred as [[United States Department of Defense|Department of Defense]] agent (and later head of [[Global Dynamics]]) [[Allison Blake]] in the [[Syfy]] comedy-drama series ''[[Eureka (2006 TV series)|Eureka]]''. Her second pregnancy was written into the storyline of the series.<ref name="tvg" /> After the series' cancellation, she was cast as the lead in the [[Lifetime (TV network)|Lifetime]] drama pilot ''The Secret Lives of Wives''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Andreeva |first=Nellie |url=https://deadline.com/2012/08/salli-richardson-to-star-in-lifetimes-secret-lives-of-wives-pilot-324690/ |title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield To Star in Lifetime's 'Secret Lives of Wives' Pilot |work=Deadline Hollywood |access-date=May 29, 2014 |date=August 25, 2012}}</ref> In 2014, Richardson was cast in a recurring role on Lifetime's ''[[The Lottery (TV series)|The Lottery]]'' as the first lady of the United States,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/live-feed/homelands-martin-donovan-joins-lifetimes-661318 |title='Homeland's' Martin Donovan Joins Lifetime's 'Lottery' (Exclusive) |work=The Hollywood Reporter |date=December 2, 2013 |access-date=May 29, 2014}}</ref> but she lost it to Shelley Conn;<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.aenetworks.com/article/lifetimes-new-drama-lottery-premieres-july-20 |title=A+E Networks – Life is Entertaining |publisher=Aenetworks.com |date=May 22, 2014 |access-date=June 4, 2014}}</ref> She also has a role on [[BET]]'s ''[[Being Mary Jane]]'' as an old friend of the lead character.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2014/04/mackenzie-porter-joins-amcs-hell-on-wheels-salli-richardson-whitfield-in-bets-being-mary-jane-711753/ |title=MacKenzie Porter Joins AMC's 'Hell on Wheels'; Salli Richardson-Whitfield in BET's 'Being Mary Jane' |work=Deadline Hollywood |access-date=May 29, 2014 |date=April 8, 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/shadowandact/tv-news-salli-richardson-whitfield-joins-being-mary-jane-omar-j-dorsey-in-ray-donovan-new-oxygen-greenlights |title=TV News: Salli Richardson-Whitfield Joins 'Being Mary Jane,' Omar J. Dorsey In 'Ray Donovan' + New Oxygen Greenlights&#124;Shadow and Act |publisher=Blogs.indiewire.com |access-date=May 29, 2014 |archive-date=April 13, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140413020141/http://blogs.indiewire.com/shadowandact/tv-news-salli-richardson-whitfield-joins-being-mary-jane-omar-j-dorsey-in-ray-donovan-new-oxygen-greenlights |url-status=dead }}</ref> In 2015, Richardson was cast in the [[Freeform (TV channel)|ABC Family]] series, ''[[Stitchers]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.thewrap.com/abc-family-adds-3-to-cast-of-first-crime-procedural-stitchers/ |title=ABC Family Adds 3 to Cast of First Crime Procedural 'Stitchers' |work=TheWrap |access-date=April 18, 2015 |date=January 14, 2015}}</ref> The series was canceled after three seasons in 2017.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.tvline.com/news/stitchers-cancelled-freeform-no-season-4-866725/|title=Stitchers Cancelled at Freeform|first1=Dave|last1=Nemetz|website=TV Line|date=September 15, 2017}}</ref>

===Directing===
Richardson has also worked as a director of episodic television, her latest being episodes 5 and 6 of the first season of Wheel of Time for Amazon. Following her directorial debut on two episodes of her show ''[[Eureka (2006 TV series)|Eureka]]'',<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://amsterdamnews.com/news/2017/mar/30/underground-director-salli-richardson-whitfield/|title='Underground' director Salli Richardson-Whitfield|website=amsterdamnews.com|date=March 30, 2017}}</ref> in 2016 she directed two episodes of [[Ava DuVernay]]'s drama series for [[Oprah Winfrey Network]], ''[[Queen Sugar]]'' (on which her husband [[Dondre Whitfield]] appears as a series regular).<ref name="QueenSugar">{{Cite news|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2016/09/ava-duvernay-queen-sugar-interview|title=Ava DuVernay and ''Queen Sugar'' Look Like the Future of Television|date=March 30, 2017|work=Essence|access-date=April 2, 2017}}</ref> In 2016, Richardson also directed an episode of the historical action-drama ''[[Underground (TV series)|Underground]]'' for [[WGN America]].<ref name="Underground">{{Cite news|url=http://www.essence.com/entertainment/underground-director-nok-aaut-age-black-female-director|title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield On ''Underground''{{'}}s "Nok Aaut" & The Age of the Black Female Director|date=March 30, 2017|work=Essence|access-date=March 31, 2017}}</ref> In 2017, she directed two episodes of BET drama ''[[Rebel (2017 TV series)|Rebel]]'', and [[Shonda Rhimes]]' ''[[Scandal (American TV series)|Scandal]]''. Her comedy directing credits include ''[[Survivor's Remorse]]'', ''[[I'm Dying Up Here]]'', ''[[Black-ish]]'' and ''[[Dear White People (TV series)|Dear White People]]''. In 2018, she also directed the 16th episode of ''[[Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.]]''{{'}}s fifth-season episode "[[Inside Voices]]".<ref name="Brodwayworld-20180406">{{cite web |url=https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/Scoop-Coming-Up-On-All-New-MARVELS-AGENTS-OF-SHIELD-on-ABC-Today-April-6-2018-20180405 |title=Scoop: Coming Up On All New MARVEL'S AGENTS OF S.H.I.E.L.D on ABC - Today, April 6, 2018 |date=April 6, 2018 |access-date=April 7, 2018 |publisher=BWW}}</ref> Her other notable directing credits include ''[[Chicago Med]]'', ''[[Luke Cage (TV series)|Luke Cage]]'', ''[[Black Lightning (TV series)|Black Lightning]]'', ''[[The Punisher (TV series)|The Punisher]]'', ''[[American Gods (TV series)|American Gods]]'', ''[[See (TV series)|See]]'' and ''[[Altered Carbon (TV series)|Altered Carbon]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://theshadowleague.com/salli-richardson-whitfield-from-screen-queen-to-directorial-maestro/|title=Salli Richardson Whitfield: From Screen Queen To Directorial Maestro|date=May 9, 2019|website=The Shadow League}}</ref>

In [[50th NAACP Image Awards|2019]], Richardson received an [[NAACP Image Award for Outstanding Directing in a Drama Series]] nomination for her work directing "I Get Physical" episode of ''Luke Cage''.<ref name="2019NAACP">{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/2019-naacp-image-awards-winners-complete-list-1191277/item/outstanding-directing-a-dramatic-series-1174419|title=Outstanding Directing in a Dramatic Series - NAACP Awards: 'Black-ish,' 'Black Panther' Top Winners List|website=The Hollywood Reporter|date=March 30, 2019}}</ref> Also in 2019, she won a [[Black Reel Awards of 2019|Black Reel Award for Outstanding Directing in a Comedy Series]] for directing "Black Like Us" episode of ''Black-ish''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://blackreelawards.com/brats-winners/|title=BRATs Winners|date=August 8, 2019|access-date=February 1, 2020|archive-date=October 27, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211027185946/http://blackreelawards.com/brats-winners/|url-status=dead}}</ref> In September 2020, she signed a project development deal with [[HBO]].<ref name=hbo>{{Cite web|last=White|first=Peter|date=September 1, 2020|title=HBO Strikes Overall Deal With Salli Richardson-Whitfield; Actor-Turned-Director To Helm Eps Of 'The Gilded Age' & Adam McKay's Lakers Drama|url=https://deadline.com/2020/09/hbo-overall-deal-salli-richardson-whitfield-1203028630/|access-date=September 2, 2020|website=Deadline|language=en}}</ref> Richardson produces and had had directed episodes of HBO period drama ''[[The Gilded Age (TV series)|The Gilded Age]]'' and ''[[Adam McKay]]'s [[Winning Time: The Rise of the Lakers Dynasty]]''.<ref name=hbo/> Also in 2020, she directed the ''Zoom Where It Happens'' special ''The One with the Diverse Cast'', a ''[[Friends]]'' one-night reboot starring [[Sterling K. Brown]], [[Uzo Aduba]], [[Ryan Michelle Bathe]], [[Aisha Hinds]], [[Kendrick Sampson]] and [[Jeremy Pope (actor)|Jeremy Pope]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2020/09/friends-all-black-cast-table-read-set-with-all-black-cast-gabrielle-union-sterling-k-brown-uzo-aduba-1234581482/|title='Friends' Table Read Set With All-Black Cast Including Sterling K. Brown & Uzo Aduba; Gabrielle Union To Host|first1=Erik|last1=Pedersen|website=Deadline|date=September 21, 2020}}</ref>

In 2024, she became the first black woman to be nominated for [[Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series|Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series]] in [[76th Primetime Emmy Awards]] for her work on "BEAT L.A." episode of ''Winning Time: The Rise of the Lakers Dynasty''.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2024/07/salli-richardson-whitfield-emmy-history-tv-drama-directing-1236018316/|title=Double Emmy Nominee Salli Richardson-Whitfield Makes History In Drama Directing, Talks ‘Winning Time’ Cancellation & ‘The Gilded Age’ Season 3|website=Deadline Hollywood|date=July 24, 2024|first=Nellie|last=Andreeva|accessdate=July 10, 2026}}</ref> 

In 2026, she became the first black woman to receive two Outstanding Directing for a Drama series nominations for her works on "My Mind Is Made Up" episode of ''The Gilded Age'' and "[[Out beyond ideas of wrongdoing and rightdoing, there is a river.]]" episode of ''[[Task (TV series)|Task]]'' in [[78th Primetime Emmy Awards]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2026/07/salli-richardson-whitfield-emmy-nominations-gilded-age-task-1236977436/|title=Salli Richardson-Whitfield Makes Emmy History Again As First Black Woman To Receive 2 Drama Series Directing Noms|website=Deadline Hollywood|date=July 8, 2026|accessdate=July 10, 2026|first=Denise|last=Petski}}</ref>

==Personal life==
In September 2002, she married fellow actor [[Dondre Whitfield]]. She and Whitfield have one daughter and one son.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://people.com/parents/dondre-and-salli-richardson-whitfield-welcome-son-dre-terrell/|title=Dondre and Salli Welcome Son Dre Terrell Whitfield |website=People|date=February 25, 2009 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20090226130758/http://celebrity-babies.com/2009/02/25/dondre-and-salli-richardson-whitfield-welcome-son-dre-terrell/ | archive-date = February 26, 2009}}</ref>
