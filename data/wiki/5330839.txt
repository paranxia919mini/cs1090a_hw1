'''''Parents''''' is a 1988 American [[horror film]]<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Boston Hassle]]|url=https://bostonhassle.com/diractors-parents-1989-dir-bob-balaban/|title=Diractors: Parents (1989) Dir. Bob Balaban|date=November 7, 2025|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260511042321/https://bostonhassle.com/diractors-parents-1989-dir-bob-balaban/|archive-date=May 11, 2026|last=Draper|first=Jack}}</ref> directed by [[Bob Balaban]] in his [[List of directorial debuts|directorial debut]], and starring [[Randy Quaid]], [[Mary Beth Hurt]], [[Sandy Dennis]], and [[Bryan Madorsky]]. A [[satire]] on mid-century American suburban life and [[dysfunctional family|dysfunctional families]],<ref name=Damaske>{{cite web|url=https://www.joblo.com/parents-best-horror-movie-you-never-saw/|work=[[JoBlo.com]]|last=Damaske|first=Damion|title=Parents (1989), Randy Quaid, Horror Comedy- Best Horror Movie You Never Saw!|date=January 9, 2021|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240712182724/https://www.joblo.com/parents-best-horror-movie-you-never-saw/|archive-date=July 12, 2024}}</ref><ref name=Foreman>{{cite web|last1=Foreman|first1=Alison|last2=Zilko|first2=Christian|url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/best-of/parents-randy-quaid-midnight-movie-cannibalism-1234897797/|work=[[IndieWire]]|title=‘Parents’ Is the Middling ’80s Cannibalism Classic Worth Reheating — Again and Again|date=August 18, 2023|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230902051835/https://www.indiewire.com/features/best-of/parents-randy-quaid-midnight-movie-cannibalism-1234897797/|archive-date=September 2, 2023}}</ref> the film centers on a 10-year old boy in 1950s [[California]] who suspects that his parents are secretly [[Human cannibalism|cannibalistic]] serial killers.

Released by [[Vestron Pictures]] in December 1988, the film received a mixed response from critics and fared poorly commercially. Randy Quaid's performance earned him an [[Independent Spirit Award]] nomination for [[Independent Spirit Award for Best Male Lead|Best Male Lead]], while Quaid and Madorsky shared [[Fantafestival|Fantafestival Award for Best Actor]] honors at [[Fantafestival]]. Madorsky was also nominated for a [[Saturn Award]].

== Plot ==
In 1958, the Laemle family—father Nick, mother Lily, and their 10-year-old son Michael—move from [[Massachusetts]] to a [[California]]n suburban neighborhood. As young boy Michael is very socially awkward and also has an overly-active imagination, he has trouble making friends at school. He is also prone to strange and disturbing dreams, such as dreaming that he has jumped into bed, only for it to collapse into a pool of blood.

Emotionally distraught from the move and the dreams, Michael is traumatized after accidentally stumbling upon his parents having sex in the living room, which he envisions as a blood-soaked affair in which they appear to be biting one another. He is further disturbed after watching his father cutting into a corpse in the Division of Human Testing at Toxico, where Nick is developing an [[Agent Orange]]–style chemical defoliant for use in jungles. Michael finds some consolation in Sheila, a classmate whose own parents, acquaintances of Nick and Lily, are concealing a [[dysfunctional family|dysfunctional marriage]].

As time progresses, Michael begins to suspect that his parents are [[Human cannibalism|cannibals]], after he seemingly discovers dismembered body parts hanging on a [[meat hook]] in the basement. Michael is convinced that what he has seen is true, much to the chagrin of his school guidance counselor, Millie Dew, who assumes Michael's claim to be a fantasy.

One afternoon, Millie goes home with Michael in order to convince him that he is imagining everything, only for the two of them to find a corpse in the basement. Michael flees to his bedroom while Millie is forced into a pantry and attacked by an unseen assailant with a butcher knife, before being bludgeoned with a golf club. When Nick and Lily arrive home, Michael attacks his father. Later that evening, Nick tries to feed Michael human meat from Millie's corpse, assuring him that he will develop a taste for it like his mother did. Lily smiles in agreement, but Michael fights back and manages to stab his father in the shoulder. In a rage, Nick tries to kill Michael, but Lily intervenes and stabs Nick in the chest before he turns the knife on her, fatally wounding her.

Michael is chased through the house by his injured father, who accidentally runs into a gas line due to his injuries. Cornering Michael in the basement, Nick breaks the gas line and then runs into a shelf of wine bottles, which he pulls down onto himself, presumably pinning him. As gas fills the room, Michael has barely enough time to escape before the gas ignites and blows up the house.

Some time later, Michael has been placed under the care of his paternal grandparents. After putting him to bed, Michael's grandparents leave him a midnight snack of a suspicious-looking meat sandwich, implying that his father's cannibalism was a learned behavior.

== Production ==
===Development===
Screenwriter [[Christopher Hawthorne]] was inspired to write an unconventional horror film centered on a family: "What I asked myself was, 'What are horror movies really about?' They are about, as what my experience was [as a child]—you're alone, you're in your bed—what's out there in the dark? And the answer to that is, it's mom and dad out there in the dark."<ref name=Leftovers/> Hawthorne felt that the film was "not a movie about a cannibalism," but rather a film "about [[alcoholism]] and abuse."<ref name=Leftovers>{{cite av media|title=Leftovers to Be with Screenwriter Christopher Hawthorne|date=2017|medium=[[Blu-ray]] short|publisher=[[Vestron Pictures]]|oclc=1015886312 |work=Parents}}</ref>

Hawthorne initially became connected with producer [[Ray Stark]] through his friendship with producer Mary Ann Page.<ref name=Leftovers/> Stark expressed interest in producing ''Parents'' after reading Hawthorne's screenplay, but [[Vestron Pictures]], who had acquired the screenplay, refused to pay Stark his requested fee.<ref name=Leftovers/> The film was ultimately produced by Bonnie Palef.

Hawthorne originally considered [[Todd Solondz]] as a director for the film, but this never reached fruition as he felt that the two had creative differences at the time.<ref name=Leftovers/> [[Bob Balaban]], an actor and first-time director, was impressed by Hawthorne's screenplay and approached Vestron to direct, after which he was hired.<ref name=Leftovers/>

===Casting===
[[Christopher Walken]] was originally considered for the role of Nick Laemle before [[Randy Quaid]] was cast in the part.<ref name=Leftovers/> Balaban, who was a personal friend of [[Mary Beth Hurt]], cast her in the role of Lily Laemle.<ref name=Leftovers/> [[Bryan Madorsky]] was cast as Michael, the young son of Nick and Lily.

===Filming===
''Parents'' was filmed in [[Toronto]], Ontario, Canada.<ref name=variety>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/1988/film/reviews/parents-1200427889/|title=Review: 'Parents'|author=<!-- Staff -->|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|year=1989|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250221082218/https://variety.com/1988/film/reviews/parents-1200427889/|archive-date=February 21, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> Exteriors were shot in Toronto on location, while the interiors of the Laemles' [[mid-century modern]] home were filmed on original sets.<ref name=InsideOut/> Balaban partly furnished the interiors with his own period furniture.{{sfn|Balaban|Palef|2017|loc=10:17}}

The film's original cinematographer, [[Ernest Day]], was replaced by [[Robin Vidgeon]] shortly after production began due to Day's wife falling ill.<ref name=InsideOut>{{cite av media|title=Inside Out with Director of Photography Robin Pidgeon|date=2017|medium=[[Blu-ray]] short|publisher=[[Vestron Pictures]]|oclc=1015886312 |work=Parents}}</ref> At the time, Vidgeon had recently completed shooting on ''[[Mr. North]]'' (1988) and ''[[Hellraiser]]'' (1987).<ref name=InsideOut/>

During filming, Balaban refused to have young Madorsky to read the film's screenplay or learn lines in advance, instead opting to "walk him through the scenes and gently tell him what to say."{{sfn|Balaban|Palef|2017|loc=4:32}} Randy Quaid met his future wife, Evi Motolanez, during production, as she worked as a personal driver for him.{{sfn|Balaban|Palef|2017|loc=16:52}}

== Release ==
''Parents'' was released theatrically in [[Buffalo, New York]] on December 9, 1988.<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Buffalo News]]|date=December 4, 1988|page=G-2|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-buffalo-news/197298830/|title=This Week|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-buffalo-news/197298915/|work=[[The Buffalo News]]|date=December 9, 1988|title=''Parents'' – Starts Today!|via=Newspapers.com|page=27}}</ref> It premiered the following month in New York City and Los Angeles on January 27, 1989.<ref name=Haun>{{cite news|last=Haun|first=Harry|title=Acting or Directing? He'll Take Both|page=35|work=[[New York Daily News]]|via=Newspapers.com|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/daily-news/197298983/|date=January 24, 1989}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=''Parents'' – Starts Today|date=January 27, 1989|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|page=15|via=Newspapers.com|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/197310507/}}</ref>

=== Home media === 
''Parents'' was released on [[DVD]] on May 25, 1999 by [[Artisan Entertainment]] in [[open matte|unmatted]] full-screen format.<ref>{{cite av media|title=Parents|oclc= 44938805|medium=[[DVD]]|year=1999|publisher=[[Artisan Entertainment]]}}</ref> The original DVD was out of print for a brief period of time, before the film was re-released in the DVD format as a double feature with the film ''[[Fear (1990 film)|Fear]]'', and presented for the first time in [[widescreen]] since its original theatrical release.

[[Lionsgate Studios|Lionsgate]] released the film on [[Blu-ray]] on January 31, 2017, as part of their [[Vestron Video#Vestron Video Collector's Series|Vestron Video Collector's Series]] line.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[DVD Talk]]|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/review/71840|title=Parents|date=February 28, 2017|last=Remer|first=Justin|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240421111957/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/review/71840|archive-date=April 21, 2024}}</ref>

== Reception ==
===Box office===
''Parents'' grossed $870,532 in the US on a budget of $3 million.<ref name=mojo/>

===Critical response===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|63||16|consensus=|access-date=May 10, 2026|ref=y}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 66 out of 100, based on 5 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Parents'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/ |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=May 10, 2026 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' rated it two out of four stars, writing that the film's tone never satisfyingly settles on satire, comedy, or horror.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://rogerebert.com/reviews/parents-1989|title=Parents|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|date=April 7, 1989|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251215134741/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/parents-1989|archive-date=December 15, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote "There is not enough weight or complexity to the material to justify the serious approach, and while the potential for considerable black comedy exists, Balaban only scratches the surface. The laughs never come."<ref name=variety/> [[Gene Siskel]] surprised Ebert on their TV show when he said that he actually enjoyed the film and found its weirdness and style entertaining. [[Caryn James]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote: "The satire of the 50s is more bland than biting, dependent on authentically garish costumes and sets. And when the horror-film scenes begin to intrude on normal life (what is hanging from the cellar ceiling, anyway?) Mr. Balaban can't make the dark elements seem comic enough to mesh with the rest of this nightmarish joke."<ref>{{cite news |last=James |first=Caryn|author-link=Caryn James |date=January 27, 1989 |title=Review/Film; Dubious Housekeeping in Balaban's <nowiki>'</nowiki>''Parents''<nowiki>'</nowiki> |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1989/01/27/movies/review-film-dubious-housekeeping-in-balaban-s-parents.html|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171220022436/https://www.nytimes.com/1989/01/27/movies/review-film-dubious-housekeeping-in-balaban-s-parents.html|archive-date=December 20, 2017}}</ref> Michael Wilmington of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' praised the lead performances of Quaid and Hurt, but criticized its screenplay, writing that it "makes sense only on its own symbolic level; strip away the symbols and the story becomes scatterbrained."<ref>{{cite news|last=Wilmington|first=Michael|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/197310587/|date=January 27, 1989|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|page=7|title=Two Perfect 'Parents,' Despite the Script|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

Dorothy Keenan of ''[[The Buffalo News]]'' praised the film as "among the strangest, most peculiar but nevertheless amusing horror films to come along in some time."<ref>{{cite news|work=[[The Buffalo News]]|date=December 13, 1988|last=Keenan|first=Dorothy|page=C-5|title=Humorous Horror Puts 'Parents' In a Frenzy}}</ref> Writing for ''[[The Washington Post]]'', both [[Hal Hinson]] and [[Desson Thomson|Desson Howe]] called it a flawed but impressive debut.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/parentsrhinson_a0a8eb.htm|title='Parents' (R)|last=Hinson|first=Hal|author-link=Hal Hinson|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|date=March 17, 1989|archive-date= March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304210018/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/parentsrhinson_a0a8eb.htm|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/parentsrhowe_a0b1e8.htm|title='Parents' (R)|last=Howe|first=Desson|author-link=Desson Thomson|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|date=March 17, 1989|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180810173538/https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/parentsrhowe_a0b1e8.htm|archive-date=August 10, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Kim Newman]] of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' gave the film a favorable assessment, deeming it an "unfairly neglected, perfectly creepy and disturbing suburban bizarro drama."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://empireonline.com/movies/parents/review/ |title=''Parents'' Review|author-link=Kim Newman|last=Newman |first=Kim |date=March 2, 2007 |website=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241214044712/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/parents-review/|archive-date=December 14, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Legacy==
The film has developed a [[cult film|cult following]].<ref>{{cite journal|title=Movies|last=Denby|first=David|authorlink=David Denby|journal=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|volume=28|number=16|date=April 17, 1995|page=128|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=UOMCAAAAMBAJ&q=%22parents%22+%22bob+balaban%22+%22cult%22&pg=PA128 |access-date=August 6, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.salon.com/2010/02/09/double_bill_4/ |title=The Perfect Double Bill: <nowiki>'</nowiki>''A Serious Man''<nowiki>' and '</nowiki>''Parents''<nowiki>'</nowiki> |last=Nelson |first=Erik |date=February 10, 2010 |website=[[Salon.com|Salon]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201226124351/https://www.salon.com/2010/02/09/double_bill_4/|archive-date=December 26, 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.filmlandempire.com/2015/07/bfi-cult-parents-by-bob-balaban.html |title=FilmLand Empire: BFI Cult: ''Parents'' by Bob Balban |last=Bitel |first=Anton |date=July 4, 2015 |website=FilmLand Empire |archive-date=May 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160505164918/http://www.filmlandempire.com/2015/07/bfi-cult-parents-by-bob-balaban.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Tyler Eschberger of ''[[Bloody Disgusting]]'' wrote in a 2022 retrospective: "The tone of 1989’s ''Parents'' is one of claustrophobia and nightmarish paranoia. An unease and dread permeate the film. Balaban employs the “is it all in their head?” trope for the majority of the film’s runtime. We aren’t sure if Michael’s suspicions are real or if he’s a troubled boy with an overactive imagination. This aspect of the narrative lends ''Parents'' an undercurrent of sadness and tragedy you might not expect at first glance."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|title=Mystery Meat: 1989’s ‘Parents’ is a Wonderfully Quirky Cannibal Movie [Hidden Gems]|date=March 18, 2022|last=Eschberger|first=Tyler|url-status=live|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3707258/mystery-meat-1989s-parents-is-a-wonderfully-quirky-cannibal-movie-hidden-gems/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220316135139/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3707258/mystery-meat-1989s-parents-is-a-wonderfully-quirky-cannibal-movie-hidden-gems/|archive-date=March 16, 2022}}</ref>
