'''''The Hateful Eight''''' is a 2015 American [[Western film|Western]] [[mystery film|mystery]] [[thriller film]] written and directed by [[Quentin Tarantino]]. It stars [[Samuel L. Jackson]], [[Kurt Russell]], [[Jennifer Jason Leigh]], [[Walton Goggins]], [[Demián Bichir]], [[Tim Roth]], [[Michael Madsen]], and [[Bruce Dern]] as eight dubious strangers who seek refuge from a blizzard in a [[stagecoach]] [[layover|stopover]] a decade after the [[American Civil War]].

Tarantino announced the film in November 2013. He conceived it as a novel and sequel to his 2012 film ''[[Django Unchained]]'' before deciding to make it a standalone film. After the script [[Internet leak|leaked]] in January 2014, he decided to abandon the production and publish it as a novel instead. In April 2014, Tarantino directed a live reading of the script at the [[Ace Hotel Los Angeles#United Artists Theater|United Artists Theater]] in Los Angeles, before reconsidering a new draft and resuming the project. [[Principal photography|Filming]] began in January 2015 near [[Telluride, Colorado]]. Italian composer [[Ennio Morricone]] composed the [[The Hateful Eight (soundtrack)|original score]], his first complete Western score in 34 years (and the last before his death in 2020), his first for a high-profile Hollywood production since 2000, and the only original score for a Tarantino film.

Distributed by [[the Weinstein Company]] in the United States, ''The Hateful Eight'' was released on December 25, 2015, in a limited [[Roadshow theatrical release|roadshow]] release on [[70 mm film]], before expanding wide theatrically five days later. The film was praised for its direction, screenplay, score, cinematography, and performances, though its editing, length, the depiction of race relations and the violent treatment of Leigh's character divided opinions. It grossed over $161 million worldwide. For his work on the score, Morricone won his only [[Academy Award for Best Original Score]] after five previous nominations. The film also earned [[Academy Awards|Oscar]] nominations for [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] (Leigh) and [[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] ([[Robert Richardson (cinematographer)|Robert Richardson]]).

In 2019, a re-edited version of the film was released as a four-episode [[miniseries]] on [[Netflix]] with the subtitle ''Extended Version''. ''The Hateful Eight'' was Tarantino's final collaboration with [[Bob Weinstein|Bob]] and [[Harvey Weinstein]] before [[Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse cases|allegations of sexual abuse]] were made against the latter in 2017, which led to Tarantino severing all ties with the brothers.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 and 700 words. -->
In the 1870s, [[bounty hunter]] and [[Union Army]] veteran cavalryman Major Marquis Warren heads to Red Rock, [[Wyoming Territory]], with three bounty corpses. His horse gives out and, faced with a blizzard, Warren hitches a ride on a stagecoach driven by O.B. Jackson. Aboard is bounty hunter John Ruth, handcuffed to fugitive "Crazy" Daisy Domergue, whom he is taking to Red Rock to be hanged. Warren and Ruth had previously bonded over Warren's personal letter from [[Abraham Lincoln]]. Chris Mannix, whose father, Erskine, led a [[Lost Cause of the Confederacy|Lost-Causer]] militia called Mannix's Marauders, claims to be Red Rock's new sheriff and joins them. During the trip, Ruth learns about the Confederate bounty on Warren's head for breaking out of and setting fire to a [[prisoner-of-war camp]].

They seek refuge from the blizzard at Minnie's Haberdashery, tended by Bob, a Mexican who claims to be watching the haberdashery in Minnie's and her husband Sweet Dave's absence. The lodge shelters local hangman Oswaldo Mobray, cowboy Joe Gage, and Confederate general Sanford Smithers, who is planning to erect a [[cenotaph]] for his missing son, Chester Charles. Suspicious, Ruth disarms all but Warren. While Mannix views Smithers as a war hero, Warren wants him dead as revenge for ordering the execution of black [[Prisoner of war|prisoners of war]] at [[Battle of Baton Rouge (1862)|Baton Rouge]]. At dinner, Mannix surmises that the Lincoln letter is fake. Warren responds to Ruth's disappointment by saying his forged letter buys him leeway with whites.

After a small talk, Warren puts one of his guns next to Smithers and tells him he knew his son, claiming that when Smithers' son tried to claim the bounty on his head, he orally raped and murdered him. When Smithers reaches for the gun, Warren kills him.

During the confrontation, the coffee is poisoned, which Daisy silently witnessed. Ruth and O.B. drink the coffee, and both begin vomiting blood; O.B. dies, and Daisy taunts the dying Ruth who realizes she is complicit in the poisoning and attacks her, but she shoots him to death with his own gun. Warren disarms Daisy, leaving her shackled to Ruth's corpse, and holds the others at gunpoint except Mannix, who nearly drank the poisoned coffee himself. Mannix suspects Gage poisoned the coffee, while Warren deduces, with evidence, that Bob is lying about looking after the haberdashery and kills him. When Warren threatens to kill Daisy, Gage admits that he poisoned the coffee. An unknown man hiding under the floorboards shoots Warren in the groin, and Mobray and Mannix shoot and wound one another.

A flashback shows Bob, Mobray, Gage, and Daisy's brother Jody arriving at the lodge hours earlier. They killed Minnie, Sweet Dave, and all their employees, but spared Smithers who was already there to create a believable setting in exchange for his silence. Once they finished hiding the bodies, cleaning the store, and hiding weapons for future use, Jody hid in the cellar as Ruth, Daisy, O.B., Warren and Mannix arrived.

In the present, Warren and Mannix, both seriously wounded, hold Daisy, Gage, and Mobray at gunpoint. When they threaten to kill Daisy, Jody surrenders and is executed by Warren. The surviving gang members claim fifteen hired guns are waiting in Red Rock and offer a deal: if Mannix kills Warren, they will spare him and allow him to collect the bounty on the dying Mobray and the deceased Bob (whose real names are revealed as "English" Pete Hicox and Marco "the Mexican", respectively). Warren kills Hicox when he tries to persuade Mannix. Warren and Mannix then kill Gage (whose real name is "Grouch" Douglas) when he grabs a pistol hidden under a table.
 
Mannix hears Daisy's proposal but deduces she is lying. When Mannix faints from blood loss, Daisy chops off Ruth's arm to move freely and goes for a gun, but Mannix reawakens and wounds her. Mannix and Warren hang Daisy from the rafters in honor of Ruth, who always brought his bounty to the gallows (which earned him the nickname "the Hangman"). As they lie dying, Mannix reads aloud Warren's fake Lincoln letter, complimenting its detail.

==Production==
===Development===
In November 2013, writer-director [[Quentin Tarantino]] said he was working on another [[Western (genre)|Western]]. He initially attempted the story as a novel, a sequel to his film ''[[Django Unchained]]'' (2012),<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2013/nov/27/quentin-tarantino-next-film-western-django-unchained |title=Quentin Tarantino says next film will be another western |last=Beaumont-Thomas |first=Ben |work=[[The Guardian]] |location=UK |date=November 27, 2013 |access-date=November 7, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151110004128/http://www.theguardian.com/film/2013/nov/27/quentin-tarantino-next-film-western-django-unchained |archive-date=November 10, 2015 |url-status=live }}</ref> titled ''Django in White Hell'' but realized that the Django character did not fit the story.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.ew.com/article/2015/12/11/quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-django-novel |title=Quentin Tarantino explains how Hateful Eight began as a Django novel |last=Staskiewicz |first=Keith |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=December 11, 2015 |access-date=January 6, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160104100518/http://www.ew.com/article/2015/12/11/quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-django-novel |archive-date=January 4, 2016 |url-status=live }}</ref> On January 11, 2014, the title was announced as ''The Hateful Eight''.<ref>{{cite news |last=Kit |first=Borys |title=Quentin Tarantino's New Movie Sets Title, Begins Casting |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/quentin-tarantinos-new-movie-sets-668946/ |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=January 11, 2014 |archive-date=November 23, 2022 |access-date=November 23, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221123041558/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/quentin-tarantinos-new-movie-sets-668946/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The film was inspired by the 1960s [[Western (genre)|Western]] TV series ''[[Bonanza]]'', ''[[The Virginian (TV series)|The Virginian]]'', and ''[[The High Chaparral]]''. Tarantino said:

{{blockquote|Twice per season, those shows would have an episode where a bunch of outlaws would take the lead characters hostage. They would come to the Ponderosa and hold everybody hostage, or go to Judge Garth's place – [[Lee J. Cobb]] played him – in ''The Virginian'' and take hostages. There would be a guest star like [[David Carradine]], [[Darren McGavin]], [[Claude Akins]], [[Robert Culp]], [[Charles Bronson]], or [[James Coburn]]. I don't like that storyline in a modern context, but I love it in a Western, where you would pass halfway through the show to find out if they were good or bad guys, and they all had a past that was revealed. I thought, 'What if I did a movie starring nothing but those characters? No heroes, no [[Michael Landon]]. Just a bunch of nefarious guys in a room, all telling backstories that may or may not be true. Trap those guys together in a room with a blizzard outside, give them guns, and see what happens.<ref>{{cite news | last=Fleming | first=Mike Jr. | date=November 10, 2014 | url=https://deadline.com/2014/11/quentin-tarantino-retirement-hateful-eight-international-release-1201280583/ | title=Quentin Tarantino on Retirement, Grand 70 MM Intl Plans For 'The Hateful Eight | website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] | access-date=March 18, 2015 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150315043043/http://deadline.com/2014/11/quentin-tarantino-retirement-hateful-eight-international-release-1201280583/ | archive-date=March 15, 2015 | url-status=live }}</ref>}}

====Antique guitar incident====
{{Quote box|width=30%|quote=We were informed that it was an accident on set... We assumed that a scaffolding or something fell on it. We understand that things happen, but at the same time we can't take this lightly. All this about the guitar being smashed being written into the script and that somebody just didn't tell the actor, this is all new information to us. We didn't know anything about the script or Kurt Russell not being told that it was a priceless, irreplaceable artifact from the Martin Museum.|source=Dick Boak, director of Martin Guitar Museum<ref name="reverb.com">{{Cite web|url=https://reverb.com/news/cf-martin-responds-to-the-destruction-of-145-year-old-guitar-on-hateful-eight-set|title=Martin Responds to "Hateful Eight" Destruction of Antique Six String|website=reverb.com|date=February 4, 2016 |access-date=July 17, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170222053647/https://reverb.com/news/cf-martin-responds-to-the-destruction-of-145-year-old-guitar-on-hateful-eight-set|archive-date=February 22, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref>}}
The guitar destroyed by Russell's character was not a prop but an antique 1870s [[C. F. Martin & Company|Martin guitar]] lent by the Martin Guitar Museum. According to sound mixer [[Mark Ulano]], the guitar was supposed to have been switched with a copy to be destroyed, but this was not communicated to Russell; everyone on the set was "pretty freaked out" at the guitar's destruction, and Leigh's reaction was genuine, though "Tarantino was in a corner of the room with a funny curl on his lips, because he got something out of it with the performance."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://reverb.com/news/the-hateful-eight-hates-on-six-strings|title="The Hateful Eight" Hates on Six Strings: Film Includes Real Destruction of Antique Martin|website=reverb.com|date=February 2, 2016 |access-date=July 17, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200305161916/https://reverb.com/news/the-hateful-eight-hates-on-six-strings|archive-date=March 5, 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> Museum director Dick Boak said that the museum was not told that the script included a scene that called for a guitar being smashed, and determined that it was irreparable. The insurance remunerated the purchase value of the guitar. As a result of the incident, the museum no longer lends props to film productions.<ref name="reverb.com"/>

===Cinematography===
Cinematographer [[Robert Richardson (cinematographer)|Robert Richardson]], who also worked with Tarantino on ''[[Kill Bill: Volume 1]]'' (2003), ''[[Kill Bill: Volume 2]]'' (2004), ''[[Inglourious Basterds]]'' (2009), and ''Django Unchained'', filmed ''The Hateful Eight'' on [[70 mm film|65 mm]], using three modern 65&nbsp;mm camera models: the [[Arriflex 765]] and the Studio 65 and the 65 HS from Panavision.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.imdb.com/title/tt3460252/technical|title=The Hateful Eight (2015) |work=IMDb|via=www.imdb.com|access-date=July 17, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180905222941/https://www.imdb.com/title/tt3460252/technical|archive-date=September 5, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was transferred to 70&nbsp;mm film for projection using [[Ultra Panavision 70]] and [[Kodak]] [[Vision 3]] film stocks: 5219, 5207, 5213 and 5203.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://ascmag.com/blog/the-film-book/ultra-panavision-70|title=Large Format: Ultra Panavision 70 - The American Society of Cinematographers|website=ascmag.com|access-date=January 21, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190122044444/https://ascmag.com/blog/the-film-book/ultra-panavision-70|archive-date=January 22, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Until the release of ''[[Dunkirk (2017 film)|Dunkirk]]'' two years later, it was the widest release in 70&nbsp;mm film since ''[[Far and Away]]'' in 1992.<ref>{{cite news |last=Pucho |first=Kristy |title=Quentin Tarantino's Hateful Eight To Be Widest 70 mm Release in 20 Years |url=https://www.cinemablend.com/new/Quentin-Tarantino-Hateful-Eight-Widest-70mm-Release-20-Years-67031.html |date=September 2014 |publisher=[[CinemaBlend]].com |access-date=December 10, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141224043022/http://www.cinemablend.com/new/Quentin-Tarantino-Hateful-Eight-Widest-70mm-Release-20-Years-67031.html |archive-date=December 24, 2014 |url-status=live }}</ref> The film uses [[Panavision]] [[anamorphic format|anamorphic lenses]] with an [[Aspect ratio (image)|aspect ratio]] of 2.76:1, a very widescreen image that was used on some films in the 1950s and 1960s.<ref name="Fischer 70mm">{{cite news |title=Quentin Tarantino Helps Get 70mm Projectors in 50 Theaters for 'The Hateful Eight'|first=Russ| last=Fischer|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/hateful-eight-70mm/ |date=June 8, 2015 |publisher=[[/Film]] |access-date=December 31, 2015 | archive-url=https://archive.today/20150608205223/http://www.slashfilm.com/hateful-eight-70mm/| archive-date= June 8, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref> The production team also avoided any use of a [[digital intermediate]] in the 70&nbsp;mm roadshow release, as this would have reduced the high native resolution of the format. It was [[Color grading|color-timed]] photochemically by [[FotoKem]], and the [[dailies]] were screened in 70&nbsp;mm.<ref>{{cite news |title=How Quentin Tarantino Resurrected Ultra Panavision 70 for 'The Hateful Eight' Roadshow |url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/thompsononhollywood/how-quentin-tarantino-resurrected-ultra-panavision-70-for-the-hateful-eight-roadshow-20150828 |first=Bill |last=Desowitz |date=December 8, 2015 |publisher=Thompson on Hollywood ([[Indiewire.com]]) |access-date=October 16, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151223110610/http://blogs.indiewire.com/thompsononhollywood/how-quentin-tarantino-resurrected-ultra-panavision-70-for-the-hateful-eight-roadshow-20150828 |archive-date=December 23, 2015 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The wide digital release and a handful of 35&nbsp;mm prints were struck from a digital intermediate, done by Yvan Lucas at Shed/Santa Monica.<ref>{{cite web |last=Prince |first=Ron |title=Robert Richardson ASC / The Hateful Eight |url=https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/robert-richardson-asc-the-hateful-eight/ |website=British Cinematographer |date=February 4, 2016}}</ref>

===Post-production===
Tarantino edited two versions of the film, one for the roadshow version and the other for general release. The roadshow version runs for two hours and fifty-two minutes, including a three-minute overture, a twelve-minute intermission, a few minutes' worth of additional dialogue and activity and a few alternate takes. Tarantino created two versions as he felt some of the footage he shot for 70&nbsp;mm would not play well on smaller screens.<ref>{{cite magazine| last=Tapley| first=Kristopher| date=October 13, 2015| url=https://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-roadshow-multiplex-70mm-1201615357/| title=Quentin Tarantino Says He Cut Two Different Versions of 'The Hateful Eight'| magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]| access-date=December 9, 2017| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171216165342/http://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-roadshow-multiplex-70mm-1201615357/| archive-date=December 16, 2017| url-status=live}}</ref> The [[British Board of Film Classification]] records show the runtime difference between the Roadshow (187 minutes) and the DCP (168 minutes) releases was 19 minutes.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Hateful Eight 70Mm Version |url=https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/the-hateful-eight-70mm-version-film-qxnzzxq6vlgtodc2nzu3 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20211118224313/https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/the-hateful-eight-70mm-version-film-qxnzzxq6vlgtodc2nzu3 |archive-date=November 18, 2021 |access-date=November 18, 2021 |website=[[British Board of Film Classification]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=The Hateful Eight |url=https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/the-hateful-eight-film-qxnzzxq6vlgtodc2nzqx |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20211118224258/https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/the-hateful-eight-film-qxnzzxq6vlgtodc2nzqx |archive-date=November 18, 2021 |access-date=November 18, 2021 |website=[[British Board of Film Classification]]}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Hateful Eight'' grossed $54.1 million in the United States and Canada and $107.1 million in other countries, for a worldwide total of $161.2 million.<ref name="BOM"/> It opened in the US with a limited release on December 25, 2015, and over the weekend, it grossed $4.9 million from 100 theaters ($46,107 per screen), finishing 10th at the box office.<ref name="limited">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2015/12/star-wars-force-awakens-christmas-weekend-box-office-record-joy-daddys-home-concussion-point-break-1201672899/|title='Star Wars' Flies To $540M in Second Best Box Office Weekend of All Time|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|first1=Anthony|last1=D'Alessandro|first2=Anita|last2=Busch|date=December 28, 2015|access-date=January 21, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180613000029/https://deadline.com/2015/12/star-wars-force-awakens-christmas-weekend-box-office-record-joy-daddys-home-concussion-point-break-1201672899/|archive-date=June 13, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> It had its wide release on December 30, grossing $3.5 million on its first day.<ref name="CinemaScore" /> The film went on to gross $15.7 million in its opening weekend, finishing third at the box office, behind ''[[Star Wars: The Force Awakens]]'' ($90.2 million) and ''[[Daddy's Home (film)|Daddy's Home]]'' ($29.2 million).<ref name="box office">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2016/01/weekend-box-office-star-wars-force-awakens-hateful-eight-new-years-2016-1201674917/|first1=Anthony|last1=D'Alessandro|first2=Anita|last2=Busch|date=January 4, 2016|title='Force Awakens' Will Beat 'Avatar' Domestic Record Tuesday; New Year's Weekend $219.3M Ticket Sales 2nd Best All-Time|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=January 21, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160125221329/http://deadline.com/2016/01/weekend-box-office-star-wars-force-awakens-hateful-eight-new-years-2016-1201674917/|archive-date=January 25, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Attempted police boycott===
In October 2015, Tarantino attended a [[Black Lives Matter]] rally and publicly commented on [[police brutality in the United States]], saying, "When I see murders, I do not stand by... I have to call a murder a murder, and I have to call the murderers the murderers." Tarantino's comments received national media attention, and several police groups in the United States pledged to boycott ''The Hateful Eight'' and his other films. Tarantino responded that he was not a "cop hater" and would not be intimidated by the calls for a boycott.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/envelope/goldstandard/la-et-mn-quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-boycott-20151103-story.html | title=Quentin Tarantino responds to police boycott calls: The complete conversation | work=Los Angeles Times | date=November 4, 2015 | access-date=November 7, 2015 | last=Whipp | first=Glenn | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151106231756/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/envelope/goldstandard/la-et-mn-quentin-tarantino-hateful-eight-boycott-20151103-story.html | archive-date=November 6, 2015 | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news | url=https://money.cnn.com/2015/11/03/media/quentin-tarantino-police-boycott/ | title=Quentin Tarantino won't be 'intimidated' by police boycott | first=Tom | last=Kludt | work=CNN | date=November 3, 2015 | access-date=January 21, 2016 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160130012442/http://money.cnn.com/2015/11/03/media/quentin-tarantino-police-boycott/ | archive-date=January 30, 2016 | url-status=dead }}</ref>

''[[Forbes]]'' said that the film, while not as commercially successful as some of Tarantino's other films, cast doubt on claims that a boycott had a strong effect on sales.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Forbes]]|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/scottmendelson/2016/01/18/no-police-boycotts-against-quentin-tarantino-didnt-cause-the-hateful-eight-to-flop/|title=No, Police Boycotts Against Quentin Tarantino didn't cause 'Hateful Eight' to Flop|last=Mendelson|first=Scott|date=January 18, 2016|access-date=September 16, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180221035653/https://www.forbes.com/sites/scottmendelson/2016/01/18/no-police-boycotts-against-quentin-tarantino-didnt-cause-the-hateful-eight-to-flop/|archive-date=February 21, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Race issues ===

Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' observed that the film rejects the Western genre's tradition of ignoring America's racial history, but felt its handling of race issues was "dumb and ill-considered", and wrote: "Tarantino doesn't make films that are 'about race' so much as he tries to burrow into the bowels of American racism with his camera and his pen. There is no way to do that and stay clean."<ref name=":0">{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2015/12/25/movies/review-quentin-tarantinos-the-hateful-eight-blends-verbiage-and-violence.html |title=Review: Quentin Tarantino's 'The Hateful Eight' Blends Verbiage and Violence |last1=Scott |first1=A. O. |work=The New York Times |date=December 24, 2015 |access-date=March 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170102164315/http://www.nytimes.com/2015/12/25/movies/review-quentin-tarantinos-the-hateful-eight-blends-verbiage-and-violence.html |archive-date=January 2, 2017 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Gender issues===
Some critics expressed an unease at the treatment of the antagonistic Daisy Domergue character, who is the subject of repeated physical and verbal abuse and finally hanged in a sequence, which, according to [[Matt Zoller Seitz]] of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', "lingers on Daisy's death with near-pornographic fascination".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-hateful-eight-2015 |title=The Hateful Eight |last=Seitz |first=Matt Zoller |website=RogerEbert.Com |access-date=March 4, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200401044038/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-hateful-eight-2015 |archive-date=April 1, 2020 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://cinema-scope.com/cinema-scope-online/10986 |title=You've Gotta Be...Kidding Me: Quentin Tarantino's The Hateful Eight |last=Nayman |first=Adam |year=2015 |website=Cinema Scope Online |publisher=Cinema Scope Magazine |location=Canada |access-date=March 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160226100352/http://cinema-scope.com/cinema-scope-online/10986/ |archive-date=February 26, 2016 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Peers">{{cite web |last=Peers |first=Juliette |author-link=Juliette Peers |date=January 24, 2016 |title='Elaborately justified misogyny': The Hateful Eight and Daisy Domergue |url=http://theconversation.com/elaborately-justified-misogyny-the-hateful-eight-and-daisy-domergue-53046 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160307211834/http://theconversation.com/elaborately-justified-misogyny-the-hateful-eight-and-daisy-domergue-53046 |archive-date=March 7, 2016 |access-date=March 5, 2016 |website=The Conversation |publisher=[[The Conversation (website)|The Conversation]] US}}</ref> [[A. O. Scott]] felt the film "mutates from an exploration of racial animus into an orgy of elaborately justified misogyny".<ref name=":0" /> Laura Bogart regarded the treatment of Daisy Domergue as a "betrayal" of the positive female characters in previous Tarantino films, such as ''[[Kill Bill]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/balder-and-dash/hipster-misogyny-the-betrayal-of-the-hateful-eight |title=Hipster Misogyny: The Betrayal of "The Hateful Eight" |last=Bogart |first=Laura |date=January 18, 2016 |publisher=RogerEbert.com |access-date=March 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200320211613/https://www.rogerebert.com/balder-and-dash/hipster-misogyny-the-betrayal-of-the-hateful-eight |archive-date=March 20, 2020 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Juliette Peers]] wrote that "compared to the stunning twists and inversions of norms that Tarantino's other works offer when presenting female characters, ''The Hateful Eight''{{'}}s sexual politics seem bleakly conservative. Daisy is feisty and highly intelligent, yet the plotline is arbitrarily stacked against her."<ref name="Peers" />

Conversely, Courtney Bissonette of ''[[Bust (magazine)|Bust]]'' praised Tarantino's history of female characters and wrote of Domergue's treatment: "This is equality, man, and it's more feminist to think that a criminal is getting treated the same despite her sex. They don't treat her like a fairy princess because she is a woman, they treat her like a killer because she is a killer."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://bust.com/movies/15393-why-quentin-tarantino-is-possibly-the-most-feminist-director-of-our-time.html|title=A Feminist Defense of Quentin Tarantino|last1=Bissonette|first1=Courtney|work=[[Bust (magazine)|Bust]]|access-date=March 7, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160308044023/http://bust.com/movies/15393-why-quentin-tarantino-is-possibly-the-most-feminist-director-of-our-time.html|archive-date=March 8, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Stephanie Zacharek]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' praised the way that Daisy laughs at Ruth's continuing violence "as if she were drawing banshee strength from the abuse", describing the depiction as "a notion that may seem like misogyny but is in fact its triumphant opposite".<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Zacharek |first1=Stephanie |author-link=Stephanie Zacharek |title=Review: Quentin Tarantino's ''Hateful Eight'' Revels in Snow, Not So Much in People |url=https://time.com/4148091/hateful-eight-movie-review-quentin-tarantino/ |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |publisher=[[Time Inc.]]<!-- Publisher at time of the article's publication --> |access-date=December 1, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151223231650/https://time.com/4148091/hateful-eight-movie-review-quentin-tarantino/ |archive-date=December 23, 2015 |date=December 22, 2015 |url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Walton Goggins]] described the two survivors cooperating in the hanging as symbolic of a positive step to erase racism: "I see it as very uplifting, as very hopeful, and as a big step in the right direction, as a celebration, as a changing of one heart and one mind."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.ew.com/article/2016/01/05/hateful-eight-ending-walton-goggins|title=The Hateful Eight ending spoilers: Walton Goggins interview|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=January 5, 2016|last=Sullivan|first=Kevin P.|access-date=April 16, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180405003557/http://ew.com/article/2016/01/05/hateful-eight-ending-walton-goggins/|archive-date=April 5, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> However, [[Sasha Stone (blogger)|Sasha Stone]], writing for ''Awards Daily,'' felt it was implausible for Domergue to "represent, somehow, all of the evil of the South, all of the racism, all of the injustice. She's a tiny thing. There is no point in the film, or maybe one just barely, when Daisy inflicts any violence upon anyone – and by then it could be argued that she is only desperately trying to defend herself. She is handcuffed to Kurt Russell, needing his permission to speak and eat, and then punched brutally in the face whenever she says anything."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.awardsdaily.com/2015/12/27/the-hateful-eight-tarantino-and-that-misogyny-word/ |title=The Hateful Eight, Tarantino and that Misogyny Word |last1=Stone |first1=Sasha |date=December 27, 2015 |publisher=[[AwardsDaily]] |access-date=March 10, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160310175724/http://www.awardsdaily.com/2015/12/27/the-hateful-eight-tarantino-and-that-misogyny-word/ |archive-date=March 10, 2016 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Tarantino intended the violence against Domergue to be shocking and wanted the audience's allegiances to shift during the story. He said: "Violence is hanging over every one of those characters like a cloak of night. So I'm not going to go, 'OK, that's the case for seven of the characters, but because one is a woman, I have to treat her differently.' I'm not going to do that."<ref name=":1">{{Cite magazine|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/hateful-eight-misogyny-quentin-tarantino-harvey-weinstein-1201667868/|title=Claims of 'Hateful Eight' Misogyny 'Fishing for Stupidity,' Harvey Weinstein Says|last=Tapley|first=Kristopher|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=March 7, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171221193036/http://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/hateful-eight-misogyny-quentin-tarantino-harvey-weinstein-1201667868/|archive-date=December 21, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> Leigh also argued that she "didn't think [the violence against Daisy] was misogynistic for a second", praising the character's personality and finding the use of violence funny.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Tapley |first1=Kristopher |title=Jennifer Jason Leigh on 'Hateful Eight,' 'Anomalisa': 'Two of the Best Roles of My Life' |url=https://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/jennifer-jason-leigh-hateful-eight-anomalisa-best-roles-1201654802/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |access-date=December 1, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151210094126/https://variety.com/2015/film/in-contention/jennifer-jason-leigh-hateful-eight-anomalisa-best-roles-1201654802/ |archive-date=December 10, 2015 |date=December 8, 2015 |url-status=live}}</ref>
