'''''I Know What You Did Last Summer''''' is a 1997 American [[slasher film]] starring [[Jennifer Love Hewitt]], [[Sarah Michelle Gellar]], [[Ryan Phillippe]], and [[Freddie Prinze Jr.]] as four teenage friends who are stalked by a hook-wielding killer one year after covering up a car accident in which they supposedly killed a man. It was directed by [[Jim Gillespie (director)|Jim Gillespie]] and written by [[Kevin Williamson (screenwriter)|Kevin Williamson]], who adapted [[I Know What You Did Last Summer (novel)|the 1973 novel]].{{sfn|Fahy|2010|p=248}} It is the first in the [[I Know What You Did Last Summer (franchise)#Films|''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' film series]].

Williamson was approached to adapt [[Lois Duncan]]'s source novel by producer [[Erik Feig]]. Where ''[[Scream (1996 film)|Scream]]'', released the previous year, contained prominent elements of [[satire]] and self-referentiality, Williamson's script for ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' reworked the novel's central plot to resemble a straightforward 1980s-era slasher film.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=183}}

''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' was released theatrically in the United States on October 17, 1997, by [[Columbia Pictures]]. It received a mixed reception from critics and grossed $125.3{{nbsp}}million on a budget of $17{{nbsp}}million. The film was [[parody|parodied]] in ''[[Scary Movie]]'' (2000) and is frequently referenced in popular culture, as well as being credited alongside ''Scream'' with revitalizing the [[slasher film|slasher genre]] in the 1990s.{{sfn|Shary|2012|p=62}}

The film was followed by three sequels{{Spaced en dash}}''[[I Still Know What You Did Last Summer]]'' (1998), ''[[I'll Always Know What You Did Last Summer]]'' (2006) and ''[[I Know What You Did Last Summer (2025 film)|I Know What You Did Last Summer]]'' (2025){{Spaced en dash}}and [[I Know What You Did Last Summer (TV series)|a 2021 television series]].

==Plot==
On [[Independence Day (United States)|July 4]], 1996, in [[Southport, North Carolina]], [[Julie James (I Know What You Did Last Summer)|Julie James]] and her friends Ray Bronson, [[Helen Shivers]], and Barry Cox drive to the beach while celebrating Helen's victory at the beauty pageant. On the way back, Barry's drunken antics cause Ray to accidentally hit a pedestrian, assuming they killed him. Julie's friend Max passes by on the road, and they hide their crime from him to buy more time to decide what to do. Barry and Helen try to dump the body in the water, but the pedestrian wakes up and grabs Helen. Barry pushes him into the water, and the group makes a pact to never discuss what happened.

One year later, Julie returns home from college for the summer. The friends have gone their separate ways, with none of them pursuing their dreams, due to trauma from the incident. Julie receives a letter stating, "I know what you did last summer!" She and Helen take the note to Barry, who suspects Max is pulling a stunt on them. Julie meets Ray, who now works as a fisherman. Max is killed by a figure in a fisherman slicker wielding a [[hook (hand tool)|hook]]. That night, Barry is ambushed by the killer, who steals his car, hitting Barry, but refraining from killing him.

Julie researches newspaper articles, believing that the man they ran over was a local named David Egan. Helen and Julie meet David's sister, Missy, at her home, under the guise that their car broke down. Missy explains that a friend of David's named Billy Blue visited her to pay his respects. That night, the killer sneaks into Helen's house and cuts off her hair while she sleeps.

The following morning, Julie finds Max's corpse wearing Barry's stolen jacket and covered in crabs in the trunk of her car. When she brings the others to see it, the body has been removed. Julie, Helen and Barry confront Ray, who claims to also have received a letter. Julie goes back to visit Missy, while Barry and Helen participate in the [[Independence Day (United States)#Customs|4th of July parade]]. Missy reveals that David allegedly committed suicide out of guilt for the death of his girlfriend, Susie Willis, in a car accident and shows David's suicide note. As the writing matches that of the note she received, Julie realizes it was not a suicide note, but a death threat. Julie realizes the man she and her friends struck in the [[Hit and run|hit-and-run]] was not David Egan.

At the Croaker Beauty Pageant, Helen witnesses Barry being murdered on the balcony, but finds no sign of the killer, or Barry. The police officer escorting her home is murdered by the killer. Helen runs to her family's store, where the killer murders her sister, Elsa. She escapes and runs toward the street, but the killer slashes her to death.

Julie finds an article mentioning Susie's father, Ben Willis, and realizes Ben was the man they had run over a year earlier, moments after he killed David to avenge his daughter. She goes to tell Ray but notices Ray's boat is called ''Billy Blue''. A fisherman knocks Ray unconscious, inviting Julie to hide on his boat. On the boat, she finds photos and articles about her and her friends and pictures of Susie. The boat leaves the docks, and the fisherman is revealed to be Ben Willis, targeting them in revenge for leaving him for dead.

Ben chases Julie below deck, where she uncovers the bodies of Helen and Barry in the icebox. Ray awakens and goes to rescue Julie. He ultimately uses the [[rigging]] to sever Ben's hand and send him overboard. He explains that he posed as David's friend and visited Missy out of guilt. The couple reconciles, relieved not to have actually killed anyone after all.

One year later, Julie is in college in [[Boston]]. As she enters the shower, Julie notices the words "I still know" written in the steam on the shower door right before a dark figure crashes through it.

==Production==
===Development and writing===
====Background====
Written by [[Kevin Williamson (screenwriter)|Kevin Williamson]], the screenplay for ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' was rushed into production—having previously been disregarded—by [[Columbia Pictures]] upon the success of the Williamson-written ''[[Scream (1996 film)|Scream]]'',
released in 1996.<ref>{{cite web |first=Michael |last=Boyle |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1196283/two-scrapped-i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-scenes-were-salvaged-for-scream/ |title=Two Scrapped I Know What You Did Last Summer Scenes Were Salvaged For Scream |work=[[/Film]] |date=February 10, 2023 |access-date=July 31, 2024}}</ref>

The film is based on the [[I Know What You Did Last Summer (novel)|1973 novel]] by Lois Duncan, a [[young adult literature|youth-oriented]] suspense novel about four teenagers who are involved in a [[hit and run|hit-and-run]] accident involving a young boy.<ref name="now" />

====Development====
Producer [[Erik Feig]] pitched the idea of a screen adaptation to [[Mandalay Entertainment]], and subsequently appointed Williamson to retool the core elements of Duncan's novel, rendering a screenplay more akin to a 1980s slasher film.{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=26}}<ref name="now">{{cite AV media |title=Now I Know What You Did Last Summer |people=Gillis, Michael (prod., dir.) |medium=Documentary short |year=2008 |work=I Know What You Did Last Summer |publisher=[[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]]}}</ref> Inspired by his father, who had been a commercial fisherman, Williamson changed the setting of the novel to a small fishing village, and made the villain a hook-wielding fisherman.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=183}}

The killer's arming of himself with a hook is a reference to the urban legend "[[the Hook]]", which the four main characters recount at the beginning of the film around a campfire.<ref name="now" /> According to Williamson, he wrote the scene as a way of indicating what was to come: "Basically what I was doing was I was setting the framework to say, 'All right, audience: That's that legend. Now here's a new one.'"<ref name="now" /> Unlike Williamson's screenplay for the film's contemporary, ''Scream'', which incorporated satire of the slasher film, ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' was written more as a straightforward slasher film.<ref name="now" /> Gillespie commented in 2008: "The joy of this film for me as a filmmaker was in taking [the] elements that we've seen before, and saying to the audience: 'Here's something you've seen before'—knowing that they're saying 'We've seen this before'—and still getting them to jump."<ref name="now" /> Gillespie also claimed that he felt Williamson's screenplay did not resemble a "slasher horror movie" and that he saw it rather as simply "a really good story" with a [[morality]] tale embedded within it.<ref name="now" />

===Pre-production===
According to producer Stokely Chaffin, the producers sought out actors who were "beautiful, but likable".<ref name="now" /> Director Gillespie recalled that, though he had been unfamiliar with the screenplay's source material, that "roughly 60 to 65%" of the young women auditioning had read the novel as children.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:08:46)</ref> Jennifer Love Hewitt, who at the time was mainly known for her role on the television series ''[[Party of Five]]'', was cast in the lead of [[Julie James (I Know What You Did Last Summer)|Julie James]] based on her "ability to project vulnerability", which the producers, director Gillespie and writer Williamson unanimously agreed upon.<ref name="now" /> Initially, Hewitt was considered for the role of Helen.<ref name="now" /> [[Melissa Joan Hart]] was offered a role, but she turned it down, because she felt that the film was a rip-off of ''Scream''.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.businessinsider.com/what-melissa-joan-hart-is-doing-today-clarissa-sabrina-teenage-witch-joey-explains-it-all-interview-2017-10 |title=Here's what Melissa Joan Hart — who played Clarissa and Sabrina the Teenage Witch — is doing today |website=[[Business Insider]]}}</ref> For the role of Barry, the crew had envisioned an actor with a "{{convert|6|ft|2|in|abbr=on}} [[quarterback]]" appearance, as the character had been written as an intimidating figure.<ref name="now" /> Ryan Phillippe was ultimately cast in the part based on his audition, despite the fact that he was not as physically tall as the script had called for.<ref name="now" /> Director Gillespie chose Freddie Prinze Jr. for the role of Ray, because he felt Prinze himself had an "everyman" quality much like the character.<ref name="now" />

Sarah Michelle Gellar was the last of the lead performers to be cast in the role of Helen.<ref name="now" /> Like Hewitt, Gellar was also known to American audiences at the time for her roles in television.<ref name="now" /> Gillespie commented on casting Gellar: "I wanted an actress that had a warmth to her, but could still come off as being a bitch."<ref name="now" /> For the supporting role of Missy, Gillespie sought an actress with significant screen presence, as the character, despite appearing in only two scenes, is central to several major plot points.<ref name="now" /> Anne Heche was cast in the role, which she recalled as being two days' worth of work that required her to "be scary".<ref name="now" />

===Filming===
[[File:Kolmer2.jpg|thumb|The cliff and rock shown at the beginning of the film, shot at Kolmer Gulch near [[Jenner, California]]]]
Scottish director Jim Gillespie was hired to direct the film after being suggested by writer Williamson.<ref name="now" /> Hewitt would later state in 2008 that Gillespie was to date her "favorite director [she's] ever worked with."<ref name="now" /> Principal photography began on March 31, 1997,{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}} and took place over a period of ten weeks<ref name="tenweeks">[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:08:12)</ref> throughout the late spring-early summer of 1997.{{efn-lr|In his 1998 audio commentary for the film, Gillespie notes that the [[California]]-shot scenes were filmed in June 1997,<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:10:28)</ref> and that the shoot lasted ten weeks.<ref name="tenweeks" /> According to Adam Rockoff, principal photography commenced on March 31, 1997.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}}}} Approximately seven weeks of the ten-week shoot took place at night, which Gillespie says was difficult for the cast and crew, and also created commotion in primary small-town locations in which they shot.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}} Gillespie devised a color scheme with cinematographer [[Denis Crossan]] which was marked by heavy [[blue]]s throughout and a notable lack of bright colors.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:24:27)</ref>
[[File:KolmerCurve.jpg|thumb|The blind curve where the car accident occurs early in the film, shot at Kolmer Gulch near Jenner, California]]
For the beginning of the film, coastal areas of [[Sonoma County, California]] stood in for North Carolina, where the film is set. The opening shots of the sun setting on a rugged coast were filmed at Kolmer Gulch, just north of the town of Jenner, on [[California State Route 1|Highway 1]].<ref name="locations">{{cite web |url=http://www.movie-locations.com/movies/i/I_Know_What_You_Did_Summer.html#.WoulM3tFzIU |title=Filming Locations for ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' |work=Movie-Locations.com |access-date=February 19, 2018 |archive-date=January 17, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180117020949/http://www.movie-locations.com/movies/i/I_Know_What_You_Did_Summer.html#.WoulM3tFzIU |url-status=dead}}</ref> The car crash scene was also filmed on Highway 1 in the same area. The scene in which the four friends are seated around a campfire on the beach next to a wrecked boat was inspired by a painting Gillespie had seen in a reference book; to achieve the image, the art department purchased an old boat in [[Bodega Bay]], cut it in half and placed it at the beach location.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:09:58)</ref>
[[File:Southport from the Water.JPG|thumb|The majority of the film was set in Southport, North Carolina]]
The remaining scenes were filmed primarily around the town of Southport, North Carolina.<ref name="locations" /> Specific sites included the Amuzu Theater, where the beauty pageant is held, the Old Yacht Basin and Southport Fish Company.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.southport-oakisland.com/listing/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-1997.html |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer (1997) |publisher=Southport-OakIsland.com |access-date=February 19, 2018 |archive-date=February 20, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180220212206/https://www.southport-oakisland.com/listing/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-1997.html |url-status=live}}</ref> Julie's house is on Short Street just north of Southport Marina.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.southporttimes.com/featured/200407.html |title=Movies Filmed in Southport, North Carolina |newspaper=Southport Times |access-date=February 19, 2018 |archive-date=October 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181018104148/http://www.southporttimes.com/featured/200407.html |url-status=live}}</ref> The daytime sequences shot on the marina show multiple vessels traversing the water; though real vessels, the boat traffic was orchestrated by a marine traffic coordinator to make the waterway appear lively.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:36:57)</ref> The Shiver's Department Store setting in the film was discovered on location in Southport by director Gillespie, who was so impressed by the location that he reworked elements of the script in order to incorporate it into the film; it eventually became the primary setting for Helen's extended chase sequence with the killer.<ref name="now" /> The exterior sequences of Julie's Boston college campus were in fact shot at [[Duke University]],<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:24:51)</ref> while the hospital sequence was filmed at Southport's [[Dosher Memorial Hospital]] in an unused wing of the hospital.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (0:43:00)</ref>

There is a climactic scene where Jennifer Love Hewitt's character walks into the middle of the street and screams to the killer "What are you waiting for?!" According to Hewitt, that scene was conceived and directed by a child who won a contest to "come on and create a moment for the movie". In an interview with ''[[Us Weekly]]'', she stated that she disliked the idea but still went through with it, and noted that the scene "became the biggest part of the movie" and that ultimately it was "a great idea".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.dailyedge.ie/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-4168038-Aug2018/ |title=That iconic moment in I Know What You Did Last Summer was directed by a kid |publisher=Daily Edge |date=August 7, 2018}}</ref>

The final sequence on the boat was shot on an actual water-bound vessel on the [[Cape Fear River]], which proved difficult for the actors and crew.<ref name="now" /> According to Gillespie, the filmmakers nearly lost the boat while attempting to dock it due to the volatile waters, after which they were forced to leave and shoot other footage until the following day.<ref name="now" />

===Post-production===
Gillespie chose to film virtually no onscreen blood as he did not want the film to be overly gratuitous in terms of violence.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}}<ref name="now" /> The scene in which Elsa has her throat slashed while standing against a glass door had originally been shot from behind without any blood appearing on the glass. However, producer Feig worried that the scene appeared "medically impossible" after which Gillespie re-shot it (post-principal photography) with a visual effect of blood spattering across the glass.<ref name="now" /> Upon test screenings of the film, Gillespie and the producers decided that a death sequence needed to occur earlier in the film to establish a sense of legitimate danger for the main characters.<ref name="now" /> The scene in which Max is murdered in the crab factory was subsequently filmed and implemented into the final cut to achieve this (in the original script, his character was not killed).<ref name="now" />

The original ending of the film featured a sequence in which Julie receives an [[email]] reading: "I Still Know".{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}} This ending was scrapped for the more dramatic ending featured in the final cut of the film, in which Julie finds the same message scrawled on a shower stall just before the killer comes crashing through the glass.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}} This footage was also shot after principal photography, on a soundstage next-door to where Hewitt was filming ''Party of Five''.<ref>[[#Gillespie|Gillespie & Mirkovich 1998]] (1:35:29)</ref>

==Release==
===Marketing===
In anticipation of its release, distributor Columbia Pictures and Mandalay Entertainment began a summer marketing campaign that presented the film as being "From the creator of ''Scream''", meaning writer Kevin Williamson.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}} [[Miramax|Miramax Films]] subsequently filed a lawsuit against Columbia and Mandalay, arguing the statement was misleading as it suggested that [[Wes Craven]], the director of ''Scream'', had been involved with the production.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=184}}

===Lawsuit===
The week following the film's theatrical release, a federal judge awarded Miramax an [[injunction]] requiring that Columbia and Mandalay remove the claim from their advertising.<ref>{{cite magazine |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |url=https://variety.com/1997/biz/news/miramax-reigns-in-court-1116674228/ |title=Miramax reigns in court |date=October 20, 1997 |access-date=April 8, 2018 |first=Paul |last=Karon |archive-date=April 8, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180408141423/http://variety.com/1997/biz/news/miramax-reigns-in-court-1116674228/ |url-status=live}}</ref> (Williamson himself had already requested its removal by this point after spotting it on a theater poster.)<ref name="lawsuit">{{cite web |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-mar-07-fi-26323-story.html |title=Miramax Wins ''Scream'' Claim Against Sony |first=James |last=Bates |date=March 7, 1998 |access-date=April 7, 2018}}</ref> Miramax won the lawsuit against Columbia and Mandalay during a March 1998 hearing. In a press release, executive [[Bob Weinstein]] noted plans to "vigorously pursue" damage claims against Columbia Pictures.<ref name="lawsuit" />

Special features included a theatrical trailer and the filmmaker's commentary.<ref>{{cite AV media |url=https://wonderbk.com/shop/product/1531825-i-know-what-you-did-last-summer |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer |medium=Motion picture |publisher=Columbia TriStar Home Video |year=1998 |format=DVD |orig-year=1997 |ISBN=978-1-861-89777-0}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' opened theatrically in North America on October 17, 1997.<ref name="bom">{{cite web |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer (1997) |work=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=April 8, 2018 |archive-date=May 31, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180531111826/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm |url-status=live}}
* [https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm Weekend] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190119200459/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm |date=January 19, 2019 }} and [https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekly&id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm weekly] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180808104507/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekly&id=iknowwhatyoudidlastsummer.htm |date=August 8, 2018 }} data</ref> The film was made on a $17&nbsp;million budget,{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=26}} and grossed $15,818,645 in 2,524 theaters in its opening weekend in the United States and Canada, ranking number one; it remained in the number one position for an additional two weekends.<ref name="bom" /> By the end of its theatrical run in December 1997, it had grossed $72,586,134 in the U.S. and Canada{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=26}} and $53&nbsp;million in other countries for a worldwide total of $126&nbsp;million.<ref name="numbers" /><ref name="bom" />

According to data compiled by [[Box Office Mojo]], ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' is the seventh highest-grossing slasher film as of 2021.<ref name="bom" />

In retrospect, Jim Gillespie said: "It was meant to be kind of a stand-alone revisit of those classic '80s horror films. It worked! The movie was number one three weeks in a row. It just clicked with the audience. The title clicked and everything just seemed to work. Third week was Halloween weekend and it was number one in its third week. I couldn't believe it stuck there for three weeks."<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a840845/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-20-years-on/ |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer 20 years on |website=[[Digital Spy]] |date=October 17, 2017 |access-date=July 2, 2021 |archive-date=November 24, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201124132646/https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a840845/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-20-years-on/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 47% based on 118 reviews, with an average rating of 5.40/10. The site's critics consensus reads: "A by-the-numbers slasher that arrived a decade too late, the mostly tedious ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' will likely only hook diehard fans of the genre."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/i_know_what_you_did_last_summer |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer (1997) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=July 23, 2025 |archive-date=January 30, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210130051659/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/i_know_what_you_did_last_summer |url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a [[weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]] score of 52 out of 100 based on reviews from 17 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |work=Metacritic |access-date=October 15, 2020 |archive-date=September 13, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100913054541/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer |url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of B− on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer |work=Cinema Score |access-date=August 8, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date=December 20, 2018 |url-status=dead}} {{small|(Requires manual search)}}.</ref>{{Better source needed|reason=[[WP:LINKROT]] an emtpy search box is not a suitable reference|date=December 2024}}

The film drew both positive and negative comparisons to ''Scream'', also written by Williamson. [[Mick LaSalle]] considered the movie inferior to its predecessor.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/FILM-REVIEW-Last-Summer-Offers-Thrills-But-2801119.php |title=FILM REVIEW -- ''Last Summer'' Offers Thrills, But No ''Scream'' / Story starts strong, but turns formulaic |first=Mick |last=Lasalle |work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]] |date=October 17, 1997 | access-date=July 5, 2017 | archive-date=August 20, 2017 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170820202459/http://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/FILM-REVIEW-Last-Summer-Offers-Thrills-But-2801119.php | url-status=live}}</ref> Richard Harrington, on the other hand, cited ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' as superior to ''Scream''; he described the newer picture as "... a smart and sharply-drawn genre-film with a moral center, and with a solid cast of young actors to hold it."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/iknowwhatyoudidlastsummerharr.htm |title=''Summer'' Time: The Living is Deadly |first=Richard |last=Harrington |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=October 17, 1997 | access-date=July 5, 2015 | archive-date=December 16, 2014 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141216161139/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/iknowwhatyoudidlastsummerharr.htm | url-status=live}}</ref> [[Derek Elley]] of ''Variety'' was also enthusiastic, calling the film a "polished genre piece with superior fright elements that should perform at better-than-average theatrical levels."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://variety.com/1997/film/reviews/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-111731125/ |title=Review:''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' |first=Derek |last=Elley |work=Variety |date=October 13, 1997 |access-date=July 4, 2015 |archive-date=September 5, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140905234745/http://variety.com/1997/film/reviews/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-111731125/ |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] gave the movie one of four stars and wrote that "The best shot in this film is the first one. Not a good sign."<ref>{{cite news |url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19971017/REVIEWS/710170304/1023 |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |access-date=November 11, 2017 |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |archive-date=April 8, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130408063721/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F19971017%2FREVIEWS%2F710170304%2F1023 |url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' praised Jennifer Love Hewitt's performance, noting that she "knows how to scream with soul".<ref>{{cite magazine |url=http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,289974,00.html |title=Movie Review: ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |access-date=June 9, 2011 |date=October 24, 1997 |archive-date=April 29, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100429084725/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,289974,00.html |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Lawrence Van Gelder of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote of the picture: "This isn't real life. It's the [[grand guignol]] of ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'', laying its claim to succeed ''Scream'' as a high-grossing and blood-drenched date-night crowd-pleaser. And why shouldn't it?"<ref>{{cite web |newspaper=The New York Times |last=Van Gelder |first=Lawrence |date=October 17, 1997 |access-date=April 7, 2018 |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/101797summer-film-review.html |title="I Know What You Did Last Summer": Creepy Guy, Ghost Stories, Teen-age Sex. Uh-Oh. |archive-date=April 8, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180408141602/https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/101797summer-film-review.html |url-status=live}}</ref> James Kendrick of the ''Q Network'' wrote that "Williamson's characters are all generic types; but they're still believable as people, and they react realistically according to the situations." Kendrick added that the film was "head and shoulders above earlier 'dead teenager' movies".<ref>{{cite web |work=Q Network |first=James |last=Kendrick |title=Review: I Know What You Did Last Summer |archive-date=July 12, 2001 |url-status=dead |url=http://www.qnetwork.com/movie/IKnowWhatYouDid.html |date=1998 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010712212759/http://www.qnetwork.com/movie/IKnowWhatYouDid.html}}</ref>

''[[TV Guide]]''{{'}}s [[Maitland McDonagh]] awarded the movie two out of five stars, noting: "Screenwriter Kevin Williamson takes a step backward and writes the kind of movie ''Scream'' mocks. You can see him now, soaking up videos of ''[[Friday the 13th (1980 film)|Friday the 13th]]'' and ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]''—not to mention the lesser likes of ''[[He Knows You're Alone]]'', ''[[Terror Train]]'' and ''[[My Bloody Valentine (film)|My Bloody Valentine]]''—and saying, 'I can do that!' And boy, does he ever."<ref>{{cite web |work=[[TV Guide]] |first=Maitland |last=McDonagh |author-link=Maitland McDonagh |access-date=November 22, 2017 |url=http://www.tvguide.com/movies/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer/review/132622/ |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer |archive-date=April 8, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180408141805/http://www.tvguide.com/movies/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer/review/132622/ |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Critic [[James Berardinelli]] credited both ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' and ''Scream'' with igniting a new boom of slasher films, adding: "There is one minor aspect of the plot that elevates ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' above the level of a typical '80s slasher flick -- it has an interesting subtext. I'm referring to the way the lives and friendships of these four individuals crumble in the wake of their accident. Guilt, confusion and doubt build in them until they can no longer stand to be with each other or look at themselves in the mirror. Sadly, this potentially-fascinating element of the movie is dismissed quickly to facilitate a higher body count. And, as I said before, a few extra deaths can only make a slasher movie better, right?"<ref>{{cite web |work=Reel Views |first=James |last=Berardinelli |author-link=James Berardinelli |url-status=dead |archive-date=October 12, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071012010830/http://www.reelviews.net/movies/i/i_know.html |url=http://www.reelviews.net/movies/i/i_know.html |title=Review: I Know What You Did Last Summer |date=1997}}</ref>

Movie historian [[Leonard Maltin]] gave the film 2 out of a possible 4 stars; he described it as "...Too routine to succeed overall...Despite being based on a young-adult novel, this is absolutely not for kids. Still, it's a classic compared to the sequel."<ref>Maltin's TV, Movie, & Video Guide</ref>

Motion picture scholar Adam Rockoff notes in his book ''Going to Pieces: The Rise and Fall of the Slasher Film, 1978–1986'' that, at the time of its release, many critics branded ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' as an imitation of ''Scream''. However, he contends that it is a "much different film", despite both screenplays being penned by the same writer:

{{Blockquote|Whereas ''Scream'' relied heavily on self-conscious references and its pop culture veneer, ''Last Summer'' was a throwback to the slasher films of the early '80s. While, like ''Scream'', it employed the services of a group of young, sexy and almost impossibly good-looking actors, ''Last Summer'' played its horror straight. Those looking for a good old-fashioned slasher film were pleasantly surprised.{{sfn|Rockoff|2016|p=182}}}}

[[Lois Duncan]], the author of the original novel, heavily criticized the film adaptation; she stated in a 2002 interview she was "appalled" her story was turned into a slasher film.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.absolutewrite.com:80/specialty_writing/lois_duncan.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160719055942/http://www.absolutewrite.com/specialty_writing/lois_duncan.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 19, 2016 |title=Lois Duncan |date=July 19, 2016 |access-date=June 1, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://screenrant.com/i-know-what-did-last-summer-movie-lois-duncan-controversy/ |first=Jack |last=Wilhelmi |title=I Know What You Did Last Summer Movie Was HATED By The Book's Author |work=[[Screen Rant]] |date=April 18, 2020 |accessdate=October 6, 2024}}</ref>

=== Sequels ===
{{Main|I Still Know What You Did Last Summer{{!}}''I Still Know What You Did Last Summer''|I'll Always Know What You Did Last Summer{{!}}''I'll Always Know What You Did Last Summer''|I Know What You Did Last Summer (2025 film){{!}}''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' (2025 film)}}

A sequel titled ''I Still Know What You Did Last Summer'' was released in 1998, with a [[direct-to-video]] film, ''I'll Always Know What You Did Last Summer'', released in 2006. In the first sequel, Hewitt, Prinze Jr. and Watson reprised their roles. The third film has very little relation to the first two, other than the premise, the villain and the producers. It featured new characters and a different setting.

In February 2023, a legacy sequel was announced to be in development, with [[Jennifer Love Hewitt]] and [[Freddie Prinze Jr.]] in negotiations to reprise their respective roles. [[Jennifer Kaytin Robinson]] was selected to direct the film from a script written by [[Leah McKendrick]], based on an idea by Robinson and McKendrick. [[Neal H. Moritz]] would serve as producer. The plot is said to be similar in approach to ''[[Scream (2022 film)|Scream]]'' (2022), in which characters from the original film are included in a story featuring a younger cast.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kroll |first=Justin |date=February 6, 2023 |title=''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' Sequel In Works At Sony With Jennifer Kaytin Robinson Directing; Jennifer Love Hewitt And Freddie Prinze Jr. In Talks To Return |url=https://deadline.com/2023/02/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-sony-jennifer-kaytin-robinson-jennifer-love-hewitt-freddie-prinze-jr-1235250808/ |access-date=February 6, 2023 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> The film was released in July 2025.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2024/05/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-release-date-set-sony-1235919726 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240516205605/https://deadline.com/2024/05/i-know-what-you-did-last-summer-release-date-set-sony-1235919726/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=May 16, 2024 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |title=Sony Dates ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' For Summer 2025 |last=Grobar |first=Matt |date=May 16, 2024 |access-date=May 16, 2024}}</ref>

== In popular culture ==
The ''[[Dawson's Creek]]'' season one episode "The Scare" spoofs ''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' alongside ''[[Scream (1996 film)|Scream]]'', all written by Williamson. The episode opens with the characters Dawson and Joey viewing the former.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Fraser |first1=Emma |title=The Scariest TV Show I Ever Watched: ''Dawson's Creek''{{'}}s 'The Scare' |url=https://www.primetimer.com/quickhits/the-scariest-tv-episode-i-have-ever-seen-dawsons-creeks-the-scare |website=primetimer.com |access-date=January 30, 2024 |date=October 25, 2019}}</ref>

''I Know What You Did Last Summer'' has been referenced in various films and television series, and its central plot was [[parody|parodied]] at length in the [[spoof film]]s ''[[Scary Movie]]'' and ''[[Shriek If You Know What I Did Last Friday the 13th]]'' (both 2000).{{sfn|Harper|2004|p=28}}

The teen drama ''[[Popular (TV series)|Popular]]'' spoofed the film in the season two episode "I Know What You Did Last Spring Break."<ref>{{cite web |title=Popular: Season 2, Episode 19 {{!}} Rotten Tomatoes |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/popular/s02/e19 |website=www.rottentomatoes.com |access-date=January 30, 2024}}</ref>

It was also [[parody|spoofed]] by [[Anthony Horowitz]] in the Diamond Brothers novella, [[I Know What You Did Last Wednesday]] (2002) and later in ''[[The Simpsons]]'' episode "[[Treehouse of Horror X]]" as "I Know What You Diddily-Iddily-Did", with [[Ned Flanders]] as the killer.{{sfn|Murphy|2009|p=172}}

In ''[[Boy Meets World]]'', the episode titled "[[And Then There Was Shawn]]" was conceived as a parody of then-recent horror films ''[[Scream (1996 film)|Scream]]'' and ''I Know What You Did Last Summer''. Jennifer Love Hewitt also appeared in the episode.<ref>{{Cite web|author=Hollywood com Staff|date=October 31, 2012|title=An Oral History of the Infamous 'Boy Meets World' Horror Parody Episode|url=http://www.hollywood.com/tv/an-oral-history-of-the-infamous-boy-meets-world-horror-parody-episode-57239771/|access-date=November 3, 2021|website=Hollywood.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Boy Meets World's best episode was an homage to a completely different genre|url=https://www.avclub.com/boy-meets-world-s-best-episode-was-an-homage-to-a-compl-1798283401|access-date=November 3, 2021|website=The A.V.
