'''''Dogtooth''''' ({{langx|el|Κυνόδοντας|Kynodontas}}) is a 2009 Greek [[Absurdist fiction|absurdist]] [[psychological drama]] film directed by [[Yorgos Lanthimos]]. Written by Lanthimos and [[Efthimis Filippou|Efthymis Filippou]], the film is about a husband and wife ([[Christos Stergioglou]] and Michelle Valley) who keep their children ([[Angeliki Papoulia]], Christos Passalis, and [[Mary Tsoni]]) ignorant of the world outside their property well into adulthood.

''Dogtooth'' is Lanthimos's third feature film. It won the [[Un Certain Regard|Prix Un Certain Regard]] at the [[2009 Cannes Film Festival]] and was nominated for [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]] at the [[83rd Academy Awards]].

==Plot==
A couple and their adult son and two adult daughters live in a fenced compound. The children have no knowledge of the outside world; their parents say they will be ready to leave once they lose a [[Canine tooth|dogtooth]], and that one can safely leave only by car. The children entertain themselves with [[endurance game]]s, such as holding their hands under hot water. They believe they have a brother on the other side of the fence to whom they throw supplies. The parents reward good behavior with stickers and bad with violence.

The father pays an employee of his factory, Christina, to come to the house and have sex with the son. Frustrated by the son's refusal to give her [[cunnilingus]], Christina trades her headband with the elder daughter in exchange for cunnilingus from her. The elder daughter convinces the younger daughter to lick her shoulder by bartering the headband. Later, the younger daughter volunteers to lick the elder again. The elder has nothing to offer in exchange, but the younger does not mind and experiments by licking other body parts.

The father visits a dog training facility and demands to have his dog returned. The trainer refuses because the dog has not finished its training, and asks: "Do we want an animal or a friend?" When the children are terrified by a stray cat in the garden, the son kills it with a pair of pruning shears. Deciding to take advantage of the incident, the father shreds his clothes, covers himself in fake blood, and tells his children that their unseen brother was killed by a cat, the most dangerous creature. After he teaches them to bark on all fours to fend off cats, the family holds a memorial service for the brother.

Christina again barters for oral sex from the elder daughter. The daughter rejects her offer of hair gel and demands the [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]] VHS tapes in her bag. She watches the films (''[[Rocky IV]]'' and ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]'') in secret and afterwards recreates scenes and quotes their dialogue. When the father discovers the tapes, he beats her with one of them, then goes to Christina's flat and hits her with her VCR, cursing her future children to be corrupted by "bad influences".

The parents decide that, with Christina no longer available, they will have the son choose one of his sisters as a new sexual partner. After fondling both sisters with his eyes closed, he chooses the elder. She is uncomfortable during their sex and afterwards threatens her brother in the style of Hollywood film dialogues.

During a performance for the parents' wedding anniversary, the younger daughter stops to rest, but the elder continues and dances the choreography from ''[[Flashdance]]'', disturbing her parents. That night, she knocks out one of her dogteeth with a dumbbell and hides in the boot of her father's car. The father discovers her tooth fragments and searches for her fruitlessly. He drives to work the next day; the car sits outside the factory, unattended.

==Production==
''Dogtooth'' was the feature film debut for Boo Productions, an [[Athens, Greece|Athens]]-based advertising company. The Greek Film Center supported the project with about [[Euro|€]]200,000, and much of the production was done with help from volunteers.<ref>{{cite news|last=Katsareas|first=Eftehia|date=3 December 2009 |url= http://edition.cnn.com/2009/SHOWBIZ/Movies/12/03/lanthimos.dogtooth/|title=The surprising Greek film winning fans abroad|work=cnn.com|publisher=[[CNN]]|access-date=8 May 2010|archive-date=17 March 2010|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100317102803/http://edition.cnn.com/2009/SHOWBIZ/Movies/12/03/lanthimos.dogtooth/|url-status=live}}</ref> Another €50,000 was offered by the production studio.<ref name= "budget">{{cite news|last=Rapold|first=Nicolas|date=24 June 2010|url= https://www.nytimes.com/2010/06/27/movies/27dogtooth.html|title=From Greece, a Parable About ... Something|work=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=24 June 2010|archive-date=3 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190503140029/https://www.nytimes.com/2010/06/27/movies/27dogtooth.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The script was completed in about 2 years, with rehearsals lasting {{frac|1|1|2}} months. The film was shot in about 1 month (in August 2008), and editing took 6 months.<ref>{{Cite news |date=28 May 2009 |title=LIFO: Συνέντευξη της Μαίρης Τσώνη |url=http://www.lifo.gr/mag/features/1479 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100815044032/http://www.lifo.gr/mag/features/1479 |archive-date=August 15, 2010 |work=LIFO}}</ref>

Anna Kalaitzidou and Christos Passalis were stage actors who were cast after having worked with Lanthimos earlier. Mary Tsoni was not a professional actress; she was a singer in a [[Punk rock|punk]] band.<ref>{{cite interview |first= Giorgos  |last=Lanthimos | interviewer= Priscilla Eyles |date=26 April 2010 |url= http://www.sound-screen.co.uk/index.php/2010/04/interview-giorgos-lanthimos-director-of-dogtooth/ |title=Interview: Giorgos Lanthimos, director of Dogtooth |work=sound-screen.co.uk |access-date=5 March 2016 |url-status=dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100706231414/http://www.sound-screen.co.uk/index.php/2010/04/interview-giorgos-lanthimos-director-of-dogtooth/ |archive-date=6 July 2010 }}</ref> Lanthimos had an open approach to both acting and visual style and felt it would look fake if he involved himself too much in the details. Only when rehearsals started did he begin to develop an idea of the style in which the film should be shot: one where he tried to combine a realistic environment with "really strict framing and a cool, surreal look to go with the narrative".<ref name="esm">{{cite interview |last=Jahn |first=Pamela |interviewer= |title=Dogtooth: Interview with Yorgos Lanthimos |url=http://www.electricsheepmagazine.co.uk/features/2010/04/05/dogtooth-interview-with-giorgos-lanthimos/ |access-date=8 May 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100425001219/http://www.electricsheepmagazine.co.uk/features/2010/04/05/dogtooth-interview-with-giorgos-lanthimos/ |archive-date=25 April 2010 |url-status=live |work=Electric Sheep Magazine |date=5 April 2010}}</ref>

==Release==
The film premiered at the 62nd [[2009 Cannes Film Festival|Cannes Film Festival]] on 18 May 2009,<ref name="PressKit">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/assets/Image/Direct/028999.pdf|title=Dogtooth &ndash; Press Kit|work=[[Cannes Film Festival]]|publisher=festival-cannes.com|access-date=23 May 2009|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110807195208/http://www.festival-cannes.com/assets/Image/Direct/028999.pdf|archive-date=7 August 2011}}</ref> and went on to screen at festivals such as the [[Toronto International Film Festival]] and the [[Maryland Film Festival]].

It was released in Greece on 11 November 2009 by Feelgood Entertainment.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://cineuropa.org/film.aspx?lang=en&documentID=108171|title=Film profile: Dogtooth|work=Cineuropa|access-date=26 January 2011|archive-date=18 January 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120118061842/http://cineuropa.org/film.aspx?lang=en&documentID=108171|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Proimakis|first=Joseph|date=2 September 2010|url=http://cineuropa.org/interview.aspx?lang=en&documentID=150006|title=Interview with Irini Souganidou • Distributor, Feelgood Entertainment|work=Cineuropa|access-date=26 January 2011|archive-date=13 July 2012|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120713014932/http://cineuropa.org/interview.aspx?lang=en&documentID=150006|url-status=live}}</ref> Verve Pictures acquired distribution rights for the United Kingdom and released it on 23 April 2010.<ref name="esm" /> [[Kino International (company)|Kino International]] released it in the United States on 25 June 2010.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Dogtooth |url=http://www.indiewire.com/film/dogtooth |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110128022736/http://www.indiewire.com/film/dogtooth |archive-date=28 January 2011 |access-date=26 January 2011 |work=[[IndieWire]]}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Greek===
Greek critic Dimitris Danikas gave ''Dogtooth'' a rating of eight out of ten ("with enthusiasm"), calling it "black, surreal, nightmarish" and writing that ''Dogtooth'' is as important for Greek cinema as [[Theodoros Angelopoulos]]'s 1970 film ''[[Reconstitution (1970 film)|Reconstitution]]''.<ref name=Danikas>{{Cite web|last=Danikas|first=Dimitris|date=22 October 2009|url=http://www.tanea.gr/default.asp?pid=2&artid=4542427&ct=4|title=Greek leadership|work=TA NEA Online|access-date=31 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120309180605/http://www.tanea.gr/default.asp?pid=2&artid=4542427&ct=4|archive-date=9 March 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> Danikas added, "Lanthimos composes and goes from one level to another like a wildcat-creator, constantly and continuously maintaining the same rigorous style. Hence the aphasia; hence the uniformity; hence the submission and the scheduled mass culture; hence also the serial killer; hence, however, the disobedience, the anarchy. As I said at the beginning: ''Dogtooth'' has the surrealism of [[Luis Buñuel|Buñuel]], the scalpel of [[Michael Haneke|Haneke]], the underground horror of a thriller without the splatter. Perfect."<ref name=Danikas /> Danikas characterized ''Dogtooth''{{'}}s [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Film|Academy Award]] nomination as "the greatest Greek triumph of recent years."<ref name="Danikas Oscar">{{Cite web|last=Danikas|first=Dimitris|date=26 January 2011|url=http://www.tanea.gr/default.asp?pid=2&ct=4&artid=4615030|title=Dogtooth Nominated for an Oscar|work=TA NEA Online|access-date=31 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110129022731/http://www.tanea.gr/default.asp?pid=2&ct=4&artId=4615030|archive-date=29 January 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> Columnist Dimitris Bouras, writing for ''[[Kathimerini]]'', mentioned "the beneficial effects that the prestigious award could have" and wrote that the nomination reveals three interesting facts: "1) in Greece we need to be extroverts (and not only in cinema), 2) exportable product is whatever has an identity, 3) ''Dogtooth''{{'}}s nomination is like an investment{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}[[manna]] from the heaven of Hollywood for the developing Greek cinema."<ref name="Kathimerini">{{Cite web|last=Bouras|first=Dimitris|date=31 January 2011|url=http://news.kathimerini.gr/4dcgi/_w_articles_civ_2_26/01/2011_430122|title=Investing Extroversion|work=Kathimerini|access-date=31 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120202211955/http://news.kathimerini.gr/4dcgi/_w_articles_civ_2_26/01/2011_430122|archive-date=2 February 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===International===
On [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has a 93% approval rating based on review from 73 critics, with an average score of 7.70/10; the site's "critics consensus" reads: "It'll be too disturbing{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}and meandering{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}for some, but ''Dogtooth'' is as disturbing and startlingly original as modern filmmaking gets".<ref name="rottentomatoes">{{cite web|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dogtooth|title=Dogtooth (Kynodontas) (2010)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=17 October 2020|archive-date=27 November 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171127044519/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dogtooth|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted average]] score of 72 out of 100 based on 18 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/dogtooth |title=Dogtooth Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=23 April 2018 |archive-date=7 April 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180407204441/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/dogtooth |url-status=live }}</ref>

Alistair Harkness of ''[[The Scotsman]]'' hailed Lanthimos as "a bold new voice on the world cinema scene, someone who might soon be elevated to a similar position as those twin pillars of Euro provocation: [[Lars von Trier]] and Michael Haneke", but added that the film is "not{{spaces}}[...] designed simply to shock in the way von Trier's work often does,{{spaces}}[...] nor does it have that annoyingly prescriptive, punitive air of superiority favoured by Haneke's films."<ref name=Harkness>{{Cite news|last=Harkness|first=Alistair|date=24 April 2010|url=http://news.scotsman.com/movies/Film-Review-Dogtooth.6248070.jp|title=Film Review: Dogtooth|work=[[The Scotsman]]|access-date=26 January 2011|location=Edinburgh|archive-date=17 February 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240217174551/https://www.scotsman.com/news|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' praised the filmmaking, finding ''Dogtooth'' "superbly shot, with some deadpan, elegant compositions, and intentionally skewiff framings".<ref name=Bradshaw>{{Cite news|author-link=Peter Bradshaw|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|date=22 April 2010|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/apr/22/dogtooth-review|title=Dogtooth|work=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=26 January 2011|location=London|archive-date=5 February 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150205215044/http://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/apr/22/dogtooth-review|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film three out of four stars, noting the director's "complete command of visuals and performances. His cinematography is like a series of family photographs of a family with something wrong with it. His dialogue sounds composed entirely of sentences memorized from tourist phrase books."<ref name=Ebert>{{Cite news|author-link=Roger Ebert|last=Ebert|first=Roger|date=7 July 2010|url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20100707/REVIEWS/100709986|title=Dogtooth|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|access-date=26 January 2011|archive-date=24 February 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110224234622/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20100707/REVIEWS/100709986|url-status=live}}</ref> Mark Olsen of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote that "All of the film's purposeful weirdness is conveyed with an unaffected simplicity that recalls the dead-aim haphazard compositions of photographer [[William Eggleston]]", concluding that, "as a film, it's pure and singular, but it's not quite fully formed enough to be what one could call truly visionary."<ref name=Olsen>{{Cite news|last=Olsen|first=Mark|date=7 January 2011|url=http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/la-et-capsules-20110107,0,358843.story|title=Movie review: 'Dogtooth'|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=26 January 2011|archive-date=21 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110121202609/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/la-et-capsules-20110107,0,358843.story|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote that the film "at times seems as much an exercise in perversity as an examination of it", and that "The static wide-screen compositions are beautiful and strange, with the heads and limbs of the characters frequently cropped. The light is gauzy and diffuse, helping to produce an atmosphere that is insistently and not always unpleasantly dreamlike. You might think of paintings by [[Balthus]] or maybe [[Alex Katz]], though the implied stories in those pictures are more genuinely evocative and haunting than the actual narrative of ''Dogtooth''."<ref name=Scott>{{Cite news|author-link=A. Several reviewers, such as Harkness and Bradshaw, made comparisons to the 2008 [[Fritzl case]], although they noted that the screenplay was written before the case emerged.<ref name=Harkness /><ref name=Bradshaw /> Scott, like Ebert, made references to [[homeschooling]].<ref name=Ebert /><ref name=Scott /> Resemblances have been noted to the 1972 Mexican film ''[[The Castle of Purity]]''.<ref name="MovieMarker">{{cite web|title=Movie Marker Dogtooth| date=23 February 2013 |url=http://moviemarker.co.uk/?p=12947|access-date=7 May 2013|archive-date=21 April 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140421103357/http://moviemarker.co.uk/?p=12947|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="AVClub">{{cite web |title=The AV Club Dogtooth |website=[[The A.V. The film's larger meaning eluded easy expression. Scott called the film "a conversation piece. Though the conversation may... be more along the lines of: 'What was that?' 'I don't know. Weird.' 'Yeah.' [shudder]. 'Weird.'"<ref name=Scott /> Olsen saw ''Dogtooth''{{'}}s substance as "part enigma, part allegory and even part [[Science fiction film|sci-fi]] in its creation of a completely alternate reality."<ref name=Olsen /> Ebert found a "message" in the film, which he put as: "God help children whose parents insanely demand unquestioning obedience to their deranged standards.... [S]ome have even described the film as a comedy. I wasn't laughing."<ref name=Ebert /> For Bradshaw, the film investigates "the essential strangeness of something society insists is the benchmark of normality: the family, a walled city state with its own autocratic rule and untellable secrets."<ref name=Bradshaw /> Harkness noted the "absolute mockery the situation makes of the perfect family ideal", where "Lanthimos isn't interested in making specific political or social points and he refuses to offer any clarifying backstory", and found ''Dogtooth''{{'}}s oddness "as organic and playful as its impact is incisor sharp."<ref name=Harkness />

In a 2012 interview, filmmaker [[David Lynch]] called ''Dogtooth'' "a fantastic comedy".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-xpm-2012-jun-22-la-et-mn-david-lynch-has-no-ideas-for-a-new-film-20120622-story.html|title=David Lynch says he doesn't have any ideas for a new film|first=Steven|last=Zeitchik|date=22 June 2012|access-date=11 March 2018|via=Los Angeles Times|archive-date=4 March 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200304202150/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-xpm-2012-jun-22-la-et-mn-david-lynch-has-no-ideas-for-a-new-film-20120622-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>
