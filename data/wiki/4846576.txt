'''''American Psycho''''' is a 2000 [[Psychological horror (film and television)|horror]] [[Dark humor|comedy film]]<!-- DO NOT JUST CHANGE THE GENRE WITHOUT FIRST REACHING A NEW CONSENSUS ON THE TALK PAGE--> directed by [[Mary Harron]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Guinevere Turner]]. Based on the [[American Psycho|1991 novel]] by [[Bret Easton Ellis]], it stars [[Christian Bale]] as [[Patrick Bateman]], a wealthy [[New York City]] [[investment banker]] who leads a double life as a [[serial killer]]. [[Willem Dafoe]], [[Jared Leto]], [[Josh Lucas]], [[Chloë Sevigny]], [[Samantha Mathis]], [[Cara Seymour]], [[Justin Theroux]], Turner, and [[Reese Witherspoon]] appear in supporting roles. The film blends [[psychological horror]] and [[black comedy]] to satirize 1980s [[yuppie]] culture and [[consumerism]], exemplified by Bateman.

Ellis considered his novel [[Unfilmability|unfilmable]]; however, producer [[Edward R. Pressman]] was determined to adapt it and bought the film rights in 1992. [[Stuart Gordon]], [[David Cronenberg]], and [[Rob Weiss]] considered directing the film before Harron and Turner began writing the screenplay in 1996. They sought to make a 1980s period film that emphasized the novel's satire. The pre-production period was tumultuous; Harron chose Bale to play Bateman, but because distributor [[Lions Gate Films]] sought [[Leonardo DiCaprio]] in the role, Harron was fired and replaced with [[Oliver Stone]]. After DiCaprio and Stone left due to creative differences, Harron was rehired and allowed to cast Bale. [[Principal photography]] began in February 1999 in Toronto and New York City.

''American Psycho'' premiered at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] on January 21, 2000, and was released theatrically in the United States on April 14. The film received mostly positive reviews, with praise for Bale's performance and the screenplay, and grossed $34 million on a $7 million budget. It has since developed a [[cult following]] and a strong presence in [[Internet meme|meme]] culture since the 2010s.<ref name="Potvin 2022">{{cite news |last1=Potvin |first1=James |title=10 Movie Characters Who Became Memes |url=https://screenrant.com/movie-characters-popular-meme-template/ |access-date=4 November 2024 |work=ScreenRant |date=16 March 2022 |archive-date=September 30, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220930224052/https://screenrant.com/movie-characters-popular-meme-template/ |url-status=live }}</ref> By December 2024, a new adaptation of the novel, directed by [[Luca Guadagnino]], was in the works.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://deadline.com/2024/10/american-psycho-movie-luca-guadagnino-1236120177/ | title=Luca Guadagnino to Direct New 'American Psycho' Movie at Lionsgate with Scott Z. Burns Set to Adapt | date=October 18, 2024 | access-date=October 18, 2024 | archive-date=November 10, 2024 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241110023628/https://deadline.com/2024/10/american-psycho-movie-luca-guadagnino-1236120177/ | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last1=Malkin |first1=Marc |last2=Keslassy |first2=Elsa |last3=Siegel |first3=Tatiana |date=December 11, 2024 |title=Austin Butler Poised to Star as Patrick Bateman in Luca Guadagnino's 'American Psycho' (EXCLUSIVE) |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/global/austin-butler-luca-guadagnino-american-psycho-1236245941/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241213120027/https://variety.com/2024/film/global/austin-butler-luca-guadagnino-american-psycho-1236245941/ |archive-date=December 13, 2024 |access-date=June 20, 2025 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries should be between 400-700 words. -->
In 1987, New York City [[investment banker]] [[Patrick Bateman]] meticulously maintains a polished social façade while harboring violent impulses. He dines at trendy restaurants, obsesses over his physical appearance, and navigates a shallow circle of wealthy associates he secretly despises, all while engaged to Evelyn Williams. During a business meeting, he and his colleagues flaunt their [[business card]]s respective designs. Bateman is enraged by the perceived superiority of his colleague Paul Allen's card. Later, he encounters a homeless man and his dog in an alley; initially offering help, Bateman instead insults and brutally kills them.

Bateman's envy intensifies over Allen's more affluent successful lifestyle, able to easily secure reservations at Dorsia, an elite restaurant Bateman cannot access. At a [[Christmas]] party, he arranges dinner with Allen, who mistakes him for another co-worker, Marcus Halberstram. During the dinner, Allen's condescending attitude fuels Bateman's resentment. Bateman gets him drunk, lures him to his apartment, and murders him with an axe. He disposes of the body and uses Allen's keys to access his apartment, leaving a message on Allen's answering machine claiming he has gone to London. When [[private investigator]] Donald Kimball questions Bateman about Allen's disappearance, mentioning sightings of Allen in London, Bateman deflects suspicion.

Bateman hires prostitutes Christie and Sabrina, engaging in sadistic acts before paying them to leave. His violence nearly arouses colleague Luis Carruthers when he attempts to strangle him in a restaurant restroom, but Carruthers misinterprets the act as a sexual advance, horrifying Bateman, who flees. Kimball's second interview heightens Bateman's paranoia, but he continues his spree, seducing a model and later toying with killing his secretary, Jean. At his apartment, Jean narrowly escapes harm after Bateman warns her that he might lose control, unaware of the model's severed head in his refrigerator.

Kimball later informs Bateman that a colleague claims to have dined with Allen on the night of his disappearance, solidifying Bateman's alibi. Kimball dismisses the idea of Allen's murder as implausible, leaving Bateman relieved yet unnerved. Bateman rehires Christie and invites acquaintance Elizabeth for a drug-fueled encounter that turns deadly. Christie discovers corpses in his apartment while fleeing, only to be killed when Bateman drops a chainsaw on her in a stairwell.

Bateman unravels after breaking off his engagement with Evelyn. An [[automated teller machine|ATM]] bizarrely displays "Feed me a stray cat", prompting him to nearly kill the kitten that had appeared, but he shoots a woman who intervenes instead. Fleeing from police, he shoots an officer, inexplicably blows up a patrol car, and kills more innocents before hiding in his office building. In a frantic voicemail to his lawyer, Harold Carnes, Bateman confesses to around 40 murders, including Allen, Christie, Elizabeth, and others.

The next day, Bateman visits Allen's apartment, now vacant and for sale. A realtor, suspicious of his [[dust mask]], denies the apartment's connection to Allen and urges him to leave. Jean, alarmed by Bateman's disturbed state, finds his journal filled with graphic sketches of murder. At lunch, Carnes mistakes Bateman for someone else, laughs off the confession, and claims he recently dined with Allen in London, undermining Bateman's reality. As his friends discuss dinner reservations and muse about [[Ronald Reagan]]'s persona, Bateman, uncertain if his crimes were real or imagined, [[narration|narrates]] his unrelenting pain and desire to inflict it on others. Recognizing that his punishment and liberation from a hollow existence will never come, he declares his confession meaningless.

==Production==
=== Early development ===
[[File:Bret Easton Ellis in Festival America.jpg|thumb|right|upright|[[Bret Easton Ellis]], who wrote the ''[[American Psycho]]'' novel and early drafts of the adaptation, in 2010.]]

The film is an adaptation of the novel ''[[American Psycho]]'' by [[Bret Easton Ellis]], which was published in 1991 amid significant controversy over its graphic depiction of violence against women. Ellis had been disappointed by the [[Less than Zero (film)|1987 film adaptation]] of his first novel, ''[[Less than Zero (novel)|Less than Zero]]'' (1985), and did not expect that anyone would be interested in adapting ''American Psycho'', which he considered possibly [[Unfilmability|unfilmable]].<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory">{{cite web |last1=Molloy |first1=Tim |title=American Psycho: An Oral History, 20 Years After Its Divisive Debut |url=https://www.moviemaker.com/american-psycho-anniversary-oral-history-christian-bale-mary-harron-bret-easton-ellis/ |website=[[MovieMaker]] |access-date=April 10, 2022 |date=April 14, 2020 |pages=1–4 |archive-date=October 9, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251009202301/https://www.moviemaker.com/american-psycho-anniversary-oral-history-christian-bale-mary-harron-bret-easton-ellis/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Nonetheless, development of a film adaptation began in 1992, after [[Johnny Depp]] expressed interest and producer [[Edward R. Pressman]] bought the [[film rights]].<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind">{{cite web |last1=Gopalan |first1=Nisha |title=American Psycho: the story behind the film |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/mar/24/fiction.breteastonellis |website=[[The Guardian]] |access-date=April 10, 2022 |date=March 23, 2000 |archive-date=February 25, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210225081010/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/mar/24/fiction.breteastonellis |url-status=live }}</ref> Pressman, to Ellis' surprise, was "obsessed" with turning ''American Psycho'' into a film.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> Ellis discussed the project with filmmaker [[Stuart Gordon]] but felt that he was unsuitable.<ref name="Movieline: Ellis">{{cite web |last1=Buchanan |first1=Kyle |title=Bret Easton Ellis on American Psycho, Christian Bale, and His Problem with Women Directors |url=http://movieline.com/2010/05/18/bret-easton-ellis-on-american-psycho-christian-bale-and-his-problem-with-women-directors/ |website=[[Movieline]] |access-date=April 10, 2022 |date=May 18, 2010 |pages=1–3 |archive-date=May 2, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140502192331/http://movieline.com/2010/05/18/bret-easton-ellis-on-american-psycho-christian-bale-and-his-problem-with-women-directors/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[David Cronenberg]] and [[Brad Pitt]] respectively became attached to direct and star, and Ellis was brought on to write the screenplay. The process was difficult for Ellis; Cronenberg did not want to use any of the restaurant or nightclub material from the novel (which he considered boring), wanted to excise the violence, and mandated that the script be 65–70 pages. Ellis considered Cronenberg's directions "insane" and ignored them.<ref name="Movieline: Ellis" /><ref name="IndieWire: EllisDraft">{{cite web |last1=Jagernauth |first1=Kevin |title=Bret Easton Ellis Says He Wrote A Script For 'American Psycho' For David Cronenberg, With Brad Pitt Attached To Star |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2016/04/bret-easton-ellis-says-he-wrote-a-script-for-american-psycho-for-david-cronenberg-with-brad-pitt-attached-to-star-83838/ |website=[[IndieWire]] |access-date=April 10, 2022 |date=April 1, 2016 |archive-date=September 24, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220924140949/https://www.indiewire.com/2016/04/bret-easton-ellis-says-he-wrote-a-script-for-american-psycho-for-david-cronenberg-with-brad-pitt-attached-to-star-83838/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> Ellis' draft departed significantly from the novel, as he had "been living with it for, like, three and a half years, four years" and had grown bored with it.<ref name="Movieline: Ellis" /> It ended with an elaborate musical sequence to [[Barry Manilow]]'s "[[Daybreak (Barry Manilow song)|Daybreak]]" atop the [[World Trade Center (1973–2001)|World Trade Center]], a change which Ellis felt exemplified how fatigued he was with the material.<ref name="Movieline: Ellis" />

The development was prolonged due to what ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called ''American Psycho''{{'s}} "literary complexity", which made adapting it to film difficult.<ref name="Variety: Fund">{{cite news |first=Benedict |last=Carver |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/lion-s-gate-to-fund-psycho-1117470459/ |title=Lion's Gate to fund 'Psycho' |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=May 5, 1998 |access-date=February 24, 2015 |archive-date=April 2, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402111229/http://variety.com/1998/film/news/lion-s-gate-to-fund-psycho-1117470459/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Cronenberg was dissatisfied with Ellis' draft and by March 1994 had sought a new draft from [[Norman Snider]];<ref name="IndieWire: EllisDraft" /><ref>{{cite news |first=David |last=Rooney |url=https://variety.com/1994/film/news/disney-wins-houston-and-washington-teaming-119623/ |title=Disney wins Houston and Washington teaming … |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=March 2, 1994 |access-date=February 24, 2015}}</ref> Ellis later recalled that Cronenberg left the project after he disliked Snider's draft even more.<ref name="IndieWire: EllisDraft" /> Ellis wrote another draft for [[Rob Weiss]] in 1995, but the film again failed to materialize.<ref name="IndieWire: EllisDraft" /> Pressman did not want to make a film that would offend people and described Ellis' draft as "completely pornographic".<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Pressman appeared at the [[1996 Cannes Film Festival]] to pre-sell the distribution rights to no avail.<ref>{{cite news |author=Peter Bart |url=https://variety.com/1997/voices/columns/fast-talkers-can-t-hold-a-candle-to-pressman-1117859584/ |title=Fast-talkers can't hold a candle to Pressman |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=May 12, 1997 |access-date=February 24, 2015}}</ref>

=== Development under Mary Harron ===
After [[Mary Harron]]'s film ''[[I Shot Andy Warhol]]'' premiered at the [[49th Cannes Film Festival]] to positive reviews in January 1996, she received a call from Roberta Hanley—who operated the production company which held the ''American Psycho'' film rights—with an offer to direct the film.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation">{{cite web |last1=Shapiro |first1=Lila |title=In Conversation: Mary Harron |url=https://www.vulture.com/2020/04/mary-harron-american-psycho-in-conversation.html |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |access-date=April 10, 2022 |date=April 22, 2020 |archive-date=April 10, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220410031637/https://www.vulture.com/2020/04/mary-harron-american-psycho-in-conversation.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> Harron had attempted to read the novel when it was originally published but found it too violent.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> However, rereading it, she realized that "just enough time ha[d] passed" to produce a [[period film]] set in the 1980s, "bring out the satire", and comment on the era, which interested her.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /><ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Harron was ambivalent towards the other "very mainstream and boring" offers she was receiving following ''I Shot Andy Warhol'' and decided to make ''American Psycho'' due to its "risky" nature.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" />

[[File:Guinevere Turner.jpg|thumb|right|upright|Co-writer [[Guinevere Turner]] in 2006.]]
Harron read the existing drafts; while she somewhat enjoyed Ellis', she felt that most were too moralistic, missing the novel's preciseness in depicting [[social privilege]].<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Harron told Pressman that she would only join the project if she could write her own screenplay.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Pressman commented that out of all the directors who attached themselves to ''American Psycho'', Harron was "the only person who actually ever conveyed a clear solution as to how to do it".<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Harron recruited [[Guinevere Turner]], with whom she had been working on what would become ''[[The Notorious Bettie Page]]'' (2005), to co-write. Turner was not a horror fan and had never heard of ''American Psycho'', but Harron convinced her that it would be a good project to pursue. Though she found the novel unsavory, Turner appreciated its blend of humor and horror and concluded that "with the right spin it could be a really subversive, feminist movie".<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" />

Harron and Turner excised most of the novel's violence outside four sequences of Bateman's murders.<ref name="NYT: Digging">{{cite web |last1=Weber |first1=Bruce |title=FILM; Digging Out the Humor in a Serial Killer's Tale |url=http://www.nytimes.com/1999/04/04/movies/film-digging-out-the-humor-in-a-serial-killer-s-tale.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=April 12, 2022 |date=April 4, 1999 |url-access=limited}}</ref> Their approach to the material and Bateman's character was influenced by [[Mario Bava]]'s ''[[giallo]]'' film ''[[Hatchet for the Honeymoon]]'' (1970), with Bava historian [[Tim Lucas]] noting that both films feature protagonists motivated by a desire for [[self-discovery]] in their killings.{{sfn|Lucas|2007|p=793}} Harron recalled facing scrutiny for depicting Bateman as [[homophobic]]—a criticism she found odd since no objections were raised over his murders. She also received requests to delve into Bateman's psychology but said that "having a very clear psychological explanation [wasn't] of great interest to me" since she found the concept generic, shallow, and unrealistic.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Harron rejected suggestions to explore Bateman's family and background; she felt it was unnecessary and that Bateman was simply "a monster".<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" />

Harron met with several actors for the role of Patrick Bateman but struggled to find a suitable candidate. She noted that "if someone isn't 100 percent on a role like [Bateman], you can't cast them and they shouldn't do it".<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> [[Billy Crudup]] was attached to the role for a month and a half,<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> but was uneasy and left the project.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Turner appreciated Crudup's honesty in admitting he could not understand the character. Harron sent the script to [[Christian Bale]],<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> but he had never read ''American Psycho'' and thus had no interest.<ref name="NYT: Digging" /> Harron contacted [[Christine Vachon]], who was working with Bale on ''[[Velvet Goldmine]]'' (1998) at the time, and Vachon told him to read the script.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Bale found the script humorous and immediately became interested, and flew to New York to audition in Harron's living room.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /><ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /><ref name="NYT: Digging" />

Bale struggled to speak in an [[American accent]] since he had been speaking in a [[Manchester accent]] for ''Velvet Goldmine'', but Harron thought it was clear he understood the role.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Like Harron, Bale was uninterested in Bateman's backstory; he saw the character as "an alien who landed in the unabashedly capitalist New York of the '80s".<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron felt he was the only one who fit the role, later saying he "saw the part the way that I did, and he got the humor of it". When auditioning others she "had the feeling a lot of the other actors kind of thought Bateman was cool". Bale, she said, did not.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron thought casting the relatively unknown Bale was risky, but "had a lot of faith in him", as ''Velvet Goldmine'' director [[Todd Haynes]] told her that Bale was "the best actor I've ever worked with".<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron and Bale, in-character as Bateman, met with Ellis for dinner, an experience Ellis told Bale later into the evening was "unnerving [him]".<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" />

=== Pre-production and casting ===
[[Lionsgate Films]] acquired the ''American Psycho'' distribution rights in May 1998 and set a budget of $10 million. Bale, [[Willem Dafoe]] and [[Jared Leto]] expressed interest and were in talks, although no actors were signed on then. Production was planned to start in August. <ref name="Variety: Fund" /><ref name="Variety: CastingLeo">{{cite news |first=Dan |last=Cox |url=https://variety.com/1998/more/news/casting-leo-has-harron-hesitant-1117471132/ |title=Casting Leo has Harron hesitant |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=May 21, 1998 |access-date=February 24, 2015 |archive-date=April 2, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402200920/http://variety.com/1998/more/news/casting-leo-has-harron-hesitant-1117471132/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Harron suggested that the slim Bale go to a gym since Bateman frequently exercises; she said that within two weeks, Bale had "totally transformed".<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Turner said Bale "became completely ripped, super tan, got his teeth turned into perfect American teeth. I think he said he was modeling himself after his lawyer, or his agent, or [[Tom Cruise]] — an amalgam of those".<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> Harron recalled that Bale drew inspiration for his performance from a televised Tom Cruise interview: "I saw Tom Cruise on a talk show last night, and there was something about that friendliness, with almost nothing behind the eyes."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tom Cruise character was cut from 'American Psycho' but he still inspired Patrick Bateman |url=https://ew.com/american-psycho-cut-tom-cruise-character-inspired-patrick-bateman-11715210 |access-date=2026-01-12 |website=EW.com |language=en |archive-date=July 23, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250723214542/https://ew.com/american-psycho-cut-tom-cruise-character-inspired-patrick-bateman-11715210 |url-status=live }}</ref> Bale received numerous warnings that starring in ''American Psycho'' was "career suicide", but this only made him more committed.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" />

Bale was relatively unknown at the time; he had been only 13 at the time of his then-most famous role, in [[Steven Spielberg]]'s ''[[Empire of the Sun (film)|Empire of the Sun]]'' (1987).<ref name="ShortList: Troubled">{{cite web |author1=''ShortList'' Team |title=The troubled birth of American Psycho |url=https://www.shortlist.com/news/the-troubled-birth-of-american-psycho |website=[[ShortList]] |access-date=April 12, 2022 |date=September 7, 2021 |archive-date=May 31, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220531170711/https://www.shortlist.com/news/the-troubled-birth-of-american-psycho |url-status=live }}</ref> Lionsgate did not want to cast him and pushed for a more famous star like [[Edward Norton]] or [[Leonardo DiCaprio]],<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> who Lionsgate was willing to pay $20 million, although the budget for the film itself would remain only $6 million. DiCaprio was considered the biggest star in the world at the time, having just starred in ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]'' (1997), then [[List of highest-grossing films|the highest-grossing film ever]]. DiCaprio was interested in playing Bateman,<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /><ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> but Harron opposed casting him, comparing the prospect to [[Demi Moore]]'s casting in the critically panned 1995 film ''[[The Scarlet Letter (1995 film)|Scarlet Letter]]''.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> She argued he was too boyish to play Bateman and that his presence would harm the film given his young female fanbase.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /><ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron refused to even consider meeting with him, despite Pressman's pleas.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Ellis did not mind the idea of DiCaprio as Bateman, though he knew this annoyed Bale and Harron.<ref name="Movieline: Ellis" />

At the [[51st Cannes Film Festival]] in May 1998, Lionsgate suddenly announced that DiCaprio had been cast as Bateman.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /><ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Though Pressman wanted Harron to stay,<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> she was fired after making it clear that she would not direct ''American Psycho'' without Bale.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Furthermore, DiCaprio wanted to work with a major director and had drafted a shortlist that included [[Danny Boyle]], [[Jonathan Demme]], [[Stanley Kubrick]], and [[Martin Scorsese]].<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /><ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> [[Oliver Stone]] was hired to direct and, after a reading with DiCaprio, Leto, and [[Cameron Diaz]],<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> began reworking the script. Stone and DiCaprio wanted to take the film in a more [[Psychological fiction|psychological]] direction in contrast to Harron's [[satire]]<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> and turn it into a [[Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (character)|Jekyll and Hyde]]-like story.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Their endeavor was beset by creative differences; Pressman said, "[DiCaprio] was looking for solutions to things that weren't problems. . . .As time went on more and more questions came into Leo's mind — which might have been about the script or other factors".<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Bale was so confident DiCaprio would depart that he turned down other roles for nine months and continued preparing.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /><ref name="GQ: Bale2011" />

DiCaprio departed in favor of Boyle's ''[[The Beach (film)|The Beach]]'' (2000), which led to Stone's withdrawal.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> Turner later said she heard from a friend that DiCaprio chose to leave after [[Gloria Steinem]], a strong critic of the novel, convinced him to abandon the project due to his young fanbase.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory">{{cite web |last1=Tenreyro |first1=Tatiana |title=Blood, Boycott, and Body Bags: An Oral History of 'American Psycho' |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/m7qzxq/blood-boycott-and-body-bags-an-oral-history-of-american-psycho |website=[[Vice (website)|Vice]] |access-date=April 11, 2022 |date=April 20, 2020 |archive-date=June 11, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240611191008/https://www.vice.com/en/article/m7qzxq/blood-boycott-and-body-bags-an-oral-history-of-american-psycho |url-status=live }}</ref> Lionsgate rehired Harron but was still against casting Bale as Bateman.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Lionsgate offered the role to [[Ewan McGregor]], who turned it down after Bale personally urged him to do so.<ref name="GQ: Bale2011">{{cite news |first=Jonathan |last=Heaf |url=http://www.gq-magazine.co.uk/entertainment/articles/2011-04/26/gq-film-christian-bale-interview |title=Christian Bale: behind the mask |work=[[GQ]] |date=April 26, 2011 |access-date=March 2, 2015 |archive-date=January 9, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150109162505/http://www.gq-magazine.co.uk/entertainment/articles/2011-04/26/gq-film-christian-bale-interview |url-status=live }}</ref> Harron spoke with [[Ben Affleck]], [[Matt Damon]], Edward Norton, and [[Vince Vaughn]], but after they all declined, Lionsgate begrudgingly agreed to hire Bale with a small $50,000 salary.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" /> Lionsgate also mandated that the budget not exceed $10 million and that recognizable actors would fill the supporting roles.<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> By that point, Dafoe, Leto, [[Reese Witherspoon]], and [[Chloë Sevigny]] were already committed; Harron and Bale unsuccessfully tried to convince [[Winona Ryder]] to play Evelyn Williams.<ref name="Vulture: Conversation" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] commenced in February 1999,<ref name="Variety: Rabble">{{cite web |last1=Kelly |first1=Brendan |title='Psycho' rabble |url=https://variety.com/1999/film/news/psycho-rabble-1117491557/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=April 13, 2022 |date=February 22, 1999 |quote=The $10 million pic begins production Sunday in Toronto. There will also be some shooting in New York. |archive-date=April 13, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220413143555/https://variety.com/1999/film/news/psycho-rabble-1117491557/ |url-status=live }}</ref> and lasted seven weeks,<ref name="NYT: Risky" /> with a budget of $7 million.<ref name="Bloody: Locations" /> [[Andrzej Sekuła]] served as [[cinematographer]]; he and Harron frequently argued, with Ross recalling that "[Sekuła] was setting up shots that in [Harron]'s mind may have been cool shots, pretty shots, but didn't tell the story she wanted to".<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> As American companies did not want to be associated with ''American Psycho'', the production team had to turn to European companies for clothes and cosmetics,<ref name="Guardian: StoryBehind" /> though these still imposed restrictions. [[Rolex]] made Harron change the novel's line "don't touch the Rolex" to "don't touch the watch", while [[Cerruti 1881]] disallowed Bale from wearing its clothes during the murder scenes.<ref>{{cite news |title=The American Psycho watch |url=https://www.esquireme.com/style/american-psycho-watch |website=[[Esquire (magazine)|Esquire]] |access-date=April 18, 2022 |date=April 26, 2015 |archive-date=July 9, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210709183757/https://www.esquireme.com/style/american-psycho-watch |url-status=live }}</ref>

Though some outdoor shots were captured in New York City, where the film is set, the majority of filming took place in downtown [[Toronto]], including the [[Toronto-Dominion Centre]], where Bateman's office is located,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1151988/how-american-psychos-filming-locations-helped-feed-the-storys-narrative/ |title=How American Psycho's Filming Locations Helped Feed The Story's Narrative |author=Brady, Erin |work=[[/Film]] |date=January 1, 2023 |access-date=August 25, 2024 |archive-date=August 25, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240825200414/https://www.slashfilm.com/1151988/how-american-psychos-filming-locations-helped-feed-the-storys-narrative/ |url-status=live }}</ref> and a variety of scenes shot in bars and restaurants around the city.<ref name="Bloody: Locations">{{cite web |last1=Clark |first1=Sean |title=Horror's Hallowed Grounds: 'American Psycho'! |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/21936/horrors-hallowed-grounds-american-psycho/ |website=[[Bloody Disgusting]] |access-date=April 13, 2022 |date=October 6, 2010 |archive-date=November 17, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191117221041/https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/21936/horrors-hallowed-grounds-american-psycho/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Anti-violence advocates petitioned [[Toronto City Hall]] to deny the production permission to film in Toronto and organized protests because of reports that [[Paul Bernardo]]—who committed serial murders and rapes in Toronto—owned a copy of the novel.<ref name="NYT: Digging" /><ref name="Variety: Rabble" />{{efn|The claim that Bernardo was inspired by ''American Psycho'' is false. His crime spree began in 1987, several years before ''American Psycho''{{'s}} publication. Additionally, author [[Stephen Williams (writer)|Stephen Williams]] wrote in ''Invisible Darkness'', a book about the Bernardo case, that Bernardo was nearly illiterate and the copy of ''American Psycho'' in his house belonged to his wife [[Karla Homolka]]; he is unlikely to have ever read it.<ref name="NYT: Risky" />}} As a result, the production faced difficulty securing shooting locations; the scenes in Bateman's office had to be filmed on a [[sound stage]] because the owners of the building that Harron intended to film in feared negative publicity.<ref name="NYT: Risky" /> Harron had been unaware of the novel's connection to the Bernardo case and sympathized with the protesters, but reasoned that she did not "want to put horrible mayhem on the screen... there's something to do here that hasn't really been done, a portrait of the late 80's that's worth putting on the screen".<ref name="NYT: Digging" /> To avoid protests, the production removed the film's title from [[daily call sheet]]s and parking permits.<ref name="NYT: Risky">{{cite web |last1=Harron |first1=Mary |title=FILM; The Risky Territory Of 'American Psycho' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2000/04/09/movies/film-the-risky-territory-of-american-psycho.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=4 November 2024 |date=April 9, 2000 |url-access=limited |archive-date=November 4, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181104042351/https://www.nytimes.com/2000/04/09/movies/film-the-risky-territory-of-american-psycho.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

Bale brought his copy of the novel to the set every day. Harron remained faithful to the novel's dialogue, so he "would kind of be skimming through it and looking at it and finding little bits and conferring in the corner with [her]".<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron stated Bale drew inspiration from [[Nicolas Cage]]'s performance in ''[[Vampire's Kiss]]'' (1989) and Tom Cruise in an interview on ''[[Late Night with David Letterman]]''.{{sfn|Cheung|2012|p=166}}<ref>{{cite news |first=Stuart |last=Heritage |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/filmblog/2009/oct/23/tom-cruise-christian-bale-american-psycho |title=Who other than Tom Cruise has inspired Christian Bale? |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=October 23, 2009 |access-date=February 24, 2015}}</ref> He kept images of 1980s figures who he felt Bateman would attempt to emulate, such as Cruise and [[Donald Trump]], in his trailer. A [[method actor]], Bale never [[Breaking character|broke character]] during the shoot—he did not socialize off-camera, always spoke with an American accent, and worked out at a gym for hours to maintain Bateman's physique.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /><ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> His behavior confused other actors, with Sevigny saying that she had never seen an actor commit to a role to such a degree. [[Josh Lucas]] later told Bale that the other actors "thought that [he] was the worst actor they'd ever seen" and did not understand why Harron fought for him.<ref name="MovieMaker: OralHistory" /> Harron nicknamed Bale "Robo-Actor" for his ability to control his [[sweat gland]]s, which she and his co-stars noticed during the business card scene.<ref name="CinemaBlend: 14Facts" />

[[File:American Psycho - Paul Allen murder scene.webm|thumb|An example of his emotional resonance, Bateman delivers an analytical monologue about [[Huey Lewis and the News]] while preparing to murder coworker Paul Allen, showcasing his detachment from humanity and the film's juxtaposition of charm and violence.<ref name="EW: Axe">{{cite magazine |last1=Lawrence |first1=Derek |title=American Psycho turns 20: Inside Paul Allen's murder — and Jared Leto's surprise |url=https://ew.com/movies/american-psycho-paul-allen-murder-the-shot/ |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |access-date=April 12, 2022 |date=April 14, 2020 |archive-date=September 25, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220925074306/https://ew.com/movies/american-psycho-paul-allen-murder-the-shot/ |url-status=live }}</ref>]]

Harron and Bale excluded Leto from rehearsals of the murder of Paul Allen so Leto's expression of shock when Bale ran at him with an axe would be genuine. The shots of Bateman swinging his axe at Allen had to be done quickly since the scene's use of [[theatrical blood]] limited the number of takes. Bale swung at a [[Poly(methyl methacrylate)|Plexiglass]]-coated camera as the crew squirted fake blood at his face. The blood covered only half of Bale's face by accident but Harron found this "a perfect metaphor for the Jekyll-and-Hyde aspect of Bateman: pristine on the outside, bloody and psychotic on the inside".<ref name="EW: Axe"/> Bale improvised Bateman's [[Moonwalk (dance)|moonwalk]], a change from the novel that Ellis initially disliked, but grew to appreciate over time.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> For the subsequent interview scenes featuring Donald Kimball, Harron shot three takes and requested that Dafoe act differently in each of them. Dafoe acted as if Kimball knew Bateman was Allen's killer in the first, only suspected him in the second, and did not suspect anything in the third. The three takes were then blended in montage to create uncertainty regarding the character's intentions.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Russell |first1=Calum |title=The incredible detail of Willem Dafoe's performance in 'American Psycho' |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/willem-dafoe-performance-in-american-psycho/ |website=Far Out Magazine |access-date=April 12, 2022 |date=May 1, 2021 |archive-date=April 11, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220411215021/https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/willem-dafoe-performance-in-american-psycho/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Harron and Turner kept most violence off-screen, but Harron wanted "one classic horror movie scary scene" with "a big explosion of violence" that embraced the novel's brutality.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> They conceived a [[threesome]] with Bateman and two women that ends with him murdering them.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> Bale had no problem appearing nude, though he wore sneakers and covered his penis with a sock.<ref name="CinemaBlend: 14Facts">{{cite web |last1=Eisenberg |first1=Eric |title=14 American Psycho Behind-The-Scenes Facts You Might Not Know |url=https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2550821/american-psycho-behind-the-scenes-facts-you-might-not-know |website=[[CinemaBlend]] |access-date=April 13, 2022 |date=July 23, 2020 |archive-date=April 13, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220413003400/https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2550821/american-psycho-behind-the-scenes-facts-you-might-not-know |url-status=live }}</ref> One of the women was portrayed by Turner, who found it "very daunting" being directed by Harron, despite having been her co-writer for years.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> The shot in which Bateman murders Turner's character while having sex with her took several takes, as it was difficult to get the theatrical blood to ooze through the sheets as they intended.<ref name="Vice: OralHistory" /> Bateman's phone confession took around 15 takes because Harron felt Bale's acting got better as he became more flushed. In contrast, a shot in which Bateman peels off a [[facial mask]] took only one take.<ref name="CinemaBlend: 14Facts" />

=== Music ===
{{main|American Psycho (soundtrack)}}

[[File:HueyLewisandtheNews06.jpg|thumb|right|The [[Huey Lewis and the News]] (pictured) song "[[Hip to Be Square]]" appears in the film.]]

[[American Psycho (soundtrack)|''American Psycho''{{'s}} soundtrack]] features licensed 1980s pop music from a variety of artists, including [[David Bowie]], [[Phil Collins]], the [[The Cure|Cure]], the [[Mediæval Bæbes]], [[New Order (band)|New Order]], [[Robert Palmer]] and [[Eric B. & Rakim]]<!-- The David Bowie and The Cure songs are not from the 1980s. The David Bowie song Something in the Air was recorded in 1999 and remixed for the film in 2000 and The Cure song, "Watching Me Fall", was recorded and remixed in 2000. -->.<ref name="Guardian: Music" /><ref name="Austin: Cale" /><ref name="AllMusic" /> Due to the film's controversial nature, obtaining the rights proved difficult. Though the production was able to obtain the rights to all necessary songs,<ref name="CinemaBlend: 14Facts" /> [[Whitney Houston]] refused to allow the use of her performance of "[[The Greatest Love of All]]", so an orchestral arrangement had to be used instead. The [[Huey Lewis and the News]] song "[[Hip to Be Square]]" appears in the film and was intended to be on the soundtrack album, but was removed, forcing [[Entertainment One Music|Koch Records]] to recall approximately 100,000 copies. [[MNRK Music Group|Koch Records]] president Bob Frank said that the removal was due to [[Huey Lewis]] objecting to the film's violence,<ref name="Guardian: Music">{{cite news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/apr/13/news |title=''American Psycho'' soundtrack in hot water |work=[[The Guardian]] |location=London, England |date=13 April 2000 |access-date=27 July 2010 |archive-date=October 15, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201015171919/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/apr/13/news |url-status=live }}</ref> but in 2013, Lewis said Frank's story was "completely made up".<ref>{{cite web |first=Andy |last=Greene |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/huey-lewis-on-30-years-of-sports-our-15-minutes-were-a-real-15-minutes-20130517 |title=Huey Lewis on 30 Years of 'Sports': 'Our 15 Minutes Were a Real 15 Minutes' |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=May 17, 2013 |access-date=July 3, 2014 |url-access=limited |archive-date=December 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171207203713/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/huey-lewis-on-30-years-of-sports-our-15-minutes-were-a-real-15-minutes-20130517 |url-status=live }}</ref> Lewis's manager Bob Brown said that "Hip to Be Square" was included on the album without their permission, which he [[wikt:speculate|speculated]] was a [[publicity stunt]].<ref name="Guardian: Music" />

The original score was composed by Welsh musician and [[Velvet Underground]] co-founder [[John Cale]], who also scored ''I Shot Andy Warhol'',<ref name="Austin: Cale" /> alongside M.J. Mynarski.<ref name="AllMusic">{{cite web |last1=Mathis |first1=Derrick |title=American Psycho Review by Derrick Mathis |url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/american-psycho-mw0000608295?1650321588754 |website=[[AllMusic]] |access-date=April 18, 2022 |archive-date=April 18, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220418224353/https://www.allmusic.com/album/american-psycho-mw0000608295?1650321588754 |url-status=live }}</ref> Cale joined because, like ''I Shot Andy Warhol'', he found Harron's script intelligent. He composed in his studio using a [[Sampler (musical instrument)|sampler]] and sent the [[Audio file format|music file]] to someone who turned it into a composition and hired [[musician]]s to record it. Harron described Cale's work as "a soulful, even melancholy sound to complement the [[soundtrack]]'s poppy brightness".<ref name="Austin: Cale" /> Cale was uninvolved with the selection of licensed music and [[Audio mixing|sound mixing]], though, for one scene that Harron wanted to be unsettling, he suggested using animal noises, like the tapes of rabbits screaming that the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation]] used against the [[Branch Davidians]] during the [[Waco siege]].<ref name="Austin: Cale">{{cite web |last1=Baumgarten |first1=Marjorie |title=The Sound of One Psycho Cracking |url=https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/2000-04-14/76842/ |website=[[The Austin Chronicle]] |access-date=April 18, 2022 |date=April 14, 2000}}</ref> The [[soundtrack album]] was released on April 4, 2000.

==Release==
As a promotion, one could register to receive e-mails "from" Patrick Bateman, supposedly to his [[Therapy|therapist]].<ref name="Howell">{{cite news |first=Peter |last=Howell |title=''American Psycho''s Web Promo Sickens Star |work=[[Toronto Star]] |location=Toronto, Ontario, Canada |date=March 8, 2000}}</ref> The e-mails, written by a writer attached to the film and approved by Bret Easton Ellis, follow Bateman's life since the events of the film. He discusses such developments as his marriage to (and impending divorce settlement with) his former secretary, Jean, his complete adoration of his son, Patrick Jr., and his efforts to triumph over his business rivals. The e-mails also describe or mention interactions with other characters from the novel, including Timothy Price (Bryce in the film version), Evelyn, Luis, Courtney, David, Detective Kimball, and Marcus. However, Bale was not happy with this kind of marketing: "My main objection is that some people think it will be me returning those e-mails. I don't like that ... I think the movie stands on its own merits and should attract an audience that can appreciate intelligent satire. It's not a slasher flick, but it's also not ''[[American Pie (film)|American Pie]]''. The marketing should reflect that".<ref name="Howell"/>

Lionsgate partnered with the [[Hollywood Stock Exchange]], an online game in which players can invest in films, actors, or musicians using fake Hollywood money, for a $51,000 promotion titled ''Make a Killing with American Psycho'', which invited players to buy 5000 shares of the film using the game's virtual currency, with real money being paid out if the film grossed over $20 million in four weeks.<ref>{{cite web |title=Today's other news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/mar/31/2 |website=[[The Guardian]] |access-date=2 August 2026 |date=31 March 2000}}</ref> This marketing ploy did little to help the film's box office but the studio's co-president [[Tom Ortenberg]] still claimed that it was a success: "The aim was to gain exposure and awareness for the picture, and we did that. Lionsgate will make a tidy profit on the picture".<ref name="guardian">{{cite news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/may/05/news1 |title=Greed appeal fails to lift ''American Psycho'' |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=5 May 2000 |access-date=7 July 2007}}</ref>

''American Psycho'' premiered at the 2000 [[Sundance Film Festival]].<ref name=guardian3>{{cite news |title=''American Psycho'' hits Sundance |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=2000-01-26 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/jan/26/sundancefilmfestival2000.festivals |access-date=2010-07-27 |archive-date=August 12, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170812100043/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/jan/26/sundancefilmfestival2000.festivals |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[Motion Picture Association of America]] (MPAA) initially gave the film an [[NC 17|NC-17]] rating for a scene featuring Bateman having a threesome with two prostitutes. The producers excised approximately 18 seconds of footage to obtain an [[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system|R-rated]] version of the film.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2000-04-14 |title=FILM REVIEW; Murderer! Fiend! (But Well Dressed) (Published 2000) |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2000/04/14/movies/film-review-murderer-fiend-but-well-dressed.html |access-date=2025-11-24 |language=en |archive-date=December 27, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191227222719/https://www.nytimes.com/2000/04/14/movies/film-review-murderer-fiend-but-well-dressed.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Marketing the film proved unusually challenging, as distributors were uncertain how audiences would interpret the mixture of violence and satire. The promotional campaign ultimately focused on the film’s critique of 1980s excess rather than its more graphic elements. Early festival screenings produced divided reactions, with some critics praising the film’s bold tonal balance and others questioning whether the satire was too subtle.<ref name=guardian4>{{cite news |title=''American Psycho'' cut to appease censors |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=2000-02-29 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2000/feb/29/news |access-date=2010-07-27}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.lionsgate-ent.com/press/28feb00.shtml |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20000815102845/http://www.lionsgate-ent.com/press/28feb00.shtml |title=American Psycho" Achieves "R" Rating From MPAA |archive-date=August 15, 2000 |date=February 28, 2000 |access-date=June 10, 2019}}</ref>

=== Box office ===

As of August 14 2026, ''American Psycho'' had grossed $34,270,556 worldwide including $15,070,285 in the United States and Canada, with a domestic opening of $4,961,015.<ref name="BOM" /> Internationally, it grossed $19,196,279. <ref name="BOM" /> The film had a total $7 million production budget.<ref name="BOM" />

===Home media===
A special-edition DVD was released on July 21, 2005.<ref>[http://www.ign.com/movies/american-psycho/dvd-758290 IGN]</ref> In the U.S., two versions of the film have been released: an [[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system|R-rated]] and unrated version. For the edited version and R-rated cinematic release in the U.S., the producers excised about 18 seconds of footage from a scene featuring Bateman having a threesome with two prostitutes. Some dialogue was also edited: Bateman orders a prostitute, Christie, to bend over so that another, Sabrina, can "see your asshole", which was edited to "see your ass". The unedited version also shows Bateman receiving [[Fellatio|oral sex]] from Christie. The uncut version was released on [[Blu-ray]] on February 6, 2007.<ref>[http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/American-Psycho-Blu-ray/135/ Blu-ray (uncut version) on blu-ray.com]</ref> A 4K Blu-ray was released with the Uncut Version on September 25, 2018, in US<ref>[https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/American-Psycho-4K-Blu-ray/208828/ 4K Blu-ray (uncut version) on blu-ray.com]</ref> and October 15 in United Kingdom.<ref>[https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/American-Psycho-4K-Blu-ray/215103/ UK 4K Blu-ray on blu-ray.com]</ref> [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]] also released the film on Blu-ray around Australia, Spain, Poland, Russia, South Africa, Bulgaria, China, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Portugal, Thailand, and Taiwan in December 2008.

In November 2025, the film had a VHS release by [[Lionsgate Films]], alongside ''[[Cabin Fever (2002 film)|Cabin Fever]]'' (2002), and ''[[Leprechaun (film)|Leprechaun]]'' (1993).<ref>{{cite web |title=All Films |url=https://lionsgatelimited.com/collections/all-films |website=lionsgatelimited.com |publisher=Lions Gate Home Entertainment Inc. |access-date=2026-04-11 |archive-date=May 27, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260527050017/https://lionsgatelimited.com/collections/all-films |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception and legacy==
''American Psycho'' debuted at the [[Sundance Film Festival]], where it polarized audiences and critics; some praised the film for its writing and performance from Christian Bale, while others criticized its violent nature.<ref name="NYT: Risky"/> Upon its theatrical release, the film received positive reviews in crucial publications, including ''[[The New York Times]]'' which called it a "mean and lean horror comedy classic".<ref name="holden">{{cite news |last=Holden |first=Stephen |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/041400psycho-film-review.html |title=Murderer! Fiend! Cad! (But Well-Dressed) |work=The New York Times |date=April 14, 2000 |access-date=4 November 2024}}</ref> {{Rotten Tomatoes prose|69||251|If it falls short of the deadly satire of Bret Easton Ellis's novel, ''American Psycho'' still finds its own blend of horror and humor, thanks in part to a fittingly creepy performance by Christian Bale.|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=american_psycho |type=m |title=American Psycho |access-date=April 4, 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 64 out of 100, based on 35 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''American Psycho'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/american-psycho |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=17 June 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{cite Metacritic |id=american-psycho |title=American Psycho |type=m |access-date=January 4, 2023}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "D" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/ |title=CinemaScore |publisher=[[CinemaScore]] |access-date=April 16, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220413083139/https://www.cinemascore.com/ |archive-date=April 13, 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three out of four stars, praising the female perspective brought by Harron and Turner because they depict Bateman not as a psychologically disturbed aberration, but as a certain type of selfish, ego-driven male behavior taken to an extreme. Ebert also described Bale as being "heroic in the way he allows the character to leap joyfully into despicability; there is no instinct for self-preservation here, and that is one mark of a good actor".<ref name="ebert">{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=April 14, 2000 |title=American Psycho |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/american-psycho-2000 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211109005113/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/american-psycho-2000 |access-date=17 November 2021 |archive-date=November 9, 2021}}</ref> In his review for the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', [[Kenneth Turan]] wrote: "The difficult truth is that the more viewers can model themselves after protagonist Bateman, the more they can distance themselves from the human reality of the slick violence that fills the screen and take it all as some kind of a cool joke, the more they are likely to enjoy this stillborn, pointless piece of work".<ref name="turan">{{cite news |date=April 14, 2000 |last=Turan |first=Kenneth |author-link=Kenneth Turan |title=American Psycho |work=Los Angeles Times |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-apr-14-ca-19332-story.html |access-date=2019-10-15 |url-access=limited}}</ref> ''[[Newsweek]]'' magazine's [[David Ansen]] wrote: "But after an hour of dissecting the '80s culture of materialism, [[narcissism]] and greed, the movie begins to repeat itself. It becomes more grisly and surreal, but not more interesting".<ref name="ansen">{{cite news |last=Ansen |first=David |url=http://www.newsweek.com/id/83755 |title=What A Total Psychopath |work=[[Newsweek]] |date=April 17, 2000 |access-date=2009-04-08 |archive-date=May 5, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080505071005/http://www.newsweek.com/id/83755 |url-status=live }}</ref> In his review for ''[[The Village Voice]]'', [[J. Hoberman]] wrote: "If anything, Bale is too knowing. He eagerly works within the constraints of the quotation marks Harron puts around his performance".<ref name="hoberman">{{cite news |last=Hoberman |first=J |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2000-04-11/film/atrocity-exhibitions/1 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209003714/http://www.villagevoice.com/2000-04-11/film/atrocity-exhibitions/1 |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 9, 2013 |title=Atrocity Exhibitions |work=[[The Village Voice]] |date=April 11, 2000 |access-date=2009-04-08}}</ref>

''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s [[Peter Travers]] compared the film favorably to its source material, noting that Harron "responded to the satiric rather than the slasher elements" of the book, resulting in "an uneven movie that nonetheless bristles with stinging wit and exerts a perverse fascination".<ref name="Travers 2000">{{cite magazine |last1=Travers |first1=Peter |title=American Psycho |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-reviews/american-psycho-103350/ |access-date=4 November 2024 |magazine=Rolling Stone |date=14 April 2000 |url-access=limited |archive-date=December 11, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241211233439/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-reviews/american-psycho-103350/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In a review for [[Slate (magazine)|''Slate'']] magazine, [[David Edelstein]] also noted the toned-down brutality and sexual content in comparison to the novel and wrote that the moment where Bateman spares his secretary is when "this one-dimensional film blossoms like a flower".<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.slate.com/articles/arts/movies/2000/04/dressed_to_kill.html |title=Dressed to Kill |website=Slate |date=April 14, 2000}}</ref> [[Owen Gleiberman]] gave the film an "A&minus;" rating, writing for ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'': "By treating the book as raw material for an exuberantly perverse exercise in '[[1980s nostalgia|80s Nostalgia]], [[Harron]] recasts the go-go years as a template for the casually brainwashing-consumer/fashion/image culture that emerged from them. She has made a movie that is really a parable of today".<ref name="gleiberman">{{cite magazine |last=Gleiberman |first=Owen |author-link=Owen Gleiberman |url=http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,275891,00.html |title=American Psycho |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=April 14, 2000 |access-date=2009-04-08 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090328224921/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0%2C%2C275891%2C00.html |archive-date=March 28, 2009 |url-status=dead}}</ref> ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine's Richard Corliss wrote that "Harron and co-screenwriter [[Guinevere Turner]] do understand the book, and they want their film to be understood as a period [[comedy of manners]]".<ref name="corliss">{{cite magazine |last=Corliss |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Corliss |url=http://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,996639,00.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101122105811/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,996639,00.html |url-status=live |archive-date=November 22, 2010 |title=A Yuppie's Killer Instinct |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |date=April 17, 2000 |access-date=2022-04-02 |url-access=limited}}</ref> [[A.O. Scott]] (also from ''The New York Times'') praised the film as well.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.nytimes.com/video/movies/1248069303457/critics-picks-american-psycho.html |access-date=4 November 2024 |title=Video: Critics' Picks: 'American Psycho' |first=Gabe |last=Johnson |newspaper=The New York Times |date=November 8, 2010 |url-access=limited}}</ref>

[[Bloody Disgusting]] ranked the film at No. 19 in its list of the "Top 20 Horror Films of the Decade", with the article praising "Christian Bale's disturbing/darkly hilarious turn as serial killer/Manhattan businessman Patrick Bateman, a role that in hindsight couldn't have been played by any other actor. ... At its best, the film reflects our own narcissism and the shallow American culture it was spawned from, with piercing effectiveness. Much of the credit for this can go to director Mary Harron, whose off-kilter tendencies are a good complement to Ellis's unique style".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/18403 |title=00's Retrospect: Bloody Disgusting's Top 20 Films of the Decade...Part 4 |date=December 15, 2009 |website=[[Bloody Disgusting]] |access-date=2010-01-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100208164216/http://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/18403 |archive-date=February 8, 2010 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Original author Ellis said, "''American Psycho'' was a book I didn't think needed to be turned into a movie", as "the medium of film demands answers", which would make the book "infinitely less interesting".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://clatl.com/culturesurfing/archives/2010/06/19/1534716-bret-easton-ellis-talks-film-adaptations-at-scad |title=Bret Easton Ellis talks film adaptations at SCAD |publisher=[[Creative Loafing]] |access-date=2010-06-19 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100624060251/http://clatl.com/culturesurfing/archives/2010/06/19/1534716-bret-easton-ellis-talks-film-adaptations-at-scad |archive-date=June 24, 2010 |url-status=dead}}</ref> He also said that while the book attempted to add ambiguity to the events and to Bateman's reliability as a narrator, the film appeared to make them completely literal before confusing the issue at the very end.<ref>{{cite web |last=Buchanan |first=Kyle |url=http://movieline.com/2010/05/18/bret-easton-ellis-on-american-psycho-christian-bale-and-his-problem-with-women-directors/ |title=Bret Easton Ellis on American Psycho, Christian Bale, and His Problem with Women Directors |publisher=Movieline |date=2010-05-18 |access-date=2013-03-28 |archive-date=May 2, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140502192331/http://movieline.com/2010/05/18/bret-easton-ellis-on-american-psycho-christian-bale-and-his-problem-with-women-directors/ |url-status=live }}</ref> On a 2014 appearance on the ''[[WTF with Marc Maron]]'' podcast, Ellis indicated that his feelings towards the film were more mixed than negative; he reiterated his opinion that his conception of Bateman as an unreliable narrator did not make an entirely successful transition from page to screen, adding that Bateman's narration was so unreliable that even he, as the author of the book, did not know if Bateman was honestly describing events that actually happened or if he was lying or even hallucinating. Ellis appreciated that the film clarified the humor for audiences who mistook the novel's violence for blatant misogyny as opposed to the deliberately exaggerated satire he had intended, and liked that it gave his novel "a second life" in introducing it to new readers. Ultimately, Ellis said "the movie was okay, the movie was fine. I just didn't think it needed to be made".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wtfpod.com/podcast/episodes/episode_552_-_bret_easton_ellis |title=WTF with Marc Maron Podcast - Episode 552 - Bret Easton Ellis |work=wtfpod.com |date=November 20, 2014}}</ref>

=== Sequel ===
A [[direct-to-video]] sequel, ''[[American Psycho 2]]'', directed by [[Morgan J. Freeman]] and starring [[Mila Kunis]], was released in 2002. The sequel's only connection with the original is the death of Patrick Bateman (played by Michael Kremko wearing a face mask), briefly shown in a flashback. The film was denounced by Bret Easton Ellis.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/304/304429p1.html |title=Bret Easton Ellis Speaks Out |publisher=IGN |date=August 21, 2001 |access-date=August 22, 2010 |archive-date=April 13, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110413174507/http://movies.ign.com/articles/304/304429p1.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=B |first=Scott |date=2001-08-21 |title=Bret Easton Ellis Speaks Out on the American Psycho Sequel |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/08/21/bret-easton-ellis-speaks-out-on-the-american-psycho-sequel |access-date=2023-03-21 |website=IGN |language=en |archive-date=February 6, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220206103754/https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/08/21/bret-easton-ellis-speaks-out-on-the-american-psycho-sequel |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2005, Kunis expressed embarrassment over the film, and spoke out against the idea of a sequel.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1501371/mila-kunis-career-thrives-despite-psycho-in-her-past/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140906231356/http://www.mtv.com/news/1501371/mila-kunis-career-thrives-despite-psycho-in-her-past/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=September 6, 2014 |title=Mila Kunis' Career Thrives Despite 'Psycho' In Her Past |date=May 6, 2005 |access-date=February 10, 2016 |publisher=MTV |last=Harris |first=Chris}}</ref>
