'''''Model'''''{{efn|The title references both the film's subject and Wiseman's template for filmmaking.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=188}}}} is a 1981 American documentary film directed by [[Frederick Wiseman]].<ref name="Slavitt1981" /> The film examines the [[Model (person)|modeling]] industry in New York City.<ref name="Unger1981">{{cite news |last=Unger |first=Arthur |author-link=Arthur Unger |title='Model,' Fred Wiseman's Latest Documentary: In the End, Brilliant |url=https://www.csmonitor.com/1981/0914/091400.html |access-date=November 7, 2025 |work=[[The Christian Science Monitor]] |date=September 14, 1981}}</ref> It was Wiseman's ninth documentary for [[WNET]], a New York PBS station.<ref name="Shales1981">{{cite news |last=Shales |first=Tom |title=Love It, Love It, Love That Face! |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1981/09/26/love-it-love-it-love-that-face/51577eb4-f148-4272-85e9-b519e9711f8e/ |access-date=November 7, 2025 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=September 25, 1981}}</ref>

The film follows the [[Zoli Agency|Zoli modeling agency]], owned by Zoltan "Zoli" Rendessy.<ref name="Slavitt1981">{{cite magazine |last=Slavitt |first=David R. |author-link=David R. Slavitt |title=Culture Note |url= |magazine=[[The New Republic]] |date=September 23, 1981 |volume=185 |issue=12 |page=40}}</ref><ref name="Brody2017">{{cite magazine |last=Brody |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Brody |title=Model |url=https://www.newyorker.com/goings-on-about-town/movies/model |access-date=November 7, 2025 |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |date=April 17, 2017}}</ref> The film shows Zoli himself, his employees, models, clients, and photographers.<ref name="Unger1981" />

==Synopsis==
Like all of Wiseman's films, ''Model'' has no narrator.<ref name="Shales1981" /> The film opens with a cityscape of lower Manhattan, showing the [[World Trade Center (1973–2001)|World Trade Center]].{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=176}} The film intercuts fashion sequences (runway shows, photo shoots, a pantyhose commercial),<ref name="Shales1981" /> Manhattan street scenes (roller skaters, an ambulance, "pedestrians who often look livelier than the models"<ref name="Bender2020">{{cite web |last=Bender |first=Abbey |title=A Classic Film Reminds Us That Modeling Is Labor |url=https://hyperallergic.com/540774/a-classic-film-reminds-us-that-modeling-is-labor/ |website=[[Hyperallergic]] |date=February 6, 2020 |access-date=November 7, 2025}}</ref>),<ref name="Slavitt1981" />{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|pp=5–6}} and shots of mannequins in store windows.{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|p=6}}

The agency screens aspiring models and connects them with clients,<ref name="Brody2017" /> in the process reducing them to labels and stereotypes,{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=181}} including the young executive, the all-American girl, the sophisticate, the streetsmart, and the [[Avon Products|Avon]] look.<ref name="Papson1981" /> Zoli tells one female aspirant who is {{convert|5|ft|6.5|in|cm|abbr=on}} tall that the height is "too short for what we do... It's a problem," but in the following scene, a woman only {{convert|0.75|in|cm|abbr=on}} taller is told, "I think we have some possibilities here."<ref name="Joseph2015">{{cite journal |last=Joseph |first=Alex |title=Second Looks: Two Films about Fashion by Frederick Wiseman |journal=Fashion Theory |date=2015 |volume=20 |issue=1 |pages=103–116 |doi=10.1080/1362704X.2015.1078137}}</ref>

The film's longest sequence, running nearly 30 minutes, is the making of a television commercial for Evan-Picone pantyhose.<ref name="Brody2017" />{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|p=7}} The director instructs the male and female model to run into each other repeatedly.<ref name="Vollmann2010">{{cite book |last=Vollmann |first=William T. |author-link=William T. Vollmann |chapter=In Memory of Us All: Some Scenes Out of Wiseman |title=Frederick Wiseman |publisher=[[Museum of Modern Art]] |location=New York |date=2010 |pages=69–76 |isbn=978-0-87070-791-9 |chapter-url=https://yaleunion.org/wiseman/}}</ref> For the same commercial, model [[Apollonia van Ravenstein]]'s leg is photographed over 79 takes to create a four-second "peacock effect."{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|p=7}}{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=211}} The film shows the completed 30-second commercial.{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|p=6}}

The film ends with an [[Oscar de la Renta]] runway show with twirling fashion models,<ref name="Slavitt1981" />{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=126}} followed by a closing shot of the Manhattan skyline at night.{{sfn|Grant|2023|pp=176-177}}

==Production==
The film was shot in 1979 and 1980<ref name="Joseph2015" /> on black-and-white film.<ref name="Papson1981">{{cite journal |last=Papson |first=Steve |title=Model |journal=[[Humanity & Society]] |date=August 1981 |volume=5 |issue=3 |pages=278–281 |doi=10.1177/016059768100500308}}</ref> Wiseman selected the Zoli agency simply because "the idea of a model agency appealed to me ... and [Zoli] agreed."<ref name="Joseph2013">{{cite web |last=Joseph |first=Alexander |title=Fashion Documents: Frederick Wiseman on His Sartorial Films |url=https://vestoj.com/fashion-documents-frederick-wiseman-on-his-films-about-fashion/ |website=Vestoj |date=December 2013 |access-date=November 7, 2025}}</ref>

Wiseman did not research the modeling industry before filming, and described the editing process as discovery: "It is all a surprise since I know very little about the subject before I begin shooting. The idea is that the film should at least in part show what I learned as a consequence of the shoot and the long period of editing."<ref name="Joseph2013" />

==Release==
The film was broadcast on PBS on September 16, 1981.<ref name="Slavitt1981" />

==Reception==
The film shows modeling as "monotonous, lacking autonomy, and devoid of internal satisfaction",<ref name="Papson1981" /> and as "a grind of rejections and retakes."<ref name="Vollmann2010" /> Novelist [[William T. Vollmann]] had expected the film to be glamorous and was surprised by its monotony.<ref name="Vollmann2010" /> The film examines how institutional routines become repetitive, drawing parallels to military operations.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=100}}

''Model'' reflects Wiseman's increasing political consciousness,{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=166}} contrasting the constructed imagery of advertising with the imperfections of the real world.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=167}} Before filming the pantyhose commercial, the New York City streets are cleaned.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=177}} Wiseman films another documentary crew shooting a shower interview with a male model,{{sfn|Armstrong|1983|p=8}} revealing that the model is wearing underwear.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=191}} A street demonstration that appears to be a feminist protest is revealed to be the filming of a commercial.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=185}} The Zoli agency serves as a lens into the broader workings of advertising, marketing, and retail.{{sfn|Grant|2023|p=167}}
