'''''The Devil Bat''''' is a 1940 [[black-and-white]] American [[horror film]] produced by [[Producers Releasing Corporation]] (PRC) and directed by [[Jean Yarborough]].<ref>[https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1129&dat=19410130&id=udZaAAAAIBAJ&sjid=dmkDAAAAIBAJ&pg=4705,6426631&hl=en "Horror Pictures on Barry Screen" (Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, January 30, 1941, p.24)]</ref><ref>[https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=2293&dat=19410202&id=QM4mAAAAIBAJ&sjid=IwIGAAAAIBAJ&pg=1037,2202726&hl=en "FILMS of the WEEK / QUEEN—"The Devil Bat"" (''The Sunday Morning Star'', Wilmington, Delaware, February 9, 1941, p.34)]</ref> The film stars [[Bela Lugosi]]<ref>[https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1955&dat=19410216&id=kfAxAAAAIBAJ&sjid=u-IFAAAAIBAJ&pg=2675,2956386&hl=en "RITZ—"Devil Bat", starring Bela Lugosi, and running Monday through Thursday, is an ingenious story about a vengeance-crazed genius who produces a deadly and terrifying species of killer-bat to wreak revenge on those he thinks oppress him" (''Reading Eagle'', February 16, 1941, p.16 / captioned photo of Lugosi holding a skull)]</ref> along with [[Suzanne Kaaren]], [[Guy Usher]], [[Yolande Mallott]] and the comic team of [[Dave O'Brien (actor)|Dave O'Brien]] and Donald Kerr as the protagonists. It was the first horror film from PRC.<ref name="tcm">[https://web.archive.org/web/20121104034353/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/5745/Devil-Bat/#overview ''The Devil Bat''] at TCM</ref>

==Plot==
Foreword:
{{blockquote|"All Heathville loved Dr. Paul Carruthers...the doctor found time to conduct certain private experiments — weird, terrifying experiments."}}

Dr. Paul Carruthers, a chemist and physician in the small town of Heathville, is offered a $5,000 bonus from his employers for his contributions to the company, a pittance compared to the million dollars in income the company earned from his work. His employers argue that he took a buyout early in the company's history instead of retaining his partnership stake. Embittered and insulted, he seeks revenge and develops a system in which ordinary [[bat]]s are enlarged to massive size, training them to be drawn to a new, pungent [[aftershave]] he is testing. He then distributes the lotion to his enemies as a "test" product.<ref>{{cite book |last =Rovin |first =Jeff |title =The Encyclopedia of Supervillains |publisher =Facts on File |date =1987 |location =New York |isbn = 0-8160-1356-X |page=101}}</ref>

Once they have applied the lotion, the chemist then releases his Devil Bats in the night, targeting the families of his employer's owners. The bats succeed in attacking and killing the owner's two sons and his partner. A hot shot reporter from the ''Chicago Register'', Johnny Layton, gets assigned by his editor to cover and help solve the murders. He and his bumbling photographer, "One-Shot" McGuire, begin to unwind the mystery.

In the climactic closing scene, Layton dumps a sample of the aftershave on Carruthers, leading the bat to attack and kill its own master. Mary, the last surviving member of her family, runs into Johnny's arms.

==Production==
PRC was a young studio when it planned to enter the horror film genre, which had been neglected by the major studios during 1937 and 1938.<ref name="Winnert">{{cite web |last1=Winnert |first1=Derek |title=The Devil Bat *** (1940, Bela Lugosi, Suzanne Kaaren, Dave O’Brien, Guy Usher) – Classic Movie Review 3,735 |url=https://www.derekwinnert.com/the-devil-bat-1941-bela-lugosi-suzanne-kaaren-dave-obrien-guy-usher-classic-movie-review-3735/ |website=DerekWinnert.com |access-date=6 May 2026}}</ref> Lugosi was beginning a comeback when he signed a contract on October 19, 1940, with PRC's Sigmund Neufeld to star in the [[Poverty Row]] studio's first horror film.<ref name="Weaver p. 15">Weaver (1993). p. 15.</ref>

The shooting of the film began a little more than one week later.<ref>Weaver (1993). p. 17.</ref> PRC was known for shooting its films quickly and cheaply, but for endowing them with a plentiful amount of horror,<ref>Weaver, Tom (1993). "Introduction" in ''Poverty Row Horrors! Monogram, PRC and Republic Horror Films of the Forties''. Jefferson, North Carolina: McFarland & Co. {{ISBN|0-89950-756-5}}. p. xiii-xiv.</ref> and ''The Devil Bat'' established this ''modus operandi''.<ref name="Weaver p. 15"/>

==Release==
Following a premiere on November 11, 1940, ''The Devil Bat'' was released theatrically on December 13, receiving wider distribution in to 1941.<ref name="Weaver p. 14" /> The film was re-released in 1945 on a double bill with ''[[Man Made Monster]]'', described by the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' as "two of the scariest features on the market."<ref>{{Cite news|title=Two Chillers Screened|author=G K.|date=Dec 15, 1945|work=Los Angeles Times|page=A5}}</ref>

Derek Winnert remarks that upon falling into [[public domain]], truncated and "poorly edited" video and DVD versions of ''The Devil Bat'' proliferated.<ref name=Winnert /> In 1990, the film was restored from original 35mm elements by Bob Furmanek and released on laserdisc by Lumivision. In 2008, Furmanek supplied his original elements to [[Legend Films]], which performed a new restoration and also created a computer-[[Film colorization|colorized]] version. Both the restored black-and-white and colorized versions were subsequently released on DVD.<ref>Footnote, [[DVD Talk]] [https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s2720lege.html review]</ref>

In 2013, the film was released on [[Blu-ray]] by [[Kino Lorber]] under its Kino Classics label,<ref>Footnote, [https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s4280bat.html DVD Savant review of August 27, 2013]</ref> with commentary by Richard Harland Smith.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Brunsting |first1=Joshua |title=Joshua Reviews Jean Yarbrough’s ''The Devil Bat'' [Blu-ray Review] |url=https://criterioncast.com/reviews/blu-ray-reviews/joshua-reviews-jean-yarbroughs-the-devil-bat-blu-ray-review |website=CriterionCast.com |access-date=5 May 2026}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
One of PRC's first horror movies, ''The Devil Bat'' was the studio's greatest horror  box-office success. <ref name="Schwartz">{{cite web |last1=Schwartz |first1=Dennis |title=DEVIL BAT, THE |url=https://dennisschwartzreviews.com/devilbat/ |website=DennisSchwartzReviews.com |access-date=5 May 2026}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On the [[review aggregator]] web site ''[[Rotten Tomatoes]]'', the film has a score of 60% based on ten reviews.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Devil Bat |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_devil_bat |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=5 May 2026}}</ref>

In the book ''Poverty Row Horrors!'' (1993), Tom Weaver calls ''The Devil Bat'' as one of Lugosi's best films for the studio.<ref>Weaver (1993). p. 19.</ref>

==Remake==
The studio remade the film, differing in almost every respect, as ''[[The Flying Serpent]]'' in 1946, starring [[George Zucco]].<ref name="Schwartz" />

==Sequels==
PRC's 1946 film ''[[Devil Bat's Daughter]]'' starred [[Rosemary LaPlanche]] as Paul Carruthers's daughter. Neither Lugosi nor any other actors reprise their roles; Carruthers is an [[unseen character]] in the latter film. In contrast to the horror elements of the original, ''Devil Bat's Daughter'' was mainly a [[psychological thriller]].

In 2015 [[Independent film|Indie]] filmmaker Ted Moehring directed the sequel ''[[Revenge of the Devil Bat]]'',<ref>[https://dreadcentral.com/news/118350/revenge-of-the-devil-bat-winging-its-way-to-fans/ Revenge of the Devil Bat Winging its Way to Fans]</ref> which stars [[Lynn Lowry]], [[Ruby Larocca]] and veteran actors [[Gary Kent]], John Link, [[Dick Dyszel]], [[George Stover]], and [[Conrad Brooks]].<ref>[https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1586906037/revenge-of-the-devil-bat?lang=de Revenge of the Devil Bat, Sequel to the 1940 horror movie The Devil Bat. ]</ref>{{Better source needed|date=July 2024}}
