'''''The Town''''' is a 2010 American [[Crime film|crime]] [[thriller film]] co-written and directed by [[Ben Affleck]], adapted from [[Chuck Hogan]]'s 2004 novel ''Prince of Thieves''.<ref name="FilmSchoolRejects">{{cite web|url=https://www.filmschoolrejects.com/news/blake-lively-goes-to-town-for-ben-affleck-neilm.php|title=Blake Lively Goes to 'Town' for Ben Affleck|last=Miller|first=Neil|date=August 27, 2006|work=Film School Rejects|access-date=September 5, 2006|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090904163107/http://www.filmschoolrejects.com/news/blake-lively-goes-to-town-for-ben-affleck-neilm.php|archive-date=September 4, 2009|df=mdy-all}}</ref><ref name=Town>{{cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/blake-lively-town-wb-legendary-88146|first=Borys|last=Kit|title=Blake Lively going to 'Town' for WB, Legendary|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=August 26, 2009|access-date=September 16, 2009|archive-date=July 5, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180705121459/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/blake-lively-town-wb-legendary-88146|url-status=live}}</ref> The film stars Affleck, [[Rebecca Hall]], [[Jon Hamm]], [[Jeremy Renner]], [[Blake Lively]], [[Titus Welliver]], [[Pete Postlethwaite]], [[Chris Cooper]] and [[Slaine (rapper)|Slaine]].

The film features a [[Boston]] [[Bank robbery|bank robber]] who begins to develop romantic feelings for a victim of one of his previous robberies but hides his identity from her. Meanwhile, he and his crew set out to get one final score by robbing [[Fenway Park]].

''The Town'' premiered at the [[Venice Film Festival]] on September 8, 2010, and was released in the United States by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] on September 17, 2010. Based on actual events, the film received positive reviews from critics for its direction, screenplay, editing, and the performances of the cast (particularly Renner) and grossed $154 million worldwide. It received various accolades, including the [[National Board of Review Award for Best Cast]], as well as Best Supporting Actor nominations at the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Academy]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor - Motion Picture|Golden Globe]], [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role|SAG]] (Renner), and [[BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role|BAFTA Awards]] (Postlethwaite, posthumously). The film was also honoured as one of the [[National Board of Review Awards 2010#Top 10 Films|top ten films of 2010]] by the [[National Board of Review]].<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-07-26|title=10 Actors Who Deserved an Oscar Nomination|url=https://www.animatedtimes.com/10-actors-who-deserved-an-oscar-nomination/|access-date=2021-08-23|website=Animated Times|language=en-US|archive-date=September 24, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924015505/https://animatedtimes.com/10-actors-who-deserved-an-oscar-nomination/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=BBC>{{Cite news|date=2011-01-18|title=Baftas 2011: surprises and sure things|language=en-GB|work=BBC News|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-12214595|access-date=2021-08-23|archive-date=August 23, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210823094515/https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-12214595|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
 <!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Four lifelong friends from [[Charlestown, Boston]]—mild-mannered Doug MacRay, volatile James "Jem" Coughlin, Albert "Gloansy" MacGloan, and Desmond "Dez" Elden—rob a bank wearing latex skeleton masks. Against Doug's wishes, Jem takes bank manager Claire Keesey hostage, but later releases her unharmed.

After discovering Claire lives in their neighborhood, Doug surveils her to find out what she has told authorities, against the wishes of Jem, who believes they should simply kill her.

Doug introduces himself to Claire at a local laundromat. Claire does not recognize Doug's voice from the bank heist. Doug tells Claire of his missing mother, who probably lives with his aunt in [[Tangerine, Florida]], and of being drafted as a professional [[ice hockey]] player. She tells him of the [[Fighting Irish]] leprechaun tattoo on the neck of one of the bank robbers (Jem); knowing that Jem will kill her if she reports this, Doug dissuades her from telling the FBI about the tattoo.

FBI [[Special Agent]] Adam Frawley suspects the bank heist crew has ties to local [[Irish Mob|Irish mobster]] Fergus "Fergie" Colm, who fronts as a [[Floristry|florist]]. During a visit to his father, Stephen, who is serving five consecutive life sentences in prison, Doug shares his plan to leave Boston for Florida.

The crew's next job is an [[armored car (valuables)|armored car]] robbery in the [[North End, Boston|North End]], for which they don latex [[nun]] masks; the job goes awry when a guard is shot, and machine gun fire and a police chase ensue. The next day, Frawley interrogates Doug and his associates but is forced to release them for lack of evidence and failure to extract a confession.

Doug asks Claire to go to Florida, and she accepts. She quits her job, unaware that Frawley has [[wiretap]]ped her phone. Frawley shares Doug's criminal file with Claire, and threatens to prosecute her as an accomplice. Shocked and distraught, she breaks up with Doug.

Doug tries to back out of an upcoming $3.5 million heist at [[Fenway Park]], angering Jem and Fergie; the latter threatens to kill Claire if Doug does not partake in the robbery and reveals that he controlled Stephen by turning Doug's mother into an addict, which ultimately led to her [[suicide]].

Doug reluctantly agrees to do the job, but swears he will kill Fergie if anything happens to Claire. At Fenway Park, Doug and Jem enter disguised as [[Boston Police Department|Boston police]] officers, with help from an inside accomplice, steal $3,500,000 in gate cash, and prepare to escape in an ambulance disguised as [[paramedic]]s.

Unbeknownst to the crew, Frawley had used Doug's ex-girlfriend (and Jem's sister) Krista's drug addiction to turn her into an informant. The FBI, tipped off by Krista, surround the perimeter alongside police and [[Massachusetts State Police|state troopers]]. Caught in a [[shootout]] with [[FBI Special Weapons and Tactics Teams|FBI SWAT]] agents, Dez and Gloansy are killed.
Frawley later spots Jem, and Jem is wounded in their exchange of gunfire. Determined not go back to prison, he commits [[suicide by cop]]. Doug witnesses his death before escaping in a police cruiser.

Knowing that Claire is in danger, Doug kills Fergie and his bodyguard Rusty. After contacting Claire, Doug spots the FBI with her, and she manages to warn him away. Doug escapes by donning an [[MBTA]] uniform from his uncle, and later boards a train to Florida. Frawley deduces that Claire tipped Doug off, but has insufficient grounds to arrest her.

Later, while Claire is gardening, she finds a buried bag containing money, a tangerine, and a note from Doug suggesting she can make better use of it and that they may meet again one day. Claire donates the money, in memory of Doug's mother, to refurbish the ice hockey arena where Doug played.

From the deck of a small house, Doug looks out over the water, forlorn, but seemingly safe in Florida.

===Pre-production===
In 2003, [[Paramount Pictures]] had optioned the rights to [[Chuck Hogan]]'s novel ''Prince of Thieves'' before it was even published, and [[Dick Wolf]] signed on to produce before the project fell through.<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr/film/brief_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=2035156|title= Par moving to Hogan's 'Town'|date= November 21, 2003|access-date= July 13, 2022|work= The Hollywood Reporter|archiveurl= https://web.archive.org/web/20040101180140/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr/film/brief_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=2035156|archivedate= January 1, 2004}}</ref><ref name="Ringer">{{Cite web |last=Siegel |first=Alan |date=2020-09-16 |title=The Oral History of 'The Town' |url=https://www.theringer.com/movies/2020/9/16/21438005/the-town-oral-history-10-year-anniversary |access-date=2022-10-27 |website=The Ringer |language=en |archive-date=October 27, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221027025239/https://www.theringer.com/movies/2020/9/16/21438005/the-town-oral-history-10-year-anniversary |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2006, director [[Adrian Lyne]] brought the novel to producer [[Graham King]]. King in turn showed it to [[Warner Bros. Entertainment|Warner Bros.]] studio, who agreed to make an adaptation directed by Lyne and written by [[Sheldon Turner]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr/film/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1002952015 |title=Lyne recruited to mastermind WB's 'Thieves' |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=2013-07-26 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060814145238/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr/film/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1002952015 |archive-date=August 14, 2006 |date=August 14, 2006}}</ref> Lyne's vision for the project was a 3–3.5 hour [[Martin Scorsese]]-styled film with a budget of $90 million, which led to creative differences with the studio and the eventual departure of Lyne from the project.<ref name="Ringer" /><ref name=thr>{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/anatomy-contender-making-town-45732 |title=Anatomy of a Contender: Making of 'The Town' |work=The Hollywood Reporter |date=November 17, 2010 |url-status=live }}</ref> By 2008, ''The Town'' was decided as the title and Ben Affleck, fresh off his directorial debut in ''[[Gone Baby Gone]]'', was brought in by Warner Bros. to serve as the film's star, director and co-writer.<ref name="Ringer" /><ref>{{cite news |last1=Fleming |first1=Michael |title=Ben Affleck moves to WB's 'Town' |url=https://variety.com/2008/film/news/ben-affleck-moves-to-wb-s-town-1117992226/ |access-date=19 November 2020 |work=Variety |date=15 September 2008 |archive-date=March 4, 2016 personally researched and understood", and invited high school classmate Aaron Stockard to work with him on the script. While Affleck had grown up in nearby [[Cambridge, Massachusetts]], he barely knew the harsh inner-city environment of Charlestown. Affleck and Stockard conducted many interviews with the Charlestown community, as well as the FBI Violent Crimes Task Force in Boston.<ref name=thr/> Later the film's actors also researched within the community to make for more believable characters and performances. Charlestown locals also joined the cast, mostly as extras.<ref>{{cite AV media|chapter=The Real People of the Town |title=The Town |type=DVD |publisher=Warner Bros. |date=2010}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film took #1 at the box office during its opening weekend, taking in $23.8 million.<ref>{{cite news |date=September 20, 2010 |title='The Town' takes box office win with $23.8M |agency=Associated Press |url=https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/sdut-the-town-takes-box-office-win-with-238m-2010sep20-story.html |access-date=December 7, 2023 |archive-date=December 7, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231207180836/https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/sdut-the-town-takes-box-office-win-with-238m-2010sep20-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> ''The Town'' grossed $92.1 million in the United States and Canada with an additional $61.8 million in other territories for a total of $154 million worldwide on a production budget of $37 million.<ref name="budget">{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/company-town-blog/story/2010-09-16/movie-projector-easy-a-expected-to-lead-the-town-devil-alpha-and-omega|title=Movie projector: 'Easy A' expected to lead 'The Town,' 'Devil,' 'Alpha and Omega'|last=Fritz|first=Ben|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=September 16, 2010|access-date=September 16, 2010|archive-date=December 8, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221208102615/https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/company-town-blog/story/2010-09-16/movie-projector-easy-a-expected-to-lead-the-town-devil-alpha-and-omega|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="BOM"/>

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''The Town'' has an approval rating of 92% based on 238 reviews, with an average rating of 7.70/10. The site's critical consensus reads, "Tense, smartly written, and wonderfully cast, ''The Town'' proves that Ben Affleck has rediscovered his [[Muses|muse]]—and that he's a director to be reckoned with."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_town|title=The Town (2010)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=March 28, 2024|archive-date=September 24, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924021308/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_town|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] gives the film a weighted average score of 74 out of 100, based on 42 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-town|title=The Town reviews|work=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=August 24, 2018|archive-date=February 16, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210216201529/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-town|url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://m.cinemascore.com |archive-url=https://archive.today/20150405021357/http://m.cinemascore.com/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=2015-04-05 |title=CinemaScore |work=cinemascore.com }}</ref>

==Factual accuracy==
A voice in the trailer of the film says: "There are over 300 bank robberies in Boston every year. Most of these professionals live in a 1-square-mile neighborhood called Charlestown." In fact, there were 23 reported bank robberies in the entire state of Massachusetts in the first quarter of 2010, compared with 49 in Illinois and 136 in California, according to the FBI.<ref name="Boston.com">{{cite web |last=Baker |first=Billy |date=September 18, 2010 |title=Robbed of its new image? Charlestown hopes not Affleck's new film is the talk of the Townies |url=https://mafilm.org/2010/09/18/robbed-of-its-new-image-charlestown-hopes-not/ |access-date=September 18, 2010 |website=The Boston Globe |archive-date=June 9, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110609193052/http://www.mafilm.org/2010/09/18/robbed-of-its-new-image-charlestown-hopes-not/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

The film drew criticism from residents of the Charlestown, Boston neighborhood due to the film's promotion as being about "the bank robbery capital of America."
<ref name="csm-bank-robbery-capital">{{cite web |url=https://news.yahoo.com/town-charlestown-really-americas-bank-robbery-capital.html |title='The Town': Is Charlestown really America's 'bank robbery capital'? |last=Manell |first=Patricia |date=17 September 2010 |website=news.yahoo.com |publisher=Yahoo News-Christian Science Monitor |access-date=18 December 2023 |quote= |archive-date=December 18, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231218041307/https://news.yahoo.com/town-charlestown-really-americas-bank-robbery-capital.html |url-status=live}}</ref> While specific bank robbery crime statistics were not available for the area, Greg Comcowich, Boston Federal Bureau of Investigation spokesman, said it was a "pretty good conclusion" that the characterization was inaccurate.<ref name="csm-bank-robbery-capital" />

The film ends with a written disclaimer: "Charlestown's reputation as a breeding ground for armed robbers is authentic. However, this film all but ignores the great majority of the residents of Charlestown, past and present, who are the same good and true people found most anywhere,"<ref>{{Cite web |last=Pride |first=Ray |date=2010-09-15 |title=Review: The Town |url=https://www.newcityfilm.com/2010/09/15/review-the-town/ |access-date=2023-02-05 |website=New City Film |language=en-US |archive-date=February 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230205230253/https://www.newcityfilm.com/2010/09/15/review-the-town/ |url-status=live}}</ref> to whom the film is dedicated.

According to a September 2010 article in ''[[The Boston Globe]]'', Charlestown was once known as an area where bank robbers were concentrated, but has not been since the mid-1990s, and the subject has been a sore point for "Townies". Now much of the neighborhood has been [[Gentrification|gentrified]]. The paper reported there is some sense of rivalry between Townies, people who lived in the historically Irish-Catholic neighborhood for decades, and "Toonies", largely white-collar workers who arrived with gentrification, but most of that has died down.<ref name="Boston.com" /> The film makes reference to the definition of "Toonies" during one of Doug and Claire's dates.

In the early 1990s, an increase in the number of bank and armored car robberies by Townies focused attention on Charlestown. In one heist in [[Hudson, New Hampshire]], two guards were killed, which is alluded to in the film; during a scene where Agent Frawley is briefing his task force, he mentions that Doug's father is serving life for a notorious robbery in [[Nashua, New Hampshire|Nashua]]. According to Frawley, the elder MacRay hijacked a "bread truck" (armored car) up to New Hampshire, and when one of the guards saw his face, he executed both of them with their own weapons. Frawley notes that this incident led to the passing of regulations prohibiting the driver from leaving the cab even if his partner is being held at gunpoint. Charles Hogan got the idea for his novel, on which the film is based, in 1995. "It was just so remarkable that this one very small community was the focus for bank robbers," he said, but he was very aware that crime was only one part of the community, and he did not want to make all residents of the neighborhood look like criminals.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Woodman |first=Tenley |date=2010-09-16 |title=Author Hogan talks about his kind of 'Town' |work=Boston Herald |url=http://news.bostonherald.com/entertainment/books/view.bg?articleid=1281784 |url-status=dead |access-date=2010-09-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110613045042/http://news.bostonherald.com/entertainment/books/view.bg?articleid=1281784 |archive-date=2011-06-13}}</ref>
