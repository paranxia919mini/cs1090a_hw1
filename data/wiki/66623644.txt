'''''Summer of Soul (...or, When the Revolution Could Not Be Televised)''''' is a 2021 American [[independent film|independent]]<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.sunjournal.com/events-calendar/#!/details/Indie-Film-Series-Summer-of-Soul/9271246/2021-08-25T17|title=Events calendar Archives|website=Lewiston Sun Journal}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://frontporchne.com/indie-prof-the-rescue-and-summer-of-soul/|title=Indie Prof: "The Rescue" and "Summer of Soul"|first=Christie|last=Gosch|date=December 28, 2021|website=Front Porch}}</ref> [[documentary film]] about the 1969 [[Harlem Cultural Festival]], directed by [[Questlove|Ahmir "Questlove" Thompson]] in his directorial debut.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://mubi.com/en/us/films/summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised|title=Summer of Soul (2021) &#124; MUBI|via=mubi.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://greenpleasantland.com|title=This Green and Pleasant Land|author=Greene, Bryan|publisher=Poverty and Race Research Action Council|date=June 2017|access-date=2022-02-08|archive-date=2022-01-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220105055754/https://greenpleasantland.com/|url-status=live}}</ref> It had its world premiere at the [[2021 Sundance Film Festival]] on January 28, 2021, where it won the Grand Jury Prize and Audience Award in the documentary categories. It had a [[limited release|limited theatrical release]] in the U.S. by [[Searchlight Pictures]] on June 25, 2021, before expanding and being released for streaming on [[Hulu]] the next weekend.

The second half of the movie's title is taken from the 1970 poem and song "[[The Revolution Will Not Be Televised]]", written by [[Gil Scott-Heron]]. It offered a sharp and satirical critique of the media's lack of coverage of [[civil rights]] activism and the reality of change and revolution taking place in the streets and on campuses across America.

The film received acclaim from critics, with particular praise given to the restoration of the footage used. It won numerous awards, including [[Critics' Choice Documentary Award for Best Documentary Feature|Best Documentary Feature]] at the [[6th Critics' Choice Documentary Awards]], where it won in all six categories in which it was nominated, [[BAFTA Award for Best Documentary|Best Documentary]] at the [[75th British Academy Film Awards]], [[Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature|Best Documentary Feature]] at the [[94th Academy Awards]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=The 94th Academy Awards (2022) |url=https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2022 |access-date=December 15, 2022 |website=[[oscars.org]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?app=desktop&v=Ao6vrTcVP_Q|title='Summer of Soul' Wins Best Documentary Feature &#124; 94th Oscars|date=May 1, 2022|via=YouTube}}</ref> and [[Grammy Award for Best Music Film|Best Music Film]] at the [[64th Annual Grammy Awards]]. It has been considered [[List of films voted the best|one of the best films]] of the 21st century.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-movies-21st-century/summer-of-soul-2021/ | title=Hollywood Reporter Critics Pick the 50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far) | website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] | date=6 April 2023 }}</ref><ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/jul/18/summer-of-soul-review-questlove-documentary-harlem-cultural-festival-1969 | title=Summer of Soul review – the best concert film ever made? | newspaper=The Observer | date=18 July 2021 | last1=Kermode | first1=Mark }}</ref>

==Synopsis==
The film examines the 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival, which took place on six Sundays between June 29 and August 24 at [[Marcus Garvey Park|Mount Morris Park]] (now Marcus Garvey Park) in [[Harlem]], using professional footage of the festival that was filmed as it happened, [[Stock footage|stock news footage]], and modern-day interviews with attendees, musicians, and other commentators to provide historical background and social context. Despite its large attendance and performers such as [[Stevie Wonder]], [[Mahalia Jackson]], [[David Ruffin]], [[Max Roach]], [[Nina Simone]], [[The 5th Dimension]], [[The Staple Singers]], [[Gladys Knight & the Pips]], [[Mavis Staples]], [[Blinky (singer)|Blinky Williams]], [[Sly and the Family Stone]], and [[the Chambers Brothers]], the festival is much less well known in the 21st century than is [[Woodstock]] (which took place on the same weekend as one of the days of the Harlem Cultural Festival). The filmmakers investigate this, among other topics.

==Production==
[[File:HarlemFestivalTapeHeader1969.jpg|thumb|left|[[Clapperboard]] from original videotape recordings of the 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival|alt=Clapperboard for original video footage]]
At the request of festival organizer and host [[Tony Lawrence (singer)|Tony Lawrence]], television producer [[Hal Tulchin]] recorded about 40 hours of footage of the 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival on videotape,<ref name=":smithsonian">{{Cite web|last=Morgan|first=Richard|date=2007-02-01|title=The Story Behind the Harlem Cultural Festival Featured in 'Summer of from which were packaged as two one-hour TV specials that were broadcast in 1969, one on [[CBS]] in July, and one on [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] in September.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Gaunt|first=James|date=2021-12-21|title=Who Is Tony Lawrence?|url=https://medium.com/the-shadow-knows/who-is-tony-lawrence-a6a3ed32ee5e|url-status=live|access-date=2022-01-13|website=The then placed in a basement,<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-07-12|title=Exclusive: Questlove On Directorial Debut 'Summer of Soul (...Or, When the Revolution Could Not Be Televised)'|url=https://theknockturnal.com/exclusive-questlove-on-directorial-debut-summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised/|access-date=2021-07-15|website=The where they sat for the next 50 years.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Minsker|first=Evan|title=Questlove's Summer of Soul Documentary Wins Sundance 2021 Grand Jury Prize|url=https://pitchfork.com/news/questloves-summer-of-soul-documentary-wins-sundance-2021-grand-jury-prize/|access-date=2021-02-05|website=Pitchfork|date=2 February 2021|language=en-us|archive-date=2021-02-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210204125547/https://pitchfork.com/news/questloves-summer-of-soul-documentary-wins-sundance-2021-grand-jury-prize/|url-status=live}}</ref> Tulchin attempted to interest broadcasters in the recordings for several years, but had little success, though some of the footage of Nina Simone was eventually used in documentaries about her.<ref name="nyt">{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2017/09/14/arts/television/hal-tulchin-90-dies-documented-a-little-seen-black-woodstock.html  |author=Richard Sandomir|title=Hal Tulchin, Who 2022 |access-date= 5 January 2022}}</ref>

In 2004, [[Joe Lauro]], a film archivist at the [[Historic Films Archive]], discovered the existence of the footage and contacted Hal Tulchin, hoping to work on a film about the festival. He digitized and cataloged the footage<ref name=":latimesbattaglio">{{Cite web|last=Battaglio|first=Stephen|date=2021-08-19|title=Meet the archivist who saved the historic footage that became 'Summer of Soul'|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2021-08-19/meet-the-archivist-who-rescued-the-concert-footage-that-became-the-summer-of-soul|url-status=live|access-date=2021-09-26|website=Los Angeles Times|language=en-US|archive-date=2021-09-19|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210919023012/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2021-08-19/meet-the-archivist-who-rescued-the-concert-footage-that-became-the-summer-of-soul}}</ref> and, in 2006, entered into a deal with [[Robert Gordon (writer and filmmaker)|Robert Gordon]] and [[Morgan Neville]] to make the film,<ref name=":smithsonian" /> but the project never saw the light of day, as Tulchin discontinued his agreement with Lauro.<ref name=":latimesbattaglio" /> Producer Robert Fyvolent, who had originally sought to work with Lauro, then acquired film and television rights to the footage from Tulchin.<ref name="nyt"/><ref>{{Cite web|last1=Tangcay|first1=Jazz|last2=Jackson|first2=Angelique|date=2021-01-28|title=How Questlove's 'Summer of Soul' Tracked Down Rare Footage of the Landmark Harlem Cultural Festival|url=https://variety.com/2021/film/news/summer-of-soul-questlove-harlem-cultural-festival-1234892950/|access-date=2021-07-15|website=Variety|language=en-US|archive-date=2021-05-10|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210510051104/https://variety.com/2021/film/news/summer-of-soul-questlove-harlem-cultural-festival-1234892950/|url-status=live}}</ref> Fyvolent began collaborating with producer David Dinerstein in 2016, and together they engaged [[RadicalMedia]] and editor Josh Pearson, and added a third producer, [[Joseph Patel]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/movies-for-grownups/info-2021/summer-of-soul-documentary.html|title='Summer of Soul' Looks Back on Harlem Cultural Festival|website=AARP}}</ref>

Director Ahmir Thompson has expressed surprise that the footage sat for so long, and that he had never heard of the festival before the producers approached him about making the film.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Tannenbaum|first=Rob|date=2021-06-29|title=Questlove celebrates 'Black joy' with 'Summer of Soul' doc|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2021-06-29/questlove-summer-of-soul-documentary-black-joy|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210725161759/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2021-06-29/questlove-summer-of-soul-documentary-black-joy|archive-date=2021-07-25|access-date=2021-09-26|website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref> Discussing its obscurity, he said: "What would have happened if this was allowed a seat at the table? How much of a difference would that have made in my life? That was the moment that extinguished any doubt I had that I could do this."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Lindahl|first=Chris|date=2021-02-02|title=Questlove on Building 'Summer of Soul' Around Awe-Inspiring Musical Moments|url=https://www.indiewire.com/video/questlove-summer-of-soul-musical-performances-1234613828/|access-date=2021-02-05|website=IndieWire|language=en|archive-date=2021-02-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210202193014/https://www.indiewire.com/video/questlove-summer-of-soul-musical-performances-1234613828/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Release==
The film premiered on January 28, 2021, at the Sundance Film Festival, where it won the Grand Jury Prize and Audience Award in the US Documentary Competition.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Goodman|first=Stephanie|date=2021-02-03|title=Questlove's 'Summer of Soul' Wins at Sundance|language=en-US|work=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/02/02/movies/sundance-winners-questlove.html|access-date=2021-02-05|issn=0362-4331|archive-date=2021-02-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210205103524/https://www.nytimes.com/2021/02/02/movies/sundance-winners-questlove.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It was acquired by [[Searchlight Pictures]] and [[Hulu]], and was released in the United States at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Los Angeles]] and the [[AMC Theatres|AMC]] [[Magic Johnson Theatres|Magic Johnson]] Harlem 9 multiplex in [[New York City]] on June 25, 2021, before expanding nationwide and being released for streaming on Hulu the next weekend.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Jackson|first=Angelique|date=2021-02-05|title=Searchlight Pictures and Hulu Pick Up Questlove's Sundance Winner 'Summer of Soul'|url=https://variety.com/2021/film/festivals/ahmir-questlove-thompson-summer-of-soul-documentary-searchlight-pictures-hulu-sundance-1234901465/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210205040552/https://variety.com/2021/film/festivals/ahmir-questlove-thompson-summer-of-soul-documentary-searchlight-pictures-hulu-sundance-1234901465/|archive-date=2021-02-05|access-date=2021-02-05|website=Variety|language=en-US}}</ref> The film was distributed internationally in theaters and on [[Disney+ Hotstar]] on July 30, 2021, and on [[Disney+]] and [[Star+]] on November 19, 2021,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Patten |first1=Dominic |title=Disney's Searchlight & Hulu Snag 'Summer Of Soul' Doc; ==Reception==
===Box office===
''Summer of Soul'' grossed $2.3 million in the U.S. and Canada, and $1.4 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $3.7 million.<ref name=BOM/>

In the film's first weekend in wide release, it grossed $650,000 from 752 theaters (for a per-venue average of $865).<ref name=opening>{{cite web |url= https://deadline.com/2021/07/questlove-summer-of-soul-specialty-box-office-1234786111/ |title= Questlove's 'Summer of Soul' Sees $650K Three-Day Weekend Opener At Specialty Box Office |date= July 4, 2021 |first= Jill |last= Goldsmith |website= [[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date= July 4, 2021 |archive-date= July 4, 2021 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210704191412/https://deadline.com/2021/07/questlove-summer-of-soul-specialty-box-office-1234786111/ |url-status= live }}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 99% (based on 222 reviews), with an average rating of 9.1/10; the website's critics consensus reads: "Deftly interweaving incredible live footage with a series of revealing interviews, ''Summer of Soul'' captures the spirit and context of a watershed moment while tying it firmly to the present."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/summer_of_soul |title=Summer of Soul (...Or, When the Revolution Could Not Be Televised) (2021) |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]] |access-date=July 7, 2024 }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average score]] of 96 out of 100 (based on 38 critics), indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web|title= Summer of Soul (...Or, When the Revolution Could Not Be Televised) Reviews|url= https://www.metacritic.com/movie/summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised|website= [[Metacritic]]|access-date= August 16, 2021|archive-date= July 1, 2021|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210701071144/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised|url-status= live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a rare "A+" average grade.<ref name=opening/>

''[[Rolling Stone]]'' praised the film as "the Perfect Movie to Kick Off Sundance 2021" and said it was "an incredible, vital act of restoration—and reclamation".<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Fear|first=David|date=2021-01-29|title='Summer of Soul' Is the Perfect Movie to Kick Off Sundance 2021|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-features/summer-of-soul-questlove-doc-1120746/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210205021535/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-features/summer-of-soul-questlove-doc-1120746/|archive-date=2021-02-05|access-date=2021-02-05|magazine=Rolling Stone|language=en-US}}</ref> ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave it five stars, writing that there is "a moment so striking and rich with power at the center of Ahmir 'Questlove' Thompson's ''Summer of Soul (…Or, When The Revolution Could Not Be Televised)'' that, while watching it, I actually forgot to breathe."<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-01-29|title=Summer of Soul review – thrilling documentary reveals a forgotten festival|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/jan/29/summer-of-soul-review-sundance-harlem-cultural-festival|access-date=2021-02-05|website=The Guardian|language=en|archive-date=2021-02-05|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210205001532/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/jan/29/summer-of-soul-review-sundance-harlem-cultural-festival|url-status=live}}</ref>

British critic [[Mark Kermode]] called the film "the best music documentary I've ever seen" in his review for [[Kermode and Mayo's Film Review]] on [[BBC Radio 5 Live]].<ref name="Kermode">{{cite video |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/m000xzml |access-date=August 23, 2021 |date=July 16, 2021 |first=Mark |last=Kermode |author-link=Mark Kermode |title=Vanessa Kirby, The Croods 2: A New Age, The Forever Purge and Summer of Soul |publisher=BBC |time=1:22:50}}</ref>

Filmmaker [[John Waters]] and former U.S. President [[Barack Obama]] cited it as among their favorite films of 2021.<ref>{{cite web|last=Segarra |first=Edward |date=December 17, 2021 |title=Barack Obama shares his favorite music, movies and books from being 'cooped up' in 2021 |work=USA Today |url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/books/2021/12/15/barack-obama-shares-his-favorite-books-list-2021/8911743002/ |access-date=May 1, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Gunts |first=Ed |date=December 1, 2021 |title=Filmmaker John Waters names his “Best Films of 2021" |work=Baltimore Fishbowl |url=https://baltimorefishbowl.com/stories/filmmaker-john-waters-names-his-best-films-of-2021// |access-date=July 7, 2025}}</ref>

In 2024, ''[[Looper (website)|Looper]]'' ranked it number 3 on its list of the "50 Best PG-13 Movies of All Time," writing "The 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival was a milestone event that featured countless Black artists performing music from all genres, but for decades, it faded into obscurity. Fortunately, director Ahmir "Questlove" Thompson's documentary, ''Summer of Soul'', reaffirms the existence, presence, and joy of this seemingly forgotten moment with restored archival footage of the performances that comprised this event."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.looper.com/806086/best-pg-13-movies-of-all-time-ranked/ | title=50 Best PG-13 Movies Of All Time Ranked | website=Looper | date=October 14, 2024 }}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{Main|Summer of Soul (soundtrack)}}
On January 21, 2022, [[Legacy Recordings|Legacy Records]] released an official [[soundtrack album]].<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-12-09|title='Summer of Soul (…Or, When The Revolution Could Not Be Televised)' Soundtrack To Be Released January 21, 2022|url=https://www.legacyrecordings.com/2021/12/09/summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised-soundtrack-to-be-released-january-28-2022/|access-date=2022-01-30|website=Legacy Recordings|language=en-US|archive-date=2022-01-30|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220130114800/https://www.legacyrecordings.com/2021/12/09/summer-of-soul-or-when-the-revolution-could-not-be-televised-soundtrack-to-be-released-january-28-2022/|url-status=live}}</ref> In an interview, Thompson said he considered including songs not in the film, but decided to stick with the music that had already been cleared for release.<ref>{{Citation|title=NPR Music Listening Party: 'Summer of Soul' Soundtrack| date=28 January 2022 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShEySuav0mI|language=en|access-date=2022-01-30|archive-date=2022-01-29|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220129150329/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShEySuav0mI|url-status=live}}</ref> The digital version of the soundtrack contains 17 songs, while the physical version contains 16, as it does not feature [[Abbey Lincoln]]'s and [[Max Roach]]'s performance of "Africa".

==Home media==
On February 8, 2022, [[20th Century Studios]] released the film on Standard Definition DVD. Bonus features include audio commentary by Thompson, who muses about re-editing a longer version that would include footage he had to cut for time, and two behind-the-scenes featurettes ("Soul Searching" and "Harlem: Then & Now").<ref>{{Cite web|last=Major|first=Michael|title=SUMMER OF SOUL Sets DVD & Digital Release|url=https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/SUMMER-OF-SOUL-Sets-DVD-Digital-Release-20220113|access-date=2022-01-31|website=Broadway World|language=en|archive-date=2022-01-31|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220131224453/https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/SUMMER-OF-SOUL-Sets-DVD-Digital-Release-20220113|url-status=live}}</ref>
