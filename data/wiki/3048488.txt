'''''The Honeymoon Killers''''' is a 1970 American [[crime film]] written and directed by [[Leonard Kastle]], and starring [[Shirley Stoler]] and [[Tony Lo Bianco]]. Its plot follows an overweight nurse who is seduced by a handsome con man, with whom she embarks on a murder spree of single women. The film was inspired by the true story of [[Raymond Fernandez and Martha Beck]], the notorious "lonely hearts killers" of the 1940s.

Filmed primarily in [[Pittsfield, Massachusetts]], production of ''The Honeymoon Killers'' began with [[Martin Scorsese]] as its appointed director. However, after Scorsese was fired early into the shoot, he was replaced by Donald Volkman, a maker of industrial films, who lasted only two weeks<ref name="nytimes.com">{{cite news | url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/10/20/movies/behind-the-filming-of-the-honeymoon-killers.html | title=Behind the Filming of 'The Honeymoon Killers' | work=The New York Times | date=20 October 1992 | last1=Grimes | first1=William }}</ref> before Kastle, who had helped develop the film, took over directing. The film's score comprises the first movement of the 6th Symphony and a section of the 5th Symphony of [[Gustav Mahler]]. Released in early 1970, the film was met with critical praise for its performances as well as its [[Realism (arts)|realism]].

''The Honeymoon Killers'' went on to achieve cult status as well as critical recognition, and was named by [[François Truffaut]] as his "favorite American film." A digital restoration of the film was released on DVD by [[The Criterion Collection]] in 2003, and again in 2015 with a new digital transfer.

==Plot==
Martha Beck is a sullen, overweight nursing administrator living in [[Mobile, Alabama]], with her elderly mother. Martha's friend Bunny surreptitiously submits Martha's name to a "lonely hearts" club, which results in a letter from Raymond Fernandez of [[New York City]]. Overcoming her initial reluctance, Martha corresponds with Ray and becomes attracted to him. He visits Martha in Alabama and seduces her. Thereafter, having secured a loan from her, Ray sends Martha a [[Dear Jane letter]], and Martha enlists Bunny's aid to call him with the (false) news that she has attempted suicide.

Ray allows Martha to visit him in New York, where he reveals he is a [[confidence trick|con man]] who makes his living by seducing and then swindling lonely women. Martha is unswayed by this revelation. At Ray's directive, and so she can live with him, Martha puts her mother in a nursing home. Martha's embittered mother disowns her for abandoning her. Martha insists on accompanying Ray at his "work." Woman after woman accepts the attentions of this suitor who goes courting while always within sight of his "sister". Ray promises Martha he will never sleep with any of the other women but complicates his promise by marrying pregnant Myrtle Young. After Young aggressively attempts to bed the bridegroom, Martha gives her a large dose of pills, and Ray puts the drugged woman on a bus. Her death thereafter escapes immediate suspicion.

[[File:Mary Jane Higby in The Honeymoon Killers.jpg|thumb|left|upright=1|Janet Fay (Mary Jay Higby) just before her murder]]
The swindlers move on to their next target, and after catching Ray becoming intimate with the woman, Martha attempts to drown herself. To placate her, Ray rents a house in [[Valley Stream]], a suburb of New York City. He becomes engaged to the elderly Janet Fay of [[Albany, New York|Albany]], and takes her to the house he shares with Martha. Janet gives Ray checks for $10,000 but then becomes suspicious of the two. When Janet tries to contact her family, Martha bludgeons her with a hammer before she and Ray strangle her. They bury her body beneath their cellar floor in her trunk, tossing into the grave the two framed depictions of Jesus that, Martha notes sarcastically, she'd told them she took everywhere she went.

Next, they spend several weeks living in [[Michigan]] with the widowed Delphine Downing and her young daughter. Delphine, younger and prettier than most of Ray's conquests, confides in Martha, hoping that she will help her persuade Ray to marry her as soon as possible because she is pregnant with Ray's child. Martha is in the midst of drugging and smothering Delphine when the woman's daughter enters the room with Ray. He shoots Delphine in the head, and Martha drowns the daughter in the cellar. Ray tells Martha that he has to move on to one more woman, this one in [[New Orleans]], and then he will marry Martha; he repeats his promise never to have sex with any of his marks. Realizing that Ray will never stop lying to her, Martha calls the police and waits calmly for them to arrive.

The [[epilogue]] takes place four months later, with Martha and Ray in jail. As she leaves the cellblock for the first day of their trial, Martha receives a letter from Ray in which he tells her that, despite everything, she is the only woman he ever loved. On-screen titles state that Martha and Raymond were electrocuted at [[Sing Sing]] on March 8, 1951.{{efn|Although the real-life Beck and Fernandez were arrested originally in Michigan and charged with the murders of the Downings, those prosecutions were suspended and they were extradited to New York to be tried for the murder of Janet Fay, because New York, unlike Michigan, had the death penalty.  It was for the murder of Fay that they were convicted and executed.}}

==Production==
===Development===
The film was the first for producer [[Warren Steibel]] (known as the producer of television's ''[[Firing Line (TV series)|Firing Line]]''), writer/director [[Leonard Kastle]] (known as a composer), and cinematographer [[Oliver Wood (cinematographer)|Oliver Wood]]. A wealthy friend of Steibel, [[Leon Levy]], suggested to Steibel that he make a film, and gave Steibel $150,000, the amount that Steibel suggested it would cost.<ref name="nytbehind"/> After deciding the film would be about [[Raymond Fernandez (serial killer)|"The Lonely Hearts Killers"]], Steibel asked Kastle, his roommate, to do some research on the subject; financial limitations led Steibel to ask his friend to write the screenplay.<ref name="nytbehind"/>

Commenting on the aims of the film, Steibel said: "We wanted to do an honest movie about murders. These are not charming people. They are sleazy people—but fascinating. You won't come out of the theatre feeling sorry for the killers like in some movies. It is not romanticized."<ref name=wood>{{cite news|page=36|date=December 9, 1969|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262421/honolulu_starbulletin/|title=Low-Budget Movie May Make Cash Registers Ring|last=Wood|first=Ben|via=Newspapers.com|work=[[Honolulu Star-Bulletin]]|location=Honolulu, Hawaii}}</ref>

===Historical accuracy===
Although the film is inspired by true events and uses the real names of [[Raymond Fernandez (serial killer)|"The Lonely Hearts Killers"]] and of those they murdered, as well as the true locations of the crimes, the film takes substantial liberties with the facts, contrary to the opening titles. Although the actual events unfolded in the late 1940s, the film is seemingly set during an undetermined period even though it was clearly filmed during the late 1960s despite an end credit appearing stating that the two were executed in 1951. The film presents the killings as commencing with Beck's entrance on the scene and as a consequence of her jealousy. In contrast, it is believed that Fernandez had murdered at least one of the victims of his swindling, Jane Wilson Thompson, before meeting Beck, in order to pose as Thompson's widower and claim title to her property, including the apartment in New York where Beck joined him.<ref>{{cite news|title=Study Love Notes for Clue to Identity of Killer's 'Irene'|work=[[Milwaukee Journal]]|location=Milwaukee, Wisconsin|date=March 5, 1949|page=16}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Fernandez, Woman Face Michigan Murder Trial|work=Kingston Daily Freeman|location=Kingston, New York|date= March 2, 1949|pages=1, 17}}</ref> There is no acknowledgment that Beck was divorced with two children,<ref>{{cite news|title=Killer Asks for Children's Photos|work=Record-Eagle|location=Traverse City, Michigan|date=September 16, 1949|page=11}}</ref> whom she abandoned on Fernandez's orders (her abandonment of her mother is substituted); nor is there mention of Fernandez's legal wife and four children in Spain. Fernandez didn't have the accent used by Lo Bianco in the film.<ref>{{cite news|title=Says He Was Charming When He Married Her|work=[[The Sun (Lowell)|The Lowell Sun]]|location=Lowell, Massachusetts|date= March 2, 1949|page=1}}</ref>

The film depicts Beck's surrender of herself and Fernandez, but that implied demonstration of remorse is contrary to the historical record: neighbors noticed the Downings' disappearance, and Beck and Fernandez were apprehended at the Downing house after returning from an evening at the movies.<ref>{{cite news|title=Lonely Heart' Killings Bared: Widow and Child Buried in Concrete|work=News-Palladium|location=Benton Harbor, Michigan|date=March 1, 1949|page=1}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title= Justice for Mr. and Mrs. Bluebeard|work=Sunday Press|location=Binghamton, New York|date=February 18, 1951|page=8-C}}</ref>

===Casting===
[[File:Shirley Stoler in The Honeymoon Killers.jpg|thumb|right|upright=1|Stoler as Martha Beck]]
[[Shirley Stoler]] and [[Tony Lo Bianco]], both stage actors in New York, were cast in the leading roles.<ref name=show/> Stoler, also a singer, was referred by her friend, [[Marilyn Chris]], who had been cast in the role of Myrtle: "It was by accident that I got the role," Stoler stated. "I had just returned from Europe where I had been singing in cafes and had no job prospect."<ref name=bg>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262067/the_boston_globe/|work=[[The Boston Globe]]|location=Boston, Massachusetts|date=February 17, 1970|page=18|title=Fat actresses don't have to play funny roles|last=Adams|first=Marjory|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Stoler and Chris had worked together in stage productions at [[The Living Theatre]] in Manhattan,<ref name=bg/> while Lo Bianco had performed on [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]].<ref name=show/>

===Filming===
Principal photography of ''The Honeymoon Killers'' began in August 1968, concluding in October.<ref name=bg/> During production, it had the [[working title]] ''Dear Martha''.<ref name=wood/> The majority of the film was shot in [[Pittsfield, Massachusetts]]<ref name=show>{{cite news|title=That's show business|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262092/the_philadelphia_inquirer/|work=[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]]|location=Philadelphia, Pennsylvania|last=Wilson|first=Barbara L.|date=February 13, 1970|page=11|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> and [[Albany, New York]]. Steibel initially hired [[Martin Scorsese]] to direct, but Scorsese was fired for working too slowly, but a few scenes that he had shot were included in the final film.{{sfn|Raymond|2014|p=20}} Scorsese conceded that he had "been fired with pretty good reason...&nbsp;It was a 200-page script and I was shooting everything in [[master shot]]s with no coverage."{{sfn|Raymond|2014|p=20}} He was replaced by Donald Volkman, a maker of industrial films, who lasted only two weeks before being replaced by Leonard Kastle.<ref name="nytimes.com"/>

Budgetary constraints meant that the actors did their own hair and makeup, and special effects were not fancy. In the scene in which Martha bludgeons Janet Fay with a hammer, "condoms containing [[glycerine]] and red dye were affixed to the head of the victim with [[plaster of Paris]]. The hammer, a [[Ochroma pyramidale|balsa-wood]] prop, had a pin at the end. When the pin pricked the condoms, the blood began to flow."<ref name="nytbehind"/>

===Musical score===
The film's score features portions of the 6th and 5th Symphonies of [[Gustav Mahler]].<ref name=turan>{{cite news|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-10-30-ca-761-story.html|date=October 30, 1992|location=Los Angeles, California|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191119092952/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-10-30-ca-761-story.html|archive-date=November 19, 2019|title=MOVIE REVIEW : 'The Honeymoon Killers' Returns With a Vengeance|author=Turan, Kenneth|author-link=Kenneth Turan}}</ref> Scholar Martin Rubin asserts that the incorporation of Mahler's symphonies "catapults us outside the often unbearable events and at the same time plunges us inside the main characters' (especially Martha's) inflated and distorted interpretations of those events."{{sfn|Rubin|1999|p=47}} Film historian Rob Craig notes that the dramatic score gives the film an "uncanny essence of a [[Greek tragedy]]."{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=193}}

==Style==
Scholars and critics have noted ''The Honeymoon Killers'' for its black-and-white photography and camerawork that evokes those of a [[documentary film]].{{sfn|Rubin|1999|pages=46–48}} Critics also observed the film's streaks of [[realism (art)|realism]] at the time of its release.{{efn|The ''Dayton Daily News'' published a review that noted the film's photography and editing contributed to a realistic depiction of events.<ref name=dayton/> Similarly, Stephen Allen of the ''[[Courier-Post]]'' likened the film to an "[[underground film]]" and remarked the realistic depiction of its characters.<ref name=allen/>}} Film scholar Martin Rubin notes that ''The Honeymoon Killers'' "contains strong undercurrents of documentary realism, but it pushes them to the point where they erode the balance of realism and [[expressionism]] that provides a basic framework for the Hollywood style."{{sfn|Rubin|1999|p=46}} Rubin likens the film to a "putrescent version of [[Norman Rockwell]]'s America."{{sfn|Rubin|1999|p=49}}

While its unglamorous depiction of violence has been noted by critics,{{sfn|Rubin|1999|pages=49–52}} Rubin contests that the film is not "completely heartless" due to the "discomfiting vulnerability of the victims and the undiluted horror of their deaths."{{sfn|Rubin|1999|p=49}} Craig, however, describes the film as a "harrowing depiction of evil."{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=192}}

==Release==
===Box office===
[[American International Pictures]] (AIP) acquired distribution rights to the film in September 1969, and went as far as producing key art, publicity stills, and promotional posters.{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=193}} Sources differ as to whether AIP actually released the film; some claim that AIP gave it a brief theatrical run before it was dropped from AIP's roster.{{efn|Craig states that AIP likely dropped the film because of its violent and controversial content.{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=193}} A September 1969 article noting that the film had received a "rave review" in ''Variety'' states that AIP would be distributing it.<ref name=rave>{{cite news|work=[[The Berkshire Eagle]]|location=Pittsfield, Massachusetts|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262552/the_berkshire_eagle/|date=September 20, 1969|page=10|via=Newspapers.com|title=Feature Film Made Here draws Rave From Variety}}</ref> However, by December, the ''New York Daily News'' had reported that Cinerama Releasing Corporation was releasing the film.<ref name=nydn/> Despite this, Craig states there is "some evidence" that AIP did serve as the film's distributor in Canada.{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=193}}}} Craig theorizes that the studio ultimately opted out of releasing the film because of its "extremely gruesome and misanthropic" content.{{sfn|Craig|2019|p=193}}

''The Honeymoon Killers'' was distributed by [[Cinerama Releasing Corporation]],<ref name=nydn>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262460/daily_news/|work=[[New York Daily News]]|location=New York City, New York|date=December 21, 1969|page=170|title=Distribution Pact|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> premiering in New York City on February 4, 1970.<ref name=afi>{{cite web|work=[[AFI Catalog of Feature Films]]|publisher=[[American Film Institute]]|location=Los Angeles, California|title=The Honeymoon Killers|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191119100609/https://catalog.afi.com/Film/20646-THE-HONEYMOONKILLERS?sid=85cd8e57-09ad-4062-8af5-28a8f2a77a5e&sr=12.015283&cp=1&pos=0|archive-date=November 19, 2019|url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/20646-THE-HONEYMOONKILLERS?sid=85cd8e57-09ad-4062-8af5-28a8f2a77a5e&sr=12.015283&cp=1&pos=0}}</ref> Despite receiving some critical praise, the film "performed weakly" at the U.S. box office.<ref name="nytbehind"/> [[Jake Eberts]] later wrote that the entire budget of $250,000 was provided by Leon Levy.{{sfn|Eberts|Illott|1990|p=12}} The film ultimately earned $11 million internationally, but Levy recouped none of his investment.{{sfn|Eberts|Illott|1990|p=12}} The film was a "modest financial success" in Britain and France.<ref name="nytbehind"/>

===Critical response===
Prior to its release, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' magazine said that ''The Honeymoon Killers'' was "made with care, authenticity and attention to detail."<ref>[https://variety.com/1968/film/reviews/the-honeymoon-killers-1200421809 ''The Honeymoon Killers''], a January 1, 1969 review from ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' magazine</ref> Later, [[Roger Greenspun]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' praised the film, writing: "Kastle's film succeeds as a kind of chamber drama of desperate attraction and violent death. Although it is profoundly involved with the quality of individual middle-class American lives, it completely subordinates anecdote to action and pathos, even the pathos of its central characters, to passion. The secondary performances range from acceptable to excellent."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1970/02/05/archives/screen-kastles-honeymoon-killerstheme-recalls-lonely-hearts-murders.html|date=February 5, 1970|last=Greenspun|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Greenspun|title=Screen: Kastle's 'Honeymoon Killers':Theme Recalls Lonely Hearts Murders|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191119090418/https://www.nytimes.com/1970/02/05/archives/screen-kastles-honeymoon-killerstheme-recalls-lonely-hearts-murders.html|archive-date=November 19, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Harvey Taylor of the ''[[Detroit Free Press]]'' also championed the performances as "excellent," particularly those of Stoler and Lo Bianco, though he conceded that the film's grittiness left him "mildly nauseated."<ref name=taylor>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39262155/detroit_free_press/|work=[[Detroit Free Press]]|location=Detroit, Michigan|page=6-C|title='The Honeymoon Killers,' A Gruesome Tale of Horror|last=Taylor|first=Harvey|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

The ''[[Dayton Daily News]]'' noted the film's realism: "Done with grainy black-and-white film, poor-to-sloppy editing, one-position camera, secondhand furniture sets—there's a tawdry truth to the bizarre story of an unreal, grotesque love affair between Martha Beck and Raymond Fernandez."<ref name=dayton>{{cite news|work=[[Dayton Daily News]]|location=Dayton, Ohio|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39260889/cchoneymoon_killersdayton_daily_news/|date=March 5, 1970|title=Tight Budget Gives Realism to 'The Honeymoon Killers'|via=Newspapers.com|page=41}}</ref> Stephen Allen of New Jersey's ''[[Courier-Post]]'' made similar observations that the photography has a "harsh documentary or [[underground film]] quality that lends an air of authenticity," and also praised the realistic casting.<ref name=allen>{{cite news|work=[[Courier-Post]]|location=Camden, New Jersey|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/39260917/cchoneymoon_killerscourierpost/|title=Crime in 1950s Starkly Depicted|page=19|via=Newspapers.com|date=February 27, 1970|last=Allen|first=Stephen}}</ref>

Upon a revival screening in 1992, [[Kenneth Turan]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' praised the film, writing: "This quality of being true to itself is ''The Honeymoon Killers''{{'}}s greatest strength. Writer-director Kastle, who unaccountably never made another feature, is in perfect control of his material here, understanding it thoroughly and making sure that everything, from the harsh lighting to the flat staging to the snippets of Mahler on the soundtrack, unite to enhance the rawness and relentlessness of the film."<ref name=turan/> A 2003 review by ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' concludes by noting that the film's "nauseous mixture of laughs and shocks, and the fact that real passion drives Kastle's characters even when they plot against each other, is what makes ''The Honeymoon Killers'' such an enduring one-off. It works, as [[Gary Giddins]] argues in the liner notes of the restored DVD edition, 'as the perfect product of the same anxious, permissive age that produced [[John Waters (filmmaker)|Waters]], ''[[Night of the Living Dead]]'', and [[blaxploitation]]. But it holds up just as well as a weirdly timeless love story with a [[body count]].'"<ref name="av">[https://www.avclub.com/the-honeymoon-killers-dvd-1798198698 The Honeymoon Killers (DVD)], a July 14, 2003 review by Keith Phipps for [[The A.V. Club]].</ref>

On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 95% of 21 critics' reviews are positive.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Honeymoon Killers |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/honeymoon_killers |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=April 30, 2026}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 82 out of 100 based on 15 critics, which the site labels as "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Honeymoon Killers |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-honeymoon-killers/ |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=April 30, 2026}}</ref>

The film was reportedly banned in [[Australia]] upon its release in 1970 until the late 1980s after being deemed "violent, indecent and obscene" by the Australian censorship board.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.refused-classification.com/censorship/film-1/american-02/#honeymoon-killers|title=The Honeymoon Killers|publisher=Refused-Classification.com|location= Australia|access-date=September 26, 2022}}</ref>

===Home media===
The film was issued on [[DVD]] by [[The Criterion Collection]] (under license from MGM, the owner of the AIP library) in July 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s896kill.html|work=[[DVD Talk]]|title=DVD Savant Review: The Honeymoon Killers|date=2003|last=Erickson|first=Glenn|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170826122537/https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s896kill.html|archive-date=August 26, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> A newly restored [[Blu-ray]] and DVD edition was released by the Criterion Collection in 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/the-honeymoon-killers/|work=[[Slant Magazine]]|title=Review: The Honeymoon Killers|date=October 2, 2015|last=Bowen|first=Chuck|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191119035114/https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/the-honeymoon-killers/|archive-date=November 19, 2019}}</ref>

==Legacy==
In the decades following its release, ''The Honeymoon Killers'' developed a [[cult following]].{{sfn|Raymond|2014|p=20}} French director [[François Truffaut]] heralded it as his "favorite American film."<ref name="nytbehind">{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/10/20/movies/behind-the-filming-of-the-honeymoon-killers.html|work=[[The New York Times]]|title=Behind the Filming of 'The Honeymoon Killers'|date=October 20, 1992|author=Grimes, William|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101202201600/https://www.nytimes.com/1992/10/20/movies/behind-the-filming-of-the-honeymoon-killers.html|archive-date=December 2, 2010}}</ref>
