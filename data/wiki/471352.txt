'''''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai''''' is a 1999 [[crime drama film]] produced, written and directed by [[Jim Jarmusch]]. It stars [[Forest Whitaker]] as the title character, a [[hitman]] for the [[American Mafia|mafia]] who adheres to the ancient warrior code of the [[samurai]], as outlined in the book of [[Yamamoto Tsunetomo]]'s recorded sayings, ''[[Hagakure]].'' The cast also features [[Cliff Gorman]], [[Henry Silva]], [[Isaach de Bankolé]], [[Victor Argo]] and [[Camille Winbush]]. The film also features [[RZA]] in his film debut.

An international co-production between the United States, France, Germany and Japan,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ghost Dog The Way of the Samurai (1999) |url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b8185d152 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161110231024/http://www.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b8185d152 |url-status=dead |archive-date=November 10, 2016 |access-date=2023-08-23 |website=BFI}}</ref> ''Ghost Dog'' premiered at the [[1999 Cannes Film Festival]], where it competed for the [[Palme d'Or]]. The film opened to largely positive critical reception,<ref name=":0">{{cite web |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai |access-date=29 April 2019 |website=[[Metacritic]] |archive-date=20 April 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180420012906/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai |url-status=live }}</ref> and was nominated for both an [[Independent Spirit Award for Best Feature]] and a [[César Award for Best Foreign Film]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Forest Whitaker |url=https://www.filmindependent.org/people/forest-whitaker/ |access-date=29 April 2019 |website=Film Independent |archive-date=26 April 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190426183849/https://www.filmindependent.org/people/forest-whitaker/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Plot==
Ghost Dog sees himself as a [[Retainer (medieval)|retainer]] of Louie, a local mobster, who saved Ghost Dog's life years earlier. While living as a hitman for the [[American Mafia]], he adheres to the code of the [[samurai]], and interprets and applies the wisdom of the ''[[Hagakure]]''.

Louie tells Ghost Dog to kill a gangster, Handsome Frank, who is sleeping with the daughter of local mafia boss Vargo. Ghost Dog arrives and kills the gangster, before seeing that the girl is also in the room; he leaves her alive, and she gives him a copy of the book ''[[Rashōmon (short story)|Rashōmon]]'' to read. To avoid being implicated in the murder of a [[made man]], Vargo and his associate Sonny Valerio decide to get rid of Ghost Dog. Louie knows practically nothing about Ghost Dog, as the hitman communicates only by [[homing pigeon]]. The mobsters start by tracing all the pigeon coops in town. They find Ghost Dog's cabin atop a building and kill his pigeons. Ghost Dog realizes he must kill Vargo and his men or they will kill him and his master.

During the day, Ghost Dog frequently visits the park to see his best friend, a French-speaking ice cream man named Raymond. Ghost Dog does not understand French and Raymond does not understand English but the two nonetheless seem to connect with each other. Ghost Dog also befriends a little girl named Pearline, to whom he lends the book ''[[Rashōmon (short story)|Rashōmon]]''.

Eventually, Ghost Dog invades Vargo's mansion and kills almost everyone single-handedly, sparing only Louie and Vargo's daughter. That night, Ghost Dog kills Sonny Valerio at his home by shooting him through a pipe. Ghost Dog expects that Louie will attack him, as he feels that Louie is obliged to avenge the murder of his boss Vargo. He goes to the park and gives Raymond all his money, helping him to stay in the country. Pearline appears and gives back ''Rashōmon'' to Ghost Dog, saying that she liked it. Ghost Dog gives Pearline his copy of ''Hagakure'' and encourages her to read it.

Though Louie feels some loyalty to Ghost Dog, he finally confronts him at Raymond's ice cream stand with Raymond and Pearline watching. Ghost Dog is unwilling to attack his master and allows Louie to kill him. His last act is to give Louie the copy of ''Rashōmon'' and encourage him to read it. Pearline takes Ghost Dog's empty gun and aims at Louie as he flees. Ghost Dog dies from his wounds, lying on the asphalt. Raymond weeps while Pearline watches Louie leave; Louie gets into a car with Vargo's daughter (who now has replaced her father as his boss). Later, Pearline reads the ''Hagakure''.

==Themes and analysis==
===Violence and cross-culturalism===
In a 2000 interview with Peter Bowen of ''[[Filmmaker (magazine)|Filmmaker]]'', Jarmusch referred to the violence in ''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai'' as "simply a reflection of the history of human beings."<ref name="Bowen 2000">{{cite web|url=https://filmmakermagazine.com/107997-the-violence-in-the-film-is-simply-a-reflection-of-the-history-of-human-beings-writer-director-jim-jarmusch-and-actor-forest-whitaker-on-ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai/|title="The Violence in the Film is Simply a Reflection of the History of Human Beings": Writer/Director Jim Jarmusch and Actor Forest Whitaker on ''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai''|last=Bowen|first=Peter|date=2000|website=[[Filmmaker (magazine)|Filmmaker]]|access-date=January 30, 2023|archive-date=January 31, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230131030749/https://filmmakermagazine.com/107997-the-violence-in-the-film-is-simply-a-reflection-of-the-history-of-human-beings-writer-director-jim-jarmusch-and-actor-forest-whitaker-on-ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai/|url-status=live}}</ref> Regarding the multiple instances in which the gangster characters or their associates watch cartoons, Jarmusch added: "As for the cartoons, it is another layer, resonance, or nuance of things. They are echoes of things happening in the story. What I don't like is that some people have said that the cartoons are there because the gangsters are very cartoon-like. I just like cartoons, and I like the idea of adults watching cartoons."<ref name="Bowen 2000" /> Bowen noted that the film features clips from ''[[The Itchy & Scratchy Show]]'', a fictional television series in ''[[The Simpsons]]'' known for its gratuitous violence.<ref name="Bowen 2000" />

Speaking on Ghost Dog as a character, Jarmusch said that, "Violence is just who Ghost Dog is. He is a warrior, and he follows a warrior code. He acts in violent situations, as a warrior must."<ref name="Bowen 2000" /> Whitaker, in the same interview, added, "To be whole, to have duality and knowing both sides of everything—I think Ghost Dog is aware of that, at least as a character. He is not content, but strong in what he knows to be the order of his life. I don't think he even views what he does as a violent act; it is just an extension of something he must do to maintain the order by which he lives."<ref name="Bowen 2000" /> Jarmusch goes on to say that violence is one of several elements in the film representative of an amalgam of cultures, stating, "America is about the synthesis of a lot of different cultures, and beauty arises out of that synthesis. I don't see this as violence of about cultures clashing but rather as being all part of one thing. The Italian guys don't even work out of an Italian restaurant anymore; it's a [[Chinese restaurant]]. Ghost Dog himself is an urban black character, but he follows a code from another culture and another century, the Japanese samurai culture."<ref name="Bowen 2000" />

=== Cinematic allusions ===
''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai'' has been interpreted by critics as a homage to ''[[Le Samouraï]]'', a 1967 crime-drama by [[Jean-Pierre Melville]] starring [[Alain Delon]]. That movie opens with a quote from an invented ''Book of Bushido'' and features a meditative, loner hero, Jef Costello. In the same manner that Ghost Dog has an electronic "key" to break into luxury cars, Costello has a huge ring of keys that enable him to steal any [[Citroën DS]].<ref name=":2">{{cite web |last=Hoberman |first=J. |date=February 29, 2000 |title=Into the Void |url=https://www.villagevoice.com/2000/02/29/into-the-void/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071017162233/http://www.villagevoice.com/film/0009,hoberman2,12881,20.html |archive-date=October 17, 2007 |access-date=December 6, 2007 |website=The Village Voice}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Thorsen |first=Tor |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai (2000) Reel Review |url=http://www.reel.com/movie.asp?MID=129128&buy=open&Tab=reviews&CID=13 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071017160804/http://reel.com/movie.asp?MID=129128&buy=open&Tab=reviews&CID=13 |archive-date=October 17, 2007 |access-date=December 6, 2007 |website=Reel.com}}</ref> The endings share a key similarity. The peculiar relationship between the protagonists of both movies and birds, as companions and danger advisers, is another common theme. The film contains a number of references to [[Seijun Suzuki]]'s ''[[Branded to Kill]]'',<ref>{{cite web |last=Wilonsky |first=Robert |date=March 23, 2000 |title=The Way of Jim Jarmusch |url=http://www.miaminewtimes.com/content/printVersion/241003 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120928073953/http://www.miaminewtimes.com/content/printVersion/241003/ |archive-date=September 28, 2012 |access-date=May 7, 2009 |work=[[Miami New Times]]}}</ref> such as when a bird lands in front of Ghost Dog's rifle scope (referencing the incident with a butterfly in Suzuki's film) and Ghost Dog shooting Sonny Valerio up the drain pipe. Both Melville and Suzuki receive "personal thanks" in the film's credits.

==Production==
''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai'' was shot mostly in [[Jersey City, New Jersey]], but the movie never mentions where the story is set. License plates reveal it is in "The Industrial State"<ref name="reverseshot">{{cite web |last=Catsoulis |first=Jeannette |date=August 8, 2005 |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai |url=http://reverseshot.org/archive/entry/691/ghost_dog_way_samurai |access-date=2006-10-13 |website=Reverse Shot |archive-date=2020-11-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201120202230/http://reverseshot.org/archive/entry/691/ghost_dog_way_samurai |url-status=live }}</ref> and a vehicle from another state has on its license plate "The Highway State", both of which are fictional [[List of U.S. state nicknames|state nicknames]].

The [[Holland Tunnel]] appears in a driving scene, however, the shot begins after the large sign at the toll plaza in Jersey City.

==Reception==
=== Box office ===
''Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai'' grossed a worldwide total of $9,380,473, of which $3,308,029 was in the United States.<ref name="boxofficemojo">{{cite web |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=ghostdog.htm |access-date=August 30, 2006 |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |archive-date=May 6, 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060506121838/http://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=ghostdog.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
Critical response to the film was largely positive. On the [[Rotten Tomatoes]] review site, the film has an 84% rating, based on reviews from 106 critics. The website's critical consensus was that the movie is "[a]n innovative blend of samurai and gangster lifestyles."<ref name="rotten">{{cite web |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/ghost_dog_the_way_of_the_samurai/ |access-date=February 27, 2025|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |archive-date=May 23, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190523151000/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/ghost_dog_the_way_of_the_samurai/ |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a score of 68 based on 31 critics' reviews.<ref name=":0" />

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film three out of four, describing it as "truly, profoundly weird".<ref name=":1" /> Ebert's review proposed ''Ghost Dog'' made the most sense if Whitaker's character were insane. "In a quiet, sweet way, he is totally unhinged and has lost all touch with reality. His profound sadness, which permeates the touching Whitaker performance, comes from his alienation from human society, his loneliness, his attempt to justify inhuman behavior (murder) with a belief system (the samurai code) that has no connection with his life or his world."<ref name=":1">{{cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=March 17, 2000 |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai Movie Review (2000) |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai-2000 |access-date=29 April 2019 |website=www.rogerebert.com |archive-date=29 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220429235242/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai-2000 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[J. Hoberman]] of ''[[The Village Voice]]'' described it as "an impeccably shot and sensationally scored deadpan parody of two current popular modes", namely hitman and [[mafia film|mafia]] films.<ref name=":2" />

=== Accolades ===
The film was nominated for the [[Grand Prix (Belgian Syndicate of Cinema Critics)|Grand Prix]] of the [[Belgian Syndicate of Cinema Critics]], the [[César Award]] for Best Foreign Film of 2000<ref name="imdb">{{cite web |title=Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai (1999) Awards & Festivals |url=https://mubi.com/films/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai/awards |access-date=November 18, 2022 |website=Mubi |archive-date=November 18, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221118153816/https://mubi.com/films/ghost-dog-the-way-of-the-samurai/awards |url-status=live }}</ref> and the [[Palme d'Or]] award at the [[1999 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/5324/year/1999.html |title=Festival de Cannes: Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai |access-date=2009-10-06 |work=festival-cannes.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110710235156/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/5324/year/1999.html |archive-date=2011-07-10 }}</ref> It was also nominated for [[Independent Spirit Award for Best Film|Best Feature]] at the [[16th Independent Spirit Awards]].<ref name="imdb" />
