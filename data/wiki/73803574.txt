'''''Youth (Spring)''''' ({{langx|zh|青春：春|qīng chūn: chūn}}; {{langx|fr|Jeunesse (Le Printemps)}}) is a 2023 documentary film directed by [[Wang Bing (director)|Wang Bing]]. It is an international co-production between China, France, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands. The film focuses on a group of young textile workers in the Chinese town of Zhili. It is the first entry in a trilogy of films that follow the same characters over an extended period of time.<ref name="Screen International">{{cite magazine|last=Leffler|first=Rebecca|date=3 May 2023|title=Pyramide seals deals on Cannes Competition title 'Last Summer'; boards Wang Bing trilogy (exclusive)|url=https://www.screendaily.com/news/pyramide-seals-deals-on-cannes-competition-title-last-summer-boards-wang-bing-trilogy-exclusive/5181600.article|magazine=[[Screen International]]|access-date=15 May 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Lemercier|first=Fabien|date=12 May 2023|title=Pyramide International presents a five-star Cannes line-up|url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/442297|work=[[Cineuropa]]|access-date=15 May 2023}}</ref>

The film was selected to compete for the [[Palme d'Or]] at the [[2023 Cannes Film Festival|76th Cannes Film Festival]], where it had its world premiere on 18 May 2023. It is the first installment in Wang Bing's ''Youth'' trilogy, followed by ''[[Youth (Hard Times)]]'' and ''[[Youth (Homecoming)]]'' in 2024.

==Synopsis==
The film focuses on a group of young textile workers in [[:zh:織里鎮|Zhili]], a town in the [[Wuxing District]] of [[Huzhou]], located 150 kilometres outside of [[Shanghai]]. Every year, young people leave their rural villages and migrate to the manufacturing town. The workers are in their twenties, some in their thirties. They sleep upstairs, in dormitories, because they come from far away, sometimes over 2,000 kilometres. Their dialects come from different regions. They work tirelessly with the hope of one day having children, buying a house or starting their own business. Friendships and romances fold and unfold as the seasons pass. Geographical dispersion, financial instability, and economic and family pressures ravish their innocence and youth. Wang Bing will spend a year with them in Zhili: at work, at home, on the Internet, every day of their professional, romantic relationships and friendships.

==Production==
The film was shot in the Chinese province of [[Zhejiang]], from 2014 to 2019. Wang Bing shot the film in the Zhili, a manufacturing town in [[Huzhou]] City. Zhili is the same location where Wang Bing shot his 2016 film ''[[Bitter Money]]''. ''Youth (Spring)'' also covers the same subject – young workers in the textile workshops. However, the films do not focus on the same people. There were six people shooting, three at the same time, with three cameras, to compensate for the city's sizeable area and the film's many characters. Wang Bing devoted himself to the project, living for several years in a nearby town. He struggled for the first three months, but eventually befriended the local business managers, who were largely preoccupied with their daily lives. Provided the production would not disturb the running of their businesses, they did not object to the film or what would or would not be shown of the working conditions of their employees.<ref name="Cahiers du Cinéma">"Wang Bing: Man in Black et Jeunesse" Interview by Charlotte Garson in Paris on 30 Janvier 2023. Interpreter: Bingyuan Xu. ''[[Cahiers du Cinéma]]''. No. 796. p. 16</ref>

''Youth (Spring)'' the first entry in a trilogy of films that follow the same characters over an extended period of time.<ref name="Screen International" /> Wang Bing filmed the people at their workplace, but also followed some of them in the opposite direction to their province of origin, to celebrate [[Chinese New Year]] with their families. In an interview with ''[[Cahiers du Cinéma]]'' on 30 January 2023, Wang Bing estimated the trilogy would run for approximately nine hours and forty minutes, and assured that 10 hours would be the limit.<ref name="Cahiers du Cinéma" />

The film is produced by House on Fire, Gladys Glover and CS Production, in co-production [[Arte France Cinéma]], Les Films Fauves (Luxembourg), Volya Films (Netherlands), Eastern-Lion Pictures and Culture Media Co., Beijing Contemporary Art Foundation, and Le Fresnoy - Studio national des arts contemporains.<ref name="Pyramide" /><ref>{{cite magazine|last=Felperin|first=Leslie|date=18 May 2023|title='Youth (Spring)' Review: Wang Bing's Absorbingly Dense and Detailed Doc Portrait of Eastern Chinese Garment Workers|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/youth-spring-review-wang-bing-1235495342/|magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=18 May 2023}}</ref>

==Release==
''Youth (Spring)'' was selected to compete for the [[Palme d'Or]] at the [[2023 Cannes Film Festival]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=The films of the Official Selection 2023 |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/press/press-releases/the-films-of-the-official-selection-2023/ |access-date=18 April 2023 |website=Festival de Cannes |date=13 April 2023 |language=en-US}}</ref> where it had its world premiere on 18 May 2023.<ref>{{Cite web |last=mraultpauillac |date=2023-05-10 |title=The Screenings Guide of the 76th Festival de Cannes |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2023/the-screenings-guide-of-the-76th-festival-de-cannes/ |access-date=2023-05-10 |website=Festival de Cannes |language=en-US}}</ref> It was also invited at the [[28th Busan International Film Festival]] in 'Icon' section and was screened on 5 October 2023.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.biff.kr/eng/html/program/prog_all_list.asp?allYear=2023 |title= The 28th Busan International Film Festival: Selection List |website=Busan International Film Festival|date=September 5, 2023|access-date=September 7, 2023|language=en}}</ref> 

The film was theatrically released in the Netherlands on 16 November 2023 by Cinema Delicatessen,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cinedeli.nl/film/youth/|title=Youth|website=cinedeli.nl|language=nl|access-date=4 January 2024}}</ref> and in France on 3 January 2024 by Les Acacias.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.acaciasfilms.com/film/jeunesse-printemps/|title=Jeunesse (Le Printemps)|website=www.acaciasfilms.com|language=fr|access-date=12 July 2023}}</ref> International sales were handled by Pyramide International, who sold the next two parts of the trilogy: ''[[Youth (Hard Times)]]'' and ''[[Youth (Homecoming)]]''.<ref name="Screen International" />

==Reception==
===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 86% based on 35 reviews, with an average rating of 7.2/10. The website's critics consensus reads: "''Youth (Spring)'' presents an earnest and poignant -- if somewhat repetitive -- look at the experiences of Chinese garment workers."<ref>{{cite Rotten Tomatoes|title=Youth (Spring)|type=m|id=youth_spring|access-date=27 March 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted average]] score of 74 out of 100, based on 14 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=youth-spring|type=movie|title=Youth (Spring)|access-date=20 March 2024}}</ref>
