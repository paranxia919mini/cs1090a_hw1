The film stars an [[ensemble cast]] consisting of [[Greg Kinnear]], [[Steve Carell]], [[Toni Collette]], [[Paul Dano]], [[Abigail Breslin]], and [[Alan Arkin]], all of whom play members of a dysfunctional family taking the youngest (Breslin) to compete in a [[child beauty pageant]]. Breslin's breakout performance in the film earned her an Academy Award nomination. The [[road movie|road film]] tackles themes of family, [[Depression (mood)|depression]], self-acceptance, and finding meaning in absurd conditions. It was produced by Big Beach Films on a budget of US$8&nbsp;million.<ref name="boxofficemojo">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=littlemisssunshine.htm|title=Little Miss Sunshine|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=August 22, 2008|archive-date=March 2, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100302004957/http://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=littlemisssunshine.htm|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Shadows">{{cite news|last=Hornaday |first=Ann |title=From Shadows to "Sunshine" |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=July 30, 2006 |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2006/07/28/AR2006072800271_pf.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121111200924/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2006/07/28/AR2006072800271_pf.html |archive-date=November 11, 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> Filming began on June 6, 2005, and took place over 30 days in [[Arizona]] and [[Southern California]].

The film premiered at the [[2006 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 20, 2006, and its distribution rights were bought by [[Fox Searchlight Pictures]] for one of the biggest deals made in the history of the festival.<ref name="RTBiggest">{{cite web|last=Duong|first=Senh|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/news/1646244/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071122000235/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/news/1646244/|title=Sundance: Searchlight Spends Big For "Little Miss Sunshine"|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|date=January 21, 2006|archive-date=November 22, 2007}}</ref> The film had a [[limited release]] in the United States on July 26, 2006, and expanded to a wider release starting on August 18.<ref name="boxofficemojo" />

''Little Miss Sunshine'' was a box office success, grossing $101 million. It garnered critical praise for its performances, as well as for its directing, screenplay and humor. It earned four nominations at the [[79th Academy Awards]], including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]]. Arndt and Arkin won [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]] and [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]], respectively, and Breslin was nominated for [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]]. It also won the [[Film Independent Spirit Awards|Independent Spirit Award]] for Best Feature and the [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture]], it won the [[Producers Guild of America Award for Best Theatrical Motion Picture]] and received [[List of accolades received by Little Miss Sunshine|numerous other accolades]].

==Plot==
Sheryl Hoover is a stressed mother of two living in [[Albuquerque, New Mexico]]. Her husband Richard is an aspiring [[motivational speaker]] and [[life coaching|life coach]]. Dwayne, Sheryl's [[Friedrich Nietzsche|Nietzsche]]-admiring teenage son from her previous marriage, has taken a [[vow of silence]] until he accomplishes his dream of becoming a [[United States Air Force|fighter pilot]]. Sheryl's older brother Frank Ginsburg, a gay scholar of [[Marcel Proust|Proust]], is living with the family after [[Suicide attempt|attempting suicide]]. Richard's foul-mouthed father Edwin is also living with the family after being evicted from a [[retirement home]] for snorting [[heroin]]. Olive, Richard and Sheryl's 7-year-old daughter, is an aspiring [[Beauty pageant|beauty queen]] coached by Edwin.

Olive learns she has qualified for the "Little Miss Sunshine" beauty pageant being held in [[Redondo Beach, California]], in two days. Richard, Sheryl, and Edwin want to support her, and Frank and Dwayne cannot be left alone, so the whole family attends. Due to financial constraints, they go on an {{convert|800|mi|km|adj=on}} road trip in their yellow [[Volkswagen Type 2|Volkswagen van]]. Family tensions play out along the way, amidst the aging van's mechanical problems. When the van's clutch breaks early on, the only way to drive it is to push it fast enough for the driver to put it into third gear, then jump in the open side door. Later, the van's horn starts honking by itself, and at one point the side door falls off.

Throughout the trip, the family suffer numerous personal setbacks and discovers their need for each other's support. While buying [[pornography]] for Edwin at a gas station, Frank embarrassingly meets the ex-boyfriend who, in leaving him for an academic rival, had prompted his suicide attempt. Richard loses an important contract with businessman Stan Grossman that would have jump-started his motivational business. Edwin snorts heroin in his room, leading to a fatal [[heroin overdose|overdose]]. When the hospital staff refuse to let the family leave his corpse behind while they go to the pageant, they smuggle it out the window and into the van's trunk. They are almost caught when a police officer pulls them over because of the honking horn, only escaping trouble due to the police officer's fascination with the pornography. 

During the final leg of the trip, Dwayne discovers he is [[Color blindness|color blind]], meaning he cannot become a pilot. This revelation prompts him to finally break his silence and shout his disdain for his family, though he apologizes after Olive calms him with a gentle hug.

After a frantic race against the clock, the family arrives at the pageant hotel. Sheryl prepares Olive for her performance while Richard makes arrangements for Edwin's body. The other contestants are slim, sexualized pre-teens who perform elaborate dance numbers with great panache. Fearing that the amateur Olive will be humiliated, Richard and Dwayne try to stop her from performing, but Sheryl insists that they "let Olive be Olive", and she goes on stage.

The hitherto-unseen dance routine Edwin had taught Olive is revealed to be a [[striptease]] performed to the [[Rocasound]] remix of [[Rick James]]' "[[Super Freak]]". Olive is oblivious to the subtext of the routine, but it horrifies and angers most of the audience, and the pageant organizer demands she be removed from the stage. Richard physically prevents this, and the family all join Olive on stage, dancing alongside her to show their support.

The family is later released from the hotel's security office on the condition that Olive never enter a beauty pageant in California again. Piling into the van with the horn still honking, they begin their trip home to Albuquerque.

When choosing the cast for the film, directors [[Jonathan Dayton and Valerie Faris]] were assisted by casting directors Kim Davis and Justine Baddeley who had worked with them on previous music videos.<ref name="TwitchFilm"/> Davis and Baddeley traveled to "every English-speaking country"<ref name="TwitchFilm"/> to search for the actress to portray Olive Hoover, and they finally chose actress [[Abigail Breslin]] through an audition when she was six. [[Paul Dano]] was cast as Dwayne two years before production began and in preparation for portraying his character, spent a few days taking his own vow of silence.{{sfn|''About the Movie''|2006|p=8}}

The role of Frank, the suicidal Proust scholar, was originally written for [[Bill Murray]], and there was also studio pressure for [[Robin Williams]].<ref name="NYTSmallFilm"/> The directing duo chose [[Steve Carell]] for the role a few months before filming began, and in an interview revealed, "When we met with Steve Carell, we didn't know he could do this based upon what he had done. But when we met with him and talked to him about the character, the tone of the movie and the way we were approaching it, he was right on the same page with us."<ref name="TwitchFilm">{{cite web|title=Little Miss Sunshine—Interview With Valerie Faris and Jonathan Dayton |last=Gullién |first=Michael |website=Twitch |date=July 20, 2006 |url=http://twitchfilm.net/interviews/2006/07/little-miss-sunshineinterview-with-valerie-faris-and-jonathan-dayton.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131110004125/http://twitchfilm.com/interviews/2006/07/little-miss-sunshineinterview-with-valerie-faris-and-jonathan-dayton.php |archive-date=November 10, 2013 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Although known to [[Comedy Central]] viewers for many years as a correspondent on the satirical news program ''[[The Daily Show|The Daily Show with Jon Stewart]]'', at the time Carell was cast he was relatively unknown in Hollywood,{{sfn|''About the Movie''|2006|p=6}} and producers of the film were worried that he was not a big enough star and did not have much acting experience.<ref name="EWSunshineBand">{{cite magazine |title=The Sunshine Band |last=Rottenberg |first=Josh |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=August 3, 2006 |url=https://ew.com/article/2006/08/04/inside-little-miss-sunshine-sundance-2006s-breakout-hit/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090427154230/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,1222576,00.html |archive-date=April 27, 2009 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Script and development===
The script was written by [[Michael Arndt]] and was originally about an East Coast road trip from [[Maryland]] to [[Florida]], but was shifted to a journey from New Mexico to California because of budget issues.<ref name="AOLTravel">{{cite news|last=Moore |first=Roger |title=Directors keep their 'Sunshine' out of the Sunshine State |work=[[Orlando Sentinel]] |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2006/08/18/directors-keep-their-sunshine-out-of-the-sunshine-state/ |date=August 16, 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120916210126/http://articles.orlandosentinel.com/2006-08-18/entertainment/SUNSHINEGUYS_1_dayton-abigail-breslin-sunshine |archive-date=September 16, 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> Arndt started the script on May 23, 2000, and completed the first rough draft by May 26.{{Sfn|Arndt|2007|pp=|p=x}} He had initially planned on shooting the film himself by raising several thousand dollars and using a [[camcorder]].<ref name="DVDCommShoot">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine-(Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt)|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=11:33|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref> Instead, he gave the screenplay to producers Ron Yerxa and Albert Berger who teamed up with Deep River Productions to find a potential director.<ref name="FindItsWay">{{cite journal|last=Ortner|first=Sherry B.|title=Little Miss Sunshine Finds Its Way|journal=Anthropology News|volume=48|issue=October 2007|pages=22–23|date=October 10, 2007|url=http://www.anthrosource.net/Abstract.aspx?issn=1541-6151&volume=48&issue=7&doubleissueno=0&article=245257&suppno=0&jstor=False|url-access=registration|doi=10.1525/an.2007.48.7.22|access-date=July 20, 2009|archive-date=December 3, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131203004916/http://www.anthrosource.net/Abstract.aspx?issn=1541-6151&volume=48&issue=7&doubleissueno=0&article=245257&suppno=0&jstor=False|url-status=dead}}</ref> Arndt included the character [[Stan Grossman]] (here played by Bryan Cranston) as a tribute to the film [[Fargo (1996 film)|''Fargo'']].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://gointothestory.blcklst.com/daily-dialogue-august-26-2017-6b3ba00cb176 |title=Daily Dialogue |last=Myers |first=Scott |date=August 26, 2017|website=Go Into the Story |publisher=[[The Black List (survey)|The Black List]]|access-date=May 10, 2023}}</ref>

The producers met directors Dayton and Faris while producing ''[[Election (1999 film)|Election]]'' and in turn gave the script to them to read in 2001. The directors commented later on the script stating: "This film really struck a chord. We felt like it was written for us."<ref name="NYTSmallFilm">{{cite news|title=A Small Film Nearly Left for Dead Has Its Day in the Sundance Rays |last=Waxman |first=Sharon |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=January 23, 2006 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2006/01/23/movies/MoviesFeatures/23sund.html?_r=2 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220615143131/http://www.nytimes.com/2006/01/23/movies/MoviesFeatures/23sund.html?_r=2 |archive-date=June 15, 2022 |url-status=live }}</ref> The script was purchased from first-time screenwriter Arndt for $250,000 by Marc Turtletaub, one of the film's producers, on December 21, 2001.{{Sfn|Press|2008|pp=|p=252}} Yerxa and Berger remained as producers as they were responsible for finding the directors and cinematographer, assisting in the ending re-shoot, and helping bring the film to the Sundance Film Festival.<ref name="LATimesProducers"/>

The film was pitched to several studios, and the only interested studio was [[Focus Features]], who wanted to film it in Canada.<ref name="NYTSmallFilm"/> After the studio attempted to have the film be centered on the character Richard Hoover, and Arndt disagreed, he was fired and replaced by another writer.<ref name="ForaInterview">{{cite web|date=February 15, 2007 |url=http://fora.tv/2007/02/15/Little_Miss_Sunshine_Shooting_Script |title=Little Miss Sunshine: The Shooting Script |publisher=FORA.tv |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101011060101/http://fora.tv/2007/02/15/Little_Miss_Sunshine_Shooting_Script#comments_section |archive-date=October 11, 2010 |url-status=usurped }}</ref> The new writer added several scenes, including Richard's confrontation with the character who dismisses his motivational technique business.<ref name="DVDCommWriter">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine – Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=46:30|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref> A corporate change brought in a new studio head and Arndt was rehired when the new writer left after four weeks of rewriting the script.<ref name="SF360">{{cite news|title=Michael Arndt, Little Mr. Sunshine|last=Guillén|first=Michael|publisher=[[San Francisco Film Society|SF360]]|date=February 23, 2007|url=http://www.sf360.org/features/2007/02/michael_arndt_l.html|url-status=usurped|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070308112240/http://www.sf360.org/features/2007/02/michael_arndt_l.html|archive-date=March 8, 2007}}</ref> After two years of pre-production, Focus Features dropped the film in August 2004. Marc Turtletaub paid $400,000 to Focus Features to buy back the rights to the film and for development costs.<ref name="NYTSmallFilm"/> He also paid for the $8&nbsp;million budget, allowing ''Little Miss Sunshine'' to then be filmed.<ref name="USAShadows">{{cite news|title='Sunshine,' out of the shadows |last=Bowles |first=Scott |work=[[USA Today]] |date=August 27, 2006 |access-date=August 28, 2006 |url=https://www.usatoday.com/life/music/news/2006-08-27-sunshine-hit_x.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090227014842/http://www.usatoday.com/life/music/news/2006-08-27-sunshine-hit_x.htm |archive-date=February 27, 2009 |url-status=live }}</ref>

====Volkswagen T2 Microbus====
[[File:LittleMissSunshineCast.jpg|thumb|right|alt=A film screenshot shows the family all seated in the Volkswagen Microbus as it is driven on a highway. The angle is from the windshield looking into the vehicle so that the majority of the interior can be seen. The mother is sitting in the front passenger seat with a dull expression on her face. The father is driving the vehicle with a similar expression. In the middle row, the daughter is looking down, listening to music from a CD player. Her uncle is seated on the right, looking to his left. In the back row, the grandfather is looking towards his grandson (whose face is slightly obscured by his father's head due to the angle). In the background, other vehicles can be seen driving on the highway.|Five modified vans were used during filming to capture various angles of the characters, including this one through the windshield.]]
When writing the script, Arndt chose the Volkswagen T2 Microbus to use for the road trip based on his experience with the vehicle and its practicality for filming: "I remember thinking, it's a road trip, what vehicle are you going to put them in? And [the] VW bus just seems logical, just because you have these high ceilings and these clean sight lines where you can put the camera. In the front windshield looking back and seeing everybody."<ref name="ForaInterview"/> Five VW Microbuses were used for the family car as some were modified for different filming techniques.<ref name="DVDCommVan5">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine – Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=23:10|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref> Three of the vans had engines, and the two without were mounted on trailers.<ref name="DVDCommVan5"/> During pre-production, the cinematographer used a basic video camera and set it up at angles inside the van to determine the best locations to shoot from during filming.{{sfn|''About the Movie''|2006|p=11}} Many of the problems associated with the van that were included in the plot (a broken clutch, a stuck horn, and a detached door), were based on similar problems that writer Arndt experienced during a childhood trip that involved the same type of vehicle.<ref name="SF360"/><ref name="DVDCommVanProblems">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine – Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=31:22|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref><ref name="DVDCommVanProblems2">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine – Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=1:01:51|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref>

In an interview, actor Greg Kinnear jokingly described how the scenes were filmed when he was driving: "I was going like 50 miles an hour [{{cvt|50|mph|km/h|disp=out}}] in this '71 VW van that doesn't have side airbags. Basically, you'd wait for this huge camera truck to come whizzing in front of us with the camera. 'Okay, go!' I mean, it was insanity; it's the most dangerous movie I've ever made."<ref name="MovieWebint">{{cite news|title=Interview: Riding In The Little Yellow Van With Little Miss Sunshine |last=Chupnick |first=Steven |work=[[MovieWeb]] |url=http://www.movieweb.com/news/NEuaGDzCvx3Vyu |date=July 26, 2006 |archive-date=June 11, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110611044836/http://www.movieweb.com/news/riding-in-the-little-yellow-van-with-little-miss-sunshine |url-status=dead}}</ref> While filming the scenes in the van, the actors would at times remain in the vehicle for three or four hours a day.<ref name="MovieWebint"/> For scenes in which Alan Arkin's character was swearing excessively, Breslin had her headphones on and could not hear the dialogue, just like her character in the film. Only when she saw the film did she know what was being said.<ref name="MovieWebint"/><ref name="CinCon">{{cite news|title=Interview: Alan Arkin and Abigail Breslin in "Little Miss Sunshine" |last=Aames |first=Ethan |website=Cinema Confidential |date=July 19, 2006 |url=http://www.cinecon.com/news.php?id=0607193 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716072312/http://www.cinecon.com/news.php?id=0607193 |archive-date=July 16, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref> On July 25, 2006, Fox Searchlight Pictures invited VW bus owners to a screening at Vineland Drive-In theater in [[Industry, California]]. ====Pageant====
[[File:LittleMissSunshinePageant.jpg|thumb|right|alt=A film screenshot showing five girls standing on stage at the pageant at the end of the film. From left to right, the first girl (wearing a one-piece swimsuit) has her left leg placed in front of her right, the second girl (wearing a two-piece swimsuit) has her legs shifted in the opposite direction, at center the main character (wearing a one-piece swimsuit) is standing straight, the fourth girl (wearing a one-piece swimsuit) has her left leg placed in front of her right, and the last girl (wearing a two-piece swimsuit) also has her left foot forward. All of the girls are smiling towards different angles. The background of the stage is decorated and it is reflected on the shiny surface of the stage.|To make Abigail Breslin's character (center) appear larger than the other girls, she wore a padded suit. Much of the equipment and costumes exhibited during the pageant were provided by actual contestants' parents.]]
Prior to writing the script, Arndt read in a newspaper about [[Arnold Schwarzenegger]] speaking to a group of high school students and saying "If there's one thing in this world I hate, it's losers. I despise them." As a result, Arndt developed his script lampooning the thought process: "And I thought there's something so wrong with that attitude&nbsp;... I wanted to&nbsp;... attack that idea that in life you're going up or you're going down&nbsp;... So to a degree, a [[child beauty pageant]] is the epitome of the ultimate stupid meaningless competition people put themselves through."<ref name="ForaInterview"/> Co-director Jonathan Dayton also commented on the importance of the pageant to the film: "As far as the pageant goes, it was very important to us that the film not be about pageants. It's about being out of place, it's about not knowing where you're going to end up&nbsp;..."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jones |first=Aliya |date=March 31, 2019 |title=How 'Little Miss Sunshine' Uses a Pageant to Bring Its Characters Together |url=https://filmschoolrejects.com/little-miss-sunshine-pageant/ |access-date=May 3, 2023 |website=[[Film School Rejects]] |language=en-US |archive-date=December 25, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241225153520/https://filmschoolrejects.com/little-miss-sunshine-pageant/ |url-status=live }}</ref> All the girls acting as participants in the beauty pageant, except Abigail Breslin, were veterans of real beauty pageants. They looked the same and performed the same acts as they had in their real-life pageants.<ref name="eFilmCritic">{{cite web|title=Interview with 'Little Miss Sunshine' Directors Valerie Faris & Jonathan Dayton |last=Voynar |first=Kim |website=eFilmCritic |date=July 26, 2006 |url=http://www.efilmcritic.com/feature.php?feature=1897 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110927120925/http://www.efilmcritic.com/feature.php?feature=1897 |archive-date=September 27, 2011 |url-status=dead}}</ref> To prepare for filming, the directors attended several pageants in Southern California and met with a coordinator to learn more about the pageant process.<ref name="TodayLN">{{cite news|title=Little Miss Vista|last=Smiley|first=Joanna |work=Today's Local News|date=September 7, 2006|url=http://www.todayslocalnews.com/?sect=lifestyles&p=1704|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071016012119/http://www.todayslocalnews.com/?sect=lifestyles&p=1704|archive-date=October 16, 2007}}</ref> A mother of a contestant in the film claimed that the film overplayed practices that the contestants go through: "Most pageants aren't quite like that, with shaving the girls' legs, spraying them with fake tans and putting on so much makeup."<ref name="TodayLN"/>

When [[Focus Features]] initially wanted to film in Canada, the directors opposed it, believing the costs of flying all of the pageant girls and their families would be excessive.<ref name="NYTSmallFilm"/> The contestants and their families instead spent two weeks filming in a hotel in [[Ventura, California|Ventura]] with most of the equipment and costumes being provided by the contestants' parents.<ref name="TodayLN"/><ref name="DVDCommPageant">{{cite video|title=Little Miss Sunshine-(Commentary by Jonathan Dayton, Valerie Faris, and Michael Arndt)|medium=DVD|publisher=[[20th Century Fox]]|time=1:17:00|date=December 19, 2006}}</ref> To make Breslin's character the "plump" figure as shown in the film, she had to wear a padded suit during filming.{{sfn|''About the Movie''|2006|p=9}} For Olive's final scene involving her dancing routine, Breslin spent two weeks preparing with a [[Choreography|choreographer]].<ref name="MovieWebint"/>

===Score===
The [[Film score|score]] for ''Little Miss Sunshine'' was written by the [[Denver, Colorado|Denver]] band [[DeVotchKa]] and composer [[Mychael Danna]].{{sfn|''About the Movie''|2006|p=12}}<ref name="linernotes">{{cite AV media notes |title=Little Miss Sunshine (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) |year=2006 |type=Vinyl [[liner notes]] |publisher=[[Lakeshore Records]] |id=MVD7635LP}}</ref> It was recorded and [[Mixing console|mixed]] by Brad Haehnel at Paramount Recording Studios in [[Los Angeles]].<ref name="linernotes" /> Performed by DeVotchKa, much of the music was adapted from their pre-existing songs, such as "How It Ends", which became "The Winner Is", "The Enemy Guns" and "You Love Me" from the album ''[[How It Ends (DeVotchKa album)|How It Ends]]'', and "La Llorona" from ''[[Una Volta]]''.

Directors Dayton and Faris were introduced to DeVotchKa's music after hearing the song "You Love Me" on Los Angeles' [[KCRW]] radio station.<ref name="CSMDeVotch">{{cite news|title=DeVotchKa's music is as offbeat as its name |last=Sullivan |first=Jim |work=[[The Christian Science Monitor]] |date=July 28, 2006 |url=https://www.csmonitor.com/2006/0728/p13s01-almp.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110524152726/http://www.csmonitor.com/2006/0728/p13s01-almp.html |archive-date=May 24, 2011 }}</ref> The directors were so impressed with the music that they purchased [[iPod]]s for cast members containing DeVotchKa albums.<ref name="RollStone">{{cite news|title=Devotchka: The Best Little Grammy-Nominated Band You've Never Heard Of |last=Hoard |first=Christian |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=February 7, 2007 |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/devotchka-the-best-little-grammy-nominated-band-youve-never-heard-of-20070207 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111202050640/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/devotchka-the-best-little-grammy-nominated-band-youve-never-heard-of-20070207 |archive-date=December 2, 2011 |url-status=live }}</ref> Mychael Danna was brought in to help arrange the pre-existing material and collaborate with DeVotchKa on new material for the film. The ''Little Miss Sunshine'' score was not eligible for Academy Award consideration due to the percentage of material derived from already written DeVotchKa songs.<ref name="78AARules">{{cite web|url=http://www.oscars.org/78academyawards/rules/index_text.html|title=78th Academy Awards Rules|publisher=[[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070415204342/http://www.oscars.org/78academyawards/rules/index_text.html|archive-date=April 15, 2007}}</ref> The DeVotchka song "Til the End of Time" received a nomination for a [[Satellite Awards 2006|2006 Satellite Award]] as "Best Original Song".<ref name="InPreAcad">{{cite news|title=The 11th Annual Satellite Awards Nominees |publisher=[[International Press Academy]] |url=http://www.pressacademy.com/satawards/ipa-sa-nominees.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101128053901/http://pressacademy.com/satawards/ipa-sa-nominees.html |archive-date=November 28, 2010 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Both DeVotchKa and Danna received [[Nominees for Grammy Awards of 2007|2007 Grammy nominations]] for their work on the soundtrack.<ref name="RollStone"/>

==Release==
===Sundance Film Festival===
[[File:Sundance-Film-Festival.jpg|alt=|upright|thumb|The film debuted at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] in Utah]]
''Little Miss Sunshine'' premiered at the [[2006 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 20, 2006. An hour after its first screening began, sales agent [[John Sloss]] of [[Cinetic Media]] also arranged screenings for studio heads at [[New Line Cinema]] and [[Paramount Pictures]]. Multiple studios showed immense interest in the film following its premiere: [[Focus Features]] (where the film had once been in development), [[Paramount Classics]], [[the Weinstein Company]], [[Miramax|Miramax Films]], [[Summit Entertainment]], and [[Warner Independent Pictures]] were among those that bid for distribution rights. Minutes after the first screening ended, [[20th Century Fox]] chairman [[Tom Rothman]] informed producer [[David T. Friendly]] via telephone that, despite Rothman not having seen the film yet, he and [[Fox Searchlight Pictures]] head [[Peter Rice (executive)|Peter Rice]] were very eager to acquire it.<ref>{{cite web|title=End of story: a record deal|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|first=John|last=Horn|date=January 23, 2006|access-date=April 28, 2026|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2006-jan-23-et-deals23-story.html|archive-date=May 17, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260517170010/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2006-jan-23-et-deals23-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> By sunrise on January 21, after hours of intense bargaining overnight, Fox Searchlight had acquired worldwide rights to the film, offering $10.5 million, plus 10% of all the gross revenues.<ref name="NYTSmallFilm"/> The deal occurred less than a day after the premiere and was one of the biggest deals made in the history of the festival; the previous year's festival had the film ''[[Hustle & Flow]]'' receive $9&nbsp;million from Paramount Classics, and in 1999, ''[[Happy, Texas (film)|Happy, Texas]]'' received $10.25&nbsp;million from Miramax Films.<ref name="NYTDealHistory">{{cite news|title=Sundance Shines Light on Changes in Market |last=Halbfinger |first=David |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=January 28, 2006 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2006/01/28/movies/MoviesFeatures/28sund.html?_r=1&pagewanted=all |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220627040518/http://www.nytimes.com/2006/01/28/movies/MoviesFeatures/28sund.html?_r=1&pagewanted=all |archive-date=June 27, 2022 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="FilmFestival">{{cite news |title=First Major Acquisitions Deal Announced |last=Mandelberger |first=Sandy |publisher=FilmFestivals.com |url=http://www.filmfestivalstv.com/sundance/2006/01/first_major_acq.html#more |date=January 22, 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110523202037/http://www.filmfestivalstv.com/sundance/2006/01/first_major_acq.html#more |archive-date=May 23, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref name="FilmFestival2">{{cite news |title=Picking up hot numbers in Park City |last=Chatelin |first=Bruno |publisher=FilmFestivals.com |date=February 2, 2006 |url=http://www.filmfestivalstv.com/sundance/2006/02/picking_up_hot_.html#more |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110120222405/http://www.filmfestivalstv.com/sundance/2006/02/picking_up_hot_.html#more |archive-date=January 20, 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Little Miss Sunshine'' received a 91% positive aggregate rating, based on 218 reviews, with an average rating of 7.8/10. ====Number of producers controversy in Academy Awards====
There was some controversy concerning how many [[Film producer|producers]] should receive an award from the [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]] for their work in producing the film. In 1999, the academy decided that up to a maximum of three producers are eligible to be included in an award for a film.<ref name="VarietyQuotes">{{cite news|title=Credits flap darkens ''Sunshine'' |last=Laporte |first=Nicole |author2=McClintock, Pamela |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=February 17, 2007 |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/news/credits-flap-darkens-sunshine-1117959714/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121114014609/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117959714?refCatId=13 |archive-date=November 14, 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> The rule was implemented to prevent numerous involved filmmakers to appear on stage when a film was receiving an award.<ref name="NYTRelax">{{cite news|title=Oscar Rules Regarding Producing Are Relaxed |last=Cieply |first=Michael |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=June 14, 2007 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/06/14/movies/14acad.html?_r=2&ref=movies&oref=slogin |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230213114725/https://www.nytimes.com/2007/06/14/movies/14acad.html?_r=2&ref=movies&oref=slogin |archive-date=February 13, 2023 |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[Producers Guild of America]] (PGA) has not set a limit of producers that can be honored for a film.<ref name="LATRiles">{{cite news |title=Academy rule riles producers |last=Goldstein |first=Patrick |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=February 27, 2007 |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/env-et-gold20feb20,1,2790699.story |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121026104834/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/news/env-et-gold20feb20,1,2790699.story |archive-date=October 26, 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In the case of ''Little Miss Sunshine'', there were five producers (Marc Turtletaub, Peter Saraf, Albert Berger, Ron Yerxa, and [[David Friendly]]) and the academy did not want to include Berger and Yerxa. The two producers were responsible for finding the script, introducing the directors to the other producers, choosing the cinematographer, assisting in the re-shoot of the ending, and helping bring the film to the Sundance Film Festival.<ref name="LATimesProducers"/><ref name="HRRule">{{cite news|title=Oscars' rule of 3 cuts Grey, Berger, Yerxa|last=Kilday|first=Gregg|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=January 27, 2007|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/news/e3i9467b31373bcdd67d07f3de7b70d59a0|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070402164429/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/news/e3i9467b31373bcdd67d07f3de7b70d59a0|archive-date=April 2, 2007}}</ref> The academy acknowledged that the two were partners in the production process, but declared that only individual producers are recognized by the academy. Deeming the two producers' work as a collective effort, the academy refused to consider either Berger or Yerxa for the award.<ref name="VarietyQuotes"/> Producer David Hoberman commented on the support for honoring all five producers, stating "If there are five people actually involved in producing a movie, there's no reason why someone who's made a good enough film to be nominated for an Academy Award should be precluded from being rewarded for the work they did."<ref name="VarietyQuotes"/> Film producer and author [[Lynda Obst]] who was affiliated with an Academy Award producer committee, also commented: "By and large, five people don't make a movie. If this is an exception, then it's a sad situation. But you don't destroy a rule for an exception."<ref name="VarietyQuotes"/>

The PGA had previously honored all five of the producers.<ref name="VarietyOver">{{cite news|title=Academy bends on 'rule of three' |last=McNary |first=Dave |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=December 14, 2007 |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/awards/academy-bends-on-rule-of-three-1117977761/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121025041940/http://www.variety.com/awardcentral_article/VR1117977761.html?nav=news&categoryid=1985&cs=1 |archive-date=October 25, 2012 |url-status=live }}</ref> Albert Berger, reacting to the academy's decision while at a panel for the film, stated "No matter what the Academy decided, we produced this movie."<ref name="LATimesProducers"/> In June 2007, the academy announced that they would allow exceptions for films that had more than three producers in the future, stating, "The committee has the right, in what it determines to be a rare and extraordinary circumstance, to name any additional qualified producer as a nominee."<ref name="VarietyOver"/>
