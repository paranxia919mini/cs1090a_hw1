'''''Past Lives''''' is a 2023 [[Romance film|romantic]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] written and directed by [[Celine Song]] in her [[feature directorial debut]]. Starring [[Greta Lee]], [[Teo Yoo]], and [[John Magaro]], it follows two childhood friends over the course of 24 years while they contemplate the nature of their relationship as they grow apart, living different lives. The plot is [[semi-autobiographical]] and inspired by real events from Song's life.<ref name="PressNotes">{{Cite web |title=Press Notes |url=https://cdn.sanity.io/files/xq1bjtf4/production/c2a1249106e176671c2b8563c1e23a41d45d1a37.pdf?dl=Press%20Notes.pdf |publisher=A24 |access-date=January 23, 2023}}</ref>

''Past Lives'' premiered at the [[2023 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 21, 2023, and was released by [[A24]] in the United States on June 2 and by [[CJ ENM]] in South Korea on a [[limited theatrical release|limited release]] on February 26, 2024, and a [[wide release]] on March 6. The film received critical acclaim and grossed $42.7 million worldwide.

It was named one of the top ten films of 2023 by the [[National Board of Review]] and the [[American Film Institute]], receiving multiple accolades, with five nominations at the [[81st Golden Globe Awards]] including for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama|Best Motion Picture - Drama]] and nominations at the [[96th Academy Awards]] for [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] and [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]]. Since its release, it has been included in lists of the best films of the 2020s and the 21st century.<ref name="Times"/><ref name="Foreman & Erbland 2024"/><ref name="Singer 2025"/>

== Plot ==
In [[Seoul]], [[South Korea]], in 1999, Na Young and Hae Sung are 12-year-old classmates who develop feelings for each other and go on a playdate set up by their parents. Shortly thereafter, Na Young's family immigrates to [[Canada]] and the two lose contact. Na Young changes her name to Nora.

In 2011, Hae Sung has finished his [[Conscription in South Korea|military service]] and Nora has moved to [[New York City]]. While calling her mother, Nora discovers on [[Facebook]] that Hae Sung had posted that he was looking for Na Young, unaware of her name change. They reconnect through video calls but are unable to visit each other, as Nora is about to attend a writer's retreat in [[Montauk, New York|Montauk]] and Hae Sung is moving to China for a Mandarin language exchange. Eventually, Nora tells Hae Sung they should stop talking for a while, as she wants to focus on her writing and life in New York.

At her retreat, Nora meets Arthur Zaturansky and they fall in love, at one point discussing the [[Korean Buddhism|Buddhist]]-derived concept of {{Transliteration|ko|inyeon}} ({{lang|ko|인연}}, [[Pratītyasamutpāda|dependent origination]]), how a relationship between two souls in the present life is influenced by interactions in thousands of past lives. Hae Sung also meets a woman, whom he begins to date.

In 2023, Arthur and Nora are married and living in New York. Hae Sung, no longer with his girlfriend, travels to visit Nora. Arthur wonders if he is a roadblock in their love story, suggesting to Nora that, if another man with similar appeal had met her at the same time, Nora would have married him to secure a [[green card]] for U.S. residency. He also expresses anxiety about cultural barriers in their relationship, highlighting Nora's habit of sleep-talking in Korean: "you dream in a language that I can't understand." Nora reassures Arthur that she loves him.

The next night, the three go out to a speakeasy. Initially, Nora translates between the two men, but eventually speaks with Hae Sung exclusively in Korean. He wonders what they were to each other in their past lives, and what would have happened if she had never left Korea and they had continued their relationship. When Nora goes to the bathroom, Hae Sung apologizes to Arthur for speaking alone with Nora, but Arthur says he is glad to have met him.

They return to Arthur and Nora's apartment. Hae Sung invites them to visit him in Korea and calls an [[Uber]]. Nora waits with him, and the two exchange long looks at each other until the car arrives. Hae Sung says that perhaps they are, at that moment, experiencing a past life, and asks Nora what their relationship will be in their next life. She says she does not know. Hae Sung says, "I'll see you then." He leaves in the car and she walks back to her apartment, crying into Arthur's arms.

== Production ==
In January 2020, it was announced [[Choi Woo-shik]] would star in the film, with [[Celine Song]] set to direct from a screenplay she wrote, and [[A24]] set to produce and distribute alongside [[Killer Films]] and [[CJ ENM]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Sneider |first=Jeff |date=January 22, 2020 |title=Exclusive: 'Parasite's Choi Woo-sik to Star in A24's Korean Romance 'Past Lives' |url=https://collider.com/parasite-star-choi-woo-sik-past-lives-a24-movie/ |website=Collider |language=en-US |access-date=January 11, 2023}}</ref>

=== Casting ===
[[Greta Lee]] auditioned for the role of Nora Moon after receiving an email with the subject line "Korean: Do you speak it?" but she was rejected originally for being too old. Lee described not getting the role as like a "bad breakup".<ref name="Mier 2023">{{cite web |last1=Mier |first1=Tomás |date=December 26, 2023 |title=Greta Lee First Got Rejected from 'Past Lives' Casting. Then They Wanted Someone 'Older' |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/greta-lee-past-lives-casting-1234936503/ |website=Rolling Stone |language=en-US |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> Song was "stuck on thinking" that Nora "had to be someone in their late 20s".<ref name="Fear 2023">{{cite web |last1=Fear |first1=David |date=June 25, 2023 |title='Past Lives': A Tour of New York City With Greta Lee and Celine Song |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/past-lives-greta-lee-celine-song-new-york-city-tour-1234773016/ |url-access=subscription |website=Rolling Stone |language=en-US |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> Around a year later, Song contacted Lee after the casting department changed direction and began looking for an older actor for the role, something that opened Song's options for casting the lead.<ref name="Mier 2023"/><ref name="Fear 2023"/> She sent her an email asking "Are you free right now?" with a [[Zoom (software)|Zoom]] link.<ref name="Fear 2023"/> Without time to prepare, Lee took the meeting with Song where she read scenes and was offered the role the same day.<ref name="Mier 2023"/> Lee was announced as part of the cast in August 2021 alongside [[Teo Yoo]] and [[John Magaro]] who joined the cast of the film, with Yoo replacing Choi.<ref>{{cite web |last=Kroll |first=Justin |date=August 2, 2021 |title=Greta Lee & Teo Yoo Set To Star In A24's 'Past Lives' |url=https://deadline.com/2021/08/greta-lee-and-teo-yoo-a24-past-lives-1234807691/ |website=Deadline |language=en-US |access-date=January 11, 2023}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
Song expressed her desire to cinematographer [[Shabier Kirchner]] to shoot ''Past Lives'' on [[35 mm movie film|35 mm film]] in order to create a film about the passage of time that felt timeless.<ref name="Kodak 2023">{{cite web |title=Kodak 35mm 3-perf film helped DP Shabier Kirchner bring radiance to Celine Song's debut movie 'Past Lives' |url=https://www.kodak.com/en/motion/blog-post/past-lives/ |website=Kodak |language=en-US |date=September 22, 2023 |access-date=March 26, 2024}}</ref> Producers were initially hesitant for Song as a first-time filmmaker to shoot on film.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/> Due to shooting on film, Song emphasized to actors what she wanted with rehearsals in order to shoot in as few takes as possible.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/> Kirchner shot ''Past Lives'' on 3-perf 35 mm film, opting for [[Kodak]] Vision3 500T 5219 [[film stock]] due to its versatility across lighting conditions in both interior and exterior scenes.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/> He used the [[Panavision]] [[Panavision cameras|Millennium XL2]] camera paired with Panavision PVintage and Ultra Speed [[Anamorphic format|anamorphic]] lenses.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Shachat |first1=Sarah |last2=Hemphill |first2=Jim |date=January 19, 2023 |title=Sundance 2023 Cinematography Survey: The Cameras and Lenses Behind 40 Narrative Films at the Fest |url=https://www.indiewire.com/feature/sundance-film-cameras-lens-arri-red-1234798122/ |website=IndieWire |language=en-US |access-date=June 11, 2023}}</ref> Kirchner cited the films ''[[Like Father, Like Son (2013 film)|Like Father, Like Son]]'' (2013) and ''[[Yi Yi]]'' (2000) as inspirational references for the cinematography style of ''Past Lives'' due to how they treated love and the economy respectively.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/> [[Principal photography]] took place during July and August 2021 at locations around New York, including the city's ferries, and under and along the [[Manhattan Bridge]]. The opening scene was shot at the Holiday Cocktail Lounge at 75 Saint Mark's Place in the [[East Village, Manhattan|East Village]].<ref name="Chevez 2024">{{cite web |last1=Chevez |first1=Samantha |date=February 8, 2024 |title=Where Past Lives was Filmed in NYC |url=https://www.untappedcities.com/past-lives-nyc-filming-locations/ |website=Untapped Cities |language=en-US |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> The bookstore scene where Nora meets her husband Arthur at a book signing was filmed at McNally Jackson Books in [[Williamsburg, Brooklyn]].<ref name="Chevez 2024"/> Nora and Hae Sung's reunion was filmed in [[Madison Square Park]] in front of the [[Statue of David Farragut (New York City)|Statue of David Farragut]].<ref name="Chevez 2024"/> The apartment sets for Hae Sung's and Nora's Skype conversations were built next to each other at Greenpoint Studios in [[Brooklyn]] where they filmed simultaneously. The carousel scene was filmed at [[Jane's Carousel]] in Brooklyn Bridge Park, Brooklyn. The production then moved to [[Seoul]] and filmed from late October to early November.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/>

=== Post-production ===
The 35 mm film [[Negative (photography)|negatives]] were [[Photographic processing|developed]] and processed at the Kodak Film Lab New York with [[4K resolution|4K]] digital scans done by Technicolor Postworks.<ref name="Kodak 2023"/> ''Past Lives'' was edited by Keith Fraase who joined the production in late 2021.<ref name="Loftus 2024">{{cite web |last1=Loftus |first1=Marc |date=February 21, 2024 |title=Oscars: ''Past Lives'' editor Keith Fraase |url=https://www.postmagazine.com/Press-Center/Daily-News/2024/Oscars-I-Past-Lives-I-editor-Keith-Fraase.aspx |website=Post Magazine |language=en-US |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> Fraase initially worked from his home in [[Westchester County, New York|Westchester]] editing [[dailies]] with Song joining each session.<ref name="Loftus 2024"/> Post-production moved to [[Company 3]] in New York once shooting had wrapped.<ref name="Loftus 2024"/>

=== Box office ===
''Past Lives'' grossed $11.3 million in the United States and Canada, and $31.4 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $42.7 million.<ref name="BOM">{{cite Box Office Mojo|id=13238346|title=Past Lives|access-date=March 3, 2025}}</ref>

The film grossed $232,266 from four theaters in its opening weekend, an average of $55,066 per venue.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Domestic 2023 Weekend 22 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2023W22/?ref_=bo_we_nav |website=Box Office Mojo |language=en-US |access-date=June 4, 2023}}</ref> By its sixth weekend, the film had a running total of $8.4 million.<ref>{{cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=July 9, 2023 |title='Insidious: The Red Door' Slams On 'Indiana Jones' With $32M+ Opening – Sunday AM Box Office Update |url=https://deadline.com/2023/07/box-office-insidious-the-red-door-indiana-jones-1235431682/ |website=Deadline |language=en-US |access-date=July 9, 2023}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
<!--UPDATE the access-date of the source that you used when you update these figures.-->{{RT prose |score=95 |count=319 |ref=yes |access-date=September 4, 2025 |consensus=A remarkable debut for writer-director Celine Song, ''Past Lives'' uses the bonds between its sensitively sketched central characters to support trenchant observations on the human condition.}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 94 out of 100, based on 52 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Past Lives'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/past-lives |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=March 8, 2024 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

Manola Darghis, in ''[[The New York Times]]'', compared the film to French romantic cinema, complimenting its restraint in the presentation of its main themes, and writing: "The movie's modesty—its intimacy, human scale, humble locations and lack of visual oomph—is one of its strengths. The characters live in homes that are pleasant yet ordinary, the kind that you can imagine hanging out in, the kind you want to hang out in. There are few big, look-at-me details, though you might notice a poster for [[Jacques Rivette]]’s 1974 classic ''[[Céline and Julie Go Boating]]'' in Nora’s father’s home office in Seoul."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Dargis |first=Manohla |author-link=Manohla Dargis |date=June 1, 2023 |title='Past Lives' Review: Longing for a Future |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2023/06/01/movies/past-lives-review.html |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331 |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> In ''[[The Guardian]]'', [[Peter Bradshaw]] praised the film highly, referencing existing comparisons to the works of [[Richard Linklater]], [[Noah Baumbach]], and [[Greta Gerwig]], and himself likening the film to [[Wong Kar-wai]]'s "[[In the Mood for Love]]". He wrote: "''Past Lives'' is a glorious date movie, and a movie for every occasion, too. As ever with films like this, there is an auxiliary pleasure in wondering how much of her own past life Song has used. It's a must-see."<ref name="Bradshaw 2023">{{cite web |last=Bradshaw |first=Peter |author-link=Peter Bradshaw |date=September 6, 2023 |title=Past Lives review – a must-see story of lost loves, childhood crushes and changing identities |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2023/sep/06/past-lives-review-a-must-see-story-of-lost-loves-childhood-crushes-and-changing-identities |website=The Guardian |language=en-GB |access-date=September 14, 2023}}</ref>

Filmmaker [[Daniels (directors)|Daniel Scheinert]], who co-directed A24's ''[[Everything Everywhere All at Once]]'' with Daniel Kwan, praised the film, saying: "It's remarkable, the way [Celine Song] pushed past the story of 'picking mister right' and the story of 'fighting to win the girl' and somehow brought her audience to this painfully relatable heartbreak on the other side of those stories. I'm inspired by it. And mystified by how the filmmaking team did it. But when I was lucky enough to see a Q&A and have some brunch with Celine, I saw how curious and thoughtful and passionate and charmingly bossy she was, and I was instantly convinced that this movie was no accident. It's a smart, confident, unique poem because Celine is a smart, confident, unique poet."<ref>{{cite web |date=December 13, 2023 |title=16 Directors Praise 2023's Best Films: Alfonso Cuarón on 'Killers of the Flower Moon,' Jane Campion on 'Priscilla' and More |url=https://variety.com/lists/directors-best-films-2023/past-lives-2/ |website=Variety |language=en-US |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref> Filmmaker [[Christopher Nolan]] also praised the film and named it one of his favorites, calling it "subtle in a beautiful sort of way".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Kluger |first=Jeffrey |date=January 29, 2024 |title=How Christopher Nolan Found Intimacy on a Grand Scale |url=https://time.com/6589108/christopher-nolan-oppenheimer-oscars-interview/ |magazine=Time |language=en-US |access-date=January 31, 2024 |archive-date=March 6, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240306180405/https://time.com/6589108/christopher-nolan-oppenheimer-oscars-interview/ |url-status=dead}}</ref> Other filmmakers, such as [[Allison Anders]], [[Reinaldo Marcus Green]], [[Andrew Haigh]], [[Rich Peppiatt]], and [[Zoe Lister-Jones]] cited it as among their favorite films of 2023.<ref>{{cite web |date=December 31, 2023 |title=38 Directors Pick Their Favorite Films of 2023 |url=https://www.indiewire.com/gallery/directors-best-movies-tv-2023|website=IndieWire}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Kneecap Director Rich Peppiatt Top Ten Feature Films of 2024 |url=https://directorsnotes.com/2024/12/30/rich-peppiatt-top-ten-2024/ |website=Director's Notes |date=December 30, 2024 |access-date=September 4, 2026}}</ref>
