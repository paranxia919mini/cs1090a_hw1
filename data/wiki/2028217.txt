'''''Inland Empire''''' is a 2006 <!--DO NOT ADD GENRES HERE — see the discussion on the talk page and get consensus first--> film written, directed, shot, edited, and scored by [[David Lynch]], who produced it alongside [[Mary Sweeney]]. The film stars [[Laura Dern]] as a [[Hollywood cinema|Hollywood]] actress who begins to take on the personality of a character she plays in a supposedly cursed film production, causing fragmented and nightmarish events to unfold. A co-production between the United States, France, and Poland, ''Inland Empire'' takes its name from [[Inland Empire|a Californian metropolitan area]]. It was completed over a three-year period, shooting primarily in [[Los Angeles]] and [[Łódź]]. The process marked several firsts for Lynch: the film was shot without a finished screenplay, instead being largely developed on a scene-by-scene basis, and was filmed entirely by Lynch on low-resolution [[digital video]] with a handheld Sony DSR-PD150 camcorder.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-11-25 |title=Attack of the zeros and ones: the early years of digital cinema, as told by David Lynch, Miranda July, Michael Mann and more |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/attack-zeros-ones-early-years-digital-cinema-told-david-lynch-miranda-july-michael-mann-more |access-date=2026-02-18 |website=BFI |language=en}}</ref>

The cast includes [[List of frequent David Lynch collaborators|frequent Lynch collaborators]] such as Dern, [[Justin Theroux]], [[Harry Dean Stanton]], and [[Grace Zabriskie]], as well as [[Jeremy Irons]], [[Karolina Gruszka]], [[Peter J. Lucas]], [[Krzysztof Majchrzak]], and [[Julia Ormond]]. Brief appearances are made by additional actors including [[Terry Crews]], [[Laura Harring]], [[Nastassja Kinski]], [[Diane Ladd]], [[William H. Macy]], and [[Mary Steenburgen]]. The voices of Harring, [[Naomi Watts]], and [[Scott Coffey]] are also heard in scenes where characters watch excerpts of Lynch's short online horror series ''[[Rabbits (web series)|Rabbits]]'' (2002). The soundtrack features a variety of musicians such as [[Beck]], [[Dave Brubeck]], [[Kroke]], [[Krzysztof Penderecki]], and [[Nina Simone]].

==Plot==

=== Prologue ===
A crackling vinyl record announces that "Axxon N., the longest-running radio play in history", is turning to "a gray winter day in an old hotel". In a hotel room, the Lost Girl—a young Polish prostitute—has an unpleasant encounter with a client. Her face is blurred. After the encounter, her face becomes clear. She turns on the television and goes [[channel surfing]], seeing an old Eastern European woman approaching a mansion before she flips to [[Rabbits (film)|a show about anthropomorphic rabbits]]. She begins to cry.

=== Narrative ===
In Los Angeles, actress Nikki Grace is waiting for the results of her audition for the lead role in the film ''On High in Blue Tomorrows'', a romantic drama about two people who have an affair. The old woman from the television screen visits her mansion. She asks Nikki about the film and then tells "an old tale": a boy passed through the doorway into the world, causing a reflection that gave birth to an evil that followed him. Then she tells a variation: a girl was lost in the marketplace—"as if half-born"—while the alley behind the marketplace was the way to the palace. The woman is certain that Nikki will get the role and insists that despite Nikki's claims to the contrary, the plot involves murder.

The next day, Nikki learns that she got the part. She celebrates with her friends while her Polish husband Piotrek watches from a distance. Devon, who plays the male lead, is warned to not sleep with Nikki, as he has a history of on-set affairs. Piotrek warns Devon of "dark consequences" for "wrong actions".

During the first rehearsal, the actors are interrupted by a disturbance on the set. Devon investigates but finds nothing. Shaken, the director confesses that they are shooting a remake of an unfinished German film entitled ''47'', itself based on a supposedly cursed Polish folktale. Production was abandoned after the two leads were found murdered "inside the story".

After filming the first few romantic scenes between their respective characters Sue and Billy, Nikki starts having difficulty distinguishing between the film script and real life. As in the script, Nikki starts a romantic affair with Devon. While they make love, she compares it to the script.

Nikki walks into an alley and enters a door named "Axxon N." It leads to the ''On High in Blue Tomorrows'' set, but also takes her back in time. Nikki realizes that she was the disturbance Devon investigated during the first rehearsal. She runs away and enters a set piece, which turns into an actual house. Inside the house, Nikki sees her husband going to bed. She hides from him in a closet, which transports her to a living room inhabited by a troupe of prostitutes.

=== Stream of consciousness ===
At this point, the narrative disintegrates into various plotlines and scenes, with the chronological order and the distinction between characters unclear or left to associations constructed by the audience.

* Nikki meets the prostitutes as they solicit customers on [[Hollywood Boulevard]], and declares, "I'm a whore";
* A woman tells a policeman that she had been hypnotized to kill someone, and reveals that she has been stabbed in the stomach with a screwdriver;
* In 1930s [[Łódź]], Poland, prostitutes interact with pimps;
* Nikki is stuck in a physically abusive marriage with her husband "Smithy" (played by the same actor as Piotrek). They live in a working-class home possibly located in Southern California's [[Inland Empire]], which Polish men periodically visit;
* In the film, Sue confronts Billy in front of his family, professing her love. Doris, Billy's wife (played by the same actress as the woman with the screwdriver) repeatedly slaps her;
* In a room above a nightclub, Nikki/Sue tells a bespectacled man that she was [[Child sexual abuse|sexually abused in her childhood]] and that her husband joined a traveling circus from Poland as a gamekeeper. She also speaks of the Phantom, a hypnotist who worked at the circus and then disappeared;
* The woman with the screwdriver remembers that the Phantom hypnotized her to kill Sue.
* Recalling the first meeting with the old woman, a 2nd visitor (played by  [[Mary Steenburgen]]) tells Nikki/Sue that their family owes a debt to a neighbor called Crimp. Nikki/Sue has an unsettling encounter with him (played by same actor for the Phantom).

Feeling stalked by the Phantom, Nikki/Sue arms herself with a screwdriver. She sees both herself and Doris on Hollywood Boulevard, and meets with the policeman above the nightclub. When she leaves, Doris stabs her with her own screwdriver. Nikki/Sue collapses at a bus stop next to three homeless people. A homeless woman tells all kinds of strange stories about her friend Niko and her pet monkey. Another holds a lighter in front of Sue's face until she dies. A crane shot reveals that Nikki is shooting her final scene of the film.

=== Climax ===
In a daze, Nikki wanders off set and into a nearby movie theater, where she sees not only ''On High in Blue Tomorrows'' but events occurring in real-time. She follows the bespectacled man upstairs and enters an apartment marked "Axxon N". Confronted by the Phantom, Nikki shoots him. The Phantom transforms into a grotesque figure with a face resembling Nikki's before dying. Nikki flees into Room 47, which houses the rabbits on television – though she fails to see them – and then meets the Lost Girl. The two women kiss and embrace, and Nikki fades away. The Lost Girl escapes from the hotel and into Smithy's house, where she happily embraces her husband and son. Nikki is back at her mansion.

The film ends with a celebration involving the troupe of prostitutes, a one-legged woman mentioned earlier, Niko and her pet monkey, and others. The women dance to [[Nina Simone]]'s "[[Sinner Man#Nina Simone recording|Sinnerman]]", while a lumberjack saws a log.

==Themes and analysis==
{{quote box|width=40%|align=right|bgcolor=#c6dbf7|quote=[T]he structure of ''Inland Empire'' differs from prior Lynch films, ''[[Lost Highway (film)|Lost Highway]]'' or ''[[Mulholland Drive (film)|Mulholland Drive]]''. It is neither a [[Möbius strip]] that endlessly circles around itself, nor is it divisible into sections of fantasy and reality. Its structure is more akin to a web where individual moments hyperlink to each other and other [[David Lynch filmography|Lynch films]]—hence the musical number that closes the film which contains obvious allusions to everything from ''[[Blue Velvet (film)|Blue Velvet]]'' to ''[[Twin Peaks]]''.|source=Zoran Samardžija, 2010<ref>{{cite journal|last=Samardžija |first=Zoran |date=February 2010 |url=https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/scope/documents/2010/february-2010/samardzija.pdf |title=DavidLynch.com: Auteurship in the Age of the Internet and Digital Cinema |journal=Scope: An Online Journal of Film and Television Studies |number=16 |access-date=2023-01-12}}</ref>}}

When asked about ''Inland Empire'', Lynch refrained from explaining the film, responding that it is "about a woman in trouble, and it's a mystery, and that's all I want to say about it."<ref name="wfaa">{{cite news |work=[[Riverside Press-Enterprise]] |title=David Lynch turns his eye to 'Inland Empire' |first=Helene |last=Blatter |url=http://www.wfaa.com/sharedcontent/dws/ent/stories/090406dnGLempire.64579170.html |date=3 September 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928045413/http://www.wfaa.com/sharedcontent/dws/ent/stories/090406dnGLempire.64579170.html |archive-date=28 September 2007}}</ref> When presenting screenings of the digital work, Lynch sometimes opened with a loosely-translated version of the ''[[Mundaka Upanishad]]'':

<blockquote>We are like the spider. We weave our life and then move along in it. We are like the dreamer who dreams and then lives in the dream. This is true for the entire universe.<ref>{{cite news |last=Guillen |first=Michael |date=24 January 2007 |title=Inland Empire—The San Rafael Film Center Q&A With David Lynch |work=[[Twitch Film]] |url=http://twitchfilm.net/archives/008819.html |access-date=21 April 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080907005745/http://www.twitchfilm.net/archives/008819.html |archive-date=7 September 2008}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|last1=Egenes |first1=Thomas |last2=Reddy |first2=Kumuda |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=BcDlPTSbEoEC&pg=PA71 |title=Eternal Stories from the Upanishads |via=[[Google Books]] |location=[[New Delhi]] |publisher=Smrti Books |year=2002 |page=71 |isbn=978-8-18-796707-1}}</ref></blockquote>

Lynch's ''[[Darkened Room]]'' has been analyzed by Kristina Šekrst as a precursor to ''Inland Empire'', creating the Lost Girl motif, along with sharing the same symbolism of the cigarette-burn hole in a silk slip and the watch.<ref name=sekrst>{{cite book|last=Šekrst|first=Kristina|chapter=Narrativism and Performativity in Absurda and Darkened Room|editor=Andrew M. Winters|publisher=Lexington Books|year=2024|title=A Critical Companion to David Lynch|url=https://philarchive.org/rec/SEKNAP}}</ref> [[New York Film Festival]] official Richard Peña summarized the film as "a plotless collection of snippets that explore themes Lynch has been working on for years", including "a [[Hollywood (industry)|Hollywood]] story about a young actress who gets a part in a film that might be cursed; a story about the smuggling of women from [[Eastern Europe]]; and an abstract story about a family of people with rabbit heads sitting around in a [[living room]]."<ref name="wfaa"/> ''[[The Guardian]]'' critic [[Peter Bradshaw]] called the film "a meditation on the unacknowledged and unnoticed strangeness of Hollywood and [[movie making|movie-making]] in general", adding that Lynch "establishes a bizarre series of worm-holes between the worlds of myth, movies and reality."<ref>{{cite news|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|author-link=Peter Bradshaw|title=Inland Empire – Film Review|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2007/mar/09/thriller|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=25 February 2018|date=2007-03-09}}</ref> Critic [[Mark Fisher (theorist)|Mark Fisher]] wrote that the film "often seems like a series of [[dream sequence]]s floating free of any grounding reality, a dream without a dreamer [in which] no frame is secure", but argued that "it is the ''film'' that is mad, not the characters in it&nbsp;... it is Hollywood itself that is dreaming".<ref name="fisher">{{cite book|last=Fisher|first=Mark|author-link=Mark Fisher (theorist)|title=The Weird and the Eerie|date=2017|publisher=Repeater Books|url=http://k-punk.abstractdynamics.org/archives/009285.html|access-date=27 February 2018|isbn=978-1-91-092438-9}}</ref> He also commented that "to see Lynch's worlds captured on digital video makes for a bizarre short-circuiting: as if we are witnessing a direct feed from the unconscious".<ref name="fisher"/>

[[Dennis Lim (film curator)|Dennis Lim]] of ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' described the film as "a three-hour waking nightmare that derives both its form and its content from the splintering psyche of a troubled Hollywood actress", and commented on Lynch's use of digital video, describing it as "the medium of [[home movie]]s, [[viral video]], and [[pornography]]—the everyday media detritus we associate more with&nbsp;... intimate or private viewing experiences than communal ones", adding that the film "progresses with the darting, associative logic of [[hyperlink]]s".<ref name=Slate>{{cite journal|last=Lim|first=Dennis|author-link=Dennis Lim (film curator)|title=David Lynch Goes Digital: Inland Empire on DVD|url=http://www.slate.com/articles/arts/dvdextras/2007/08/david_lynch_goes_digital.html|journal=[[Slate Magazine]]|access-date=25 February 2018|date=2007-08-23}}</ref> Scholar Anne Jerslev has argued that the film "constitutes multiple and fractured modes of perception in a world of digital screens".<ref name=jerslev/> Jerslev further contends that the film features "formal similarities with a [[website]]'s hyperlinked layering of screens/windows, constantly disclosing new worlds from new points of view", but according to theorist [[Steven Shaviro]] "it also builds on cinematic codes, even as it [[deconstruction|deconstructs]] them".<ref name=jerslev>{{cite journal|last=Jerslev|first=Anne|title=The post-perspectival: screens and time in David Lynch's Inland Empire|journal=Journal of Aesthetics & Culture|date=21 March 2012|volume=4|issue=1|article-number=17298|doi=10.3402/jac.v4i0.17298|doi-access=free}}</ref>

David Sweeney interprets ''Inland Empire'' as a film that is, at least thematically, about [[reincarnation]]. He connects this theme to Lynch's interest in [[Theosophy]] and draws parallels with [[James Joyce]]'s ''[[Ulysses (novel)|Ulysses]]'' and the [[Netflix]] series ''[[The OA]]'', highlighting shared motifs of reincarnation and alternate lives.<ref name=sweeney>{{cite book|last=Sweeney|first=David|chapter=Past and Possible Lives in Inland Empire|editor=Andrew M. Winters|publisher=[[Lexington Books]]|year=2024|title=A Critical Companion to David Lynch|isbn=978-1-66692-8105}}</ref>

The ''[[Austin Chronicle]]''<nowiki/>'s Marc Savlov wrote that ''Inland Empire'' "revels in a kind of [[post-9/11]] [[Dissociative disorder|disassociative disorder]] as perpetual panic attack".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Savlov |first=Marc |date=2009-12-25 |title=A Decade Under the Influence |url=https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/2009-12-25/931434/ |access-date=2025-02-05 |website=[[Austin Chronicle]] |language=en-US}}</ref> During an interview about ''[[Lost Highway (film)|Lost Highway]]'' (1997), Lynch was asked about whether he "ever had that feeling of [[Dissociation (psychology)|disassociation]] from yourself". He responded, "Oh, yeah. There's really many characters inside of us".<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Powell |first=Jeremy |date=2014 |title=David Lynch, Francis Bacon, Gilles Deleuze: The Cinematic Diagram and the Hall of Time |journal=Discourse |publisher=[[Project Muse]] |volume=36 |issue=3 |page=325 |doi=10.13110/discourse.36.3.0309 |jstor=10.13110/discourse.36.3.0309 |issn=1522-5321}}</ref>

==Production==
===Background===
''Inland Empire'' was the first Lynch feature to be completely shot in [[digital video]]; it was shot in [[Standard-definition television|standard definition]] with a hand-held [[Sony DSR-PD150]] by Lynch himself.<ref name=Perez/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Seibold |first=Witney |date=2022-11-13 |title=Why David Lynch Chose To Shoot Inland Empire Digitally |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1093854/why-david-lynch-chose-to-shoot-inland-empire-digitally/ |access-date=2023-01-12 |website=[[/Film]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Lynch stated that he would no longer use film to make motion pictures.<ref name=variety>{{cite news |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |first=Adam |last=Dawtrey |date=11 May 2005 |url=https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/lynch-invades-an-empire-1117922566/ |title=Lynch invades an 'Empire'; Digital pic details a mystery |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140110015512/http://variety.com/2005/film/news/lynch-invades-an-empire-1117922566/ |archive-date=10 January 2014}}</ref> He explained his preference, stating that the medium gives one "more room to dream", and more options in post-production.<ref name=Slate/> Much of the project was shot in [[Łódź]], Poland, with local actors, such as [[Karolina Gruszka]], [[Krzysztof Majchrzak]], [[Leon Niemczyk]], Piotr Andrzejewski and artists of the local circus Cyrk Zalewski. Some filming was also done in [[Los Angeles]], and Lynch returned from Poland in 2006 to complete it. He then edited the final results in [[Final Cut Pro]] in his home office over a six-month period.<ref name="post">{{cite web |last=Blair |first=Ian |date=February 1, 2007 |title=Director's Chair: David Lynch – 'Inland Empire' |url=http://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2007/February-1-2007/DIRECTORS-CHAIR-DAVID-LYNCH-INLAND-EMPIRE.aspx |access-date=19 March 2018 |website=[[Post Magazine]]}}</ref> He edited the film alone without input from his then-wife [[Mary Sweeney]], who often edited his films, which she attributed to the lack of "a real organized script to go by [...] no one knew what was going on except him".<ref name="post"/>

Lynch shot the film without a complete screenplay. Instead, he handed each actor several pages of freshly written dialogue each day.<ref name="grf4"/><ref name=Perez/> In a 2005 interview, he described his feelings about the shooting process:
"I've never worked on a project in this way before. I don't know exactly how this thing will finally unfold&nbsp;... This film is very different because I don't have a script. I write the thing scene by scene and much of it is shot and I don't have much of a clue where it will end. It's a risk, but I have this feeling that because all things are unified, this idea over here in that room will somehow relate to that idea over there in the pink room."<ref name="hwise">{{cite news |last1=Attwood |first1=Chris |last2=Roth |first2=Robert |date=September 2005 |title=A Dog's Trip to the Chocolate Shop – David Lynch |work=Healthy Wealthy N' Wise |url=http://www.healthywealthynwise.com/article.asp?Article=3062 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20051201160734/http://www.healthywealthynwise.com/article.asp?Article=3062 |archive-date=2005-12-01}}</ref>

Interviewed at the [[63rd Venice International Film Festival|Venice Film Festival]], Laura Dern admitted that she did not know what ''Inland Empire'' was about or the role she was playing, but hoped that seeing the film's premiere at the festival would help her "learn more".<ref name="grf4"/> Justin Theroux also stated, "I couldn't possibly tell you what the film's about, and at this point, I don't know that [David Lynch] could. It's become sort of a pastime—Laura and I sit around on set trying to figure out what's going on."<ref name="wfaa"/> In an [[NPR]] interview, Dern recounted a conversation she had with Jeremy Alter, one of the film's newer producers; he had asked her if Lynch was joking when he requested a one-legged woman, a monkey, and a lumberjack by 3:15 p.m. Dern replied, "Yeah, you're on a David Lynch movie, dude. Sit back and enjoy the ride." They began filming with the individuals Lynch had requested by 4 p.m.<ref name="npr">{{cite web |website=[[NPR]] |first=Andrea |last=Shea |date=17 December 2006 |url=https://www.npr.org/templates/story/story.php?storyId=6637676 |title=David Lynch's Latest Endeavor Breaks New Ground}}</ref>

===Financing and distribution===
Lynch financed much of the production from his own resources, with longtime artistic collaborator and ex-wife Mary Sweeney producing. The film was also partially financed by the French production company [[StudioCanal]], which had provided funding for three previous Lynch films. StudioCanal wanted to enter the film in the [[2006 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web |title=David Lynch continues work on DV project Inland Empire |url=https://movieweb.com/david-lynch-continues-work-on-dv-project-inland-empire/ |date=May 12, 2005 |url-status=live |website=[[MovieWeb]] |access-date=2023-01-12 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210512120141/https://movieweb.com/david-lynch-continues-work-on-dv-project-inland-empire/ |archive-date=2021-05-12}}</ref> Instead, it premiered at Italy's [[63rd Venice International Film Festival|Venice Film Festival]] on 6 September 2006, where David Lynch also received the [[Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement]] award for his "contributions to the art of cinema". The film premiered in the United States on 8 October 2006 at the [[New York Film Festival]].<ref name="NYFF">{{cite web |date=November 15, 2006 |title=David Lynch Announces Distribution Partnerships and Theatrical Release Dates for Inland Empire |url=http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/david-lynch-announces-distribution-partnerships-and-theatrical-release-dates-for-inland-empire-56396397.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304215520/http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/david-lynch-announces-distribution-partnerships-and-theatrical-release-dates-for-inland-empire-56396397.html |archive-date=March 4, 2016 |access-date=28 July 2015 |website=[[PR Newswire]]}}</ref> The film received a limited release in the US beginning on 15 December 2006; distribution was handled by the specialist company 518 Media.<ref>{{cite web |title=Inland Empire release details |url=http://www.comingsoon.net/films.php?id=17007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181106010541/https://www.comingsoon.net/movie/inland-empire-2006 |archive-date=November 6, 2018 |website=[[ComingSoon.net]]}}</ref>

Lynch hoped to distribute the film independently, saying that with the entire industry changing, he thought he would attempt a new form of distribution as well.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2006-10-10 |title=David Lynch to Self-Distribute Inland Empire |url=https://movieweb.com/david-lynch-to-self-distribute-inland-empire/ |access-date=2023-01-12 |website=[[MovieWeb]] |language=en}}</ref> He acquired the rights to the DVD and worked out a deal with StudioCanal in an arrangement that allowed him to distribute the film himself, through both digital and traditional means.<ref>{{cite web |last=Goldstein |first=Gregg |date=11 October 2006 |title=Filmmaker Lynch to self-distribute 'Inland Empire' |url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/search/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1003223922 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070922171752/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/search/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1003223922 |archive-date=2007-09-22 |access-date=2023-01-12 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]}}</ref> A North American DVD release occurred on 14 August 2007. Among other special features, the DVD included a 75-minute featurette, ''More Things That Happened'', which compiled footage elaborating on Sue's marriage to Smithy, her unpleasant life story, the Phantom's influence on women, and the lives of the prostitutes on Hollywood Boulevard. 15 years after the North American DVD release, [[The Criterion Collection]] released a two-disc Blu-ray on 21 March 2023.<ref name="DVDbeaver">{{cite web |last1=Tooze |first1=Gary |title=Inland Empire Blu-ray - Laura Dern |url=http://www.dvdbeaver.com/film2/DVDReviews32/inland_empire.htm |website=DVDbeaver |access-date=24 February 2025}}</ref>

==Release and reception==
===Distribution and box office===
The film was screened at several film festivals around the world, most notably the [[Venice Film Festival]], the [[New York Film Festival]], the [[Thessaloniki Film Festival]], the [[Camerimage|Camerimage Film Festival]], the [[Fajr International Film Festival]], the [[San Francisco Independent Film Festival|San Francisco IndieFest]], the [[International Film Festival Rotterdam]] and the [[Festival Internacional de Cine Contemporáneo de la Ciudad de México|Mexico City International Contemporary Film Festival]].

In October 2006, Lynch bought US distribution rights to the film from StudioCanal, which he would retain in any later deals made. The following month, he announced his intent to self-distribute the film theatrically via his company Absurda and 518 Media, stating: "A conventional distributor is a heartache, and I'm finished with that. With self-distribution, I'm able to shape the outcome of the film so much more." Lynch embarked on a 10-city promotional tour in January 2007 with a cow, explaining "I ate a lot of cheese during the film, and it made me happy. I'm looking forward to meeting theater owners and getting out among the people with the cow." Lynch signed a service deal with [[Rhino Entertainment]], which distributed the film on home video with [[Rykodisc|Ryko Distribution]] on 14 August 2007.<ref name="RYKO">{{cite web |title=Inland Empire |url=http://www.rykodistribution.com/artist_title.php?artist_id=10825&media_id=20361 |url-status=usurped |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070516221854/http://www.rykodistribution.com/artist_title.php?artist_id=10825&media_id=20361 |archive-date=16 May 2007 |website=[[Rykodisc|Ryko Distribution]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Lynch set to self-release 'Empire'|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|first=Gregg|last=Goldstein|date=16 November 2006|access-date=2 December 2023|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/lynch-set-release-empire-143369/}}</ref>

518 Media released ''Inland Empire'' to two theaters in the [[Cinema of the United States|United States]] on 6 December 2006, grossing a total of $27,508 over its opening weekend. It later expanded to its widest release of fifteen nationwide theaters, ultimately grossing $861,355 at the American box office. In other countries outside the United States, ''Inland Empire'' grossed $3,176,222, bringing the film's worldwide total gross to $4,037,577.<ref name=boxofficemojo/> It was released on 20 August 2007 in the [[Cinema of the United Kingdom|United Kingdom]], by [[StudioCanal UK|Optimum Releasing]],<ref name=Play.com>{{cite web |url=http://www.play.com/DVD/DVD/4-/3229343/-/Product.html?searchstring=inland+empire&searchsource=0 |title=Play.com – Buy Inland Empire |website=[[Play.com]] |access-date=13 August 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110521152214/http://www.play.com/DVD/DVD/4-/3229343/-/Product.html?searchstring=inland+empire&searchsource=0 |archive-date=21 May 2011}}</ref> 4 October 2007 in the Benelux, by [[A-Film]],<ref name="A-Film">{{cite web|url=http://www.a-film.be/dvd.php?id=00015231 |website=[[A-Film]] |title=Details of BE & NL DVD release |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070929084421/http://www.a-film.be/dvd.php?id=00015231 |archive-date=29 September 2007}}</ref> and 6 August 2008 in [[Cinema of Australia|Australia]], by [[Madman Entertainment]].<ref name="Madman">{{cite web |title=Inland Empire |url=http://www.madman.com.au/actions/catalogue.do?releaseId=8532&method=view |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121231013516/http://www.madman.com.au/actions/catalogue.do?releaseId=8532&method=view |archive-date=2012-12-31 |access-date=13 August 2012 |website=[[Madman Entertainment]]}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 72% of 118 critics gave the film a positive review, with an average rating of 7.6/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "Typical David Lynch fare: fans of the director will find ''Inland Empire'' seductive and deep. All others will consider the heady surrealism impenetrable and pointless."<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes" /> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 73 out of 100, based on 25 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/inland-empire|title=Inland Empire – Reviews, Ratings, Credits and More|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=5 May 2020}}</ref>

[[Manohla Dargis]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' classified ''Inland Empire'' as "fitfully brilliant" after the New York Film Festival screening.<ref>{{cite news |last=Dargis |first=Manohla |date=2006-10-06 |title=At the New York Film Festival, a Global Glimpse of the State of the Cinema |language=en-US |work=[[The New York Times]] |url-access=subscription |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2006/10/06/movies/06fest.html |access-date=2023-01-12 |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> [[Peter Travers]], the film critic for ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' magazine wrote, "My advice, in the face of such hallucinatory brilliance, is that you hang on."<ref name=rs>{{cite magazine |title=Inland Empire Review |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/reviews/movie/11692273/review/12626084/inland_empire |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061208184344/http://www.rollingstone.com/reviews/movie/11692273/review/12626084/inland_empire |archive-date=8 December 2006 |first=Peter |last=Travers |author-link=Peter Travers |date=21 November 2006 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |access-date=6 August 2012}}</ref> ''[[The New Yorker]]'' was one of the few publications to offer any negative points about the film, calling it a "trenchant, nuanced film" that "quickly devolves into self-parody".<ref>{{cite news |last=Brody |first=Richard |date=11 December 2006 |title=Inland Empire: The Film File |newspaper=[[The New Yorker]] |url=http://www.newyorker.com/arts/reviews/film/inland_empire_lynch |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080307053736/http://www.newyorker.com/arts/reviews/film/inland_empire_lynch |archive-date=March 7, 2008}}</ref> [[Jonathan Ross (television presenter)|Jonathan Ross]], presenter of the [[BBC]] programme ''[[Film 2007]]'', described it as "a work of genius&nbsp;... I think".<ref name=Film2007>{{Cite episode |title=Inland Empire review |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rIT6KiVtDRM |access-date=2023-01-12 |series=[[Film... (TV programme)#Jonathan Ross (1999–2010)|Film 2007]] |first=Jonathan |last=Ross |author-link=Jonathan Ross (television presenter) |network=[[BBC One]] |date=2007-05-05}}</ref> Damon Wise of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' magazine gave it five stars, calling it "A dazzling and exquisitely original riddle as told by an enigma"<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Wise |first=Damon |date=2007-02-23 |title=Inland Empire |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/inland-empire-review/ |access-date=2023-01-12 |magazine=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]}}</ref> and Jim Emerson (editor of [[RogerEbert.com]]) gave it 4 stars out of 4: "When people say ''Inland Empire'' is Lynch's ''[[Sunset Boulevard (film)|Sunset Boulevard]]'', Lynch's ''[[Persona (1966 film)|Persona]]'' or Lynch's ''[[8½]]'', they're quite right, but it also explicitly invokes connections to [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'', [[Jean-Luc Godard]]'s ''[[Pierrot le Fou]]'', [[Luis Buñuel|Buñuel]] and [[Salvador Dalí|Dalí]]{{hsp|'s}} ''[[Un Chien Andalou]]'', [[Maya Deren]]'s LA-experimental ''[[Meshes of the Afternoon]]'' (a Lynch favorite) and others".<ref>{{cite news |last=Emerson |first=Jim |date=25 January 2007 |title=Go Inland, young woman! |work=[[RogerEbert.com]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/inland-empire-2007 |access-date=2023-01-12}}</ref> However, Carina Chocano of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote that "the film, which begins promisingly, disappears down so many rabbit holes (one of them involving actual rabbits) that eventually it just disappears for good".<ref name="LATimes">{{cite news |last=Chocano |first=Carina |date=15 December 2006 |title=Inland Empire |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=http://www.calendarlive.com/movies/reviews/cl-et-inland15dec15,0,3659742.story |access-date=6 August 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061218135317/http://www.calendarlive.com/movies/reviews/cl-et-inland15dec15,0,3659742.story |archive-date=2006-12-18}}</ref>

''Inland Empire'' tied the second-best film of 2007 by ''[[Cahiers du Cinéma]]''<ref name=caltech/> and was listed among ''[[Sight & Sound]]''{{'}}s films of the decade,<ref name=BFI/> as well as ''[[The Guardian]]''{{'}}s "10 most underrated movies of the decade".<ref name=guardian/> In 2025, it was one of the films voted for the "Readers' Choice" edition of ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century," finishing at number 164.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=[[The New York Times]] |date=2 July 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref> In ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]''<nowiki/>'s 2025 list of "101 Best Movie Performances of the 21st Century", Dern was placed 19th.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-08-25 |title=The 101 Best Movie Performances of the 21st Century |url=https://www.theringer.com/2025/08/25/movies/101-best-movie-acting-performances-since-2000-21st-century |access-date=2025-08-29 |website=[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]] |language=en}}</ref>

===Legacy===
''Inland Empire'' has been named as an example of the [[Liminal space (aesthetic)|liminal space aesthetic]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=McQueen’s subverted domestic bliss |url=https://hero-magazine.com//shows/mcqueens-subverted-domestic-bliss |access-date=2026-04-01 |website=Hero |language=en}}</ref> with [[Owen Gleiberman]] of [[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] citing the film as a progenitor to the 2026 psychological horror film ''[[Backrooms (film)|Backrooms]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gleiberman |first=Owen |date=2026-05-27 |title=‘Backrooms’ Review: Experimental Horror Comes Out of the Margins in Kane Parsons’ YouTube-Gone-A24 Head Trip |url=https://variety.com/2026/film/reviews/backrooms-review-chiwetel-ejiofor-kane-parsons-1236758845/ |access-date=2026-05-29 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref>

''Inland Empire'' also served as an influence to the [[analog horror]] [[web series]] known as ''[[Petscop]]''.<ref name="NTR">{{cite web|last=Peters|first=Lucia|title=The Weird Part Of YouTube: The Making Of "''No Through Road''" And The Power Of Unanswered Questions|url=https://theghostinmymachine.com/2020/11/16/the-weird-part-of-youtube-the-making-of-no-through-road-and-the-power-of-unanswered-questions-found-footage-stevenage-broomhill-farm-time-loop|website=The Ghost in My Machine|access-date=November 16, 2020|date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116175053/https://theghostinmymachine.com/2020/11/16/the-weird-part-of-youtube-the-making-of-no-through-road-and-the-power-of-unanswered-questions-found-footage-stevenage-broomhill-farm-time-loop/|archive-date=November 16, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://egmnow.com/theres-something-hiding-in-petscop/ |title=There's Something Hiding in Petscop |last=Moyer |first=Philip |date=March 18, 2020 |magazine=[[Electronic Gaming Monthly]] |publisher=EGM Media LLC |access-date=January 26, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-01-23 |title=Behind the Music of "Petscop," The Cursed Video Game That Never Was |url=https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/petscop-soundtrack-interview |access-date=2024-07-28 |website=[[Bandcamp Daily]]}}</ref> In addition, the film inspired numerous horror works including a short film-turned-web series ''[[No Through Road (web series)|No Through Road]]''.<ref name="NTR" />

The name "Inland Empire" is used in the 2019 detective video game ''[[Disco Elysium]]'' to describe a skill used by the protagonist.

===Restoration===
''Inland Empire'' was restored and remastered by [[Janus Films]] in 2022, and was screened throughout the year beginning on April 8.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Pak |first1=J. P. |last2=Zufelt |first2=Sebastian |date=2022-04-12 |title=Review: David Lynch embraces the digital age in 'Inland Empire' |url=https://nyunews.com/arts/film/2022/04/12/inland-empire-review/ |access-date=2023-01-12 |newspaper=[[Washington Square News]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Screening Schedule |url=https://inlandempire.official.film/screening-schedule |website=Inland Empire official |access-date=12 January 2023 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220406215659/https://inlandempire.official.film/screening-schedule |archive-date=April 6, 2022}}</ref> The restoration of the film and soundtrack was overseen by David Lynch.<ref name=Perez>{{cite web|url=https://theplaylist.net/inland-empire-restoration-trailer-david-lynch-april-20220324/|title='Inland Empire' Restoration Trailer: David Lynch's Surreal DV Nightmare Comes Back To Theaters In April|last=Perez|first=Rodrigo|website=ThePlaylist.net|date=March 24, 2022|access-date=April 1, 2022|language=en}}</ref> For the restoration the original upscaled HD footage from the editing process was first downscaled back to [[Standard-definition television|standard definition]] to discard "false detail", then converted to [[4K resolution|4K]] using an AI upscaling algorithm.<ref name="janus">{{Cite web |title=Inland Empire Remastering Note |url=https://s3.amazonaws.com/criterion-production/janus_promo_packages/687-/InlandEmpire_RemasterNote_web_original.pdf |access-date=2022-04-08 |website=[[Janus Films]]}}</ref> This version of the film was released on Blu-Ray by [[The Criterion Collection]].<ref name="DVDbeaver"/>

===Accolades===
Lynch attempted to promote Dern's performance and increase her chances of scoring nomination for the [[79th Academy Awards|2007]] [[Academy Award for Best Actress]] by campaigning with a live cow,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2006/scene/awards/lynch-cow-campaign-for-oscar-1117954009/|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |title=Lynch, cow campaign for Oscar; Helmer touts 'Inland Empire' thesp Dern, cheese |first=Alex |last=Romanelli |date=15 November 2006 |access-date=6 August 2012}}</ref> though she was ultimately not recognized by the members of the [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences|Academy]].<ref>{{cite episode |title=David Lynch interview - Inland Empire Review |series=[[Film 2007]] |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rIT6KiVtDRM |access-date=12 January 2023 |date=March 7, 2007 |time=3m22s |via=YouTube}}</ref>
