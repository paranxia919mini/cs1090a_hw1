'''''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex'''''{{efn|Known in Japan as {{Nihongo|'''''Mobile Armored Riot Police: Stand Alone Complex'''''|攻殻機動隊 {{ruby-ja|Stand Alone Complex|スタンド・アローン・コンプレックス}}|Kōkaku Kidōtai Sutando Arōn Konpurekkusu}}||lead=yes}} is a Japanese [[anime]] television series produced by [[Production I.G]] and based on [[Masamune Shirow]]'s [[manga]] ''[[Ghost in the Shell (manga)|Ghost in the Shell]]''. It was written and directed by [[Kenji Kamiyama]], with original character design by Hajime Shimomura and a soundtrack by [[Yoko Kanno]]. The first season aired from October 2002 to October 2003 and was positively received by critics. A second season, titled '''''Ghost in the Shell: S.A.C. 2nd Gig''''',{{efn|Known in Japan as {{Nihongo|'''''Mobile Armored Riot Police: S.A.C. 2nd Gig'''''|攻殻機動隊 {{ruby-ja|S.A.C. 2nd Gig|エス・エー・シー セカンド ギグ}},|Kōkaku Kidōtai Esu Ē Shī Sekando Gigu}}||lead=yes}} aired from January 2004 to January 2005. Critical response to the series was generally positive.

The series centers on the members of an elite law enforcement unit known as [[Public Security Section 9]] as they investigate cybercrime and terrorism cases; the cases in the first season often are connected to their pursuit of an elite "Super Class A" hacker and corporate terrorist known only as "The Laughing Man", whose actions end up creating the series' titular "Stand Alone Complex". A series of associated short comic animations, titled ''Tachikomatic Days'', aired after each episode. These shorts star the [[Tachikoma]] "think-tanks" from the main series, and they typically relate directly to the story of the preceding ''Stand Alone Complex'' episode.

The first season was adapted into a feature-length [[OVA]] titled ''The Laughing Man'', which was released in 2005. The series also received video game spin-offs for the [[PlayStation 2]], [[PlayStation Portable]], and [[mobile phone]]s. ==Plot==
{{Further|List of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex episodes|label 1=List of ''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex'' episodes}}

===Premise===
{{See also|List of Ghost in the Shell characters{{!}}List of ''Ghost in the Shell'' characters}}
[[File:Section 9 operators in 2nd GIG.jpg|left|thumb|The members of Section 9 (from bottom left to top right): Paz, Borma, Saito, Batou, Motoko, Togusa, Aramaki, and Ishikawa]]
The series takes place in the year 2030, where many people have become [[cyborgs]] with [[prosthetic]] bodies. Primarily set in {{Nihongo|New Port City|新浜市|Niihama-shi|}}, in the fictional [[Japanese prefecture]] of {{Nihongo|Niihama|新浜県|Niihama-ken|}}, the series follows the members of [[Public Security Section 9]], a special-operations task-force made up of former military officers and police detectives. While the group investigates various crimes, both seasons feature ongoing investigations into two incidents that embroil the group in corruption within other branches of the Japanese government.

===Season 1===
The first season of ''Stand Alone Complex'' focuses on the [[Laughing Man (Ghost in the Shell)|Laughing Man]] incident, wherein a [[Hacktivism|hacktivist]] ultimately reveals to [[Motoko Kusanagi|Major Motoko Kusanagi]] that he had discovered that several [[micromachine]] manufacturing corporations, [[State-corporate crime|in association with the Japanese government]], [[Political corruption|suppressed]] information on an inexpensive cure to a debilitating cyberization disease [[Big Pharma conspiracy theory|in order to profit]] from the more expensive micromachine treatment. Following this, he abducted one of the owners of the company and attempted to force him to reveal the truth on live television, resulting in the hacker live-hacking everyone's vision and cameras at the event to cover his face with the stylized laughing face logo that became synonymous with his image.<ref name="intothenetwork" />

His popularity spawned several genuine imitators, resulting in the series' titular Stand Alone Complex. After an investigation by the authorities causes him to resurface in the present, Section 9 discovers these companies and several Japanese politicians later used the Laughing Man's image to garner public support and profit, and they begin a campaign, spearheaded by Togusa and the Major, to disseminate the truth. This ultimately leads to the Cabinet labeling them as domestic terrorists and forcibly disbanding them, resulting in the capture of several members and Kusanagi's apparent death. However, it is all a ruse to deceive the government, and the very alive and well members of Section 9 regroup to bring the micromachine corporations and corrupt politicians to justice, resulting in the dissolution of the current Japanese government.<ref name="intothenetwork" />

===Season 2===
The second season, ''2nd Gig'', set two years after the events of the first season, explores the political and social ramifications of the two world wars that took place prior to the events of the series.<ref>{{cite web|url= http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/02_/s08_/index.html#c|title= Interview: Kenji Kamiyama|work= Production I.G.|access-date= 16 November 2011|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20121027173642/http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/02_/s08_/index.html#c|archive-date= 27 October 2012|url-status= live}}</ref> At the time of the Third and the Fourth World Wars, about three million Asians became refugees and were invited into Japan as a source of cheap labor. These "invited refugees", based on the reclaimed island of [[Dejima]], soon became unemployed in the post-war period, and their social unrest borders on outright war.<ref name="2ndOVA">{{cite web|url= http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/39_/index.html|title= Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Individual Eleven|publisher= Production I.G|access-date= 16 November 2011|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20111125202252/http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/39_/index.html|archive-date= 25 November 2011|url-status= live}}</ref>

Section 9's involvement in the refugee issue begins after they successfully stop a hostage crisis caused by a [[Domestic terrorism|domestic terrorist]] group known as the Individual Eleven, after which newly elected Prime Minister Yoko Kayabuki officially reinstates the organization. The group seems to be modeling themselves after the [[May 15 Incident]], where a group of naval officers assassinated the Prime Minister and then gained the support of the public, and hope to stir up the refugees' spirits by fighting for them against the Japanese government.

The group also comes into contact with [[Kazundo Goda|Kazundo Gōda]], head of the Cabinet Intelligence Service, who gets assistance from the group in defusing several instances between the refugees as well as assisting him in transporting [[plutonium]] through Dejima, result in several failures and refugee deaths, further straining relations.

Section 9 ultimately discovers that Gōda has been manipulating both events behind the scenes, leaking the social virus that creates the Individual Eleven ideology and creating a new Stand Alone Complex, as well as the intentional failures with the refugees. However, he cannot account for the charismatic [[Hideo Kuze]] who genuinely believes in the best for the refugees and helps rally for their independence from Japan. Throughout the investigation, Kusanagi discovers she may know Kuze from her childhood. Ultimately, Gōda is found guilty of his part in the refugee incidents and killed before he can defect to the American Empire, but not before his ministrations also result in the death of Kuze.

===Film===
In the film ''[[Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex – Solid State Society]]'', set two years following the resolution of the Individual Eleven incident, the Major has left Section 9, and Togusa is now field commander, leading an investigation into several deaths of refugees from the Siak Republic, which results in the discovery of a government computer system coordinating the kidnappings of 20 thousand abused children who have had their cyberbrains replaced and placing them under the care of several senior citizens made comatose by another government program that takes care of all of their bodily needs.

Their investigation, which brings the Major out of hiding, reveals an entity known as the Puppeteer behind the kidnappings. The Puppeteer is a [[Rhizome (philosophy)|rhizome]] formed by the collective will of the senior citizens, and the Siak Republic's intent to use the kidnapped children in their plans leads to their downfall. However, the investigation further reveals that a member of the House of Representatives is also using the children for his nationalistic purposes. Section 9 and the Major infiltrate a welfare center where the MP brainwashes the children, resulting in the Puppeteer revealing that the senior citizens wished to give the children free will in their future, with the politician interfering in that new plan.

==Production==
[[File:Kenji Kamiyama.jpg|thumb|left| Director [[Kenji Kamiyama]].]]
''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex'' was animated by [[Production I.G]], and produced by [[Bandai Visual]], [[Bandai Entertainment]], [[Dentsu]], [[Nippon Television Network]], [[Tokuma Shoten]], [[Victor Entertainment]], and [[Manga Entertainment]].<ref name="ProductionIGSACcredits">{{cite web|url=http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/03_/s03_/index.html|title=Staff & Cast|work=Production I.G|access-date=2 September 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110517202729/http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/03_/s03_/index.html|archive-date=17 May 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> The series was directed and written by [[Kenji Kamiyama]], with additional screenwriters including Junichi Fujisaku, [[Yoshiki Sakurai]], [[Shōtarō Suga]], [[Dai Satō]], Nobutoshi Terado, Yutaka Ōmatsu, and Yūichirō Matsuka. Masamune Shirow, author of the original ''Ghost in the Shell'' manga, provided plot for several episodes, sketches of characters and mechanical designs (including the [[Tachikoma]]), and gave his approval to the scripts before production.<ref name="ANN-Interview">{{cite web|url= http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2002-10-03|title= Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Interview|access-date= 4 November 2011|publisher= Anime News Network|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20111109030148/http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2002-10-03|archive-date= 9 November 2011|url-status= live}}</ref> The series was produced with an 800 million yen investment. Kenji Kamiyama decided to make the anime television series as a "relative" to the manga and film, serving as a separate parallel world from both.<ref name="intothenetwork" />

For the second season, [[Mamoru Oshii]] contributed with his ideas to the concept of the entire series and initial planning stages.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/02_/index.html|title=Ghost in the Shell: S.A.C. 2nd Gig|publisher=Production I.G|access-date=7 January 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121204091610/http://www.productionig.com/contents/works_sp/02_/index.html|archive-date=4 December 2012|url-status=live}}</ref> In an interview, Oshii described his role in 2nd Gig as supervising the entire series and writing the plots for each episode.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.popmatters.com/film/interviews/oshii-mamoru-040923.shtml|title=THERE IS NO APHRODISIAC LIKE INNOCENCE|access-date=2008-09-19|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081013054536/http://www.popmatters.com/film/interviews/oshii-mamoru-040923.shtml|archive-date=2008-10-13|url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Dai Sato]] stated that they were initially going to concentrate on how Japan was going to participate in war after [[9/11]] as intended to portray a fictional future. But by the time they were working on the episodes, the [[Iraq War]] had already started and Japan's Self-Defense Force was sent to Iraq. During this time Japan also had an election. Sato continued to state that he created the "Individual Eleven" episodes to express irresponsibility of the Japanese people when they voted for the politicians that planned to send Japanese troops to Iraq and Afghanistan.<ref name="Mechademia4-interview">{{cite book|last=Scally|first=Deborah| author2=Drummond-Mathews, Angela| author3=Hairston, Marc.|title= Mechademia 4: War/Time|chapter=Interview with Murase Shūkō and Satō Dai|publisher=[[University of Minnesota Press]]|pages=330–333|isbn=978-0-8166-6749-9|date=3 September 2007}}</ref>

===Music===
{{Main| music of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex}}
[[File:Yoko Kanno.jpg|thumb|[[Yoko Kanno]] composed the music for the ''Stand Alone Complex'' series.]]
The soundtrack for the series was composed by [[Yoko Kanno]] and produced by [[Victor Entertainment]]. The first season's opening theme is "Inner Universe" performed by [[Origa]] (written by Origa and [[Shanti Snyder]]). The lyrics are in Russian, English, and Latin. The first season's ending theme is "Lithium Flower" performed by [[Scott Matthew]] (written by Tim Jensen). When the series was later re-broadcast on terrestrial television in Japan, "Inner Universe" was replaced with "GET9", performed by jillmax (written by Tim Jensen), while "Lithium Flower" was replaced with "I Do", written and performed by [[Ilaria Graziano]].{{citation needed|date=February 2015}}

The opening theme for ''S.A.C. 2nd Gig'' is "Rise" performed by Origa (written by Tim Jensen), while the ending theme is "Living Inside the Shell" performed by [[Steve Conte]] (written by Shanti Snyder). ''2nd Gig'' also used alternate opening and closing themes when it was re-broadcast on terrestrial television, with "Rise" was replaced by "CHRisTmas in the SiLenT ForeSt" performed by Ilaria Graziano (written by Shanti Snyder), and "Living Inside the Shell" was replaced by "Snyper", performed by Ilaria Graziano (written by Tim Jensen).<ref group=Note>In the series' soundtracks, "Snyper" is titled "From the Roof Top～Somewhere in the Silence (Sniper's Theme)".</ref>

==Related media==

===OVAs===
A feature-length [[Original video animation|OVA]] titled ''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex – The Laughing Man'' was released in Japan on 23 September 2005, and in North America on 2 October 2007. The OVA retells the first season of the anime television series with minor alterations to the storyline to accommodate an abbreviated take on the Laughing Man affair. Some additional animation and voice work was also added. Although the Japanese-language version retained the voice cast from the anime series, the English version has a new cast of voice actors. The dialogue recording for the English version was produced by [[Ocean Productions]].<ref name="intothenetwork" /> A Blu-ray version was released on 22 December 2010.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jbook.co.jp/p/p.aspx/4044738/s/|script-title =ja: 攻殻機動隊 STAND ALONE COMPLEX The Laughing Man(Blu-ray)|language=ja|publisher=Jbook|access-date=13 February 2012|archive-date=9 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200909202836/https://honto.jp/netstore/pd-dvd_83902775.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

''Ghost in the Shell: S.A.C. 2nd Gig – Individual Eleven'' is a feature-length OVA which retells the events of ''S.A.C. 2nd Gig'', altered to focus on both the Individual Eleven investigation and the relationship between Hideo Kuze and Motoko Kusanagi. Newly animated scenes and a remixed soundtrack are also included.<ref name="2ndOVA.1">''Individual Eleven'' features the same voice cast as ''The Laughing Man'' and was released in Japan on 27 January 2006./5w6ucvbNQ|archive-date=29 January 2011</ref>

==Reception==
===Season 1===
''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex'' received mostly positive reviews from critics, who praised the series' high quality of animation and the musical score by [[Yoko Kanno]].<ref name="ManiaChris">{{cite web|url=http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-vol-1_article_76211.html|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Vol. #1|last=Beveridge|first=Chris|date=19 June 2004|work=Mania.com (formerly AnimeOnDVD)|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081122111142/http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-vol-1_article_76211.html|archive-date=22 November 2008}}</ref><ref name="THEM">{{cite web|url=http://www.themanime.org/viewreview.php?id=611|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex|last=Tucker|first=Derrick|work=T.H.E.M. Anime Reviews|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140726023124/http://www.themanime.org/viewreview.php?id=611|archive-date=26 July 2014}}</ref><ref name="ANNR">{{cite web|url=http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/ghost-in-the-shell-s.a.c./dvd-1|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Vol. #1|last=Santos|first=Carlo|work=[[Anime News Network]]|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090211032715/http://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/ghost-in-the-shell-s.a.c./dvd-1|archive-date=11 February 2009|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="DVDverdict">{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/gitsvol1.php|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Vol. #1|last=Lineberger|first=Robert|date=5 August 2004|work=DVDverdict|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080708231953/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/gitsvol1.php|archive-date=8 July 2008|url-status=live}}</ref> In particular, reviewers reacted positively to the world of high-technology that ''Stand Alone Complex'' presents,<ref name="THEM" /><ref name="ANNR" /><ref name="Locus">{{cite web|url=http://www.locusmag.com/2006/Features/Person_GhostInTheShell.html|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex|last=Person|first=Lawrence|date=15 January 2006|work=Locus Online|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140726022303/http://www.locusmag.com/2006/Features/Person_GhostInTheShell.html|archive-date=26 July 2014}}</ref> described as "believably futuristic".<ref name="THEM" /> Lawrence Person from ''Locus Online'' wrote that "the world of [Stand Alone Complex] is recognizably our own, or rather, one recognizably extrapolated from modern Japan. While parts of the technology seem unlikely in the time-frame allotted, none seems impossible."<ref name="Locus" />

In addition, several reviews mentioned what they perceived to be overarching themes explored in the series as a result of the setting; specifically, the meaning of humanity in a world where the lines between man and machine were becoming increasingly blurred,<ref name="ANNR" /><ref name="Locus" /> as well as various societal issues that might emerge as a result of advances in technology.<ref name="ManiaChris" /><ref name="Locus" />

Reviewers agreed that the main "Laughing Man" storyline was satisfying, characterizing it variously as "interesting",<ref name="Locus" /> "complex",<ref name="ManiaDani">{{cite web|url=http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-vol-2_article_77030.html|title=Review of Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex Vol. #2|last=Moure|first=Dani|date=21 February 2005|work=Mania.com (formerly AnimeOnDVD)|access-date=9 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081122110950/http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-vol-2_article_77030.html|archive-date=22 November 2008|url-status=dead}}</ref> and "engaging".<ref name="THEM" /> Reaction to the series' "Stand Alone" episodes, however, was mixed: some reviewers considered these episodes to be boring and, in some cases, the worst episodes of the series,<ref name="THEM" /><ref name="Locus" /> while others contended that they allowed further development of the characters and the futuristic setting.<ref name="ManiaChris" /><ref name="ManiaDani" />

Certain commentators specifically focused on the series' success or failure in achieving the high standards set by the critically acclaimed 1995 film ''[[Ghost in the Shell (1995 film)|Ghost in the Shell]]''. Opinion in this regard was divided: although these reviewers took an overall favorable view,<ref name="ManiaChris" /><ref name="THEM" /><ref name="DVDverdict" /> some reviews criticized the quality of animation,<ref name="THEM" /> while others felt that the series' story lacked the depth of the original film.<ref name="DVDverdict" /> This "lack of depth", however, was attributed to the fact that ''Stand Alone Complex'' is an action-oriented series with a limited episode length in which to explore deeper themes.<ref name="DVDverdict" />

Bolstering the generally positive reviews of the series, ''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex'' won an Excellence Prize (Animation Division) at the 2002 [[Japan Media Arts Festival]],<ref name="JMediaArtsFest">{{cite web|url=http://plaza.bunka.go.jp/english/festival/2002/animation/000398/|title=Excellence Prize, Animation Division: ''Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex''|publisher=[[Japan Media Arts Festival]]|year=2002|access-date=2 September 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718215654/http://plaza.bunka.go.jp/english/festival/2002/animation/000398/|archive-date=18 July 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> a [[Tokyo Anime Award|Notable Entry Award]] at the 2003 [[Tokyo International Anime Fair]],<ref name="IntAnimeFair">{{cite web|url=http://www.tokyoanime.jp/tafweb/taf2003/e/compe/jyushou_list.html|title=Award-winning List|work=Tokyo International Anime Fair|access-date=12 January 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071220211810/http://www.tokyoanime.jp/tafweb/taf2003/e/compe/jyushou_list.html|archive-date=20 December 2007}}</ref> and was featured in June 2004 by [[Newtype USA]], with a "double scoop" cover story.<ref name="Madman">{{cite web|url=http://www.madman.com.au/actions/catalogue.do?releaseId=4117&method=view|title=Newtype USA Vol. 3, #6 (June 2004)|work=Madman Entertainment|access-date=9 February 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090904021204/http://www.madman.com.au/actions/catalogue.do?releaseId=4117&method=view|archive-date=4 September 2009}}</ref> The Japan Media Arts Festival provided a brief summary of ''Stand Alone Complex'', calling it a "completely original television series{{nbsp}}... entertaining and easy to understand".<ref name="JMediaArtsFest" />

===Season 2===
Chris Beveridge of Mania gave ''2nd Gig'' an overall score of A praising its coloring stating, "Colors are gorgeous and solid, especially all the various areas of large soft colors that look to be amazingly solid and with no visible break-up even during pausing."<ref>{{cite web|last=Beveridge|first=Chris|title=Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex (2nd Gig) SE Vol. #1|url=http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-2nd-gig-se-vol-1_article_77134.html|publisher=AnimeOnDVD|access-date=7 November 2011|date=16 September 2005|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111227020519/http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-2nd-gig-se-vol-1_article_77134.html|archive-date=27 December 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> Mark Thomas of ''Mania'' also gave it an overall score of A, stating: "It has plenty of good action and thought provoking plot points."<ref>{{cite web|last=Thomas|first=Mark|title=Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex 2nd Gig Set (Anime Legends)|url=http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-2nd-gig-set-anime-legends_article_115453.html|publisher=Mania.com|access-date=7 November 2011|date=8 February 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111227020338/http://www.mania.com/ghost-shell-stand-alone-complex-2nd-gig-set-anime-legends_article_115453.html|archive-date=27 December 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> Andy Patricio of IGN rated the first DVD volume 9/10 praised the animation, stating: "Artwork is richer; this is easily the best-looking anime Production I.G. has ever produced. CG is richer and more widely used. Animation is more complex and fluid, although it's still a little jerky, which is common in TV shows."<ref>{{cite web|last=Patricio|first=Andy|title=Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex – 2nd Gig (Vol. 1)|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/685/685922p1.html|publisher=IGN|access-date=7 November 2011|date=6 February 2006|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120505080059/http://dvd.ign.com/articles/685/685922p1.html|archive-date=5 May 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>

Joe Luscik of Animefringe ranked the series 4.5 out of 5 praising the animation stating it "is just awesome".<ref>{{cite web|last=Luscik|first=Joe|title=Ghost in the Shell: S.A.C. 2nd Gig Vol. 1|url=http://www.animefringe.com/magazine/2005/11/review/02.php|publisher=Animefringe|access-date=11 November 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110910090340/http://www.animefringe.com/magazine/2005/11/review/02.php|archive-date=10 September 2011|url-status=live}}</ref> Adam Arseneau of DVD Verdict praised the direction of the 2nd season compared to the first stating, "The tongue-in-cheek humor that made the show clever and endearing is still present, but the atmosphere feels more violent, more urban and dystopic, as if no longer afraid to address the social problems of the future."<ref>{{cite web|last= Arseneau|first= Adam|title= Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex 2nd Gig Vol. 1|url= http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/gits2ndgigvol1.php|publisher= DVD Verdict|access-date= 17 December 2011|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120401093718/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/gits2ndgigvol1.php|archive-date= 1 April 2012|url-status= live}}</ref>

The third DVD volume of the series was one of top-selling series in North America in 2006.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2006-06-14/top-selling-anime-releases-of-2006|title=Top Selling Anime Releases of 2006|date=14 June 2006|access-date=19 June 2022}}</ref> It was also nominated for the Best Animated TV DVD honor category in the TV DVD Awards.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-09-30/anime-nominated-for-tv-dvd-awards|title=Anime Nominated for TV DVD Awards|date=30 September 2008|access-date=19 June 2022|archive-date=20 June 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220620003302/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-09-30/anime-nominated-for-tv-dvd-awards|url-status=live}}</ref> In the American Anime Awards from 2007, Mary Elizabeth McGlynn earned the award of "Best Actress".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2007-02-27/american-anime-awards|title=American Anime Awards Updates|website=[[Anime News Network]]|date=27 February 2007}}</ref>
