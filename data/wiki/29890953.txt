'''''Compensation''''' is a 1999 [[Independent film|independent]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama]] film produced, co-edited and directed by [[Zeinabu irene Davis]] and written by Marc Arthur Chéry. The film is about two parallel love stories set in turn-of-the-century and present-day Chicago, with both stories concerning a relationship between a [[Deafness|deaf]] woman and a hearing man. The story is inspired by the 1906 poem of the same name from early African-American writer [[Paul Laurence Dunbar]].<ref name="Sundance Film Festival" /> The film stars Michelle A. Banks and [[John Earl Jelks]] in the leading roles. The early part of the story is shot like a [[silent film]]. Though the film was not released until 1999, filming took place in 1993.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |title=20th Anniversary of Professor Zeinabu Davis' Film "Compensation" – Communication Department |url=https://unquote.ucsd.edu/communication/20th-anniversary-of-professor-zeinabu-davis-film-compensation/ |access-date=2022-12-07 |website=unquote.ucsd.edu |date=September 27, 2019 |language=en-US}}</ref>

The film premiered at 1999 [[Atlanta Film Festival|Atlanta Film and Video Festival]] and was later screened at the 2000 [[Sundance Film Festival]] in the Dramatic Feature category.<ref name="Sundance Film Festival">{{Cite web |title=Compensation |url=https://history.sundance.org/films/2334/compensation |website=Sundance Institute Archives}}</ref> The film was also shown at the [[Museum of Photographic Arts]] in [[San Diego, California|San Diego]],<ref>{{cite web |date=23 May 2000 |title=Award-Winning Film by UCSD Filmmaker to Premiere June 2 at San Diego Museum of Photographic Arts |url=http://ucsdnews.ucsd.edu/newsrel/soc/dzdavispremiere.htm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220916205141/https://ucsdnews.ucsd.edu/archive/newsrel/soc/dzdavispremiere.htm |archive-date=2022-09-16 |accessdate=1 January 2012 |website=UCSD Social Sciences}}</ref> the Independent Film Market in [[New York City|New York]], and the [[Pan African Film Festival]] in [[Los Angeles]]. Film critic [[Richard Brody]] has described the film as "one of the greatest American independent films ever made."<ref name=":0">{{Cite magazine |last=Brody |first=Richard |date=2021-06-05 |title=What to Stream, Urgently: "Compensation," a Modern Classic Rescued |url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/what-to-stream-urgently-compensation-a-modern-classic-rescued |access-date=2022-12-07 |magazine=The New Yorker |language=en-US}}</ref>

In 2024, the [[Library of Congress]] selected the film for preservation in the [[National Film Registry]], designating it "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant,".<ref name="2024NFR">{{cite web |date=December 17, 2024 |title=25 Films Added to National Film Registry for Preservation |url=https://newsroom.loc.gov/news/25-films-named-to-national-film-registry-for-preservation/s/55d5285d-916f-4105-b7d4-7fc3ba8664e3 |access-date=December 17, 2024 |website=Library of Congress}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |date=2024-12-17 |title=National Film Registry: 'Dirty Dancing', 'Beverly Hills Cop', 'Star Trek II: The Wrath Of Khan' & 'Texas Chainsaw Massacre' Among 25 Movies Added This Year |url=https://deadline.com/2024/12/national-film-registry-2024-1236205258/ |access-date=2024-12-17 |website=Deadline |language=en-US|last2=Tartaglione|first2=Nancy}}</ref>

==Plot==
{{Expand section|date=June 2026}}
In 1910, Malindy, an educated seamstress who mobilizes against [[Racial segregation|segregation]] at her [[Schools for the deaf|school for the deaf]], meets and falls in love with Arthur, a hearing man and an [[Literacy|illiterate]] migrant worker from Mississippi. Malindy teaches Arthur [[American Sign Language|ASL]] and how to read and write. In the present day, artist Malaika and librarian Nico also fall in love after a series of encounters. Both couples revel in the splendor of romance and a possible lifetime commitment, only to be forced to deal with the dreaded diseases of their respective times: [[tuberculosis]] and [[AIDS]]. In both stories, the couples also face opposition from those that do not think a relationship between a hearing person and deaf person will be compatible.

==Production==
The film was not initially conceived as a story featuring deaf performers until Davis saw actress Michelle A. Banks in a production of ''[[Waiting for Godot]]'' in [[Saint Paul, Minnesota|Saint Paul]], Minnesota and was impressed by her performance.<ref name="Ian Grey" /> Davis' husband, Marc Arthur Chéry, revised the script to make her and other characters deaf. Davis also cast deaf actors in other roles and hired deaf technicians for the film crew.<ref name="Ian Grey" />

The entire film was shot in Chicago, Illinois to pay tribute to the director's previous residence and to expose audiences to the beauty of the city. Chicago locations included the Ephphatha Evangelical Lutheran Church of the Deaf, the Legler Branch of the [[Chicago Public Library]], and the [[Indiana Dunes National Park|Indiana Dunes National Lakeshore]]. Filming took place from July to August 1993.<ref name="University of Buffalo, Department of African American Studies">{{Cite web |title=Black Women in Film: One Filmmaker's Journey |url=http://wings.buffalo.edu/cas/aas/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100612235618/http://wings.buffalo.edu/cas/aas/ |archive-date=June 12, 2010 |website=[[University of Buffalo]]}}</ref>

[[Reginald R. Robinson]], a noted [[ragtime]] composer and performer, served as the film's period music score composer.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Heath Jr. |first=Glenn |date=May 9, 2020 |title=Loving so Deeply: An interview with Zeinabu irene Davis |url=https://afterglow.substack.com/p/loving-so-deeply-an-interview-with |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201129205920/https://afterglow.substack.com/p/loving-so-deeply-an-interview-with |archive-date=2020-11-29 |access-date=2022-12-07 |website=Afterglow}}</ref> Robinson incorporated the piano and mandolin into all his compositions for the film. Atiba Y. Jali, served as the contemporary music score composer.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Obejas |first=Achy |date=March 5, 1999 |title='Compensation's' Love Stories Have Broader Scope |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1999-03-05-9903050441-story.html |url-status=dead |access-date=2022-12-07 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20221207130204/https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1999-03-05-9903050441-story.html |archive-date=2022-12-07}}</ref> Jali combined traditional instruments like the drums and flute with less commonly used instruments such as the balafou, [[berimbau]] and [[yidaki]].

==Release==
The film was screened at the 1999 [[Atlanta Film and Video Festival]], the 1999 [[Toronto International Film Festival]], and the 2000 [[Sundance Film Festival]].<ref name="TCM" /><ref name="WMM" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=Compensation |url=https://www.wmm.com/catalog/film/compensation |access-date=2022-12-07 |website=Women Make Movies |language=en-US|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190915130840/https://www.wmm.com/catalog/film/compensation/|archive-date=September 15, 2019}}</ref>

The film went unreleased on VHS and DVD for many years until 2021, when the [[The Criterion Collection|Criterion Channel]] made ''Compensation'' available for streaming.<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=December 29, 2020 |title=The Criterion Channel's January 2021 Lineup |url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/7224-the-criterion-channels-january-2021-lineup |access-date=2022-12-07 |website=The Criterion Collection |language=en}}</ref>

A 4K restoration was undertaken by [[The Criterion Collection]], The [[UCLA Film and Television Archive]], and Wimmin With a Mission Productions in conjunction with The [[Sundance Institute]] from a scan of the 16mm original camera negative. The 5.1 surround soundtrack was mastered from DAT tapes by the UCLA Film and Television Archive. Newly created open captions have been implemented, designed by Alison O’Daniel in collaboration with the Compensation Caption Creative Team. The restoration screened as part of the Revivals section of the 62nd [[New York Film Festival]]<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-22 |title=62nd New York Film Festival Revivals Announced |url=https://www.filmlinc.org/nyff2024/daily/62nd-new-york-film-festival-revivals-announced/ |access-date=2024-12-17 |website=Film at Lincoln Center |language=en}}</ref> and the 60th [[Chicago International Film Festival]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Compensation |url=https://www.chicagofilmfestival.com/event/compensation/ |access-date=2024-12-17 |website=Cinema Chicago |language=en-US|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240929064529/https://www.chicagofilmfestival.com/event/compensation/|archive-date=September 29, 2024}}</ref> The restoration was screened in theaters on February 21, 2025, distributed by [[Janus Films]].<ref>{{Cite tweet |user=JanusFilms |number=1869010081213522278 |title= We're thrilled that COMPENSATION by Zeinabu irene Davis has been selected for preservation by the National Film Registry. The 4K rejuvenation comes to theaters 2/21/25! |date=2024-12-17 |access-date=December 17, 2024}}</ref>

==Critical reception==
On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, 93% of 15 critics' reviews are positive.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/compensation_1999|title=Compensation|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=February 22, 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 100 out of 100, based on 4 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Compensation Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/compensation/?ftag=MCD-06-10aaa1c |access-date=2026-06-19 |website=Metacritic |language=en}}</ref>
[[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' hailed the film as "beautiful and poignant", saying "Davis is adept at not only illuminating the challenges of love crossing the hearing barrier but also in suggesting that such a challenge does not somehow miraculously protect the couple from the even-greater challenges that can turn anyone's life upside-down in any era."<ref name="Kevin Thomas" /> Thomas called the film "an important achievement, illuminating and captivating, and it deserves the chance to see the widest audience possible."<ref name="Kevin Thomas">{{Cite news |last=Thomas |first=Kevin |author-link=Kevin Thomas (film critic) |date=2000-02-24 |title='Compensation' Rewards |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-feb-24-ca-1905-story.html |access-date=2022-12-07}}</ref>

Ian Grey of the ''[[Baltimore City Paper]]'' praised ''Compensation'' as "brilliant", citing Davis' " use of intertitles and subtitles (for, respectively, the film's early-1900s and present-day sequences) as a way to address the limitations of spoken language while simultaneously honoring contemporary African filmmaking techniques, which emphasize visuals over dialogue."<ref name="Ian Grey">{{Cite news |last=Gray |first=Ian |title=Silence Is Golden |work=[[Baltimore City Paper]] |url=http://www2.citypaper.com/film/review.asp?rid=5893 |url-status=dead |access-date=2022-12-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080110115302/http://www2.citypaper.com/film/review.asp?rid=5893 |archive-date=10 January 2008|date=April 26, 2000}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' praised the film as "dreamy, atmosphere reveries, rich in humor and social observation", and called it a "small, quiet, enchanting film about characters who endure and prevail and trust themselves."<ref name="Chicago Sun times">{{Cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=2000-01-25 |title=Tucci's movie full of secrets |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/tuccis-movie-full-of-secrets |access-date=2022-12-07 |website=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> Rick Lyman of ''[[The New York Times]]'' noted the film is "structurally daring".<ref name="The NY Times">{{Cite news |last=Lyman |first=Rick |date=4 February 2000 |title=Taking a Chance |work=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2000/02/04/movies/at-the-movies.html |url-status=live |access-date=2022-12-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221217204857/https://www.nytimes.com/2000/02/04/movies/at-the-movies.html |archive-date=December 17, 2022 }}</ref> Poet [[Elizabeth Alexander (poet)|Elizabeth Alexander]] commended how the original poem that inspired the film appeared to "move this very contemporary filmmaker narratively to the past but technically forward to innovation".<ref name="book">{{cite book |last1=Alexander |first1=Elizabeth |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=b_mXa0NV9UUC&q=zeinabu+irene+davis |title=Power and Possibility: Essays, Reviews, and Interviews |year=2007 |isbn=978-0472069378 |pages=14 |publisher=University of Michigan Press |author-link=Elizabeth Alexander (poet)}}</ref>

In the journal ''Critical Methodologies'', Greg Dimitriadis called ''Compensation'' "...a powerful film. It is boldly experimental. Illness and death are presented as forces that persons cannot control. The death of a loved one is blunted by the gift of love itself. That gift compensates for life’s losses. In showing this, Davis’s film brings great dignity and respect to the situations of African American women and men. At the same time, her film honors African American culture and history."<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Denzin |first=N.K. |year=2003 |title=Screening Race. Cultural Studies |url=https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1532708603239266?journalCode=csca |journal=Critical Methodologies |volume=3 |issue=1 |pages=22–43 |doi=10.1177/1532708603239266 |s2cid=220778889 |via=[[SAGE Journals]]|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

In a 2019 review for ''[[The New Yorker]]'', [[Richard Brody]] also praised the film, writing "Davis incorporates a copious and evocative set of archival photographs into the earlier time’s action, and she films them with a sense of avid and dramatic curiosity that conjure the historical period with a moving immediacy. Filming in black-and-white, she develops a virtual historical archeology, lavishly detailed and alive to the aesthetic spirit of the time."<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Brody |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Brody |date=2019-05-02 |title=Three Boldly Personal Visions of Black History in a Great New Film Series |url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/three-boldly-personal-visions-of-black-history-in-a-great-new-film-series |access-date=2022-12-07 |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |language=en-US}}</ref>
