'''''Myra Breckinridge''''' is a 1970 American [[comedy film]] based on [[Gore Vidal]]'s 1968 [[Myra Breckinridge|novel of the same name]]. The film was directed by [[Mike Sarne|Michael Sarne]], and featured [[Raquel Welch]] in the title role. It also starred [[John Huston]] as Buck Loner, [[Mae West]] as Leticia Van Allen, [[Farrah Fawcett]], [[Rex Reed]], Roger Herren, and [[Roger C. Carmel]]. [[Tom Selleck]] made his film debut in a small role as one of Leticia's "studs", and [[Dan Hedaya]] has his first uncredited appearance as a hospital patient.<ref>{{cite web| title=Myra Breckinridge| url=https://www.tvguide.com/movies/myra-breckinridge/2000033926/| website=[[TV Guide]]| access-date=October 17, 2023}}</ref> [[Theadora Van Runkle]] was costume designer for the film, though [[Edith Head]] designed West's costumes.<ref>{{cite book| last1=Chierichetti| first1=David |last2=Head| first2=Edith| authorlink2=Edith Head| title=Edith Head: The Life and Times of Hollywood's Celebrated Costume Designer| url=https://archive.org/details/edithhead00davi| url-access=limited| publisher=HarperCollins| date=March 4, 2003| isbn=978-0-0601-9428-4| page=[https://archive.org/details/edithhead00davi/page/180 180]}}</ref>

Like the novel, the picture follows the exploits of Myra Breckinridge, a transgender woman who has undergone a [[gender reassignment surgery|sex change]] operation. Claiming to be her own widow, she manipulates her uncle Buck into giving her a position at his [[acting school]], where she attempts to usurp [[Hollywood, Los Angeles|Hollywood]]'s [[social order]] by introducing [[femdom]] into the curriculum.

The picture was controversial for its sexual explicitness (including acts like [[rape of males|female-on-male rape]]), but unlike the novel, it received little to no critical praise and has been cited as [[List of 20th century films considered the worst#Myra Breckinridge (1970)|one of the worst films ever made]].<ref name="laws">{{cite book| last=Moran| first=Leslie J.| author2=Christie, Ian| author3=Sandon, Emma| others=Loizidou, Elena| title=Law's Moving Image| publisher=Routledge Cavendish| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=zsE7nqoeMSAC&q=editions:PDZCG9jN8pUC| date=December 6, 2012| isbn=978-1-1353-1172-8| page=87}}</ref>  In subsequent decades, the film has developed a [[Cult film|cult]] following.<ref>{{cite book| last1=Moran| first1=Leslie| first2=Elena| last2=Loizidou| first3=Ian| last3=Christie| first4=Emma| last4=Sandon| title=Law's Moving Image| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=zsE7nqoeMSAC&q=breckinridge| date=December 6, 2012| page=86| publisher=Routledge-Cavendish| isbn=978-1-1353-1172-8| access-date=October 17, 2023}}</ref>

==Plot==
Myron Breckinridge flies to [[Copenhagen]] to get a sex change, becoming the beautiful Myra. Returning to America, Myra goes to her uncle Buck Loner's acting school, where she pretends to be her own widow and claims that it was Myron's will that she receive half the school, or $500,000; when Loner demurs, she asks that she be given a teaching job there to provide for herself. Buck reluctantly agrees, while launching an investigation into the veracity of Myra's claims.

Although she is ostensibly assigned an [[etiquette]] class, Myra instead philosophizes about the [[semiotics]] of the [[Golden Age of Hollywood]] while also introducing concepts of [[femdom]] into the curriculum. In debates with Myron—who physically manifests to Myra to discuss their plan—it is revealed that Myra has come to the academy with the intention of "the destruction of the last vestigial traces of traditional manhood in the race in order to realign the sexes, thus reducing population while increasing human happiness and preparing for its next stage.”

On campus, Myra becomes obsessed with a pair of young lovers named Rusty Godowski and Mary Ann Pringle, whom she believes embody all the traits of American [[gender norms]]. One night, on the pretext of arranging for him to undergo a physical exam, Myra ties Rusty to a table and [[Rape of males|rapes him]] with a [[Strap-on dildo|strap on]]. The [[sexual assault]] causes Rusty to abandon Mary Ann. Myra uses the pair's breakup to move in on Mary Ann herself, encouraging her to experiment with [[bisexuality]]. Myra's pursuit of Rusty and Mary Ann is paralleled with the life of Leticia van Allen, a female casting agent who habitually seduces the young men who come to her for auditions. Leticia and Myra briefly cross paths when Leticia comes to the school scouting for talent. Following her assault of Rusty, Myra sends him to Leticia, who claims Rusty as her own lover.

Buck continues his investigation, ultimately uncovering evidence that Myron never died and that no death certificate exists for him. Confronted with the truth, Myra admits to the truth and strips naked before a horrified Buck, revealing the scars from her [[vaginoplasty]].

Myra continues her pursuit of Mary Ann, who turns her down, telling her that she wishes she were a man. The next day, the manifestation of Myron—claiming that Myra has become too ambitious—runs her down in a car.

Myron awakens in the hospital from the beginning of the film, where it's indicated he has been admitted for a car accident, not gender affirmation; his nurse is Mary Ann. Looking at his bedside table, Myron sees a magazine featuring an article on Raquel Welch.

==Production==

===Development===
[[File:Myra Breckinridge - Theatre ad - 29 July, 1970.png|thumb|upright|Theatrical advertisement from 1970]]
Film rights for [[Gore Vidal]]'s novel ''[[Myra Breckinridge]]'' were sold for a reported $750,000 - $900,000 including a percentage of the profits and a fee covering Vidal writing the screenplay.<ref>{{cite news| title=Look Who Says the Novel Is Dead| last=Haber| first=Joyce| newspaper=Los Angeles Times| date=April 29, 1968| page=C34| url=https://www.newspapers.com/search/#query=Look+Who+Says+the+Novel+Is+Dead&lnd=1}}</ref><ref name="Daly 412">{{cite news| title=Swinging Into Disaster| last=Daly| first=Steven| newspaper=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]| date=April 2001| page=412| url=https://archive.vanityfair.com/article/2001/4/swinging-into-disaster}}</ref> Vidal wrote a draft, but the job was ultimately assigned to [[David Giler]], who wrote a draft in three weeks. Vidal told Giler how much he liked the draft.<ref name="los" />

[[Mike Sarne|Michael Sarne]] had just made ''[[Joanna (1968 film)|Joanna]]''. [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]]'s head of production, [[Richard D. Zanuck|Dick Zanuck]], said "he came to me when we had two lousy scripts and said he knew how to do it. He had some good ideas."<ref name="los" /> Zanuck introduced Sarne to the film's producer, [[Robert Fryer]], who was so impressed that the studio hired Sarne to write a script. The final draft of the script would be the tenth.<ref name="los" /> The original director was [[Bud Yorkin]]. Producer Jim Cresson said "we thought he would play it too safe" and the studio ended up giving the job of directing to Sarne.<ref name="los" />

There were months of speculation over who would play the title role. Raquel Welch was cast in July 1969.<ref>{{cite news| title='Myra' Title Role for Raquel| newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]| date=July 18, 1969| page=D9| url=https://www.newspapers.com/search/#query=MOVIE+CALL+SHEET%3A+%27Myra%27+Title+Role+for+Raquel&lnd=1}}</ref><ref>{{cite news| title=Raquel Welch to Star In 'Myra Breckenridge'| url=https://www.nytimes.com/1969/07/18/archives/raquel-welch-to-star-in-myra-breckenridge.html| newspaper=[[The New York Times]]| date=July 18, 1969| page=16| url-access=subscription}}</ref> The next major casting was [[Mae West]], accepting her first film role since 1943. (She said she had turned down ''[[Pal Joey (film)|Pal Joey]]'' and ''[[The Art of Love (1965 film)|The Art of Love]]''.) West was introduced to producer [[Robert Fryer]] via [[George Cukor]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Thomas|first=Kevin|date=August 31, 1969|title=Mae West: in Great Form|work=[[The Washington Post]]|page=93}}</ref> "It's a return, not a comeback," said West. "[[Hard to Handle (song)|Hard to Handle]]".<ref name="mae" /> She was paid $350,000.<ref>{{cite news| title=Hollywood Today: Mr. Untouchable?| first=Norma Lee| last=Browning| newspaper=[[Chicago Tribune]]| url=https://chicagotribune.newspapers.com/search/#query=Mr.+Untouchable&ymd=1969-10-23| date=October 23, 1969| page=E1}}</ref> In addition, West was allowed to arrive no earlier than 5 p.m. and had final approval over Welch's wardrobe as well as her own.<ref>{{cite news| title=How Myra Breckinridge became Raquel Welch's wildest movie role| first=Lester Fabian| last=Braithwaite| for getting then-unknown [[Tom Selleck]] cast as one of her studs in the film.<ref>{{cite news| title=Kiss & Tell Tom Selleck Goes Straight to the Truth Discussing Role as Gay in 'In & Out'| ===Shooting===
Filming was laden with controversy due to Michael Sarne being granted complete control over the project. Sarne quickly went over budget due to his unorthodox techniques, which included spending up to seven hours at a time by himself, "thinking", leaving the cast to wait around on set for him to return so that filming could commence.<ref name=agony>{{cite journal| url=http://www.agonybooth.com/myra_breckinridge/| title=Myra Breckinridge (1970)| journal=The Agony Booth| date=August 24, 2003}}</ref> Additionally, Sarne spent several days filming tables of food for a dream sequence which, in addition to being non-essential to the plot, appears in the film for only a few seconds.<ref name=agony/> According to many accounts, Sarne encouraged bickering among cast members. After the failure of this film, he was never asked by an American studio to direct another film.

There were also reports of conflicts between Welch and West, who came out of a 27-year retirement to play Leticia Van Allen.<ref name="complete">{{cite book| last1=Tuska| first1=Jon| last2=Tyler| first2=Parker| authorlink2=Parker Tyler| title=The Complete Films of Mae West| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=rPR4OC_8zqcC&q=The+Complete+Films+of+Mae+West&pg=PA176| publisher=Citadel Press| date=1992| isbn=978-0806513591| page=183}}</ref><ref>{{cite book| last=Prono| first=Luca| title=Encyclopedia of Gay and Lesbian Popular Culture| publisher=ABC-Clio| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Y3FYzMPH2OwC&q=Breckenridge| date=2008| isbn=978-0-3133-3599-0| page=270}}</ref>  Fawcett said that they projected their dislike towards each other onto her and stopped talking to her and that she would cry in her dressing room, afraid to come out.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.nytimes.com/1977/12/23/archives/at-the-movies-farrah-gets-her-teeth-into-first-leading-movie-role.html| title=At the Movies| newspaper=The New York Times| date=December 23, 1977}}</ref> Furthermore, some 1940s- and 1950s-era film actors who appeared in ''Myra Breckinridge'' were upset that footage from their old films was inserted into the movie to punctuate some of the gags and the film's rape sequence. After the film was previewed in [[San Francisco]], the [[White House]] demanded that footage from the 1937 film ''[[Heidi (1937 film)|Heidi]]'', featuring [[Shirley Temple]], be removed due to Temple's role as a United States ambassador. [[Loretta Young]] also successfully sued to have footage of herself from ''[[The Story of Alexander Graham Bell]]'' removed from the film.<ref name="complete" /> Commenting on this, [[Rex Reed]], who co-starred and was then a columnist, said "This was a film where the lawsuits really flew".

"I've never seen so many personality conflicts on one picture," said Richard Zanuck. "Fryer has quit three times. I don't think there's anyone on this movie who hasn't been fired or quit three times. Including me."<ref name="los">{{cite news| title=Shell Shock on the 'Myra' Set--and It's Not a War Film| first=Joyce| last=Haber| newspaper=Los Angeles Times| url=https://www.newspapers.com/search/#lnd=1&query=Shell+Shock+on+the+%27Myra%27+Set&ymd=1969-10-19&p_county=Los+Angeles+County%2C+California| date=October 19, 1969| page=U1}}</ref> "I feel sorry for Bob [producer Robert Fryer]," said Zanuck. "Raquel is always nervous during a film. Rex isn't exactly easy. And Sarne is rough. Much tougher than he looks."<ref name="los" /> "Fryer is a really nice man," said Sarne. "We just disagree on everything."<ref name="los" /> "He tells everybody on this picture we're diametrically opposed, which we are," said Fryer. "I want to do a comedy. He wants to do a fantasy. He's trying to superimpose 1964 [[Federico Fellini|Fellini]]—not Fellini, mind you, but 1964 Fellini—on a subject matter which is way out to begin with."<ref name="los" /> "I don't understand it," said Giler. "[[Robert F. Kennedy|Bobby Kennedy]] and [[John F. Kennedy|Jack Kennedy]], they were assassinated. But no one touches Sarne. Sarne's script for ''Myra'' should be hermetically sealed."<ref name="los" />

==Rating==
''Myra Breckinridge'' was one of two films with an [[X-rated#United States|X rating]] to be released by 20th Century Fox in 1970 (the other being ''[[Beyond the Valley of the Dolls]]'', which became a satirical [[Camp (style)|camp classic]]).<ref>{{cite book| last=Frasier| first=David K.| title=Russ Meyer-The Life and Films: A Biography and a Comprehensive, Illustrated and Annotated Filmography and Bibliography| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=pmZSBqUyslEC&q=Breckenridge| publisher=McFarland| date=June 28, 2010| isbn=978-0-7864-8063-0| page=129}}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/84433/myra-breckinridge#articles-reviews| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121019054058/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/84433/Myra-Breckinridge/#articles-reviews| url-status=dead| archive-date=October 19, 2012| title=Myra Breckinridge (1970): Articles| website=[[Turner Classic Movies]]| access-date=October 18, 2023}}</ref>

==Release==

The film premiered at the [[Olympia Theatre (New York City)|Criterion Theatre]] in [[New York City]] on the evening of June 23, 1970. A crowd of roughly 2,000 people formed around the barriers of the red carpet to catch a glimpse of Raquel Welch and Mae West, who arrived separately. The crowd became riotous, which led to injuries and arrests, as people smashed windows and clashed with police. Michael Sarne did not attend the premiere.<ref name="Daly 412"/> It opened the next day at the Criterion and at Loew's Tower East in New York City, as well as at Loew's Hollywood Theatre in [[Los Angeles]], before expanding nationwide on July 1.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|title=Everything You've Heard About Myra Breckinridge Is True (advertisement)|url=https://archive.org/details/sim_variety_1970-05-27_259_2/page/n13/mode/2up?view=theater|date=May 27, 1970|page=15}}</ref>

==Reception and legacy==
Upon its release, the film drew fiercely negative reviews and was a box office flop.<ref>{{cite book| last=Hadleigh| first=Boze |authorlink=Boze Hadleigh| title=The Lavender Screen: The Gay and Lesbian Films: Their Stars, Makers, Characters, and Critics| publisher=Citadel Press| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=H7u2PmlUDpwC&q=Breckenridge| date=January 1, 2001| isbn=978-0-8065-2199-2| page=108}}</ref> ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' stated "''Myra Breckinridge'' is about as funny as a [[Child sexual abuse|child molester]]. It is an insult to intelligence, an affront to sensibility and an abomination to the eye." The critic added that the film was "so tasteless, it represents some sort of nadir in the history of American cinema".<ref>{{cite journal |url=http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,878382,00.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080513095014/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,878382,00.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=May 13, 2008 |title=Cinema: Some Sort of Nadir |journal=[[Time magazine|TIME]] |date=July 6, 1970 |accessdate=February 6, 2015}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] gave the film half of one star out of four, writing, "Screenwriters David Giler and Michael Sarne have mangled Gore Vidal's sexy and clever novel, ''Myra Breckinridge,'' in adapting it for the screen. Gratuitous nudity and oafish direction have replaced wit and mystery."<ref>{{cite news| author-link=Gene Siskel| last=Siskel| first=Gene| date=June 29, 1970| url=https://chicagotribune.newspapers.com/clip/40395608/| title=Breckinridge| newspaper=Chicago Tribune| page=Section 2, p. 10}}</ref> Herb Kelly wrote in ''[[The Miami News]]'': "I now nominate ''Myra Breckinridge'' as the worst movie ever made&nbsp;... nothing can touch it for tastelessness and boredom".<ref>{{cite news |last=Kelly |first=Herb |title='Breckinridge' Worst Ever |work=The Miami News |date=August 29, 1970| url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/40395524/}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote that the film "plunges straight downhill under the weight of artless direction".<ref>{{cite news| title=Myra Breckinridge| url=https://variety.com/1969/film/reviews/myra-breckinridge-1117793381/| date=December 31, 1969| newspaper=Variety}}</ref> The film is also cited in the book ''[[The Fifty Worst Films of All Time]]''. [[Gore Vidal]] disowned the film, calling it "an awful joke".<ref>{{cite book| last1=Hoberman |first1=J.| authorlink1=J. Hoberman| last2=Rosenbaum| first2=Jonathan| authorlink2=Jonathan Rosenbaum| title=Midnight Movies| publisher=Harper & Row| date=January 1, 1983| isbn=978-0060909901| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=C3QJAAAAIAAJ&q=breckinridge| page=268}}</ref><ref>{{cite book| last=Conner| first=Floyd| title=Hollywood's Most Wanted: The Top 10 Book of Lucky Breaks, Prima Donnas, Box Office Bombs, and Other Oddities| publisher=Brassey's| year=2002| isbn=978-1-5748-8480-7| page=[https://archive.org/details/hollywoodsmostwa0000conn/page/65 65]| url=https://archive.org/details/hollywoodsmostwa0000conn/page/65}}</ref> Film historian [[Leonard Maltin]] gave the film a BOMB (his lowest possible score). In his movie guide, he states that the film "tastelessly exploits many old Hollywood favorites through film clips". He also calls the film "as bad as any movie ever made".

In a 2012 interview with Mark Peikert, Welch said of the film, "The only good thing about that was the clothes."<ref>{{cite journal| title=Catching Up With Raquel Welch| url=http://www.out.com/entertainment/popnography/2012/02/09/catching-raquel-welch| date=9 February 2012| last=Peikert| first=Mark| journal=[[Out (magazine)|Out]]}}</ref> [[Rotten Tomatoes]] gives the film a rating of 24% from 21 reviews.<ref>[https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/myra_breckinridge Rotten Tomatoes]</ref>

==Home media==
In 2004, ''Myra Breckinridge'' was released on DVD with minor changes: to make the film's ending (that the title character never had a sex change) clearer, the ending sequence was changed to a [[black-and-white]] format. Since its release, it has developed a fan following.<ref name="laws" />
