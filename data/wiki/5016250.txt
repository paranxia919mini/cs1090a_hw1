'''''Watchmen''''' is a 2009 American [[superhero film]] based on [[Watchmen|the comic book series]] by [[Dave Gibbons]],{{efn|The graphic novel's author and co-creator, [[Alan Moore]], chose to have his name removed from the credits.<ref name="Perez">{{cite web |last=Perez |first=Rodrigo |title=Alan Moore Removes His Name From 'Watchmen' Credits, Abdicates All Royalty Checks To Artist Dave Gibbons |url=https://theplaylist.net/alan-moore-removes-his-name-fro-20080721/ |website=The Playlist |access-date=April 8, 2022 |date=July 21, 2008}}</ref>}} and published by [[DC Comics]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=April 24, 2022 |title=Watchmen Creator Alan Moore Has Surprising Advice For Writers |url=https://screenrant.com/watchmen-creator-alan-moore-surprising-advice-for-writers/ |access-date=July 6, 2022 |website=ScreenRant |language=en-US}}</ref> Directed by [[Zack Snyder]], and written by [[David Hayter]] and [[Alex Tse]], the film stars [[Malin Akerman|Malin Åkerman]], [[Billy Crudup]], [[Matthew Goode]], [[Carla Gugino]], [[Jackie Earle Haley]], [[Jeffrey Dean Morgan]], and [[Patrick Wilson]]. A dark and [[dystopian]] deconstruction of the superhero genre, the film is set in an [[alternate history]] in the year 1985 at the height of the [[Cold War]], as a group of mostly retired American [[superheroes]] investigate the murder of one of their own before uncovering an elaborate and deadly conspiracy with which they are all connected.

For nearly two decades from October 1987 until October 2005, a [[live-action film]] adaptation of the ''Watchmen'' series became stranded in [[development hell]]. Producers [[Lawrence Gordon (producer)|Lawrence Gordon]] and [[Joel Silver]] began developing the project at [[20th Century Fox]], later moving it to [[Warner Bros. Pictures]], the sister company of ''Watchmen'' publisher DC Comics, and hiring director [[Terry Gilliam]], who eventually left the production and deemed the complex comic "[[unfilmable]]". During the 2000s, Gordon and [[Lloyd Levin]] collaborated with [[Universal Pictures]], [[Revolution Studios]] and [[Paramount Pictures]] to produce the film. Directors [[David Hayter]], [[Darren Aronofsky]], and [[Paul Greengrass]] were attached to the project before it was canceled over budget disputes. In October 2005, the project returned to Warner Bros., where Snyder was hired to direct. Paramount remained as its international distributor, whereas Warner Bros. would distribute the film in the United States. However, Fox sued Warner Bros. for copyright violation arising from Gordon's failure to pay a buy-out in 1991, which enabled him to develop the film at the other studios. Fox and Warner Bros. settled this before the film's release, with Fox receiving a portion of the gross. [[Principal photography]] began in [[Vancouver]], in September 2007. As with his previous film ''[[300 (film)|300]]'' (2006), Snyder closely modeled his [[storyboards]] on the comic but chose not to shoot all of ''Watchmen'' using [[chroma key|green screens]] and opted for real sets instead.

Following its world premiere at the [[Odeon Leicester Square]] in London on February 23, 2009, the film was released in the United States on March 6, by Warner Bros. Pictures. The film underperformed at the box office, grossing $187 million against a production budget of $130–138 million.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Watchmen#tab=summary | title=Watchmen (2009) - Financial Information }}</ref> Greg Silverman (former Warner Bros. executive) said that the film did later become profitable through streaming and home video sales.<ref name="twitter.com">{{Cite web | url=https://twitter.com/gregsilverman/status/1686034340323667968 | title=Greg Silverman on X | website=twitter.com | date=July 31, 2023}}</ref>

The film received mixed reviews from critics, who praised the striking visuals and the faithfulness to the source material, but criticized the complexity of the plot.<ref name=independent>{{cite news|last=Shepard|first=Jack|title=Watchmen at 10: The fascinating story of how the 'unfilmable' comic book series finally made it to the big screen|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/watchmen-10-anniversary-zack-snyder-terry-gilliam-darren-aronofsky-paul-greengrass-a8792791.html|website=[[The Independent|Independent]]|access-date=February 23, 2019|date=February 23, 2019|archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174200/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/watchmen-10-anniversary-zack-snyder-terry-gilliam-darren-aronofsky-paul-greengrass-a8792791.html |url-status=live }}</ref> A [[DVD]] based on elements of the ''Watchmen'' universe was released, including an animated adaptation of the ''Tales of the Black Freighter'' [[Story within a story|comic within the story]] voiced by [[Gerard Butler]] and a [[Mockumentary|fictional documentary]] titled ''Under the Hood'' detailing the older generation of superheroes from the film's backstory.<ref name="uth">{{cite news |last=Thill |first=Scott |title=Watchmen Back Story Unspools in Under the Hood DVD |url=https://www.wired.com/underwire/2009/03/watchmen-backst/ |work=Wired.com|access-date=July 9, 2010|date=March 23, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174158/https://www.wired.com/2009/03/watchmen-backst/|url-status=live}}</ref> A director's cut with 24 minutes of additional footage was released in July 2009. The "Ultimate Cut" edition incorporated the animated comic ''Tales of the Black Freighter'' into the narrative as it was in the original graphic novel, lengthening the runtime to 3 hours and 35 minutes, and was released on November 10, 2009. The director's cut is generally viewed as an improvement over the theatrical release.<ref name="independent" />

== Plot ==
In 1985, a man living in a [[Manhattan]] apartment watches news about escalating [[Cold War]] tensions and the response from five-term President [[Richard Nixon]] when an unknown assailant attacks and hurls him to the street below. Throughout the opening credits, a montage reviews the rise of costumed crime-fighters from 1939 to 1977, culminating in public backlash and the passage of an anti-vigilante act.

[[Rorschach (character)|Rorschach]], a vigilante detective who operates illegally, discovers that the dead man was Edward Blake, better known as "the [[Comedian (character)|Comedian]]", a costumed hero who worked for the government. Suspecting that other vigilantes could be attacked, Rorschach warns members of his former team, the Watchmen.<ref>{{Cite news|last=McMillan|first=Graeme|date=November 14, 2008|title=Who Names The Watchmen?|url=https://io9.gizmodo.com/5087386/who-names-the-watchmen|url-status=live|department=[[io9]] (Movies)|work=[[Gizmodo]]|publisher=[[Gizmodo Media Group]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180820042957/https://io9.gizmodo.com/5087386/who-names-the-watchmen|archive-date=August 20, 2018|access-date=August 19, 2018|quote=<!--punctuation and capitalization slightly modified from original-->As those who've read the original series know, neither of the story's two superteams are actually called 'the Watchmen'—There are the original Minutemen, and then the more modern Crimebusters—[s]o why is someone in the trailer telling the audience that 'the Watchmen are... over'?}}</ref> Rorschach's former partner [[Nite Owl|Dan Dreiberg]] believes he is paranoid, but relays his concerns to [[Adrian Veidt]], a crime-fighter turned businessman, but he also dismisses the concerns. Rorschach later visits [[Doctor Manhattan]], a physicist whose accidental superpowers make him a national security asset, but Manhattan is preoccupied with energy research and ignores him.

At Blake's funeral, Manhattan, Veidt and Dreiberg each recall the Comedian's pessimism in his later years about the Watchmen's<!-- see above --> mission. After the service, a lone mourner pays his respects. Rorschach tracks down and questions the mourner, former supervillain Edgar Jacobi. Jacobi says that Blake had recently broken into his apartment while he was sleeping — tearful, unmasked, and incoherent. Rorschach is astonished but doubts that Jacobi would tell a lie so bizarre. During a press interview with Doctor Manhattan, an investigative journalist tells him that several people who had been in contact with Manhattan have developed cancer, including his former girlfriend. As other reporters mob Manhattan with questions, he snaps and exiles himself to [[Mars]]. Alone, Manhattan reflects on his existence and his regrets at allowing himself to be turned into a weapon. In his absence, the [[Warsaw Pact]] countries make aggressive moves, and Nixon prepares for war.

Veidt survives an assassination attempt, suggesting that Rorschach's "mask-killer" theory is correct. Dreiberg takes in [[Silk Spectre|Laurie Jupiter]], a second-generation vigilante and estranged lover of Manhattan, to whom Dreiberg is attracted. Rorschach's investigation of the assassin leads him back to Jacobi. Rorschach finds Jacobi dead and himself framed for Jacobi's murder. After a battle with police, Rorschach is arrested and unmasked as a low-born vagrant. In prison, Rorschach defends his vigilantism to a psychiatrist, saying he cannot ignore evil and the people who cause it. Dreiberg and Jupiter, after donning their costumes and saving multiple people from a burning building, have sex. Imprisoned crime boss Big Figure stages a riot as a cover for assassinating Rorschach. The attack fails, and Rorschach kills Big Figure and his accomplices before leaving the prison with Dreiberg and Jupiter who have arrived to break him out.

Manhattan teleports Jupiter to Mars while Dreiberg joins Rorschach's investigation of the Blake murder. Evidence points them to Veidt as the mastermind; they find him at an Antarctic hideout, where he has just overseen the activation of Doctor Manhattan's energy reactors in [[New York City]] and other locations across the planet. On Mars, Jupiter tries to convince Manhattan that humanity is worth saving. She succeeds only when he learns that Jupiter is Blake's illegitimate daughter, a fact so unlikely (as Blake had once tried to [[rape]] Jupiter's mother) that it restores his respect for life.

Veidt admits orchestrating Manhattan's exile, staging the assassination, framing Rorschach, and killing Blake, who was spying on his activities. He has also executed the final step of his plan: turning the world against Manhattan by rigging his reactors to explode, killing 15 million people. Manhattan returns with Jupiter to a devastated New York, pieces together what has happened, and teleports to Veidt's hideout. After a brief struggle, Veidt shows him that the world's countries have put aside their rivalries to focus on a common enemy: Doctor Manhattan.

Realizing the logic of Veidt's plan, the Watchmen<!-- see above --> agree to keep his secret, except for Rorschach, whom Manhattan reluctantly kills to preserve the new global peace. Manhattan departs permanently for another galaxy while Dreiberg rebukes Veidt's moral sacrifice, and Jupiter finally comes to terms with her parentage. A New York tabloid editor, disgusted that there is no war to report on, tells a staff member to choose something from the "crank file", which contains Rorschach's journal.

=== The Minutemen ===

* [[Carla Gugino]] as Sally Jupiter / Silk Spectre: <br /> A retired superheroine, mother of Laurie Juspeczyk and the first Silk Spectre. Gugino's character ages from 25 years old in the 1940s to 67 years old in the 1980s, and the then-37-year-old actress wore prosthetics to reflect the aging process. When telling his agent he did not want the part, he was told to continue reading it and find out how important his character was.<ref name="empire"/> Morgan found the role a challenge, explaining, "For some reason, in reading the novel, you don't hate this guy even though he does things that are unmentionable [like beat up and sexually assault Jupiter]. == Production ==
{{Main|Production of Watchmen (2009 film)}}

In 1986, producers [[Lawrence Gordon (producer)|Lawrence Gordon]] and [[Joel Silver]] acquired film rights to ''Watchmen'' for [[20th Century Fox]].<ref name="thomson">{{Cite news |last=Thompson |first=Anne|title=Filmmakers intent on producing new comic-book movies|work=[[Sun-Sentinel]]|date=August 26, 1986|author-link=Anne Thompson (film critic)|url=https://www.sun-sentinel.com/1986/08/26/filmmakers-intent-on-producing-new-comic-book-movies/|access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref> After author [[Alan Moore]] declined to write a screenplay based on his story, Fox enlisted screenwriter [[Sam Hamm]]. Hamm rewrote ''Watchmen''{{'}}s complicated ending, making a "more manageable" conclusion involving an assassination and a time paradox.<ref name="hughes">{{Cite book |last=Hughes |first=David |title=The Greatest Sci-Fi Movies Never Made |publisher=Chicago Review Press; updated and expanded edition Titan Books (2008) |date=April 22, 2002 |isbn=978-1-84576-755-6 |chapter=Who Watches the ''Watchmen''? – How The Greatest Graphic Novel of Them All Confounded Hollywood }}</ref> Fox put the project into [[turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]] in 1991,<ref name="turnaround">{{Cite news |last=Cieply |first=Michael |title=Battle Over 'Watchmen' Surrounds a Producer|work=[[The New York Times]]|date=September 20, 2008 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2008/09/20/movies/20watc.html?pagewanted=all |access-date=September 20, 2008|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174225/https://www.nytimes.com/2008/09/20/movies/20watc.html?pagewanted=all|url-status=live}}</ref> and the project was moved to [[Warner Bros. Pictures]], where [[Terry Gilliam]] was attached to direct and [[Charles McKeown]] to rewrite the script. Gilliam and Silver were only able to raise $25 million for the film, a quarter of the necessary budget, because their previous films had gone overbudget.<ref name="hughes"/> Gilliam eventually left ''Watchmen'', describing the comic as "[[unfilmable]]", and Warner Bros. dropped the project.<ref>{{Cite news |date=November 13, 2000 |author=Willow Green |title=Python Won't Bite For ''Watchmen'' |work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire Online]] |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/python-wont-bite-watchmen/ |access-date=September 1, 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174206/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/python-wont-bite-watchmen/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In October 2001, Gordon partnered with [[Lloyd Levin]] and [[Universal Pictures]], hiring [[David Hayter]] to write and direct.<ref name=watches>{{Cite news|author=Stax|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/10/27/david-hayter-watches-the-watchmen|title=David Hayter Watches ''The Watchmen''|work=[[IGN]]|date=October 27, 2001|access-date=September 23, 2006|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174210/https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/10/27/david-hayter-watches-the-watchmen|url-status=live}}</ref> Hayter and the producers left Universal due to creative differences,<ref>{{Cite news |last=Kit |first=Borys |url=http://www.thebookstandard.com/bookstandard/news/hollywood/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1001700508 |title='Watchmen' on Duty at Warner Bros. |work=The Book Standard |date=December 19, 2005 |access-date=September 25, 2006 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060328114516/http://www.thebookstandard.com/bookstandard/news/hollywood/article_display.jsp?vnu_content_id=1001700508 |archive-date=March 28, 2006 }}</ref> and Gordon and Levin expressed interest in setting up ''Watchmen'' at [[Revolution Studios]]. The project did not hold together at Revolution Studios and subsequently fell apart.<ref name="revpar">{{cite web |last=Linder |first=Brian |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/07/23/aronofsky-still-watching-watchmen|title=Aronofksy Still Watching ''Watchmen''|website=IGN|date=July 23, 2004|access-date=October 18, 2008|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174234/https://www.ign.com/articles/2004/07/23/aronofsky-still-watching-watchmen|url-status=live}}</ref> In July 2004, it was announced [[Paramount Pictures]] would produce ''Watchmen'', and [[Michael Bay]] was considered to direct. Eventually, they attached [[Darren Aronofsky]] to direct Hayter's script. Producers Gordon and Levin remained attached, collaborating with Aronofsky's producing partner, Eric Watson.<ref>{{Cite news|author=Borys Kit|url=https://www.allbusiness.com/services/motion-pictures/4916505-1.html|title=''Watchmen'' unmasked for Par, Aronofsky|work=The Hollywood Reporter|date=July 23, 2004|access-date=September 23, 2006}}</ref> [[Paul Greengrass]] replaced Aronofsky when he left to focus on ''[[The Fountain]]''.<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Kit |first1=Borys |first2=Liza |last2=Foreman |url=https://www.allbusiness.com/services/amusement-recreation-services/4748794-1.html|title=Greengrass, Par on ''Watchmen''|work=The Hollywood Reporter|date=November 22, 2004|access-date=September 23, 2006}}</ref> Ultimately, Paramount placed ''Watchmen'' in turnaround.<ref>{{Cite news | url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/someone-watch-watchmen/|title=Someone To Watch Over ''Watchmen''|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire Online]]|date=June 7, 2005|access-date=September 23, 2006|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174209/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/someone-watch-watchmen/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Ten weeks before the film's release date, a Federal judge ruled that Fox controlled its distribution rights.<ref>{{cite court|vol=630|reporter=F.Supp.2d|opinion=1140|year=2008|litigants=Twentieth Century Fox Film Corp. v. [[Dave Gibbons]] became an adviser on Snyder's film, but Moore has refused to have his name attached to any film adaptations of his work.<ref name="Alan Moore - Films">{{cite web|last=MacDonald|first=Heidi|url=https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20050530/29889-moore-leaves-dc-for-top-shelf-.html|title=Moore Leaves DC for Top Shelf|work=[[Publishers Weekly]]|date=May 30, 2005|access-date=August 13, 2009}}</ref> Moore has stated he has no interest in seeing Snyder's adaptation; he told ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' in 2008, "There are things that we did with ''Watchmen'' that could only work in a comic, and were indeed designed to show off things that other media can't."<ref>{{Cite magazine|first=Nisha|last=Gopalan|url=https://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,20213067_20213068_20213004,00.html|title=Alan Moore Still Knows the Score!|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=July 16, 2008|access-date=July 22, 2008|archive-date=August 28, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080828180836/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,20213067_20213068_20213004,00.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> While Moore believes that David Hayter's screenplay was "as close as I could imagine anyone getting to ''Watchmen''," he asserted he did not intend to see the film if it were made.<ref name="history">{{Cite magazine|last1=Jensen|first1=Jeff|url=https://ew.com/books/2005/10/21/watchmen-oral-history/|title=''Watchmen'': An Oral History|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=October 25, 2005|access-date=February 2, 2007|archive-date=March 9, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090309093847/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,1120854_5,00.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

Regarding the changed ending in which Dr. Manhattan is blamed for nuclear devastation instead of an extraterrestrial [[Watchmen#Plot|squid]], Snyder "figured it took about 15 minutes to explain [the squid's appearance] correctly; otherwise, it's pretty crazy."<ref name=CarrollSpoilers>{{cite web|last=Carroll|first=Larry|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1605542/zack-snyder-explains-his-key-watchmen-changes-on-spoilers/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20201218235630/http://www.mtv.com/news/1605542/zack-snyder-explains-his-key-watchmen-changes-on-spoilers/|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 18, 2020|title=Zack Snyder Explains His Key 'Watchmen' Changes On 'Spoilers' |publisher=[[MTV]]|date=February 20, 2009|access-date=December 20, 2020}}</ref> By omitting the squid Snyder felt that he could give more time to explore and develop the existing characters.<ref name=CarrollSpoilers/> Oscar Gonzalez of [[CNET]] stated that "Because of this change, however, the movie is not canon in regards to the ''Watchmen'' TV series."<ref>{{cite web|last=Gonzalez|first=Oscar|url=https://www.cnet.com/news/watchmen-hbo-101-guide-to-the-comics-behind-the-series/|title=Watchmen 101: Your guide to the comics behind HBO's new series|publisher=[[CNET]]|date=December 27, 2019|access-date=December 20, 2020}}</ref> Earlier drafts had Veidt die, but Snyder reversed this change.<ref>{{cite web|last=Carroll|first=Larry|url=https://www.mtv.com/news/1605378/|archive-url=https://archive.today/20201220062553/https://www.mtv.com/news/1605378/|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 20, 2020|title='Watchmen' Director's Cut To Hit Theaters In July, Zack Snyder Reveals On MTV 'Spoilers' |publisher=[[MTV]]|date=February 19, 2009|access-date=December 20, 2020}}</ref>

== Music ==

Both a soundtrack and excerpts from [[Tyler Bates]]' [[film score]] were released as albums on March 3, 2009. == Reception ==

Snyder's previous comic book adaptation, earned.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.the-numbers.com/interactive/newsStory.php?newsID=4078|title=Watchmen Starts with $4.550 Million at Midnight|publisher=The-Numbers|date=March 6, 2009|access-date=March 10, 2009|archive-date=November 16, web|url=https://movieweb.com/watchmen-gets-biggest-r-rated-rollout/|title=Watchmen Gets Biggest R-Rated Rollout!|date=March 5, 2009 }}</ref> ''Watchmen''{{'}}s opening weekend is the highest of any Following its first week at the box office, ''Watchmen'' saw a significant drop in attendance. By the end of its second weekend, the film brought in $17,817,301, finishing second on that weekend's box office chart. The 67.7% overall decrease was at the time of its release one of the highest for a major comic book film.<ref name=second>{{cite web| url= https://www.boxofficemojo.com/article/ed3833201668/| title= Weekend Report: 'Witch' Blasts Off, 'Watchmen' Burns Out| website= Box Office Mojo| date= March 16, 2009| access-date= August 12, 2009| last= Gray| first= Brandon| archive-date= November 16, 2020| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174228/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/article/ed3833201668/| url-status= live}}</ref> Losing two-thirds of its audience from its opening weekend, the film finished second for the weekend of March 13–15, 2009.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2009W11/ |title=March 13–15, 2009 Weekend Studio Estimates |website=Box Office Mojo |access-date=March 15, 2009 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174229/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2009W11/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The film continued to drop about 60% in almost every subsequent weekend, leaving the top ten in its fifth weekend, and the top twenty in its seventh.<ref name=weekend/> ''Watchmen'' crossed the $100 million mark on March 26, its twenty-first day at the box office,<ref name="DBO"/> and finished its theatrical run in the United States on May 28, having grossed $107,509,799 in 84 days. The film had grossed one fifth of its ultimate gross on its opening day, and more than half of that total by the end of its opening weekend.<ref name="DBO"/>

''Watchmen'' was the 31st-highest-grossing film of 2009,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Domestic Box Office For 2009 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/2009/ |access-date=June 21, 2023 |website=Box Office Mojo}}</ref> and the sixth-highest-grossing R-rated film of the year, behind ''[[The Hangover]]'', ''[[Inglourious Basterds]]'', ''[[District 9]]'', ''[[Paranormal Activity]]'', and ''[[It's Complicated (film)|It's Complicated]]''.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/yearly/chart/mpaarating.htm?yr=2009&rating=R&p=.htm|title=2009 Yearly Box Office by MPAA Rating|work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=March 12, 2009|archive-date=August 30, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090830200854/http://boxofficemojo.com/yearly/chart/mpaarating.htm?rating=R&yr=2009&p=.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> At the North American box office, ''Watchmen'' currently sits in the lower half of the forty-six films based on a DC Comics comic book, narrowly ahead of 1997's ''[[Batman & Robin (film)|Batman & Robin]]''.<ref>{{cite news |title=DC Comics Adaptations Comparison |website=Box Office Mojo |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/brand/bn3664968194/ |url-status=live |access-date=March 10, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174237/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/ |archive-date=November 16, 2020}}</ref>

''Watchmen'' earned $26.6 million in 45 territories overseas; of these, Britain and France had the highest box office with an estimated $4.6 million and $2.5 million, respectively.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.reuters.com/article/entertainmentNews/idUSTRE5250NQ20090310|title='Watchmen' falls short of expected box office take|work=[[Reuters]]|date=March 8, 2009|access-date=March 10, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174231/https://www.reuters.com/article/entertainmentNews/idUSTRE5250NQ20090310|url-status=live}}</ref> ''Watchmen'' also took in approximately $2.3 million in Russia, $2.3 million in Australia, $1.6 million in Italy, and $1.4 million in South Korea.<ref>{{cite news|first=Frank|last=Segers|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/filmNews/idUSTRE52818920090309|title="Watchmen" rules overseas box office|work=Reuters|date=March 9, 2009|access-date=March 10, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174233/https://www.reuters.com/article/filmNews/idUSTRE52818920090309|url-status=live}}</ref> The film collected $77,873,014 in other territories, bringing its worldwide total to $185,382,813.<ref name="Mojo" />

=== Critical response ===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Watchmen'' has {{a or an|{{RT data|score}}}} approval rating based on 304 reviews, and an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The site's critical consensus reads: "Gritty and visually striking, ''Watchmen'' is a faithful adaptation of Alan Moore's graphic novel, but its complex narrative structure may make it difficult for it to appeal to viewers not already familiar with the source material."<ref name="rt">{{Cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/watchmen|title=Watchmen|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140916115359/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/watchmen/|archive-date=September 16, 2014|url-status=live}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], which assigns a [[weighted mean|weighted average]] rating reviews from mainstream critics, the film has a score of 56 out of 100, based on 39 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="meta">{{cite news |title=''Watchmen'' (2009): Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/watchmen |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174245/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/watchmen |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |access-date=February 22, 2009 |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=CNET Networks}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale; the primary audience was older men.<ref>{{Cite magazine|author=Joshua Rich|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2009/SHOWBIZ/Movies/03/08/boxoffice.ew/|title='Watchmen' takes box office lead|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=March 8, 2009|access-date=March 8, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174247/https://edition.cnn.com/2009/SHOWBIZ/Movies/03/08/boxoffice.ew/|url-status=live}}</ref><!--Please do not add IMDb ratings. [[WP:UGC]] They are an unreliable representation of fan reaction.!-->

Patrick Kolan of ''[[IGN Australia]]'' awarded it a perfect 10/10 and wrote, "It's the ''Watchmen'' film you always wanted to see, but never expected to get."<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2009/02/24/watchmen-au-review|title=Watchmen AU Review|website=IGN AU|date=February 24, 2009|last=Kolan|first=Patrick|access-date=August 13, 2020|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174232/https://www.ign.com/articles/2009/02/24/watchmen-au-review|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] gave it four out of four stars and wrote, "It's a compelling visceral film—sound, images and characters combined into a decidedly odd visual experience that evokes the feel of a graphic novel."<ref>{{cite web |date=March 4, 2009 |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Watchmen movie review & film summary (2009) |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/watchmen-2009 |website=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |access-date=September 17, 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174242/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/watchmen-2009 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Richard Corliss of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' concluded, "this ambitious picture is a thing of bits and pieces," yet "the bits are glorious, the pieces magnificent."<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://time.com/time/arts/article/0,8599,1883200-1,00.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090309205952/http://www.time.com/time/arts/article/0,8599,1883200-1,00.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=March 9, 2009 |title=Watchmen Review: (A Few) Moments of Greatness|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|TIME]] |last=Corliss |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Corliss |date=March 4, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009}}</ref> Jonathan Crocker of ''[[Total Film]]'' awarded it 4/5 stars, writing, "It's hard to imagine anyone watching the ''Watchmen'' as faithfully as Zack Snyder's heartfelt, stylised adap. Uncompromising, uncommercial, and unique."<ref>{{Cite news|date=February 24, 2009|title=Watchmen (18)|url=https://www.gamesradar.com/watchmen-1/|last=Crocker|first=Jonathan|work=Total Film |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20090225074040/http://www.totalfilm.com/reviews/cinema/watchmen  |archive-date =  February 25, 2009|url-status=dead |access-date=April 19, 2019 }}</ref> When comparing the film with the original source material, Ian Nathan of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' felt that while "it isn't the graphic novel... Zack Snyder clearly gives a toss, creating a smart, stylish, decent adaptation."<ref>{{cite web |date=January 1, 2000 |author=Ian Nathan |title=Watchmen |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/watchmen-review/ |website=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |access-date=September 17, 2020 |archive-date=November 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174232/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/watchmen-review/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Nick Dent of ''[[Time Out (company)|Time Out]] Sydney'' gave the film 4 out of 5 in his review of February 25, praising the film's inventiveness but concluding: {{cquote|While ''Watchmen'' is still as rich, daring, and intelligent an action film as there's ever been, it also proves Moore absolutely right [that ''Watchmen'' is inherently un-filmable]. As a comic book, ''Watchmen'' is an extraordinary thing. As a movie, it's just another movie, awash with sound and fury.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.timeoutsydney.com.au/film/reviews/watchmen.aspx |title=Watchmen Review |work=[[Time Out (company)|Time Out]] Sydney |last=Dent |first=Nick |access-date=August 1, 2020 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101103064245/http://www.timeoutsydney.com.au/film/reviews/watchmen.aspx |archive-date=November 3, 2010 }}</ref>}}

Some critics who wrote negative reviews disliked the film's use and depiction of the [[Cold War]]-period setting, stating that the film's attempt to use the 1980s fears that never came to pass felt dated, and that Snyder's slavish devotion to faithfully adapting the source material as literally as possible did not allow his work to exhibit a creative distinctiveness of its own, and that as a result, the film and its characters lacked vitality and authenticity.<ref name="blight"/><ref name="reader"/><ref name="hopeless"/><ref name="varreview"/><ref name="powbam"/><ref name="owen"/> Philip Kennicott of ''[[The Washington Post]]'', for example wrote, "''Watchmen'' is a bore [...] It sinks under the weight of its reverence for the original."<ref name="blight">{{cite news | url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2009/03/04/AR2009030403970.html|title=Blight 'Watchmen'|newspaper=The Washington Post|last=Kennicott|first=Philip|date=March 5, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009|archive-date=June 4, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110604200208/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/content/article/2009/03/04/AR2009030403970.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Devin Gordon wrote for ''[[Newsweek]]'', "That's the trouble with loyalty. Too little, and you alienate your core fans. Too much, and you lose everyone—and everything—else."<ref name="trouble">{{cite news|url=https://www.newsweek.com/id/186958/page/1|title=The "Watchmen" Movie and the Trouble With Loyalty|work=[[Newsweek]]|last=Gordon|first=Devin|date=February 28, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009|archive-date=April 1, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090401144638/http://www.newsweek.com/id/186958/page/1|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Owen Gleiberman's ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' review reads, "Snyder treats each image with the same stuffy hermetic reverence. He doesn't move the camera or let the scenes breathe. He crams the film with bits and pieces, trapping his actors like bugs wriggling in the frame."<ref name="owen">{{cite magazine | url=https://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,20262574,00.html|title=Movie Review: Watchmen (2009)|magazine=Entertainment Weekly|first=Owen|last=Gleiberman|date=March 2, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174253/https://ew.com/article/2009/03/04/watchmen-2/|url-status=dead}}</ref> "[Snyder] never pause[s] to develop a vision of his own. The result is oddly hollow and disjointed; the actors moving stiffly from one overdetermined tableau to another," said Noah Berlatsky of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]''.<ref name="reader">{{cite news | url=https://onfilm.chicagoreader.com/movies/capsules/34160_WATCHMEN|work=Chicago Reader|title=Watchmen|first=Noah|last=Berlatsky|access-date=June 18, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174248/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/film/Section?oid=846987&path=|url-status=live}}</ref>

David Edelstein of ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' agrees: "They've made the most reverent adaptation of a graphic novel ever. But this kind of reverence kills what it seeks to preserve. The movie is embalmed."<ref name="hopeless">{{cite news | url=https://nymag.com/movies/reviews/55005/|title=Hopelessly Devoted|work=New York Magazine|first=David|last=Edelstein|date=February 27, 2009|access-date=June 18, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174239/https://nymag.com/movies/reviews/55005/|url-status=live}}</ref> Joe Morgenstern of ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'' wrote, "Watching 'Watchmen' is the spiritual equivalent of being whacked on the skull for 163 minutes. The reverence is inert, the violence noxious, the mythology murky, the tone grandiose, the texture glutinous."<ref name="powbam">{{cite news | url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB123629428724445423|title=Pow! Bam! 'Watchmen' Batters Public|first=Joe|last=Morgenstern|date=March 6, 2009|work=[[Wall Street Journal]]|access-date=June 18, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174244/https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB123629428724445423|url-status=live}}</ref> Donald Clarke of ''[[The Irish Times]]'' was similarly dismissive: "Snyder, director of the unsubtle ''300'', has squinted hard at the source material and turned it into a colossal animated storyboard, augmented by indifferent performances and moronically obvious music cues."<ref name="irish">{{cite news | url=https://www.irishtimes.com/newspaper/theticket/2009/0306/1224242314804.html|newspaper=The Irish Times|title=Watchmen Review|first=Donald|last=Clarke|date=March 6, 2009|access-date=June 18, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174240/https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/watchmen-1.716611|url-status=live}}</ref>

The trade magazines ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' and ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' were even less taken with the film. Justin Chang of ''Variety'' commented, "The movie is ultimately undone by its own reverence; there's simply no room for these characters and stories to breathe of their own accord, and even the most fastidiously replicated scenes can feel glib and truncated,"<ref name="varreview">{{Cite journal|last=Chang|first=Justin|url=https://variety.com/2009/film/features/watchmen-1200473457/|title=Watchmen (review)|journal=Variety|date=June 2, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009|archive-date=November 16, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116174310/https://variety.com/2009/film/features/watchmen-1200473457/|url-status=live}}</ref> and Kirk Honeycutt of ''The Hollywood Reporter'' writing, "The real disappointment is that the film does not transport an audience to another world, as ''300'' did. Nor does the third-rate [[Raymond Chandler|Chandler]]-esque narration by Rorschach help...Looks like we have the first real flop of 2009."<ref name="hrreview">{{Cite journal|last=Honeycutt|first=Kirk|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/film-reviews/film-review-watchmen-1003945726.story|title=Film Review: ''Watchmen''|journal=The Hollywood Reporter|date=February 26, 2009|access-date=August 12, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090228142902/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/film-reviews/film-review-watchmen-1003945726.story|archive-date=February 28, 2009}}</ref>

Analyzing the divided response, Geoff Boucher of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' felt that, like ''[[Eyes Wide Shut]]'', ''[[The Passion of the Christ]]'', or ''[[Fight Club]]'', ''Watchmen'' would continue to be a talking point among those who liked or disliked the film. Boucher felt in spite of his own mixed feelings about the finished film, he was "oddly proud" that the director had made a faithful adaptation that was "nothing less than the boldest popcorn movie ever made. Snyder somehow managed to get a major studio to make a movie with no stars, no 'name' superheroes and a hard R-rating, thanks to all those broken bones, that oddly off-putting Owl Ship sex scene and, of course, the unforgettable glowing blue penis."<ref>{{Cite news|author=Geoff Boucher|title=Is 'Watchmen' the 'Fight Club' of superhero films?|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=March 10, 2009|url=http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/herocomplex/2009/03/is-watchmen-the.html|access-date=March 13, 2009|archive-date=March 13, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090313182012/http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/herocomplex/2009/03/is-watchmen-the.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2023, director [[Christopher Nolan]] said that Snyder's version of ''Watchmen'' was ahead of its time and that it should have been released "post-''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|Avengers]]''". He added that "The idea of a superhero team, which it brilliantly subverts, wasn't a thing yet in movies."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/christopher-nolan-zack-snyder-watchmen-ahead-of-its-time-1234930386/|website=IndieWire|title=Christopher Nolan Calls Zack Snyder's 'Watchmen' a Film 'Ahead of Its Time': It Should Have Been Released Post-'Avengers'|date=November 2023|author=Samantha Bergeson}}</ref>
