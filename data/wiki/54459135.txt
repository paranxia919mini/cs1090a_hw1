'''''The Annihilation of Fish''''' is a 1999 American [[romance film]] directed by [[Charles Burnett (director)|Charles Burnett]] and starring [[Lynn Redgrave]], [[James Earl Jones]] and [[Margot Kidder]].<ref name="V">{{cite web|last=McCarthy|first=Todd|title=Review: 'The Annihilation of Fish'|date=September 22, 1999|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|url=https://variety.com/1999/film/reviews/the-annihilation-of-fish-1117752169/|access-date=January 2, 2024}}</ref><ref name="FT">{{cite web|first=Merle|last=Bertrand|title=The Annihilation of Fish|date=May 18, 2000|website=[[Film Threat]]|url=https://filmthreat.com/uncategorized/the-annihilation-of-fish/|access-date=January 2, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=TOG1EhsX-jEC&dq=the+annihilation+of+fish+charles+burnett&pg=PR36|last1=Burnett|first1=Charles|last2=Kapsis|first2=Robert E.|title=Charles Burnett: Interviews|year=2011|publisher=University Press of Mississippi|isbn=9781604739503|page=xxxvi}}</ref>

==Plot==
{{more plot|section|date=January 2024}}
Wistful and lonely Flower 'Poinsettia' Cummings is freshly out of a relationship with the dead composer [[Giacomo Puccini]], while Jamaican immigrant Obadiah 'Fish' Johnson has just been released from a mental institution that cannot cure him of the antagonistic demon that follows him wherever he goes. When the two become neighbors, they embark on an unlikely romance.

==Reception==
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|76|6.7|29}}<ref>{{cite web|title=''The Annihilation of Fish''|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_annihilation_of_fish_2000/|access-date=September 9, 2026}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 63 out of 100, based on 9 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Annihilation of Fish'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-annihilation-of-fish |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=September 9, 2026 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

Merle Bertrand of ''[[Film Threat]]'' wrote, "Romantic comedies have become something of a tired staple in indie filmmaking, these days. Yet, odd as it may seem, it’s the unlikely interracial geriatric chops on display in ''The Annihilation of Fish'' that breathe new life into the genre."<ref name="FT" /> [[Todd McCarthy]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote, "It’s hard to imagine what the filmmakers were thinking when they put this project together, in that it’s a picture about two oldsters with very little forward momentum, no subplots and the barest of production values. The stars and director Charles Burnett have names to reckon with, but they’ll all have to write this one off as a misguided bit of whimsy."<ref name="V" />

Upon the film's re-release in 2025, Robert Daniels of [[RogerEbert.com]] gave the film four out of four stars and wrote, "In ''The Annihilation of Fish'', age is the accumulation of scars. Aching memories, unhealed bruises and pent-up desires remain alive even when many of the sights and sounds that once decorated one's existence are dormant. For James Earl Jones and Lynn Redgrave, two actors who were already in the autumn of their lives when the film was initially released a quarter century ago, age appears to have been an especially potent subject."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-annihilation-of-fish-film-review-2025|title=The Annihilation of Fish|first=Robert|last=Daniels|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|date=February 2025|access-date=February 14, 2025}}</ref>

== Preservation ==
''The Annihilation of Fish'' was restored by the [[UCLA Film & Television Archive]] and [[The Film Foundation]], in collaboration with [[Milestone Films]], from the 35mm original picture and track negatives. Restoration funding was provided by the [[Mellody Hobson|Hobson]]/[[George Lucas|Lucas]] Family Foundation. The restoration had its west coast premiere at the 2024 UCLA Festival of Preservation.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Annihilation of Fish |website=[[UCLA Film & Television Archive]] |url=https://cinema.ucla.edu/events/2024/04/05/annihilation-of-fish |access-date=February 5, 2025}}</ref>
