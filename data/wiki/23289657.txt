'''''Can She Bake a Cherry Pie?''''' is a 1983 American [[comedy film]] directed by [[Henry Jaglom]]. It was screened in the ''[[Un Certain Regard]]'' section at the [[1983 Cannes Film Festival]]. The film takes place in and was filmed in the [[New York City]] borough of [[Manhattan]]. It was released to mixed reviews.

==Plot==
Zee, a middle-aged musician who was abandoned by her husband, goes to a cafe in Manhattan where she orders the entire dessert section while crying. Eli, a middle-aged divorced social worker, notices and comes over to start a conversation to cheer her up. The two of them later start a relationship, but that relationship has problems that include jealousy, mistrust, and the fear of marriage. Things improve and they became a happy couple, despite peculiarities such as Eli using a meter to measure his heart rate during sex and hanging upside down in the closet. The main supporting character is Larry, who tries to seduce Zee.

==Production==
''Can She Bake a Cherry Pie?'' was filmed on the [[Upper West Side]] of Manhattan, at locations within a 10-block radius of the [[American Museum of Natural History]].<ref name="New">{{cite web |url=https://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9E04EEDA1F39F93BA35751C1A965948260 |title=Screen: Karen Black In Cherry Pie |last=Maslin |first=Janet |date=December 8, 1983 |website=The New York Times |access-date=July 16, 2017}}</ref> Some of the places in the area do not exist anymore.<ref name="DVD">{{cite web |url=http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/60778/henry-jaglom-collection-vol-2-the-comedies/ |title=Henry Jaglom Collection vol. 2: The Comedies (Sitting Ducks / Can She Bake a Cherry Pie? / New Year's Day) |last=Galbraith IV |first=Stuart |date=May 21, 2013 |website=DVD Talk |access-date=July 16, 2017}}</ref> The filmmakers relied on the regular pedestrian traffic to background the scenes, rather than hiring [[Extra (acting)|extras]].<ref name=cin/>

[[Karen Black]], who stars as Zee, composed the music for the film.<ref name="Off">{{cite book |last=Skorman |first=Richard |date=1989 |title=Off-Hollywood Movies: A Film Lover's Guide |publisher=Harmony Books |page=[https://archive.org/details/offhollywoodmovi00skor/page/87 87] |isbn=0517568632 |url=https://archive.org/details/offhollywoodmovi00skor/page/87 }}</ref> Cameos include [[Orson Welles]] as a magician and [[Larry David]] as Eli's friend.<ref name="New"/><ref name="DVD"/>

The title of the movie comes from the lyric in the folk song "[[Billy Boy]]".<ref name=cin>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/100737712/|title='Pie' is a Slice of Charm|newspaper=[[The Cincinnati Enquirer]]|date=June 8, 1984|page=60|via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref>

==Release==
The film was originally released at the [[1983 Cannes Film Festival]] in the ''[[Un Certain Regard]]'' section.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/films/can-she-bake-a-cherry-pie |title=Can She Bake a Cherry Pie |website=Festival De Cannes |access-date=July 16, 2017}}</ref> It later opened at only one New York theatre, the 68th Street Playhouse.<ref name=cin/> It was released on VHS by itself<ref name="TLA"/> and was released on DVD as a part of Henry Jaglom Collection vol. 2: The Comedies, along with ''[[Sitting Ducks (film)|Sitting Ducks]]'' and ''[[New Year's Day (1989 film)|New Years Day]]''. The film was presented in widescreen and the only special features for the film were trailers.<ref name="DVD"/>

==Reception==
Critical reaction was mixed. Film critic [[David Thomson (film critic)|David Thomson]] wrote:
<blockquote>''Cherry Pie'' is an actors' film in that it grows out of their personalities. It is as loose and unexpected as life, but as shaped and witty as a great short story. In truth, a new kind of film, about love, talk, happiness and failure, about acting and speaking, about New York and Karen Black, and about the way in which a germ of a situation and good players can build an absorbing, vital picture ... 'Cherry Pie' is a unique, memorable and daring film and could be the surprise hit of the year. It shows us that there is still a chance for confident, inspired charm in movies.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/172402706/|title=Can She Bake a Cherry Pie?|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=5 February 1984|page=392|via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref></blockquote>

A ''[[Sun-Sentinel]]'' reviewer called the film "endearing".<ref>{{cite web |last=Russell |first=Candice |date=June 15, 1990 |title=Jaglom's 'New Year's Day' Leaves Annoying Whine Hangover |url=https://www.sun-sentinel.com/1990/06/15/jagloms-new-years-day-leaves-annoying-whine-hangover/ |access-date=September 19, 2024 |work=[[Sun-Sentinel]]}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref> A review in the book ''TLA Film, Video, and DVD Guide 2002-2003'' said: "Pure emotion meets pure logic and the result is an oddly charming couple".<ref name="TLA">{{cite book |last=Bleiler |first=David |date=November 3, 2001 |title=TLA Film, Video, and DVD Guide 2002-2003: The Discerning Film Lover's Guide |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=KyQEAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA95 |publisher=Macmillan |page=95 |isbn=9780312282097}}</ref> On the other hand, ''[[People (magazine)|People]]'' called the plot "aimless" and incoherent.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://people.com/archive/picks-and-pans-review-can-she-bake-a-cherry-pie-vol-21-no-2/|title=Picks and Pans Review: Can She Bake a Cherry Pie?|date=January 16, 1984|access-date=July 26, 2017|work=[[People (magazine)|People]]}}</ref> A ''[[TV Guide (magazine)|TV Guide]]'' review said: "Decent performances are not enough to save this movie, in which some scenes stretch on much longer than they should, making the genuinely funny moments few and far between".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.tvguide.com/movies/can-she-bake-a-cherry-pie/review/123283/ |title=Can She Bake A Cherry Pie? |website=TV Guide |access-date=July 16, 2017}}{{dead link|date=July 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>

The film received two and a half stars in the book ''Off-Hollywood Movies''.<ref name="Off"/> It is considered one of Jaglom's more successful films.<ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OJAcf31BweQC&pg=PA163|title=Contemporary British and Irish Film Directors: A Wallflower Critical Guide|editor-first=Yoram |editor-last=Allon|editor2-first=Del|editor2-last=Cullen|editor3-first=Hannah|editor3-last=Patterson|year=2001|publisher=Wallflower Press|isbn=1903364213|page=163}}</ref>
