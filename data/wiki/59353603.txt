'''''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness''''' is a 2022 American<!--Do NOT add horror as it is not a main genre per [[MOS:FILMGENRE]]--> [[superhero<!--Do NOT add horror as it is not a main genre per [[MOS:FILMGENRE]]--> film]] based on [[Marvel Comics]] featuring the character [[Doctor Strange]]. Produced by [[Marvel Studios]] and distributed by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]], it is the sequel to ''[[Doctor Strange (2016 film)|Doctor Strange]]'' (2016) and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 28th film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Sam Raimi]], written by [[Michael Waldron]], and stars [[Benedict Cumberbatch]] as [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]], alongside [[Elizabeth Olsen]], [[Chiwetel Ejiofor]], [[Benedict Wong]], [[Xochitl Gomez]], [[Michael Stuhlbarg]], and [[Rachel McAdams]]. In the film, Strange must protect [[America Chavez (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|America Chavez]] (Gomez), a teenager capable of traveling the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]], from the [[Wanda Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Scarlet Witch]] (Olsen).

''Doctor Strange'' director and co-writer [[Scott Derrickson]] had plans for a sequel by October 2016. He signed to return as director in December 2018, when Cumberbatch was confirmed to return. The film's title was announced in July 2019 along with Olsen's involvement, while [[Jade Halley Bartlett]] was hired to write the film that October. Derrickson stepped down as director in January 2020, citing creative differences. Waldron and Raimi joined the following month and started over, adding elements of the horror genre that Raimi had worked with previously and making Wanda the villain of the film, continuing her story from the [[Disney+]] miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021). Filming began in November 2020 in London but was put on hold in January 2021 due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. Production resumed by March 2021 and concluded in mid-April in [[Somerset]]. Shooting also occurred in [[Surrey]] and Los Angeles. With a production budget of $350.6&nbsp;million, ''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' is one of the [[List of most expensive films|most expensive films ever made]].

''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' premiered at the [[Dolby Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on May 2, 2022, and was released in the United States on May 6, as part of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] of the MCU. The film received generally positive reviews from critics and grossed $955.8&nbsp;million worldwide, making it the [[2022 in film#Highest-grossing films|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2022]].

== Plot ==
A [[demon]] chases [[America Chavez (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|America Chavez]] and [[Defender Strange|a variant of Stephen Strange]] in the space between universes while searching for the ''[[Book of Vishanti (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Book of Vishanti]]''. Strange is killed and Chavez accidentally creates a portal that transports herself and Strange's corpse to [[Earth-616 (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Earth-616]],{{efn|The film gives the name Earth-616 to the main reality depicted in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]].<ref name="UniverseDesignations" />}} where that universe's version of [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]] rescues Chavez from another demon{{efn|Identified off-screen as [[Gargantos]]<ref name="GargantosName" />}} with help from [[Wong (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wong]], the [[Sorcerer Supreme]]. Chavez explains that the beings are hunting her because she has the power to travel through the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]], but is unable to consciously control it.

Recognizing witchcraft runes on the demon, Strange consults [[Wanda Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wanda Maximoff]] but soon realizes that she is responsible for the attacks. After acquiring the ''[[Darkhold (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Darkhold]]'' and becoming the [[Scarlet Witch]], Wanda believes that taking Chavez's powers and traveling through the multiverse will allow her to reunite with [[Billy Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Billy]] and [[Tommy Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tommy]], the children she created during her time in [[Westview, New Jersey|Westview]].{{efn|As depicted in the television miniseries ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021)<ref name="RSWVOralHistory" />}} When Strange refuses to surrender Chavez, Wanda attacks [[Kamar-Taj (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kamar-Taj]], kills many sorcerers, and captures Wong. Chavez accidentally transports herself and Strange to [[Earth-838]]. Wanda uses the ''Darkhold'' to "[[Dreamwalking (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|dreamwalk]]", taking control of her Earth-838 counterpart who lives a suburban life with her own Billy and Tommy. After a surviving sorceress, Sara, sacrifices herself to destroy the ''Darkhold'' and break the dreamwalk, an enraged Wanda forces Wong to lead her to [[Mount Wundagore (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mount Wundagore]], the source of the ''Darkhold''{{'s}} power, to reestablish the dreamwalk.

While searching for help, Strange and Chavez are apprehended by Earth-838's Sorcerer Supreme, [[Karl Mordo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Karl Mordo]], and brought before the [[Illuminati (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Illuminati]], a group consisting of Mordo, [[Captain Carter#Earth-838|Peggy Carter]], [[Black Bolt (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Blackagar Boltagon]], [[Maria Rambeau]], [[Reed Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Reed Richards]], and [[Charles Xavier (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Charles Xavier]]. They explain that through reckless use of their universe's ''Darkhold'' in an attempt to defeat [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)#Other universes|Thanos]], [[Supreme Strange|Earth-838's Strange]] triggered a universe-destroying "[[Incursion (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|incursion]]". After defeating Thanos, the Illuminati executed their Strange to prevent him from causing more harm. Mordo believes that Earth-616's Strange is similarly dangerous, but Wanda reestablishes her dreamwalk at Mount Wundagore and arrives in her Earth-838 counterpart's body before the Illuminati can pass judgment. She brutally kills all the Illuminati except Mordo, whom Strange subdues before fleeing with Chavez. The two escape with help from the Earth-838 version of Strange's ex-fiancé, [[Christine Palmer (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Christine Palmer]], a scientist working with the Illuminati.

Strange, Chavez, and Palmer enter the space between universes to find the ''Book of Vishanti'', which is the antithesis of the ''Darkhold'', but Wanda appears and destroys it. She then takes over Chavez's mind and uses her powers to send Strange and Palmer to an incursion-destroyed universe. Strange defeats the destroyed universe's [[Sinister Strange|Strange]], who has been corrupted by his ''Darkhold'', and then uses that ''Darkhold'' to dreamwalk into the body of his deceased counterpart on Earth-616. With Wong's help, Strange saves Chavez from Wanda and encourages Chavez to use her abilities. Chavez transports Wanda to Earth-838, where she sees Billy and Tommy fearfully recoiling from her while crying for their real mother. Realizing her errors, Wanda relents and uses her powers to bring down Mount Wundagore, simultaneously destroying all copies of the ''Darkhold'' throughout the multiverse and sacrificing herself in the process. Chavez returns Strange and Palmer to their respective universes.

Some time later, Kamar-Taj is under repair and the surviving sorcerers, joined by Chavez, continue training. Strange develops a [[third eye]] as a result of using the ''Darkhold'' and dreamwalking into a corpse. In a [[mid-credits scene]], Strange is approached by a sorceress{{efn|Identified off-screen as [[Clea (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Clea]]<ref name="CleaTheron" />}} who warns that his actions have triggered an incursion that he must help repair. Strange follows her into the [[Dark Dimension (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Dark Dimension]].

== Production ==
=== Development ===
''[[Doctor Strange (2016 film)|Doctor Strange]]'' (2016) co-writer [[C. Robert Cargill]] stated in April 2016 that [[Marvel Studios]] felt some initial ideas for the film from him and director [[Scott Derrickson]] highlighted too much of the "weird stuff" associated with the comic book character [[Doctor Strange]] to feature in an [[origin story]], but told the duo to hold onto them for potential future films.<ref name="CargillPodcast" /> Derrickson revealed in October that he had plans for a sequel, expressing his love for the character and the visual possibilities that come with him. Derrickson said the first film was "the tip of an iceberg. There's so much progress that can be made." He wanted to follow the example of the film ''[[The Dark Knight]]'' (2008) and introduce a villain in the sequel that would allow them to "go deep [and have] a more visceral experience". Star [[Benedict Cumberbatch]] had signed on for at least one more Doctor Strange film.<ref name="DenofGeekOct2016" /> Derrickson expressed interest in featuring the villain [[Nightmare (Marvel Comics)|Nightmare]],<ref name="IGNOct2016" /> and further exploring the characters [[Jonathan Pangborn]] and [[Hamir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hamir]] after their small roles in the first film. He explained that he had been "kept in the loop" on how the ''Avengers'' films were using Strange due to his close relationships with [[Russo brothers|Joe Russo]]—the co-director of the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU) films ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018) and ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)—and Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]].<ref name="DerricksonSRNov2016" /> ''Doctor Strange'' co-writer [[Jon Spaihts]] expressed interest in seeing the character [[Clea (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Clea]] appear in a sequel.<ref name="CleaSequelPotential" /> In April 2017, Derrickson was reported to be returning for a sequel, beginning work after fulfilling his commitments to the television series ''[[Locke & Key (TV series)|Locke & Key]]'' (2020–2022).<ref name="DerricksonSequelReturn" />

[[File:Scott Derrickson (48470896191) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|Original director Scott Derrickson announcing the film at the 2019 San Diego Comic-Con]]
By December 2018, Derrickson had quietly finalized a deal to direct the sequel, with Cumberbatch, [[Benedict Wong]], and [[Rachel McAdams]] reprising their respective roles of Dr. [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stephen Strange]], [[Wong (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wong]], and [[Christine Palmer (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Christine Palmer]]. Marvel was beginning to search for a writer,<ref name="DerricksonSequelConfirmed" /><ref name="DerricksonSequelConfirmed2" /> with ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' stating that the script would be written throughout 2019 for a planned filming start in early 2020 and a potential release in May 2021.<ref name="DerricksonSequelConfirmed" /> Feige and Derrickson officially announced the sequel at [[San Diego Comic-Con]] in July 2019, revealing the title to be ''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' and confirming a release date of May 7, 2021.<ref name="ComicCon2019" /> Derrickson wanted the sequel to be the first scary MCU film and explore more of the gothic and horror elements from the comic books than the first installment did,<ref name="ComicCon2019" /><ref name="ComicCon2019Scary" /> with co-producer [[Richie Palmer]] explaining that the film would use the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]] to explore "each definition of the word madness" and the idea that "the scariest monsters are the ones that dwell within us".<ref name="PalmerTitleMeaning" /> Feige revealed that the [[Disney+]] series ''[[WandaVision]]'' (2021) would directly set up the film, with [[Elizabeth Olsen]] reprising her role of [[Wanda Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wanda Maximoff / Scarlet Witch]] from the series.<ref name="ComicCon2019" /><ref name="EWWVNov2020CoverStory" /> Additionally, the concept of the multiverse was introduced to the MCU in the [[Loki season 1|first season]] of ''[[Loki (TV series)|Loki]]'' (2021),<ref name="LokiS1TieIn" /> and explored further in the film ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' (2021), which is set before ''Multiverse of Madness'' and sees Cumberbatch reprising his role as Strange.<ref name="MarvelDisney+TimelineJun2022" /><ref name="DigitalSpyNWHDec2021" /> After ''Loki''{{'s}} [[For All Time. Always.|first-season finale]] was released, [[Tom Hiddleston]] was reported to be reprising his role as [[Loki (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Loki]] in ''Multiverse of Madness'',<ref name="THRLokiJuly2021" /> but ultimately did not appear.<ref name="NoLoki" />

[[Jade Halley Bartlett]], an "up-and-coming screenwriter", was hired to write the sequel's screenplay in October 2019.<ref name="Bartlett" /> Barlett said she had developed the film alongside Derrickson, Feige, and other Marvel Studios executives [[Louis D'Esposito]], [[Eric Hauserman Carroll]], and Palmer for around a year, a process she felt was fun and exciting, and called the creative team scholars of the comic books. She was provided with a binder of every ''[[Doctor Strange (comic book)|Doctor Strange]]'' comic book printed for reference to aid her writing.<ref name="BartlettJan2024" /> In December, Feige described the multiverse as "the next step in the evolution of the MCU", and said this film would "crack it wide open" in a way that would have repercussions for the Disney+ series and the next films in Marvel Studios' [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] slate.<ref name="CCXP2019Feige" /> Later in the month, he clarified that the sequel would not be a horror film, as some reports had described it following the Comic-Con announcement, but it would be a "big MCU film with scary sequences". Feige compared these sequences to the films ''[[Raiders of the Lost Ark]]'' (1981), ''[[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom]]'' (1984), ''[[Gremlins]]'' (1984), and ''[[Poltergeist (1982 film)|Poltergeist]]'' (1982), and said Derrickson was good at being "legitimately scary" due to his background making horror films. Feige also revealed that the film would introduce several surprising new characters to the MCU, including one that Marvel Studios had been looking for a way to use in an MCU film for some time;<ref name="FeigeNYFA" /> this was later revealed to be [[America Chavez (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|America Chavez]], who was included because her powers are related to the multiverse.<ref name="AmericaPowersD23Mag" /> Chavez's co-creator [[Joe Casey]] was not paid for the character's appearance in the film after he rejected a compensation offer from [[Marvel Comics]] that he described as a "pittance".<ref name="ChavezTHRApril2022" />

=== Pre-production ===
In January 2020, Marvel Studios and Derrickson announced that he would no longer direct the film due to creative differences. In separate statements, they both said they were thankful for their collaboration so far and Derrickson would remain an executive producer on the film.<ref name="DerricksonExit" /> This was not expected to delay filming, which was set for May 2020.<ref name="DerricksonExit" /><ref name="WaldronColliderJune2021" /> Cargill explained that he and Derrickson conceived a story that went in a different direction from what Marvel wanted,<ref name="CargillMay2021" /> and the pair had not yet written a draft of the screenplay so the final film would not be derivative of their work.<ref name="CargillDraftFeb2020" /> Derrickson said leaving the film was a difficult decision, but he did not want to compromise on a film that was different from what he wanted to make. His choice to leave was made easier by the fact that he was able to immediately begin work on ''[[The Black Phone]]'' (2022), another film he wanted to make.<ref name="CargillMay2021" /> Cumberbatch was not consulted regarding the director change and said he was sad to hear about it, but he respected the decision and how it was handled.<ref name="CumberbatchTHR" /> Some reports suggested that Derrickson left the film because he wanted to make a "no-holds-barred weird, gnarly, scary movie" similar to [[A24]]'s films ''[[The Witch (2015 film)|The Witch]]'' (2015) or ''[[Hereditary (film)|Hereditary]]'' (2018), and Marvel Studios did not want to go in that direction, but Feige denied this and said the studio loved that idea. He explained that it was their intention for the film to serve as a guide into a "much creepier side" of the MCU, even after Derrickson's departure,<ref name="FeigeDerrickson" /> and they wanted to explore the "mind-bending frightening side" of the multiverse.<ref name="BTSFeaturette" />

[[File:Sam Raimi by Gage Skidmore 2.jpg|thumb|upright|Sam Raimi took over as director during pre-production]]
[[Sam Raimi]] entered negotiations to take over as director by early February.<ref name="Raimi" /> At that time, [[Chiwetel Ejiofor]] was expected to reprise his role as [[Karl Mordo (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Karl Mordo]], McAdams was no longer expected to appear,<ref name="Raimi" /> and [[Michael Waldron]] was hired to rewrite the film's script after serving as the [[head writer]] of ''Loki''{{'s}} first season.<ref name="WaldronTHR" /><ref name="WaldronCollider" /> Raimi signed on to direct the film a few weeks later.<ref name="WaldronVanityFair" /> He had been reluctant to direct another Marvel superhero film after the mixed critical reaction to ''[[Spider-Man 3]]'' (2007),<ref name="RaimiCollider" /> feeling that he needed a break from that genre,<ref name="RaimiAMC" /> but he accepted the job because of the challenge of getting the film into production right away and because he was a fan of the Doctor Strange character and Derrickson's work on the first film.<ref name="RaimiCollider" /><ref name="RaimiAMC" /> Raimi was curious how making big-budgeted films had changed since his prior film, ''[[Oz the Great and Powerful]]'' (2013), and he also had to familiarize himself with the existing MCU storylines and characters for the film.<ref name="RaimiAMC" /> Waldron had three weeks to write a new script draft based on the work done by Derrickson and Bartlett. He said this was "almost impossible",<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> and Raimi felt "very rushed and panicked" trying to meet the May 2020 production start date.<ref name="RaimiRollingStone" /> The [[COVID-19 pandemic]] began to impact film productions during those first three weeks,<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> and pre-production started taking place remotely.<ref name="VarietyMar2020" /> Filming was initially still on track to begin in May 2020,<ref name="WaldronColliderJune2021" /> until Disney shifted much of their Phase Four slate of films due to the pandemic, moving ''Multiverse of Madness''{{'s}} release date to November 5, 2021.<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> Raimi was relieved by this delay,<ref name="RaimiRollingStone" /> which allowed him and Waldron to start writing the script from scratch in a reasonable time, and make the film their own.<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /><ref name="WaldronFFWComicBookJune2021" /> Bartlett had written a draft before the COVID-19 pandemic and said that Raimi and Waldron's version was different from the one she developed with Derrickson.<ref name="BartlettJan2024" />

Waldron watched the director's [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' films]] (2002–2007) so he could identify Raimi's strengths and write toward them.<ref name="WaldronRaimiSpidey" /> He described ''Multiverse of Madness'' as Raimi's return to "big superhero movies", and said it would have every aspect of a Sam Raimi film,<ref name="WaldronFFW/FilmJune2021" /> including a "slightly scarier direction" which he felt Raimi had a strong track record with.<ref name="WaldronFFWComicBookJune2021" /><ref name="WaldronFFW/FilmJune2021" /> Palmer said Waldron's work introducing the multiverse in ''Loki'' allowed Marvel Studios to "jump into telling a good story" without having to re-explain those ideas for the audience, and helped bring "a lot of heart to [the science fiction] concepts".<ref name="PalmerWaldronScript" /> Waldron also used his experience from writing the series ''[[Rick and Morty]]'' (2013–present), which helped him "introduce these big sci-fi concepts in ways that were digestible, palatable to the audience and without getting them bogged down in the boring details".<ref name="WaldronWriting/Film" /> Rather than just expanding the stakes of the film, Waldron saw the multiverse as the "emotional heart" of the story that could be used in personal ways, such as using "[[Alternative universe (fan fiction)|what ifs]]" and alternate versions of characters to reflect on the film's protagonists, and exploring the characters' right or wrong choices through their alternate versions. Waldron watched ''Doctor Strange'' several times, saying he became a "student" of that film, to understand Strange's story in the original. He also looked at how the character had developed through his other MCU appearances,<ref name="WaldronSFX" /> and Raimi praised how Waldron's imagination and knowledge of Marvel history contributed to the script.<ref name="RaimiRollingStone" /> The creative team saw early production work for the Disney+ animated series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'' (2021–2024) to learn how Strange was portrayed in that series.<ref name="WhatIfStrange" />

Waldron described Wanda as "the biggest best bullet" that they had,<ref name="WaldronHeroNation" /> and said she was destined to become a villain in the MCU, as she did in the comics, especially after the end of ''WandaVision'' revealed "to her that the family she's built is not real. Then she gets the ''[[Darkhold (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Darkhold]]''... and learns that there is a real version of her children out there. And if you've got the Book of the Damned whispering in your ear long enough that your kids are out there and you could go get 'em, maybe that can push you to do some terrible things."<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> If she became a villain at the end of the film, he knew another project would get to have the "fun" of using her as a villain. He also wanted to avoid the film getting "overstuffed" by adding a different multiverse-related villain such as [[Jonathan Majors]]'s [[Kang the Conqueror (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Kang the Conqueror]],<ref name="WaldronHeroNation" /> and felt that this would create a "watered-down version of Wanda [who] wouldn't be the protagonist, and she wouldn't really be the antagonist".<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> Olsen was hesitant about playing the villain after believing that she would just be "in an ensemble thing", but she came to feel that the combination of ''WandaVision'' and ''Multiverse of Madness'' allowed her to create a sympathetic character in the series who the audience would therefore have conflicted feelings about when seeing the film.<ref name="OlsenVillainVariety" /> Waldron worked closely with Olsen and ''WandaVision'' head writer [[Jac Schaeffer]] to ensure Wanda's story was a satisfying continuation of the series,<ref name="WaldronColliderJune2021" /> which Waldron read the scripts for and watched in its entirety while writing the script. He wanted to mirror Wanda's struggles with control following the events of that series with Strange's own need for control following ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame''.<ref name="WaldronWatchedWandaVision" /> Raimi also studied the series despite not fully seeing it, particularly key moments that directly impacted their plans for the film, to make sure the film maintained "a proper through line and character-growth dynamic" for her character.<ref name="RaimiRollingStone" /> It was important to Olsen that the film not repeat what was done in ''WandaVision'' and instead be an evolution of the character, and she requested several adjustments to the script to avoid moments that she found too similar to the series. To justify the many killings that Wanda commits in the film, Olsen focused on the character's perspective of those people being in her way and not listening to her after the loss of her children in the series.<ref name="OlsenVillainVariety" />

After a planned appearance by Cumberbatch in ''WandaVision'' was removed late in the development of the series, rewrites to the ''Multiverse of Madness'' script were required which Feige described as a "wonderful combination of very dedicated coordination, and chaos".<ref name="CumberbatchWandaVision" /> These changes include how Strange and Wanda meet in the film.<ref name="RSWVOralHistory" /> The story of ''Multiverse of Madness'' is still set up by ''WandaVision'', but the film was developed to also work for viewers who did not watch the series.<ref name="WorkWithWandaVision" /> For consistency with the ''Darkhold''{{'s}} appearance, the film's props department designed the prop that was used for ''WandaVision'' and then reused it for the film.<ref name="BobbittDarkholdDesign" /> While writing his first draft, Waldron felt the film needed to "get drunk [and] find the madness in the multiverse" by introducing alternate universe versions of known Marvel characters. This led to the introduction of the [[Illuminati (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Illuminati]], a [[secret society]] from the comics. Waldron had been watching the film ''[[Aliens (film)|Aliens]]'' (1986) as inspiration for the script's thriller tone, and enjoyed how the "badass" space marines "just get slaughtered" by the titular aliens to set the latter up as scarier villains for the rest of that film. He wanted to do something similar with Wanda by introducing the Illuminati as a group of characters that fans would be excited to see and then have Wanda kill them all. The idea of Strange taking control of a dead body for the end of the film came from Waldron and Palmer discussing how he would be able to confront Wanda from a different universe, with the corpse of another version of Strange having been already established at the start of the film. Raimi, Feige, and Cumberbatch were all excited about the idea, despite Raimi initially wanting to avoid a [[zombie]] sequence to not repeat what he had done previously with zombie films.<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" />

The film's release was shifted again at the end of April 2020, this time to March 25, 2022, after [[Sony Pictures]] rescheduled ''No Way Home'' to the November 2021 date.<ref name="SonyPhase4Delay" /> Until then, Waldron had been writing the script expecting ''Multiverse of Madness'' to be released before ''No Way Home''. He was in contact with ''No Way Home'' writers [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]] and [[Erik Sommers]] throughout the writing process, and after the release date change they adjusted their respective scripts so instead of ''Multiverse of Madness'' "blowing the lid on the multiverse" it would pick up after ''No Way Home'' with Strange already having multiverse experience. This change had a "knock-down effect" on the rest of the script.<ref name="WaldronHeroNation" /> There had been early plans to include Strange's new costume from ''Multiverse of Madness'' and America Chavez in ''No Way Home'',<ref name="ChurchyardSpideyPlans" /><ref name="ChavezTHRApril2022" /> while a brief cameo of [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] was planned for ''Multiverse of Madness'', but the pandemic ultimately prevented those plans from coming to fruition.<ref name="ChurchyardSpideyPlans" /> Raimi noted that where ''No Way Home'' saw characters from other universes enter the MCU, ''Multiverse of Madness'' features characters from the MCU entering the multiverse and exploring different universes.<ref name="RaimiFandango" /> Ejiofor confirmed his involvement in June,<ref name="EjioforJun2020" /> and [[Xochitl Gomez]] joined the cast in October.<ref name="Gomez" /> Cumberbatch revealed that month that filming would start in London by the beginning of November,<ref name="Fall2020Filming" /><ref name="LondonFilming" /> with the production not impacted by a national lockdown in England that was set for November 5 to December 2 [[COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom#Second wave|due to increased COVID-19 cases]].<ref name="UKLockdown" />

=== Filming ===
[[Principal photography]] began in November 2020 in London,<ref name="FilmingStart" /><ref name="OlsenLondonFilming" /> under the [[working title]] ''Stellar Vortex''.<ref name="WorkingTitle" /> Filming was delayed from an initial May 2020 start date due to the COVID-19 pandemic.<ref name="WaldronColliderJune2021" /> Olsen began filming her scenes by November 25,<ref name="OlsenLondonFilming" /> shooting [[Back-to-back film production|back-to-back]] with ''WandaVision'',<ref name="OlsenBtBFilming" /> and found it odd to transition from the series back to starring in an MCU film.<ref name="OlsenNYFAComicBookJuly2021" /><ref name="OlsenNYFACinemaBlendJuly2021" /> At the beginning of December, filming was taking place at [[Longcross Studios]] in [[Longcross]], Surrey,<ref name="LongcrossFilming" /> where several New York City blocks and sets for the Sanctum Sanctorum were built by production designer Charlie Wood.<ref name="LongcrossBacklots" /> McAdams soon signed on to return as Palmer,<ref name="McAdams" /> shortly before Feige officially confirmed her involvement on December 10 along with that of Ejiofor, Wong, and Gomez. He also revealed that Gomez would portray America Chavez.<ref name="DisneyInvestorDay" /><ref name="MarvelDec2020" /> Cumberbatch had begun filming his scenes by then, after completing his work on ''No Way Home''.<ref name="OlsenLondonFilming" /><ref name="CumberbatchFilming" /><ref name="CumberbatchSM3" /> He said the film was more collaborative than his previous MCU appearances, for which he felt like he was "just along for the ride".<ref name="CumberbatchCollaboration" />

Many of the actors, including Olsen,<ref name="OlsenRaimiShooting" /> Cumberbatch,<ref name="CumberbatchTHR" /> and Wong, were excited to be working with Raimi.<ref name="WongColliderJuly2021" /> The director said many "dimensionalized visuals" were used during shooting to tell the film's story,<ref name="BTSFeaturette" /> as he was able to use his preferred camera techniques such as using the camera and perspective to create a feeling of anxiety for audiences.<ref name="OlsenRaimiShooting" /><ref name="CumberbatchTHR" /><ref name="OlsenVFPodJune2021" /> [[John Mathieson (cinematographer)|John Mathieson]] served as cinematographer,<ref name="WaldronVanityFair" /> and shot the film with [[Panavision cameras#Panaflex Millennium|Panavision DXL2]] [[IMAX]]-certified cameras.<ref name="IMAXCameras" /> Raimi encouraged improvisation,<ref name="CumberbatchTHR" /><ref name="WongColliderJuly2021" /> explaining that the cast could adapt their interactions due to their knowledge of their own characters and that the script often changed shortly before filming for those scenes began.<ref name="CastColliderInterview" /> The ending of the film was still "up in the air" when filming began, and Cumberbatch said it took some time for the team to decide on one that tied together all of the characters' stories.<ref name="CumberbatchEndingScript" /> Waldron explained that they felt the ending was too happy following all the "bad shit" that occurs in the film and wanted to show that Strange would suffer consequences for his actions—including possessing the corpse of a different version of himself—which tied into Mordo's warning from the first film: "The bill comes due." This led to them adding the ending scene where Strange develops a third eye in his forehead, and Waldron felt this was the sort of twist that horror films often end on.<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> Elaborating on that tone, Olsen said they were going for a "horror show vibe",<ref name="OlsenApril2021" /> comparing it to Raimi's ''[[Evil Dead]]'' films with aspects of the horror genre such as "constant fear". She felt it was "more than a glossy ''[[Indiana Jones (franchise)|Indiana Jones]]'' movie" and would be darker than those films,<ref name="OlsenVFJune2021" /><ref name="OlsenVFPodJune2021" /> with Raimi trying to make "the scariest Marvel movie"<ref name="OlsenNYFAComicBookJuly2021" /><ref name="OlsenNYFACinemaBlendJuly2021" /> and create "as much tension for the audience" as possible.<ref name="BTSFeaturette" />

Olsen shot for three weeks before pandemic restrictions increased near the end of December due to a [[COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom#Second wave|surge of COVID-19 cases in the United Kingdom]], with another lockdown in England announced from January 6, 2021.<ref name="OlsenFilmingHalt" /><ref name="OlsenUSATodayJan2021" /> The scenes of Scarlet Witch massacring the Illuminati were shot at the [[British Museum]] of London, with Marvel using [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]] to add a new room with a glass entrance and a few statues for the background.<ref name="IlluminatiFilmingBM" /> Filming resumed in early 2021, and at some point, Cumberbatch had to temporarily pause filming after being in close contact with a member of the production who had a false positive test.<ref name="CumberbatchTHR" /> In mid-March, Cumberbatch said they were in the middle of production,<ref name="CumberbatchFilming" /> and Ejiofor had begun filming his scenes in London by then.<ref name="EjioforFilming" /> Filming occurred at Broomfield Hill Car Park in [[Richmond Park]] for the week of March 25.<ref name="RichmondBroomfieldFilming" /> Shooting took place at [[Freemasons' Hall, London]], for a few weeks in April.<ref name="FreemasonsChurchFilming" /> On April 15, Feige said they were in the final week of filming,<ref name="FeigeFilming" /> with shooting taking place from that day until April 17 at [[Burrow Hill Cider Farm]] in Somerset. [[Jett Klyne]] and [[Julian Hilliard]], who respectively portrayed Wanda's sons [[Tommy Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tommy]] and [[Billy Maximoff (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Billy]] in ''WandaVision'', were reportedly on set at the farm,<ref name="BurrowHillFilming" /> and were confirmed to be in the film in April 2022.<ref name="HilliardKlyneConfirmed" />

=== Post-production ===
[[Bob Murawski]] and [[Tia Nolan]] co-edited the film.<ref name="TallericoEbert" /> Some additional photography had been completed by mid-September 2021,<ref name="OlsenReshootsEnd" /> with Olsen and Wong completing their work.<ref name="OlsenReshootsEnd" /><ref name="WongColliderJuly2021" /> The film was once again delayed in October, this time to May 6, 2022.<ref name="October2021Delay" /> Later that month, Cumberbatch said they were in the middle of more additional photography, with further reshoots set for November and December,<ref name="CumberbatchReshootsTime" /> over at least six weeks in Los Angeles. ''The Hollywood Reporter'' said this photography was "significant" and included two weeks dedicated to filming that could not be completed during principal photography because of actor availability issues and COVID-related slowdowns.<ref name="THRReshoots" /> Cumberbatch confirmed the reshoots were being used to make adjustments to already shot material, while also filming parts that "were just impossible to do [during principal photography] because of logistics, COVID etc".<ref name="CumberbatchReshootsReason" /> ''The Hollywood Reporter'' later reported that the reshoots would allow the film to have "more fun with the multiverse" by adding more cameo appearances and variants of established characters, similar to ''No Way Home'' and the first season of ''Loki''.<ref name="ReshootsConclude" /> Waldron created a fourth multiverse world for the film to shoot during the reshoots, which ultimately did not appear in the film.<ref name="RaimiAdditionalMcAdams" /> The film's teaser trailer was released in late December and confirmed the involvement of [[Michael Stuhlbarg]] as [[Nicodemus West (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nicodemus West]], reprising his role from ''Doctor Strange''.<ref name="/FilmTeaser" /> It also revealed that Cumberbatch would portray multiple versions of Strange in the film.<ref name="DigitalSpyNWHDec2021" /> The reshoots reportedly [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] during the week of December 13,<ref name="ReshootsConclude" /> but additional filming continued in early January 2022. The latter was completed on January 8.<ref name="AdditionalPhotographyConclude" /> By the end of the month, Raimi had assembled a cut of the film that would be presented to test audiences, and said there was potential for more reshoots to occur if the test screenings found that clarification or improvement were needed;<ref name="RaimiMoreReshootsPossible" /> Cumberbatch was working on further reshoots in the United Kingdom by March 13.<ref name="CumberbatchMarReshoots" />

[[Patrick Stewart]], who portrayed [[Charles Xavier (film character)|Charles Xavier / Professor X]] in [[20th Century Fox]]'s [[X-Men (film series)|''X-Men'' film series]], was revealed to be appearing in the film in an undisclosed role with the release of the full trailer in February 2022;<ref name="GizmodoTrailer" /><ref name="CBRTrailer" /> Stewart was believed to be reprising the role of Xavier,<ref name="VarietyStewartTrailer" /> which had previously been the subject of speculation.<ref name="CBRTrailer" /> A poster for the film, released at the same time, additionally teased the inclusion of [[Captain Carter|Peggy Carter / Captain Carter]], a character who was introduced in ''What If...?''.<ref name="PosterCaptainCarter" /> Stewart initially denied that he was in the film,<ref name="StewartTrailerResponse" /> but soon admitted that he was.<ref name="StewartAppearConfirm" /> The trailer also revealed that Topo Wresniwiro would reprise his role of [[Hamir (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Hamir]] from ''Doctor Strange''.<ref name="Hamir" /><ref name="WresniriroHamir" /> A creature resembling prominent Doctor Strange villain [[Shuma-Gorath]] appears in the film, but it is given the name [[Gargantos]], which comes from a different comic book character, because the rights to the name Shuma-Gorath belong to [[Heroic Signatures]].<ref name="GargantosName" /> Gargantos' eye was modeled after that of Olsen's to foreshadow her role as the film's antagonist.<ref name="GargantosEye" /> The cosmic entity the [[Living Tribunal (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Living Tribunal]] also makes a brief appearance.<ref name="LivingTribunal" /> Discussing Stewart's involvement, as well as assumptions based on the trailer that the Illuminati would be appearing in the film, Palmer said returning actors would not necessarily be portraying characters that had been seen before, and if Marvel Studios were to introduce the Illuminati they would do a "more MCU-driven" version of the group.<ref name="PalmerEmpireStewartIlluminati" />

The Illuminati's appearance was officially confirmed in March;<ref name="IlluminatiConfirmed" /> and the team features actors portraying new versions of their characters from previous projects: Stewart plays a [[Charles Xavier (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|new version of Xavier]] that combines elements from his previous performances, including a line of dialogue from the film ''[[X-Men: Days of Future Past]]'' (2014), with elements from ''[[X-Men: The Animated Series]]'' (1992–1997);<ref name="WaldronIlluminatiVariety" /> Ejiofor plays a new version of Mordo; [[Hayley Atwell]] portrays Captain Carter after voicing the ''What If...?'' version; [[Lashana Lynch]] plays a different version of [[Maria Rambeau|Maria Rambeau / Captain Marvel]] from the one she portrayed in the MCU film ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019); and [[Anson Mount]] portrays an alternate version of [[Black Bolt (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Blackagar Boltagon / Black Bolt]] from his role in [[Marvel's ABC television series]] ''[[Inhumans (TV series)|Inhumans]]'' (2017). Also in the group is [[John Krasinski]] as [[Fantastic Four (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Fantastic Four]] member [[Reed Richards (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Reed Richards]]<!-- Not referred to as Mister Fantastic in the film -->. Krasinski had been a popular suggestion for the role by fans for some time, especially since the announcement of a [[The Fantastic Four: First Steps|new ''Fantastic Four'' film]] set in the MCU.<ref name="IlluminatiMembers" /> Mount was unexpectedly called by Feige and reached out by Raimi to reprise his role as Black Bolt, which he was grateful and honored to do due to his joy at working with the latter.<ref name="MountsCasting" /> When Lynch was approached to return, she assumed she would be playing an ancestor of Rambeau and was surprised to learn she would be an alternate version of Captain Marvel.<ref name="LynchReturn" /> Atwell was disappointed by her role in the film, and felt Carter's death was a "complete disservice" to the character. She had tried to suggest changes while on set which were dismissed.<ref name="AtwellCarterDeathDisservice" /> The Illuminati sequence was filmed in pieces, with many of the actors not being on set together. Krasinski's footage was filmed during reshoots to replace a planned cameo appearance by [[Daniel Craig]], who chose not to film the role because he did not believe a single day of filming was worth the risk of catching COVID-19 and spreading it to his family.<ref name="KrollCraig" /><ref name="KrollCraig2" /> Craig was reportedly set to portray the character [[Balder the Brave]] in the scene.<ref name="KrollCraig" /> Olsen did not meet Krasinski while shooting the scene in which Scarlet Witch kills Richards, and she instead filmed the scene with a [[stand-in]].<ref name="OlsenKrasinskisStandIn" /> Mount filmed his scenes during reshoots while Stewart filmed his scenes as [[Jean-Luc Picard]] for the [[Paramount+]] series ''[[Star Trek: Picard]]'' (2020–2023), without the presence of Ejiofor and before Krasinski's contract was signed, with other actors filling for the roles, substituting their shots and transplanting faces.<ref name="MountsShooting" /> Due to safety protocols to prevent the spread of COVID-19, Stewart and other leading actors filmed their scenes in isolation without other actors present, which he found to be frustrating and disappointing.<ref name="StewartFilming" />

Waldron said the final line-up of the Illuminati was similar to the one in his first draft,<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> but with some other suggestions coming from Feige. Waldron considered including [[Namor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Namor]], a member of the Illuminati in the comics, but Marvel Studios did not allow the character to appear in any MCU projects at [[Ryan Coogler]]'s request before the film ''[[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever]]'' (2022), in which he was played by [[Tenoch Huerta Mejía]];<ref name="WaldronIlluminatiVariety" /><ref name="Namor" /> Waldron's first draft also included an Earth-838 counterpart of [[Evangeline Lilly]]'s [[Hope van Dyne|Hope van Dyne / Wasp]] as a member of the Illuminati, who would have been killed by Wanda clapping her hands to kill her.<ref name="Wasp838" /> [[Storyboard]]s by artist Soren Bendt for the Illuminati massacre scene suggest that, in addition to Balder the Brave, an Earth-838 counterpart of [[Obadiah Stane (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Obadiah Stane / Iron Monger]], who was previously portrayed by [[Jeff Bridges]] in the first MCU film ''[[Iron Man (2008 film)|Iron Man]]'' (2008), was also considered for the group's lineup.<ref name="IronMonger838" /> There were also rumors that [[Tom Cruise]] would be portraying an alternate version of [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]] known as [[Superior Iron Man]] in the film after being in contention for the role back in the 1990s before [[Robert Downey Jr.]] was cast as the [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|title character]] in 2006. Waldron expressed interest in the idea, but Cruise was never approached due to his filming schedule for the films ''[[Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning Part One]]'' (2023) and ''[[Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning]]'' (2025).<ref name="WaldronRollingStone" /> Other rumors about potential cameos included an appearance of [[Ryan Reynolds]] reprising his role as [[Wade Wilson (film character)|Wade Wilson / Deadpool]] from the Fox films ''[[Deadpool (film)|Deadpool]]'' (2016) and ''[[Deadpool 2]]'' (2018);<ref name="ReynoldsTruthTeller" /> Waldron confirmed there had been discussions about bringing Reynolds in to play Deadpool before they decided that the film was not "the right place" to feature him.<ref name="DeadpoolDroppedCameo" /> Another character introduced in the film is Clea, with [[Charlize Theron]] cast in the role. Waldron said they wanted to include her in the film but felt they needed to resolve Strange's relationship with Christine Palmer first, so the character's introduction was saved for the [[mid-credits scene]] to set her up as a potential new love interest in a future story.<ref name="WaldronClea" />

[[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM), [[Digital Domain]], [[Framestore]], [[Luma Pictures]], Capital T, [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], [[Wētā FX]], [[Trixter (company)|Trixter]], Crafty Apes, and [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]] worked on the visual effects of the film,<ref name="VarietyVFX" /><ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> in which an estimated 2,000 VFX shots were created.<ref name="THRVFX" /> Framestore worked on the multiverse jumping sequence with Strange and Chavez, while Luma Pictures worked on the Gargantos fight scene.<ref name="VarietyVFX" /> Perception also designed the film's main-on-end title sequence, which employs colorful [[Rorschach test]] visuals because Raimi wanted the sequence to "both complement and contrast the film".<ref name="MainTitles" /> Animators from [[Disney]] worked to create another version of the [[Walt Disney Productions]] film ''[[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937 film)|Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs]]'' (1937), with a different color palette, for a scene in ''Multiverse of Madness''.<ref name="AudioCommentary" /> Graham Churchyard served as costume designer and created most of the costumes for the film, except for those worn by Black Bolt and Richards, both of which could not be completed in time due to [[Travel during the COVID-19 pandemic|travel restrictions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic]]. They were created through visual effects instead.<ref name="Costumes" /> In late August 2022, Waldron revealed that one of the first drafts of the film's script included a post-credits scene involving a seemingly alive Richards, but the idea was scrapped.<ref name="WaldronRichards" /> Another draft of the script included a scene of Strange meeting a variant of himself named Melvin, who would have been depicted as a [[street magic]]ian with no mystical abilities.<ref name="AudioCommentary" /> Gomez noted that Marvel asked for numerous rewrites of the script from Waldron.<ref name="GomezRewrites" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (soundtrack){{!}}''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' (soundtrack)}}
''Doctor Strange'' composer [[Michael Giacchino]] was set to return for the sequel by October 2019, when Derrickson was set as director.<ref name="Giacchino" /> After Raimi took over, [[Danny Elfman]] was hired as composer; Elfman previously worked with Raimi on the films ''[[Darkman]]'' (1990), ''[[A Simple Plan (film)|A Simple Plan]]'' (1998), ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' (2002), ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' (2004), and ''[[Oz the Great and Powerful]]'' (2013).<ref name="Elfman" /> By February 2021, Elfman had begun working on music to be used during filming, but would not begin work on the actual score for several months.<ref name="Elfman" /> In January 2022, Elfman said he was completing work on the score,<ref name="ElfmanJan2022" /> including conducting an orchestra at [[Abbey Road Studios]] in London.<ref name="ElfmanJan2022" /><ref name="RaimiRollingStone" /> Elfman's frequent collaborator [[Steve Bartek]] worked on the score remotely over [[Zoom (software)|Zoom]].<ref name="ElfmanJan2022" /> A soundtrack album featuring Elfman's score was released digitally by [[Hollywood Records]] and [[Marvel Music (record label)|Marvel Music]] on May 4,<ref name="Soundtrack" /> with three "spoiler tracks" that were excluded from the initial release being made available on May 20.<ref name="SpoilerTracks" />

Elfman referenced Giacchino's ''Doctor Strange'' theme in a similar way to how he used [[Alan Silvestri]]'s theme from the MCU film ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012) when working on its sequel ''[[Avengers: Age of Ultron]]'' (2015).<ref name="ElfmanMarch2021" /> The score also features the song "[[WandaVision: Episode 2 (Original Soundtrack)|WandaVision!]]" by [[Kristen Anderson-Lopez]] and [[Robert Lopez]], from the ''WandaVision'' episode "[[Don't Touch That Dial]]", when Wanda is first introduced; Silvestri's "[[Captain America: The First Avenger (soundtrack)|Captain America March]]" theme from the MCU film ''[[Captain America: The First Avenger]]'' (2011) for Atwell's Captain Carter appearance;<ref name="ThemeSongs" /> and the main theme from the animated series ''[[X-Men '97]]'' (2024–present) by [[the Newton Brothers]], which is an updated version of the main theme from ''X-Men: The Animated Series'' composed by [[Ron Wasserman]], for the introduction of Xavier. For legal reasons, the latter theme is credited to ''The Animated Series'' music executives [[Haim Saban]] and [[Shuki Levy]].<ref name="ThemeSongs" /><ref name="XMen97ComposersInverse" />

== Marketing ==
A [[teaser trailer]] for the film was played after the end credits of ''Spider-Man: No Way Home'',<ref name="DigitalSpyNWHDec2021" /> before being released online on December 22, 2021.<ref name="TeaserOnline" /> Matt Webb Mitovich of ''[[TVLine]]'' called it a "rousing, thrilling trailer" and noted the ending that revealed an alternate version of Strange. He believed this was [[Doctor Strange Supreme]], an alternate version of the character introduced in ''What If...?''.<ref name="TVLineTeaser" /> ''[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]]''{{'s}} Alex Welch felt it was a "suitably psychedelic teaser, full of revelations and fun moments that should leave Marvel fans gobsmacked", and suggested the film would tie together "many of the multiversal threads left hanging" from ''No Way Home'', ''WandaVision'', and the first seasons of ''Loki'' and ''What If...?''.<ref name="InverseTeaser" /> Writing for ''[[Decider (website)|Decider]]'', Alex Zalben believed the film was a "direct result" of ''What If...?'', which "instantly mak[es] the animated series far more important than Marvel fans might have originally realized".<ref name="DeciderTeaser" /> James Grebey at ''[[Syfy Wire]]'' said there was "a very ominous aura to the whole trailer".<ref name="SyfyWireTeaser" /> Daniel Chin of ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]'' felt the trailer had "creepy imagery" and that it was "expected to separate itself from other Marvel projects even further by weaving in horror elements", while also noting how the film won't have to introduce the multiverse, and can instead explore it even further, and also how it'll be the "first real attempt to blend existing story lines from its Disney+ series with its big-screen properties", while he thought made it the "first major crossover event in Phase 4".<ref name="TheRingerTeaser" /> Also in December, merchandise began to be made available with the reveal of [[Marvel Legends]] figures based on the film.<ref name="LegendsFigures" />

A teaser aired during [[Super Bowl LVI]] on February 13, 2022, with the full-length trailer debuting online afterward.<ref name="CBRTrailer" /> Justin Carter at ''[[Gizmodo]]'' highlighted how the trailer continued the first film's "visually trippy" approach to alternate dimensions while adding more horror elements, and was also excited by the brief appearance of America Chavez,<ref name="GizmodoTrailer" /> while his colleague Germain Lussier felt, compared to the teaser, the trailer "raised the stakes exponentially with all manner of wild revelations and images".<ref name="GizmodoLussierTrailer" /> ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'s}} Devan Coggan also felt the trailer was "trippy" and added that it "seems to have more in common with Raimi's iconic horror films".<ref name="EWTrailer" /> ''[[The Verge]]''{{'s}} Charles Pulliam-Moore said the trailer did a better job conveying the film's plot than the teaser, and he felt the film would be the "culmination of {{em|all}} the big-picture troubles" teased in ''WandaVision'' and ''Loki''.<ref name="VergeTrailer" /> Many commentators noted the revelation of Patrick Stewart's involvement in the trailer and the implication he could reprise his ''X-Men'' role of Charles Xavier / Professor X,<ref name="GizmodoTrailer" /><ref name="CBRTrailer" /><ref name="PolygonStewartTrailer" /><ref name="VarietyStewartTrailer" /> which led to speculation that ''Multiverse of Madness'' would introduce the MCU version of the Illuminati.<ref name="/FilmIlluminatiTrailer" /><ref name="ComicBookIlluminati" /><ref name="PolygonIlluminati" /><ref name="GizmodoIlluminati" /> RelishMix reported that the trailer had 93.12&nbsp;million views in 24 hours across [[Facebook]], [[Twitter]], [[YouTube]], and [[Instagram]], which was the top trailer among those airing during the Super Bowl according to their metrics. Disney reported 143 million online views across Twitter, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, [[TikTok]], [[Snapchat]], and [[Google search]]es, and 55 million broadcast views.<ref name="TrailerViews" />

[[Funko Pops]] based on the film were revealed in March 2022.<ref name="FunkoPops" /> In April, [[Procter & Gamble]] released a commercial promoting the film and [[Tide (brand)|Tide]] laundry detergent featuring Wong and the [[Cloak of Levitation (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Cloak of Levitation]].<ref name="TideCommercial" /> Later that month, Disney showcased the opening sequence of the film at [[CinemaCon]].<ref name="CinemaCon" /> The film also partnered with [[G Fuel]] and [[Xbox]], which created custom cobranded console kits and controllers inspired by the film, while also featuring a [[Jaeger-LeCoultre|Jaeger-Le Coultre]] watch in the film. It also partnered with [[Mori Building Company]], a Japanese urban landscape developer, which turned its café and menu into an interactive photo op experience to promote the film. Additional partnerships included [[Cadillac]], [[Maybank]], [[Shell US]] in the [[Asia–Pacific]] (APAC) region, and also [[Xiaomi]] and [[T-Mobile US|T-Mobile]] in the [[Europe, the Middle East and Africa]] (EMEA) region.<ref name="DeadlineOpening" /><ref name="XboxPromo" /><ref name="GQWatches" /><ref name="ShellPromo" /> Three episodes of the series ''[[Marvel Studios: Legends]]'' were released on April 29, exploring Doctor Strange, Wong, and Scarlet Witch using footage from their previous MCU appearances.<ref name="LegendsEps" /> Eight [[VTuber]]s from [[Hololive Production]] promoted the film in a special pre-premiere stream event on YouTube on May 3.<ref name="Hololive" /> Disney spent a total of $150&nbsp;million promoting the film.<ref name="DeadlineProfit" />

== Release ==
=== Theatrical ===
''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' held its world premiere at the [[Dolby Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on May 2, 2022.<ref name="WorldPremiere" /><ref name="DolbyTheatre" /> The film was released in several countries, including Germany, Italy, Sweden, and South Korea on May 4, 2022, in the United Kingdom on May 5,<ref name="UKRelease" /> and in the United States on May 6.<ref name="October2021Delay" /> It was released in [[4DX]], [[RealD 3D]], IMAX, [[Dolby Cinema]], [[ScreenX]], and [[Superscreen]] formats.<ref name="PremiumFormatReleases" /><ref name="PremiumFormatReleases2" /> It was originally set for release on May 7, 2021,<ref name="ComicCon2019" /> but was pushed back to November 5, 2021, due to the COVID-19 pandemic,<ref name="Phase4Delay" /> before it was further shifted to March 25, 2022, after Sony rescheduled ''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' to November 2021.<ref name="SonyPhase4Delay" /> In October 2021, it was shifted once again to its final May 2022 date.<ref name="October2021Delay" /> The film is part of Phase Four of the MCU.<ref name="PhaseFour" />

In April 2022, ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' confirmed that the film would not be released in Saudi Arabia due to the inclusion of America Chavez, a gay character, given [[LGBTQ rights in Saudi Arabia#Censorship|the region's censorship of LGBTQ references]].<ref name="PersianGulfCensorship" /> Nawaf Alsabhan, Saudi Arabia's general supervisor of cinema classification, said the film had not been banned from the country but revealed that Disney was "not willing" to grant their request of cutting "barely 12 seconds" of a scene in which Chavez refers to her "[[Two mothers|two moms]]".<ref name="AlsabhanSaudiArabia" /> The film had been set to release in several [[Persian Gulf]] countries on May 5, before advanced tickets were removed from cinema websites in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Qatar. Tickets were still available in the United Arab Emirates, which ''The Hollywood Reporter'' stated was an indication that the film could still be released there.<ref name="PersianGulfCensorship" /> [[IMAX Corporation]] also confirmed that the film would not be released in Egypt.<ref name="EgyptCensorship" /> Cumberbatch was disappointed by the countries' decisions to not release the film, saying: "We've come to know from those repressive regimes that their lack of tolerance is exclusionary to people who deserve to be not only included, but celebrated for who they are and made to feel part of a society and a culture and not punished for their sexuality. It feels truly out of step with everything that we've experienced as a species, let alone where we're at globally more as a culture, but frankly, it's just even more reason why this isn't tokenism to include an LGBTQ+ community member".<ref name="CumberbatchBanReaction" /> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' reported in late April that the film was unlikely to be released in China after a newspaper box for ''[[The Epoch Times]]'', a newspaper that opposes the [[Chinese Communist Party]] (CCP), was noticed in the film's trailer. It had been submitted for review to Chinese officials by that time.<ref name="ChineseReleaseUnlikely" /> In mid-May, Disney CEO [[Bob Chapek]] said the situation was complicated but noted that the film was already commercially successful without a release in China.<ref name="ChinaChapek" />

=== Home media ===
The film began streaming on [[Disney+]] on June 22, 2022,<ref name="Disney+Release" /> with the option to view the theatrical version of the film or an IMAX Enhanced version.<ref name="Disney+ReleaseIMAX" /> It was released by [[Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment]] on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD-Video|DVD]] on July 26. The home media includes audio commentary, deleted scenes, a gag reel, and various behind-the-scenes featurettes.<ref name="BluRayRelease" /> According to [[Samba TV]], ''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' was watched by 2.1&nbsp;million U.S. households within its first five days of being available on Disney+; this was comparable to the viewership of ''[[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings]]'' (2021) and ''[[Eternals (film)|Eternals]]'' (2021).<ref name="SambaTVFirstFiveDayViewership" /> According to the streaming aggregator [[JustWatch]], ''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' was the sixth most streamed film in the United States in 2022.<ref name="JustWatchMostStreamed" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
<!-- Worldwide -->
''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' grossed $411.3{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada and $544.4{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $955.8{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="BOM" /> It was the [[2022 in film#Highest-grossing films|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2022]].<ref name="Highest2022" /> ''[[Forbes]]'' estimated the film made a profit of $127.3&nbsp;million.<ref name="ForbesBudget" /> The film grossed $42&nbsp;million in ticket presales through [[Fandango Media|Fandango]], and sold the most tickets on the platform since ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' (2021), while also surpassing ticket presales for ''[[The Batman (film)|The Batman]]'' (2022) within 24 hours.<ref name="FandangoPresales" /><ref name="CinemaCon" /> Its worldwide opening weekend of $452.4&nbsp;million was the [[List of highest-grossing openings for films|ninth-largest opening ever]], and marked the highest May debut of all-time for [[IMAX]] ($33&nbsp;million).<ref name="DeadlineInternationalBoxOffice" />

<!-- US and Canada -->
In the United States and Canada, the film grossed $187.4{{nbsp}}million in its opening weekend, surpassing the $151{{nbsp}}million opening weekend gross of ''[[Spider-Man 3]]'' (2007) to become the highest of Sam Raimi's career. It grossed $90.7{{nbsp}}million on its opening day, which included $36{{nbsp}}million from Thursday previews. The preview was the second-best preview during the pandemic behind ''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' and the overall eighth-largest preview performance, while the opening-day gross was the seventh-highest in industry history. The film's $90.7 million opening day tally is also the largest single-day gross in May of all time.<ref name="PostTrakDeadline" /> In its second weekend, the film grossed $61 million, becoming one of the MCU's biggest second-weekend box office drops. The 67% decline was attributed by ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' to the "bad word of mouth" on the film and its CinemaScore grade, while EntTelligence saw more than 17% downsize of available seats for the film, resulting in fewer showtimes which also led to the decline.<ref name="Deadline2ndWknd" /> In its third weekend, the film grossed $31.6{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="THRThirdWknd" /> The film grossed $17{{nbsp}}million in its fourth weekend for a running total of $376.1{{nbsp}}million, passing ''The Batman''{{'}}s $369.3{{nbsp}}million to become the highest-grossing film of 2022 (later surpassed by ''[[Top Gun: Maverick]]'').<ref name="Deadline4thWknd" /><ref name="MaverickHighest" /> It ended up being the [[List of American films of 2022#Box office|fourth highest-grossing film of 2022]] in this region.<ref name="2022DomesticBO" />

<!-- Outside of the US and Canada -->
Outside the United States and Canada, ''Multiverse of Madness'' grossed $265&nbsp;million from 49 markets within its opening weekend, landing at number one in numerous markets. The film grossed $27.2{{nbsp}}million from 20 markets on its opening day, surpassing the first-day overall results of ''[[Doctor Strange (2016 film)|Doctor Strange]]'' (2016) by 153% and ''The Batman'' by 210%, but falling behind ''No Way Home'' by 4%. The film had the highest opening day gross for a film released during the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] in the Philippines and Thailand, with $1.2{{nbsp}}million in both markets, and the second-best ditto in France ($3{{nbsp}}million), Italy ($2.2{{nbsp}}million), and Germany ($1.8{{nbsp}}million). In Malaysia, the film grossed $1.6{{nbsp}}million on its opening day to become the second highest in the country's history. The film grossed $10.3{{nbsp}}million from its previews in Latin America, including from Mexico ($3.5{{nbsp}}million) and Brazil ($2.7{{nbsp}}million).<ref name="DeadlineInternationalBoxOffice" /> {{As of|2022|06|12|df=us}}, the film's largest markets are the United Kingdom ($51.4{{nbsp}}million), South Korea ($49.2{{nbsp}}million), Mexico ($40.6{{nbsp}}million), Brazil ($33.1{{nbsp}}million), and France ($27.7{{nbsp}}million)<!--Top 5 markets are sufficient, per WP:INDISCRIMINATE-->.<ref name="DeadlineInternationalTopMarkets" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of {{RT data|score}}, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' labors under the weight of the sprawling MCU, but Sam Raimi's distinctive direction casts an entertaining spell."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=doctor_strange_in_the_multiverse_of_madness |type=movie |title=Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness |access-date=January 6, 2022}}{{cbignore}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 60 out of 100, based on 65 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=doctor-strange-in-the-multiverse-of-madness |type=movie |title=Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness |access-date=May 14, 2022}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale, while [[PostTrak]] reported 82% of audience members gave it a positive score, with 69% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="PostTrakDeadline" />

Critics had varied opinions on the film, though many praised Sam Raimi's direction for its distinctive style. Don Kaye of ''[[Den of Geek]]'' and [[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' both noted Raimi's touch, with Kaye appreciating the comic book weirdness and Gleiberman acknowledging the excessive CGI but enjoying Raimi's flair.<ref name="KayeDenOfGeek" /><ref name="GleibermanVariety" /> Similarly, Leah Greenblatt of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' found Raimi's direction "wildly refreshing", and Pete Hammond of ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' likened the film's storytelling to Raimi's earlier horror works, such as ''[[The Evil Dead]]''.<ref name="GreenblattEW" /><ref name="HammondDeadline" /> David Ehrlich of ''[[IndieWire]]'' felt that Doctor Strange's character had been diluted and offered a mixed view on Raimi's impact,<ref name="EhrlichIndieWire" /> while Brian Tallerico of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' criticized the film's characterization and excessive reliance on CGI, labeling it a "Frankenstein movie".<ref name="TallericoEbert" /> Similarly, Alonso Duralde of ''[[TheWrap]]'' compared the film unfavorably to ''[[Everything Everywhere All at Once]]'' (2022), criticizing the writing and lack of character development, though he did appreciate the score and visuals.<ref name="DuraldeWrap" /> Overall, while ''Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness'' received praise for its unique direction under Raimi and strong performances, particularly from [[Elizabeth Olsen]], the film's heavy reliance on CGI, its plot complexities, and issues with character development left some critics divided.<ref name="DeforeTHR" /><ref name="ChangLAT" /><ref name="JolinEmpire" /><ref name="BradshawGuardian" />
