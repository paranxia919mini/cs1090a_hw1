'''''Encanto''''' is a 2021 American animated musical [[fantasy comedy film]] directed by [[Jared Bush]] and [[Byron Howard]], and written by [[Charise Castro Smith]] and Bush. Produced by [[Walt Disney Animation Studios]], its original songs were written by [[Lin-Manuel Miranda]], and the original [[Film score|musical orchestral score]], composed by [[Germaine Franco]]. The film stars the voices of [[Stephanie Beatriz]], [[María Cecilia Botero]], [[John Leguizamo]], {{ill|Mauro Castillo|es}}, [[Jessica Darrow]], [[Angie Cepeda]], [[Carolina Gaitán]], [[Diane Guerrero]], and [[Wilmer Valderrama]]. The film follows a multigenerational [[Colombians|Colombian]] family, the Madrigals, led by a matriarch (Botero) whose children and grandchildren—except for [[Mirabel Madrigal]] (Beatriz)—receive magical gifts from a miracle, which they use to help the people in their rural community, called the Encanto. When Mirabel learns that the family is losing their magic, she sets out to find out why and save the family and house.

''Encanto'' premiered at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on November 3, 2021, and was released in the United States on November 24 over a 30-day theatrical run. It was also released in [[RealD 3D]], [[Dolby Cinema]], 2D and [[D-Box Technologies|D-BOX]] formats. Despite <!-- do not link this to box office bomb without a reliable source -->underperformance at the box office, grossing $261.3 million worldwide against a $120–150 million budget, it was a ratings success after its release on [[Disney+]] on December 24, 2021. The film received acclaim for its characterization, music, animation, voice acting, emotional depth, and cultural fidelity; reviews named [[magic realism]] and [[transgenerational trauma]] as the film's core concepts. Publications have described ''Encanto'' as a cultural phenomenon, and executives had begun referring to it as a "franchise" within a year of release.<ref name="Fuster">{{Cite web |last=Fuster |first=Jeremy |date=February 9, 2022 |title=''Encanto'' Touted as a Disney Franchise as CEO Credits Streaming for Its Wider Success |url=https://www.thewrap.com/encanto-disney-theaters-streaming |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220213072515/https://www.thewrap.com/encanto-disney-theaters-streaming/ |archive-date=February 13, 2022 |access-date=February 13, 2022 |website=[[TheWrap]]}}</ref> The film was nominated for three awards at the [[94th Academy Awards]], winning [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature|Best Animated Feature]], and received [[List of accolades received by Encanto|numerous other accolades]], including the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Animated Feature Film|Golden Globe Award for Best Animated Feature]] and the [[BAFTA Award for Best Animated Film]].

The film's associated merchandise has seen significant success, and its [[Encanto (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] became a breakout sensation, reaching number one on the US [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] and UK [[UK Compilation Chart|Compilation Albums]] charts; "[[We Don't Talk About Bruno]]" and "[[Surface Pressure]]" were its two most successful songs, with the former topping both the US [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] and [[UK Singles Chart]] for multiple consecutive weeks. A theme park attraction based on the film is set to open at [[Disney's Animal Kingdom]] in 2027.<ref name="wdwmagic.com">{{cite web|url=https://www.wdwmagic.com/attractions/animal-kingdom/news/11aug2024-disney-announces-new-encanto-themed-ride-for-animal-kingdom.htm|title= Disney Announces New Encanto-Themed Ride for Animal Kingdom|website= WDW Magic|date=August 11, 2024|access-date=August 12, 2024}}</ref>

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot length must be between 400 and 700 words. Current count is 616. Please do not add anything without removing something first or making sure it is absolutely necessary! -->
An armed conflict forces young couple Pedro and Alma Madrigal to flee their home village in [[Colombia]] with their infant triplets, Julieta, Pepa, and [[Bruno Madrigal|Bruno]]. Pedro is killed, but the candle Alma carries gains a magical power that repels the attackers and creates Casita, a sentient house located in a magical realm bordered by mountains. 

Fifty years later, a new village thrives under the candle's protection, which grants "[[Superpower (ability)|gifts]]" to each Madrigal descendant when they turn five, which they use to serve the villagers. Bruno, who was vilified and scapegoated for his [[precognition]], disappeared ten years earlier, and Julieta's youngest daughter, [[Mirabel Madrigal|Mirabel]], inexplicably did not receive a gift.

On his fifth birthday, Pepa's youngest son, Antonio, is granted the ability to communicate with animals. During the celebration, the Madrigals unwittingly exclude Mirabel from being in a family photo. Mirabel sees Casita cracking and the candle's flame flickering and alerts her family, but Casita repairs itself, causing disbelief in Mirabel. After overhearing Alma lament the candle's fading that night, Mirabel resolves to restore Casita's magic herself. Her older sister Luisa, who possesses [[superhuman strength]], confesses to Mirabel that she feels overwhelmed by her constant obligation to help the villagers using her strength, which has been faltering. She suggests to Mirabel that Bruno's room, located inside a forbidden tower in Casita, may hold the secret to the fading magic.

Mirabel finds a cave in Bruno's room that holds pieces of his last vision made of opaque emerald. Alma and the family warn Mirabel not to investigate further, but she reassembles the pieces and sees herself with Casita cracking behind her. Mirabel's father Agustín accidentally discovers Mirabel and warns her to keep the vision a secret, but they are overheard by Pepa's daughter Dolores, who possesses supernatural hearing. Mirabel's oldest sister [[Isabela Madrigal|Isabela]], who can make plants and flowers instantly grow at will, is scheduled to be engaged to the Madrigals' neighbor Mariano Guzmán. Amidst Mariano's proposal, an awkward dinner, and Dolores telling everyone about Mirabel's discovery, Casita begins to crack again, and everyone loses control of their gifts.

Mirabel follows rats carrying the pieces of the vision to a secret passage behind a portrait, where she finds Bruno, who reveals he never left Casita, and his visions alternate between Mirabel saving Casita and destroying it, leading him to believe she is the key to the fate of Casita's magic. Not wanting Mirabel to be hurt, he destroyed the vision and isolated himself. At Mirabel's insistence, Bruno conjures another vision that depicts Mirabel and Isabela, who have a strained relationship, hugging, which strengthens the candle.

Mirabel attempts to apologize to a frustrated Isabela, who confesses she is burdened by her image of perfection and does not want to marry Mariano. Mirabel helps Isabela use her powers more freely and they hug. Alma arrives and angrily blames Mirabel for causing the family's misfortunes out of spite for not having a gift. Mirabel gets into an argument with Alma's behavior and asserts that her harmful expectations for her family are the true cause of Casita dying, which horrifies Alma. In the ensuing argument, Casita is destroyed by the cracks as Mirabel tries to save the candle, which extinguishes in her hands, leaving the Madrigals powerless. Distraught and heartbroken, Mirabel runs away from home.

The next day, Alma finds Mirabel tearfully sitting beside the river where Pedro was killed by the soldiers and where the miracle had begun. Alma realizes that her obsession with preserving the magic made her oblivious to how her expectations and pressure were harming the family. She expresses remorse, reconciles with Mirabel, and reunites with Bruno. The three return to the village and assemble the other Madrigals to rebuild Casita, and the townspeople join in, restoring the family's gifts and reviving Casita. The Madrigals celebrate and take another family photo, this time including Mirabel and Bruno.

During the five years it took to develop the film, Howard and Bush's "true north" was the theme of perspective, "about how you see the other people in your family and how you're seen".<ref name="Steele"/> They began by brainstorming ideas on a [[whiteboard]].<ref name="Valdivia">{{Cite web |last=Valdivia |first=Pablo |date=February 11, 2022 |title=Abuelo Pedro's Death In "Encanto" Was Originally Supposed To Be Much Darker, And 72 Other "Encanto" Behind-The-Scenes Details |url=https://www.buzzfeed.com/pablovaldivia/encanto-facts-jared-bush |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220211221005/https://www.buzzfeed.com/pablovaldivia/encanto-facts-jared-bush |archive-date=February 11, 2022 |access-date=February 11, 2022 |website=BuzzFeed}}</ref> Early on, they made "three important discoveries" about families which became the basis of the film's story: "1) ''most'' of us don't feel truly seen by our families, 2) ''most'' of us carry burdens we never let our families see, and 3) ''most'' of us are oblivious that nearly ''all of us'', especially within our own families, feel the exact same way".<ref name="art book">{{Cite book |last=Jones |first=Juan Pablo Reyes Lancaster |title=The Art of Encanto |date=November 23, 2021 |publisher=[[Chronicle Books]] |isbn=978-1-79720-086-6 |location=San Francisco}}</ref>{{rp|8}} In turn, it was Miranda who suggested that the "vibrant, broad spectrum" of [[Latin American music]] could "best capture" the complexity of family relationships.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|8}}

Early in development, the production team spoke with many therapists and psychologists. All were asked who would be treated best by the parents in a four-person family; every answer differed. Bush concluded: "It's about how you perceive yourself in the family."<ref name="Gray">{{Cite web |last=Gray |first=Tim |date=February 28, 2022 |title='Encanto' Writers on Bruno's Viral Popularity and the Madrigal's Complex Family Dynamics: 'We Spoke to a Lot of Therapists' |url=https://variety.com/2022/film/awards/encanto-bruno-popularity-madrigal-family-1235190706 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220311045033/https://variety.com/2022/film/awards/encanto-bruno-popularity-madrigal-family-1235190706/ |archive-date=March 11, 2022 |access-date=March 13, 2022 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> The crew also consulted family members and Disney Animation people.<ref name="Hullender1"/>

Howard and Bush started to discuss [[Culture of Latin America|Latin American culture]] at length with Juan Rendon and Natalie Osma, who had previously worked with them on the making-of documentary ''Imagining Zootopia''.<ref name="Brown"/><ref name="Steele"/> Rendon and Osma both happened to be from Colombia and repeatedly drew upon their personal experiences with [[Colombian culture]] in their discussions, which caused Howard, Bush, and Miranda to focus their research on that country.<ref name="Brown"/> Rendon and Osma became the first two of several cultural experts hired by Disney Animation as consultants on the film,<ref name="Brown"/> who collectively formed what Disney called the "Colombian Cultural Trust".<ref name="Steele"/><ref name="Carey">{{Cite web |last=Carey |first=Meredith |date=November 24, 2021 |title=On Location: The Colombian Towns, Architecture, and Music that Inspired Disney's Encanto |url=https://www.cntraveler.com/story/on-location-encanto-disney |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211227095044/https://www.cntraveler.com/story/on-location-encanto-disney |archive-date=December 27, 2021 |access-date=December 27, 2021 |website=[[Condé Nast Traveler]]}}</ref>

In 2018, Rendon and Osma accompanied Howard, Bush, and Miranda on a research trip to Colombia.<ref name="Brown"/> During their two weeks in the country,<ref name="Steele"/> they met with architects, chefs, and artisans to immerse themselves in the country's culture.<ref name="Carey"/> They also visited the [[Gabriel García Márquez]] foundation.<ref name="Carey"/> They visited big cities like [[Bogotá]] and [[Cartagena, Colombia|Cartagena]], but they found inspiration in small towns such as [[Salento, Quindío|Salento]] (terrain) and [[Barichara]] (architecture).<ref name="Carey"/> Bush noticed that "every town we went to had a very specific personality", because of how the country's mountainous terrain divides and isolates them.<ref name="Steele"/>

According to Disney fan club publication ''Disney twenty-three,'' this isolation became the key to placing the Madrigals' residence in a "remote 'encanto'—that is, a place that's 'charmed', or spiritually blessed, a domain where magic and reality merge".<ref name="Steele"/> As Colombian tourist guide Alejandra Espinosa Uribe explained, Colombians are surrounded by "sacred lands that feel magical, and we coexist with them, not questioning their existence".<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|30}}

In Barichara, they befriended Espinosa Uribe, who showed them around the town, and later hired her to consult on the film's historical and cultural authenticity.<ref name="HerreraGómez">{{Cite web |last=Herrera Gómez |first=Gabriela |date=December 17, 2021 |title=Ella es 'la verdadera' Mirabel Madrigal de Encanto, la película de Disney |url=https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/encanto-de-disney-la-historia-secreta-detras-de-mirabel-639578 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211227095035/https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/encanto-de-disney-la-historia-secreta-detras-de-mirabel-639578 |archive-date=December 27, 2021 |access-date=December 27, 2021 |website=[[El Tiempo (Colombia)|El Tiempo]] |language=es}}</ref> Uribe was an inspiration for several aspects of the film's protagonist Mirabel, including her curly black hair, large eyeglasses, and gestures.<ref name="HerreraGómez"/> The design of Mirabel's skirt was inspired by traditional skirts woven in the [[Vélez, Santander|Vélez]] area.<ref name="HerreraGómez"/>

The final version of the film is deliberately vague as to the timeframe in which it is set, but drew inspiration from early-20th-century Colombia.<ref name="HerreraGómez"/> After exploring the 1950s, the directors decided to shift the look of the film to the early 1900s and to use a "folkloric Colombian aesthetic".<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|22}} At the beginning of the 20th century, the country endured the [[Thousand Days' War]], which resulted in the populations of entire villages [[Internally displaced person|fleeing to save themselves]] as depicted in the film.<ref name="HerreraGómez"/>

=== Writing ===
As the film steadily became more complex, with an entire family to develop, multiple songs, and a rich cultural setting with a deep tradition of [[magical realism]], Howard and Bush realized they needed a second screenwriter to help write the screenplay.<ref name="Steele"/> They selected Charise Castro Smith for her strong background in magical realism and experience with handling "real-world family dynamics".<ref name="Steele"/> Bush described her as a "godsend", as she provided "a foundation of heart, vulnerability and authenticity". Castro Smith sought to create a distinct, imperfect, and completely human character in Mirabel, one that spoke to the lives of many Latinas while also being relatable to viewers globally.<ref name="Ratner-Arias">{{Cite web |last=Ratner-Arias |first=Sigal |date=October 15, 2021 |title=Charise Castro Smith brings Latin experience to 'Encanto' |url=https://apnews.com/article/entertainment-lifestyle-colombia-new-york-movies-b34a37efb110300393cc2282e2b6821a |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220330030405/https://apnews.com/article/entertainment-lifestyle-colombia-new-york-movies-b34a37efb110300393cc2282e2b6821a |archive-date=March 30, 2022 |access-date=March 17, 2022 |publisher=[[Associated Press]]}}</ref>

From working on ''Moana'', Miranda knew that film's protagonist, [[Moana (character)|Moana]], originally had eight brothers before they were removed to streamline the film's plot.<ref name="Cavna"/> As Miranda had expected from that experience, Disney Animation initially resisted moving forward with a dozen main characters for ''Encanto''.<ref name="Cavna"/> Miranda deliberately wrote the film's opening number, "The Family Madrigal", to prove to Disney Animation that it was possible to efficiently introduce such a large family and its internal dynamics to the audience.<ref name="Cavna"/> Because of the film's 90-minute runtime, the filmmakers struggled to make the center family feel genuine, fleshed out, and human, and to give them arcs.<ref name="Hullender1">{{Cite interview |last=Howard |first=Byron |interviewer=Tatiana Hullender |title=Byron Howard, Jared Bush, & Charise Castro Smith Interview: Encanto |last2=Bush |first2=Jared |last3=Castro Smith |first3=Charise |url=https://screenrant.com/encanto-movie-director-interview |access-date=March 17, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220127045855/https://screenrant.com/encanto-movie-director-interview/ |archive-date=January 27, 2022 |url-status=live |website=[[Screen Rant]] |date=September 29, 2021}}</ref>

The crew worked hard to develop ''Encanto'' upon instantly identifiable family [[archetype]]s. The team focused on pushing past the archetypes and showing that family members are more complicated than the "masks" imposed on them.<ref name="Brown"/> Each member of the Madrigal family is inspired by a common family archetype if it were exaggerated and made magical; for example, Isabela and Luisa are based on that of the [[wikt:golden child|golden child]] and rock, respectively.<ref name="Fleming">{{Cite web |last=Fleming |first=Ryan |date=January 11, 2022 |title=''Encanto'' Directors on Colombian Influences and Magical Realism: 'Magic That Was Born Out Of Emotion' |url=https://deadline.com/2022/01/jared-bush-byron-howard-charise-castro-smith-encanto-disney-animation-dialogue-1234908399 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220313143928/https://deadline.com/2022/01/jared-bush-byron-howard-charise-castro-smith-encanto-disney-animation-dialogue-1234908399/ |archive-date=March 13, 2022 |access-date=March 13, 2022 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> Bush explained that this method prevented each gift from feeling arbitrary, instead being prescribed by emotions and personality.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|26}}

Like most Disney Animation films, ''Encanto'' went through "many different versions" in its development, as various story ideas were explored and discarded.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|17}} The ideas discarded along the way include: 
* a young modern-day woman is transported by a magical doorknob to another world;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|17}} 
* Agustín was the family patriarch who discovered Encanto and personally built Casita there;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|17}} 
* setting the film expressly in the 1950s featuring cosmopolitan and modern fashions,<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|22}} as well as [[motor vehicle]]s and a trip to the big city where Alma had originally come from;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|146}} 
* Casita was an extension of only Alma's personality, rather than the entire family;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|41}} 
* following the story of multiple generations and Casita over a 100-year timespan;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|49}} 
* Mirabel was desperately trying to find her magical gift, rather than merely expressing frustration with not being granted one;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|71}} 
* the colors of Mirabel's world as seen from her perspective would have reflected her emotional state as she narrated the first two acts, and then changed over to more realistic colors when the film shifted to Alma's point of view in the third act;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|76}} 
* Luisa's room would have been made out of stone and appeared "boring" to reflect her sense of responsibility, but would have concealed a "secret exit" to a hidden room similar to a "theme park" where she could have fun;<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|94}} 
* Isabela at one point had a "dorky" suitor named Bubo who came from the big city.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|153}}

After the film's release, Bush revealed via Twitter several more ideas which had been discussed and set aside: Alma gave birth to the triplets at the river, at the moment of Pedro's death; Isabela and Mirabel's mutual hostility culminated in a fistfight; and Mirabel celebrated her [[quinceañera]].<ref name="Valdivia"/> According to Howard, as the plot evolved over several years, "the core of the whole film" was always the relationship between Mirabel and Alma.<ref name="Steele"/><ref name="art book"/>{{rp|116}}

=== Design ===
''Encanto'' was the "hardest film" to date for heads of animation Renato dos Anjos and Kira Lehtomäki because they were asked to fully develop a dozen characters, as opposed to other animated films which primarily feature two or three characters out of a large cast.<ref name="Davis">{{Cite web |last=Davis |first=Victoria |date=November 23, 2021 |title=How Choreography and Color Helped Shape the Vibrant Characters of 'Encanto' |url=https://www.awn.com/animationworld/how-choreography-and-color-helped-shape-vibrant-characters-encanto |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211125234516/https://www.awn.com/animationworld/how-choreography-and-color-helped-shape-vibrant-characters-encanto |archive-date=November 25, 2021 |access-date=December 31, 2021 |website=[[Animation World Network]]}}</ref> Accordingly, the characters required a degree of complexity that Disney Animation had previously never achieved.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zahed |first=Ramin |date=October 28, 2021 |title=All in the Familia Colombiana: Crafting Disney's ''Encanto'' |url=https://www.animationmagazine.net/features/all-in-the-familia-colombiana-crafting-disneys-encanto |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211130164523/https://www.animationmagazine.net/features/all-in-the-familia-colombiana-crafting-disneys-encanto/ |archive-date=November 30, 2021 |access-date=April 20, 2022 |website=[[Animation Magazine]]}}</ref> Journalist Edna Liliana Valencia Murillo was the [[Afro-Caribbean]] consultant and contributed greatly towards Félix, Dolores, and Antonio's designs.<ref name="Nechamkin">{{Cite interview |last=Bové |first=Neysa |interviewer=Sarah Nechamkin |title=How a Pair of Real-Life Sisters Brought Encanto to Life Through Clothing |last2=Bové |first2=Lorelay |url=https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a39284016/encanto-costumes-interview |access-date=April 20, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220322211646/https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a39284016/encanto-costumes-interview/ |archive-date=March 22, 2022 |url-status=live |website=[[Elle (magazine)|Elle]] |date=March 2, 2022}}</ref> According to Howard, certain characters' [[Black Hispanic and Latino Americans|Afro-Latin]] hairstyles were essential.<ref name="Hofferman"/> Disney veterans Ian Gooding and Lorelay Bové were hired as production designers for the film. Lorelay and her sister, costume design lead Neysa Bové, hoped to represent the film's various communities well. Neysa requested Colombian consultants, which included an [[anthropologist]] and botanist.<ref name="Nechamkin"/>

Instead of fabrics similar to [[neoprene]] that are often depicted in films, the characters' costumes are generally loose. The clothes are meant to appear as though they are from Colombia. The designers emphasized embroidery, texture, and movement; each costume—except Isabela's—is asymmetric, with a handmade feeling.<ref name="Nechamkin"/> In ''Encanto'', personalities and family ties are represented through color. For example, Pepa and Félix's side of the family wear warm tones of orange, yellow, and red—an homage to his Caribbean ancestry.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|29}}

By contrast, Julieta and Agustín's side wear cool tones of blues, purples, and greens. Alma "sits in the middle", with darker hues, such as purple, black, and brown.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|29}} Each Madrigal has a symbol on their clothing that denotes their gift, such as barbells on Luisa's skirt and Pepa's sun earrings. Due to her lack of a gift, Mirabel has symbols of the other family members' gifts embroidered across her skirt,<ref name="Cooper">{{Cite web |last=Cooper |first=Gael Fashingbauer |date=January 14, 2022 |title=13 fun facts about Encanto, from Mirabel's gift to hidden Easter eggs |url=https://www.cnet.com/culture/entertainment/13-fun-facts-about-encanto-from-mirabels-gift-to-hidden-easter-eggs |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220416104027/https://www.cnet.com/culture/entertainment/13-fun-facts-about-encanto-from-mirabels-gift-to-hidden-easter-eggs/ |archive-date=April 16, 2022 |access-date=April 16, 2022 |website=[[CNET]]}}</ref> in addition to her face and name.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mason |first=Courtney |date=January 2, 2022 |title=Encanto Subtly Revealed Its Mirabel Power Twist At The Beginning |url=https://screenrant.com/encanto-mirabel-gift-power-outfit-family-detail-reveal |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220102185042/https://screenrant.com/encanto-mirabel-gift-power-outfit-family-detail-reveal/ |archive-date=January 2, 2022 |access-date=April 16, 2022 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref>

The residents of the ''Encanto'' dress in neutral tones; thus, the main family are distinguished by their vivid colors.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|29}} They needed to be distinctive while not standing out, which visual development artist Jin Kim said occasionally made them harder to design than the main cast.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|148}} Disney used simple silhouettes for the villagers' clothes to make them seem and feel similar to typical residents from the Colombian coffee region. They incorporated depictions of the [[Sombrero vueltiao]] from the [[Zenú]]&nbsp;Indigenous Community and the Sombrero Aguadeño from the Aguadas Community.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|149}}

The animators were challenged by the directors to make Mirabel distinctively different from all prior Disney heroines; she had to be both capable and imperfect, but not merely just clumsy.<ref name="Davis"/> The Casita was inspired not only by traditional Colombian homes but films in which houses come alive, especially ''[[Beetlejuice]]'' (1988).<ref name="Davis"/>

The production team learned in their research that music, dance, and rhythm are core elements of everyday life in Colombia.<ref name="Fay">{{Cite web |last=Fay |first=Kim |date=Winter 2021 |title=Step Into Choreomation |url=https://keyframemagazine.org/2021/12/06/step-into-choreomation/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211208181224/https://keyframemagazine.org/2021/12/06/step-into-choreomation/ |archive-date=December 8, 2021 |access-date=December 31, 2021 |website=Keyframe}}</ref> As a result, ''Encanto'' was the first Disney Animation film to have choreographers involved in the development process from start to finish, meaning they worked closely with the production team to develop songs, characters, and story.<ref name="Davis"/><ref name="Fay"/>

This is in contrast to older films where the story was already in place by the time a choreographer was hired to consult on specific scenes.<ref name="Fay"/> For ''Encanto'', Disney Animation initially hired African-American choreographer [[Jamal Sims]], who insisted on immediately hiring Colombian-American dancer Kai Martinez as the film's animation reference consultant.<ref name="Davis"/><ref name="Fay"/> They worked together with a team of dancers to prepare choreography reference footage for each scene, and then gave feedback to the animators on scenes as they were animated.<ref name="Davis"/><ref name="Fay"/> They developed different dance styles for different characters; Luisa's style is [[reggaeton]], while Mirabel's style is [[Cali, Colombia|Cali]] [[Salsa (dance)|salsa]].<ref name="Fay"/>

For selection and animation of the plants and flowers that Isabela could make grow, the production team consulted with Colombian botanist Felipe Zapata. His advice allowed appropriate plants to be selected and then shown in accurate detail. The river of [[Jacaranda mimosifolia|jacarandas]] and [[strangler fig]]s and the cascade of [[Drosera|sundew]] that Isabela produces are all based on this input. Plants in the background scenery are also realistic. These included the iconic, but now vulnerable [[Ceroxylon quindiuense|wax palm]] and dramatic [[Cecropia]] trees. Economically important plants, like [[Coffea|coffee]], also appear.<ref name="MoBG2021">{{Cite web |last=Moody |first=Cassidy |date=January 28, 2022 |title=The plants of Encanto |url=https://discoverandshare.org/2022/01/28/the-plants-of-encanto/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220131112531/https://discoverandshare.org/2022/01/28/the-plants-of-encanto/ |archive-date=January 31, 2022 |access-date=January 31, 2022 |website=Missouri Botanical Garden}}</ref>

Over 800 people, 108 of them animators, were involved in the production of ''Encanto''.<ref name="Renken">{{Cite web |last=Renken |first=Leslie |date=February 15, 2022 |title=This Bartonville native is living his dream career with Disney's new 'Encanto' movie |url=https://www.pjstar.com/story/entertainment/movies/2022/02/15/bartonville-native-worked-on-disneys-animated-film-encanto/6677460001/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220216164943/https://www.pjstar.com/story/entertainment/movies/2022/02/15/bartonville-native-worked-on-disneys-animated-film-encanto/6677460001/ |archive-date=February 16, 2022 |access-date=February 16, 2022 |website=[[Journal Star (Peoria)|Journal Star]]}}</ref> Disney Animation was planning to send many of its animators to Colombia starting on March 15, 2020, in preparation for the film's transition from development to production. This second research trip had to be cancelled after the onset of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. The studio's animators ended up having to [[work remotely]] with the Colombian Cultural Trust; for example, Espinosa Uribe gave the animators a [[virtual tour]] of Colombia with the help of her cell phone.<ref name="HerreraGómez"/> Diane Guerrero sent the studio's designers photographs of Colombian food taken by her relatives in Colombia.<ref name="Valdivia"/>

On June 22, 2020, Miranda confirmed on ''[[Good Morning America]]'' that he and Bush were working on an animated Disney film set in Colombia, with Bush and Byron Howard directing, and Charise Castro Smith co-directing.<ref name=Miranda2020>{{Cite web |last=Evans |first=Greg |date=June 22, 2020 |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda Confirms New Disney Animated Movie Set In Colombia |url=https://deadline.com/2020/06/lin-manuel-miranda-new-disney-animated-movie-colombia-good-morning-america-1202966143/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200622202046/https://deadline.com/2020/06/lin-manuel-miranda-new-disney-animated-movie-colombia-good-morning-america-1202966143/ |archive-date=June 22, 2020 |access-date=June 22, 2020 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref>

On June 18, 2020, the tentative title was revealed to be ''Encanto''. The project was also confirmed to be the film Miranda was involved in, and it was reported to be about a girl in a magical family. On December 10, 2020, the project was officially confirmed at a Disney Investor Day meeting, where a clip was shown, a fall 2021 release was announced, and magical realism was referenced.<ref>{{Cite web |last=<!--Not stated--> |date=December 10, 2020 |title=The Walt Disney Company Investor Day 2020 |url=https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/app/uploads/2020/12/Disney_Investor_Day_2020_transcript.pdf |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210807123345/https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/app/uploads/2020/12/Disney_Investor_Day_2020_transcript.pdf |archive-date=August 7, 2021 |access-date=June 20, 2021 |website=The Walt Disney Company |page=63}}</ref> According to Bush, development of the film's script [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] in August 2021, and production on the film wrapped a month later.<ref name="Valdivia"/> Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, many crew members worked together remotely for over a year and did not meet each other in person until Disney put on a socially distanced outdoor screening of the final version of the film.<ref name="Renken"/>

=== Cinematography ===
''Encanto'' is the first [[Walt Disney Animation Studios]] film since ''[[Winnie the Pooh (2011 film)|Winnie the Pooh]]'' (2011) to employ the taller [[Aspect ratio (image)#1.85:1|1.85:1 aspect ratio]] instead of [[Aspect ratio (image)#2.39:1|2.39:1]], a decision made to "get closer to the characters". This [[Layout (computing)|layout]] was supplemented by [[romanticism|romantic]] lighting.<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|168}} According to Howard, ''romantic'' (meaning heightened in spirit) is the film's key word in terms of the approach towards cinematography and lighting. The latter was also inspired by magical realism, due to the genre's heavy influence on the writing and characters.<ref name="Hofferman">{{Cite web |last=Hofferman |first=Jon |date=December 2, 2021 |title='Encanto' Pushes Animation Boundaries While Celebrating the Fundamentals of Family |url=https://www.awn.com/animationworld/encanto-pushes-animation-boundaries-while-celebrating-fundamentals-family |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220311075505/https://www.awn.com/animationworld/encanto-pushes-animation-boundaries-while-celebrating-fundamentals-family |archive-date=March 11, 2022 |access-date=March 11, 2022 |website=[[Animation World Network]]}}</ref>

The directors aimed to use the film's optics to portray strong emotions. Alessandro Jacomini and Daniel Rice—directors of cinematography and lighting, respectively—stated this use "would amplify, exaggerate, and distort perception, which is very in line with Mirabel's point of view as a narrator". Her feelings of being left out were also shown using emotive lighting. The directors frequently reminded the crew of the emotional subtext&nbsp;of every&nbsp;scene as well as the intricacies of the individual parts. That subtext was applied&nbsp;by Warner, Jacomini, and Rice in elaborate&nbsp;sequences, such as the song "[[Surface Pressure]]".<ref name="art book"/>{{rp|168}}

''[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]'' writer Leila Cobo said, following the success of Colombian musicians like [[Shakira]], [[Maluma]] and [[J Balvin]] in the U.S., Colombia is "finally seen and not just heard" via ''Encanto''. == Other media ==
=== ''Encanto at the Hollywood Bowl'' ===
''Encanto at the Hollywood Bowl'' is a 2022 live musical hosted by [[Lin-Manuel Miranda]], which includes some of the film's cast members performing the songs from the film. The stage musical was performed on November 11 and 12 at the [[Hollywood Bowl]], and a concert film version was released on December 28, 2022 on [[Disney+]].<ref>{{cite web |first=Reichert |last=Corinne |date=October 28, 2022 |url=https://www.cnet.com/culture/entertainment/encanto-live-hits-the-hollywood-bowl/ |title='Encanto' Live Hits the Hollywood Bowl }}</ref>

=== ''Nightmares and Sueños'' ===
The  [[Young adult literature|young adult novel]] ''Nightmares and Sueños'', written by Alex Segura, is a prequel to the film that focuses on seventeen-year-old Bruno and shows why people do not talk about him.<ref name="Bruno book">{{cite web |last=Aguila |first=Ed |title='Encanto' Prequel Releasing Soon! |url=https://insidethemagic.net/2024/03/new-encanto-installment-coming-soon-ea1/ |website=Inside the Magic |access-date=March 17, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240317000830/https://insidethemagic.net/2024/03/new-encanto-installment-coming-soon-ea1/ |archive-date=March 17, 2024 |date=March 10, 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref> It released on December 3, 2024.<ref name="Nightmares release date">{{cite web |title=Encanto: Nightmares and Sueños |url=https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/encanto-alex-segura/1145011219 |website=[[Barnes & Noble]] |access-date=April 19, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240419012816/https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/encanto-alex-segura/1145011219 |archive-date=April 19, 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Theme park attractions ===
On March 21, 2024, Disney announced that a new ''Encanto'' stage show, ''¡Celebración Encanto!'' would temporarily run from June 10 through September 6, 2024. However, it was later announced that it would become a full-time entertainment offering, and is currently permanent. The show is held multiple times daily at the CommuniCore Plaza in the World Celebration area at [[Epcot]], and features live appearances from Mirabel and Bruno.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://wdwnt.com/2024/03/breaking-communicore-hall-plaza-opening-date-at-epcot-announced/|title= BREAKING: CommuniCore Hall & Plaza Opening Date at EPCOT Announced|website= WDW News Today|date=March 21, 2024|access-date=March 22, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-20 |title=Celebración Encanto Extended Plus Food, Entertainment, and More Coming to Disney World for Hispanic Heritage Month |url=https://wdwnt.com/2024/08/celebracion-encanto-extended-plus-food-entertainment-and-more-coming-to-disney-world-for-hispanic-heritage-month/ |access-date=2025-03-20 |website=WDW News Today |language=en-US}}</ref>

====Upcoming theme park ride====
In August 2024, it was announced that an ''Encanto'' ride is in development for [[Disney's Animal Kingdom]], scheduled to open in 2027 as part of a new Tropical Americas section of the park, which will replace DinoLand U.S.A.. The ride will take guests on a tour of the Casita on the day of Antonio's gift ceremony, celebrating his connection with animals.<ref>{{cite news|last=Ace|first=Shannen|url=https://wdwnt.com/2024/08/first-encanto-attraction-coming-to-disneys-animal-kingdom/|title=First 'Encanto' Attraction Coming to Disney's Animal Kingdom|website=wdwnt.com|date=August 11, 2024|accessdate=June 12, 2025}}</ref> In December 2025, it was announced that the original voice actors will return for the ride.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ace |first=Shannen |date=2025-12-15 |title=Original 'Encanto' Cast Returning for New Ride at Disney's Animal Kingdom |url=https://wdwnt.com/2025/12/original-encanto-cast-returning-new-ride-animal-kingdom/ |access-date=2025-12-16 |website=WDW News Today |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Future ==
Jared Bush and Charise Castro Smith said they are open to a potential Disney+ series. Bush stated he would be happy to see a show about any member of the family, and Castro Smith shared that Miranda was very interested in creating a show about Dolores.<ref name="Future">{{Cite web |last=Betts |first=Emma-Jane |date=November 24, 2021 |title=Encanto director thinks a Disney Plus TV series is the 'right thing to do' |url=https://www.thedigitalfix.com/encanto/director-disney-plus-tv-series |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220206144654/https://www.thedigitalfix.com/encanto/director-disney-plus-tv-series |archive-date=February 6, 2022 |access-date=February 6, 2022 |website=Digital Fix}}</ref> Then–Disney CEO [[Bob Chapek]] described ''Encanto'' as the company's latest franchise during a February 2022 [[earnings call]].<ref name="Fuster"/> However, Miranda revealed in December 2024, three years after the film's release: "We haven't talked about any ''Encanto'' sequels".<ref name="Bahr">{{cite news |last1=Bahr |first1=Sarah |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda on 'Mufasa' and the Secret to a Disney Hit |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/22/arts/music/lin-manuel-miranda-mufasa.html |work=The New York Times |date=December 22, 2024 |url-access=subscription}}</ref>

In March 2025, John Leguizamo reported that the possibility of a sequel is in the works, once the team in charge comes up with a storyline.<ref>{{cite web |author=Sharareh Drury and Aliza Sessler |date=March 19, 2025 |title=John Leguizamo Teases Possible ''Encanto'' Sequel Where 'All We Do Is Talk About Bruno' (Exclusive) |url=https://people.com/john-leguizamo-teases-possible-encanto-sequel-11700219 |website=People.com}}</ref>
