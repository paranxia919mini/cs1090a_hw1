'''Jenni Olson''' (born October 6, 1962) is a writer, archivist, historian, consultant, and non-fiction filmmaker based in Berkeley, California. She co-founded the pioneering LGBT website [[PlanetOut.com]].<ref name="OFFSC">{{cite web|author=Diduck|first=Ryan|date=June 2006|title=Inside the Homo Studio: with Jenni Olson|url=http://www.offscreen.com/biblio/essays/inside_jenni_olson|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200717165936/https://offscreen.com/view/inside_jenni_olson|archive-date=17 July 2020|access-date=27 June 2021|work=Offscreen}}</ref> Her two feature-length essay films — ''[[The Joy of Life]] (2005)'' and ''[[The Royal Road]] (2015)'' — premiered at the [[Sundance Film Festival]].

Olson's work as an experimental filmmaker and her expansive personal collection of LGBTQ film prints and memorabilia were acquired in April 2020 by the [[Harvard Film Archive]],<ref name="HarvardFilmArch">{{cite web |title=Jenni Olson Queer Film Collection - Collection |url=https://harvardfilmarchive.org/collections/jenni-olson-queer-film-collection |website=Harvard Film Archive |access-date=28 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref> and her reflection on the last 30 years of LGBT film history was published as a chapter in ''The Oxford Handbook of Queer Cinema'' from [[Oxford University Press]] in 2021.<ref>{{cite web |title=Oxford Handbooks - Oxford University Press |url=https://global.oup.com/academic/content/series/o/oxford-handbooks-ohbk/?type=listing&subjectcode1=1793239%7CAHU00010&subjectcode1=1793239%7CAHU00010&subjectcode2=1802307%7CAHU01750&prevNumResPerPage=20&prevSortField=1&sortField=8&resultsPerPage=20&lang=en&cc=us |website=global.oup.com |access-date=28 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref> In 2020, she was named to the [[Out Magazine]] Out 100 list.<ref>{{cite web |title=See the Full 2020 Out100 List Here |url=https://www.out.com/print/2020/11/19/see-he-full-2020-out100-list-here#media-gallery-media-22 |website=www.out.com |access-date=28 July 2021 |language=en |date=19 November 2020}}</ref> In 2021, she was recognized with the prestigious Special [[TEDDY Award]] at the [[Berlin Film Festival]].<ref name="theguardian.com">{{Cite web|date=2021-06-15|title=Jenni Olson: 'I remember walking out of the movie theatre like, "Yeah, I'm a cowboy!"'|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2021/jun/15/jenni-olson-i-remember-walking-out-of-the-movie-theatre-like-yeah-im-a-cowboy|access-date=2021-06-27|website=the Guardian|language=en}}</ref> She also campaigned to have a barrier erected on the Golden Gate Bridge to prevent suicides.<ref name="NYT">{{cite news|first=Andrew|last=Blum|title=Suicide Watch|date=March 20, 2005 |work=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2005/03/20/movies/MoviesFeatures/20blum.html?ex=1166677200&en=5f58d5b57b6cf65f&ei=5070|accessdate=October 3, 2015}}</ref> Since 2021, Olson has led the Social Media Safety Program at [[GLAAD]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.advocate.com/media/2021/7/29/jenni-olson-lead-glaads-social-media-safety-program |title=Jenni Olson to Lead GLAAD's Social Media Safety Program |first=Trudy |last=Ring |work=[[The Advocate (LGBT magazine)|The Advocate]] |date=July 29, 2021 |access-date=March 10, 2022 }}</ref>

==Biography==
Olson was born and raised in [[Falcon Heights, Minnesota]], and was educated at the [[University of Minnesota]] where she earned her degree in Film Studies. In 1986, while still a student, Olson co-founded the Minneapolis/St.Paul Lesbian, Gay, Bi & Transgender Film Festival, initially under the name Lavender Images.<ref name="SFC14-10">{{cite news|last=Ford|first=Dave|date=October 15, 2004|title=S.F. film historian's latest book uses movie posters to shed light on changing mainstream views of lesbians and gay men|work=SFGate|publisher=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|url=http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2004/10/15/WBG0F96VMV1.DTL&hw=Jenni+Olson&sn=001&sc=1000|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110524230419/http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2004/10/15/WBG0F96VMV1.DTL&hw=Jenni+Olson&sn=001&sc=1000|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 24, 2011|access-date=27 June 2021}}</ref>  Olson was inspired in this move by [[Vito Russo]]'s book, ''[[The Celluloid Closet (book)|The Celluloid Closet]]''. In 1992, Olson was hired by the company [[Frameline]] and moved to San Francisco to work as guest curator on the [[San Francisco International Lesbian and Gay Film Festival]], before being appointed co-director alongside [[Mark Finch]].<ref name="SFC14-10"/> After three years, Olson left this position to co-found the website PlanetOut.com.<ref name="SFist">{{cite web|url=http://www.sfist.com/archives/2005/07/08/interview_jenni_olson.php|title=Interview: Jenni Olson|access-date=2006-12-19|author=Matt Baume|authorlink=Matt Baume|date=July 8, 2005|publisher=SFist|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060830090334/http://www.sfist.com/archives/2005/07/08/interview_jenni_olson.php|archive-date=August 30, 2006}}</ref> Olson worked as director of entertainment and e-commerce for the site, as well fulfilling the same roles for [[Gay.com]]. She created the PopcornQ section of the PlanetOut.com website, basing the section on her book ''The Ultimate Guide to Lesbian & Gay Film and Video.''<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.glbtq.com/contributors/bio_262.html |title=Contributor Biography: Jenni Olson |access-date=2006-12-19 |publisher=glbtq.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061017000200/http://www.glbtq.com/contributors/bio_262.html |archive-date=2006-10-17 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.joyoflifemovie.com/castandcrew.html|title=Cast and Crew|accessdate=2006-12-19|work=The Joy of Life official website|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20061208065147/http://www.joyoflifemovie.com/castandcrew.html|archivedate=2006-12-08}}</ref>

In 1997, Olson attended the [[Sundance Festival]] and arranged, along with [[Outfest]] executive director [[Morgan Rumpf]], a small brunch aimed at fellow queer attendees. The event has happened annually since then being co-presented by PlanetOut.com and Outfest until 2005. Since the demise of PlanetOut, it is now presented solely by Outfest.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://48hills.org/2021/06/queer-film-hero-jenni-olsen-honored-with-top-teddy-award/ |title=Queer film hero Jenni Olsen honored with top 'Teddy' award |last=Grady |first=Pam |date=June 16, 2021 |website=48 Hills |access-date=September 30, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210616174616/https://48hills.org/2021/06/queer-film-hero-jenni-olsen-honored-with-top-teddy-award/ |archive-date=June 16, 2021}}</ref> The sponsors described it in 2005 as "the premiere gay and lesbian industry event during Sundance".<ref>{{cite press release|title=here! Networks to Present 10th Annual Outfest Queer Brunch at Sundance Film Festival |publisher=here! Networks & [[Outfest]]: The Los Angeles Gay and Lesbian Film Festival |date=December 8, 2005 |url=http://www.outfest.org/archives/ad.news10.html |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120911043617/http://www.outfest.org/archives/ad.news10.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=September 11, 2012 |access-date=2006-12-19 }}</ref> By March 2005, Olson was named Director of E-Commerce & Consumer Marketing for [[Wolfe Video]]/Wolfe Releasing.<ref name="OFFSC"/>

In 2021, she was recognized with the Special [[TEDDY Award]] at the [[Berlin Film Festival]]— for her service to the [[LGBTQ]] film community.<ref name="theguardian.com"/>

Olson is currently co-director of The Bressan Project, devoted to restoring and re-releasing the films of pioneering gay filmmaker [[Arthur J. Bressan Jr.]]<ref>{{cite web |title=Home |url=https://bressanproject.wixsite.com/website |website=The Bressan Project |access-date=28 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref> Her work as a film historian includes the [[Lambda Literary Award]]-nominated<ref>{{cite web |title=Jenni Olson - Artist |url=https://www.macdowell.org/artists/jenni-olson |website=MacDowell |access-date=28 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref> ''The Queer Movie Poster Book'' and her many vintage movie trailer presentations (''Homo Promo'', ''Afro Promo'', etc.). Jenni's film criticism has appeared in numerous publications including [[Filmmaker Magazine]], [[The Advocate (LGBT magazine)|The Advocate]], and the [[San Francisco Bay Guardian]] and she is currently a film columnist for [[Logo TV]]'s NewNowNext.<ref>{{cite web |title=Jenni Olson {{!}} NewNowNext |url=http://www.newnownext.com/author/jenni_olson/ |website=LOGO News |access-date=28 July 2021}}</ref>

In July 2021, Olson also joined [[GLAAD]] to lead their Social Media Safety Program.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.advocate.com/media/2021/7/29/jenni-olson-lead-glaads-social-media-safety-program |title=Jenni Olson to Lead GLAAD's Social Media Safety Program |first=Trudy |last=Ring |work=[[The Advocate (LGBT magazine)|The Advocate]] |date=July 29, 2021 |access-date=March 10, 2022 }}</ref>

==Career==
[[File:The Royal Road — hi-res poster.jpg|thumb|''[[The Royal Road]]'' (2015)]]
Olson initially compiled trailers into documentary features, showing ''Homo Promo'', her compilation of vintage gay movie trailers at the Amsterdam Gay & Lesbian Film Festival in 1991,<ref name="BLFJ">{{cite journal|last=Morris |first=Gary |date=August 2004 |title=I Changed My Socially Constructed Sexual Identity! |journal=Bright Lights Film Journal |issue=45 |issn=0147-4049 |url=http://www.brightlightsfilm.com/45/queer.htm |archive-url=http://arquivo.pt/wayback/20090714035002/http://www.brightlightsfilm.com/45/queer.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=2009-07-14 |access-date=2006-12-19 }}</ref> and her work in this area has been recognised as instructional in teaching students contextualisation.<ref>{{Cite news|title=Spilling out onto Castro Street|first=Marc|last=Siegel|periodical=Jump Cut|number=41|date=May 1997|pages=131–136}}</ref>

She continued compiling trailers throughout the 1990s, with her last such compilation released {{As of|2006|alt=to date}} being ''Bride of Trailer Camp'', released in 2001 (others in the series include: "Trailer Camp", "Neo Homo Promo", "Afro Promo", "Trailers Schmailers").  During this period Olson also wrote ''Ultimate Guide to Lesbian & Gay Film and Video'' (1996).  The book was based on Olson's BA thesis.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.library.yale.edu/rsc/gayles/gayfilm.html |title=Drama and Film|accessdate=2006-12-19|date=2006-08-05|work=Gay & Lesbian Research Guide|publisher=Yale University Library|quote=originated as her B.A. thesis at the University of Minnesota|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20060902031844/http://www.library.yale.edu/rsc/gayles/gayfilm.html|archivedate=2006-09-02}}</ref>  Her next book was ''The Queer Movie Poster Book'' ([[Chronicle Books]], 2005). This book was suggested in 1991 by [[Stuart Marshall]], who recommended Olson pitch the idea to London's [[Gay Men's Press]]. Although the book was turned down by both them and [[Serpent's Tail]], to whom the idea was pitched as a follow-up to her previous book, Olson was eventually commissioned to write the book in 2002 (published in 2005, it went on to be a [[Lambda Literary Awards]] nominee). Olson based the work in part on her own collection of such material, which she has subsequently donated to San Francisco’s [[GLBT Historical Society]].<ref name="BLFJ"/> Her collection was exhibited at the San Francisco Public Library in 2004, with Olson delivering an accompanying lecture.<ref name="SFC14-10"/>

In 2005, Olson released ''[[The Joy of Life]]'', her debut feature, which won Best Outstanding Artistic Achievement at the 2005 [[Outfest]] and at the 2005 Newfest received Best U.S. Narrative Screenplay,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://joyoflifemovie.com/pressplaydates.html|title=Press Materials, Reviews, Upcoming Screenings|accessdate=2006-12-19|publisher=Official website|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20061102132137/http://www.joyoflifemovie.com/pressplaydates.html|archivedate=2006-11-02}}</ref> and has been favorably reviewed in a number of publications. It garnered Olson the [[Marlon Riggs Award]] by the [[San Francisco Film Critics Circle]] in 2005. Working on the film led Olson to pen an open letter to the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' on the matter of the [[Golden Gate Bridge]]'s position as the top suicide landmark in the world. Her former colleague, Mark Finch, had jumped from the bridge on January 14, 1995, and Olson used this event to inform her own film. Her letter was published on the tenth anniversary of Finch's death and supported the [[Psychiatric Foundation of Northern California]]'s launching of a campaign for a barrier to be installed on the bridge.<ref>{{cite news|first=Jenni|last=Olson|page=B9|title=Power Over Life and Death: Another toll goes up on the Golden Gate Bridge|date=January 14, 2005|publisher=San Francisco Chronicle|url=http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2005/01/14/EDGSMAPFFA1.DTL&hw=Jenni+Olson&sn=015&sc=120|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110524230545/http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2005/01/14/EDGSMAPFFA1.DTL&hw=Jenni+Olson&sn=015&sc=120|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 24, 2011}}</ref>  Olson also distributed her film to the bridge's board of directors, noting "several of the bridge directors told me they appreciated seeing the film and found it illuminating",<ref name="SFist"/> and in March 2005, the board voted to explore the installation of a barrier to prevent jumping.<ref name="NYT"/>

Olson's 2009 short film, ''575 Castro St.'' was shot on the empty Castro Camera store set of the Academy Award-winning drama ''[[Milk (2008 American film)|Milk]]''.
