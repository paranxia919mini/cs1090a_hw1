'''''Max et les ferrailleurs''''' ({{langx|en|Max and the Junkmen|italic=yes}}) is a 1971 [[crime drama]] film directed by [[Claude Sautet]], based on the novel of the same name by Claude Néron. The film stars [[Michel Piccoli]] and [[Romy Schneider]], with [[François Périer]] and [[Georges Wilson]] in supporting roles.

==Plot==
Born into a wealthy family of French vintners, Max is a loner who devoted himself entirely to his obsession: the arrest of criminals.<ref name="nytimes">{{cite web |last=Scott |first=A. O. |author-link=A. O. Scott |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/08/10/movies/claude-sautets-max-et-les-ferrailleurs.html |title=Claude Sautet's 'Max et les Ferrailleurs' |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=9 August 2012 |access-date=6 April 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120814083848/http://www.nytimes.com/2012/08/10/movies/claude-sautets-max-et-les-ferrailleurs.html |archive-date=14 August 2012}}</ref> A former judge he is a police inspector and he sees a new band of burglars escape. This failure is still fresh in his mind when he meets Abel who has become a scrap thief and plunders construction sites with a small band of hoodlums around Nanterre. Max plans to encourage them to commit something big and catch them on the spot. Posing as a client, he meets Lily,<ref name="laweekly">{{cite web|url=http://www.laweekly.com/film/french-crime-master-claude-sautets-1971-film-max-and-the-junkmen-finally-arrives-in-la-2612609|title=French Crime Master Claude Sautet's 1971 Film ''Max and the Junkmen'' Finally Arrives in L.A.|first=Scott|last=Foundas|date=10 January 2013|work=[[LA Weekly]]|access-date=6 April 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151005005051/http://www.laweekly.com/film/french-crime-master-claude-sautets-1971-film-max-and-the-junkmen-finally-arrives-in-la-2612609|archive-date=5 October 2015}}</ref> a young German-born prostitute who is the companion of Abel.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cia.edu/cinematheque/film-schedule/2013/02/max-and-the-junkmen|title=Max And The Junkmen|publisher=[[Cleveland Institute of Art]]|access-date=6 April 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130311072808/http://cia.edu/cinematheque/film-schedule/2013/02/max-and-the-junkmen|archive-date=11 March 2013}}</ref> He pretends to be the director of a small bank branch which receives significant amounts of money at regular intervals. He ensures the support of his police commissioner. Max fails however to reveal his role as instigator. Gradually, some feeling arises between Max and Lily. But Max keeps a reserved attitude and merely influences the scrap through her. Finally, guessing the band ready for action, he communicates an ideal date to commit robbery. On the scheduled day, the police await them and they are arrested. Later in the police station, Rosinsky (the top cop in the bank's district) reveals to Max that he wants all collaborators brought to justice, including Lily. Distraught, Max tries to save her and ends up threatening Rosinsky. In an argument, Max pulls out his gun and kills him.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/2013/01/10/max-and-the-junkmen-a-police-procedural-that-moves-at-paris-pace/|title=''Max and the Junkmen'' Review|author=|date=|work=chicagotribune.com|access-date=6 April 2017}}</ref>

==Production==
After directing two [[crime films]] with ''[[Classe tous risques]]'' (1960) and ''[[L'Arme à gauche]]'' (1965), director [[Claude Sautet]] was about to move on from the crime films when one of his producers suggested him to read the novel ''Max et les Ferrailleurs'' by {{ill|Claude Néron|fr}}. The novel had been optioned by the Swiss company Sonocam in April 1969, and led to Sautet signing on with the Sonocam to develop it into a film.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=99}} 

''Max et les Ferrailleurs'' was an [[international co-production]] between France and Italy with the Paris-based Lira Films, the [[Genève]]-based Sonocam and Fida Cinematografica in Rome.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=98}} The production changed as by March 1970, Sonocam delegated the production to Lira Films while standing in as an associate producer. Fida Cinematografica ultimately contributed 20$ of the 4 million [[franc]] budget.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=99}} 

Both [[Alain Delon]] and [[Yves Montand]] were suggested by producers to play the role of Max while [[Catherine Deneuve]] and [[Marlène Jobert]] were sought for the role of Lily. Jobert declined to make the film as she did not want to play a sex worker.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=99}} [[Romy Schneider]] ultimately took the role.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|pp=98-99}}

Filming began in August 18, 1970 and lasted two months. It was mostly shot in Paris-suburbs with [[Alfortville]] and [[Créteil]] standing in for [[Nanterre]].{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=100}}

==Release==
''Max et les Ferrailleurs'' was released in France on February 17, 1971.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=98}} It had 1,903,243 entries in France, becoming the 16th highest grossing film in France in the annual box office.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=105}}

It was released in Italy as ''Il commissario Pelissier'' on March 12, 1971.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=98}} It had its first release in the United States as [[El Paso]]'s Museum of Art and History and later in Los Angeles in 1998.{{sfn|Curti|Lafond|2026|p=105}}
