'''''The Dark Crystal''''' is a 1982 [[Dark fantasy|dark]] [[fantasy film]] directed by [[Jim Henson]] and [[Frank Oz]], produced by [[Gary Kurtz]] and Henson, with a screenplay by [[David Odell]] based on a story conceived by Henson. The film was produced and financed by [[ITC Entertainment]] and [[The Jim Henson Company]] and distributed by [[Universal Pictures]]. It features the voices of [[Stephen Garlick]], [[Lisa Maxwell (actress)|Lisa Maxwell]], [[Billie Whitelaw]], [[Percy Edwards]], and [[Barry Dennen]]. Set on a fictional planet, the film revolves around [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Jen|Jen]] and [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Kira|Kira]], two [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Gelflings|Gelflings]] on a quest to restore balance to the world of [[Fantasy world|Thra]] and overthrow the evil, ruling [[Skeksis]] by restoring a powerful broken Crystal.

The film was promoted as the first major [[Live action|live-action]] motion picture without any human actors, featuring characters realized through groundbreaking [[animatronics]] created by [[Jim Henson's Creature Shop]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |last=Harmetz |first=Aljean |date=August 23, 1982 |title=And Now, A Movie Without Any Humans at All |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1982/08/23/movies/and-now-a-movie-without-any-humans-at-all.html |access-date=July 3, 2025 |work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> Many creatures, such as the Gelflings, required as many as four puppeteers to achieve full movement and expression. In addition to directing, Henson and Oz also performed several characters alongside regular [[The Muppets|Muppets]] collaborators [[Kathryn Mullen]], [[Dave Goelz]], [[Steve Whitmire]], and [[Louise Gold]]. The primary [[concept artist]] was fantasy illustrator [[Brian Froud]], famous for his distinctive [[fairy]] and [[Dwarf (folklore)|dwarf]] designs; Froud later collaborated with Henson on his subsequent fantasy film ''[[Labyrinth (1986 film)|Labyrinth]]'' (1986). The [[film score]] was composed by [[Trevor Jones (composer)|Trevor Jones]].

''The Dark Crystal'' initially received mixed reviews from mainstream critics; while being criticized for its darker, more dramatic tone in contrast to Henson's previous works, it was praised for its narrative, production design, and characters. Over the years, it has been re-evaluated by critics and has garnered a [[cult film|cult following]].<ref name=Cult/>

An [[Emmy Award]]-winning [[prequel]] television series, ''[[The Dark Crystal: Age of Resistance]]'', premiered on [[Netflix]] in 2019 and ran for one season.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries are 400 to 700 words. -->
On the blighted [[planet]] Thra 1,000 years earlier, a powerful [[crystal]] cracked and two new races appeared: the cruel [[Skeksis]], who use the crystal's power to extend their lives, and gentle Mystics, the [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#The urRu/Mystics|urRu]],{{efn|The Mystics are called "urRu" once on-screen as they enter the crystal chamber.}} who dwell in a secluded valley. Among the Mystics is Jen, a young [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Gelflings|Gelfling]] adopted after the Skeksis slaughtered his clan. As the Great [[Conjunction (astronomy)|Conjunction]] of the world's three suns draws near, the dying Mystic Master instructs Jen to fulfill a prophecy to heal the crystal by first retrieving a missing shard from the oracle [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Aughra|Aughra]]. If Jen fails to complete his quest before the three suns meet, the Skeksis will rule forever. The Master then dies, and the Skeksis Emperor dies simultaneously. The Skeksis General successfully challenges the Chamberlain for succession in a "trial by stone" and banishes him from the castle. When the Skeksis learn of Jen's existence, they send their army of giant [[crab]]-like [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Garthim|Garthim]] to capture him, with the cunning Chamberlain following.

Jen meets Aughra and enters her [[orrery]]. Offered several shards, he chooses one that responds when he plays the Mystics' chord on his flute. Before Aughra can explain Jen's mission, the Garthim arrive and destroy the orrery, taking Aughra prisoner as Jen flees. Hearing the crystal's call, the Mystics leave their valley and journey to the castle. On his journey through a forest swamp, Jen meets [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Kira|Kira]], a female Gelfling. The two learn more about each other when they accidentally "dreamfast", sharing each other's memories. They stay for a night with the [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Podlings|Podlings]] who raised Kira, only for them and Kira's pet [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Fizzgig|Fizzgig]] to flee when the Garthim raid the village. They are nearly caught, but the Chamberlain orders the Garthim back.

Jen and Kira discover a ruined Gelfling city where a prophecy is inscribed:

{{blockquote|"When single shines the triple sun,<br>What was sundered and undone<br>Shall be whole, the two made one<br>By Gelfling hand or else by none."}}

Jen realizes that he must take the shard to the castle. The Chamberlain approaches and begs them to come to the castle with him. The Gelflings flee and reach the castle on [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#Landstriders|Landstriders]], intercepting the Garthim that raided Kira's village. They attack to free the Podlings but are cornered. Kira grabs Jen and Fizzgig and reveals wings, an attribute possessed only by female Gelfling, that she uses to glide into the castle's dry moat. They enter the castle through the catacombs while, above, the Skeksis Scientist uses the crystal's rays to extract [[Elixir of life|vital essence]] from Podlings. The Emperor drinks the essence and finds that it has only temporary restorative effects, unlike Gelfling essence which was more potent. The Chamberlain tries again to seize the Gelflings, and Jen stabs his hand with the shard; elsewhere the Mystic Chanter notices a wound on his hand. Enraged, the Chamberlain buries Jen in a cave-in and takes Kira as a gift to the Emperor. The Emperor reinstates him and orders Kira drained of essence. Aughra, imprisoned in the laboratory, tells Kira to call the captive animals for help. They break free and attack the Scientist, who deflects the draining prism before falling into the fiery crystal shaft; on a rocky plain, the Mystic Alchemist vanishes in flames. Aughra frees herself while Jen, awakened by Kira's call, climbs up the shaft to the laboratory.

The Gelflings make their way to a hall overlooking the crystal chamber, where the Skeksis gather for the conjunction ceremony. When the Skeksis spot them and order the Garthim to attack, Jen leaps onto the crystal but drops the shard. Kira glides down to the chamber, grabs the shard and throws it to Jen before the High Priest stabs her fatally. As the suns align Jen plunges the shard into the crystal, producing a force that throws him aside. The Garthim disintegrate and the drained Podlings regain their vitality while the dark stone covering the castle crumbles to reveal a crystalline structure. The Mystics arrive and use the crystal's light to draw the Skeksis to themselves, merging into angelic [[Characters and races of The Dark Crystal#urSkeks|urSkeks]].

The urSkek leader tells Jen that they sundered themselves and damaged the crystal a thousand years ago, upsetting the world's balance. They revive Kira in gratitude and ascend toward the suns, leaving the crystal to light the rejuvenated world.

==Production==
===Development===
{{rquote|right|The spiritual kernel of ''The Dark Crystal'' is heavily influenced by [[Seth Material|Seth]]. I've always felt that the idea of perfect beings split into a good mystic part and an evil materialistic part which are reunited after a long separation is Jim's response to the teachings of that book. Jim admitted that he didn't understand the book himself, and that everyone would understand it—or not understand it—in their own way. But he thought it opened up a whole different way of looking at reality, which I think was one of his goals in the making of ''The Dark Crystal''.|Screenwriter David Odell<ref name="odell2012"/>}}

Jim Henson's inspiration for the visual aspects of the film came around 1975–76,<ref name="fantasticfilms">[http://www.bookmice.net/darkchilde/dark/ff.html Fantastic Films] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130617110016/http://www.bookmice.net/darkchilde/dark/ff.html |date=June 17, 2013 }} #32, February, 1983</ref> after he saw an illustration by Leonard B. Lubin in a 1975 edition of [[Lewis Carroll]]'s poetry showing crocodiles living in a palace and wearing elaborate robes and jewelry.<ref name="odell2012">David Odell (2012), "Reflections on Making ''The Dark Crystal'' and Working with Jim Henson". In: Froud, B., Dysart, J., Sheikman, A. & John, L. ''The Dark Crystal: Creation Myths, Vol. II''. Archaia. {{ISBN|978-1-936393-80-0}}</ref><ref name="mcara2013">McAra, Catriona (2013) A Natural History of "The Dark Crystal": The Conceptual Design of Brian Froud. In: ''The Wider Worlds of Jim Henson''. McFarland, Jefferson, pp. 101-116.</ref><ref name="froud2003">Brian Froud (2003), "A Journey into ''The Dark Crystal''". In: Froud, B. & Llewellyn, J. J., ''The World of the Dark Crystal''. Pavilion Books. {{ISBN|1-86205-624-2}}</ref> The film's conceptual roots lay in Henson's short-lived ''[[The Land of Gorch]]'', which also took place in an alien world with no human characters.<ref name="mcara2013"/> According to co-director [[Frank Oz]], Henson's intention was to "get back to the darkness of the original [[Grimms' Fairy Tales]]", as he believed that it was unhealthy for children to never be afraid.<ref>Peter Hartlaub, [http://blog.sfgate.com/parenting/2007/06/28/qa-frank-oz-on-henson-dark-crystal-and-the-kwik-way/ Q&A: Frank Oz on Henson, “Dark Crystal” and the Kwik Way] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130510192454/http://blog.sfgate.com/parenting/2007/06/28/qa-frank-oz-on-henson-dark-crystal-and-the-kwik-way/ |date=May 10, 2013 }}, ''SFGate'', (Jun 28, 2007)</ref>

Henson formulated his ideas into a 25-page story he entitled "The Crystal", which he wrote whilst snowed in at an airport hotel.<ref name="odell2012"/> Henson's original concept was set in a world called Mithra, a wooded land with talking mountains, walking boulders and animal-plant hybrids. The original plot involved a malevolent race called the Reptus group, which took power in a coup against the peaceful Eunaze, led by Malcolm the Wise. The last survivor of the Eunaze was Malcolm's son Brian, who was adopted by the Bada, Mithra's mystical wizards.<ref name="mithra">Jim Henson, ''The Mithra Treatment'' [DVD special Feature]. ''The Dark Crystal: Collector's Edition'', Dir. Jim Henson & Frank Oz. 1982. Colombia Tristar Home Entertainment, 2003. DVD.</ref>

This draft contained elements in the final product, including the three races, the two funerals, the quest, a female secondary character, the Crystal, and the reunification of the two races during the Great Conjunction. "Mithra" was later abbreviated to "Thra", due to similarities the original name had with an ancient [[Mithra|Persian deity]].<ref name="odell2012"/> The character Kira was also at that point called Dee.<ref name="froud2003"/>

Most of the philosophical undertones of the film were inspired by [[Jane Roberts]]'s collection of writings ''[[Seth Material]]'', which Henson found very inspiration. Henson kept multiple copies of ''Seth Speaks'', and insisted that Froud and screenwriter [[David Odell]] read it prior to collaborating for the film. Odell later wrote that Aughra's line "He could be anywhere then", upon being told by Jen that his Master was dead, could not have been written without having first read Roberts' material.<ref name="odell2012"/>

The Bada were renamed "Ooo-urrrs", which Henson would pronounce "very slowly and with a deep resonant voice". Odell simplified the spelling to urRu, though they were ultimately named Mystics in the theatrical cut. The word "Skeksis" was initially meant to be the plural, with "Skesis" being singular, though the distinction was dropped early in the filming process. Originally, Henson wanted the Skeksis to speak their own [[constructed language]], with the dialogue [[Subtitle (captioning)|subtitled]] in English.<ref name="odell2012"/> Henson and Oz abstained from voicing their characters, believing their voices were too recognizable to audiences from their work on [[the Muppets]].<ref name=":0" />

Accounts differ as to who constructed the language, and on what it was based. [[Gary Kurtz]] stated that the Skeksis language was conceived by author [[Alan Garner]], who based it on [[Egyptian language|Ancient Egyptian]],<ref name="kurtz">Hutchinson, David. "[https://archive.org/stream/starlog_magazine-066/066#page/n17/mode/2up Producing the world of The Dark Crystal: A new direction for the man behind ‘Star Wars" and "Empire" Gary Kurtz]". ''Starlog: The Magazine of the Future''. 66 (January 1983):19-20.</ref> while Odell stated it was he who created it, and that it was formed from [[Indo-European languages|Indo-European]] [[Root word|root]]s. This idea was dropped after [[test screening]] audiences found the captions too distracting, but the original effect can be observed in selected scenes on the various DVD releases.<ref name="odell2012"/> The language of the Podlings was based on [[Serbo-Croatian]], with Kurtz noting that audience members fluent in [[Slavic languages]] could understand individual words, but not follow entire sentences.<ref name="kurtz"/>
[[File:Gordale Beck flowing down Gordale Scar - geograph.org.uk - 872118.jpg|thumb|[[Gordale Scar]], one of the locations used in filming]]
The film was shot at [[Elstree Studios (Shenley Road)|Elstree Studios]] from April to September 1981, with exterior scenes being shot in various locations: [[Scottish Highlands]], [[Gordale Scar]], [[North Yorkshire]], and [[Twycross]], [[Leicestershire]]. Once filming was completed, the film's release was delayed after [[Lew Grade]] sold [[ITC Entertainment]] to [[Robert Holmes à Court]], who was skeptical of the film's potential, due to the bad reactions at the preview and the need to re-voice the film's soundtrack. The film was afforded minimal advertisement and release until Henson bought the film rights from Holmes à Court and funded its release with his own money.<ref name="odell2012"/>

===Design===
[[Brian Froud]] was chosen as concept artist after Henson saw one of his paintings in the book ''Once upon a time''.<ref name="froud2003"/> The characters in the film are elaborate puppets, and none are based on humans or any other specific [[Earth]] creature. Before its release, ''The Dark Crystal'' was billed as the first live-action film without any humans on screen, and "a showcase for cutting-edge animatronics".{{sfn|Wright|2005}}

The hands and facial features of the ground-breaking animatronic puppets in the film were controlled with relatively primitive rods and cables, although radio control later took over many of the subtler movements.{{sfn|Rickitt|2000|p=225}} Human performers inside the puppets supplied basic movement for the larger creatures, which in some cases was dangerous or exhausting.{{sfn|Bacon|1997|p=24}} [[Switzerland|Swiss]] mime Jean-Pierre Amiel led a team of dancers, acrobats and others in performing the Mystics,<ref name="world">Making-of. ''The World of the Dark Crystal''. Dir. Jim Henson & Frank Oz. 1982. Colombia Tristar Home Video, 1999. DVD.</ref> with Amiel himself performing the Weaver Mystic. Both Jen's dying Mystic master and the Ritual Guardian were performed by [[Brian Meehl|Brian Muehl]], who was a recurring puppeteer on ''[[Sesame Street]]'' at the time, and many of the other Mystic performers would go on to work on the ''[[Star Wars]]'' film ''[[Return of the Jedi]]'', as would two of the Skeksis' puppeteers, [[Mike Quinn (puppeteer)|Mike Quinn]] (Slave Master) and [[Tim Rose (actor)|Tim Rose]] (Treasurer).

When conceptualizing the Skeksis, Henson had in mind the [[seven deadly sins]], though because there were ten Skeksis, some sins had to be invented or used twice.<ref name="world"/> Froud originally designed them to resemble deep sea fish,<ref name="light">Making-of. ''Reflections of the Dark Crystal: Light on the Path of Creation''. Dir. Michael Gillis. Sony Pictures Home Entertainment, 2007. DVD.</ref> but later designed them as "part reptile, part predatory bird, part dragon", with an emphasis on giving them a "penetrating stare."<ref name="world"/> Each Skeksis was conceived as having a different "job" or function, thus each puppet was draped in multicolored robes meant to reflect their personalities and thought processes.<ref name="light"/>

Each Skeksis suit required a main performer (from Jim Henson's main team of puppeteers, most of whom had previously worked on ''[[The Muppet Show]]''), whose arm would be extended over his or her head in order to operate the creature's facial movements, while the other arm operated its left hand. Another performer would operate the Skeksis' right arm. A team of four technicians operated the Skeksis' hand and face animatronics. The Skeksis performers compensated for their lack of vision by having a monitor tied to their chests.<ref name="shard">Making-of. ''Reflections of the Dark Crystal: Shard of Illusion''. Dir. Michael Gillis. Sony Pictures Home Entertainment, 2007. DVD.</ref> The Chamberlain Skeksis, in particular, was built with 21 electronic components.

One Skeksis puppet, identified as "SkekZok", is displayed at the [[Museum of the Moving Image]].

In designing the Mystics, Froud portrayed them as being more connected to the natural world than their Skeksis counterparts. Henson intended to convey the idea that they were purged of all materialistic urges, yet were incapable of acting in the real world. Froud also incorporated geometric symbolism throughout the film in order to hint at the implied unity of the two races.<ref name="light"/> The Mystics were the hardest creatures to perform, as the actors had to walk on their haunches with their right arm extended forward and carrying the puppet's heavy headpiece. Henson stated he could hold a position in a Mystic costume for only 5–10 seconds.<ref name="world"/>
[[File:Gelfling costumes British Library 2024.jpg|thumb|right|Gelfling costumes displayed at the [[British Library]] (2024)]]
The Gelflings were designed and sculpted by [[Wendy Froud|Wendy Midener]]. They were difficult to perform, as they were meant to be the most human creatures in the film, and thus their movements, particularly their gait, had to be as realistic as possible. During scenes when the Gelflings' legs were off-camera, the performers walked on their knees in order to make the character's movements more lifelike.<ref name="shard"/> According to Odell, the character Jen was Henson's way of projecting himself into the film.<ref name="light"/> Jen was originally meant to be blue, in homage to the [[Hinduism|Hindu]] deity [[Rama]], but this idea was scrapped early on.<ref name="mcara2013"/>

Aughra was originally envisioned as a "busy, curious little creature" called Habeetabat, though the name was rejected by Froud, who found the name too similar to Habitat, a retailer he despised. The character was re-envisioned as a seer or prophetess, and renamed Aughra. In selecting a voice actor for Aughra, Henson was inspired by [[Zero Mostel]]'s performance as a "kind of insane bird trying to overcome Tourettes syndrome" on ''[[Watership Down (film)|Watership Down]]''. Although the character was originally voiced by Oz, Henson wanted a female voice, and subsequently selected [[Billie Whitelaw]].<ref name="odell2012"/>

The character Fizzgig was invented by Oz, who wanted a character who served the same function as the Muppet poodle Foo-Foo, feeling that, like [[Miss Piggy]], the character Kira needed an outlet for her caring, nurturing side.<ref name="odell2012"/> The character's design was meant to convey the idea of a "boyfriend-repellant", to contrast the popular idea that it is easier to form a bond with a member of the opposite sex with the assistance of a cute dog.<ref name="light"/>

The Podlings were envisioned as people in complete harmony with their natural surroundings, thus Froud based their design on that of potatoes.<ref name="world"/> Their village was modeled on the Henson family home.<ref name="light"/>

In designing the Garthim, Froud took inspiration from the discarded carapaces of his and Henson's lobster dinners.<ref name="froud2003"/><ref name="light"/> The Garthim were first designed three years into the making of the film, and were made largely of fiberglass. Each costume weighed around {{convert|70|lb|round=5}}, thus Garthim performers still in costume had to frequently be suspended on racks in order to recuperate.<ref name="world"/>

''The Dark Crystal'' was the last film in which cinematographer [[Oswald Morris]] involved himself in before retiring. He shot all the footage with a "light flex", a unit placed in front of the camera which gave a faint color tint to each scene in order to give the film a more fairy-tale atmosphere similar to Froud's original paintings.<ref name="world"/>

===Music===
The film's soundtrack was composed by [[Trevor Jones (composer)|Trevor Jones]], who became involved before shooting had started.<ref name="Hoover2010">{{cite book|last1=Hoover|first1=Tom|title=Soundtrack Nation: Interviews with Today's Top Professionals in Film, Videogame, and Television Scoring, 1st Ed.|date=2010|publisher=Cengage Learning|isbn=9781435457621|language=en}}</ref> Jones initially wanted to compose a score which reflected the settings' oddness by using acoustical instruments, electronics and building structures. This was scrapped in favor of an orchestral score performed by the [[London Symphony Orchestra]] once Gary Kurtz became involved, as it was felt that an unusual score would alienate audiences. The main theme of the film is a composite of the Skeksis' and Mystics' themes.<ref>[[#CITEREFCinemascore|Cinemascore 2011]].</ref> Jones wrote the baby Landstrider theme in honor of his newly born daughter.<ref>[http://www.oscars.org/video/watch/ev_dark_crystal.html "The Dark Crystal" at Oscars Outdoors] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120906182921/http://www.oscars.org/video/watch/ev_dark_crystal.html |date=September 6, 2012 }}, Oscars.org (September 4, 2012)</ref>

Early in development, [[Vangelis]] composed music for the film.<ref>{{cite web |last = Higgins |first = Chris |date = 5 December 2013 |title = Watch the Restored Original Cut of The Dark Crystal |url = https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/54077/watch-restored-original-cut-dark-crystal |website = [[Mental Floss]] |location = [[New York City]], [[New York (state)|New York]], [[United States|United States of America]] |publisher =  |url-status = dead |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20131211011805/http://mentalfloss.com/article/54077/watch-restored-original-cut-dark-crystal |archive-date = 11 December 2013 |access-date = 21 May 2026}}</ref> A workprint used to determine which background music best suited the film was later discovered, containing two versions of the film, one scored by Jones and one by Vangelis, although the tape only had enough storage to preserve fifteen minutes of the version with Vangelis' score.<ref>{{cite web |author = parrisj |date = 19 December 2014 |title = The Dark Crystal Workprint |url = https://archive.org/details/TDCBGMTestWorkprint |website = [[Internet Archive]] |location = [[San Francisco]], [[California]], [[United States|United States of America]] |publisher =  |url-status = |archive-url =  |archive-date = |access-date = 21 May 2026}}</ref> 

==Release and reception==

===Box office===
''The Dark Crystal'' was released in 858 theaters in North America on December 17, 1982, and finished third for the weekend with a gross of $4,657,335, behind ''[[Tootsie]]'' and ''[[The Toy (1982 film)|The Toy]]'', performing better than some people expected.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=1|date=December 21, 1982|title='Tootsie,' 'Toy' And 'Dark Crystal' Win Big At National Box-Office|last=Ginsberg|first=Steven}}</ref> In its initial weekends, it had a limited appeal with some audiences for various reasons, including parental concerns about its dark nature when contrasted with Henson's family-friendly Muppet franchise.{{sfn|Scheib|2010}} In its third weekend, it moved up to second place nationally with a gross of $5,405,071 from 1,052 screens.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=1|date=January 4, 1983|title=Box-Office Off To Running Start}}</ref> It made $40,577,001 in its box office run, managing to turn a profit. The film became the 16th highest-grossing film of 1982 within North America.<ref>{{Cite web |title=1982 Worldwide Box Office |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/world/1982/?sort=domesticGrossToDate&ref_=bo_ydw__resort#table |access-date=2025-03-03 |website=Box Office Mojo}}</ref> To date, it remains as one of the [[List of highest-grossing puppet films|highest-grossing puppet films]] of all time, particularly for its domestic gross.<ref>{{Cite web |date=30 March 2012 |title=Highest-grossing puppet movie |url=https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/397770-highest-grossing-puppet-movie |access-date=March 3, 2025 |website=Guinness World Records}}</ref>

It made £2.4 million in the UK.<ref name="org">{{cite web|url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-back-to-the-future-the-fall-and-rise-of-the-british-film-industry-in-the-1980s.pdf|page=21|title=Back to the Future: The Fall and Rise of the British Film Industry in the 1980s - An Information Briefing|website=British Film Institute|date=2005|access-date=November 29, 2020|archive-date=October 27, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201027172649/https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-back-to-the-future-the-fall-and-rise-of-the-british-film-industry-in-the-1980s.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Dark Crystal'' was re-released by [[Fathom Entertainment]] in select theaters on October 12 and 13, 2025.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.fathomentertainment.com/releases/the-dark-crystal-2025/|title=The Dark Crystal|publisher=Fathom Entertainment|access-date=October 10, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251003214010/https://www.fathomentertainment.com/releases/the-dark-crystal-2025/|archive-date=October 3, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical reception===
The film received a mixed response upon its original release, but has earned a more positive reception in later years, becoming a favorite with fans of Henson and fantasy.{{sfn|Von Gunden|1989|pages=30–31}} [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' negatively reviewed the film, describing it as a "watered down [[J. R. R. Tolkien]]... without charm as well as interest."{{sfn|Canby|1982}} [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] gave it a more positive assessment in the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'': "Unlike many screen fantasies, ''The Dark Crystal'' casts its spell from its very first frames and proceeds so briskly that it's over before you realize it. You're left with the feeling that you have just awakened from a dream."{{sfn|Thomas|1982}} [[Richard Corliss]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine wrote: "The invention is impressive, but there is little indication of the Henson-Oz trademark: a sense of giddy fun. Audiences nourished on the sophisticated child's play of the ''"[[Sesame Street]]"'' Muppets and the music-hall camaraderie of ''The Muppet Show'' may not be ready to relinquish pleasure for awe as they enter ''The Dark Crystal''{{'}}s palatial cavern."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Corliss |first=Richard |url=https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,953673,00.html |title=Cinema: Magical, Mystical Muppet Tour |magazine=Time |page=82 |date=January 3, 1983 |access-date=December 29, 2020 |archive-date=August 20, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200820165513/http://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,953673,00.html |url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised the film as "a dazzling technological and artistic achievement ...that could teach a lesson in morality to youngsters at the same time it is entertaining their parents."<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://variety.com/1982/film/reviews/the-dark-crystal-1200425408/ |title=Film Reviews: The Dark Crystal |magazine=Variety |date=December 15, 1982 |access-date=December 29, 2020 |archive-date=August 20, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200820184817/https://variety.com/1982/film/reviews/the-dark-crystal-1200425408/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Gary Arnold of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' wrote the main characters were "the softest and potentially weakest figures" in the film, but nevertheless, "''The Dark Crystal'' leaves no doubt that Jim Henson and his colleagues have reached a point where they can create and sustain a powerfully enchanting form of cinematic fantasy."<ref>{{cite news |last=Arnold |first=Gary |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1982/12/21/the-finest-in-fantasy/071f3340-a43e-4c92-88e2-120d62d070fd/ |title=The Finest in Fantasy |newspaper=The Washington Post |page=C1 |date=December 21, 1982 |access-date=November 3, 2022 |archive-date=November 3, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221103161704/https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1982/12/21/the-finest-in-fantasy/071f3340-a43e-4c92-88e2-120d62d070fd/ |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] of ''[[The Chicago Tribune]]'' awarded the film {{frac|2|1|2}} out of four stars in which he felt "...the resultant absence of dramatic tension cripples 'Crystal,' which doesn't have much going for it save for weird characters, who look like they just walked in from the bar scene in ''[[Star Wars]]''. In fact, a lot of this movie looks like it was ripped off from ''Star Wars''."<ref>{{cite news |last=Siskel |first=Gene |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/66302492/clipped-from-the-chicago-tribune/ |title=Lack of dramatic tension dooms Jim Henson's 'Dark Crystal' |date=December 20, 1982 |work=The Chicago Tribune |at=Section 5, p. 4 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211223022021/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/66302492/clipped-from-the-chicago-tribune/ |archive-date=December 23, 2021 |access-date=December 29, 2020 |via=Newspapers.com}} {{Open access}}</ref> [[Colin Greenland]], reviewing for ''[[Imagine (game magazine)|Imagine]]'' magazine, stated that "''The Dark Crystal'' is a technical masterpiece with splendid special effects work by a team two dozen strong. It may be that they did well to keep the story simple and then lavish a wealth of detail on it, rather than go for a more complicated fantasy and fail."<ref name="Imagine4">{{cite magazine |last=Greenland |first=Colin |author-link=Colin Greenland |title=Film Review |magazine=[[Imagine (AD&D magazine)|Imagine]] |issue=4 |page=37 |date=July 1983}}</ref>

John Nubbin reviewed ''The Dark Crystal'' for ''[[Different Worlds]]'' magazine and stated that "it very well may be that he who laughs last actually does laugh best. ''The Dark Crystal'' has all the earmarks of a winner, at exactly the time when we need one."<ref name="dw26">{{cite journal | last = Nubbin |first = John | title = Cinema News & Reviews | journal = [[Different Worlds]] | issue = 26 | pages =45 |date=January 1983}}</ref>

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of {{RT data|score}} based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The website's critical consensus reads: "''The Dark Crystal''{{'}}s narrative never quite lives up to the movie's visual splendor, but it remains an admirably inventive and uniquely intense entry in the Jim Henson canon."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dark_crystal/ |title=The Dark Crystal (1982) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=July 30, 2022 |archive-date=May 13, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190513224312/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/dark_crystal/ |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] it has a weighted average score of 66 out of 100 based on reviews from 13 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Dark Crystal |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-dark-crystal |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=February 5, 2021 |archive-date=October 23, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201023050200/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-dark-crystal |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Novelization==
A [[tie-in]] [[novelization]] of the film was written by [[A. C. H. Smith]]. Henson took a keen interest in the novelization, as he considered it a legitimate part of the film's world rather than just an advertisement. He originally asked [[Alan Garner]] to write it, but Garner declined on account of prior engagements. Henson and Smith met several times over meals to discuss the progress of the manuscript. According to Smith, their only major disagreement had arisen over his dislike of the Podlings, which he considered "boring". He included a scene in which a Garthim carrying a sackful of Podlings fell down a cliff and crushed them. Henson considered this scene to be an element of "gratuitous cruelty" that did not fit well into the scope of the story. In order to assist Smith in his visualizing the world of ''The Dark Crystal'', Henson invited him to visit Elstree Studios during filming.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dsoundz.co.uk/2012/08/07/labyrinth-the-25th-anniversary-podcast/#.UhTs-D91jIU|title=Jim Henson's Labyrinth – The 25th Anniversary Podcast with ACH Smith and Sam Downie – Sam Downie|website=Dsoundz.co.uk|access-date=October 30, 2017|archive-date=November 7, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171107021211/http://dsoundz.co.uk/2012/08/07/labyrinth-the-25th-anniversary-podcast/#.UhTs-D91jIU|url-status=dead}}</ref> In June 2014, [[Archaia Entertainment]] reprinted the novelization, with included extras such as some of Brian Froud's illustrations and Jim Henson's notes.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tfaw.com/Profile/Jim-Hensons-Dark-Crystal-HC-Novel___451278|title=Jim Hensons Dark Crystal HC Novel @TFAW.com|website=Things From Another World|access-date=October 30, 2017|archive-date=December 7, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151207235632/http://www.tfaw.com/Profile/Jim-Hensons-Dark-Crystal-HC-Novel___451278|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Cancelled sequel==
During the development phase of ''The Dark Crystal'', director Jim Henson and writer David Odell discussed ideas for a possible sequel. Almost 25 years later, Odell and his wife Annette Duffy pieced together what Odell could recall from these discussions to draft a script for ''The Power of the Dark Crystal''.{{sfn|Carroll|2006}} [[Genndy Tartakovsky]] was initially hired in January 2006 to direct and produce the film through [[The Orphanage (company)|The Orphanage]] animation studios in California.<ref>[[#CITEREFHensonCompany2006|The Jim Henson Company 2006]].</ref>

However, faced with considerable delays, the Jim Henson Company announced a number of significant changes in a May 2010 press release: It was going to partner with Australia-based Omnilab Media to produce the sequel, screenwriter [[Craig Pearce]] had reworked Odell and Duffy's script, and directing team [[The Spierig Brothers|Michael and Peter Spierig]] were replacing Tartakovsky. In addition, the film would be released in stereoscopic 3D.<ref>[[#CITEREFHensonCompany2010|The Jim Henson Company 2010]].</ref>

During a panel held at the [[Museum of the Moving Image (New York City)|Museum of the Moving Image]] on September 18, 2011, to commemorate the legacy of Jim Henson, his daughter Cheryl revealed that the project was yet again on hiatus.{{sfn|Hill|2011}} By February 2012 Omnilab Media and the Spierig brothers had parted ways with the Henson Company due to budgetary concerns; production on the film has been suspended indefinitely.{{sfn|Swift|2012}} In May 2014, [[Lisa Henson]] confirmed that the film was still in development, but it was not yet in pre-production.<ref name="vulture.com">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.vulture.com/2014/05/henson-properties-status-report-dark-crystal-fraggle-rock.html?_r=true|author=Watkins, Gwynne|title=Checking in on ''The Dark Crystal'', ''Labyrinth'', and Other Jim Henson Company Franchises|magazine=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|publisher=New York Media LLC|access-date=May 16, 2014|date=May 16, 2014|archive-date=May 18, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140518004921/http://www.vulture.com/2014/05/henson-properties-status-report-dark-crystal-fraggle-rock.html?_r=true|url-status=live}}</ref>

Ultimately, plans for a feature film were scrapped, and the unproduced screenplay was adapted into a 12-issue comic book series ''[[The Power of the Dark Crystal]]'' from [[Archaia Entertainment|Archaia Comics]] and [[Boom! Studios|BOOM! Studios]], released in 2017.<ref>{{cite news|title='Dark Crystal' Sequel Finally Coming to Life Thanks to Comic Book Series|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/dark-crystal-sequel-finally-coming-life-thanks-comic-book-series-949591|last=McMillan|first=Graeme|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=November 21, 2016|access-date=November 21, 2016|archive-date=November 22, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161122071954/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/dark-crystal-sequel-finally-coming-life-thanks-comic-book-series-949591|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Spin-offs==
===Prequel comics===
''[[Legends of the Dark Crystal]]'', an [[original English-language manga]] written by [[Barbara Kesel]] with art by Heidi Arnhold, Jessica Feinberg, and Max Kim, was published by [[Tokyopop]]. Its story is set hundreds of years before the events of ''The Dark Crystal'', after the Great Conjunction which saw the splitting of the urSkeks into the Mystics and the Skeksis, but before the extermination of the Gelflings. The first volume of the series came out November 2007, followed sometime later by the second in August 2010. A third installment had been originally planned but was canceled and subsequently merged into the second volume.{{sfn|Arnhold|2010}}

Another comic book prequel, ''The Dark Crystal: Creation Myths'', was published by [[Archaia Entertainment]] as a series of three graphic novels.<ref>[[#CITEREFArchaia|Archaia Entertainment 2011]].</ref> The Henson Company and Archaia began collaborating on this project in late 2009.{{sfn|Warmoth|2010}} A brief preview was made available on [[Free Comic Book Day]] in May 2011,<ref>[[#CITEREFDiamond|Diamond Comic Distributors 2010]].</ref> and the first installment was released January 2012, shortly thereafter spending two weeks on [[The New York Times Best Seller list|''The New York Times'' Best Seller list]] of hardcover graphic books. In February 2013, the second installment was officially released. The third and final part was released in October 2015.

===Prequel novels===
On July 1, 2013, an announcement was made by The Jim Henson Company, in association with [[Grosset and Dunlap]] (a publishing division of Penguin Group USA) that they would be hosting a Dark Crystal Author Quest contest to write a new ''Dark Crystal'' novel, as a prequel to the original film. It would be set during a Gelfling Gathering. The winning author was J.M. (Joseph) Lee of Minneapolis, Minnesota, whose story, "The Ring of Dreams," was selected from almost 500 contest submissions.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childrens/childrens-book-news/article/63236-penguin-and-jim-henson-company-team-up-for-dark-crystal-project.html|author=Lodge, Sally|title=Penguin and ''Jim Henson Company'' Team Up for Dark Crystal Project|work=[[Publishers Weekly]]|publisher=publishersweekly.com|access-date=July 10, 2014|date=July 8, 2014|archive-date=July 16, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140716173737/http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childrens/childrens-book-news/article/63236-penguin-and-jim-henson-company-team-up-for-dark-crystal-project.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

The novel series consists of four books: ''Shadows of the Dark Crystal'', released on June 28, 2016; ''Song of the Dark Crystal'', released July 18, 2017; ''Tides of the Dark Crystal'', released December 24, 2018; and ''Flames of the Dark Crystal'', released on August 27, 2019. Together, the novels serve to establish the setting of the Netflix series ''[[The Dark Crystal: Age of Resistance]]'', focusing on adventures of some of the series' side characters.

==In other media==
* A book entitled ''[[The World of the Dark Crystal|The World of The Dark Crystal]]'', written and illustrated by [[Brian Froud]], was released at the same time as the film. The book, written as the annotated translation of the ''Book of Aughra'' by fictional Oxford professor "J.J. Llewellyn", expands greatly on the world of "Thra", detailing its conditions and history, as well as providing some additional story background.
* An illustrated children's storybook version, ''The Tale of the Dark Crystal'', written by Donna Bass and illustrated by Bruce McNally.
* A board game called ''[[List of Milton Bradley Company products|The Dark Crystal Game]]'' was also released in 1982 by [[Milton Bradley Company|Milton Bradley]].
* A book-and-cassette adaptation was released in 1983 by [[Walt Disney Records|Disneyland Records]] as part of its [[List of Disney novelizations#Disney Read-Along|''Read-Along Adventures'' series]].
* In 1983, a graphic adventure  [[The Dark Crystal (video game)|based on the film]] was released for the [[Apple II]] and [[Atari 8-bit computers]].
* [[Marvel Comics]] published a comic book adaptation of the film by writer [[David Anthony Kraft]] and artists [[Bret Blevins]], [[Vince Colletta]], Rick Bryant, and [[Richard Howell (comics)|Richard Howell]] in ''[[Marvel Comics Super Special|Marvel Super Special]]'' #24.<ref>{{Gcdb issue|id= 36957|title= ''Marvel Super Special'' #24}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal|last= Friedt|first= Stephan|title= Marvel at the Movies: The House of Ideas' Hollywood Adaptations of the 1970s and 1980s|journal= [[Back Issue!]]|issue= 89|pages= 66–67|publisher= [[TwoMorrows Publishing]]|date= July 2016|location= Raleigh, North Carolina}}</ref>
* ''[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]]'' commissioned six of the film's costume designers to fashion clothes based on the characters of the film.{{sfn|Falk|2012|p=141}}
* Music duo [[The Crystal Method]] used samples from the film in the song "Trip Like I Do", released on their 1997 album ''[[Vegas (The Crystal Method album)|Vegas]]''.
* In February 2011, Sandstorm Productions – a firm that partnered with various design studios to facilitate the development and distribution of board games and [[collectible card game]]s – revealed that it had acquired the license to produce games based on various Henson properties, including ''The Dark Crystal''.<ref>[[#CITEREFICv2Feb2011|ICv2 February 1, 2011]].</ref> Before any definitive plans were made, however, Sandstorm went out of business in June 2012.
* Archaia announced plans for a [[role-playing game]] based on ''The Dark Crystal'' at the August 2011 [[Gen Con]] gaming convention, intending to publish it later the following year. Like its [[Origins Award]]-winning ''[[Mouse Guard]]'' game, ''The Dark Crystal'' was to be designed by [[Luke Crane (game designer)|Luke Crane]] and use mechanics similar to that of ''[[The Burning Wheel]]'',<ref>[[#CITEREFICv2Aug2011|ICv2 August 24, 2011]].</ref><ref>[[#CITEREFRichardson|Richardson 2011]].</ref> but it was not released.
* In August 2013, [[Black Phoenix Alchemy Lab]] - a company that produces body and household blends with a dark, romantic Gothic tone - debuted the first of their licensed ''The Dark Crystal'' perfumes. The debut included four Skeksis blends: skekUng the Garthim-Master, skekNa the Slave-Master, skekTek the Scientist and skekZok the Ritual-Master.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.facebook.com/darkcrystal|title=Security Check Required|website=Facebook.com|access-date=October 30, 2017|archive-date=October 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181008004115/https://www.facebook.com/darkcrystal|url-status=live}}</ref>
* In the ''[[Jim Henson's Creature Shop Challenge]]'' episode "Return of the Skeksis", the competing creature designers had to work in teams of three to build a Skeksis that has been banished to different parts of Thra and has been called back to the Skeksis Castle.
*The song "Return to Oz" by the band [[Scissor Sisters]] on the album ''Scissor Sisters'' (2004) features a reference to the film's antagonists the [[Skeksis]]: ''The Skeksis at the rave meant to hide deep inside their sunken faces and their wild, rolling eyes, But their callous words reveal that they can no longer feel.''
* The song "Skeksis" on the album ''[[Alien (Strapping Young Lad album)|Alien]]'' by Canadian band [[Strapping Young Lad]] is named after the film's antagonists; the song itself contains an interpolation of the film's theme melody.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.teamrock.com/features/2015-10-13/the-12-best-devin-townsend-songs-by-devin-townsend|title=The 12 best Devin Townsend songs, by Devin Townsend|date=October 13, 2015|website=Teamrock.com|access-date=October 30, 2017|archive-date=January 25, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160125043645/http://www.teamrock.com/features/2015-10-13/the-12-best-devin-townsend-songs-by-devin-townsend|url-status=live}}</ref> Singer-songwriter [[Devin Townsend]] would later base [[Ziltoid the Omniscient]] on the characters from the film.
* ''Dark Crystal Tales'' by Cory Godbey, a children's book of short stories, was released in August 2017.
* A book entitled ''The Dark Crystal: Age of Resistance: Inside the Epic Return to Thra'' was released in November 2019, two months after the Netflix series premiered. It details the making of the series and features concept art, interviews, set photography, and more.
* In October 2020, a guide to the characters and creatures from the Dark Crystal universe called ''The Dark Crystal Bestiary: The Definitive Guide to the Creatures of Thra'' was released.
* In January 2021, the River Horse company has announced that it is developing a role-playing game set in the world of both the original film and the Netflix prequel series called ''The Dark Crystal Adventure Game'' that was released in November 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://411mania.com/games/dark-crystal-rpg-adventure-in-the-works-from-river-horse/ |title=Dark Crystal RPG Adventure In the Works From River Horse |website=411MANIA |access-date=February 5, 2021 |archive-date=January 27, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210127093509/https://411mania.com/games/dark-crystal-rpg-adventure-in-the-works-from-river-horse/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dark-crystal-ttrpg-announcement-river-horse/ |title=The Dark Crystal is Getting a Tabletop RPG |website=Comicbook.com |date=January 4, 2021 |access-date=January 11, 2021 |archive-date=January 5, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210105083421/https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dark-crystal-ttrpg-announcement-river-horse/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=January 4, 2021 |author=Gavin Sheehan |title=River Horse Will Be Making A Tabletop RPG For The Dark Crystal |url=https://bleedingcool.com/games/river-horse-will-be-making-a-tabletop-rpg-for-the-dark-crystal/ |website=bleedingcool.com |access-date=January 11, 2021 |archive-date=January 5, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210105160914/https://bleedingcool.com/games/river-horse-will-be-making-a-tabletop-rpg-for-the-dark-crystal/ |url-status=live }}</ref>
* In March 2021, it was announced that the [[Royal Opera House]] will adapt the film into a [[ballet]] entitled ''The Dark Crystal: Odyssey''. It was directed and choreographed by [[Wayne McGregor]] with music by Joel Cadbury and is described as a "coming-of-age story" for family audiences.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.roh.org.uk/news/the-royal-opera-house-reveals-highlights-of-its-first-full-season-since-2019 |title=The Royal Opera House reveals highlights of its first full Season since 2019 |access-date=April 2, 2021 |archive-date=April 1, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210401194121/https://www.roh.org.uk/news/the-royal-opera-house-reveals-highlights-of-its-first-full-season-since-2019 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://io9.gizmodo.com/the-dark-crystal-will-return-as-a-ballet-1846539646 |title=The Dark Crystal Will Return...as a Ballet |date=March 24, 2021 |access-date=April 2, 2021 |archive-date=March 31, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210331214021/https://io9.gizmodo.com/the-dark-crystal-will-return-as-a-ballet-1846539646 |url-status=live }}</ref> It ran from May 13 to June 4, 2023, under the name ''UniVerse: A Dark Crystal Odyssey''.
* In the video game ''[[Lost Records: Bloom & Rage|Lost Records: Bloom and Rage]]'', a tape of the movie can be found in the character Swann’s room.
