'''''Bram Stoker's Dracula''''' is a 1992 American [[Gothic film|Gothic]] [[horror film]] co-produced and directed by [[Francis Ford Coppola]] and written by [[James V. Hart]], based on the eponymous 1897 novel ''[[Dracula]]'' by [[Bram Stoker]].<ref name="Los Angeles Times">{{cite news|title=A look inside Hollywood and the movies. : REALLY SCARY KIDS : Before the Crypt Opens on Dracula, a Little Surgery|work=Los Angeles Times|date=June 7, 1992|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-06-07-ca-284-story.html|access-date=November 22, 2010|first=David J.|last=Fox|url-status=live|archive-url=http://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/20101122010611/http://articles.latimes.com/1992-06-07/entertainment/ca-284_1_count-dracula|archive-date=November 22, 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=COMMENTARY : A Shtick Through the Heart : Francis Ford Coppola's sympathetic Count Dracula is a radical departure from previous versions. When Dracula wreaks havoc in [[London]], Professor [[Abraham Van Helsing]] (Hopkins), an expert in vampirism, is summoned to bring an end to his reign of terror.

[[Principal photography]] began at [[Sony Pictures Studios]] and [[Universal Studios Lot]] on October 14, 1991, and concluded on January 31, 1992, in addition to reshoots at [[Saint Sophia Cathedral, Los Angeles|Saint Sophia Greek Orthodox Cathedral]] in [[Los Angeles]]. The closing credits theme "[[Love Song for a Vampire]]" was written and performed by [[Annie Lennox]].

== Plot ==
In 1462, [[Vlad Dracula]] returns from a victory in [[Night Attack at Târgoviște|his campaign]] against the [[Ottoman Empire]] to find his beloved wife Elisabeta has committed [[suicide]] after his enemies falsely reported his death. A [[Romanian Orthodox Church|Romanian Orthodox]] priest tells him that his wife's soul is damned to [[Hell]] for her suicide. Enraged, Vlad desecrates the chapel and renounces [[Christian God|God]], declaring he will rise from the grave to avenge Elisabeta with all the powers of darkness. He then stabs his sword into the chapel's stone cross and drinks the blood that pours from it, becoming a [[vampire]].

In 1897, [[solicitor]] [[Jonathan Harker]] takes the elderly [[Transylvania]]n [[Count Dracula]] as a client from his colleague [[Renfield|R. M. Renfield]], who has gone insane and is now an inmate in Dr. [[John Seward|Jack Seward]]'s asylum. Jonathan travels to Dracula's castle in Transylvania to arrange Dracula's real estate acquisitions in [[London]]. There, he meets Dracula, who finds a picture of his fiancée [[Mina Harker|Mina Murray]] and believes she is Elisabeta reincarnated. Dracula leaves Jonathan to be fed upon by [[Brides of Dracula|his brides]], while he sails to England with Transylvanian soil, taking up residence at Carfax Abbey, which he recently purchased.

In London, Dracula, in [[werewolf]] form, hypnotically seduces and bites Mina's best friend [[Lucy Westenra]], with whom Mina is staying while Jonathan is in Transylvania. Lucy's deteriorating health and behavioral changes prompt former suitors [[Quincey Morris]] and Dr. Seward, along with her fiancé [[Arthur Holmwood]] to summon Dr. [[Abraham Van Helsing]], Seward's mentor, who recognizes Lucy as being the victim of a vampire. Dracula, appearing young and handsome during daylight, meets and charms Mina. Mina develops feelings for Dracula, accompanying him on several outings. When Mina receives word from Jonathan—who has escaped the castle and recovered at a convent—she travels to Romania to marry him. A heartbroken Dracula transforms Lucy into a vampire. Van Helsing, Holmwood, Seward, and Morris kill the undead Lucy the following night.

After he and Mina return to London, Jonathan and Van Helsing lead the others to Carfax Abbey, where they destroy the Count's boxes of soil. Dracula enters the asylum and kills Renfield for warning Mina of his presence. He visits Mina, who is staying in Seward's quarters, and confesses that he murdered Lucy and has been terrorizing Mina's friends. Though furious at first, Mina admits that she still loves him and remembers Elisabeta's previous life; at her insistence, Dracula begins transforming her into a vampire. The hunters burst into the bedroom, and Dracula, in a bat humanoid form, claims Mina as his bride before escaping as a swarm of rats. As Mina changes, Van Helsing hypnotizes her and learns via her connection with Dracula that he is sailing home in his last remaining box. The hunters depart for [[Varna, Bulgaria|Varna]] to intercept him, but Dracula reads Mina's mind and evades them. Van Helsing and Mina travel to the [[Borgo Pass]] and the castle, while the other hunters try to stop the Romani from transporting Dracula.

At night, Van Helsing and Mina are approached by Dracula's brides. Mina succumbs to their chanting and attempts to seduce Van Helsing. Before Mina can feed on his blood, Van Helsing places a [[communion wafer]] on her forehead, leaving a mark that slows her transformation. He surrounds them with a ring of fire to protect them from the brides, then kills the brides the following morning. Dracula's carriage arrives at the castle, pursued by the hunters. A fight between the hunters and Romani ensues. Morris is fatally stabbed in the back and Dracula bursts from his coffin at sunset. Jonathan slits his throat with a [[kukri]] knife while Morris stabs him in the heart with his knife. Van Helsing and Jonathan allow Mina to retreat with the Count while Morris dies in the arms of Seward, comforted by his friends.

In the chapel where he renounced God, Dracula lies dying. He and Mina share a kiss as the candles adorning the chapel light up and the cross repairs itself. Dracula reverts to his younger self and asks Mina to give him peace. Mina thrusts the knife through his heart. As he dies, the mark on her forehead disappears, freeing her from his curse. She then decapitates him and gazes up at a fresco of Vlad and Elisabeta ascending to heaven together, finally reunited.

== Themes ==
{{See also|Dracula#Major themes{{!}}''Dracula'' § Major themes}}

===The Other===
In Stoker's original novel, the Count is described through various terms of otherness. His foreign image and customs are translated into the monstrous "Other", leading to an identity for the reader between the foreign and the monstrous. Dracula as a foreign "Other" is set as the marked term. The characters of Jonathan Harker and Mina Murray in the novel represent the [[British Empire]], which provides the unmarked, or normal, perspective in ''Dracula''.<ref>''Bram Stoker's Dracula: Sucking Through the Century, 1897–1997'' by Carol Margaret Davison and Paul Simpson-Housley (2013)</ref> According to Deborah S. Wilson, "Dracula, produced at the apogee of Britain's Empire, projects anxious themes of invasion and colonization upon a foreign culture, embodied in Count Dracula himself. As the feudal lord of a mysterious Balkan country, Dracula himself is described in consistently orientalist terms, terms that stress his racial alterity in all but purely negative terms".<ref>''Bodily Discursions: Genders, Representations, Technologies'' (1997) by  Deborah S. Wilson, Christine Moneera Laennec, p.105</ref> Coppola's film reflects the change in attitude toward the racial Other that occurs over roughly a century by the 1990s, as in the film the general story and plot remain the same with certain important details changed to reflect the difference in racial understanding: the emotional connection between Mina Murray and Dracula in the film stretches beyond the racial boundaries and allowing a genuine [[interracial relationship]] to occur.<ref>''Race, Philosophy, and Film'' (2013) by Mary K. Bloodsworth-Lugo,  Dan Flory, p.198</ref> According to John Allen Stevenson, the threat of the racial "Other" was "the real horror of Dracula, for he is the ultimate social adulterer, whose purpose is nothing if it is not to turn good Englishwomen...like Mina away from their own kind and customs".<ref>''A Vampire in the Mirror: The Sexuality of Dracula'' (1988) by John Allen Stevenson</ref> Meanwhile, in the film the interracial relationship between vampire (Dracula) and human (Mina) becomes permissible, culminating in the union of the human and the Other.<ref>''Race, Philosophy, and Film'' (2013) by Mary K. Bloodsworth-Lugo,  Dan Flory, p.198-199</ref>

===Occultism===

Coppola's film makes a direct connection between Dracula's vampiric origin and [[occultism]]/[[satanism]]. In the opening scene of the film, after learning that his wife committed suicide and is denied salvation, Dracula denies God and makes a deal with the dark forces. This is symbolically verified with Dracula plunging his sword into the [[crucifix]], which immediately starts to bleed excessive amounts of blood that Dracula drinks as a sign of "bloodpact" with [[the Devil]].<ref>''The Lure of the Dark Side: Satan and Western Demonology in Popular Culture'' (2014) by Christopher H. Partridge, Eric S. Christianson</ref>

===AIDS pandemic===
Upon release, ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} [[Frank Rich]] suggested that the film drew upon the prevalent fear of [[HIV/AIDS]] in the 1990s. Coppola, according to Rich, gives to the viewers a movie that both frightens and arouses them by playing off their unchecked fear of the spread of AIDS as an invasion of the national bloodstream.<ref>{{cite news|last=Rich|first=Frank|date=December 6, 1992|title=The New Blood Culture|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/12/06/style/the-new-blood-culture.html|newspaper=The New York Times|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131203115938/https://www.nytimes.com/1992/12/06/style/the-new-blood-culture.html|archive-date=December 3, 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Van Helsing comments to his medical students that civilization and "[[wikt:syphilization|syphilization]]" advanced together; this is viewed as much as a commentary on Coppola's times—during the spread of HIV/AIDS—as it was on Stoker's (who may have died of syphilis, as speculated in some biographies of Stoker).<ref>''Post/modern Dracula: From Victorian Themes to Postmodern Praxis'' (2009) by  John S. Bak, p.14</ref>

== Production ==
=== Development and casting ===
[[Winona Ryder]] initially brought [[James V. Hart]]'s screenplay to the attention of [[Francis Ford Coppola]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Spelling |first=Ian |date=March 1993 |title=Midnight Ryder |magazine=Fangoria #120 |publisher=Starlog Group, Inc. }}</ref> The director had agreed to meet with her so the two could clear the air after she was cast in ''[[The Godfather Part III]]'' to play [[Mary Corleone]] in September 1989 when the previous choice [[Rebecca Schaeffer]] was murdered by an obsessed fan [[Robert John Bardo]] two months earlier. This caused production delays on that film, and Ryder's eventual withdrawal led her to believe Coppola disliked her.<ref name="ET">Rohrer, Trish Dietch. [https://ew.com/article/1992/11/20/revamping-dracula/ Coppola's Bloody Valentine], ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', November 20, 1992, accessed September 6, 2011.</ref> According to Ryder: "I never really thought he would read it. He was so consumed with ''Godfather III''. As I was leaving, I said, 'If you have a chance, read this script.' He glanced down at it politely, but when he saw the word Dracula, his eyes lit up. It was one of his favorite stories from camp."<ref name="orlandosentinel.com">{{Cite web|url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1992-11-17-9211170579-story.html|title=Old Books Such As 'Dracula' Are In Winona Ryder's Blood|agency=Dallas Morning News |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190722141640/https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1992-11-17-9211170579-story.html |archive-date=July 22, 2019 |website=OrlandoSentinel.com |date=November 17, 1992}}</ref> Ryder also explained that "what attracted me to the script is the fact that it's a very emotional love story, which is not really what you think of when you think about Dracula. Mina, like many women in the late 1800s, has a lot of repressed sexuality. Everything about women in that era, the way those corsets forced them to move, was indicative of repression. To express passion was freakish".<ref name="orlandosentinel.com" /> Coppola was also attracted to the sensual elements of the screenplay and said that he wanted portions of the picture to resemble an "erotic dream".<ref name="Mas" /> To prepare for ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'', as the movie would be called, Coppola screened ''[[Citizen Kane]]'', ''[[Ivan the Terrible (1944 film)|Ivan the Terrible]]'', and ''[[Chimes at Midnight]]''.<ref>''The Coppolas: A Family Business'' (2012) by  Vincent LoBrutto, Harriet R. === Costume, set, hair, and makeup design ===
[[File:Dracula armor - Coppola - Sarah Stierch 01.jpg|thumbnail|Dracula's armor on display at Coppola's winery in California]]
Coppola chose to invest a significant amount of the budget in costumes in order to showcase the actors, whom he considered the "jewels" of the feature.<ref name="ET" /><ref name="Mas" /> The film itself was built on the stylistic premise that the costumes were of greater importance to the film's execution than the sets. Due to this, designer [[Eiko Ishioka]] was given immense freedom to design not only Dracula's clothes but also his personae. Ishioka decided that Dracula would be "male and female, old and young, ugly and handsome, animal and human."<ref name="auto1">{{Cite news |last=Dworkin |first=Susan |date=1992-11-08 |title=A Vicious Undertaking |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/11/08/magazine/a-vicious-undertaking.html |access-date=2024-08-29 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

Coppola had a team of artists—veteran production artist [[Mentor Huebner]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=Forbidden Planet Sketchpad |url=https://theasc.com/articles/forbidden-planet-sketchpad |access-date=2024-07-20 |website=The American Society of Cinematographers |language=en}}</ref> future [[DreamWorks Animation]] veteran [[Peter Ramsey]],<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Bradley |first=Anthony Breznican, Wulf |date=2021-04-16 |title=Oscar–Winner Peter Ramsey's Life Story Could Be Its Own Movie |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2021/04/peter-ramsey-spider-man-into-the-spider-verse-oscars |access-date=2024-07-20 |magazine=Vanity Fair |language=en-US}}</ref> and future ''[[Hellboy]]'' creator [[Mike Mignola]]<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-11-12 |title=How Francis Ford Coppola Breathed New Life into 'Bram Stoker's Dracula' • Cinephilia & Beyond |url=https://cinephiliabeyond.org/dracula/ |access-date=2024-07-20 |language=en-US}}</ref>—[[storyboard]] the entire film in advance to carefully illustrate each planned shot. This process created around a thousand images.<ref name="ET" /> He turned the drawings into a choppy animated film—an [[animatic]]—with added music, and spliced in scenes from the French version of ''[[Beauty and the Beast (1946 film)|Beauty and the Beast]]'' that [[Jean Cocteau]] directed in 1946 along with paintings by [[Gustav Klimt]] and other symbolist artists.<ref name="ET" /> He showed the animated film to his designers to give them an idea of the mood and theme he was aiming for. Coppola also asked the set costume designers to simply bring him designs which were "weird". {{"'}}Weird' became a code word for 'Let's not do formula{{'"}}, he later recalled. {{"'}}Give me something that either comes from the research or that comes from your own nightmares.' I gave them paintings, and I gave them drawings, and I talked to them about how I thought the imagery could work."<ref name="ET" />

The film's hair and makeup designer, [[Michèle Burke]], recalls: "Francis didn't want the typical Dracula that had already been done in Hollywood. He wanted something different; a new Dracula without the widow's peak, cape, or pale-white skin." Burke says she used her Catholic upbringing and angelic imagery for design inspiration, as well as the 19th-century attire created by costume designer Eiko Ishioka.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2019/10/8583410/vampire-halloween-makeup-famous-characters|title=The Sexiest Vampires To Inspire Your Halloween Makeup This Year|first=Samantha|last=Sasso|website=www.refinery29.com|access-date=November 16, 2019|archive-date=November 6, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191106132359/https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2019/10/8583410/vampire-halloween-makeup-famous-characters|url-status=live}}</ref>

Because Dracula's scenes did not start filming until very late into the production, Oldman had plenty of downtime to come up with additional ideas for his character. According to Coppola, "He'd [Oldman] get together with the makeup designer [[Greg Cannom]] and, before I knew it, we were going to have not just one monster but five monsters in the film."<ref name="auto1" /> For Oldman, the makeup required for the role was taxing: for the elderly Dracula, he spent nearly seven hours in the makeup chair, then after ten hours on the set, spent another hour-and-a-half having it removed.<ref>''Dracula FAQ: All That's Left to Know About the Count from Transylvania'' (2015) by Bruce Scivally, p.154</ref>

=== Filming ===
Due to delays and cost overruns on some of Coppola's previous projects such as ''[[Apocalypse Now]]'' and ''[[One from the Heart]]'', Coppola was determined to complete ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'' on time and on budget. To accomplish this, he filmed on sound stages to avoid potential troubles caused by inclement weather.<ref name="ET" /><ref name="Mas" />

While preparing to play Dracula, Oldman took lessons with a singing teacher in order to drive his voice one octave lower.<ref name="auto1" /> Coppola brought his principal actors to his seventeen-hundred-acre Napa Valley winery and estate for improvisations and exercises. The actors read the novel aloud, went through the script, and sat down for communal dinners. The idea was to break down barriers and establish relationships that would translate to the film. The summer-camp atmosphere extended to all the principal actors except Oldman. The director instructed Elwes, Campbell, and Grant to go horseback-riding and hot-air ballooning together, getting to know each other and their characters better, and sent Ryder and Frost off to do things together, while Oldman was kept isolated from the cast;<ref>Dracula FAQ: All That's Left to Know About the Count from Transylvania (2015) by Bruce Scivally, p.153</ref> this extended to living arrangements throughout filming, with all the vampire hunters living on one property and Oldman living on another. The rest of the cast met Oldman for the first time on set during rehearsals, after which they would not see him again until filming.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/cary-elwes-mi7-princess-bride-saw-glory-twister-1235052447/ | title=Cary Elwes on 'MI:7' Production Snags, His Return to Rom-Coms and Standing Firm Against a 'Princess Bride' Reboot | website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] | date=November 24, 2021 }}</ref>

Coppola brought in acting coach Greta Seacat to coach Frost and Ryder for their erotic scenes, as he felt uncomfortable discussing sexuality with the young actresses.<ref name="ET" /> However, he did ask Oldman to speak seductively off camera to Frost while they were filming a scene in which she writhed alone in her bed in ecstasy.<ref name="Forever.org" /> She later classified the things Oldman said to her as "very unrepeatable".<ref name="Forever.org" /><ref name="ET" /><ref name="Frost">Diamond, Jamie. {{Deprecated archive|url=https://archive.today/20130122130732/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,312626,00.html|title="She's Hot, She's Sexy, She's Undead"}}, ''Entertainment Weekly'', December 11, 1992, accessed September 6, 2011.</ref> Winona Ryder found the intensity of Oldman's acting style too much at times; the two fell out early in the filming process and had difficulty working together from then on. Coppola stated, "they got along and then one day they didn't—absolutely didn't get along. None of us were privy to what had happened."<ref name="Forever.org">{{cite news| url=https://winona-ryder.org/library/1992-2/neck-romance/| title=Neck Romance| author=Rachel Abramowitz| publisher=winona-ryder.org| date=December 1, 1992| access-date=August 22, 2018| archive-date=August 23, 2018| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180823105543/https://winona-ryder.org/library/1992-2/neck-romance/| url-status=live}}</ref> Ryder has referred to the "trauma" of the experience and said that she "felt there was a danger" while working with Oldman.<ref>{{cite magazine| url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2018/08/keanu-reeves-winona-ryder-destination-wedding-movie-interview?mbid=social_twitter| title=Winona Ryder and Keanu Reeves, Really, Really Love Each Other| author=Julie Miller| magazine=Vanity Fair| date=August 22, 2018| access-date=August 22, 2018| archive-date=July 30, 2021| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210730085518/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2018/08/keanu-reeves-winona-ryder-destination-wedding-movie-interview?mbid=social_twitter| url-status=live}}</ref> However, she has also referred to her friction with Oldman as "teen drama", stating, "He [Gary] was going through a divorce, and I think I can say this because he's pretty open about it, but he's been sober for a long time now, and he's raised three kids, and he's a dream. He's a good friend of mine now".<ref>{{cite magazine| url=https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/winona-ryder-1| title=Winona Ryder| author=Stephen Mooallem| magazine=Interview Magazine| date=April 23, 2013| access-date=September 8, 2018| archive-date=November 17, 2020| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201117180139/https://www.interviewmagazine.com/film/winona-ryder-1| url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2020, Ryder also stated that Reeves and Hopkins once refused Coppola's direction to verbally abuse her to make her cry during a scene that required an emotional reaction.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Winona Ryder Says Keanu Reeves Refused Direction to Verbally Abuse Her on Dracula Set|url=https://people.com/movies/winona-ryder-says-keanu-reeves-refused-direction-dractula/|access-date=May 21, 2021|website=People|language=en|archive-date=May 21, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210521223306/https://people.com/movies/winona-ryder-says-keanu-reeves-refused-direction-dractula/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2020/06/keanu-reeves-winona-ryder-cry-dracula-1234568867/|title=Winona Ryder Says Keanu Reeves Refused to Make Her Cry on 'Dracula' Set|first1=Zack|last1=Sharf|date=June 22, 2020|access-date=June 25, 2020|archive-date=June 24, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200624213208/https://www.indiewire.com/2020/06/keanu-reeves-winona-ryder-cry-dracula-1234568867/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://ew.com/movies/winona-ryder-keanu-reeves-dracula-refused-verbal-abuse/|title=Winona Ryder says Keanu Reeves refused to verbally abuse her on 'Dracula' set|website=EW.com|access-date=June 25, 2020|archive-date=June 25, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200625105148/https://ew.com/movies/winona-ryder-keanu-reeves-dracula-refused-verbal-abuse/|url-status=live}}</ref> However, Coppola denied this and described the situation as him instructing Oldman—in character—to whisper improvised words both to her and other actors on set to scare them. === Special effects ===
Coppola was insistent that he did not want to use any kind of contemporary special effects techniques such as [[computer-generated imagery]] when making the movie, instead wishing to use antiquated effects techniques from the early history of cinema, which he felt would be more appropriate given that the film's period setting coincides with the [[History of film|origin of film]]. He initially hired a standard visual effects team, but when they told him that the things he wanted to achieve were impossible without using modern digital technology, Coppola disagreed and fired them, replacing them with his son [[Roman Coppola]]. As a result, all of the visual effects seen in the film were achieved without the use of [[Optical printer|optical]] or computer-generated effects, instead using [[Practical effect|on-set]] and [[In-camera effect|in-camera]] methods. For example, any sequences that would have typically required the use of [[compositing]] were instead achieved by either [[Rear projection effect|rear projection]] with actors placed in front of a screen with an image projected behind them, or through [[multiple exposure]] by shooting a background slate then rewinding the film through the camera and shooting the foreground slate on the same piece of film, all the while using [[Matte (filmmaking)|matting]] techniques to ensure that only the desired areas of film were exposed. [[Forced perspective]]s were often employed to combine [[miniature effect]]s or [[matte painting]]s with full-sized elements, or create distorted views of reality, such as holding the camera upside down or at odd angles to create the effect of objects defying the laws of physics.<ref name="Roman">{{cite video|people=[[Roman Coppola]], [[Francis Ford Coppola]] |title=In Camera: The Naïve Visual Effects of 'Bram Stoker's Dracula' |medium=[[Documentary]]|publisher=[[Columbia Pictures|AMC]]|date=2001}}</ref> When filming Dracula's [[Point-of-view shot|POV]], Roman took individual images with his camera in an erratic way, sometimes only a few random frames per second, and then sudden bursts of several frames per second. For Lucy's movements, she did her performance backwards, and the film then processed in reverse.<ref>{{Cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=vzq805jZSAoC&dq=Coppola+Dracula+POV+erratic+randomly+missing+frames+reverse+motion+Lucy&pg=PA130 |title=Horror Film Aesthetics: Creating the Visual Language of Fear |isbn=978-0-7864-5834-9 |access-date=August 4, 2023 |archive-date=November 7, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231107044717/https://books.google.com/books?id=vzq805jZSAoC&dq=Coppola+Dracula+POV+erratic+randomly+missing+frames+reverse+motion+Lucy&pg=PA130 |url-status=live |last1=Sipos |first1=Thomas M. |date=January 10, 2014 |publisher=McFarland }}</ref>

== Reception ==
=== Critical response ===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 69% based on 124 reviews, with an average rating of 6.4/10. The site's critics consensus reads, "Overblown in the best sense of the word, Francis Ford Coppola's vision of Bram Stoker's ''Dracula'' rescues the character from decades of campy interpretations—and features some terrific performances to boot."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/bram_stokers_dracula/|title=Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=September 26, 2024|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140509201056/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/bram_stokers_dracula/|archive-date=May 9, 2014 }}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 57 out of 100, based on 17 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Bram Stoker&#39;s Dracula'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/dracula |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=November 1, 2023 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://m.cinemascore.com |title=CinemaScore |work=cinemascore.com}}</ref>

=== Box office ===
''Bram Stoker's Dracula'' opened at [[List of 1992 box office number-one films in the United States|number one at the US box office]] with a November record of $30,521,679, beating ''[[Back to the Future Part II]]''.<ref>{{cite news|title=Dracula Takes a Big Bite Out of Box Office |work=Los Angeles Times|date=November 16, 1992|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-11-16-ca-521-story.html|access-date=November 22, 2010|first=David J.|last=Fox|url-status=live|archive-url=http://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/20101123131751/http://articles.latimes.com/1992-11-16/entertainment/ca-521_1_francis-ford-coppola|archive-date=November 23, 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Weekend Box Office 'Dracula' Counts for Plenty|work=Los Angeles Times|date=November 17, 1992|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-11-17-ca-508-story.html|access-date=November 22, 2010|first=David J.|last=Fox|url-status=live|archive-url=http://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/20101123152312/http://articles.latimes.com/1992-11-17/entertainment/ca-508_1_weekend-box-office|archive-date=November 23, 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=12|title=Top opening weekends of November|date=November 15, 1994}}</ref> This record was quickly surpassed by ''[[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-11-23-ca-624-story.html |title=Holy Cow! 'Home 2' Hauls in Box-Office Moola |website=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=November 23, 1992 |access-date=August 25, 2022 |archive-date=August 25, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220825163340/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-11-23-ca-624-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The film dropped off in subsequent weeks, losing 50.8% of its audience after its first weekend in release<ref>[https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=bramstokersdracula.htm Bram Stoker's Dracula] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110929035509/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=bramstokersdracula.htm |date=September 29, 2011 }}. boxofficemojo.com</ref> and exiting the top five after three weeks. It became a box-office hit, grossing $82,522,790 in the United States and Canada, becoming the 15th-highest-grossing film of the year.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/box-office-records/domestic/all-movies/cumulative/released-in-1992 |title=Top 1992 Movies at the Domestic Box Office |website=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] |access-date=October 24, 2019 |archive-date=May 6, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190506142324/https://www.the-numbers.com/box-office-records/domestic/all-movies/cumulative/released-in-1992 |url-status=live }}</ref> The film set an opening weekend record in the United Kingdom of $4 million, beating the record set by ''[[Batman Returns]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|page=38|date=February 8, 1993|title='Bodyguard', 'Beauty' blockbuster-ready|last=Groves|first=Don}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=26|date=February 2, 1993|url=https://variety.com/1993/film/news/col-tristar-u-k-high-set-by-dracula-103595/|title=Col Tristar U.K. high set by 'Dracula'|access-date=January 25, 2021|archive-date=January 30, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210130233236/https://variety.com/1993/film/news/col-tristar-u-k-high-set-by-dracula-103595/|url-status=live}}</ref> It also held the record for having the biggest opening weekend for an [[18 (British Board of Film Classification)|18 certificate]] film until 1997 when ''[[Alien Resurrection]]'' surpassed it.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.screendaily.com/uk-audiences-devour-hannibal/405062.article|title=UK audiences devour Hannibal}}</ref> Internationally, the film grossed another $133,339,902 for a total worldwide gross of $215,862,692,<ref name=numbers>[https://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1992/0BRMS.php Movie Dracula – Box Office Data, News, Cast Information] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090419225227/http://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1992/0BRMS.php |date=April 19, 2009 }} from The Numbers</ref> making it the ninth-highest-grossing film of the year worldwide.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/box-office-records/worldwide/all-movies/cumulative/released-in-1992 |title=Top 1992 Movies at the Worldwide Box Office |website=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] |access-date=October 24, 2019 |archive-date=June 13, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190613172124/https://www.the-numbers.com/box-office-records/worldwide/all-movies/cumulative/released-in-1992 |url-status=live }}</ref>

As of 2023, adjusted for inflation, ''Bram Stoker's Dracula''{{'}}s box office is $473.5 million, making it one of the highest-grossing vampire movies of all time.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://movieweb.com/highest-grossing-vampire-movies-of-all-time-adjusted-inflation/#dracula-1979-mdash-132-2-million | title=20 Highest-Grossing Vampire Movies of All Time (Adjusted for Inflation) | date=December 5, 2023 | access-date=February 14, 2024 | archive-date=December 6, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231206075627/https://movieweb.com/highest-grossing-vampire-movies-of-all-time-adjusted-inflation/#dracula-1979-mdash-132-2-million | url-status=live }}</ref>

== Home media ==
In 1993, the film received both a standard [[VHS]] release and a limited edition VHS release, the latter being a box set in the shape of a coffin. The limited edition release contained the film on VHS, which included a behind-the-scenes [[Documentary film|documentary]], and the original ''Dracula'' [[novel]] by Bram Stoker in [[paperback]]. Grey, [[gothic art|gothic]] statue heads (as seen on the original film poster) adorned the front cover of the book against a gray stone background. That same year, the [[Criterion Collection]] released a special edition [[LaserDisc]] of the film.<ref>{{Cite web |last=WILK |first=Julien |title=Bram Stoker's Dracula: Special Edition |url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/04954/CC1335L/Bram-Stokers-Dracula:-Special-Edition |website=LaserDisc Database}}</ref>

''Dracula'' was first released to [[DVD]] in 1999<ref>[https://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/bramstoker.php DVD Verdict Review – ''Bram Stoker's Dracula''] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091029215020/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/bramstoker.php |date=October 29, 2009 }} July 6, 1999</ref> and again as a [[Superbit]] DVD in 2001.<ref>[https://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculasuperbit.php DVD Verdict Review – ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'': Superbit Edition] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090101125448/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculasuperbit.php |date=January 1, 2009 }} December 18, 2001</ref> The DVD included several extra features: filmographies, the original theatrical trailer, a documentary (''Dracula: The Man, The Myth, The Legend''), costume designs, and DVD trailers. The Superbit version did not contain any extra features.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdbeaver.com/film/DVDCompare2/dracula.htm|title=Bram Stoker's Dracula|website=www.dvdbeaver.com|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171029172938/http://www.dvdbeaver.com/film/DVDCompare2/dracula.htm|archive-date=October 29, 2017}}</ref>

A two-disc Collector's Edition DVD<ref>[https://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculace.php DVD Verdict Review – ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'': Collector's Edition] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091028020111/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculace.php |date=October 28, 2009 }} October 22, 2007</ref> and [[Blu-ray]]<ref>[https://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculabluray.php DVD Verdict Review – ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'' (Blu-Ray)] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090414030838/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/draculabluray.php |date=April 14, 2009 }} October 4, 2007</ref> was released in 2007. Special features include an introduction and [[audio commentary]] by director Francis Ford Coppola, [[Deleted scene|deleted and extended scenes]], teaser and full-length [[Trailer (promotion)|trailers]], and the documentaries "The Blood Is the Life: The Making of ''Dracula''", "The Costumes Are the Sets: The Design of Eiko Ishioka", "In Camera: The Naïve Visual Effects of ''Dracula''", and "Method and Madness: Visualizing ''Dracula''".

A 4K release was put out in 2017, sourced from a new scan of the original negatives.{{Citation needed|date=June 2024}}

== Legacy ==
The film had a considerable effect on popular culture and vampire representation in media. Costume design by [[Eiko Ishioka]] created a new image for the Count and for the first time freed him from the black cape and evening wear the character had become associated with since [[Bela Lugosi]]'s [[Dracula (1931 English-language film)|portrayal in 1931]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/eiko-ishioka-japanese-costume-designer-google-doodle-bram-stokers-dracula-gary-oldman-winona-ryder-a7836536.html|title=Celebrating Eiko Ishioka's extraordinary costumes for Bram Stoker's Dracula|website=[[Independent.co.uk]]|date=July 12, 2017|access-date=June 22, 2018|archive-date=June 23, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180623004842/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/eiko-ishioka-japanese-costume-designer-google-doodle-bram-stokers-dracula-gary-oldman-winona-ryder-a7836536.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was also a landmark in vampire horror as it is the first vampire film and the only ''Dracula'' adaptation to win Oscars.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.oscars.org/news/how-apocalypse-now-poster-led-oscar-winning-costumes-bram-stokers-dracula|title=How an Apocalypse Now Poster Led to Oscar-Winning Costumes for Bram Stoker's Dracula|date=November 4, 2015|website=Oscars.org|access-date=January 16, 2020|archive-date=January 2, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200102115748/https://www.oscars.org/news/how-apocalypse-now-poster-led-oscar-winning-costumes-bram-stokers-dracula|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film is seen as a game changer by many critics, which established a tone and style that redefined cinematic vampires. It created a host of new vampire film [[Trope (cinema)|tropes]], like retractable fangs, vampires turning into literal bat-men, and a [[steampunk]] aesthetic.<ref name="thedigitalbits.com">{{Cite web|url=http://thedigitalbits.com/columns/history-legacy-showmanship/coppola-dracula-25th|title=Coppola's Bite: Remembering "Bram Stoker's Dracula" on its 25th Anniversary|first=Michael|last=Coate|website=thedigitalbits.com|date=March 4, 2023|access-date=May 16, 2020|archive-date=July 1, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200701030842/https://thedigitalbits.com/columns/history-legacy-showmanship/coppola-dracula-25th|url-status=live}}</ref> Author Bruce Scivally states, "''Bram Stoker's Dracula'' is significant in the way that ''[[The Exorcist]]'' and ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'' were significant, in showing that a horror story can be worthy of an A-list cast and production values, and that a truly imaginative filmmaker can take even a story as hoary as Dracula and give it a new luster."<ref name="thedigitalbits.com" />

Coppola's film began a cycle of prestige monster movies with big stars and name directors, as well as high production values and lavish costumes: ''[[Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (film)|Mary Shelley's Frankenstein]]'' (1994), directed by [[Kenneth Branagh]] and starring [[Robert De Niro]] as Frankenstein's Monster; ''[[Wolf (1994 film)|Wolf]]'' (1994), directed by [[Mike Nichols]] and starring [[Jack Nicholson]] as a werewolf; and ''[[Mary Reilly (film)|Mary Reilly]]'' (1996), directed by [[Stephen Frears]], and starring [[John Malkovich]] as Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde and [[Julia Roberts]] as a maid who develops a crush on the mad doctor and his crooked other self.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/horror-fancy-clothes-1990s-cycle-prestige-monster-movies | title=Horror in fancy clothes: The 1990s cycle of prestige monster movies | date=December 14, 2022 | access-date=November 8, 2023 | archive-date=December 23, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221223161359/https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/horror-fancy-clothes-1990s-cycle-prestige-monster-movies | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3542468/full-moon-frights-wolf-aka-i-was-a-middle-aged-werewolf/ | title=&#91;Full Moon Frights&#93; Jack Nicholson Becomes a Middle-Aged Werewolf in 'Wolf' | date=January 21, 2019 | access-date=November 8, 2023 | archive-date=November 8, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231108153955/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3542468/full-moon-frights-wolf-aka-i-was-a-middle-aged-werewolf/ | url-status=live }}</ref> Coppola's film also influenced the next major vampire movie/literary adaptation: in 1994, ''[[Interview with the Vampire (film)|Interview with the Vampire]]'', directed by [[Neil Jordan]], was released starring [[Tom Cruise]] as [[Lestat de Lioncourt]], [[Brad Pitt]] as [[Louis de Pointe du Lac|Louis]],  and [[Kirsten Dunst]] as Claudia. According to Jordan: "Up to that point, Francis Ford Coppola with ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'', he introduced opulence and theatricality. Normally, before that one, I always thought of vampire movies as cheap, cobbled together, brilliant use of minimal resources. Francis made it this epic, didn't he? So when I was given the opportunity to make ''Interview with the Vampire'', I thought, 'Oh, it would be really great to expand on that epic sense of darkness and to give these characters huge, kind of romantic destinies and longings and feelings.'"<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/1266199-interview-neil-jordan-on-marlowe-interview-with-the-vampire | title=Interview: Neil Jordan on Marlowe & Interview with the Vampire | date=February 14, 2023 | access-date=December 13, 2023 | archive-date=December 13, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231213113940/https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/1266199-interview-neil-jordan-on-marlowe-interview-with-the-vampire | url-status=live }}</ref>
