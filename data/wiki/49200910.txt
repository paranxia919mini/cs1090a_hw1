'''''Hunt for the Wilderpeople''''' is a 2016 New Zealand [[adventure comedy film]] produced, written and directed by [[Taika Waititi]], based on the 1986 novel ''Wild Pork and Watercress'' by [[Barry Crump]]. [[Sam Neill]] and [[Julian Dennison]] star as "Uncle" Hector and Ricky Baker: a [[father figure]] and foster son who become the targets of a manhunt after fleeing into the [[The bush#New Zealand|New Zealand bush]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Ricky Baker, a child in the New Zealand [[foster care]] system who has frequently run away or been let go by other foster homes, is taken in by Bella and her husband Hector. Though Hector remains distant from Ricky, interested only in [[Hunting in New Zealand|hunting]], Ricky grows close to Bella. When she suddenly dies of a stroke, Hector is informed that Ricky will be relocated by state services, represented by the agent Paula Hall.

Not wanting to return to the foster care system, Ricky sloppily fakes his own death, accidentally burning down Hector and Bella's shed, and runs off into the [[The bush|bush]]. Hector goes after him, and in the process, injures his ankle. The two are forced to remain in the bush until Hector's ankle heals. Meanwhile, Paula finds the wreckage at Bella and Hector's farm and claims that Hector has kidnapped Ricky, launching a nationwide manhunt for the pair.

After a period of time, Hector's ankle has mostly healed, and the pair find a hut in the bush. They see their pictures in a newspaper article about the manhunt before they are suddenly discovered by three hunters returning to the hut. Hector and Ricky manage to escape – but, due to Ricky's innocent and ignorant nature, he accidentally suggests Hector has molested him. The manhunt intensifies and Ricky becomes nationally recognised.

While wandering around together, Ricky and Hector warm up to each other, with Ricky telling him about his fears and anxieties concerning the foster care system. When they enter an empty hut again for more supplies, they find a park ranger suffering from a diabetic attack. Hector remains with the man whilst Ricky goes down to a nearby town for help. He finds a young girl Kahu and her father, who recognise him but do not turn him into the authorities. 

Ricky becomes smitten with Kahu, and accidentally falls asleep and stays the night. By the time he reaches the hut the next morning, it is surrounded by police and Hector has fled. When facing Paula across a gully she tries to bribe him, asking him to say that Hector was a sex offender in return for not going to juvenile detention. However, he declines and catches up with Hector. Hector's reputation has improved for helping the ranger, as the media changes their view of him.

When Ricky and Hector are reunited, however, they rob the same hunters as before, causing his public image to decline again. Later, the pair are attacked by a wild boar. In the attack, Hector's dog Zag is wounded, forcing Hector to shoot him. After his burial, Ricky reveals that he has been carrying Bella's ashes, so they scatter them into a waterfall high in a mountain range.

A man named Psycho Sam, who is living on his own, is discovered by Ricky and Hector. He permits them to stay overnight. After five months of survival in the wild and numerous near misses, they are eventually found due to Sam connecting Ricky's mobile to the internet. The pair are apprehended while trying to escape in "Crumpy", Sam's red [[Toyota Hilux#Fourth generation (N50, N60, N70; 1983)|1980s Toyota Hilux]], and Ricky unintentionally shoots Hector.

Hector is sent to prison for around a year, whilst Ricky stays with Kahu and her father. After his release, they reunite and continue their life of adventure.

== Production ==
Waititi first began to adapt the book in 2005, and completed multiple different drafts.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/703677-taika-waititi-wilderpeople-ragnarok#/slide/1|title=Taika Waititi Talks Wilderpeople and Ragnarok|date=2016-07-19|newspaper=ComingSoon.net|language=en-US|access-date=2016-11-03}}</ref> The early drafts stayed true to the book. However, later versions departed from it in several ways.<ref name=":1">{{Cite news |last=Covert |first=Colin |date=July 15, 2016 |title=Next big thing: 'Wilderpeople' director about to hit mainstream with 'Thor' sequel |newspaper=Star Tribune |url=http://www.startribune.com/next-big-thing-wilderpeople-director-about-to-hit-the-mainstream-with-thor-sequel/386832311/ |url-status=dead |access-date=2016-11-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160715154007/http://www.startribune.com/next-big-thing-wilderpeople-director-about-to-hit-the-mainstream-with-thor-sequel/386832311/ |archive-date=2016-07-15}}</ref> Julian Dennison was cast by Waititi from earlier work they did together on a commercial.<ref name=":0" />

The film had a budget of approximately NZ$4.5 million,<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |date=2016-07-22 |title=Taika Waititi: "Hunt For The Wilderpeople" Isn't So Different From "Thor: Ragnarok" |language=en-US |newspaper=[[Fast Company]] |url=https://www.fastcocreate.com/3061545/master-class/taika-waititi-hunt-for-the-wilderpeople-isnt-so-different-from-thor-ragnarok |access-date=2016-11-03}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/news/article.cfm?c_id=1501119&objectid=11616556|title=Hunt for the Wilderpeople sets new box office record|date=2016-04-04|newspaper=[[The New Zealand Herald]] |language=en-NZ|access-date=2016-11-03}}</ref> of which $2 million came from the [[New Zealand Film Commission]].

The film was shot over 5 weeks, on locations including the [[Central Plateau, New Zealand|Central Plateau]], Ahuroa and the [[Waitākere Ranges]]. Almost the entire film was shot using a single camera.<ref>{{cite web |first=Steve|last=Kilgallon|date=April 7, 2016 |title=Behind the scenes of Wilderpeople: mud, snow, kids and fake pigs |url=http://www.stuff.co.nz/entertainment/film/78529380/behind-the-scenes-of-wilderpeople-mud-snow-kids-and-fake-pigs |access-date=2024-04-15 |website=[[Stuff (website)|Stuff]] }}</ref>

==Release==
The film premiered at the [[2016 Sundance Film Festival]] on 22 January 2016<ref>{{Cite news |date=January 25, 2016 |title=Taika Waititi's Critically Acclaimed 'Hunt for the Wilderpeople' Premieres at Sundance |work=Little Black Book |url=https://www.lbbonline.com/news/taika-waititis-critically-acclaimed-hunt-for-the-wilderpeople-premieres-at-sundance |access-date=2022-12-29}}</ref> and was released in cinemas on 31 March 2016 in New Zealand by Piki Films and [[Madman Entertainment]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |date=2016-01-27 |title=The Orchard Lands 'Hunt For The Wilderpeople' In $2 Million Deal – Sundance |url=https://deadline.com/2016/01/hunt-for-the-wilderpeople-the-orchard-sundance-film-festival-1201691068/ |access-date=2022-12-29 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|archive-date=29 December 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221229120534/https://deadline.com/2016/01/hunt-for-the-wilderpeople-the-orchard-sundance-film-festival-1201691068/ |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Lang |first=Brent |date=2016-09-25 |title=The Orchard Releases Digital Sales Results for ''Hunt for the Wilderpeople'' (Exclusive) |url=https://variety.com/2016/film/news/the-orchard-releases-digital-sales-results-for-hunt-for-the-wilderpeople-exclusive-1201869872/ |access-date=2022-12-29|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> The film opened in Australian cinemas on 26 May 2016 and received a [[limited theatrical release]] in North America on 24 June 2016.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Record-breaking run for Hunt for The Wilderpeople |url=https://www.nzfilm.co.nz/news/record-breaking-run-hunt-wilderpeople |access-date=2025-08-11 |website=New Zealand Film Commission |language=en}}</ref> The film was released on [[DVD]] and [[Blu-ray]] in North America on 25 October 2016 by [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]].<ref name=":1" />

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film grossed NZ$1,263,000 in New Zealand on its opening weekend, the highest grossing opening weekend for a New Zealand film (as defined by the [[New Zealand Film Commission]]), ahead of ''[[What Becomes of the Broken Hearted? (film)|What Becomes of the Broken Hearted?]]''{{-'}}s $912,000 record in 1999.<ref name="openingweekend" /><{{efn|1=While the films of ''[[The Lord of the Rings (film series)|The Lord of the Rings]]'' and ''[[The Hobbit (film series)|The Hobbit]]'' trilogies had higher grossing opening weekends, they do not meet the definition of a New Zealand film per the New Zealand Film Commission Act 1978.}} It went on to gross NZ$12 million.

Internationally, as of October 2016, the film has grossed [[A$]]10,935,319 in Australia, [[US$]]507,380 in the UK and US$5,137,201 in North America.

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Hunt for the Wilderpeople'' received a score of 97%, based on 201 reviews, with an average rating of 7.9/10. The site's critical consensus reads: "The charmingly offbeat ''Hunt for the Wilderpeople'' unites a solid cast, a talented filmmaker, and a poignant, funny, deeply affecting message."<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hunt_for_the_wilderpeople |title= Hunt For the Wilderpeople (2016) |work= Rotten Tomatoes |access-date= 3 January 2021}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a score of 81 out of 100, based on 30 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/hunt-for-the-wilderpeople |title= Hunt For the Wilderpeople |work= Metacritic |access-date= 2 July 2016}}</ref>

In his review, Hamish Popplestone remarked: "Though both flawed, Neill's and Dennison's characters are so, so charming on-screen and are fully apt at weaving through the dramatic, comedic, and sad points of the script."<ref>{{cite web |last=Popplestone |first=Hamish |url=http://salient.org.nz/2016/04/hunt-for-the-wilderpeople/ |title=Hunt for the Wilderpeople — Salient |website=Salient.org.nz |date=2016-04-17 |access-date=2017-01-03 |archive-date=5 January 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170105010022/http://salient.org.nz/2016/04/hunt-for-the-wilderpeople/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' magazine named ''Hunt for the Wilderpeople'' the number one film for 2016.<ref>{{cite web|author1=John Nugent |author2=Emma Thrower |author3=Phil De Semlyen |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/best-films-2016/ |title=The best movies of 2016, Feature &#124; Movies – Empire |location=GB |website=Empireonline.com |date=2016-12-21 |access-date=2017-01-03}}</ref>
