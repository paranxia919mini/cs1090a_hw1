'''''Amar Akbar Anthony''''' is a 1977 Indian [[Hindi cinema|Hindi]]-language [[masala film]] directed and produced by [[Manmohan Desai]] and written by [[Kader Khan]]. Released in [[India]] on 27 May 1977, the film stars an [[ensemble cast]] of [[Vinod Khanna]], [[Rishi Kapoor]], [[Amitabh Bachchan]], [[Neetu Singh]], [[Parveen Babi]], [[Shabana Azmi]], [[Nirupa Roy]] and [[Pran (actor)|Pran]]. The plot focuses on three brothers separated in childhood who are adopted by families of different faiths; [[Hinduism]], [[Islam]] and [[Christianity]]. They grow up to be a [[police officer]], a [[qawwali]] singer and the owner of a country [[Bar (establishment)|bar]], respectively. The [[soundtrack]] album was composed by [[Laxmikant–Pyarelal|Laxmikant-Pyarelal]] and the lyrics were written by [[Anand Bakshi]]. The film earned {{INRConvert|155|m|year=1977|mode=historical}} at the Indian [[box office]], becoming the [[List of highest-grossing Indian films|highest-grossing Indian film]] of that year, alongside ''[[Dharam Veer (film)|Dharam Veer]]'' and ''[[Hum Kisise Kum Naheen]]''.<ref name=box/>

[[Religious tolerance]] became a landmark theme in [[Bollywood]] [[masala film]]s,<ref name="Dwyer2005">{{cite book|last=Dwyer|first=Rachel|title=100 Bollywood films|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=4EBNAQAAIAAJ|publisher=Lotus Collection, [[Roli Books]]|date=2005|page=14|isbn=978-81-7436-433-3|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160920193359/https://books.google.com/books?id=4EBNAQAAIAAJ&hl=en|archive-date=20 September 2016}}</ref> building on the masala formula pioneered a few years earlier by [[Nasir Hussain]]'s ''[[Yaadon Ki Baaraat]]'' (1973).<ref>{{cite news|last=Sharma|first=Devansh|title=Yaadon Ki Baaraat: Nasir Hussain's 1973 potboiler initiated Hindi cinema's transformation into 'Bollywood'|url=https://www.firstpost.com/entertainment/yaadon-ki-baaraat-nasir-hussains-1973-potboiler-initiated-hindi-cinemas-transformation-into-bollywood-5469141.html|publisher=[[Firstpost]]|date=2 November 2018|access-date=5 February 2019|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190209180212/https://www.firstpost.com/entertainment/yaadon-ki-baaraat-nasir-hussains-1973-potboiler-initiated-hindi-cinemas-transformation-into-bollywood-5469141.html|archive-date=9 February 2019 }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Manwani|first=Akshay|title=Yaadon Ki Baaraat: The quintessential bollywood film |url=https://www.dnaindia.com/bollywood/report-yaadon-ki-baaraat-the-quintessential-bollywood-film-2573936|work=[[Daily News and Analysis]]|date=8 January 2018|access-date=5 February 2019|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190209124341/https://www.dnaindia.com/bollywood/report-yaadon-ki-baaraat-the-quintessential-bollywood-film-2573936|archive-date=9 February 2019}}</ref> ''Amar Akbar Anthony'' also had a lasting impact on pop culture with its catchy songs, quotable one-liners, and the character of Anthony Gonsalves (played by Bachchan). It won several awards at the [[25th Filmfare Awards]], including [[Filmfare Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]], [[Filmfare Award for Best Music Director|Best Music Director]] and [[Filmfare Award for Best Editing|Best Editing]]. It was later remade in [[Tamil language|Tamil]] as ''[[Shankar Salim Simon]]'' (1978), in [[Telugu language|Telugu]] as ''[[Ram Robert Rahim]]'' (1980),<ref>{{cite news |author=ET Bureau|title=Transcending language barrier|url=http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/Features/Business_of_Bollywood/Transcending_language_barrier/articleshow/3504534.cms|publisher=[[The Economic Times]]|date=20 September 2008|access-date=12 June 2012|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121008025845/http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/Features/Business_of_Bollywood/Transcending_language_barrier/articleshow/3504534.cms|archive-date=8 October 2012}}</ref> and in [[Malayalam]] as ''[[John Jaffer Janardhanan]]'' (1982). In [[Pakistan]], the film was unofficially remade in [[Punjabi language|Punjabi]] as ''Akbar Amar Anthony'' (1978).<ref>{{cite news|last=Rabe|first=Nate|title=Sounds of Lollywood: The big difference between 'Amar Akbar Anthony' and its Pakistani rip-off|url=https://scroll.in/reel/848494/sound-of-lollywood-the-big-difference-between-amar-akbar-anthony-and-its-pakistani-rip-off|publisher=[[Scroll.in|Scroll]]|date=2 September 2017|access-date=13 July 2020|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200715121318/https://scroll.in/reel/848494/sound-of-lollywood-the-big-difference-between-amar-akbar-anthony-and-its-pakistani-rip-off|archive-date=15 July 2020}}</ref>

== Plot ==
{{plot|date=March 2026}} <!--per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries should be between 400 and 700 words-->
Kishanlal, a [[chauffeur]], is released from [[prison]] on [[Indian Independence Day|15 August 1955]], after serving a sentence for the fatal [[Hit and run|hit-and-run]] [[accident]] committed by his employer, [[Crime boss|crime lord]] Robert. 

Despite Robert's promises, Kishanlal returns home to find his wife Bharati suffering from [[tuberculosis]] and their three sons starving. Enraged, Kishanlal confronts Robert to beg for his assistance, only to be humiliated, abused and disowned. Turning the tables on Robert's [[Henchman|henchmen]], Kishanlal escapes in a car loaded with smuggled [[Gold bar|gold bullion]], pursued by Robert's goons. Arriving home, Kishanlal finds Bharati gone; her [[suicide note]] explains her shame and despair. Taking his sons with him, he eludes pursuit to place the boys at the foot of [[Mahatma Gandhi]]'s statue in [[Sanjay Gandhi National Park|Borivali National Park]], leaving the eldest in charge. He drives off to mislead Robert's goons, but after a fiery car crash both the police and Robert's people believe Kishanlal and his sons are dead.

The three children become separated.  Amar, the eldest, runs after his father, is knocked down by Robert's goons and adopted by [[Superintendent (police)|Police Superintendent]] Khanna (a [[Hindus|Hindu]] by faith). Middle son Anthony goes in search of food for his crying baby brother; left alone, Raju is found and adopted by kindly [[Muslims|Muslim]] tailor Darji Ilahabadi. Returning to find no one, Anthony shelters from a [[storm]] outside a nearby [[List of Catholic churches in India|Catholic church]], falling asleep from exhaustion. The [[parish]] [[Priesthood in the Catholic Church|priest]], Father Gonsalves, discovers him and Bharati's suicide note and adopts him. Each will be reared in the religions of their adoptive fathers: Hindu, Islam and Christianity.

Elsewhere: Bharati is caught in the storm and struck blind by a falling branch - divine punishment for leaving her sons. She is rescued and dropped off home by Mr. Ilahabadi, but cannot see Raju beside him; at home she is told her family is dead. Kishanlal escapes the car crash and finds Robert's gold; returning to the empty park a rich man – he is devastated to be alone.

Twenty-two years pass.  Bharati, now a [[flower seller]], is the victim of a hit-and-run accident outside a church and is rushed to a [[private hospital]] by Anthony, the [[Liquor license]] of the neighborhood. Raju, now called Akbar and a popular ''[[Qawwali]]'' singer, is at the hospital to romance Dr. Salma Ali because her grumpy father Taiyyab Ali disapproves of him. Amar is the police inspector who  arrives to check on the accident case. All three men [[donating blood|donate blood]] to save Bharati's life, unaware they are related. Later, Amar tracks down [[highway]] [[robbery|robbers]] and meets Lakshmi, forced by her abusive criminal stepmother and stepbrother Ranjeet to act as [[Bait (luring substance)|bait]] to protect her elderly, [[wheelchair]]-using grandmother. Together they take evidence and arrest the stepmother, and Amar gives sanctuary to Lakshmi and her grandmother in his home.

With Robert's gold Kishanlal became a wealthy crime lord in his own right. Kidnapping Robert's infant daughter Jenny in revenge for his lost family, he reared her as his 'niece' and sent her to school abroad. He also destroyed Robert's organization, forcing a grovelling Robert to work for him while begging for his daughter's return. In the middle of a smuggling operation Kishanlal's gang are raided by police: in the chaos Robert takes a crate of Kishanlal's smuggled gold and shoots Superintendent Khanna (Amar's 'father'), badly wounding him. With the gold Robert reinstates himself as a crime lord and plans to get Jenny back and kill Kishanlal, hiring Ranjeet in the process.

Jenny returns from abroad and Anthony falls head over heels in love with her during [[Easter Sunday]] services. Bharati, taken hostage by Robert, suffers a minor head injury while escaping;  miraculously, her sight is restored at a festival hosted by Akbar in honor of [[Sai Baba of Shirdi]]. Further, she identifies Akbar as Raju from a childhood photograph with Mr. Ilahabadi, while he recognizes her as the blind woman he drove home that fateful night.

Akbar rescues Salma and Taiyyab Ali from a [[Structure fire|house fire]] set by Taiyyab's blackmailing former mistress. A grateful Taiyyab gives permission for Akbar and Salma's marriage, leading to Akbar and Amar discovering they are brothers,  Kishanlal and Bharati their parents.

Events take a drastic turn when Kishanlal is double-crossed by Jenny's bodyguard Zebisco, who takes her to Robert in exchange for her hand in marriage. Father Gonsalves and Lakshmi see her abducted, but Lakshmi is also taken by Ranjeet and Robert murders Father Gonsalves – planting Kishanlal's [[Kali]] [[talisman]] as a 'clue.' Through Father Gonsalves' death Anthony discovers that Kishanlal and Bharati are his parents and Amar and Akbar his brothers.

The brothers take action to infiltrate Robert's [[mansion]]. Akbar, made suspicious when strangers try to collect Jenny's wedding dress, disguises himself as an elderly dithering tailor who goes with them to make last minute alterations. He sends a letter to his adopted father which Salma reads, and she joins him as his 'assistant/wife' to help the girls escape. Amar and Anthony waylay and impersonate the [[one-man band]] and Catholic priest on their way to Robert's to marry Jenny and Zebisco. Rejoicing in their reunion, the brothers celebrate as they go after Robert, Zebisco, Ranjeet and the remaining goons.

Happy that he is alive, Bharati is distraught to learn Kishanlal will go to prison for his crimes, but he reassures her: their reunited family is the only thing that matters to him. Superintendent Khanna, recovered from Robert's attack, briefly releases Kishanlal so that he may embrace his sons. The film ends with the brothers and their sweethearts joyfully driving into the sunset.

<!--https://books.google.com/books?id=VwKNAgAAQBAJ&printsec=frontcover-->''Amar Akbar Anthony'' has a cinematic antecedent in [[Yash Chopra]]'s 1965 film ''[[Waqt (1965 film)|Waqt]]'', in which a father's three sons are separated from each other. ''Waqt'' also inspired the 1976 super-hit diamond jubilee Pakistani film ''[[Talash (1976 film)|Talash]]'', starring [[Shabnam]] and [[Nadeem Baig (actor)|Nadeem]]. However, ''Amar Akbar Anthony'' was slated to release in 1975, prior to ''Talash'''s release.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Dasgupta|first1=Rohit K.|first2=Sangeeta|last2=Datta|title=100 essential Indian films|publisher=[[Rowman & Littlefield]]|date=2019|page=6|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=YT9xDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA1|isbn=9781442277984|access-date=19 November 2020|archive-date=31 August 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230831171001/https://books.google.com/books?id=YT9xDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA1|url-status=live}}</ref>

Prayag Raj wrote the film's screenplay, while [[Kader Khan]] wrote the dialogue.<ref name="mint">{{cite web|url=http://www.livemint.com/Leisure/toFX50k1D6H0dep49vQXVK/Excerpt--Amar-Akbar-Anthony.html|title=Excerpt: Amar Akbar Anthony|date=3 August 2013|website=[[Mint (newspaper)|Mint]]|access-date=6 August 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130806021336/http://www.livemint.com/Leisure/toFX50k1D6H0dep49vQXVK/Excerpt--Amar-Akbar-Anthony.html|archive-date=6 August 2013}}</ref>

The character of Anthony Gonsalves was named after the famous composer and teacher [[Anthony Gonsalves|of the same name]], whose pupils included Pyarelal (of [[Laxmikant–Pyarelal]], the composer duo of the film) and [[R. D. Burman]].<ref name="ndtv" /><ref name="Booth3">[[#Bo|Booth, p. 3]]</ref> Director Manmohan Desai had planned for Amitabh's character to be named “Anthony Fernandes,” with Bakshi's song entitled “My Name is Anthony Fernandes.” However, the song didn't go well with Laxmikant-Pyarelal. Composer Pyarelal then recalled his famous violin teacher and suggested that the character's last name be changed to “Gonsalves.”<ref name="ndtv" /><ref name="Booth5">[[#Bo|Booth, p. 5]]</ref> The nonsensical monologue preceding the "My Name Is Anthony Gonsalves" sequence was taken in part from an 1878 speech by British politician [[Benjamin Disraeli]] in reference to [[W. E. Gladstone]].<ref name="ndtv" />

===Filming===
''Amar Akbar Anthony'' was Manmohan Desai's first film as an independent film producer. The film was shot over a month at [[Ranjit Studios]] in [[Mumbai]]. Some exterior and interior shots were filmed at the [[Basilica of Our Lady of the Mount, Bandra|Mount Mary Church]] in [[Bandra]], Mumbai and at the Don Bosco School, Wadala, Mumbai respectively.<ref name="ndtv">{{cite web|title=10 things you didn't know about Amar Akbar Anthony|url=http://movies.ndtv.com/bollywood/10-things-you-didn-t-know-about-amar-akbar-anthony-358856|last=Roy|first=Gitanjali|date=8 May 2013|website=NDTV Movies|access-date=3 August 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130715073914/http://movies.ndtv.com/bollywood/10-things-you-didn-t-know-about-amar-akbar-anthony-358856|archive-date=15 July 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|last=San Miguel|first=Helio|title=Mumbai|publisher=Intellect Books|series=World Film Locations|date=2012|page=42|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Q0_v3jr6TCAC&pg=PA42|isbn=978-1-84150-632-6}}</ref> Shooting was scheduled so that the entire cast didn't have to appear together except for the climactic sequence and the title song ("Anhoni Ko Honi"), where they all perform as a group. However, the shooting went over schedule, which required [[Rishi Kapoor]] and [[Shabana Azmi]] to shoot their scenes separately so they could leave towards the end of production to work on other films.

==Analysis==
''Amar Akbar Anthony'' incorporates a strong element of secularism<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.thequint.com/entertainment/bollywood/muslims-in-the-movies-the-good-the-bad-the-khiji|title=Muslims in the movies: The good, the bad, and the Khilji|last=Mohamed|first=Khalid|date=31 January 2018|website=[[The Quint]]|access-date=3 February 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180202012739/https://www.thequint.com/entertainment/bollywood/muslims-in-the-movies-the-good-the-bad-the-khiji|archive-date=2 February 2018}}</ref> within a Bollywood masala film. Analysts such as Virdi (2003) and Kavoori & Punathambekar (2008) note that the themes of Desai's "magnum opus" include [[religious pluralism]] and [[Indian nationalism|secular nationalism]].<ref name="Virdi2003">{{cite book|last=Virdi|first=Jyotika|title=The cinematic imagiNation: Indian popular films as social history|publisher=[[Rutgers University Press]]|date=2003|page=36|url=https://archive.org/details/cinematicimagina0000vird|isbn=978-0-8135-3191-5}}</ref><ref name="KavooriPunathambekar2008">{{cite book|last1=Kavoori|first1=Anandam P.|first2=Aswin|last2=Punathambekar|title=Global Bollywood|publisher=[[New York University Press|NYU Press]]|date=2008|page=128|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2CqERCzWn5gC&pg=PA128|isbn=978-0-8147-2944-1}}</ref> [[Philip Lutgendorf]] hints that the separation of the three children on Indian Independence Day is akin to the [[Partition of India]].<ref name="uiowa">{{cite web|url=https://indiancinema.sites.uiowa.edu/amar-akbar-anthony|title=Amar Akbar Anthony|last=Lutgendorf|first=Philip|author-link=Philip Lutgendorf|date=2014|website=Indian cinema|publisher=[[University of Iowa]]|access-date=6 August 2013|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130627002402/http://www.uiowa.edu/~incinema/AmarAkbarAnthony.html|archive-date=27 June 2013}}</ref> Similarly, [[Vijay Mishra]] (2013) argues that the film reaffirmed India's "liberal ethos."
<ref>{{cite book|last=Mishra|first=Vijay|title=Bollywood cinema: Temples of desire|publisher=[[Routledge]]|date=2013|page=203|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=e_8OtRb2cggC&pg=PA203|isbn=978-1-135-31099-8}}</ref> The three religions represented by the titular characters are the "pillars of the nation:" when they work together, they can restore life to their mother (represented when they donate blood during the opening title sequence) and beat any evil (symbolised by their common villain).<ref name="Damsteegt2003">{{cite book|editor-last=Damsteegt|editor-first=Theo|title=Heroes and heritage: The protagonist in Indian literature and film|publisher=[[Leiden University]]|date=2003|page=217|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=tLPZAAAAMAAJ|isbn=978-90-5789-090-1}}</ref> The characters' reunion with their parents completes the [[Nationalism|nationalistic]] [[allegory]],<ref name="Virdi2003" /><ref name="KavooriPunathambekar2008" /> suggesting that what was lost at independence can be regained.<ref name="Nochimson2011">{{cite book|last=Nochimson|first=Martha P.|title=World on film: An introduction|publisher=[[Wiley (publisher)|John Wiley & Sons]]|date=23 September 2011|page=266|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=c3Kn7dsGGA0C&pg=PA266 |isbn=978-1-4443-5833-9}}</ref>

The film's masala style is evident in its plot and characters. According to Varia (2013), ''Amar Akbar Anthony'' was conceived as a tragedy but later incorporated many other genres.<ref>{{cite book|last=Varia|first=Kush|title=Bollywood: Gods, glamour, and gossip|publisher=[[Columbia University Press]]|date=2013|page=34|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DAtnAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA34|isbn=978-0-231-50260-3}}</ref> Dickson (2016) commented that the film featured a plot that was so complex that it would "give even [[William Shakespeare|Shakespeare]] migraines."<ref>{{cite book|last=Dickson|first=Andrew|title=Worlds elsewhere: Journeys around Shakespeare's globe|publisher=[[Henry Holt and Company]]|date=2016|page=200|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=RdaLCgAAQBAJ&pg=PA200|isbn=978-0-8050-9735-1|access-date=12 December 2019|archive-date=31 August 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230831171005/https://books.google.com/books?id=RdaLCgAAQBAJ&pg=PA200|url-status=live}}</ref> Some authors also highlight the archetypal character of the suffering and self-sacrificing mother (Roy).<ref name="Breckenridge1995">{{cite book|editor-last=Breckenridge|editor-first=Carol A.|editor-link=Carol Breckenridge|title=Consuming modernity: Public culture in a South Asian world|publisher=[[University of Minnesota Press]]|date=1995|page=166|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=t6Wbnd7XgbIC&pg=PA166|isbn=978-0-8166-2305-1}}</ref> However, [[Dinesh Raheja]] concludes that "ultimately, the show belongs to Amitabh Bachchan. In a tailor-made role, he has the audience in stitches. Despite his playing an implausible character, one quickly surrenders one's reservations in favour of a rollicking romp."<ref>{{cite web |last=Raheja |first=Dinesh |author-link=Dinesh Raheja |date=22 March 2003 |url=https://www.rediff.com/movies/report/dinesh/20030322.htm |title=Amar Akbar Anthony: Whoop-it-up fun! |website=[[Rediff.com]] |access-date=3 February 2018 |archive-date=24 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170724231921/http://www.rediff.com/movies/report/dinesh/20030322.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

''Amar Akbar Anthony''{{'}}s soundtrack was composed by [[Laxmikant–Pyarelal|Laxmikant-Pyarelal]], with lyrics penned by [[Anand Bakshi]]. It proved as popular and successful as the film itself.{{Citation needed|reason=Measure for determining the soundtrack's popularity and success is not given|date=November 2020}} The vinyl record, released on [[Polydor Records|Polydor]], was the first LP that was coloured pink.{{Citation needed|date=November 2020}}

Some of the biggest names in the Indian music industry of the time provided vocals for the songs:
* [[Kishore Kumar]] for [[Amitabh Bachchan]]
* [[Mukesh]] and [[Mahendra Kapoor]] for [[Vinod Khanna]]

==Release==
The [[The Emergency (India)|Emergency Period]] delayed the release of several of [[Manmohan Desai]]'s films. As a result, four of Desai's films, ''[[Dharam Veer (film)|Dharam Veer]]'', ''[[Chacha Bhatija]]'', ''[[Parvarish (1977 film)|Parvarish]],'' and ''Amar Akbar Anthony'', were released in 1977.<ref name="nytimes">{{cite news|last=Whitener|first=Brian|title=Amar Akbar Anthony|url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/146552/Amar-Akbar-Anthony/overview|access-date=6 August 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20071113014125/http://movies.nytimes.com/movie/146552/Amar-Akbar-Anthony/overview|department=Movies & TV Dept.|work=[[The New York Times]]|date=2007|archive-date=13 November 2007}}</ref><ref name="Ganti2013">{{cite book|last=Ganti|first=Tejaswini|title=Bollywood: A guidebook to popular Hindi cinema|publisher=[[Routledge]]|date=5 March 2013|page=223|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Elj3r-nAOFwC&pg=PA223|isbn=978-0-415-58384-8}}</ref> Incidentally, all of these would be amongst the top-grossing films of the year.<ref name=box/>

===Marketing===
For the film's marketing, [[eraser]]s with the images of Vinod Khanna, Rishi Kapoor, and Amitabh Bachchan were sold to students.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://m.filmfare.com/features/100-filmfare-days-49-amar-akbar-anthony-6373-1.html|title=100 Filmfare Days: 49- Amar Akbar Anthony|date=10 June 2014|website=[[Filmfare]]|access-date=3 February 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180201133749/https://m.filmfare.com/features/100-filmfare-days-49-amar-akbar-anthony-6373-1.html|archive-date=1 February 2018}}</ref> Posters, postcards, and song booklets of the film were sold in shops.{{Citation needed|date=November 2020}}  Colourful vests and metal crosses that were similar to the ones worn by Bachchan in the film achieved popularity.{{Citation needed|date=November 2020}}

==Reception==
The film grossed {{INRConvert|155|m|year=1977|mode=historical}} at the Indian box office and was the highest-grossing Bollywood film at the [[List of Bollywood films of 1977|Indian Box Office for the year 1977]].<ref name=box>{{cite web|url=http://boxofficeindia.com/showProd.php?itemCat=183&catName=MTk3Nw==|title=Box office 1977|date=2011|website=[[Box Office India]]|access-date=6 August 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121012163829/http://boxofficeindia.com/showProd.php?itemCat=183&catName=MTk3Nw%3D%3D|archive-date=12 October 2012}}</ref> It has since been regarded as one of the most iconic films of Indian cinema.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.livemint.com/Consumer/bQ0uThQTjybnvoYe13twlJ/70-iconic-films-of-Indian-cinema.html|title=70 iconic films of Indian cinema|date=18 August 2017|website=[[Mint (newspaper)|Mint]]|access-date=3 February 2018|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171026163502/http://www.livemint.com/Consumer/bQ0uThQTjybnvoYe13twlJ/70-iconic-films-of-Indian-cinema.html|archive-date=26 October 2017}}</ref>

Adjusted for inflation, the film has grossed approximately {{INR}}423 crores ($51 million) as of 2023.
