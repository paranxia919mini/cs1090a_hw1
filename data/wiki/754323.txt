'''''Josie and the Pussycats''''' is a 2001 [[Musical film|musical]] [[comedy film]] co-produced by [[Universal Pictures]] and [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Pictures]]. Written and directed by [[Harry Elfont]] and [[Deborah Kaplan]], the film is based on both the [[Josie and the Pussycats (comics)|Archie Comics series]] and the [[Josie and the Pussycats (TV series)|Hanna-Barbera cartoon]] of the same name. Filmed entirely in [[Vancouver]], Canada,<ref>{{cite web |title="It's dead on arrival": Josie and the Pussycats bombs at the box office |url=https://graffitiwithpunctuation.com/opinion/its-dead-on-arrival-josie-and-the-pussycats-bombs-at-the-box-officenbsp |website=Graffiti With Punctuation |publisher=graffitiwithpunctuation.com |access-date=26 February 2021 |archive-date=February 13, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220213124259/https://graffitiwithpunctuation.com/opinion/its-dead-on-arrival-josie-and-the-pussycats-bombs-at-the-box-officenbsp |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Siegel |first1=Tatiana |title='Top Five's' Rosario Dawson: What I Really Think of 'Josie and the Pussycats' and 5 Other Career Confessions |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/top-fives-rosario-dawson-what-755482 |website=Hollywood Reporter |publisher=The Hollywood Reporter |access-date=26 February 2021 |date=15 December 2014 |archive-date=April 17, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210417025741/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/top-fives-rosario-dawson-what-755482 |url-status=live }}</ref> the film features [[Rachael Leigh Cook]], [[Tara Reid]], and [[Rosario Dawson]] as the Pussycats, with [[Alan Cumming]], [[Parker Posey]], [[Gabriel Mann]], [[Paulo Costanzo]], and [[Missi Pyle]] in supporting roles.

A girl band, suddenly and inexplicably thrown into the spotlight with a number one hit, find themselves in the middle of a conspiracy to manipulate the public by delivering subliminal messages through pop music.

It was released in the United States on April 11, 2001 by [[Universal Pictures]] with the international distribution held by [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer]] (through [[20th Century Fox]]). The film's soundtrack album was well received, charting at No. 16 on the [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] and quickly achieving Gold status. However, the film itself received mixed reviews, and was a [[box-office bomb|commercial failure]] upon its initial release, grossing $14.9 million against a $22–39 million budget. It later gained some success as a [[cult film]].

==Plot==
 <!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->         
Successful [[boy band]] DuJour, signed to the pop music [[record label]] MegaRecords, are set to perform on a world tour. While on an airplane, they confront MegaRecords label executive Wyatt Frame to express concern about a strange backing track they have discovered on their single "DuJour Around the World". The band members subsequently get into a fight, and Wyatt parachutes out of the jet, leaving it to descend and presumably kill the band.

Landing outside the town of [[Riverdale (Archie Comics)|Riverdale]], Wyatt searches for a replacement band. He plays DuJour's most recent track at a music store, causing the young crowd to respond to the [[Subliminal stimuli|subliminal messaging]], and causes the kidnapping of a "free-thinking" individual who dislikes the music. Wyatt eventually encounters the Pussycats: lead vocalist and guitarist Josie McCoy, drummer Melody Valentine, and bassist and backup vocalist Valerie Brown. The struggling local [[Pop-punk|pop-punk band]] and their manager, Alexander Cabot, accept Wyatt's offer of a major record deal despite its seeming implausibility, and they fly to [[New York City]]. Wyatt renames the band "Josie and the Pussycats", arguing that the public needs to identify with the lead singer.

Meanwhile, MegaRecords CEO Fiona, in an underground meeting with world government representatives, details how the [[United States government]] has conspired with the music industry to hide subliminal messages in pop music to indoctrinate teenagers into buying trendy consumer products, thus helping to build a robust economy from the money the teenagers earn from minimum wage jobs such as babysitting. Musicians who discover the hidden messages are made to disappear via staged plane crashes, drug overdoses, controversial scandals, and similar disasters.

Josie and the Pussycats' first single "Pretend to Be Nice" is released and, due to subliminal messaging, instantly becomes successful, soon topping the charts. However, Valerie increasingly resents the attention the label gives Josie rather than the band as a whole, while Melody's behavioral perception makes her suspicious of Fiona and Wyatt. Fiona orders Wyatt to kill Valerie and Melody before they uncover the conspiracy. The Pussycats are supposedly sent to an appearance on [[MTV]]'s ''[[Total Request Live|TRL]]'' where [[Carson Daly]] and his impersonator attempt to kill them with baseball bats, though the girls survive due to their attackers' incompetence.

Rather than allow her to attend a gig by her friend Alan M., claiming it was cancelled, Wyatt instead gives Josie a copy of the group's latest single "You Don't See Me", which contains a subliminal message track designed to brainwash her into desiring a solo career. After arguing with her bandmates to the point where they leave, Josie realizes that the single caused the fight. Josie searches for Valerie and Melody, and with the help of Alexander and his sister, Alexandra, she confirms her suspicions when she uses a [[Mixing console|mixing board]] to isolate the subliminal track. However, Fiona and her cronies catch Josie unaware.

MegaRecords has organized a giant [[pay-per-view]] concert that will be [[Streaming media|streamed online]], wherein they plan to unleash a major subliminal message via themed cat-ears headsets that viewers must buy to hear the audio. Fiona and Wyatt plan for Josie to perform solo while holding Melody and Valerie hostage, threatening to kill them in a staged car explosion if Josie does not comply. However, the badly injured members of DuJour arrive and thwart the pair's plan. It is revealed that DuJour survived the plane crash by landing the plane in the middle of a [[Metallica]] concert, where they were severely assaulted by concertgoers, though Les of the group received fewer injuries due to his familiarity with "[[Enter Sandman]]". Les also reveals that they sent Melody a message earlier warning her to "Beware of the Music".

DuJour charges at Fiona, Wyatt, and their security guards only to stumble due to their injuries, giving Josie time to free Melody and Valerie. During the tussle, Fiona accidentally destroys the Megasound 8000, the machine used to generate the subliminal messages, revealing the new message to be one that would make Fiona universally popular. Fiona suffers a breakdown, revealing that her [[lisp]] made her a social outcast in high school. Wyatt recognizes Fiona as Lisa "Lithping Litha" Snyder, a former high school classmate, and reveals that his own appearance and British accent are a disguise. He is actually an [[Albinism in humans|albino]], and his high school nickname was "White Ass Wally". 

The government agents colluding with Fiona arrive. However, with the conspiracy exposed, they arrest the pair as scapegoats to cover up their involvement in the scheme. They reveal that they were going to turn on Fiona regardless, as the government intended to abandon the idea of spreading subliminal messages via music, having decided that movies were a far more effective format.

Josie, Valerie, and Melody perform the concert together. Alan M. arrives and confesses his love for Josie, who returns his feelings and kisses him onstage. The concert audience removes their headsets at Josie's suggestion and, able to judge the band on its own merits for the first time, roar their approval.

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film grossed $14,866,015 at the U.S. box office, less than its production budget, an estimated $22–39 million, resulting in a domestic [[box-office bomb|box office bomb]].<ref name="boxmojo" /> Kaplan and Elfont both later attributed the film's failure to their lack of consideration of the fact that the film would be marketed to pre-pubescent girls: "I don't know why we weren't thinking that based on the property, we kind of forgot that's who they were going to sell it to, but that's when that panic set in. They're not going to sell it to the people who are going to understand this movie, and the people they're selling it to aren't going to get it. And that's kind of what happened."<ref name="Wieselman"/>

===Critical reception===
The film received mixed reviews. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 54% based on reviews from 126 critics. The site's consensus states: "This live-action update of ''Josie and the Pussycats'' offers up bubbly, fluffy fun, but the constant appearance of [[product placement]]s seems rather hypocritical."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/josie_and_the_pussycats |title=Josie and the Pussycats |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=2026-04-20 |archive-date=September 18, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210918140309/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/josie_and_the_pussycats |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film scores a 47 out of 100, based on 29 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/josie-and-the-pussycats |title=Josie and the Pussycats |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=March 4, 2020 |archive-date=February 3, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220203115129/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/josie-and-the-pussycats |url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a B grade on a scale from A to F.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title=JOSIE AND THE PUSSYCATS (2001) B |website=[[CinemaScore]] |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date=2018-12-20 }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film one-half of a star out of a possible four, commenting that "Josie and the Pussycats are not dumber than the [[Spice Girls]], but they're as dumb as the Spice Girls, which is dumb enough".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/josie-and-the-pussycats-2001 |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Josie and the Pussycats movie review (2001) |date=April 11, 2001 |website=rogerebert.com |access-date=February 1, 2020 |archive-date=September 18, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210918134709/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/josie-and-the-pussycats-2001 |url-status=live }}</ref> Ebert had given the British girl group's 1997 feature film ''[[Spice World (film)|Spice World]]'' the same rating.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/spice-world-1998 |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |title=Spice World movie review & film summary (1998) |date=January 23, 1998 |website=rogerebert.com |access-date=March 23, 2023 |archive-date=March 23, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230323080106/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/spice-world-1998 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Joe Leydon]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' gave a positive review in which he praised the film's satire, music, and performances. Leydon wrote, "Sensationally exuberant, imaginatively crafted and intoxicatingly clever, ''Josie and the Pussycats'' shrewdly recycles a trifling curio of 1970s pop-culture kitsch as the linchpin for a freewheeling, candy-colored swirl of comicbook adventure, girl-power hijinks and prickly satirical barbs."<ref name="Leydon">{{cite news |last1=Leydon |first1=Joe |author-link=Joe Leydon |title=Josie and the Pussycats |url=https://variety.com/2001/film/reviews/josie-and-the-pussycats-1200467948/ |access-date=24 October 2023 |work=Variety |date=April 6, 2001}}</ref> He concluded, "To a degree that recalls the flashy [[Great Depression|Depression era]] musicals and the nuclear-nightmare horror shows of the '50s, pic vividly conveys key aspects of the zeitgeist without ever stinting on the crowdpleasing fun and games. It's made for the megaplexes, but it's also one for the time capsule."<ref name="Leydon" />

==Legacy and reappraisal==
In the years subsequent to its initial release, ''Josie and the Pussycats'' has been reappraised by critics, and has found success as a [[cult film]].<ref name="Mancuso">{{cite web |last1=Mancuso |first1=Vinnie |title=Not a Subliminal Message: 'Josie and the Pussycats' Is a Misunderstood Masterpiece |url=https://collider.com/why-josie-and-the-pussycats-is-underrated/ |website=Collider |access-date=24 October 2023 |date=April 12, 2021}}</ref> The film has been praised for its satirical take on [[Culture of the United States|American pop culture]], and for its prescience in satirizing [[product placement]] and the corporatization of the music industry.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/looking-back-at-josie-and-the-pussycats/|title=Looking back at Josie And The Pussycats|work=Den of Geek|access-date=2019-01-03|language=en|archive-date=September 18, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210918134707/https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/looking-back-at-josie-and-the-pussycats/|url-status=live}}</ref> A year after the film's release, Kaplan met [[U2]] frontman [[Bono]], who told her he loved the film.<ref name="Wieselman"/> Elfont later elaborated that Bono "totally got it. That was within a year of it coming out, when we still thought people were going to get it and it would open people's eyes up to the music business. ... Until social media, we would always say, 'Well, at least Bono got it.'"<ref name="Wieselman"/>

Evaluating the film for ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' in 2009, [[Nathan Rabin]] opined that the film is "funny, sly and sweet" and "a sly, sustained spoof of consumerism", rating the film as a "secret success".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.avclub.com/totally-jerking-case-file-147-josie-and-the-pussycats-1798218007 |title=Totally Jerking Case File 147:Josie and the Pussycats |publisher=The Onion |work=[[The A.V. Club]] |date=September 30, 2009 |access-date=2009-10-07 |archive-date=October 4, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091004183841/http://www.avclub.com/articles/totally-jerking-case-file-147-josie-and-the-pussyc,33511/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Mikael Wood of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote in 2017 that the film's "sharply satirical vision of the hyper-commercial record industry feels only more relevant."<ref>{{cite web |date=Sep 25, 2017 |last=Wood |first=Mikael |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/la-et-ms-josie-and-the-pussycats-20170925-story.html |title=Misunderstood upon its release, 'Josie and the Pussycats' was ahead of its time |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |access-date=2020-01-01 |archive-date=September 18, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210918134707/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/music/la-et-ms-josie-and-the-pussycats-20170925-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

On September 26, 2017, to commemorate the first vinyl release of the film's soundtrack, ''Josie and the Pussycats'' was screened by [[Alamo Drafthouse]] at the [[Ace Hotel]] in Los Angeles, followed by a Q&A panel with Kaplan, Elfont, Cook, Reid, and Dawson and a performance of songs from the film by Hanley.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Salud |first=April |date=September 27, 2017 |title=Josie and the Pussycats Rock LA Reunion Celebrating Vinyl Reissue & The Most Authentic Fake Band Ever |url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/concerts/7980908/josie-and-the-pussycats-reunion-los-angeles-recap |access-date=2018-01-03 |magazine=Billboard |archive-date=September 18, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210918140211/https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/concerts/7980908/josie-and-the-pussycats-reunion-los-angeles-recap |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.mtv.com/news/neuty5/josie-and-the-pussycats-kay-hanley-riverdale |last=Hughes |first=Hilary |title=Josie & The Pussycats Are Way More Than A Fictional Band — Just Ask Kay Hanley |website=[[MTV]] |date=November 22, 2017 |access-date=March 23, 2023 |archive-date=April 7, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230407123349/https://www.mtv.com/news/neuty5/josie-and-the-pussycats-kay-hanley-riverdale |url-status=dead }}</ref> Additionally, an [[oral history]] on the film was featured in ''[[The Fader]]''.<ref name="Mandel"/>

Author Maria Lewis released the podcast ''Josie and the Podcats'', which told the story of the film's production and its unlikely road to cult hit, in late 2020.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Josie and the Podcats - The definitive Josie and the Pussycats Podcast |url=https://www.oneheatminute.com/josie-and-the-podcats |access-date=2025-08-31 |website=One Heat Minute Productions |language=en-US}}</ref>

DuJour appears in the 2021 ''[[Robot Chicken]]'' special "The Bleepin' Robot Chicken Archie Comics Special", with Green, Meyer and Faison reprising their roles and Cook reprising her role of Josie.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Burlingame |first=Russ |date=April 21, 2021 |title=Robot Chicken's Archie Comics Special To Include Josie and the Pussycats Movie Reunion |url=https://comicbook.com/movies/news/robot-chicken-special-to-include-josie-and-the-pussycats-20th-an/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221229030222/https://comicbook.com/movies/news/robot-chicken-special-to-include-josie-and-the-pussycats-20th-an/ |archive-date=December 29, 2022 |access-date=March 11, 2023 |website=comicbook.com}}</ref>

Also in 2021, the book ''Best Movie Ever: An Oral History of Deborah Kaplan & Harry Elfont's Josie and the Pussycats'' was released.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Steiner |first=Chelsea |date=2021-02-16 |title=We'll Read This Oral History of 'Josie and the Pussycats' |url=https://www.themarysue.com/we-will-read-this-oral-history-of-josie-and-the-pussycats/ |access-date=2025-08-31 |website=The Mary Sue |language=en}}</ref>

==Home media==
''Josie and the Pussycats'' was released on [[VHS]] and [[DVD]] by [[Universal Studios Home Video]] on August 21, 2001. The film's theatrical PG-13 rating from the [[MPAA]] in the United States caused some contention with licenser [[Archie Comics]],<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://archiecomics.com/josies_house/movie_banners/movie_banner_template2.html |title=Heh, Josie, PG 13 -- say it isn't so!! What's up with that?!? |last=Silberkleit |first=Michael |date=2001-08-11 |website=archiecomics.com |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010811130900/http://www.archiecomics.com/josies_house/movie_banners/movie_banner_template2.html |archive-date=2001-08-11 |access-date=2020-04-10}}</ref> and a "Family-Friendly" PG-rated version was released alongside the theatrical version on home media. The "Family-Friendly" version omitted a great deal of profanity and sexual references. The theatrical version was presented in the Widescreen (1.85:1) format while the "Family-Friendly" version was presented in the Full Screen (1.33:1) format.

The movie was released on home media internationally by [[MGM Home Entertainment]] (through [[20th Century Fox Home Entertainment]]) on December 17, 2001.<ref>{{Cite web|url = https://www.amazon.co.uk/Josie-Pussycats-Rachael-Leigh-Cook/dp/B00005RESK|title = Josie and the Pussycats|website = Amazon UK|date = December 17, 2001|access-date = June 5, 2021|archive-date = June 5, 2021|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210605130357/https://www.amazon.co.uk/Josie-Pussycats-Rachael-Leigh-Cook/dp/B00005RESK|url-status = live}}</ref>

To coincide with the film's 20th anniversary, the film was released on [[Blu-ray]] for the first time through Mill Creek Entertainment on September 21, 2021, with most of the extras from the DVD release carried over.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=29220 |website=Blu-ray.com |date=September 2, 2021 |title=Josie and the Pussycats 20th Anniversary Edition Blu-ray |access-date=September 25, 2021 |archive-date=September 25, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210925230235/https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=29220 |url-status=live }}</ref>
