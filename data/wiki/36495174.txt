'''''The Bling Ring''''' is a 2013 American [[crime film]] written and directed by [[Sofia Coppola]]<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2013/jul/04/sofia-coppola-the-bling-ring-interview|title=Sofia Coppola on The Bling Ring: 'What these kids did really took ingenuity'|last=Gilbey|first=Ryan|website=[[The Guardian]]|date=July 4, 2013|access-date=May 16, 2017|archive-date=May 1, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170501195718/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2013/jul/04/sofia-coppola-the-bling-ring-interview|url-status=live}}</ref> featuring an [[ensemble cast]] led by [[Emma Watson]], [[Katie Chang]], [[Israel Broussard]], [[Taissa Farmiga]], Claire Julien, Georgia Rock and [[Leslie Mann]]. It is based on the 2010 ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]'' article "The Suspects Wore [[Christian Louboutin#Shoes|Louboutins]]" by [[Nancy Jo Sales]], which dealt with a real-life gang known as the [[Bling Ring]]. The story follows a group of fame-obsessed teenagers who use the internet to track celebrities' whereabouts to burgle their homes.

The film was an [[international co-production]] by producers in the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and Japan. Coppola began developing a screenplay based on the burglaries in December 2011, with [[Francis Ford Coppola]] executive producing through [[American Zoetrope]] and [[Roman Coppola]] as producer. She saw it as a departure from her previous works by focusing on a group of teenagers who are "products of our growing reality TV culture", exploring consumerist tabloid culture and obsession with fame.<ref name=DailyTelegraph/> Casting took place in early 2012 with Coppola choosing mostly young, unknown actors for the main roles. [[Principal photography]] occurred between March and April that same year in [[Los Angeles, California]]. [[Brian Reitzell]] served as supervisor for the film's soundtrack and co-wrote the musical score with [[Daniel Lopatin]].

''The Bling Ring'' had its world premiere on May 16, 2013, in the [[Un Certain Regard]] section of the [[2013 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="Cannes">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2013/04/bling-ring-cannes-2013-un-certain-regard-opening-film-477254/|title='Bling Ring' Will Open Cannes' Un Certain Regard Sidebar|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|date=April 17, 2013|access-date=April 18, 2013|archive-date=April 19, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130419092903/http://www.deadline.com/2013/04/bling-ring-cannes-2013-un-certain-regard-opening-film/|url-status=live}}</ref> In the United States, it had a [[limited theatrical release]] by [[A24]] on June 14, 2013, before opening [[wide release|wide]] one week later. It received generally positive reviews from critics, with many praising the cast and Coppola's "stylish" direction; Watson in particular received critical acclaim for her performance. Others criticized the film for its morally ambiguous approach towards the subject matter.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ryan |first1=Tim |title=Critics Consensus: Monsters University is Certified Fresh |url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/critics-consensus-monsters-university-is-certified-fresh/ |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=March 1, 2019 |date=June 21, 2013 |archive-date=November 28, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171128100420/https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/critics-consensus-monsters-university-is-certified-fresh/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The film grossed $20 million worldwide against its $8 million budget.

''The Bling Ring'' represents the final work of cinematographer [[Harris Savides]], who died of [[brain cancer]] while the film was in post-production, to whom the film is dedicated.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-bling-ring-2013|title=''The Bling Ring'' Movie Review & Film Summary (2013)|first=Ignatiy|last=Vishnevetsky|website=RogerEbert.com|date=June 21, 2013|access-date=September 28, 2013|archive-date=October 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131002115900/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-bling-ring-2013|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film faced criticism for its portrayal of race and departures from the real-life case. Critics accused the film of [[Whitewashing in film|whitewashing]] and omitting the perspectives of immigrants and people of color.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Jones |first=Ellen E. |date=2024-02-02 |title=Beyond the pale: where are all the films about ‘whiteness’? |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2024/feb/02/films-about-whiteness-race-sofia-coppola-wes-anderson |access-date=2026-06-15 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=June 20 |first=Meagan Hatcher-Mays {{!}} |last2=2013 {{!}} 8:30pm |title=How Sofia Coppola Whitewashed The Bling Ring |url=https://www.jezebel.com/how-sofia-coppola-whitewashed-the-bling-ring-514020537 |access-date=2026-06-15 |website=Jezebel |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Plot==
Quiet teenager Marc Hall transfers to [[Indian Hills High School (Agoura Hills, California)|Indian Hills High School]] in [[Agoura Hills, California]]. He befriends fame-obsessed Rebecca Ahn. While at a party at her house, Rebecca persuades Marc to sneak away with her and steal valuables from unlocked vehicles on the streets. As they begin hanging out regularly, Marc starts to view Rebecca as a sister figure.

One day, when Marc mentions that a wealthy acquaintance of his is vacationing in [[Jamaica]], Rebecca convinces Marc to join her in breaking into the empty house, where she steals money, a handbag similar to one that her idol [[Lindsay Lohan]] has, cash and the keys to a [[Porsche]], that they use to flee the scene. They go on a shopping spree with the money, buying luxury items. Marc visits a nightclub with Rebecca and meets her friend Chloe Tainer, along with sisters Nicki and Sam Moore. The group notices celebrities [[Kirsten Dunst]] and [[Paris Hilton]]. Later, while researching Paris on the internet, Marc and Rebecca learn she is out of town. They find her address, go to her house and on finding a key under the doormat, [[Breaking and entering|break in]]. They go through Paris's belongings and take some of her jewelry, including a bracelet that Rebecca later flaunts to Nicki, Sam, and Chloe at a party.

At Nicki's request, Rebecca and Marc take her, Sam, and Chloe back to Paris's house. They marvel at Paris's lavish lifestyle and steal shoes, bags, dresses, cash, and jewelry. A drunk Chloe gets into an accident while driving away; she is arrested and sentenced to community service. Marc and Rebecca return to rob Paris's house a third time and are nearly discovered by security. They then rob the home of [[Audrina Patridge]], using the internet to determine when she will not be home, and later sell unwanted items at an outdoor market.

The group burgles the home of [[Megan Fox]], Nicki's younger sister Emily squeezing through a [[pet door]] to get in. Nicki discovers a handgun inside, and Sam playfully threatens Marc with it before taking it to her boyfriend Rob's home. When he grabs her, the gun goes off, though no one is injured. The group later robs the home of [[Orlando Bloom]] and his girlfriend, [[Miranda Kerr]]. Marc finds a case filled with seven of Orlando's [[Rolex watches]] and a roll of cash. Chloe helps Marc sell the watches to her friend Ricky, a nightclub manager. The teens later return to Paris's house with Rob, who finds and steals large amounts of jewelry.

A news report containing [[Closed-circuit television|security]] footage from the robbery at Patridge's home concerns Marc, but Rebecca is undeterred. She instigates a burglary at the home of [[Rachel Bilson]], and the group ultimately breaks into Lindsay Lohan's house and robs it. Shortly thereafter, Rebecca moves to Las Vegas with her father, ostensibly due to troubles at home, leaving some of her stolen items with Marc. Media coverage of the [[Hollywood Hills]] burglaries intensifies, the public labeling the criminals "The Bling Ring". The authorities identify the group using security footage, statements from peers who heard the teens brag about their exploits, and photographs of the stolen items posted by the group on social media sites. Marc, Nicki, Chloe, Rebecca, Rob, and Ricky are arrested; Sam, who was not caught on camera, is able to avoid suspicion.

A remorseful Marc cooperates with the police, informing them of details of the burglaries, much to the chagrin of Rebecca, whom he identifies as the ringleader. A ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]'' journalist interviews Marc, still apparently exhibiting regret for his actions, but amazed at how much attention he has got and Nicki, who claims the others were at fault and she is a religious humanitarian. When the group is prosecuted, they receive varying amounts of jail time and are ordered to pay millions of dollars in [[restitution]] for the stolen items. Marc is transported to prison, alone among various older criminals.

Some time later, Nicki recounts her 30 days in jail on a talk show and reveals that Lindsay was in the same facility at the same time. She then turns to the camera and promotes her website, where people can learn everything about her "journey".

==Production==
===Development===
In December 2011, it was reported that [[Sofia Coppola]] was developing a screenplay for a film based on the Bling Ring burglaries, to be directed and produced by herself. Her father, [[Francis Ford Coppola]], executive produced the project through his [[American Zoetrope]] production company.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.huffingtonpost.com/2011/12/05/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring_n_1129890.html|title=Sofia Coppola's Bling Ring Film In Development: Report|website=[[The Huffington Post]]|first=Lucy|last=Blodgett|date=December 5, 2011|access-date=April 17, 2020|archive-date=August 28, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170828125518/http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2011/12/05/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring_n_1129890.html|url-status=live}}</ref> In April 2012, it was announced that financing had been set up with [[NALA Films]] and [[Roman Coppola]] would also serve as a producer.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2012/film/news/financing-set-for-sofia-coppola-film-1118052194/|title=Financing set for Sofia Coppola film|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|first=Dave|last=McNary|date=April 2, 2012|access-date=December 11, 2017|archive-date=December 16, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171216102016/http://variety.com/2012/film/news/financing-set-for-sofia-coppola-film-1118052194/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Coppola  described the [[Bling Ring|group of teenage criminals]] as "products of our growing reality TV culture".<ref name=DailyTelegraph>{{Cite news|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/10126910/Sofia-Coppola-interview-The-Bling-Ring-isnt-my-world.html|title=Sofia Coppola interview: 'The Bling Ring isn't my world'|last=Hiscock|first=John|journal=The Daily Telegraph|date=July 4, 2013|access-date=April 15, 2018|language=en-GB|issn=0307-1235|archive-date=April 17, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180417110919/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/10126910/Sofia-Coppola-interview-The-Bling-Ring-isnt-my-world.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The female characters in the film were seen as a departure from Coppola's previous works centered around the female perspective. Discussing the difference between the female perspective in ''Lost in Translation'' versus ''The Bling Ring'' she says that instead of a woman trying to find herself in a new foreign country, ''The Bling Ring'' deals with "girls trying on other people's stuff to find themselves". Although ''The Bling Ring'' deals with more consumerist and gaudy sense of style and culture Coppola says the film was "just really fun to indulge this style that's so different from my own. I'm more associated with being understated and [with] good taste, I think, and it's fun to be really obnoxious."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/style/sofia-coppola-talks-about-the-bling-ring/2013/06/13/ddff23ee-d376-11e2-a73e-826d299ff459_story.html|title=Sofia Coppola talks about 'The Bling Ring'|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|language=en|access-date=November 16, 2018|archive-date=November 17, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181117063154/https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/style/sofia-coppola-talks-about-the-bling-ring/2013/06/13/ddff23ee-d376-11e2-a73e-826d299ff459_story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Filming===
Production primarily took place in and around [[Los Angeles, California]], in March and April 2012, notably in [[West Hollywood]], [[Lynwood, California|Lynwood]], and [[Venice, Los Angeles|Venice]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.popsugar.com/Emma-Watson-Bling-Ring-Set-LA-Pictures-22344795|title=Emma Watson on The Bling Ring Set in LA|website=Pop Sugar|first=Lindsay|last=Miller|date=March 23, 2012|access-date=February 27, 2013|archive-date=May 1, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130501091424/http://www.popsugar.com/Emma-Watson-Bling-Ring-Set-LA-Pictures-22344795|url-status=live}}</ref> Some scenes were filmed in the celebrity victims' homes and at the Century Regional Detention Facility in Lynwood, California.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/movies/article/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-goes-a24-73381|title=Sofia Coppola's 'The Bling Ring' Goes to A24|website=[[TheWrap]]|first=Lucas|last=Shaw|date=January 16, 2013|access-date=April 17, 2020|archive-date=October 20, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161020064317/http://www.thewrap.com/movies/article/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-goes-a24-73381/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was shot digitally using [[Red Epic|RED Epic]] cameras.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Cinematographer |first=British |date=2015-05-27 |title=Christopher Blauvelt / The Bling Ring |url=https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/christopher-blauvelt-the-bling-ring/ |access-date=2026-03-28 |website=British Cinematographer |language=en-GB}}</ref>

Cinematographer [[Harris Savides]] was diagnosed with brain cancer in 2010. During production, his illness forced him to exit the production, and he was replaced by his longtime camera operator [[Christopher Blauvelt]].<ref name=":0" /> Savides and Blauvelt are jointly credited as cinematographer on the final film. Savides died in October 2012, shortly after principal photography wrapped, and the film was dedicated to his memory.<ref name=":0" />

'''''The Bling Ring: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack''''' was supervised by frequent Coppola collaborator [[Brian Reitzell]]. The soundtrack album was released on June 11, 2013, by [[Def Jam Recordings]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/The-Bling-Ring-Original-Soundtrack/dp/B00D3R2I48/|title=The Bling Ring: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack [Explicit] [+digital booklet]|website=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=July 25, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130725224312/http://www.amazon.com/The-Bling-Ring-Original-Soundtrack/dp/B00D3R2I48|url-status=live}}</ref> It contains a mix of music ranging between such genres as [[Hip hop music|hip-hop/rap]], [[krautrock]], and [[Electronic music|electronic]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Pelly|first=Jenn|title=Sofia Coppola's Bling Ring Features Kanye, Frank Ocean, M.I.A., More, Score by Oneohtrix Point Never|url=https://pitchfork.com/news/50774-sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-features-kanye-frank-ocean-mia-more-score-by-oneohtrix-point-never/|website=[[Pitchfork Media]]|access-date=June 12, 2013|date=May 16, 2013|archive-date=June 10, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130610095429/http://pitchfork.com/news/50774-sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-features-kanye-frank-ocean-mia-more-score-by-oneohtrix-point-never/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The musical score for the film was written by Reitzell in collaboration with Daniel Lopatin, known mostly under the recording name of [[Oneohtrix Point Never]].<ref>{{cite web|title=The Bling Ring Movie Soundtrack To Be Released June 11th By Def Jam Recordings|url=http://www.islanddefjam.com/artist/news_single.aspx?nid=13401&artistID=7389|website=[[Def Jam Recordings]]|access-date=June 12, 2013|date=May 16, 2013|archive-date=November 18, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231118134959/https://www.universalmusic.com/?nid=13401&artistID=7389|url-status=live}}</ref> Coppola's husband's band, [[Phoenix (French band)|Phoenix]], also contributed the title track from their album ''[[Bankrupt!]]''.<ref name="RollingStone">{{cite magazine|last=Mertens|first=Max|title=Inside 'The Bling Ring' Soundtrack|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/news/secrets-of-the-bling-ring-soundtrack-20130606|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|access-date=June 12, 2013|date=June 6, 2013|archive-date=June 13, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130613051646/http://www.rollingstone.com/movies/news/secrets-of-the-bling-ring-soundtrack-20130606|url-status=live}}</ref>

Reitzell worked closely with Coppola to find contemporary music that would fit within the film's setting. After being contacted for song contributions, rapper [[Kanye West]] recommended Reitzell use [[Frank Ocean]]'s then-unreleased "Super Rich Kids."

==Distribution==
[[File:Sofia Coppola Cannes 2013.jpg|thumb|200px|Coppola at the 2013 Cannes Film Festival for the premiere]]

===Release===
In January 2013, [[A24]] acquired domestic distribution rights to the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2013/01/a24-acquires-sofia-coppola-directed-bling-ring-405943/|title=''The Bling Ring'' release date – June Target for Sofia Coppola Film|date=January 16, 2013|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|first=Mike Jr.|last=Fleming|access-date=October 5, 2015|archive-date=October 6, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151006040900/http://deadline.com/2013/01/a24-acquires-sofia-coppola-directed-bling-ring-405943/|url-status=dead}}</ref> It opened the [[Un Certain Regard]] section of the [[2013 Cannes Film Festival]] on May 16.<ref name="Cannes"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.huffingtonpost.com/2013/05/16/bling-ring-reviews_n_3285576.html|title='The Bling Ring' Reviews: Sofia Coppola's Burglar Bunch Film Debuts At Cannes|date=May 16, 2013|website=[[The Huffington Post]]|first=Christopher|last=Rosen|access-date=June 8, 2013|archive-date=June 3, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130603103414/http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2013/05/16/bling-ring-reviews_n_3285576.html|url-status=live}}</ref> About the premiere in [[Cannes]], Coppola said, "It seems like the perfect setting for ''The Bling Ring'' when you see people walking around in their heels. It's a glamorous place, so it feels appropriate." The film closed the 39th [[Seattle International Film Festival]] on June 9, 2013.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/movies/column-post/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-close-seattle-film-festival-88476|title=Sofia Coppola's 'The Bling Ring' to Close Seattle Film Festival|website=[[TheWrap]]|date=April 30, 2013|access-date=June 8, 2013|archive-date=May 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130502013632/http://www.thewrap.com/movies/column-post/sofia-coppolas-bling-ring-close-seattle-film-festival-88476|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2013/04/bling-ring-2013-seattle-film-fest-closing-night/|title='Bling Ring' To Close 2013 Seattle Film Fest|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|date=April 30, 2013|access-date=June 8, 2013|archive-date=January 1, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140101022244/http://www.deadline.com/2013/04/bling-ring-2013-seattle-film-fest-closing-night/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Home media===
''The Bling Ring'' was released via [[digital distribution|digital download]] on September 6, 2013, and on [[DVD]] and [[Blu-ray]] on September 17, 2013, by [[Lionsgate Home Entertainment]]. The extras include a behind the scenes making-of documentary, a short piece about the real [[Bling Ring]], and a tour by Bling Ring target [[Paris Hilton]] of her home (which was a crime scene as well as a filming location).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/The-Bling-Ring/dp/B00DYVUBYM/|title=The Bling Ring (2013)|website=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=August 6, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130806134059/http://www.amazon.com/The-Bling-Ring/dp/B00DYVUBYM|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
In its debut weekend in the United States, the film opened in five theaters and earned $214,395, for a per-theater average of $42,879.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2013/06/specialty-box-office-bling-ring-sparkles-522102/|title=Specialty Box Office: 'Bling Ring' Sparkles With A Haute Debut|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|first=Brian|last=Brooks|date=June 16, 2013|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=July 24, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130724060420/http://www.deadline.com/2013/06/specialty-box-office-bling-ring-sparkles/|url-status=live}}</ref> It was Coppola's best opening per-theater average, beating out ''[[Lost in Translation (film)|Lost in Translation]]''{{'s}} intake of $40,221 for each of 23 locations in 2003. The following weekend, ''The Bling Ring'' expanded to 650 theaters, earning $2 million, for a per-theater average of $3,080.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/news/?id=3695&p=.htm|title=Weekend Report: 'Monsters' and Zombies Both Win On Busy Weekend|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|first=Ray|last=Subers|date=June 23, 2013|access-date=April 17, 2020|archive-date=June 26, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130626112326/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/news/?id=3695&p=.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> The film went on to gross $5.8 million domestically and $20 million worldwide.<ref name="BOM"/>

===Critical response===
[[Review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported that 59% of 207 critics gave the film a positive review, with an average rating of 6.3/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "While it's certainly timely and beautifully filmed, ''The Bling Ring'' suffers from director Sofia Coppola's failure to delve beneath the surface of its shallow protagonists' real-life crimes."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_bling_ring_2013/|title=''The Bling Ring (2013)''|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=January 9, 2026|archive-date=August 8, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200808004521/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_bling_ring_2013|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] has assigned the film an average score of 66 out of 100, based on 40 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-bling-ring|title=''The Bling Ring''|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=October 14, 2013|archive-date=October 5, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005033143/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-bling-ring|url-status=live}}</ref> The film has drawn comparisons to the [[Day-Glo Color Corp.|Day-Glo]] cinematography of [[Harmony Korine]]'s ''[[Spring Breakers]]'', which was also released under A24 Films.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/the-bling-ring-at-cannes-film-festival-2013-sofia-coppolas-movie-about-celebrity-starring-emma-watson-opens-8620319.html|title=The Bling Ring at Cannes Film Festival 2013: Sofia Coppola's movie about celebrity starring Emma Watson opens|website=[[The Independent]]|first=Jake|last=Coyle|date=May 17, 2013|access-date=June 8, 2013|archive-date=June 8, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130608024452/http://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/the-bling-ring-at-cannes-film-festival-2013-sofia-coppolas-movie-about-celebrity-starring-emma-watson-opens-8620319.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[File:Emma Watson 2, 2013.jpg|thumb|left|upright|[[Emma Watson]]'s performance received critical acclaim.]] 
[[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' had a positive opinion, writing, "Watching ''The Bling Ring'', the audience is invited to understand the impulses of these child-woman thieves, even as Coppola stands firmly apart from their craziness and sees them for who they are."<ref name="OGleiberman">{{cite magazine|url=http://insidemovies.ew.com/2013/05/16/cannes-girls-gone-wild-in-the-bling-ring/|title=Cannes 2013: The girls have gone wild in 'The Bling Ring,' Sofia Coppola's most provocative film yet|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=May 16, 2013|first=Owen|last=Gleiberman|access-date=June 7, 2013|archive-date=June 7, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130607072351/http://insidemovies.ew.com/2013/05/16/cannes-girls-gone-wild-in-the-bling-ring/|url-status=live}}</ref> Robbie Collin of ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' stated, "Everything comes together for the good here: visuals, performances, raucous soundtrack, Coppola's teasing flirtation with, yet ultimate lack of commitment to, some kind of concrete morality."<ref>{{cite web|title=Cannes 2013: The Bling Ring, review|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/cannes-film-festival/10061235/Cannes-2013-The-Bling-Ring-review.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130517102630/http://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/cannes-film-festival/10061235/Cannes-2013-The-Bling-Ring-review.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 17, 2013|first=Robbie|last=Collin|date=May 16, 2013|website=[[The Daily Telegraph]]|access-date=June 7, 2013}}</ref> Todd McCarthy of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' was less positive, stating, "Coppola's attitude toward her subject seems equivocal, uncertain; there is perhaps a smidgen of social commentary, but she seems far too at home in the world she depicts to offer a rewarding critique of it."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movie/bling-ring/review/523429|title=The Bling Ring: Cannes Review|first=Todd|last=McCarthy|date=May 16, 2013|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=June 7, 2013|archive-date=June 7, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130607105228/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movie/bling-ring/review/523429|url-status=live}}</ref>

Watson's performance as Nicki Moore was critically acclaimed. [[Richard Roeper]] called her "comedic gold," <ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.richardroeper.com/reviews/theblingring.aspx|title=The Bling Ring Review|website=RichardRoeper.com|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=March 23, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160323223616/http://www.richardroeper.com/reviews/theblingring.aspx|url-status=live}}</ref> while Gleiberman wrote that Watson "proves that her willingness to take chances is only growing and that she's an actress serious enough to turn a line like 'Your butt looks awesome!' into something that reveals character." Cath Clarke of ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' commented positively on Watson, saying, "The real story here isn't the good-girl-goes-bad stunt casting; it's that Watson can act. Against the odds, the ''[[Harry Potter (film series)|Harry Potter]]'' star gives a sharp, knowing smart performance as Nicki."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.timeout.com/london/film/the-bling-ring|title=The Bling Ring (15)|date=July 2, 2013|first=Cath|last=Clarke|access-date=August 1, 2013|website=Time Out|archive-date=July 31, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130731085434/http://www.timeout.com/london/film/the-bling-ring|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' wrote, "Watson is sensational as Nicki, an underage club girl and actress wanna-be, who lives in a universe of [[Valley girl|Valley Girl]] narcissism eons away from Hogwarts."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-bling-ring-20130613|title=The Bling Ring|date=June 13, 2013|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|first=Peter|last=Travers|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=June 17, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130617015203/http://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-bling-ring-20130613|url-status=live}}</ref> Even critics who gave the film overall negative reviews singled out Watson for praise, with Peter Howell of the ''[[Toronto Star]]'' stating, "The undistinguished young cast of ''The Bling Ring'' has just one standout, and that's Emma Watson, who plays one of the most vacuous of the juvenile thieves. We know her best as the brainy Hermione Granger from the ''Harry Potter'' movies, and she can obviously do brainless equally well."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.thestar.com/entertainment/movies/2013/06/21/the_bling_ring_review_lifestyles_of_the_vacant_and_greedy.html|title=The Bling Ring review: Lifestyles of the Vacant and Greedy|date=June 21, 2013|first=Peter|last=Howell|access-date=August 1, 2013|website=[[Toronto Star]]|archive-date=August 21, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130821044551/http://www.thestar.com/entertainment/movies/2013/06/21/the_bling_ring_review_lifestyles_of_the_vacant_and_greedy.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Joe Neumaier of ''[[New York Daily News]]'' wrote, "Watson, though, does a great imitation of hollow-eyed gaze; her character is the one who tries to parlay notoriety into success (everyone else can, she figures). The one-time ''Harry Potter'' star captures the slack-jawed fan only too well."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nydailynews.com/entertainment/tv-movies/bling-ring-movie-review-article-1.1371518|title='The Bling Ring': movie review|website=[[Daily News (New York)|New York Daily News]]|date=June 13, 2013|first=Joe|last=Neumaier|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304114426/http://www.nydailynews.com/entertainment/tv-movies/bling-ring-movie-review-article-1.1371518|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Criticism===
The Bling Ring received criticism from several commentators regarding its portrayal of race and its adaptation of the real-life "Bling Ring" burglaries.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=2013-06-13 |title=The Immigrant You Won't See in Sofia Coppola's 'Bling Ring' {{!}} Colorlines |url=https://colorlines.com/articles/immigrant-you-wont-see-sofia-coppolas-bling-ring |access-date=2026-06-15 |website=colorlines.com |language=en}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[Jezebel (website)|Jezebel]]'', Meagan Hatcher-Mays argued that the film minimized the racial diversity of the actual group and centered its narrative on white participants, describing the adaptation as an example of [[Whitewashing in film|whitewashing]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=June 20 |first=Meagan Hatcher-Mays {{!}} |last2=2013 {{!}} 8:30pm |title=How Sofia Coppola Whitewashed The Bling Ring |url=https://www.jezebel.com/how-sofia-coppola-whitewashed-the-bling-ring-514020537 |access-date=2026-06-15 |website=Jezebel |language=en-US}}</ref> In ''[[ThinkProgress]]'', Alyssa Rosenberg argued that the film's depiction of affluent white teenagers engaging with [[hip-hop culture]] raised questions about [[white privilege]] and [[cultural appropriation]], while giving limited attention to the racial dynamics surrounding the real events.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rosenberg |first=Alyssa |date=2013-07-02 |title=‘The Bling Ring,’ White Privilege, And The Appropriation Of Hip-Hop |url=https://archive.thinkprogress.org/the-bling-ring-white-privilege-and-the-appropriation-of-hip-hop-cb8f7a2c918c/ |access-date=2026-06-15 |language=en-US}}</ref> Aura Bogado of [[ColorLines|''Colorlines'']] criticized the film for omitting the perspectives of immigrants and people of color affected by the burglaries, particularly Colombian immigrant Diana Tamayo, whose experiences were not depicted in the film. Bogado argued that the omission obscured issues of race, class, and immigration that were present in the real-life case.<ref name=":1" />
