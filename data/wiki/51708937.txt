'''''The Death of Louis XIV''''' ({{langx|fr|'''La Mort de Louis XIV'''}}) is a 2016 [[historical drama]] film directed by [[Albert Serra]] and starring [[Jean-Pierre Léaud]]. Set in 1715, it depicts the final days of [[Louis XIV]] of France. The film had its world premiere at the [[2016 Cannes Film Festival]] on 19 May 2016.<ref name="festival-cannes"/> It was released in France on 2 November 2016<ref name="allocine"/> and received positive reviews from critics.

==Plot==
After returning from the hunt on 9 August 1715, [[Louis XIV]] lies in his bed. Due to his deteriorating cardiovascular health, an [[embolism]] in his leg becomes gangrenous. From that point on, he becomes increasingly frail and unable to perform his royal duties.
Although he temporarily rallies, he relapses rapidly and acquires a fever due to the infection. Word of the King's worsening medical condition spreads through the Court at [[Versailles]], from which his chief physician forbids further travel. 
The Royal Family tries to censor the flow of information about the King's deterioration. Four doctors from the [[University of Paris]] medical faculty are then summoned to provide diagnosis and care for the ailing monarch, but [[gangrene]] is only diagnosed when his chief doctor, [[Georges Mareschal]], makes an incision in his diseased leg and diagnoses it from his subsequent analysis of the wound site. Although Mareschal advises [[amputation]], the King refuses to contemplate it and starts preparation for his imminent death instead. He receives the [[last sacraments]] of confession and the rite of [[Anointing|final unction]], while also organizing a subsequent regency for his young great-grandson, the future King [[Louis XV]] with the assistance of his wife [[Madame Maintenon]] and [[Louis Auguste, Duke of Maine]], one of his older [[illegitimate]] children, who is appointed regent until the five year old Louis XV reaches maturity.
[[François de Neufville, 2nd Duke of Villeroy]] is appointed Louis XV's legal guardian until his maturity, after which the dying monarch provides his successor with advice about how to rule wisely after his death. Several [[Quackery|quacks]] attempt to 'heal' the King, but he is now succumbing rapidly as his gangrene worsens. On 30 August, he lapses into a [[coma]], before his eventual death on 1 September 1715, at 8.15 am. 
After a [[post mortum]] medical [[autopsy]], his heart and intestines are separated from the rest of his body as his earlier wishes dictated. On 9 September 1715, after lying in state, his body is interred within the [[Basilica of Saint-Denis]]<ref>{{cite web|title=The Death of Louis XIV {{!}} Press Kit |date=June 2016|url= http://enfilade18thcfiles.wordpress.com/2016/06/the-death-of-louis-xiv-press-kit-english.pdf|website=enfilade18thcfiles.wordpress.com}}</ref>

==Production==
Director [[Albert Serra]] and producer Thierry Lounas wrote the screenplay for the film.<ref name="cineuropa300123">{{cite web|url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/300123/|title=Albert Serra shooting Last Days of Louis XIV|website=Cineuropa|first=Fabien|last=Lemercier|date=15 October 2015|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> [[Jean-Pierre Léaud]] played the lead role.<ref name="cineuropa300123"/> For the film, Albert Serra did not have any rehearsals.<ref name="mubi">{{cite web|url=https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/discussing-the-death-of-louis-xiv-with-albert-serra|title=Discussing "The Death of Louis XIV" with Albert Serra|website=[[Mubi (streaming service)|Mubi]]|first=Daniel|last=Kasman|date=10 September 2016|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> Filming took place in the surroundings of the [[Château de Hautefort]] in 2015.<ref name="cineuropa300123"/> It took 15 days to shoot the film in total.<ref name="mubi"/>

==Release==
The film had its world premiere at the [[2016 Cannes Film Festival]] on 19 May 2016.<ref name="festival-cannes">{{cite web|url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/69-editions/retrospective/2016/actualites/articles/louis-xiv-s-death-throes-by-albert-serra|title=Louis XIV's death throes by Albert Serra|website=[[Cannes Film Festival]]|date=19 May 2016|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> It was released in France on 2 November 2016.<ref name="allocine">{{cite web|url=http://www.allocine.fr/film/fichefilm_gen_cfilm=234860.html|title=La Mort de Louis XIV|website=[[AlloCiné]]|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
On review aggregator website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 88% based on 56 reviews, and an average rating of 6.9/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''The Death of Louis XIV'' will frustrate viewers out of sync with its deliberate pace, but those with the patience to settle in may be rewarded with a thoughtful, finely detailed drama."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_death_of_louis_xiv_2017|title=The Death of Louis XIV (La mort de Louis XIV)|publisher=Rotten Tomatoes|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 76 out of 100, based on 16 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-death-of-louis-xiv|title=The Death of Louis XIV|publisher=Metacritic|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref>

Boyd van Hoeij of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' called the film "[Albert Serra's] most accessible work to date."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/death-louis-xiv-la-mort-895712|title='The Death of Louis XIV' ('La Mort de Louis XIV'): Cannes Review|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|first=Boyd van|last=Hoeij|date=19 May 2016|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> Glenn Kenny of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote, "As for Mr. Serra, while he often enjoys playing the foppish provocateur in his interviews, his film is sober, meticulous and entirely convincing in its depiction of period and mortality."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2017/03/30/movies/the-death-of-louis-xiv-review.html|title=Review: 'The Death of Louis XIV' Has a Riveting Performance at Its Heart|website=The New York Times|first=Glenn|last=Kenny|date=30 March 2017|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> Ben Kenigsberg of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote, "As minimalist as Serra's films can be, they are rarely boring, and often given to wry wit."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2016/film/reviews/the-death-of-louis-xiv-review-cannes-1201798961/|title=Film Review: 'The Death of Louis XIV'|work=Variety|first=Ben|last=Kenigsberg|date=20 June 2016|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref>

[[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film 5 out of 5 stars, writing, "At 73, Jean-Pierre Léaud gives what could be the performance of his career as Louis XIV."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/jul/12/the-death-of-louis-xiv-review-albert-serra-jean-pierre-leaud|title=The Death of Louis XIV review – a quietly amazing portrait of the end of life|website=The Guardian|first=Peter|last=Bradshaw|authorlink=Peter Bradshaw|date=12 July 2017|accessdate=14 March 2020}}</ref> Allan Hunter of ''[[Screen International]]'' also commented that "It is easily the actor's best role and most noteworthy performance in some time."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.screendaily.com/reviews/the-death-of-louis-xiv-review/5104215.article|title='The Death of Louis XIV': Review|website=[[Screen International]]|first=Allan|last=Hunter|date=19 May 2016|accessdate=27 September 2016}}</ref>
