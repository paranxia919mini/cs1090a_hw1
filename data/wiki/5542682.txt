'''''Possession''''' is a 1981 [[psychological horror]] [[psychological drama|drama]] film directed by [[Andrzej Żuławski]] and written by Żuławski and [[Frederic Tuten]]. The plot obliquely follows the relationship between an international spy ([[Sam Neill]]) and his wife ([[Isabelle Adjani]]), who begins exhibiting increasingly disturbing behavior after asking for a divorce.

''Possession'', an [[co-production (media)|international co-production]] between France and West Germany, was filmed in [[West Berlin]] in 1980. Żuławski's only English-language film, it premiered at the [[1981 Cannes Film Festival|34th Cannes Film Festival]], where Adjani won the [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] award for her performance. The screenplay was written during the painful divorce of Żuławski from actress [[Małgorzata Braunek]]. While not commercially successful either in Europe or in the United States, with the latter only receiving a heavily edited cut on its initial release, the film eventually acquired [[cult film|cult status]] and has been more positively appraised in later years.

== Plot ==
<!--Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700.-->
Mark is a [[Intelligence service|spy]] who returns home to [[West Berlin]] after a mission to discover that his wife, Anna, is seeing another man and wants to [[Marital separation|separate]]. Mark turns over their apartment and custody of their young son, Bob, to Anna. After a long drinking spree, Mark returns to find Bob alone and unkempt. He resolves to be Bob's primary caregiver and unsuccessfully pressures Anna to end her extramarital relationship with a phone call. Anna leaves during the night, allegedly to stay with her friend Margie.

Mark meets Bob's teacher, Helen, who inexplicably looks identical to Anna except for her lighter hair and green eyes. Mark visits and attacks Anna's lover, Heinrich, who beats him down. Mark returns home to find Anna with Bob, and the couple argue, culminating in Mark repeatedly striking Anna; she then leaves.

Mark hires a detective to tail Anna and interrogates Anna about their relationship, to which Anna responds by cutting her neck with an [[electric knife]]. Mark intervenes, treats her wound, and deliberately cuts his arm with the knife. The detective follows Anna to her derelict apartment and calls to inform Mark of the address. The detective poses as a safety inspector and searches the apartment. He finds a bizarre creature in the bathroom, after which Anna kills him. Mark withholds Anna's address from Heinrich. Helen helps Mark care for Bob and reveals that Bob has been experiencing [[Night terror|sleep terrors]]. After putting Bob to bed, Helen and Mark have sex.

Anna kills Zimmermann, the detective's work superior and romantic partner, after he finds the tentacled creature and the detective's body in her apartment. Heinrich leaves Mark footage of Anna hurting one of her ballet students by holding her in a difficult pose to test her willpower, telling Heinrich that their relationship is similar, and describing a conflict between Faith and Chance, as well as the pain that her affair will cause to Mark.

Anna returns and tells Mark that she is caring for her Faith in her apartment after violently miscarrying it in the [[Berlin U-Bahn|subway]] while he was gone. After Anna leaves, Mark reveals her address to Heinrich. Anna shows Heinrich the creature and dismembered corpses before stabbing him. Heinrich flees and calls Mark to arrange a meeting at a bar. Mark discovers the body parts at Anna's apartment, but Anna and the creature are gone. Mark meets Heinrich, kills him in the bar's toilet, and stages it as an accidental death. He sets Anna's apartment on fire and rides home on Heinrich's motorbike. He discovers that Anna has stolen Zimmerman's car and killed Margie, who saw the creature. Mark and Anna have sex. Anna takes the creature to Margie's place. Mark takes Bob to Helen's apartment, then drives to Margie's place, where he witnesses Anna having sex with the creature. Mark visits Heinrich's mother, who kills herself by overdosing on pills.

Mark resists pressure from two former associates to rejoin their mission. He later finds them approaching Margie's place with a police officer. Mark forces a taxi driver to ram the police car as a distraction to allow Anna to escape in Zimmermann's car, though Margie's body is dislodged from the trunk, and Mark is badly wounded in a shootout and motorbike accident while fleeing. He enters a building and climbs its staircase until he collapses. He is found by Anna and the creature, now a fully formed [[doppelgänger]] of Mark with green irises. Mark raises his gun to shoot it, but a hail of bullets from the police below strikes him and Anna down. Mark and Anna share a final kiss before Anna kills herself with his gun, and Mark jumps to his death. The doppelgänger climbs the stairs and mysteriously compels a woman to fire on the police so that he can escape.

Helen is babysitting Bob when her doorbell rings. Bob implores her not to open the door, but Helen ignores him and walks towards the door. Bob races to the bathroom and drowns himself in the bath. The sounds of sirens, planes, and explosions fill the air outside. Mark's doppelgänger presses his hands on the glass door pane as Helen stares straight ahead, her green eyes shining.

==Themes==
Trying to classify ''Possession'', critics drew parallels with [[Roman Polanski]]'s ''[[Repulsion (film)|Repulsion]]'' and [[David Cronenberg]]'s ''[[The Brood]]''. Despite being referred to as a [[psychological drama]],<ref name="timeout"/><ref name="blu"/> [[psychological horror]],<ref name="Collider"/> and [[supernatural horror]],<ref name="RemakeHR" /><ref name="RemakeEmp">{{Cite web |first=Jordan |last=King |date=2024-06-13 |title=Robert Pattinson Producing Possession Remake With Smile Director Parker Finn |url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/news/robert-pattinson-producing-possession-remake-with-smile-director-parker-finn/ |access-date=2024-06-13 |website=Empire |language=en}}</ref> the genre of the film is still a matter of controversy.<ref name="bfi"/><ref name="Wilkins 2011">{{cite web|url=https://slantmagazine.com/film/review/possession-5938|work=Slant Magazine|title=Possession: Film Review|date=29 November 2011|access-date=10 June 2014|last=Wilkins|first=Budd|archive-date=14 July 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140714181033/http://www.slantmagazine.com/film/review/possession-5938|url-status=live}}</ref>{{sfn|Pyzik|2014}}<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.filmjournal.com/content/film-review-possession|title=Film Review: Possession|work=[[Film Journal International]]|date=2 December 2011|access-date=19 March 2018|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180320105443/http://www.filmjournal.com/content/film-review-possession|archive-date=20 March 2018}}</ref> [[J. Hoberman]] commented, "Made with an international cast in still-divided Berlin, the movie starts as an unusually violent breakup film, takes an extremely yucky turn toward ''Repulsion''-style psychological breakdown, escalates into the avant-garde splatterific [[body horror]] of the '70s (''[[Eraserhead]]'' or ''The Brood''), and ends in the realm of pulp metaphysics as in ''[[I Married a Monster from Outer Space]]''."<ref name="vv"/>

A number of critics deny the creature with tentacles exists within physical reality: it may be a reflection of Anna's psychosis; the product of Mark's inflamed consciousness, unable to accept his wife's betrayal;<ref name="goddard"/> or a kind of revenge of the film's director ([[Andrzej Żuławski|Żuławski]]) traumatized by his own divorce from his ex-wife.<ref name="pyzik">{{cite web |author=Pyzik |first=Agata |date=2016-02-26 |title=Romantic Trauma: Andrzej Zulawski Remembered |url=http://thequietus.com/articles/19750-zulawski-article |access-date=2017-06-30 |publisher=The Quietus}}</ref>

Some who have written about ''Possession'' have paid attention to the motif of [[doppelgänger]] throughout the film. Both spouses die, but they are replaced by doubles, ideal models of husband and wife. Anna "grows" a double of Mark from the creature, an indefatigable lover who is always by her side. The real Mark finds a copy of Anna in the person of the school teacher Helen{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}she is a gentle character and does not demand anything from Mark, being an "ideal housewife".<ref name="bfi"/>{{sfn|Mazierska|Goddard|2014|p=247}}

=== Sociopolitical context ===
[[File:Berlin Wall death strip, 1977.jpg|220px|thumb|right|alt=A photograph showing the Czech hedgehogs and a guard tower at the Berlin Wall.|[[Berlin Wall]] in 1977]]
As in the case of ''[[The Devil (1972 film)|The Devil]]'', the director placed political subtext under the layer of expressive horror after deliberately choosing Berlin as it was the least remote point from Poland and [[Warsaw Pact|other countries of the European socialist bloc]]. The plot of ''Possession'' is not limited to an autobiographical description of a difficult breakup, separation and marital disintegration in family relations{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}at that time Żuławski also experienced a final separation from Poland.{{sfn|Mazierska|Goddard|2014|p=245}}<ref name="goddard">{{cite web |url=https://www.academia.edu/15266526 |author=Michael Goddard |title=Beyond Polish Moral Realism: The Subversive Cinema of Andrzej Żuławski |access-date=2017-06-16 |archive-date=12 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230112184859/https://www.academia.edu/15266526 |url-status=live }}</ref>{{efn|Żuławski himself tells more about this in an interview with Daniel Bird. The recording of the interview can be found in the supplementary material for the British DVD release of the film, published in 2010 by the company Second Sight, and also 2014 Blu-ray release by Mondo Vision.<ref name="goddard"/>}} Two houses in the film{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}the modern one, which is Mark and Anna's apartment, and the old abandoned house in [[Kreuzberg]], where Anna hides the squid-like creature{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}are located next to the Wall. The film contains elements of a [[spy thriller]]. Mark, an intelligence agent, leaves his job for his family. Anna leaves her family to become an "agent of the dark forces". The confrontation ends with death for both, and in the last frames of the film, there is a direct allusion (the sounds of sirens and the rumble of explosions) to the armed conflict that began in the city divided in two, which could end in a [[nuclear warfare|nuclear apocalypse]].{{sfn|Pyzik|2014}}<ref name="kinoart1">{{Cite magazine |url=http://old.kinoart.ru/archive/2009/10/n10-article18 |title=Шрам. Кино о Стене |trans-title=Scar. Cinema about the Wall |author=Ekaterina Petrovskaya |magazine=[[Iskusstvo Kino]] |issue=10 |year=2009 |language=ru |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220701225406/http://old.kinoart.ru/archive/2009/10/n10-article18 |archive-date=1 July 2022 |access-date=24 September 2020 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="goddard" />

Scholar Bartłomiej Paszylk writes that the metaphors present in the film also represent "a disintegrating country. The very fact that the film takes place in Cold War-era West Berlin is quite significant for the metaphor of divorce{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}the wall that separates it from East Berlin being a symbol of disconnection of what was once united{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}but [Żuławski's] additional intention might have been for the Berlin wall to symbolize the [[Iron Curtain]], and for Germany to symbolize Poland, a country he had to leave in order to keep making movies."{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=163}}

== Production ==
Żuławski approached [[Danièle Thompson]] and asked if she would work on a film. After receiving a script about 20 pages long, Thompson suggested [[Frederic Tuten]]; thus, Żuławski went to [[New York City|New York]] to meet him. They worked on the script for the film in New York and Paris while Żuławski was in a state of deep depression.<ref name="makingof">{{cite AV media|people=Bird, Daniel (Director)|year=2009|title=The Other Side of The Wall: The Making of POSSESSION|medium=Making-of documentary|url=https://vimeo.com/76870497|location=Germany|access-date=28 August 2020|publisher=Mondo Vision (Blu-ray)|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221216071322/https://vimeo.com/76870497|archive-date=16 December 2022|url-status=live}}</ref> In 1976, he divorced actress [[Małgorzata Braunek]]. Żuławski recalled how he once returned home late in the evening and found his five-year-old son Xavier alone in the apartment, smeared with jam, after his wife left him alone for several hours{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}this scene was directly reflected in ''Possession''.{{sfn|Żuławski|Kim|2011}} A year and a half later, following the authorities' halting of work on the film ''[[On the Silver Globe (film)|On the Silver Globe]]'' in 1978, the director faced a ''[[de facto]]'' ban and was forced to leave Poland. While emigrating, he continued to linger on suicidal thoughts, which initially had improved by starting to work on a new film.<ref>{{cite web |author=Szczypiński |first=Adrian |year=2006 |title=Na srebrnym globie. Epilog. |trans-title=On the silver globe. Epilogue. |url=http://www.film.org.pl/prace/na_srebrnym_globie/glob4.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160105034606/http://www.film.org.pl/prace/na_srebrnym_globie/glob4.html |archive-date=2016-01-05 |access-date=2017-06-15 |work=film.org |language=pl}}</ref>{{sfn|Pyzik|2014}}

Żuławski and the film's producer, Marie-Laure Reyre, immediately chose Isabelle Adjani as Anna. By this time, Adjani had already become a [[celebrity]], but the producers had reasons to expect that she would accept the offer. After an unsuccessful attempt to start a career in [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]] (released in 1978, [[Walter Hill]]'s ''[[The Driver]]'' failed at the box office), Adjani decided to return to [[European cinema]]. She starred in ''[[Nosferatu the Vampyre]]'' (1979), but it had not yet been possible to repeat her success in being nominated for the [[Academy Award]] for her role in ''[[The Story of Adele H.]]'' (1975). However, Adjani's management company turned down the offer, and the filmmakers chose the next candidate [[Judy Davis]], whose work in the film ''[[My Brilliant Career (film)|My Brilliant Career]]'' (1979) impressed Żuławski. [[Sam Neill]], a less well-known actor who appeared with Davis in the same film, was chosen for the role of Mark. Davis was hesitating over whether to take the role, so Adjani eventually accepted the offer.<ref name="tcm">{{cite web |url=https://www.tcm.com/this-month/article/443278 |title=Possession (1981) |author=Chris Fujiwara |publisher=Turner Classic Movies |access-date=2017-06-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180323155310/http://www.tcm.com/this-month/article/382629%7C443278/Possession.html |archive-date=23 March 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The role was emotionally exhausting for Adjani. In one of the interviews, she stated that it took her several years to recover from her performance, which [[J. Hoberman]] called "a veritable [[aria]] of [[hysteria]]".<ref name="vv">{{cite web |url=https://www.villagevoice.com/2011/11/30/extreme-sex-addiction-in-shame-extreme-everything-in-possession/ |title=Extreme Sex Addiction in Shame; Extreme Everything in Possession |work=The Village Voice |author=J. Hoberman |author-link=J. Hoberman |date=2011-11-30 |access-date=2017-06-15 |archive-date=13 September 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180913082317/https://www.villagevoice.com/2011/11/30/extreme-sex-addiction-in-shame-extreme-everything-in-possession/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> It was rumored that she attempted suicide after filming completed,<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Toronto International Film Festival]]|url=https://www.tiff.net/the-review/some-ways-of-looking-at-isabelle-adjani/|title=Some Ways of Looking at Isabelle Adjani|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160729012704/https://www.tiff.net/the-review/some-ways-of-looking-at-isabelle-adjani/|last=Sen|first=Mayukh|date=27 June 2016|archive-date=29 July 2016}}</ref> which was confirmed by Żuławski.<ref>{{cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qp06QcQ6bOY#t=1m49s |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/qp06QcQ6bOY |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live|title=Andrzej Żuławski on working with Isabelle Adjani in "Possession" (1981)|via=[[YouTube]]|publisher=Artur Wilson|date=26 July 2018|access-date=25 August 2020|author=Jakub Skoczeń}}{{cbignore}}</ref> ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' magazine compared the behavior of her character to the actions of "a dervish of unrestrained emotion and pure sexual terror".<ref name="timeout">{{cite web |url=http://www.timeout.com/film/reviews/71312/possession.html |title=Possession Review |work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]] |author=Tom Huddleston |access-date=2017-06-15 |language=en |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221115180742/https://www.timeout.com/movies/possession |archive-date=15 November 2022 |url-status=live }}</ref>
{{blockquote|There were two takes. This scene was filmed at five in the morning, when the subway was closed. Sam Neill has also commented on the rigours of filming: "I call it the most extreme film I've ever made, in every possible respect, and he asked of us things I wouldn't and couldn't go to now [''sic'']. And I think I only just escaped that film with my sanity barely intact."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds/play/m000rwtm|title=Kermode and Mayo's Film Review - Sam Neill, Rams, Malcolm and Marie and Greenland|website=[[BBC Sounds]]|publisher=BBC|date=5 February 2021|access-date=8 February 2021|archive-date=15 February 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210215025533/https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds/play/m000rwtm|url-status=live}}</ref>

The budget for ''Possession'' was $2.4 million, and a 12-week shoot was scheduled. The director chose [[Berlin]] as the setting for the story because of its proximity to the [[Communist Bloc|Communist world]]. [[Principal photography]] began on 7 July 1980 in [[West Berlin]], and most of the film was shot next to [[Berlin Wall|the Wall]], in the [[Kreuzberg]] section of West Berlin.<ref name="makingof"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/far-flung-correspondents/no-exorcist-can-handle-possession|work=RogerEbert.com|title=No Exorcist Can Handle Possession|date=9 February 2012|access-date=29 August 2020|last=Oleszczyk|first=Michał|archive-date=24 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200924030150/https://www.rogerebert.com/far-flung-correspondents/no-exorcist-can-handle-possession|url-status=live}}</ref> The "surrealist, clean quality" Żuławski wanted for the film was aided by the [[Steadicam]] work of camera operator Andrzej J. Jaroszewicz and [[Bruno Nuytten]]'s cinematography and lighting.<ref name="tcm"/> [[Carlo Rambaldi]], a famed Italian special effects artist and the creator of the ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'' animatronic head, assisted in creating the tentacle creature featured in the film.<ref>{{cite web|last=Freer|first=Ian|url=https://empireonline.com/movies/features/carlo-rambaldi/|title=The Genius of Carlo Rambdali|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|date=12 August 2012|access-date=12 March 2016|archive-date=5 March 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305072723/http://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/carlo-rambaldi/|url-status=live}}</ref>

After an initial limited theatre release in the United Kingdom, the film was banned as one of the notorious "[[Video nasty|video nasties]]".<ref name="bfi"/>{{sfn|Pyzik|2014}} On American screens, it came out in a heavily edited 81-minute cut version from Limelight International Films on October 28, 1983,<ref>{{Cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |date=1983-10-28 |title=FILM: 'POSSESSION,' BLOOD AND HORROR, WITH ISABELLE ADJANI |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1983/10/28/movies/film-possession-blood-and-horror-with-isabelle-adjani.html?searchResultPosition=1 |access-date=2025-02-01 |work=New York Times |pages=Section C, Page 10}}</ref> having lost more than a third of its runtime;{{sfn|Maltin|1994|p=1022}} the distributor turned ''Possession'' into a creature based horror film, almost completely eliminating the psychological horror aspects and main theme of a marriage breakdown. This version was ridiculed by the American press as an example of "a cheap [[Grand Guignol]]" and had no public success.<ref name="vv"/><ref name="Wilkins 2011" />

A new 4K restoration of the film by [[Metrograph]] premiered in United States at [[Fantastic Fest]] in September 2021 and expanded nationwide on October 15.<ref name="Collider">{{cite web |title=Possession 4K Restoration Getting a Theatrical Release |url=https://collider.com/possession-4k-restoration-theatrical-release/ |website=Collider |date=7 August 2021 |access-date=20 October 2021 |archive-date=18 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230118203426/https://collider.com/possession-4k-restoration-theatrical-release/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Box office===
''Possession'' had a modest total of 541,120 admissions in France.<ref name="JPBox Office">{{cite web|url=http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=7306|title=Possession|work=JP's Box-Office|access-date=19 March 2018|archive-date=20 March 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180320043925/http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=7306|url-status=live}}</ref> In the United States, it was released on 28 October 1983 and grossed $1.1 million at the box office.<ref name="The Numbers">{{cite web |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Possession-(1983)#tab=summary |title=Possession (1983) |work=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] |access-date=19 March 2018 |archive-date=12 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230112184909/https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Possession-(1983)#tab=summary |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="BOM">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0082933/|website=Box Office Mojo|title=Possession|access-date=29 August 2020|archive-date=12 January 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230112184859/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0082933/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Home media===
Although the film was banned from distribution in the United Kingdom, it was later released uncut on VHS and [[DVD]] in 2000 by [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]].<ref>{{cite book|title=Possession VHS|isbn=0764009745}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|title=Possession DVD|asin=6305839980}}</ref> In 2014, Mondo Vision released a region-free [[Blu-ray]] of the film featuring the uncut version. This release was available in a standard special edition, as well as a limited edition numbered to 2,000 units.<ref name=blu>{{cite web|url=https://screenanarchy.com/2014/07/blu-ray-review-the-possession-release-by-mondo-vision-owns-all-others-gallery.html|work=[[Screen Anarchy]]|title=Blu-ray Review: The POSSESSION Release By Mondo Vision Owns All Others|last=Vijn|first=Ard|date=7 July 2014|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190809000720/https://screenanarchy.com/2014/07/blu-ray-review-the-possession-release-by-mondo-vision-owns-all-others-gallery.html|archive-date=9 August 2019|access-date=9 August 2019|url-status=live}}</ref>

The Australian distributor Umbrella Entertainment released a region-free Blu-ray edition in 2022,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://thedigitalbits.com/item/possession-umb-2022-bd|work=The Digital Bits|last=Salmons|first=Time|date=24 May 2022|title=Possession (1981) (Blu-ray Review)|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250319194805/http://thedigitalbits.com/item/possession-umb-2022-bd|archive-date=19 March 2025}}</ref> followed by a [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K UHD Blu-ray]] on 25 October 2023.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://thedigitalbits.com/item/possession-umb-2023-uhd|work=The Digital Bits|title=Possession (1981) (4K UHD Review)|date=24 January 2024|last=Salmons|first=Tim|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240206015322/https://thedigitalbits.com/item/possession-umb-2023-uhd|archive-date=6 February 2024}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical reception===
''Possession'' received lukewarm critical response when it was initially released in the summer of 1981.<ref name="makingof"/> [[Derek Malcolm]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' stated that, while Żuławski displayed talent and the special effects were unforgettable, the film itself was far too serious for its own good.<ref name="malcom82">{{cite web |last1=Malcom |first1=Derek |title=No Sex Please... Derek Malcom reviews a film that attacks pornography, and other new releases |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/34703429/the_guardian/ |via=Newspapers.com |work=The Guardian |access-date=5 February 2020 |page=11 |date=24 June 1982 |archive-date=9 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190809003853/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/34703429/the_guardian/ |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Leonard Maltin]] wrote of the film: "Adjani 'creates' a monster, to the consternation of husband Neill, lover Bennent—and the viewer", ultimately deeming the film a "confusing drama of murder, horror, intrigue, though it's all attractively directed".{{sfn|Maltin|1994|p=1022}} [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote, "At times, the living-color ''Possession'' recalls [[Roman Polanski]]'s black-and-white ''[[Repulsion (film)|Repulsion]]'', though only because Miss Adjani is required to slice up as many male victims as [[Catherine Deneuve]] did in the earlier, far better film."<ref name="times83">{{cite web|last1=Canby|first1=Vincent|authorlink1=Vincent Canby|title=Film: 'Possession,' Blood And Horror, With Isabelle Adjani|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1983/10/28/movies/film-possession-blood-and-horror-with-isabelle-adjani.html|work=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=5 February 2020|date=28 October 1983|archive-date=5 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200205175833/https://www.nytimes.com/1983/10/28/movies/film-possession-blood-and-horror-with-isabelle-adjani.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' gave the film a positive review, praising Żuławski's direction, symbolism, and pacing, writing "mass of symbols and unbridled, brilliant directing meld this disparate tale into a film that could get cult following on its many levels of symbolism and exploitation".<ref name="variety83">{{cite web |title=Variety Reviews – Possession – Film Reviews – - Review by Variety Staff |url=https://variety.com/review/VE1117794093?refcatid=31 |work=Variety |access-date=5 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121108042301/https://variety.com/review/VE1117794093?refcatid=31 |archive-date=8 November 2012 |date=13 October 1983}}</ref>

Harry Haun of the ''[[New York Daily News]]'' alternately panned the film, awarding it one-and-a-half out of four stars and writing that Adjani's "prize-winning mad-act is impossible to appraise because the film it's in is outlandishly unhinged as well...&nbsp;Just about any dialogue accompanying this mess would seem ludicrous".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/44027507/daily_news/|work=[[Daily News (New York)|Daily News]]|location=New York|title='Possession': repulsion|last=Haun|first=Harry|via=Newspapers.com|date=28 October 1983|page=Friday 5|access-date=11 February 2020|archive-date=12 January 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230112184912/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/44027507/daily-news/|url-status=live}}</ref> The ''[[Philadelphia Daily News]]''{{'}}s Joe Baltake deemed the film a "boringly camp-elegante attempt by a group of reputable French, German and Polish filmmakers" and assessed Adjani's performance as "babbling, incoherent yet arresting". Baltake also criticized the massive amount of the film that was left out of the American version (approximately 50 minutes).<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/44027653/philadelphia_daily_news/|work=[[Philadelphia Daily News]]|location=Philadelphia, Pennsylvania|date=14 November 1983|via=Newspapers.com|page=39|last=Baltake|first=Joe|title='Possession' an Exorcise in Futility|access-date=11 February 2020|archive-date=12 January 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230112184920/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/44027653/philadelphia-daily-news/|url-status=live}}</ref> Writer [[Maitland McDonagh]] believed the film's initial release in America where it was marketed as a straight horror film and screened in [[grindhouse]] theaters was a mistake, and it should have instead been released in [[art house]] theaters where it would have gained a better reception.<ref>{{Cite web |last=McDonagh |first=Maitland |date=December 2, 2011 |title=Film Review: Possession |url=http://www.filmjournal.com/filmjournal/content_display/reviews/specialty-releases/e3i5a667ed6f38844ae319e873d90dafde0 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121107152307/http://www.filmjournal.com/filmjournal/content_display/reviews/specialty-releases/e3i5a667ed6f38844ae319e873d90dafde0 |archive-date=November 7, 2012 |access-date=August 16, 2024 |website=[[Film Journal International]] |publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media|Mediabistro Holdings]]}}</ref>

===Legacy===
In the years following its release, ''Possession'' accrued a [[cult following]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Burns |first=Sean |date=November 11, 2021 |title=At the Brattle, abstract cult films 'Possession' and 'Arrebato' are returning to the screen |url=https://www.wbur.org/news/2021/11/11/possession-arrebato-brattle-theatre |access-date=2024-08-16 |website=[[WBUR]] |language=en}}</ref>{{sfn|Mazierska|Goddard|2014|p=245}}<ref name="tcm" /> Film scholar Bartłomiej Paszylk deemed it "one of the most enigmatic and uncompromising horror movies in the history of cinema".{{sfn|Paszylk|2009|p=163}}

Writer [[Kim Newman]] considers ''Possession'' to be a "[[kitsch]] film", noting: "Zulawski takes his film too seriously, but it's fun all the same&nbsp;... [he] goes mad with his swooping camera, has everything in shot painted in blue and encourages his stars to attack their roles with a kind of stylised hysteria rare outside Japanese theatre."{{sfn|Newman|2011|p=190}} Newman also likened elements of Adjani's character to that of [[Samantha Eggar]] in ''[[The Brood]]'' (1979).{{sfn|Newman|2011|p=190}} Tom Huddleston of ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' gave the perfect-star rating, and wrote "There are plenty of movies which seem to have been made by madmen. ''Possession'' may be the only film in existence which is itself mad: unpredictable, horrific, its moments of terrifying lucidity only serving to highlight the staggering derangement at its core. Extreme but essential viewing."<ref name="timeout" /> Similarly, ''[[Slant Magazine]]''{{'}}s Budd Wilkins gave the film 4/4 stars, saying that "Many directors have taken full advantage of Adjani's exotic, ethereal French beauty; only Zulawski saw beyond the exquisite surface to something unsettling. Most disconcerting is the way Adjani can register almost demonic ill-intent while never losing some trace of the alluring."<ref name="Wilkins 2011" /> Ben Sachs from ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' called it a "confounding masterpiece".<ref name="chicagoreader">{{cite web |url=https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/andrzej-zulawskis-possession-at-gene-siskel-film-center/Content?oid=6379947 |title=Andrzej Zulawski's Possession, uncut |publisher=[[Chicago Reader]] |first=Ben |last=Sachs |date=16 May 2012 |access-date=2020-09-22 |archive-date=16 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201116181100/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/andrzej-zulawskis-possession-at-gene-siskel-film-center/Content?oid=6379947 |url-status=live }}</ref> Dennis Schwartz from ''Ozus' World Movie Reviews'' gave the film a grade of "C+", calling it "[an] uncompromising demented cult oddity".<ref name="schwartz10">{{cite web |last1=Schwartz |first1=Dennis |title=Possession – Dennis Schwartz Reviews |url=https://dennisschwartzreviews.com/possession1981/ |website=DennisSchwartzReviews.com |date=5 August 2019 |publisher=Ozus World Movie Reviews |access-date=5 February 2020 |archive-date=5 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200205175836/https://dennisschwartzreviews.com/possession1981/ |url-status=live }}</ref> 

The film is included in ''[[Sight & Sound]]''{{'}}s The Greatest Films of All Time list.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Greatest Films of All Time |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/greatest-films-all-time |access-date=2023-08-06 |website=BFI |language=en |archive-date=18 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210318214144/https://www2.bfi.org.uk/greatest-films-all-time |url-status=live }}</ref> Michael Brooke of ''Sight & Sound'' commented in 2011, "Although it's easy to see why it was pigeonholed as a horror film, its first half presents what is still one of the most viscerally vivid portraits of a disintegrating relationship yet committed to film, comfortably rivalling [[Lars von Trier]]'s ''[[Antichrist (film)|Antichrist]]'', [[David Cronenberg]]'s ''The Brood'' and [[Ingmar Bergman]]'s ''[[Scenes from a Marriage]]''."<ref name="bfi">{{cite web | url=http://www.bfi.org.uk/sightandsound/review/5639 | title=Possession | first=Michael |last=Brooke | publisher=[[Sight & Sound]] | access-date=23 April 2011 | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110514122650/http://www.bfi.org.uk/sightandsound/review/5639 | archive-date=14 May 2011}}</ref> Reviewing the Blu-ray release of the film in 2013, Michael Dodd of ''Bring The Noise'' was similarly impressed with what he called "an intense exploration of marital breakdown". He argued that this made ''Possession'' "one of the few horror films that successfully builds a back story for its main characters".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bringthenoiseuk.com/201307/films/film-review-possession|title=FILM REVIEW: Possession |last=Dodd |first=Michael |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923194801/http://www.bringthenoiseuk.com/201307/films/film-review-possession |archive-date=23 September 2015 |url-status=dead |work=Bring the Noise UK |access-date=19 March 2018}}</ref> Reviewing the film's Blu-ray release, Andrew Pollard of the British magazine ''[[Starburst (magazine)|Starburst]]'' rated the film eight out of ten stars, calling it "a visceral, violent, erratic and piercing effort that pokes and prods its audience any chance it gets"; Pollard would also praise the performances of Adjani and Neill, practical effects and unsettling tone.<ref name="Pollard 2013">{{cite web|last=Pollard|first=Andrew|title=Blu-ray Review: POSSESSION (1981)|url=https://www.starburstmagazine.com/reviews/blu-ray-review-possession-1981|publisher=[[Starburst (magazine)|Starburst]]|access-date=December 15, 2022|date=26 June 2013|archive-date=15 December 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221215183345/https://www.starburstmagazine.com/reviews/blu-ray-review-possession-1981|url-status=live}}</ref> In his review of the 4K restoration, David Fear of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' lauded the film as "a body-horror answer to ''[[Kramer vs. Kramer]]''" and stated that the restoration was "jaw-droppingly beautiful".<ref name="Fear2021">{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/possession-most-notorious-cult-movie-ever-1242928/|magazine=Rolling Stone|title=There Are Crazed, Manic Cult Movies — and Then There's 'Possession'|date=17 October 2021|access-date=18 January 2023|last=Fear|first=David|archive-date=23 October 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221023152606/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/possession-most-notorious-cult-movie-ever-1242928/|url-status=live}}</ref>

On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 87% of 45 critic reviews are positive for ''Possession''. Its consensus reads, "Blending genres as effectively as it subverts expectations, ''Possession'' uses powerful acting and disquieting imagery to grapple with complex themes."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/possession_1983 |title=Possession (1983) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=17 December 2025 |archive-date=13 September 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220913001015/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/possession_1983 |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film earned a weighted average score of 75 out of 100, based on 10 reviews, signifying "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/possession-1983 |title=Possession (1983) Reviews |publisher=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=3 October 2021 |archive-date=1 October 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211001060220/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/possession-1983 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The music video for [[Massive Attack]]'s "Voodoo in My Blood" (2016) pays homage to the subway scene, with [[Rosamund Pike]] wearing a maxi-dress and dancing wildly in a pedestrian underpass. Director [[Ringan Ledwidge]] acknowledged the influence, calling himself "a huge fan of" ''Possession''.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Denney |first=Alex |date=26 February 2016 |title=The cult inspirations behind Massive Attack's new video |work=[[Dazed Digital]] |url=https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/30051/1/the-cult-inspirations-behind-massive-attack-s-new-video |access-date=16 September 2023 |archive-date=28 September 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230928171101/https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/30051/1/the-cult-inspirations-behind-massive-attack-s-new-video |url-status=live }}</ref>
