{{Nihongo|'''''The Wind Rises'''''|風立ちぬ|Kaze Tachinu|{{lit|The Wind Has Risen}}|lead=yes}} is a 2013 Japanese [[Anime|animated]] [[historical drama film]] written and directed by [[Hayao Miyazaki]] based on his 2009 manga of the same name. Produced by [[Studio Ghibli]] and distributed by [[Toho]], the film stars the voices of [[Hideaki Anno]], [[Miori Takimoto]], [[Hidetoshi Nishijima (actor)|Hidetoshi Nishijima]], [[Masahiko Nishimura]], [[Morio Kazama]], [[Keiko Takeshita]], [[Mirai Shida]], [[Jun Kunimura]], [[Shinobu Otake]], and [[Nomura Mansai]].

The film portrays a fictionalised account of the life of Japanese [[aeronautical engineer]] [[Jiro Horikoshi]], in particular his engineering career from his time at the [[University of Tokyo]] in 1923 to the first test flight of the [[Mitsubishi Ka-14]] on 4 February 1935.<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Green |first1=William |last2=Swanborough |first2=Gordon |title=The Zero Precursor...Mitsubishi's A5M| magazine=[[Air Enthusiast]] |issue=19 |date=August–November 1982 |pages=26–43}}</ref> Juxtaposed with the historical events is a fictional romance of Horikoshi's, inspired by the similarly named semi-autobiographical novel ''[[The Wind Has Risen]]'' by [[Tatsuo Hori]]. The film was originally intended to be Miyazaki's final feature film,<ref>{{Cite web |date=2013-09-07 |script-title=ja:宮崎駿監督「この世は生きるに値する」 引退会見の全文 |url=https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXNZO59386140W3A900C1000000/ |access-date=2023-10-11 |publisher=[[The Nikkei]] |language=ja |archive-date=February 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160205030127/http://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXNZO59386140W3A900C1000000/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|last=Highfill|first=Samantha|date=September 6, 2013|url=https://ew.com/article/2013/09/06/hayao-miyazaki-retirement/|title=Hayao Miyazaki on his retirement: 'This time I am quite serious'|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=October 27, 2019|archive-date=October 21, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131021053757/http://insidemovies.ew.com/2013/09/06/hayao-miyazaki-retirement/|url-status=dead}}</ref> before Miyazaki reversed his decision and eventually directed ''[[The Boy and the Heron]]''.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-11-14 |script-title=ja:宮崎駿監督、新作タイトルは「君たちはどう生きるか」：朝日新聞デジタル |url=https://www.asahi.com/articles/ASKBX5T4ZKBXUCLV008.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211114113038/https://www.asahi.com/articles/ASKBX5T4ZKBXUCLV008.html |url-status=dead |archive-date= November 14, 2021 |access-date=2023-09-13 |website=Asahi Shimbun}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Piedra |first=Josh |date=October 28, 2017 |title=Hayao Miyazaki's Final Film Title is How Do You Live? |language=en-US |work=The Outerhaven |url=http://www.theouterhaven.net/2017/10/hayao-miyazaki-how-do-you-live/ |url-status=live |access-date=March 3, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180221054442/http://www.theouterhaven.net/2017/10/hayao-miyazaki-how-do-you-live/ |archive-date=February 21, 2018}}</ref>

Released on 20 July 2013 in Japan, ''The Wind Rises'' was the highest-grossing Japanese film of 2013. Though it caused some political controversy and criticism in Asia, it was met with critical acclaim. The film was nominated for several awards, including the [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature]], the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Foreign Language Film]], and the [[Japan Academy Prize for Animation of the Year]], winning the latter.

== Plot ==
[[File:Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Oe Factory Tokei-dai 20150517.JPG|thumb|Mitsubishi aircraft manufacturing factory, [[Nagoya]]]]
[[File:Manpei hotel02s1440.jpg|thumb|Mampei Hotel, Karuizawa]]
<!-- Per WP:FilmPlot, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words. -->
In 1918, a young [[Jiro Horikoshi]] longs to become a pilot, but his nearsightedness prevents it. Inspired by a magazine, he begins having recurring dreams of flying with his idol, Italian aircraft designer [[Giovanni Battista Caproni]], aboard Caproni's aircraft. Caproni tells him that he has never flown a plane in his life, and that building planes is better than flying them. Five years later, following the failure of the [[Caproni Ca.60]], Jiro is an aeronautical engineering student at [[Tokyo Imperial University]]. While travelling home from a visit with family, he meets a young girl, Nahoko Satomi, travelling with her maid Kinu. The [[Great Kantō earthquake]] suddenly hits, and Kinu's leg is broken. Jiro helps Nahoko carry her to Nahoko's family home, leaving without exchanging names.

In 1925, Jiro graduates with his friend [[Kiro Honjo]], and both are employed at aeroplane manufacturer [[Mitsubishi Heavy Industries|Mitsubishi]] amidst the [[Great Depression]]. They are assigned to perfect a fighter plane, the [[Mitsubishi 1MF9]], for the [[Imperial Japanese Army|Imperial Army]]. During a test, it breaks apart in midair while attempting to pass 200 knots and is rejected. Pivoting their plans, Mitsubishi sends Jiro and Honjo to the [[Weimar Republic]] in 1929 to obtain a production licence for a [[Junkers G.38]], intending to build a bomber. Although [[Hugo Junkers]] welcomes them, the two men are blocked from obtaining complete plans by the ''[[Sicherheitspolizei (Weimar Republic)|Sicherheitspolizei]]''. With them and their coworkers discouraged by how far back Japan's aeronautics technology is from the rest of the world, Jiro returns to Japan, while Honjo stays and eventually develops the [[Mitsubishi G4M]].

In early 1932, Jiro is promoted to chief designer for a fighter plane competition sponsored by the [[Imperial Japanese Navy|Imperial Navy]], but his design, the [[Mitsubishi 1MF10]], fails testing in 1933 and is rejected. Disappointed, he takes a vacation at a summer resort in [[Karuizawa]]. There he reunites with an adult Nahoko, who has been searching for him since they first met. The two quickly develop a romance, assisted by a German tourist he calls Castorp. Critical of [[Nazi Germany]], Castorp privately tells Jiro that [[Adolf Hitler]] has apprehended Junkers for resisting [[Nazism]], and that Germany must be stopped from declaring another world war, this time allied with Japan. He then flees arrest from the [[Special Higher Police]].

Later, Nahoko is diagnosed with [[tuberculosis]], so Jiro asks Nahoko's father for his blessing to marry her, and the two are engaged. However, Nahoko wishes to wait until she recovers to marry, and moves back in with her family. Wanted in connection with Castorp, Jiro hides at his supervisor Kurokawa's home while he works on a new fighter project for the Imperial Navy. Jiro briefly leaves when Nahoko suffers from a [[pulmonary haemorrhage]]. After Jiro briefly tends to her, Nahoko decides to check into a mountain [[sanatorium]] to recover, but cannot bear being apart from Jiro and returns to be with him. Kurokawa and his wife marry the two and allow the couple to stay in their home with Nahoko's father's permission. Jiro's sister Kayo, a doctor, warns Jiro that his marriage to Nahoko will end tragically as tuberculosis is incurable. Though Nahoko's health deteriorates, she and Jiro enjoy their fleeting time together.

Jiro leaves for the test flight of his new prototype aeroplane, the [[Mitsubishi Ka-14]]. Knowing that she will die soon, Nahoko leaves farewell letters for Jiro, her family, and friends and discreetly leaves the house in a vain attempt to return to the sanatorium. At the test site, Jiro is distracted from his success by a gust of wind, suggesting Nahoko's passing. 

In 1945, after Japan has [[Surrender of Japan|lost]] [[World War II]], Jiro dreams of Caproni again, regretting that his plane was used for war. Caproni comforts him, saying that Jiro's dream of building beautiful aeroplanes was nonetheless realised, in the form of his masterpiece—the [[Mitsubishi A6M|A6M 'Zero' fighter]]. Nahoko's spirit also appears, encouraging her husband to live on. After her spirit departs, Jiro and Caproni walk together into their shared kingdom of dreams.

''The Wind Rises'' was directed by [[Hayao Miyazaki]],<ref name="cinematoday20121213">{{cite web |url= https://www.cinematoday.jp/news/N0048613 |script-title= ja:ジブリ新作、2作一挙公開！宮崎駿＆高畑勲作品でジブリ史上初！ |date= 13 December 2012 |access-date= 27 December 2012 |work= Cinema Today |url-status= live |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20121216014409/http://www.cinematoday.jp/page/N0048613 |archive-date= 16 December 2012 |df= dmy-all }}</ref> his first feature film since ''[[Ponyo]]'' in 2008.<ref name="ann20121213"/>

Miyazaki began to conceive a story to illustrate the life of Jiro Horikoshi in 2008.<ref name=YahooTW>{{cite web|last=劉黎兒|script-title=zh:人物專訪／風起了 聽宮崎駿內心的聲音|url=https://tw.news.yahoo.com/人物專訪／風起了-聽宮崎駿內心的聲音上-040127364.html|website=tw.news.yahoo.com|date=18 September 2013 |language=zh|publisher=Yahoo Taiwan|access-date=2 October 2014|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141006102137/https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E4%BA%BA%E7%89%A9%E5%B0%88%E8%A8%AA%EF%BC%8F%E9%A2%A8%E8%B5%B7%E4%BA%86-%E8%81%BD%E5%AE%AE%E5%B4%8E%E9%A7%BF%E5%85%A7%E5%BF%83%E7%9A%84%E8%81%B2%E9%9F%B3%E4%B8%8A-040127364.html|archive-date=6 October 2014}}</ref> He published the story as a manga series in the ''Model Graphix'' magazine's April 2009 to January 2010 issues as a continuation of his ''[[Hayao Miyazaki's Daydream Data Notes|Delusional Notes]]'' series, and it was collected into one volume in October 2015.<ref>{{Cite book |last=宮崎 |first=駿 |script-title=ja:風立ちぬ 宮崎駿の妄想カムバック |date=8 October 2015 |publisher=Dainippon Kaiga |isbn=978-4-499-23167-1 |language=Japanese}}</ref> The title is an [[allusion]] to [[Paul Valéry]]'s 1920 poem "Le Cimetière Marin" (''The Graveyard by the Sea'').<ref name="welt"/> The film also references [[Tatsuo Hori]]'s 1937 semi-autobiographical novel ''[[The Wind Has Risen]]'' ({{nihongo2|風立ちぬ| help=yes}}),<ref name="p88VisialGuide">{{cite book|title=The Wind Rises Visual Guide|date=20 July 2011|language=ja|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|isbn=978-4-04-110510-8|page=88}}</ref> and uses visuals to depict Hori's words.<ref name=YahooTW/> The manga portrayed certain characters as [[anthropomorphised]] pigs, employing a design reminiscent of that seen in Miyazaki's film ''[[Porco Rosso]]'' (1992).<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.slashfilm.com/studio-ghibli-titles-new-films-from-hayao-miyazaki-and-isao-takahata-grave-of-the-fireflies-picked-up-for-us-re-release/|title= Studio Ghibli Titles New Films From Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata; 'Grave of the Fireflies' Picked Up For US Re-Release|author= Russ Fischer|date= 21 November 2012|access-date= 24 November 2012|work= slashfilm.com|url-status= live|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20130202013957/http://www.slashfilm.com/studio-ghibli-titles-new-films-from-hayao-miyazaki-and-isao-takahata-grave-of-the-fireflies-picked-up-for-us-re-release/|archive-date= 2 February 2013|df= dmy-all}}</ref>

The story in the manga follows the historical account of Horikoshi's aircraft development up to 1935 (the year of the Mitsubishi A5M's maiden flight),<ref name="p77VisialGuide">{{cite book|title=The Wind Rises Visual Guide|date=20 July 2011|language=ja|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|isbn=978-4-04-110510-8|page=77}}</ref> and intertwines with fictional encounters with Caproni and Nahoko Satomi ({{nihongo2|里見 菜穂子| help=yes}}).<ref>{{cite book|title=The Wind Rises Visual Guide|date=20 July 2011|language=ja|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|isbn=978-4-04-110510-8|page=92}}</ref> The scenes with Nahoko in the manga included elements from the novel ''The Wind Has Risen'',<ref name=YahooTW/> in which Tatsuo Hori wrote about his life experience with his fiancée, Ayako Yano ({{nihongo2|矢野 綾子| help=yes}}), before she died from tuberculosis. The name ''Nahoko Satomi'' was created from the female protagonist of another novel by Tatsuo Hori, ''Nahoko'' ({{nihongo2|菜穂子| help=yes}}).<ref>{{cite book|title=The Wind Rises Visual Guide|date=20 July 2011|language=ja|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|isbn=978-4-04-110510-8|page=93}}</ref>

Characters frequently discuss [[Thomas Mann]]'s novel ''[[The Magic Mountain]]'', and, in a letter to Nahoko, Jiro names his fleeing German friend "Mr. Castorp" after its protagonist.<ref name="p60VisialGuide">{{cite book|title=The Wind Rises Visual Guide|date=20 July 2011|language=ja|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|isbn=978-4-04-110510-8|page=60}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Sachs|first=Ben|title=''The Wind Rises'': On a higher plane|url=https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/the-wind-rises-hayao-miyazaki-jiro-horikoshi/Content?oid=12535079|access-date=12 August 2015|work=[[Chicago Reader]]|date=19 February 2014|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150920121936/http://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/the-wind-rises-hayao-miyazaki-jiro-horikoshi/Content?oid=12535079|archive-date=20 September 2015}}</ref> The character himself is a caricature of Stephen Alpert, who was once the executive director of Ghibli's international division. He left the company in 2011 for personal reasons, but was instrumental in Ghibli's overseas expansion. He was asked to return to Japan to model for the character's appearance and a suitable voice.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.scifijapan.com/articles/2013/09/08/the-wind-rises-production-notes|title=The Wind Rises Production Notes|publisher=scifijapan.com|access-date=23 January 2016|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303204025/http://www.scifijapan.com/articles/2013/09/08/the-wind-rises-production-notes/|archive-date=3 March 2016}}</ref>

After the release of ''Ponyo'', Miyazaki wanted his next film to be a sequel to the film, but producer [[Toshio Suzuki]] proposed to adapt the manga ''The Wind Has Risen'' instead. At first, Miyazaki rejected the proposal because he created the manga as a hobby and considered its subjects not suitable for children, the traditional audience of Studio Ghibli's features. However, he changed his mind when a staff member suggested that "children should be allowed to be exposed to subjects they are not familiar with".<ref name=nikkei>{{cite web|script-title=zh:宮崎駿專訪：時代追上了我|url=http://zh.cn.nikkei.com/trend/traditional-culture/6141-20130812.html|website=nikkei.com|language=zh|publisher=日經中文網|access-date=2 October 2014|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141006091744/http://zh.cn.nikkei.com/trend/traditional-culture/6141-20130812.html|archive-date=6 October 2014}}</ref> He was also inspired to make the film after reading a quote from Horikoshi: "All I wanted to do was to make something beautiful".<ref name="latimes20130815">{{cite news | url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/moviesnow/la-et-mn-the-wind-rises-trailer-miyazaki-20130815,0,314360.story | title={{-'}}The Wind Rises': Hayao Miyazaki's new film stirs controversy | work=[[Los Angeles Times]] | date=15 August 2013 | access-date=16 August 2013 | author=Keegan, Rebecca | url-status=live | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130817191230/http://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/moviesnow/la-et-mn-the-wind-rises-trailer-miyazaki-20130815,0,314360.story | archive-date=17 August 2013 | df=dmy-all }}</ref>

=== Music ===

The 32-track [[film score]] was composed and conducted by [[Joe Hisaishi]], and performed by the [[Yomiuri Nippon Symphony Orchestra]]. It features [[Yumi Matsutoya]]'s 1973 song {{nihongo|"[[Hikō-ki Gumo]]"|ひこうき雲}}. Matsutoya previously collaborated with Studio Ghibli on ''Kiki's Delivery Service'', which features her songs {{nihongo|"[[Rūju no Dengon]]"|ルージュの伝言}} and {{nihongo|"Yasashisa ni Tsutsumaretanara"|やさしさに包まれたなら}}. === Controversy ===
In Japan, ''The Wind Rises'' received criticism from both the Japanese political right<ref name="latimes20130815"/> and from the Japan Society for Tobacco Control.<ref name="guardian20130823">{{cite web |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2013/aug/23/hayao-miyazaki-film-wind-rises |title=Japanese animator under fire for film tribute to warplane designer |first=Justin |last=McCurry |work=The Guardian|date=23 August 2013 |access-date=25 August 2013 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170113021415/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2013/aug/23/hayao-miyazaki-film-wind-rises |archive-date=13 January 2017  }}</ref> Miyazaki added to the controversy by publishing an essay in which he criticised the proposal by Japan's right-wing [[Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)|Liberal Democratic Party]] to change [[Article 9 of the Japanese Constitution|Article 9]] of the [[Japanese Constitution]] to allow Japan to remilitarise.<ref name="latimes20130815"/><ref name="guardian20130823"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Miyazaki |first=Hayao |script-title=ja:憲法を変えるなどもってのほか |trans-title=Other than changing the Constitution |url=https://www.ghibli.jp/docs/0718kenpo.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130719122305/https://www.ghibli.jp/docs/0718kenpo.pdf |archive-date=2013-07-19 |access-date=2020-06-27 |website=Studio Ghibli}}</ref>

Miyazaki also attracted political criticism from South Korean internet users, who argued that the Zero represents Japanese military aggression and that many planes were assembled by Korean [[forced labour]].<ref name="guardian20130823"/> Miyazaki told South Korean journalists that "[Horikoshi] was someone who resisted demands from the military...I wonder if he should be liable for anything just because he lived in that period."<ref name="guardian20130823"/> In an interview with the ''[[Asahi Shimbun]]'', Miyazaki said he had "very complex feelings" about World War II since, as a [[pacifist]], he felt militarist Japan had acted out of "foolish arrogance". However, he also said that the [[Zero plane]] "represented one of the few things we Japanese could be proud of—[they] were a truly formidable presence, and so were the pilots who flew them".<ref name="guardian20130823"/>
