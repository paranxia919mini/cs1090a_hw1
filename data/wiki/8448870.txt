'''''Kaagaz Ke Phool''''' ({{Translation|''Paper Flowers''}}){{efn|The implied meaning is that all happiness in this life [Flowers] is fake [Paper], although it might appear real. The protagonist of this film [a character played by [[Guru Dutt]]] had at one point in time, all the happiness this world had to offer him. However, it was only due to his success. Once he failed to deliver success, people gradually moved away from him [a situation, very poignantly described by [[Kaifi Azmi]] in another of his songs from the same film ''[[Bichde Sabhi Bari Bari]]'' (Every one moved away from me gradually) and also by extremely sobering thought ''Matlab ki duniya hai saari'' (the whole world only needs favors from you; once you are unable to deliver that, they will silently move away). The philosophical message that this film appeared to deliver was this - Don't be arrogant, when you are successful; a time will inevitably come, when this success will disappear. Thus all your success and happiness is fake or paper flowers.{{cn|date=May 2026}}}} is a 1959 Indian [[Hindi]]-language [[romantic drama film]] produced and directed by [[Guru Dutt]], who also played the lead role in the film along with [[Waheeda Rehman]]. It is the first Indian film in [[CinemaScope]] and the last film officially directed by Dutt.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Sood|first=Samira|date=2020-05-16|title=Kaagaz Ke Phool is Guru Dutt's masterclass in filmmaking and heartbreak|url=https://theprint.in/features/kaagaz-ke-phool-is-guru-dutts-masterclass-in-filmmaking-and-heartbreak/421852/|access-date=2021-04-16|website=ThePrint|language=en-US|archive-date=19 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210419050247/https://theprint.in/features/kaagaz-ke-phool-is-guru-dutts-masterclass-in-filmmaking-and-heartbreak/421852/|url-status=live}}</ref> It marked a technical revolution in Indian [[cinematography]] and is widely considered to be ahead of its time.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.thehindu.com/todays-paper/tp-features/tp-metroplus/Kaagaz-ke-Phool-1959/article15391096.ece|title=Kaagaz ke Phool 1959|newspaper=The Hindu|date=11 October 2008|via=www.thehindu.com|archive-date=16 December 2024|access-date=28 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241216091710/https://www.thehindu.com/todays-paper/tp-features/tp-metroplus/Kaagaz-ke-Phool-1959/article15391096.ece|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=":0" />

The film was a [[box office bomb]] in its time owing to its theme which the masses could not identify with, but was later resurrected as a [[Cult following|cult classic]] in the 1980s.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-07-18|title=Kaagaz ke Phool: 59 years later|url=https://indianculturalforum.in/2018/07/18/kaagaz-ke-phool-59-years-later-guru-dutt/|access-date=2021-04-17|website=Indian Cultural Forum|language=en-US|archive-date=17 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210417153004/https://indianculturalforum.in/2018/07/18/kaagaz-ke-phool-59-years-later-guru-dutt/|url-status=live}}</ref> Its failure, both critically and commercially, severely impacted Guru Dutt and brought his [[Guru Dutt Films|studio]] on the verge of ruins. Dutt never officially directed a film after this.<ref name="Usman">{{Cite book|last=Usman|first=Yasser|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=W_sKEAAAQBAJ&q=Kaagaz+Ke+Phool+SD+Burman+this+is+just+your+story&pg=PT143|title=Guru Dutt: An Unfinished Story: An Unfinished Story|date=2021-01-07|publisher=Simon and Schuster|isbn=978-93-86797-89-6|language=en}}</ref> The film's music was composed by [[S. D. Burman]] and the lyrics were written by [[Kaifi Azmi]] and [[Shailendra (lyricist)|Shailendra]] (for one song ''"''Hum Tum Jise Kehta Hai''"''). Today ''Kaagaz ke Phool'' is a part of syllabi in many [[film school]]s.<ref>{{Cite web|title=100 Filmfare Days: Day 25 - Kaagaz Ke Phool|url=https://www.filmfare.com/features/100-filmfare-days-day-25-kaagaz-ke-phool-6152.html|access-date=2021-04-17|website=filmfare.com|language=en|archive-date=17 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210417172333/https://www.filmfare.com/features/100-filmfare-days-day-25-kaagaz-ke-phool-6152.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It is considered to be the finest [[self-reflexive]] film to have ever been made in India.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Chatterji|first=Shoma A.|title=60 years of Kaagaz Ke Phool: The most outstanding self-reflexive film of Indian cinema|url=https://www.cinestaan.com/articles/2019/jan/2/17790/60-years-of-kaagaz-ke-phool-the-most-outstanding-self-reflexive-film-of-indian-cinema|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210417174525/https://www.cinestaan.com/articles/2019/jan/2/17790/60-years-of-kaagaz-ke-phool-the-most-outstanding-self-reflexive-film-of-indian-cinema|url-status=live|archive-date=17 April 2021|access-date=2021-04-17|website=www.cinestaan.com}}</ref>

==Synopsis==
The film tells, in flashback, the story of Suresh Sinha, a famous film director. His marriage to Veena is on the rocks, due to her wealthy family seeing filmmaking, as a job, lacking in social status. He is also denied access to his daughter, Pammi, who is sent to a private boarding school, in [[Dehradun]].

On a rainy night, Sinha meets a woman, Shanti, and gives her his coat. She comes to the film studio to return the coat, unintentionally disrupting the shooting, by walking in front of the camera. While reviewing the [[Dailies|rushes]], Sinha recognises her potential as a star and casts her as Paro, in ''[[Devdas]]''. Shanti goes on to become a rage and an acclaimed star. Shanti and Suresh, two lonely people, come together. Their liaison is hotly debated in gossip columns and results in Pammi's friends tormenting her, in school. Pammi pleads with Shanti to leave Sinha's life and allow her parents' marriage another chance. Moved by Pammi's plea, Shanti throws away her career and becomes a school teacher in a small village. 

Pammi decides to live with her father, who fights his in-laws in court, but loses custody of Pammi to Veena, who is now his ex-wife. Losing custody of Pammi, along with Shanti's departure from films, drives Suresh to alcohol. This marks the beginning of his downfall, both personally and professionally, in his career and consequent decline in his fortunes. Meanwhile, Shanti is forced to return to films since she has a contract with the studio. Her producer agrees to hire Suresh because of Shanti, but Suresh's self-respect will not let him return and owe his job to Shanti's star status; so she is unable to help him, as he is too far gone for redemption. In the final scene, remembering his glorious past, he dies in the director's chair in an empty film studio, a lonely and forgotten man.

==Production==
# The film is widely considered to be an [[autobiographical]] reflection of Guru Dutt's personality, his deepening disillusionment with the film industry and the nature of fame it brings, and on the widely written-about alleged relationship between Dutt and his protégé, Waheeda Rehman. Dutt was married to singer [[Geeta Dutt|Geeta Roy Chowdhuri]] (later Geeta Dutt) at the time, and his heavy drinking and the rumours of his passionate romance with Rehman didn't help. Others believe it to be a homage to [[Gyan Mukherjee]] (known for directing [[Kismet (1943 film)|''Kismet'']]), whom Guru Dutt had joined in 1950. Waheeda Rehman has denied that the story was based on Dutt's own life augmenting that Dutt had never tasted failure as all his films were successful at the time of film's production unlike the protagonist who suffered failure.<ref name="Miguel2012">{{cite book|author=Helio San Miguel|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Q0_v3jr6TCAC&pg=PA22|title=World Film Locations: Mumbai|date=2012|publisher=Intellect Books|isbn=978-1-84150-632-6|pages=22}}</ref><ref name=":0" />

[[V. K. Murthy]], a frequent collaborator of Dutt, served as the [[cinematographer]] and won the [[Filmfare Award for Best Cinematography|Filmfare Best Cinematographer Award]] for his work in the film. [[M. R. Acharekar]] was the [[art director]] and went on to receive the [[Filmfare Award for Best Art Direction]]. [[Bhanu Athaiya]], who would later receive an [[Academy Award for Best Costume Design|Academy Award]] for [[Gandhi (film)|''Gandhi'']] (1982), handled the [[costume design]]. Ramnord Research Labs, headed by S. V. Rau, did the final [[film processing]].

In an interview filmed for Khalid Mohammed's 2025 documentary, "The Master", Shyam Benegal recounts that this film was originally shot with a different cast, and upon a lukewarm reception during a preview screening for some of his director friends, Guru Dutt reshot it in its entirety with a different cast.

== Release and reception ==
The commercial and critical failure of ''Kaagaz Ke Phool'' was an intense disappointment for Guru Dutt. He had invested a great deal of love, money, and energy in this film. All subsequent films from his studio were, thereafter, officially helmed by other directors since Guru Dutt felt that his name is anathema to box office.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2008-07-09|title=Guru Dutt: Remembering a genius|url=https://www.hindustantimes.com/entertainment/guru-dutt-remembering-a-genius/story-qAv6dxRGSSoEaFtwoQKMxI.html|access-date=2021-04-17|website=Hindustan Times|language=en}}</ref><ref name=":0" />

In the writing of Sathya Saran's book ''[[Ten Years with Guru Dutt|Ten Years with Guru Dutt: Abrar Alvi’s Journey]]'', [[Abrar Alvi]], who wrote the screenplay and dialogues of ''Kaagaz Ke Phool'', told ''Saran'' that he believed the masses just couldn't identify with Suresh's trauma over his personal heartbreak and his lack of professional creative freedom, given that he was reasonably well off and he still had a job, while they struggled to put two meals on their table. He believed viewers couldn't feel for what they saw as the self-indulgent angst of a privileged man.<ref name=":0" />

== Legacy ==
In the 2002 ''[[Sight & Sound]]'' critics and directors' poll, ''Kaagaz Ke Phool'' was ranked #160 in the list of [[List of films considered the best|greatest films of all time]].<ref>{{cite web|year=2002|title=2002 Sight & Sound Top Films Survey of 253 International Critics & Film Directors|url=http://www.cinemacom.com/2002-sight-sound.html|access-date=2009-04-19|publisher=Cinemacom|archive-date=4 June 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120604162906/http://www.cinemacom.com/2002-sight-sound.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It was listed in [[British Film Institute]]'s Top 20 Indian Films in 2002 at #11;<ref>{{Cite web|date=2011-05-15|title=BFI {{!}} Features {{!}} Top 20 Indian Films|url=http://www.bfi.org.uk/features/imagineasia/guide/poll/india/|url-status=dead|access-date=2021-04-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110515101729/http://www.bfi.org.uk/features/imagineasia/guide/poll/india/|archive-date=15 May 2011}}</ref> [[NDTV]]'s listed it in "India's 20 greatest films", citing, "''Kaagaz Ke Phool'', Guru Dutt's most melancholic film ever, takes his deepening disillusionment with the world and its guardians to a new level of despair. The iconic actor-director plays a filmmaker on the skids. He is unable to get his point of view across to the people who matter and life is a constant struggle, both personally and professionally. The masses failed to connect with the film, but Kaagaz Ke Phool has lived longer than many money-spinners of the golden era of Hindi cinema''."''<ref>{{Cite web|date=2012-02-21|title=India's 20 greatest films » NDTV Movies|url=http://movies.ndtv.com/PhotoDetail.aspx?Page=14&ID=1124#talk|access-date=2021-04-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120221192614/http://movies.ndtv.com/PhotoDetail.aspx?Page=14&ID=1124#talk|archive-date=21 February 2012}}</ref> It was listed in [[CNN- IBN|CNN-IBN]]'s 100 Greatest Indian Films of all time in 2013.<ref>{{Cite web|title=100 Years of Indian Cinema: The 100 greatest Indian films of all time|url=https://www.news18.com/photogallery/movies/100-years-of-indian-cinema-the-100-greatest-indian-films-of-all-time-903065.html|access-date=2021-08-25|website=[[News18]]|language=en|archive-date=13 August 2019|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190813155555/https://www.news18.com/photogallery/movies/100-years-of-indian-cinema-the-100-greatest-indian-films-of-all-time-903065-1.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It was ranked #6 in ''[[Outlook (Indian magazine)|Outlook Magazine]]''{{'}}s poll of the 25 leading Indian directors for Hindi cinema's best films in 2003.<ref>{{Cite web|title=6.
