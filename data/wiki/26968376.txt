'''''Certified Copy''''' ({{langx|fr|'''Copie conforme'''}}) is a 2010 [[art film]] written and directed by [[Abbas Kiarostami]]. Set in [[Tuscany]], the film focuses on a British writer ([[William Shimell]]) and a French antiques dealer ([[Juliette Binoche]]), whose relationship undergoes an odd transformation over the course of a day. The film is a co-production of France, Italy and Belgium.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://collections-search.bfi.org.uk/web/Details/ChoiceFilmWorks/150746340|title=Certified Copy|publisher=[[British Film Institute]]|access-date=26 July 2025}}</ref> The dialogue is in English, French and Italian.<ref>{{cite web|last=Cheshire|first=Godfrey|date=23 May 2012|url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2303-certified-copy-at-home-and-abroad|title=Certified Copy: At Home and Abroad|publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]]|access-date=4 June 2013}}</ref>

''Certified Copy'' premiered at the [[2010 Cannes Film Festival]], where Binoche won the [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] award for her performance. Critically acclaimed, the film is considered to be among the best of the year and the decade.

==Plot==
British writer James Miller makes a stop in [[Arezzo]], Tuscany, on a tour to promote his new book, ''Certified Copy'', which argues that, in art, issues of authenticity are irrelevant because every reproduction is itself an original, and even the original is a copy of another form. A French antiques dealer, who is never named, attends the event with her 11-year-old son in order to have Miller sign several copies of the book she has purchased, but has to leave early because her son is hungry and becomes a distraction. She leaves her phone number with Miller's book's translator. Later, her son suggests that she is romantically interested in Miller, which she denies.

Miller and the woman later meet at her shop. Though she had intended for them to browse antiques together, he instead suggests that they go for a drive, as long as they are back for his 9 p.m. train. On their aimless trip, Miller signs six copies of his book for the woman, including a copy for her sister, Marie, in which he inscribes a note that the woman dislikes. The two debate the premise of his book, including the relationship between authenticity and simplicity. During the conversation, which occasionally becomes strained, Miller points out that even the ''[[Mona Lisa]]'' is a "copy" of the woman who modeled for the painting, real or imagined.

The woman takes Miller to an art museum to see another famous "copy", but he is uninterested, saying that he has already finished work on the book and is not interested in further examples. The woman becomes increasingly distraught as she complains about her rebellious son to Miller, but he attempts to rationalize her son's behavior, frustrating the woman.

At his insistence, they visit a nearby café for coffee. When Miller steps out to take a phone call, the café owner begins speaking to the woman, mistaking Miller for her husband. The antiques dealer plays along as the café owner espouses the importance of marriage, regardless of the flaws of one's partner. Miller returns and the woman tells him about the café owner's assumption of their relationship.

Once they leave the café, the nature of their discussion changes, and they begin to speak in a combination of French and English instead of just English (Miller states that he only learned French, not Italian, in school). They also begin to behave as though they are a married couple, referring to the woman's son as "our" son. She expresses unhappiness over their 15th wedding anniversary the previous night, during which he fell asleep while she was getting ready in the bathroom, and tells him his constant traveling for work has made him emotionally distant.

Walking through the city, they encounter an older couple and discuss their interpretations of a nearby statue. The older man pulls Miller aside and offers unsolicited "fatherly" advice: that a small gesture of affection could mend the rift between Miller and his wife. As Miller and his wife walk away, he places his hand on her shoulder; when they go into a restaurant, she goes to the bathroom and applies lipstick and puts on earrings.

When she returns to the table, Miller is angry, having clashed with the waiter over the quality of the wine. They begin to fight and Miller leaves the restaurant, though he waits for her outside. They soon find themselves at the hotel where Miller and his wife spent their honeymoon, though Miller does not remember the specifics of the hotel.

The woman goes inside and asks to tour the room; she and Miller reminisce about their wedding night, although Miller does not remember many details. The woman appeals to Miller, saying that they should be more accepting of each other's faults, because the alternative is loneliness. He appears to rebuff her, saying he still has to catch his 9 p.m. train. Miller then goes into the bathroom and looks into the mirror; when nearby church bells begin to toll at 8 p.m., he leaves the bathroom.

==Production==
Abbas Kiarostami and Juliette Binoche first met and became friends in the mid-1990s. Since then they had both desired to work together and in 2008 Binoche appeared briefly in Kiarostami's experimental film ''[[Shirin (film)|Shirin]]''. During a visit to [[Tehran]] by Binoche, Kiarostami told Binoche the synopsis of ''Certified Copy'' as a casual anecdote, which she said that she fully believed until he confessed to having made it up.<ref name=europe1>{{cite web|last=Attali|first=Danielle|date=16 May 2010|url=http://festival-cannes.europe1.fr/festival-de-cannes/2010/Actu/Vertigineuse-Juliette-Binoche-186627/|title=Vertigineuse Juliette Binoche|language=fr|publisher=[[Europe 1]]|access-date=17 May 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100519053136/http://festival-cannes.europe1.fr/festival-de-cannes/2010/Actu/Vertigineuse-Juliette-Binoche-186627/|archive-date=19 May 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> According to Kiarostami, studying the reactions of Binoche as she listened to the story was a vital part of the film's further development. "The film started to build according to the story that I was telling, but also according to my knowledge of her as a woman with her vulnerability, with her sensitivity, with what I knew about her soul, about her relationship with her children."<ref name=varietyqa>{{cite magazine|last=Vivarelli|first=Nick|date=18 May 2010|url=https://www.variety.com/index.asp?layout=festivals&jump=story&id=3628&articleid=VR1118019551|title=Q&A: 'Certified' director Abbas Kiarostami|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=20 May 2010}}</ref>

The film suffered from several delays and changes in the cast. Filming was originally supposed to start in October 2007 with English as the principal language.<ref>{{cite web|last=Carver|first=Antonia|date=20 May 2007|url=http://www.screendaily.com/english-language-debut-for-kiarostami/4032665.article|title=English-language debut for Kiarostami|website=[[Screen Daily]]|access-date=17 April 2010}}</ref> A second attempt was planned for March 2008, this time mainly in French and with [[Sami Frey]] opposite Binoche.<ref>{{cite web|last=Tartaglione-Vialatte|first=Nancy|date=2 November 2007|url=http://www.screendaily.com/kiarostamis-first-non-iranian-feature-to-be-shot-in-french/4035672.article|title=Kiarostami's first non-Iranian feature to be shot in French|website=[[Screen Daily]]|access-date=19 April 2010}}</ref> Next up to be attached was [[François Cluzet]], with filming scheduled to begin in May 2009.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Delignières|first=Clémentine|date=5 February 2009|url=http://www.lesinrocks.com/cine/cinema-article/article/abbas-kiarostami-en-francais|title=Abbas Kiarostami en français|language=fr|magazine=[[Lesinrocks.fr]]|access-date=19 April 2010}}</ref> At some point [[Robert De Niro]] was in negotiation for the role, but eventually it was offered to William Shimell, a [[baritone]] opera singer who had never acted in a film before.<ref>{{cite news|date=13 July 2009|url=http://www.leparisien.fr/loisirs-et-spectacles/en-toscane-avec-juliette-binoche-13-07-2009-578511.php|title=En Toscane avec Juliette Binoche|language=fr|newspaper=[[Le Parisien]]|access-date=16 April 2010}}</ref> Kiarostami had met Shimell in 2008 during his debut as an opera director, an adaptation of ''[[Così fan tutte]]'' for the [[Aix-en-Provence Festival]], in which Shimell performed as Don Alfonso.<ref>{{cite news|last=Higgins|first=Charlotte|date=7 May 2010|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/may/07/abbas-kiarostami-certified-copy-cannes|title=Kiarostami reels in British opera singer's first film role|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=8 May 2010}}</ref> "When I saw him, I immediately perceived in him the strength, finesse and humour of the character", Kiarostami commented on his choice.<ref name=europe1 />

Production was led by MK2 in co-production with [[France 3 Cinéma]] and the Italian company BiBi Film. Funding was granted by the [[National Center of Cinematography and the moving image|CNC]] and via pre-sales to [[Groupe Canal+|Canal+]]. The budget was €3.8 million.<ref>{{cite web|last=Lemercier|first=Fabien|date=16 April 2010|url=https://cineuropa.org/newsdetail.aspx?lang=en&documentID=143119|title=Bouchareb, Haroun and Kiarostami under French banner|website=[[Cineuropa]]|access-date=16 April 2010}}</ref>

Filming finally began on 8 June 2009 on location in [[Tuscany]], using locations in [[Cortona]], [[Lucignano]] and [[Arezzo]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Tartaglione|first=Nancy|date=8 June 2009|url=http://www.screendaily.com/news/production/europe/kiarostamis-certified-copy-begins-shooting-in-italy/5002248.article|title=Kiarostami's Certified Copy begins shooting in Italy|website=[[Screen Daily]]|access-date=16 April 2010}}</ref> Kiarostami is normally known for making films with amateur actors and almost no budget, but said he had no difficulties in making the transition to European cinema: "This was the simplest film for me to work on—even more simple than the work I've done on my shorts, because I was working with a professional team both in front of and behind the camera." He also noted how he for once felt free to express whatever he wanted in the film.<ref>{{cite news|last=Lake|first=Ed|date=18 October 2009|url=http://www.thenational.ae/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20091019/ART/710189962|title=Final comment|newspaper=[[The National (Abu Dhabi)|The National]]|access-date=24 April 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100316124950/http://www.thenational.ae/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F20091019%2FART%2F710189962|archive-date=16 March 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref>

''[[The Guardian]]'' reported that this was significantly better than how Kiarostami's films usually perform in the United Kingdom.<ref>{{cite news|last=Gant|first=Charles|date=7 September 2010|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/filmblog/2010/sep/07/last-exorcism-jennifer-aniston-steve-carell|title=The Last Exorcism drives out Jennifer Aniston and Steve Carell from UK cinemas|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=13 September 2010}}</ref> [[IFC Films]] acquired the rights for American distribution and Madman for Australia and New Zealand.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Leffler |first=Rebecca |date=20 May 2010 |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film-festival/cannes/news/e3id31d4e02483751f2953f1569fba0cbe7 |title=IFC takes 'Certified Copy' for U.S. |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=20 May 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100522174204/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film-festival/cannes/news/e3id31d4e02483751f2953f1569fba0cbe7 |archive-date=22 May 2010}}</ref>

During the release, the film was often billed as the director's first film to be made outside his native Iran, but in reality it was only his first dramatic feature film to be shot abroad. It was preceded by the 2001 documentary ''[[ABC Africa]]'' and a segment in the 2005 anthology film ''[[Tickets (film)|Tickets]]''.<ref name=varietyqa /> In Iran, the film was automatically banned by the [[Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance]]. Jamal Shourjeh from the ministry's Cinematic Department explained: "''Certified Copy'' has been produced by a non-Iranian in Italy in [[western culture]]; therefore, it should have a license for national screening".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Leffler |first=Rebecca |date=18 August 2009 |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film/news/e3i2ea12a163e397089814f7ea9f933b9f3 |title=Abbas Kiarostami's 'Copy' banned in Iran |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=16 April 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090826124325/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film/news/e3i2ea12a163e397089814f7ea9f933b9f3 |archive-date=26 August 2009}}</ref> Iran's deputy culture minister [[Javad Shamaqdari]] later said that the film would remain banned from general screening, citing Juliette Binoche's attire in the film as the main reason, but that it would be allowed to be shown "in some private circles and universities".<ref>{{cite magazine|date=29 May 2010|url=https://variety.com/2010/film/markets-festivals/iran-scraps-local-copy-1118019952/|title=Iran scraps local 'Copy'|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=21 March 2025}}</ref>

==Reception==
The French reception was very positive. "Through strokes of chased dialogue and close-ups, Abbas Kiarostami achieves a universal film, between levity and drama, about the feelings that are diluted over time", wrote Renaud Baronian in ''[[Le Parisien]]''. Baronian also liked the lead performances, finding that Shimell "impresses with his restraint" while Binoche "proves overwhelming".<ref>{{cite news|last=Baronian|first=Renaud|date=18 May 2010|url=http://www.leparisien.fr/festival-cannes/copie-conforme-fascinant-18-05-2010-926156.php|title=" Copie conforme " : fascinant|language=fr|newspaper=[[Le Parisien]]|access-date=19 May 2010|quote=A coups de dialogues ciselés et de plans serrés, Abbas Kiarostami aboutit à un film universel, entre légèreté et drame, sur les sentiments qui se diluent au fil du temps. Dans les rôles principaux, William Shimell, un chanteur lyrique dont c'est le premier rôle, épate par sa retenue et Juliette Binoche se révèle bouleversante.}}</ref> Nicholas Schiavi of ''Excessif'' rated the film with four stars out of five and applauded Kiarostami's craft: "With a formally refined technique and an incredible sense of frames within frames, the Iranian filmmaker offers a lesson in cinema. Each framework is a fixed organic tableau where the depth of field and the games of mirrors shape a sensory architecture. One can perceive the remains of the past and the possibilities of an idyll."<ref>{{cite web|last=Schiavi|first=Nicholas|date=18 May 2010|url=http://www.excessif.com/cinema/critique-copie-conforme-5855905-760.html|title=Un superbe film de (dés)amour|language=fr|website=Excessif|publisher=[[TF1]]|access-date=19 May 2010|quote=Avec une technique formellement épurée et un sens inouï des surcadrages, le cinéaste iranien propose une leçon de cinéma. Chaque plan fixe est un tableau organique où la profondeur de champ et les jeux de miroirs façonnent une architecture sensorielle. On y aperçoit les vestiges du passé et les possibilités d'une idylle.|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100521053905/http://www.excessif.com/cinema/critique-copie-conforme-5855905-760.html|archive-date=21 May 2010|url-status=dead}}</ref> Emmanuèle Frois of ''[[Le Figaro]]'' wrote: "Inspired by [[Roberto Rossellini]], Abbas Kiarostami signs his own ''[[Journey to Italy]]'' without plagiarizing the original." Frois noted how believable she found the couple with their "desires, disappointments, misunderstandings, hopes", and how the narrative alternates between drama and comedy, "like in life."<ref>{{cite news|last=Frois|first=Emmanuèle|date=18 May 2010|url=http://www.lefigaro.fr/cinema/2010/05/18/03002-20100518ARTFIG00539--copie-conforme-le-voyage-en-italie-d-abbas-kiarostami.php|title=" Copie Conforme ": le voyage en Italie d'Abbas Kiarostami|language=fr|newspaper=[[Le Figaro]]|access-date=19 May 2010|quote=S'inspirant de Roberto Rossellini, Abbas Kiarostami signe son propre Voyage en Italie sans plagier l'original. Il filme le couple dans sa vérité et ses mensonges, les apparences trompeuses, les non dits, les éternels reproches, les désirs, les désenchantements, les malentendus, les espoirs, vains. [...] Abbas Kiarostami bat la mesure sur une partition alternant drame et comédie. Comme dans la vie.}}</ref>

The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] indicates that 89% of the film's American and British reviews were overall positive, based on 136 reviews, with an average rating of 7.8/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "The main stars are absolutely perfect in this absorbing, existential drama that dissects human relationships."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/certified_copy|title=Certified Copy|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=26 July 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 82 out of 100, based on 23 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/certified-copy |title=Certified Copy |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=16 March 2018}}</ref>

In ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'', Deborah Young called ''Certified Copy'' "a delicate, bittersweet comedy". Young wrote that Binoche was given "a chance to display her noteworthy gifts as a comedienne, switching effortlessly from English to French and Italian to build a character that is resentful, manipulative and seductive all at once", and that Shimell's "elegant cool is very close to [[George Sanders]]'", referring to Sanders' role in ''[[Journey to Italy]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Young |first=Deborah |date=18 May 2010 |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film-festival/cannes/reviews/e3i6478fc41cf5464a55f6a7c79b1732a95 |title=Certified Copy – Film Review |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=19 May 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100521045602/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film-festival/cannes/reviews/e3i6478fc41cf5464a55f6a7c79b1732a95 |archive-date=21 May 2010}}</ref> [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' was less impressed: "It is a film that is pregnant with ideas, and for aspiring to a cinema of ideas Kiarostami is to be thanked and admired. But the simple human inter-relation between the two characters is never in the smallest way convincing, and there is a translated, inert feel to the dialogue." Bradshaw rated the film with two stars out of five and said that it sometimes looks like "the work of a highly intelligent and observant space alien who still has not quite grasped how Earthlings actually relate to each other."<ref>{{cite news|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|author-link=Peter Bradshaw|date=2 September 2010|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/sep/02/certified-copy-review|title=Film review: Certified Copy|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=2 September 2010}}</ref> [[David Denby (film critic)|David Denby]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' called the film "fascinating, beautiful, and intentionally enraging: a brilliant return to form from a director whose work, in the past, has joined modernist game-playing to ethical propriety and modesty." In a review of Kiarostami's next film, ''[[Like Someone in Love (film)|Like Someone in Love]]'', Denby further described ''Certified Copy'' as "nearly perverse in its ambiguity... an intricately conjoined double fiction, each tale uneasily reverberating inside its linked opposite."<ref>{{cite magazine |last= Denby |first= David |title= Dangerous Liaisons |magazine= [[The New Yorker]] |volume= 89 |issue= 3 |date= 4 March 2013 |page= 80}}</ref> [[Stephen Holden]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' compared the film to, and suggested it has drawn inspiration from, several European films from the 1950s and early 1960s, including ''[[Journey to Italy]]'', ''[[L'Avventura]]'' and ''[[Last Year at Marienbad]]'', as well as several films from the 1990s and 2000s: ''[[In the Mood for Love]]'', ''[[Before Sunrise]]'' and ''[[Before Sunset]]''.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Holden|first=Stephen|author-link=Stephen Holden|date=10 March 2011|url=https://movies.nytimes.com/2011/03/11/movies/juliette-binoche-in-kiarostamis-certified-copy-review.html|title=On the Road, Packing Querulous Erudition|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=21 March 2011}}</ref>

Keith Uhlich of ''[[Time Out New York]]'' named ''Certified Copy'' the best film of 2011, calling it Kiarostami's strongest work.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Uhlich|first=Keith|date=13 December 2011|title=The Best (and Worst) Films of 2011: Keith Uhlich's Picks|magazine=[[Time Out New York]]|url=https://www.timeout.com/newyork/film/the-best-and-worst-films-of-2011-keith-uhlichs-picks|access-date=16 June 2020|archive-date=22 June 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200622203249/https://www.timeout.com/newyork/film/the-best-and-worst-films-of-2011-keith-uhlichs-picks|url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2020, Uhlich named it the best film of the 2010s.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Uhlich|first=Keith|date=21 January 2020|title=Decade-Dance: 10 for '10s|url=https://www.keithuhlich.com/2020/01/decade-dance-10-for-10s.html|access-date=16 June 2020|website=Keith Uhlich}}</ref>

Binoche received the [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] award at the Cannes prize ceremony on 23 May 2010. The actress used her acceptance speech to bring attention to the Iranian director [[Jafar Panahi]]. Panahi had been set to be in the festival's jury, but was held and imprisoned by the Iranian regime during the event.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Chang|first=Justin|date=23 May 2010|url=https://www.variety.com/article/VR1118019731.html?categoryid=13&cs=1|archive-url=https://archive.today/20121203034252/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1118019731.html?categoryid=13&cs=1|url-status=dead|archive-date=3 December 2012|title='Uncle Boonmee' wins Palme d'Or|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=23 May 2010}}</ref> ''Certified Copy'' was later voted the forty-sixth greatest film since 2000 in an international critics' poll conducted by [[BBC]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films |title=The 21st century's 100 greatest films |publisher=BBC |date=23 August 2016 |access-date=26 January 2017}}</ref> In July 2025, it ranked number 91 on ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-movies-of-the-21st-century-1235200512/ | title=100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | date=July 1, 2025}}</ref> It was also one of the films voted for the "Readers' Choice" edition of ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century," finishing at number 313.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref>
