'''''News from Home''''' is a 1976<ref>{{cite web |url=https://chantalakerman.foundation/works/news-from-home/ |title=News from Home |publisher=Fondation Chantal Akerman |access-date=23 January 2024}}</ref> [[French language|French-language]] [[avant-garde]] [[documentary film]] directed by [[Chantal Akerman]]. The film consists of long takes of locations in [[New York City]] set to Akerman's [[voice-over]] as she reads letters that her mother sent her between 1971 and 1973 when Akerman lived in the city. Despite being relatively obscure, it has been featured in both [[Sight and Sound|''Sight and Sound''<nowiki/>'s]] director's and critic's poll to determine the greatest films of all time.

==Production==
In November 1971, at the age of 21, Belgian film director Chantal Akerman moved to New York City. There she took petty jobs, made films and befriended filmmakers such as [[Jonas Mekas]] and the cinematographer [[Babette Mangolte]], who became one of her recurring collaborators. According to Akerman, she spent the period living "like a vagabond."<ref name=brilliantdecade>{{cite web |last=Anderson |first=Melissa |date=19 January 2010 |url=http://www.movingimagesource.us/articles/her-brilliant-decade-20100119 |title=Her Brilliant Decade |work=[[Moving Image Source]] |publisher=[[Museum of the Moving Image (New York City)|Museum of the Moving Image]] |access-date=6 December 2010 |url-status=live |archive-date=21 April 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130421180340/http://www.movingimagesource.us/articles/her-brilliant-decade-20100119 }}</ref> Principal photography for ''News from Home'' took place in the summer of 1976, three years after Akerman had returned to Belgium and after she had achieved critical success with the 1975 film ''[[Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles]]''. Locations chosen for ''News from Home'' correspond to the areas where Akerman used to take walks, which include the [[Times Square – 42nd Street (New York City Subway)|Times Square subway station]] and a long shot driving up [[Tenth Avenue (Manhattan)|Tenth Avenue]] from [[List of numbered streets in Manhattan#30th Street|30th]] to 49th Streets in [[Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan|Hell's Kitchen]]. Other scenes were filmed in [[Tribeca]] and on the [[Staten Island Ferry]]. The sound was not recorded along with the images, but was added later.<ref name=brilliantdecade />

==Home media==
''News from Home'' was released on [[DVD]] in France through Carlotta Films on 18 April 2007.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.evene.fr/cinema/films/news-from-home-8388.php |title=News from home - film de Chantal Akerman |language=French |work=Evene |publisher=[[Le Figaro|Figaromedias]] |access-date=7 December 2010}}</ref> In 2010, [[The Criterion Collection]] released the film on DVD through its "Eclipse" series, as part of a set called ''Chantal Akerman in the Seventies''. The set includes four feature films that Akerman directed in the 1970s as well as a number of short films.<ref>{{cite web |last=Kehr |first=Dave |date=31 January 2010 |title=The Evolving Vision of a Belgian Auteur |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2010/01/31/movies/homevideo/31kehr.html |work=[[The New York Times]] |access-date=6 December 2010}}</ref> ''News from Home'' is included on the first disc of the set, called ''The New York Films''.<ref name=brilliantdecade />

On 23 January 2024, The Criterion Collection released ''News from Home'', along with eight other works by Akerman, on [[Blu-ray]] as part of the box set ''Chantal Akerman Masterpieces (1968–1978)''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.criterion.com/boxsets/7045-chantal-akerman-masterpieces-19681978 |title=Chantal Akerman Masterpieces, 1968–1978 |website=Criterion.com |publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]] |access-date=23 January 2024}}</ref>

==Reception==
''News from Home'' has been met with highly positive critical reception, especially for its depiction of alienation.<ref name="auto">{{Cite web |last=Rebanal |first=Jaime |date=2019-11-08 |title=‘News from Home’ Review: Finding Solace in the Alienation from Your So-Called Home |url=https://cinemafromthespectrum.com/2019/11/08/news-from-home-review-finding-solace-in-the-alienation-from-your-so-called-home/ |access-date=2026-09-18 |website=Cinema from the Spectrum |language=en}}</ref> The film has a [[List of films with a 100% rating on Rotten Tomatoes|100% rating]] on [[Rotten Tomatoes]], based on 8 reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/news_from_home |title=News From Home |website=Rotten Tomatoes}}</ref> In the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'', [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] called it "one of the best depictions of the alienation of exile that I know."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://enzian.org/film/news-from-home/ |title=News From Home |website=Enzian Theater}}</ref> As appreciation of Chantal Akerman's films increased, News from Home gained stature among critics.<ref name="auto"/>

In the [[The Sight and Sound Greatest Films of All Time 2022|2022 Sight and Sound critic's poll]], the film received 45 votes from critics and was ranked in 52nd place, tying with [[Ali: Fear Eats the Soul]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Greatest Films of All Time |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/greatest-films-all-time |access-date=2026-09-18 |website=British Film Institute |language=en}}</ref><ref name="auto1">{{Cite web |title=News from Home (1976) |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/5b0cea46-7c07-5707-a102-ddb7677c51b8/news-from-home |access-date=2026-09-18 |website=British Film Institute |language=en}}</ref> In the [[The Sight and Sound Greatest Films of All Time 2022|2022 Sight and Sound director's poll]], the films received 10 votes from directors and was ranked in 72nd place, tying with [[Shoah (film)|''Shoah'']], ''[[The Searchers]]'', [[Modern Times (film)|''Modern Times'']], ''[[Touki Bouki]]'', ''[[Sans Soleil]]'', ''[[The Spirit of the Beehive]]'', ''[[The Seventh Seal]]'', [[The Red Shoes (1948 film)|''The Red Shoes'']], [[Chinatown (1974 film)|''Chinatown'']], ''[[Ikiru]]'', [[L'Argent (1983 film)|''L'Argent'']], ''[[The Conversation]]'', [[Kes (film)|''Kes'']], ''[[A Separation]]'', [[Salò, or the 120 Days of Sodom|''Salo, or the 120 Days of Sodom'']], [[Where Is the Friend's House?|''Where is the Friends House'']], and ''[[A Brighter Summer Day]]''.<ref name="auto1"/><ref>{{Cite web |title=Directors’ 100 Greatest Films of All Time |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/directors-100-greatest-films-all-time |access-date=2026-09-18 |website=British Film Institute |language=en}}</ref>

It was listed as the third best Chantal Akerman film by opinion blog Films Fatale in 2026, behind ''[[D'Est]]'' and ''[[Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles]]''. The blog theorized "Akerman is trying to find peace and belonging and allowing us to know our concerns are shared."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-03-05 |title=Filmography Worship: Ranking Every Chantal Akerman Film |url=https://www.filmsfatale.com/blog/2026/3/5/filmography-worship-ranking-every-chantal-akerman-film |access-date=2026-09-18 |website=Films Fatale |language=en-US}}</ref>
