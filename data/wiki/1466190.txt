'''''Carol for Another Christmas''''' is a 1964 American TV movie, written by [[Rod Serling]] as a modernization of [[Charles Dickens]]' 1843 novella ''[[A Christmas Carol]]'' and a plea for global cooperation. It was the first in a [[United Nations television film series|planned series of television specials]] developed to promote the [[United Nations]] and educate viewers about its mission.<ref name="tvinciguerra">{{cite news |last=Vinciguerra |first=Thomas |date=December 20, 2007 |title=Marley Is Dead, Killed in a Nuclear War |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/12/20/fashion/20CAROL.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |location=[[New York City]] |page=G12 |access-date=December 21, 2015 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151221183619/http://www.nytimes.com/2007/12/20/fashion/20CAROL.html?_r=3 |archive-date=December 21, 2015 }}</ref> Originally televised on the [[American Broadcasting Company]] (ABC) network on December 28, 1964,<ref name=tvinciguerra /><ref>{{citation |date=December 31, 1964 |title=Radio-TV |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=xcADAAAAMBAJ&pg=PA66 |journal=[[Jet (magazine)|Jet]]|location=[[Chicago, Illinois]]|publisher=[[Johnson Publishing Company]] |page=66 |access-date=December 15, 2015}}</ref> it was not shown again for 48 years, until [[Turner Classic Movies]] (TCM) broadcast it on December 16, 2012.<ref name=tcmsched>{{cite web |url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/963811/a-carol-for-another-christmas |title=Carol For Another Christmas, A (1964) – Overview |publisher=[[Turner Classic Movies]] |url-status=dead|archive-date=December 10, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121210054745/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/963811/Carol-for-Another-Christmas-A/ |access-date=December 21, 2015}}</ref><ref name=hadley>{{cite web |last=Hadley |first=Mitchell |url=http://www.tvparty.com/xmas-carol-for-another-christmas.html |title=Carol For Another Christmas |publisher=TVparty! |url-status=live|archive-date=January 15, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130115093031/http://www.tvparty.com/xmas-carol-for-another-christmas.html |access-date=December 21, 2015}}</ref>

The film was the only television program ever directed by [[Joseph L. Mankiewicz]],<ref name=tvinciguerra /> and includes [[Peter Sellers]]' first performance after recovering from a near-fatal heart attack earlier in 1964. The film also features [[Sterling Hayden]], who had previously costarred with Sellers in ''[[Dr. Strangelove]]''<ref name=lobianco>{{cite web |last=LoBianco |first=Lorraine |url=https://www.tcm.com/this-month/article/535539 |title=Carol For Another Christmas |publisher=[[Turner Classic Movies]] |url-status=live|archive-date=December 21, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151221191021/http://www.tcm.com/this-month/article.html?id=518186%7C535539 |access-date=December 21, 2015}}</ref> and [[Britt Ekland]], who was married to Sellers at the time.

==Plot==
On [[Christmas Eve]], rich American industrialist Daniel Grudge (Hayden) sits alone in a dark room of his mansion playing a record of a [[World War II]]-era popular song, "[[Don't Sit Under the Apple Tree (with Anyone Else but Me)]]" by [[The Andrews Sisters]]. He looks at a framed display of war medals on the wall and seems about to cry. He shuts off the record player, but as he leaves the room, he hears the record start to play again of its own accord, despite the player being shut off. Downstairs, he meets a visitor, his nephew Fred (Gazzara). Grudge caustically notes that Fred always comes to him for help with various causes and asks what cause he is promoting this time. Fred complains that Grudge used his influence to cancel a [[Cultural diplomacy|cultural exchange]] program that Fred's university had planned with a Polish counterpart. In the ensuing argument with Fred, Grudge takes the [[isolationism|isolationist]] position that the United States should stay out of international affairs and not participate in cultural exchange programs, [[foreign aid]] to the needy, or discussions at the United Nations. Grudge distrusts foreign countries, and contends that the U.S. should build up its arsenal, including [[nuclear weapon]]s, and make sure other countries know the U.S. is willing to use them. Fred disagrees, arguing that the U.S. should help all people in need and foster international communication in order to avoid future wars and nuclear destruction. As Fred leaves, he reminds his uncle that they have one thing in common: their love for Grudge's son Marley, who was killed in World War II on Christmas Eve 1944.

After Fred leaves, Grudge once again hears the record playing upstairs, and sees a short vision of the deceased Marley sitting at the dining room table. Suddenly, Grudge finds himself on the deck of a fogbound [[troopship]] carrying coffins draped in flags, guarded by soldiers at attention. A soldier on board introduces himself as the [[Ghost of Christmas Past]] (Lawrence) and explains that the ship is carrying the dead of all nations from [[World War I]]. Through the fog, Grudge spots a second vessel carrying World War II's dead, and learns they are in a huge convoy, carrying the dead from conflicts through history. The Ghost suggests that the way to stop the killing is to spend more time talking, since when talking stops, fighting starts. He and Grudge revisit a scene from Grudge's past in which Grudge, a Navy commander, accompanied by his [[Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service|WAVE]] driver (Saint), visited a hospital in devastated [[Hiroshima]] and saw Japanese schoolchildren whose faces had been destroyed by the [[Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki|atom bomb]].

Grudge walks through a door and meets the [[Ghost of Christmas Present]] (Hingle), who is feasting on an excessively large [[Christmas dinner]] on Grudge's dining table. This new Ghost turns on a light and shows Grudge that next to the dining room is an [[internment camp]] full of [[displaced person]]s from different nations who are poor, hungry, and lacking adequate shelter. These people search through the snow for food as the Ghost eats in front of them. When Grudge criticizes the Ghost for this behavior, the Ghost reminds Grudge of his earlier statement to Fred that refusing donations to the needy would make them less needy and more self-reliant. The Ghost harangues Grudge with statistics and information about needy people in the world and finally in a fit of anger pulls the tablecloth, dumping huge amounts of leftover food on the floor. Grudge cannot stand any more and runs away into the dark.

Grudge emerges into destroyed ruins that he recognizes as having been his local [[town hall]], where he encounters the [[Ghost of Christmas Future]] (Shaw). This Ghost explains that the town hall was wrecked in a disastrous nuclear conflict that killed most of the world's people. A handful of survivors enter, led by a [[demagogue]] called "Imperial Me" (Sellers) who wears a [[Santa suit]] and a [[cowboy hat]] cut into a crown. The crowd cheers as Imperial Me is paraded in and gives a speech exhorting each person to act as an individual in their own self-interest. Grudge watches his butler, Charles (Rodriguez), try unsuccessfully to convince the crowd that acting collectively for the greater good of all is essential for humanity's survival. Imperial Me and the crowd mock Charles as crazy and beat him. Finally Imperial Me has Charles brought forward and charges him with treason. Charles tries to escape but is shot dead by a little boy in a cowboy outfit. Grudge's cook Ruby (Teer) weeps over Charles' body, while the crowd, led by Imperial Me, enthusiastically prepares to first kill the people across the river who had approached them wanting to talk, and then kill off each other until only one person is left. An agitated Grudge asks the Ghost if this is the world "as it must be, or as it might be", but the Ghost simply leaves without answering.

A shaken Grudge awakens back in the real world on Christmas morning, on the floor of his intact study with the phone in his hand. His nephew Fred appears and says that Grudge called him at 3 a.m. and asked him to stop by on his way to church. Grudge apologizes to Fred for his statements of the previous evening and, without explaining the reason for his change of heart, indicates cautious support for the United Nations and international diplomacy as a way to prevent future wars. Grudge further shows his new [[internationalism (politics)|internationalism]] by enjoying a radio broadcast of the children of UN delegates singing [[Christmas carol]]s in their native languages. Fred leaves and Grudge, rather than have Charles serve him on a tray as usual, goes into the kitchen to have his Christmas morning coffee with Charles and Ruby.

==Production==
''Carol for Another Christmas'' was the first in a [[United Nations television film series|series of television specials commissioned by the United Nations]] with the goal of educating viewers about the mission and work of the UN and thereby gaining more widespread support. (Six specials were originally planned, but only four were produced.) The nonprofit Telsun Foundation was formed to develop the programs, and Xerox agreed to contribute $4 million to underwrite the costs of production and air time, thus allowing the programs to be broadcast without commercial interruption.<ref name=hadleyun>{{cite web |url=http://www.tvparty.com/fall-un.html |title=The UN Goes to the Movies |last1=Hadley |first1=Mitchell |website=TVparty.com |publisher=TVparty! |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080512021227/http://www.tvparty.com/fall-un.html |archive-date=May 12, 2008 |access-date=December 21, 2015}}</ref><ref name=adams>{{cite news |last=Adams |first=Val |date=April 9, 1964 |title=6 Dramas on U.N. Planned For TV |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1964/04/09/6-dramas-on-un-planned-for-tv.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |location=[[New York City]] |url-access=subscription |access-date=December 21, 2015 }}</ref>

The film was the only television work ever done by Mankiewicz, who, according to [[Phil Hall (US writer)|Phil Hall]], was happy to have work following the damage done to his reputation by ''[[Cleopatra (1963 film)|Cleopatra]]'' the previous year.<ref name=hall>{{cite web |url=https://www.filmthreat.com/features/28672/ |title=The Bootleg Files: Carol For Another Christmas |last=Hall |first=Phil |author-link=Phil Hall (US writer) |website=Filmthreat.com |publisher=[[Film Threat]] |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101220041743/http://www.filmthreat.com/features/28672/ |archive-date=December 20, 2010 |access-date=December 21, 2015}}</ref>
The actors involved with the production reportedly agreed to waive their fees due to the nature and perceived importance of the program.<ref name=adams /> Peter Sellers, who at the time was reported to charge $750,000 or more, appeared for only $350, the [[Screen Actors Guild]] weekly minimum.<ref name=laurent>{{cite news |last=Laurent |first=Lawrence |date=December 30, 1964 |title=Radio and Television: Why All the Fuss Over Xerox Series? |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |location=[[Washington, D.C.]]|page=B8}}</ref>

Filming took place at the Michael Myerberg Studios located on Long Island, New York,<ref name=lobianco /><ref>{{cite news |last=Thompson |first=Howard |date=December 17, 1964 |title=Movie Review: Santa Claus Conquers the Martians (1964): Santa vs. Martians |url=https://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9507E7DA123CEE32A25754C1A9649D946591D6CF |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |location=[[New York City]] |access-date=December 23, 2015 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924092422/http://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9507E7DA123CEE32A25754C1A9649D946591D6CF |archive-date=September 24, 2015 |quote=Mr. Jacobson made his little picture at the Michael Myerberg Studios on Long Island. }}</ref> during the fall of 1964.<ref name=sanantonio>{{cite news |date=September 5, 1964 |title=CBS Backs Musical Version of Prizewinning Comedy |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/29707571/ |newspaper=[[San Antonio Express-News]] |location=[[San Antonio, Texas]] |page=7-D|agency=New York Times News Service| via=Newspapers.com |url-access=subscription |access-date=December 21, 2015 }}</ref> Henry Mancini wrote the theme music, also waiving his usual fee.<ref name=adams /> It later appeared on his 1966 Christmas album ''[[A Merry Mancini Christmas]]''.<ref name=lobianco />

Peter Fonda's scenes as Grudge's late son Marley were cut from the film prior to release, leaving only brief glimpses of Fonda reflected in a glass door and sitting mute at a table.<ref name=hall /> However, Fonda's image remains in the film in the form of a large portrait of Marley hanging prominently in Grudge's study where several scenes take place.<ref name=lobianco /> Although advance publicity articles listed [[Richard Harris]], [[Godfrey Cambridge]] and [[Christopher Plummer]] among the cast, none of these actors appear in the finished film.<ref name=sanantonio /><ref>{{cite news |date=October 25, 1964 |title=UN Drama Will Have Allstar Cast |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/26034653/ |newspaper=[[The Fresno Bee]] |location=[[Fresno, California]] |page=15-TV|agency=[[United Press International|UPI]]| via=Newspapers.com |url-access=subscription |access-date=December 21, 2015 }}</ref>

The main character was originally named Benjamin Grudge so that his name could be shortened to the pun "B. Grudge", but ABC viewed this as an insult to 1964 Republican Presidential candidate [[Barry Goldwater]], whose initials were also B.G., and the character's name was changed to Daniel Grudge, while the broadcast was postponed until eight weeks after the election.<ref name=hall />

==Reception==
Following its original 1964 broadcast, reviews were mixed. While some critics thought the program's lecturing style was appropriate to get its message across to a mass TV audience,<ref>{{cite news |date=January 3, 1965 |title=Critic's Corner: Excerpted Reviews on Recent TV Shows |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/30510457/ |newspaper=[[Press-Telegram|Independent Press-Telegram]] |location=[[Long Beach, California]] |page=Tele-Vues 10 |access-date=December 30, 2015 |via=Newspapers.com |url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Ace |first=Goodman |author-link=Goodman Ace |date=January 30, 1965 |title=Top of My Head: Cleopatra for Another Christmas|magazine=[[Saturday Review (U.S. magazine)|Saturday Review]] |location=[[New York City]] |publisher=[[McCall Corporation]] |page=10 }}</ref> others found it preachy, long-winded or dull.<ref name=hadleyun /><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Shayon |first=Robert Lewis |author-link=Robert Lewis Shayon |date=January 16, 1965 |title=TV and Radio: Warm Scrooge, Cold Grudge |magazine=[[Saturday Review (U.S. magazine)|Saturday Review]] |location=[[New York City]] |publisher=[[McCall Corporation]] |page=39 }}</ref> ''Variety'' described the program as "generalized to the extreme" and ultimately "a disappointment".<ref name=tvinciguerra /> The ''Los Angeles Times'' similarly expressed "disappointment" with the "windy, tedious play",<ref name=smith2>{{cite news |last=Smith |first=Cecil |date=December 31, 1964 |title=The TV Scene: A Look Forward to 'Hamlet' Sunday |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |location=[[Los Angeles, California]]|page=B14}}</ref> complaining that it was "more tract than drama" and saying the opening scene between Grudge and Fred "sounded like the North Hollywood High School debating society and must have cost the play many a bored viewer who turned it off."<ref name=smith1>{{cite news |last=Smith |first=Cecil |date=December 30, 1964 |title=The TV Scene: U.N. Drama Off on Wrong Foot |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |location=[[Los Angeles, California]]|page=C10}}</ref> [[Jack Gould]] in ''The New York Times'' went even further, calling ''Carol'' "a pretentious and wearing exercise in garrulous ineptitude, one of the more dismaying TV disappointments in several seasons."<ref name=gould1>{{cite news |last=Gould |first=Jack |author-link=Jack Gould |date=December 29, 1964 |title=TV: Weak Premiere of U.N. Series: A.B.C. Offers 'Carol for Another Christmas' |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |location=[[New York City]]|page=39}}</ref> After receiving letters from viewers disagreeing with his review, Gould wrote a second opinion piece calling the film "condescending, pretentious and dull", "platitudinous propaganda", and "an exercise in heavy-handed sermonizing that the U.N. is good for you."<ref name=gould2>{{cite news |last=Gould |first=Jack |author-link=Jack Gould |date=January 10, 1965 |title=Criticism: The Critic's View |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |location=[[New York City]]|page=X15}}</ref> ''The Washington Post'' printed a generally positive review, but wrote that the film "failed" because "[t]o most of the audience...the lesson that was being stressed has already been learned."<ref name=laurent /> Despite expressing dissatisfaction with the overall program, critics still praised the performances of some of the actors, particularly Sellers, Lawrence and Hingle.<ref name=tvinciguerra /><ref name=laurent /><ref name=smith1 />

Public reaction was mixed. Gould acknowledged in ''The New York Times'' that based on letters received, "many disagreed" with his sharp criticism of the film, in some cases suggesting that the program was "entitled to generous appreciation, if only because its espousal of the United Nations was so much more worthwhile than the average escapist Hollywood fare."<ref name=gould2 /> However, according to Phil Hall, audiences disliked the film's heavy-handed approach.<ref name=hall /> In addition, many right-wing viewers were fundamentally opposed not only to ''Carol'' but to the overall idea of television programs promoting the UN, and the [[John Birch Society]] had organized a letter-writing campaign against the film even before it was broadcast.<ref name=hadleyun /> Xerox, the sponsor, claimed to have received an approximately equal number of verified letters (about 6,000) for and against the UN film series.<ref name=laurent />

Later, ''Carol''{{'}}s production supervisor, C.O. "Doc" Erickson, described the film as "overdone", saying, "It was too long, too tiring, and beat you over the head too much." [[Marc Scott Zicree]], author of ''[[Twilight Zone literature|The Twilight Zone Companion]]'' (1982), has cited ''Carol'' as an example of Serling's more "didactic" writing. Serling biographer Gordon F. Sander noted that unlike much of Serling's screenwriting dealing with social change, ''Carol'' was depressing and did not end on an optimistic note, possibly because Serling in 1964 was influenced by the then-recent [[assassination of John F. Kennedy]] and the [[Lyndon B. Johnson|Johnson]] administration's [[Vietnam War#Gulf of Tonkin and Johnson's escalation, 1963–1969|escalation of U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War]].<ref name=tvinciguerra />

Mankiewicz]] and ABC)<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.emmys.com/bios/joseph-l-mankiewicz |title=Joseph L. Mankiewicz |publisher=[[Academy of Television Arts & Sciences|Television Academy]] |website=Emmys.com |access-date=December 23, 2015}}</ref> and Best Art Direction or Scenic Design ([[Gene Callahan]] and Jack Wright),<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.emmys.com/awards/nominations/award-search?search_api_views_fulltext=gene+callahan&submit=Search&field_celebrity_details_field_display_name=&field_show_details_field_nominee_show_nr_title=&field_show_details_field_network=All&field_show_details_field_production_company=All&field_nominations_year=1949-01-01+00%3A00%3A00&field_nominations_year_1=2015-01-01+00%3A00%3A00&field_award_category=All |title=Awards Search – Gene Callahan |publisher=[[Academy of Television Arts & Sciences|Television Academy]] |website=Emmys.com |access-date=December 23, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151224110804/http://www.emmys.com/awards/nominations/award-search?search_api_views_fulltext=gene+callahan&submit=Search&field_celebrity_details_field_display_name=&field_show_details_field_nominee_show_nr_title=&field_show_details_field_network=All&field_show_details_field_production_company=All&field_nominations_year=1949-01-01+00%3A00%3A00&field_nominations_year_1=2015-01-01+00%3A00%3A00&field_award_category=All |archive-date=December 24, 2015 |url-status=dead }}</ref> although it did not win in either category.<ref name=lobianco />

==Later rebroadcasts and releases==
Following its initial ABC broadcast, ''Carol for Another Christmas'' was not rebroadcast for nearly five decades. During that time, the film was not commercially available, although it could be seen at the [[Paley Center for Media]] in New York and Los Angeles and the [[UCLA Film and Television Archive]] in Los Angeles. Some [[16 mm]] prints were sold on the educational market, and [[bootleg recording|bootlegs]] were also circulated.<ref name=hall /> In 2000, clips from the film appeared in the [[David Leaf]]/John Scheinfeld documentary ''[[The Unknown Peter Sellers]]''.<ref>{{cite AV media |people=[[David Leaf]] and John Scheinfeld (directors, producers and writers) |date=September 5, 2000 |title=The Unknown Peter Sellers |medium=Documentary film |language=English |location=[[New York City]] |publisher=Winstar |ASIN=B00004U0FP}}</ref>

After 48 years, ''Carol for Another Christmas'' was finally rebroadcast when Turner Classic Movies (TCM) telecast it on December 16, 2012.<ref name=tcmsched /> Since then, TCM has aired the film annually in December<ref>{{cite web |url= http://emanuellevy.com/comment/carol-for-another-christmas-1964-joseph-l-mankiewicz-only-tv-film-penned-by-rod-stering-starring-sterling-hayden/|title=Carol For Another Christmas (1964): Joseph L. Mankiewicz' Only TV Film, Penned by Rod Stering [sic], Starring Sterling Hayden |last1=Levy |first1=Emanuel |author-link=Emanuel Levy|date=December 14, 2015 |website=EmanuelLevy.com |publisher=Emanuel Levy: Cinema 24-7 |access-date=December 22, 2015 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222063853/http://emanuellevy.com/comment/carol-for-another-christmas-1964-joseph-l-mankiewicz-only-tv-film-penned-by-rod-stering-starring-sterling-hayden/ |archive-date=December 22, 2015 | quote=Turner Classic Movies (TCM) began showing ''Carol for Another Christmas'' in December 2012, and continued to do so each December.}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Pennington|first=Gail |date=December 8, 2014 |title=Christmas Day By Day: Your Holiday TV Calendar |url=https://www.stltoday.com/entertainment/television/gail-pennington/christmas-day-by-day-your-holiday-tv-calendar/article_cd833160-bb67-5268-9371-fe80622dfdcf.html |newspaper=[[St. Louis Post-Dispatch]] |location=[[St. Louis, Missouri]] |access-date=December 25, 2015 }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Lawrence |first=Christopher |date=November 26, 2015 |title=Your 2015 Christmas Special Viewing Guide |url=http://www.reviewjournal.com/opinion/columns-blogs/christopher-lawrence/your-2015-christmas-special-viewing-guide |newspaper=[[Las Vegas Review-Journal]] |location=[[Las Vegas, Nevada]] |access-date=December 25, 2015 |url-status=live |archive-date=December 12, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151212162936/http://www.reviewjournal.com/opinion/columns-blogs/christopher-lawrence/your-2015-christmas-special-viewing-guide }}</ref> and occasionally at other times, such as September 2015 in connection with a birthday tribute to Sellers and on April 16, 2024 in honor of Henry Mancini's 100th birthday.<ref>{{cite web |last=CBHagman|date=December 8, 2014 |title=TCM Schedule for Tuesday, September 1: TCM Spotlight: Five Came Back - John Huston |url=http://www.democraticunderground.com/10303132 |website=democraticunderground.com |access-date=December 25, 2015 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151226034943/http://www.democraticunderground.com/10303132 |archive-date=December 26, 2015 }}</ref> TCM has also made the film available for limited-time on-demand streaming via TCM.com. Until December 2021, the print used by TCM did not contain Mancini's theme and instead substituted a recording of children singing traditional carols. In December 2021, TCM aired a print in which the original theme music had been reunited with the film.<ref name=hadley />

The film was released on DVD on December 13, 2016.{{cn|date=June 2026}} In December 2021, it was available to stream on HBO Max.{{cn|date=June 2026}} It is also available free on YouTube.{{cn|date=June 2026}}
