'''''Kings of the Road''''' ({{langx|de|Im Lauf der Zeit}}, "In The Course of Time"<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/richard-brody/where-wim-wenders-went-wrong|title=Where Wim Wenders Went Wrong|first=Richard|last=Brody|magazine=The New Yorker |date=4 September 2015|publisher=|via=www.newyorker.com}}</ref>) is a 1976 German [[road film]] directed by [[Wim Wenders]]. It was the third part of Wenders' "[[Road Movie trilogy|''Road Movie'' trilogy]]" which included ''[[Alice in the Cities]]'' (1974) and ''[[The Wrong Move]]'' (1975). It was the unanimous winner of the [[International Federation of Film Critics|FIPRESCI]] Prize at the [[1976 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/im-lauf-der-zeit/ |title=Festival de Cannes: Kings of the Road |accessdate=2024-02-13|work=festival-cannes.com}}</ref>

==Plot==
Projection-equipment repair mechanic Bruno Winter meets depressed Robert Lander after the latter has driven his car into a river in a half-hearted suicide attempt following a break-up with his wife. Bruno allows Robert to ride with him while his clothes dry, rarely speaking while Bruno drives along the Western side of the [[East Germany|East German]] border in a repair truck, servicing equipment at worn-out movie theaters.

While out on the road, Bruno and Robert encounter several people in various states of despair, including a man whose wife has committed suicide by driving her car into a tree. Robert also drops in on his elderly father to berate him for disrespecting Robert's mother. After Bruno and Robert have a minor brawl after a conversation about Robert and his wife, Robert finally leaves Bruno, though Bruno later spots him riding a train. Bruno continues his service calls to theatres, including one that no longer screens films because the owner regards modern films as exploitative.

== Production ==
The film contains many long shots without dialogue, including an outdoor defecation scene, and it was filmed in black and white by long-time Wenders collaborator [[Robby Müller]].<ref name="100 Greatest">{{cite news|last=Malcolm|first=Derek|title=Wim Wenders: Kings of the Road|url=https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2000/oct/26/artsfeatures1|accessdate=18 April 2012|newspaper=The Guardian|date=26 October 2000}}</ref>

''Kings of the Road'' was shot in black and white, wide-screen (5:3) format, which is explicitly mentioned in the titles. Only the first scene of the film where Winter and Lander meet was scripted; everything else was improvised by the actors.<ref name=Trivia>{{cite web|title=Wim Wenders: Portraits Along the Road - Trivia|url=http://janusfilms.com/wenders/trivia.html|website=Janus Films|accessdate=30 August 2015|archive-date=31 August 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150831180935/http://janusfilms.com/wenders/trivia.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Wim Wenders shot {{convert|49000|m|abbr=on}} of film and the final cut was {{convert|4760|m|abbr=on}}. The camera used was an [[Arriflex 35|ARRI 35 BL]]. The negative material from Kodak (Plus-X and Four-X) copied to [[ORWO]] positive.<ref name="Zeit">Fritz Müller-Scherz (Hrsg.): ''Im Lauf der Zeit'', Frankfurt a.M.: Zweitausendeins, 1976 (Der komplette Film in 1256 Standbildern)</ref>

The songs that are played in Bruno's portable single-disc player are: "[[The More I See You]]" by [[Chris Montez]], "[[Just Like Eddie]]" by [[Heinz Burt|Heinz]], and "[[King of the Road (song)|King of the Road]]" by [[Roger Miller]].

Other songs on the soundtrack of the film were written and performed by the German [[krautrock]] group [[Improved Sound Limited]]'''.'''

The cost of production was [[Deutsche Mark|DM]] 730,800 (then equivalent to US$315,000). The film was financed with a screenplay premium of the Federal Ministry of the Interior of DM 250,000.<ref name="Zeit"/>

In his documentary, ''White Walls'' director Mike Schlömer shot footage along the [[inner-German border]] between [[Lüneburg]] and [[Hof, Bavaria|Hof]], where Wim Wenders shot footage.<ref>Mario Schrader ''Kleine Kinos ganz groß. Ein Streifzug durch 100 Jahre Kinogeschichte im Landkreis Helmstedt.'' BoD, 2009, S. 136 ff. {{ISBN|978-3839113950}}</ref>

It was the first film Wenders made through his new production company Road Movies Produktion. He shot it in black and white because he thought that it was much more realistic and natural than color.<ref>Wakeman, John. World Film Directors, Volume 2, 1945-1985. New York: The H. W. Wilson Company. 1988. p. 1171.</ref>

== Reception ==
In Germany, the Lexicon of International Film wrote that "Wim Wenders' film combines the captivating clarity and epic serenity of a classic Bildungsroman with the mythic qualities of American genre film… Directed in a craftmansly, impeccable style, space itself allows for the unfolding of characters, thoughts and landscapes".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.zweitausendeins.de/filmlexikon/?wert=32167&sucheNach=titel|title=Zweitausendeins. Filmlexikon FILME von A-Z - Im Lauf der Zeit|website=www.zweitausendeins.de}}</ref> Wolf Donner of ''Die Zeit'' said that "motions, sequences of confusingly beautiful and suggestive shots, highly poetic compositions and technical perfection make up the particular charm of this three-hour-long black-and-white film. [...] Scenes shot in a nocturnal mist, in the half-glow of the evening and morning, a profound depth of field, a variety of lenses, iridescent effects in the interaction of filters, natural and artificial light, long shots where entire landscapes seem illuminated: these formal qualities always simultaneously bring out the dual meaning of this itinerancy, the nowhereness of this trip, the between-space outside of ordinary reference to reality.  The artisanly virtuosity of ''Kings of the Road'' will get cinephiles hooked".<ref>Wolf Donner in [http://www.zeit.de/1976/11/zwei-traeumer-unterwegs/komplettansicht Die Zeit], March 5, 1976</ref>

Film Critic [[Derek Malcolm]] ranked ''Kings of the Road'' 89 on his list of his 100 favourite movies. Malcolm says that Wenders "achieves a palpable sense of time, place and atmosphere, and of how everybody is affected by their tiny spot in history".<ref name="100 Greatest"/> It has been compared to ''[[Easy Rider]]'' and ''[[Two-Lane Blacktop]]'' and called the ultimate road movie.<ref>Wakeman. p. 1171.</ref> Richard Combs wrote that "alienation is not really Wenders subject, although his lonely, self-obsessed heroes might suggest as much".<ref>Wakeman. 1172.</ref>

The film won the FIPRESCI Prize at the 1976 Cannes Film Festival<ref name="festival-cannes.com"/> and the Gold Hugo grand prize at the [[Chicago International Film Festival]].<ref>Nick Harkin and Carly Leviton, "[http://www.chicagofilmfestival.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/Chicago-International-Film-Festival-50-year-Retrospective.pdf 50 YEARS OF MEMORIES: HIGHLIGHTS FROM THE HISTORY OF THE CHICAGO INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL]", ''[[Chicago International Film Festival]]'', URL accessed 10 July 2016.</ref>

[[Richard Eder]], writing in ''[[The New York Times]]'', however, noted that while it had "a number of compelling and witty scenes," its length, nearly "three hours long" was "at least an hour too much."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Eder |first=Richard |date=1976-10-03 |title='Kings' Travel Long Screen Road |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1976/10/03/archives/kings-travel-long-screen-road.html |access-date=2025-06-30 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

== Home video ==
''Kings of the Road'' was released in 1987 as a VHS tape and in 2008 as a region 2 DVD with English subtitles.<ref>{{cite AV media |title=Kings of the Road |oclc=276647247 |date=2008 |medium=DVD (region 2)}} German with English subtitles.</ref><ref>{{cite AV media |title=Kings of the Road |date=1987 |medium=VHS |oclc=276647247}} German language with English subtitles.</ref> In 2016, [[The Criterion Collection]] released the film in Region 1 on [[DVD]] and [[Blu-ray]], along with ''Alice in the Cities'' and ''Wrong Move'', as ''Wim Wenders: The Road Trilogy''.<ref>Peter Sobczynski, "[http://www.rogerebert.com/demanders/on-the-road-again-wim-wenders-the-road-trilogy-comes-to-criterion-blu-ray ON THE ROAD AGAIN: WIM WENDERS: THE ROAD TRILOGY COMES TO CRITERION BLU-RAY]", ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', 1 June 2016, URL accessed 9 June 2016.</ref>
