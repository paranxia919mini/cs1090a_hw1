'''''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang''''' is a 2005 American [[neo-noir]] [[black comedy]] [[crime film]] written and directed by [[Shane Black]] (in his directorial debut) and starring [[Robert Downey Jr.]], [[Val Kilmer]], [[Michelle Monaghan]], and [[Corbin Bernsen]]. The script is partially based on the [[Brett Halliday]] novel ''Bodies Are Where You Find Them'' (1941) and interprets the classic [[hardboiled]] literary genre in a [[tongue-in-cheek]] fashion. The film was produced by [[Joel Silver]], with [[Susan Downey|Susan Levin]] and Steve Richards as executive producers.

Shot in [[Los Angeles]] between February 24 and May 3, 2004, the film debuted at the [[2005 Cannes Film Festival]] on May 14, 2005,<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/4254587/year/2005.html|title=Festival de Cannes: Kiss Kiss Bang Bang|access-date=December 12, 2009|work=Festival Cannes|archive-date=August 3, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140803045300/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/4254587/year/2005.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and was released in the United States on October 21, 2005. It received positive reviews from critics and grossed approximately $16 million worldwide.<ref name="mojo" />

==Plot==
While fleeing from a burglary gone south, Harry Lockhart stumbles into an ongoing audition in New York City.<ref>{{Cite news |date=November 11, 2005 |title=Imagine pulp fiction for the 'Seinfeld' crowd |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-atlanta-constitution-imagine-pulp-fi/171735364/ |access-date=2025-05-05 |work=The Atlanta Constitution |pages=E1 |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> As he pretends to be an actor in order to evade the police, he has an emotional breakdown over his remorse. Casting director Dabney Shaw mistakes this for [[method acting]] and sends him to Los Angeles for screen testing.

At a party in L.A., Harry meets his childhood crush Harmony Lane and "Gay" Perry van Shrike, a private detective hired to give him on-the-job tutelage for his screen test. The party is hosted by Harlan Dexter, a retired actor who has recently resolved a feud over his wife's inheritance with his estranged daughter, Veronica.

During a stakeout at a [[Big Bear Lake]] cabin, Harry and Perry witness two men dump a car in the lake. Noticing a female body in the trunk, Perry shoots the lock in a rescue attempt but accidentally hits the corpse. Because of this, they decide against reporting the incident, as it would appear Perry killed her. When Harry returns to his hotel room, Harmony shows up and tells him of her sister, Jenna; she supposedly killed herself, but Harmony is convinced it was murder. Due to his connection to Perry, Harmony mistakenly thinks Harry is a detective and asks him to investigate. After Harmony leaves, Harry finds the corpse from the lake in his bathroom, so he calls Perry and the two of them dispose of it at the side of the road.

The following morning, Harmony tells Harry about how Jenna was sexually abused by their father when they were young, and that Harmony helped her cope with it by falsely suggesting she was adopted. Later, the body from the lake is identified as Veronica Dexter, from which Perry concludes that the killers were at the party and convinces Harry to leave L.A., as his life may be in danger. At the airport, Harry talks to one of Harmony's friends and realizes that Jenna was the person who hired Perry to do the stakeout at Big Bear Lake in the first place. Harry, Perry and Harmony reunite at a party and find out that Jenna had come to L.A. in search of her biological father and mistakenly thought it was Harlan Dexter.

After the party, Harry is beaten up by the two thugs from the lake. Harmony starts to drive him to the hospital, but on the way she notices a pink-haired woman whom Perry was hired to follow. Realizing this was a trap for Perry, she abandons an unconscious Harry in the car to warn him. One of the thugs is killed in an ensuing shootout, and the frightened pink-haired woman steals Harmony's car, unwittingly driving Harry to her home. There, the other thug kills the pink-haired woman, and Harry kills him in retaliation. Back at his hotel, Harry confesses to Harmony that he is a small-time burglar; she, in turn, confesses that she slept with his best friend in high school, which angers Harry, and he throws her out.

The next day, Perry awakens Harry because Harmony left Perry a voicemail about the case before apparently disappearing. Harry and Perry investigate a mental hospital owned by Harlan Dexter and discover that Veronica was locked in there; they deduce that Harlan must have hired Jenna to act as her replacement to end the inheritance feud, then had both girls killed once he got the money. They also get a call from Harmony, who is alive and well, right as Harlan and his henchmen capture the two of them. While Harry and Perry escape, Harmony steals the van used to transport Veronica's body to the crematorium. A violent car chase ensues on the highways of L.A., during which Harry manages to kill Harlan and his lackeys, but not before both he and Perry are critically wounded as well.

Harry wakes up in the hospital to find Perry alive, and together with Harmony they learn the truth. Harlan never actually met Jenna; the replacement for Veronica was another woman who, when not impersonating her, wore a pink wig. While stalking Harlan, Jenna must have seen him in bed with "Veronica", and become devastated to find the man she believed to be her biological father engaged in incest. She hired Perry to expose Harlan, then took her own life. At Jenna's funeral, Perry confronts her bedridden father and tells him he was the one who truly murdered her. Harry leaves behind his life as a burglar and settles in L.A. to work as Perry's assistant.

==Production==
Following the poor box office of ''[[The Long Kiss Goodnight]]'' and a rejection letter from the [[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]], [[Shane Black]] decided he would attempt something out of the action genre.<ref name=thr/> Following the example of [[James L. Brooks]], Black attempted to make a [[romantic comedy]], "a quirky story of two kids in [[Los Angeles|L.A.]]" Brooks liked Black's first draft, but felt his later attempts were losing focus.<ref name=gq/> Trying to salvage what he had liked, Brooks suggested Black imagine [[Jack Nicholson]] from ''[[As Good as It Gets]]'' playing Nicholson's role from ''[[Chinatown (1974 film)|Chinatown]]''.<ref name=v2003>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2003/film/columns/king-of-the-spec-scripts-rises-once-again-1117885477/|title=King of the spec scripts rises once again|first=Michael|last=Fleming|date=May 2, 2003|website=Variety|access-date=December 11, 2017|archive-date=December 15, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171215000200/https://variety.com/2003/film/columns/king-of-the-spec-scripts-rises-once-again-1117885477/|url-status=live}}</ref> This led Black to add action elements — "I said, you know, 'Fuck it. I have to put a murder in it'" — and re-work the screenplay, adding the character of detective "Gay" Perry,<ref name=gq/> who Black said was an attempt to break stereotypes, as he had never seen "the [[Gay men|gay guy]] who kicks down the door, shoots everyone, and bails your ass out before".<ref name=prem/> Old detective novels were a major influence, with Black saying he tried to re-invent the genre "using realistic characters, in a modern setting, but with the spirit of the 1950s and 1960s".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2005/film/awards/black-back-in-onscreen-action-1117931441/Black|title=Review: 'Black'|first=Jordan|last=Mintzer|date=July 31, 2009|website=Variety|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161128195207/http://variety.com/2009/film/reviews/black-1200475182/|archive-date=November 28, 2016}}</ref> The crime plot drew from [[Brett Halliday]]'s ''Bodies Are Where You Find Them'', and Black homaged [[Raymond Chandler]] by splitting the film into chapters named after Chandler's books ("Trouble Is My Business", "The Lady in the Lake", "The Little Sister", "The Simple Art of Murder", and for the epilogue: "Farewell, My Lovely").<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.film4.com/special-features/interviews/shane-black-on-kiss-kiss-bang-bang |title=Shane Black on Kiss Kiss Bang Bang |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130427035034/http://www.film4.com/special-features/interviews/shane-black-on-kiss-kiss-bang-bang |archive-date=April 27, 2013 |website=[[Film4]]}}</ref>

The script, then titled ''You'll Never Die in This Town Again'', was rejected by various studios before [[Joel Silver]], who gave Black his first break producing ''[[Lethal Weapon]]'' and ''[[The Last Boy Scout]]'', decided to produce it.<ref name=v2003/> The leading role of the film, now retitled ''L.A.P.I.'', had been considered for [[Benicio del Toro]], [[Hugh Grant]] and [[Johnny Knoxville]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.joblo.com/movie-news/3489|title=Knoxville's next|date=February 3, 2004|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181023080045/http://www.joblo.com/movie-news/3489|archive-date=October 23, 2018|website=[[JoBlo.com]]}}</ref> [[Robert Downey Jr.]] learned about the film from his then-girlfriend [[Susan Downey|Susan Levin]], who worked as Silver's assistant,<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/features/robert-downey-jr-20051103 |title=Q&A: Silver in ''[[Gothika]]'', the producer and Black brought him in to audition.<ref name=chud/> Downey was eventually cast as they liked his readings and knew he could fit into the small $15 million budget, as his career had been in a downfall following his time in prison.<ref name=thr>{{cite web|url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/features/lethal-weapon-wunderkind-party-boy-892186|title='Lethal Levin also suggested bringing in [[Val Kilmer]], who coincidentally had been long interested in making a comedy.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.indielondon.co.uk/Film-Review/kiss-kiss-bang-bang-val-kilmer-interview|title=IndieLondon: Kiss Kiss Bang Bang - Val Kilmer Before [[principal photography]] began, the title became ''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' because Black felt it was a "blunt and austere title" that described how the plot was "half romantic comedy and half murder mystery".<ref name=chud>{{cite web|url=http://www.chud.com/5030/interview-shane-black-kiss-kiss-bang-bang/|title=Interview: Shane Black (Kiss Kiss, Bang Bang)|date=November 9, 2005|last=Merchan|first=George|website=CHUD.com|access-date=November 27, 2016|archive-date=November 28, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161128050317/http://www.chud.com/5030/interview-shane-black-kiss-kiss-bang-bang/|url-status=live}}</ref> To achieve a [[neo-noir]] look, Black screened 1960s films of the genre, such as ''[[Harper (film)|Harper]]'' and ''[[Point Blank (1967 film)|Point Blank]],'' to cinematographer [[Michael Barrett (cinematographer)|Michael Barrett]] and production designer Aaron Osborne. Osborne, in particular drew inspiration from the detective book covers by illustrator [[Robert McGinnis]], who was also brought in to draw the covers for the fictional Johnny Gossamer novels that appear in the film.<ref name=notes>{{cite web|url=http://static.thecia.com.au/reviews/k/kiss-kiss-bang-bang-production-notes.rtf|title=''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' production notes|access-date=November 27, 2016|archive-date=September 19, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210919103953/http://static.thecia.com.au/reviews/k/kiss-kiss-bang-bang-production-notes.rtf|url-status=dead}}</ref> The Hollywood party that opens the film was filmed in Black's own Los Angeles mansion.<ref name="gq">{{cite magazine |last=Baron |first=Zach |date=May 16, 2016 |title=Why Shane Black's The Nice Guys Was 15 Years in the Making |url=https://www.gq.com/story/shot-callers-shane-black-the-nice-guys |magazine=[[GQ]] |access-date=September 9, 2017 |archive-date=September 17, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170917213939/https://www.gq.com/story/shot-callers-shane-black-the-nice-guys |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Release==
''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' was screened out of competition at the 2005 [[Cannes Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/comfy-fest-nest-1117921324/|title=Comfy fest nest|first=Alison|last=James|date=April 19, 2005|website=Variety|access-date=December 11, 2017|archive-date=July 22, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230722033549/https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/comfy-fest-nest-1117921324/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film premiered at the [[TCL Chinese Theatre|Chinese Theatre]] on October 17, 2005, as the opener of the [[Hollywood Film Festival]].<ref name=prem>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2005/scene/markets-festivals/kiss-and-tell-time-2-1117931302/|title='Kiss' and tell time|first=Maressa|last=Brown|date=October 20, 2005|website=Variety|access-date=December 11, 2017|archive-date=September 17, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170917213850/http://variety.com/2005/scene/markets-festivals/kiss-and-tell-time-2-1117931302/|url-status=live}}</ref> ''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' was released on DVD on June 13, 2006.<ref>{{cite news |title=Kiss Kiss, Bang Bang On DVD June 13th |url=https://movieweb.com/kiss-kiss-bang-bang-on-dvd-june-13th/ |access-date=22 February 2023 |work=[[MovieWeb]] |date=March 23, 2006 |archive-date=February 23, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230223001939/https://movieweb.com/kiss-kiss-bang-bang-on-dvd-june-13th/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Box office===
The film opened in [[limited release]] in 8 theaters on October 21 in the United States, grossing $180,660 in its opening weekend. From its release until mid-November, the film's distribution was expanded every weekend due to its favorable critical reviews. It stayed in release in the United States until early January.<ref name="mojoopening">{{cite web|title=''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' (2005) - Weekend Box Office|work=Box Office Mojo|url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=kisskissbangbang.htm|access-date=April 15, 2006|archive-date=June 12, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180612142942/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=kisskissbangbang.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> ''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' grossed a total of $4,243,756 in the United States and Canada.<ref name="mojo"/> It grossed far more outside the United States, accounting for just over 70% of the film's worldwide gross, accumulating $11,541,392.<ref name="mojo"/> The film ended up grossing $15,785,148 worldwide, earning back its budget.<ref name="mojo"/> Downey was disappointed at the low box office intake, but said ''Kiss Kiss Bang Bang'' "ended up being my calling card to ''[[Iron Man (2008 film)|Iron Man]]''", as his performance got the interest of director [[Jon Favreau]]. ''Iron Man'' would mark Downey's career resurrection, and Black was even brought in to co-write and direct the sequel ''[[Iron Man 3]]''.<ref name=thr/>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 86%, based on 180 reviews, and an average rating of 7.50/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "Tongue-in-cheek satire blends well with entertaining action and spot-on performances in this dark, eclectic neo-noir homage."<ref>{{cite web |title=Kiss Kiss, Bang Bang (2005) |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/kiss_kiss_bang_bang/ |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=July 1, 2020 |archive-date=September 15, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240915174211/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/kiss_kiss_bang_bang |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score 72 out of 100, based on 37 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/kiss-kiss-bang-bang |title=Kiss Kiss Bang Bang |work=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=December 10, 2015 |archive-date=April 26, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120426211200/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/kiss-kiss-bang-bang |url-status=live }}</ref>
