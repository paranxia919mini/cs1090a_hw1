'''''House of 1000 Corpses''''' is a 2003 American [[black comedy]] [[horror film]]<ref name=":0">{{Cite web | url=https://screenrant.com/house-1000-corpses-rob-zombie-best-movie-firefly-trilogy-reason/ | title=Why House of 1000 Corpses is the Best Movie in Rob Zombie's Firefly Trilogy | website=[[Screen Rant]] | date=October 11, 2020 | access-date=January 22, 2022 | archive-date=January 22, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220122203557/https://screenrant.com/house-1000-corpses-rob-zombie-best-movie-firefly-trilogy-reason/ | url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web | url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/345280/netflix-unleashes-house-of-1000-corpses-this-halloween/ | title=Netflix Unleashes HOUSE OF 1000 CORPSES This Halloween | date=September 24, 2020 | access-date=January 22, 2022 | archive-date=January 22, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220122203919/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/345280/netflix-unleashes-house-of-1000-corpses-this-halloween/ | url-status=live |last=Sprague|first=Mike}}</ref> written, co-scored, and directed by [[Rob Zombie]] in his [[List of directorial debuts|directorial debut]], and the first film in the ''Firefly'' film series. It stars [[Sid Haig]], [[Bill Moseley]], [[Sheri Moon Zombie|Sheri Moon]]<!--At the time of filming, she was yet to be married and is billed as simply "Sheri Moon" in the credits.-->, [[Karen Black]], [[Rainn Wilson]], [[Chris Hardwick]], [[Tom Towles]], [[Erin Daniels]], [[Jennifer Jostyn]], [[Walton Goggins]], and [[Dennis Fimple]] in his final role. The plot centers on a group of teenagers who are kidnapped and tortured by a psychopathic family during [[Halloween]] after traveling across the country to write a book.

Inspired by 1970s horror films such as ''[[The Texas Chain Saw Massacre]]'' (1974), ''[[The Hills Have Eyes (1977 film)|The Hills Have Eyes]]'' (1977), and ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' (1978),<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ifc.com/videos/house-of-1000-corpses |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012031522/http://www.ifc.com/videos/house-of-1000-corpses |archive-date=October 12, 2013 |work=IFC (The Independent Film Channel) |title=House of 1000 Corpses |access-date=2012-07-08 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all }}</ref> Zombie conceived the film while designing a haunted house attraction for [[Universal Studios Hollywood]], where filming took place in 2000 on the backlots and in [[Valencia, California]]. When the studio shelved the film fearing that it would receive an [[NC-17 rating]],<ref name="fangoria">{{cite journal|journal=Fangoria|issue=199|pages=20–25|title=Death to False Horror|author=Szulkin, David}}</ref> Zombie reacquired the rights. They were eventually sold to [[Lions Gate Entertainment]], who released the film in April 2003. Despite receiving an unfavorable response from critics, it went on to gross $16 million worldwide. Since its release, the film has achieved a [[cult following]], was developed into a haunted house attraction by Zombie for Universal Studios, and was followed by two sequels: ''[[The Devil's Rejects]]'' (2005) and ''[[3 from Hell]]'' (2019).

==Plot==
On October 30, 1977, amateur criminals Karl and Richard Wick attempt an armed robbery at a gas station/horror museum, but are killed by the owner, [[Captain Spaulding (Rob Zombie character)|Captain Spaulding]], and his assistant, Ravelli. Later that night, Jerry Goldsmith, Bill Hudley, Mary Knowles, and Denise Willis are on the road in hopes of writing a book on offbeat [[roadside attraction]]s. When the four meet Spaulding, who is also the owner of "The Museum of Monsters & Madmen", they learn of the local legend of Dr. Satan. As they take off in search of the tree from which Dr. Satan was hanged, they pick up a young free-spirited [[hitchhiker]] named Baby, who claims to live only a few miles away. Shortly after, a mysterious figure appears hidden in some overgrowth and shoots out their vehicle's tire with a shotgun. The group thinks it is just a blown out tire and so Baby takes Bill to her family's house to get a tow truck. Moments later, Baby's half-brother, Rufus, picks up the stranded passengers and takes them to the family home.

There, they meet Baby's family: her adopted brother Otis Driftwood, her deformed giant half-brother Tiny, Mother Firefly, and Grandpa Hugo. While being treated to dinner, Mother Firefly explains that her ex-husband, Earl, had previously tried to burn Tiny alive, along with the Firefly house after he suffered a psychotic breakdown. After dinner, the family puts on a Halloween show for their guests and Baby offends Mary by flirting with Bill. After Mary threatens Baby, Rufus tells them their car is repaired. As the couples leave, Otis and Tiny, disguised as [[scarecrow]]s, attack them in the driveway and take them captive. The next day, Otis kills Bill and mutilates his body, turning him into a [[Fiji Mermaid]]. Mary is tied up in a room and tormented by Otis, Denise is tied to a bed dressed up as a doll for Halloween, and Jerry is partially [[Scalping|scalped]] for failing to guess Baby's favorite movie star.

When Denise does not come home, her father Don calls the police to report her missing. Two deputies, George Wydell and Steve Naish, find the couples' abandoned car in a field with a dead, mutilated cheerleader in the trunk (one of five cheerleaders who went missing over a week ago, as explained in a news broadcast; an earlier scene established that the cheerleaders were being tortured, raped and killed by the Firefly family). Don, a former policeman, is called to the scene to help the deputies search. They arrive at the Firefly house and Wydell questions Mother Firefly about the missing teens. Mother Firefly shoots Wydell in the head and kills him; Don and Steve are then killed by Otis when they find more bodies of missing cheerleaders in the barn, along with a barely conscious Mary.

Later that night, the three remaining teenagers are dressed as rabbits and taken out to an abandoned well. Otis torments Denise using the skin of her dead father's face as a mask. Mary attempts to run away, but is tracked down and stabbed to death by Baby moments later. Otis and the family burn the bodies on a [[pyre]].

Meanwhile, Jerry and Denise are placed in a coffin and lowered into a well, where a group of Dr. Satan's failed experiments break open the coffin and pull Jerry away, leaving Denise to find her way through an underground lair. As she wanders through tunnels filled with mutilated corpses, she encounters Dr. Satan and a number of [[Intellectual disability|mental patients]]; Jerry is on Dr. Satan's operating table being [[Vivisection|vivisected]], and dies as Denise screams. Dr. Satan orders his mutated gargantuan assistant, who turns out to be Mother Firefly's ex-husband Earl, to capture Denise, but Denise outwits him and escapes by crawling to the surface as Earl is crushed by falling debris in the collapsing tunnel.

She makes her way to the main road where she encounters Captain Spaulding, who gives her a ride in his car. She passes out from exhaustion in the front seat, and Otis suddenly appears in the back seat with a knife. Denise later wakes up to find herself strapped to an operating table, surrounded by Dr. Satan and Earl, who survived the cave-in. The movie ends with Denise screaming in horror and the words "The End?" displayed before the end credits.

The idea for ''House of 1000 Corpses'' came to Zombie while designing a haunted house attraction with that title for the studio.<ref name=cdinterview /> In 1999, [[MTV News]] announced [[Madonna]] was considering producing a horror film Zombie would write and direct called ''The Legend of the 13 Graves'', which would center on "a group of road-trippers traveling through America who wind up encountering a suitably sick and twisted family." The film would have been co-produced by Madonna and her partner, [[Guy Oseary]], through Madguy Films.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1428833/rob-zombie-madonna-may-team-up-for-horror-flick/ |title=Rob Zombie, Madonna May Team Up for Horror Flick |author=MTV News Staff |work=[[MTV News]] |publisher=[[Viacom (2005–present)|Viacom]] |date=March 29, 1999 |access-date=October 24, 2025 |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201101021545/http://www.mtv.com/news/1428833/rob-zombie-madonna-may-team-up-for-horror-flick/ |archive-date=November 1, 2020 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> 

Universal responded positively to Zombie's pitch.<ref name=cdinterview /> Zombie later stated, "I was in the office of the head of production or something and he asked me if I had any movie ideas and I pitched him ''Corpses'', which was very rough at the time, because I wasn't ready and I made it up on the spot. He liked it, I went home, wrote a 12-page treatment and met up with them. Two months later, we were shooting."<ref name="theguardian">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2003/sep/19/popandrock|title=Interview: Rob Zombie|website=theguardian.com|date=September 19, 2003|access-date=2015-01-31|archive-date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304220102/http://www.theguardian.com/film/2003/sep/19/popandrock|url-status=live|last=Petridis|first=Alexis}}</ref> Production on the film began in May 2000<ref name="fangoria" /> and was finished by Halloween of 2000.<ref name=cdinterview /> The house was launched the following year, although the title was changed to "American Nightmare" due to the film's shelving.<ref name=cdinterview /> Despite the name change, the house still featured numerous references to the film, and the theatrical trailer played while customers waited.<ref name=cdinterview />

The film's starting budget was $3–4 million, though its final budget is debatable.<ref name="fangoria" /> Zombie first claimed that the film was made solely with the initial $4 million but later described a budget between $7 million and $14 million.<ref>{{cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kVffEFtxKhM| archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211118/kVffEFtxKhM| archive-date=2021-11-18 | url-status=live|title=Rob Zombie explains why House of 1000 Corpses cost so much to make.|date=28 February 2014|work=YouTube}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Zombie later admitted that he initially knew he did not have the funding for a good ending but gambled that if he shot what he could on what remained of his budget, the studio would kick in more funds to make a better ending. "I knew the ending sucked, so I let it suck and they said, 'The movie's great but the ending sucks' and I know. So they gave me more money and we shot a more elaborate ending, bigger sets, the whole razzamatazz."<ref name=cdinterview /> The original film featured more characters, including a [[skunk ape]], and featured footage of the four teenagers on their road trip.<ref name="slasherama">{{cite web|url=http://www.slasherama.com/features/robzombie.HTML|title=Slasherama Interviews Rob Zombie|publisher=slasherama.com|access-date=2015-01-31|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924102547/http://www.slasherama.com/features/robzombie.HTML|url-status=dead}}</ref> Universal hoped that the film would focus more on the group of kids, but Zombie knew "nobody gives a shit about the kids".<ref name=slasherama /> Zombie claimed the film was not initially meant to feature elements of [[black humor]], saying it "turned out a little wackier and campier than I originally intended. But as we were shooting, that's the tone that it was turning out to be. Movies sometimes dictate their own course, so I just sort of went with it."<ref name="avclub">{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/rob-zombie-1798208595|title=avclub Interviews Rob Zombie!|date=August 2, 2005|publisher=avclub.com|access-date=April 7, 2026|archive-date=June 13, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180613094941/https://www.avclub.com/rob-zombie-1798208595|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film was shot on a 25-day shooting schedule. Two weeks were spent filming on the Universal Studios Hollywood backlots—the house featured in the film is the same house used in ''[[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas (film)|The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas]]'' (1982), and it can be seen on Universal Studios' tram tours.<ref name="fangoria" /><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.thestudiotour.com/ush/backlot/chickenranch.shtml|work=The Studio Tour: Universal Studios Hollywood|title=The Chicken Ranch|access-date=2012-07-08|archive-date=July 1, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120701005638/http://www.thestudiotour.com/ush/backlot/chickenranch.shtml|url-status=live}}</ref> Zombie stated that filming on the lot was at times difficult, as the amusement park was often open and ruined takes.<ref name=cdinterview /> The remaining 11 days of the shoot were spent on a ranch in [[Valencia, California]].<ref name="fangoria" /> The scene involving Bill being transformed into "Fishboy" was initially much longer, featuring gory details of the creation of the monster.<ref name=slasherama /> Zombie stated the scene was created after Universal passed on the film.<ref name=slasherama /> Scenes featuring Baby masturbating with a skeleton, along with other cutaway scenes, were filmed in Zombie's basement after initial filming for the project had concluded. Zombie later cited home recordings from the [[Charles Manson|Manson family]] as inspiration for the Firefly family's "bizarre" rants. Zombie often filmed two versions of scenes, one of them less gory, in an attempt to please Universal.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://screenrant.com/rob-zombie-house-1000-corpses-lost-footage-explained/|title=House of 1000 Corpses' Lost Footage Explained|work=[[Screen Rant]]|publisher=Valnet Inc.|first=Jack|last=Wilhelmi|date=January 1, 2020|access-date=April 30, 2024}}</ref>

Jake McKinnon could not see well when dressed as The Professor, and almost hit actress Erin Daniels with a real ax during the film's climax. Zombie later said he had simply hoped Daniels would move out of the way in time. When Denise calls her father from a telephone booth, a sign for a missing dog head can be seen hanging in the booth; this was in fact a real item found by Zombie and used for the film. In the early stages of the film, Grandpa Hugo was to have been revealed as the murderous Dr. Satan, who at the time was simply referred to as the mad doctor. The legend of the mad doctor was to be a ploy by the Firefly family to lure victims in, though this idea was later scrapped. This led to Grandpa Hugo receiving much less screen time. The character of Dr. Satan was inspired by a 1950s billboard-sized poster advertising a "live spook show starring a magician called Dr. Satan" that Zombie has in his house. <ref>{{Citation | last = Ulmer | first = Gary | title = Interview with the Zombie | periodical = Dark Realms Magazine | pages = 14–16 | issue = 11 | date = Summer 2003 | url = http://www.monolithgraphics.com/darkrealms.html | access-date = September 7, 2009 | archive-date =  | archive-url =  | url-status =  }}</ref>

The film's main cast consisted of the murderous Firefly family, the four teenagers and various police officers attempting to find the group, among others.<ref name="horrorhound">{{citation|last =Newell|first =Eric|title=House of 1000 Corpses|journal=Horror Hound|volume=1|issue=36|pages=26–33| year= 2012}}</ref> Sid Haig was cast as Captain Spaulding, a man who dresses as a clown and owns a gas station and museum of curiosities.<ref name="horrorhound"/> Haig claimed he had to "get in touch with [his] own insanity" for the role.<ref name=aboutfilm>{{cite web|url=http://www.aboutfilm.com/features/devilsrejects/castinterviews.htm|title=Devil's Rejects - Cast Interviews|access-date=2015-01-31|archive-date=August 7, 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070807134110/http://www.aboutfilm.com/features/devilsrejects/castinterviews.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> His relation to the Firefly family is not revealed in the film, though he is working with them to some extent; however, the sequel establishes that he is the father of Baby. Bill Moseley starred as Otis B. Driftwood, who was adopted into the Firefly family. Sheri Moon Zombie portrayed Baby Firefly, who became known for her high pitched laugh and sexual nature.<ref name=slasherama /> Karen Black was cast as Mother Firefly, the protective mother to the family.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://loudwire.com/rob-zombie-fan-help-house-of-1000-corpses-karen-black-cancer-battle/|title=Rob Zombie Rallies Fans to Help 'House of 1000 Corpses' Actress Karen Black's Cancer Battle|work=Loudwire|date=March 30, 2013 |access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 10, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150910110824/http://loudwire.com/rob-zombie-fan-help-house-of-1000-corpses-karen-black-cancer-battle/|url-status=live|last=Childers|first=Chad}}</ref> [[Matthew McGrory]] portrayed Tiny Firefly, a tall man who was left deformed after a house fire started by his father.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/life/people/2005-08-10-mcgrory-obit_x.htm|title=Matthew McGrory Obit|access-date=2015-03-09|archive-date=July 26, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150726095144/http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/life/people/2005-08-10-mcgrory-obit_x.htm|url-status=live|date=August 10, 2005|website=Associated Press|via=USA Today}}</ref> [[Robert Allen Mukes]] portrayed Rufus "RJ" Firefly Jr. [[Dennis Fimple]] was selected to play Grampa Hugo Firefly. He died following filming, and the finished product was dedicated to his memory.<ref name="horrorhound"/>

The names of members of the Firefly family were taken from the names of [[Groucho Marx]] characters. Captain Spaulding was a character in ''[[Animal Crackers (1930 film)|Animal Crackers]]'' (1930), Otis B. Driftwood was a character in ''[[A Night at the Opera (film)|A Night at the Opera]]'' (1935), Rufus T. Firefly was taken from ''[[Duck Soup (1933 film)|Duck Soup]]'' (1933), and lastly Hugo Z. Hackenbush derived from ''[[A Day at the Races (film)|A Day at the Races]]'' (1937). Despite only allusions to this being made in ''House of 1000 Corpses'', it is more prevalent in the film's sequel, with the names becoming integral to the plot.<ref name="ifc.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.ifc.com/fix/2013/07/five-surprising-facts-about-rob-zombies-house-of-1000-corpses|title=Five Fun Facts About Rob Zombie's House of 1000 Corpses|work=www.ifc.com|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=August 12, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150812011032/http://www.ifc.com/fix/2013/07/five-surprising-facts-about-rob-zombies-house-of-1000-corpses|url-status=live|date=July 18, 2003|last=Locker|first=Melissa}}</ref> Zombie acknowledged that viewers were meant to "root for" the Firefly family as opposed to the group of teens, though claims it wasn't intentional: "Yeah, I wanted the audience to cheer 'em. I didn't consciously think of it at the time, because I was trying to make Bill and Jerry likeable. But it's like when you saw ''[[Beetlejuice]]'' and you could tell all [[Tim Burton]] cared about was Beetlejuice."<ref name=slasherama />

[[Erin Daniels]] portrayed Denise Willis. [[Chris Hardwick]] was cast as Jerry Goldsmith, a young man who was seen as "hyper" and "wise-cracking".<ref name="ifc.com"/> The character Bill Hudley was portrayed by [[Rainn Wilson]]. ===Music===
{{main|House of 1000 Corpses (soundtrack)}}
The [[Film score|score for the film]] was composed by Zombie, alongside Canadian producer [[Scott Humphrey]]. Much of the production work for the soundtrack to the film was done in Humphrey's studio, The Chop Shop.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.thechopshop.com/ |title=The Chop Shop |publisher=The Chop Shop |access-date=2012-10-28 |archive-date=September 28, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180928222408/http://www.thechopshop.com/ |url-status=usurped }}</ref><ref name="about">{{cite web|url=http://www.thechopshop.com/about.html |title=The Chop Shop |publisher=The Chop Shop |access-date=2012-10-28 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120426065635/http://www.thechopshop.com/about.html |archive-date=April 26, 2012 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> The film's score featured similar musical themes to Zombie's releases, consisting of heavy metal influences. [[MTV]] said the music mixed "snippets of ominous hillbilly dialogue with grim horror movie rock".<ref name=MTVmusic>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1469829/rob-zombie-prepping-new-lp-movie-builds-brick-house-with-lionel-richie/|title=Rob Zombie Prepping New LP, Movie; Builds 'Brick House' With Lionel Richie|website=[[MTV]]|date=February 7, 2003|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=June 12, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180612113251/http://www.mtv.com/news/1469829/rob-zombie-prepping-new-lp-movie-builds-brick-house-with-lionel-richie/|url-status=dead}}</ref> While making the movie, Zombie joked with his manager that he should do a cover of "[[Brick House (song)|Brick House]]" (1977), originally performed by [[Commodores]]. His manager later got both [[Lionel Richie]] and rapper [[Trina]] to appear on a cover of the song with Zombie, under the title "Brick House 2003".<ref name=MTVmusic /> Aside from making audio clips and snippets for the film, Zombie recorded a variety of new songs for the film's soundtrack. The song "[[House of 1000 Corpses (song)|House of 1000 Corpses]]", taken from Zombie's album ''[[The Sinister Urge (album)|The Sinister Urge]]'' (2001), is also present.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/r633897|title=House of 1000 Corpses - Original Soundtrack|website=[[Allmusic]]|access-date=July 2, 2022|archive-date=April 11, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230411045138/https://www.allmusic.com/|url-status=live}}</ref> The soundtrack, released on March 25, 2003, made an appearance on the [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]] chart in the United States. The soundtrack to the film is isolated on home video releases of the film as a separate audio track.{{citation needed|date=May 2018}} Later in 2019, Rob Zombie announced that the soundtrack would be released on vinyl by the label [[Waxwork Records]]. The record included an essay by Zombie and a 12x12" photo booklet of behind the scenes photos.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://consequence.net/2019/06/rob-zombie-film-trilogy-soundtrack-vinyl-3-from-hell-tracks/|title=Rob Zombie Announces Vinyl Film Trilogy Soundtrack|author=Hadusek, Jon|date=12 June 2019|access-date=7 August 2020|archive-date=January 17, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230117203744/https://consequence.net/2019/06/rob-zombie-film-trilogy-soundtrack-vinyl-3-from-hell-tracks/|url-status=live|website=Consequence of Sound}}</ref>

==Release==
Prior to agreeing to release the film through Universal, Zombie reportedly told the studio of the film's nature, stating "I was really blatant when I talked to them. I didn't want to get into a situation where they thought I was making something mainstream. And I told them that I wanted to make a drive-in movie, something very gritty and nasty and weird."<ref name=avclub /> Production of the film was completed in 2000, and was set for release through Universal. The studio completed a theatrical trailer for the film, which was shown in theaters and prior to the Universal ride created by Zombie.<ref name=avclub /> Zombie later received a call for a meeting with Stacey Snider, head of Universal. Zombie recalled fearing that the studio would demand a re-shoot, though he later learned that Snider's fears of the film receiving an [[NC-17 rating]] had led to the company's refusal to release the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.axs.com/news/rob-zombie-announces-new-movie-3i-rocking-dead-10999|title=Rob Zombie announces new movie '3I': Rocking dead|work=AXS |date=May 18, 2014|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923210212/http://www.axs.com/news/rob-zombie-announces-new-movie-3i-rocking-dead-10999|url-status=live|last=Yeanoplos|first=Kevin}}</ref> The film remained shelved for several months, with Zombie eventually purchasing the rights to the film from Universal. Zombie claimed that many urged him to scrap the film following the fallout with Universal, though he continued to search for a new distributor.

Zombie later made a deal with [[MGM]] to release the film, with MGM slating an October 2002 release for the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.blabbermouth.net/news/rob-zombie-signs-deal-with-mgm-for-release-of-house-of-1000-corpses|title=ROB ZOMBIE Signs Deal With MGM For Release Of "House Of 1000 Corpses"|date=June 3, 2002|publisher=BlabberMouth.net|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924152642/http://www.blabbermouth.net/news/rob-zombie-signs-deal-with-mgm-for-release-of-house-of-1000-corpses|url-status=live}}</ref> MGM later refused to release the film following a controversial remark from Zombie claiming that the company had no morals for releasing the film.<ref name="lentmag">{{cite web|url=http://lentmag.com/consider-the-zombie/|title=Consider the Zombie|author=Paul|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=February 27, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150227195906/http://lentmag.com/consider-the-zombie/|url-status=dead|first=Andrew}}</ref> Zombie later announced plans to release the film himself, without the backing of a production company.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.blabbermouth.net/news/rob-zombie-s-house-of-1000-corpses-gets-dropped-by-mgm/|title=Rob Zombie's "House Of 1000 Corpses" Gets Dropped By Mgm - Blabbermouth.net|work=BLABBERMOUTH.NET|date=July 22, 2002|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924152639/http://www.blabbermouth.net/news/rob-zombie-s-house-of-1000-corpses-gets-dropped-by-mgm/|url-status=live}}</ref> Despite this, Zombie eventually caught the eye of [[Lions Gate Entertainment]], the final studio to sign on for the project.<ref name=lentmag /> Lions Gate, attempting to venture into new types of films, hoped releasing a horror film would provide more opportunities.<ref name=lentmag /> The film was cut and edited in an attempt to achieve an R-rating, with Zombie claiming that most of the cut footage featured Sherri Moon Zombie's character.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1454969/rob-zombies-house-of-1000-corpses-finds-a-home/|title=Rob Zombie's 'House of 1000 Corpses' Finds A Home|publisher=MTV|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=March 1, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160301083225/http://www.mtv.com/news/1454969/rob-zombies-house-of-1000-corpses-finds-a-home/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The film has a 21% "rotten" rating on film review website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], based on 86 reviews, with a [[weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]] of 3.7/10. The site's consensus reads: "There's an abundance of gore in this derivative horror movie, but little sense or wit."<ref>{{cite web|title=House of 1000 Corpses|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/house_of_1000_corpses/|website=Rotten Tomatoes|access-date=March 7, 2025|archive-date=January 9, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230109101042/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/house_of_1000_corpses|url-status=live}}</ref> It has a [[Metacritic]] score of 31 based on 15 critics, signifying "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/house-of-1000-corpses|title=Metacritic House of 1000 Corpses|website=Metacritic|access-date=July 30, 2015|archive-date=October 31, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151031014513/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/house-of-1000-corpses|url-status=live}}</ref>

Since its initial release, the film has gone on to gain a [[cult following]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/the-new-cult-canon-the-devils-rejects-1798215251|title=The New Cult Canon: The Devil's Rejects|work=avclub.com|date=2008-11-25|access-date=2020-06-23|archive-date=June 26, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200626072423/https://film.avclub.com/the-new-cult-canon-the-devils-rejects-1798215251|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.film.com/movies/re-views-house-of-1000-corpses-2003|title=Re-Views: House of 1000 Corpses (2003)|work=Film.com|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924033708/http://www.film.com/movies/re-views-house-of-1000-corpses-2003|url-status=usurped}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3310064/rob-zombie-wants-make-devils-rejects-sequel-prequel/|title=Rob Zombie Wants to Make 'Devil's Rejects' Sequel (or Prequel) -|work=bloody-disgusting.com|date=August 25, 2014|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=June 27, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150627081749/http://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3310064/rob-zombie-wants-make-devils-rejects-sequel-prequel/|url-status=live|last=Cooper|first=Patrick}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://crypticrock.com/the-devils-rejects-a-cult-classic-10-years-later/|title=The Devil's Rejects a Cult Classic 10 Years Later|work=CrypticRock|date=August 3, 2015|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=March 10, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160310230804/http://crypticrock.com/the-devils-rejects-a-cult-classic-10-years-later/|url-status=live}}</ref> In his 2007 review of the Blu-ray release, Christopher Monfette of ''[[IGN]]'' called the film "fun as hell", writing, "''House of 1000 Corpses'' is a messy film—veering this way and that across the genre map with no discernible destination. But viewed less a movie and more as an experience, the film offers a certain degree of inspired insanity and a healthy dose of carnival-like madness".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ign.com/articles/2007/10/01/house-of-1000-corpses-blu-ray-review|title=House of 1000 Corpses Blu-Ray Review|website=IGN|date=October 1, 2007|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924155123/http://www.ign.com/articles/2007/10/01/house-of-1000-corpses-blu-ray-review|url-status=live}}</ref> Zombie has acknowledged the film's cult status, stating, "Now, a decade later, it's become a pretty loved movie among people. It's great that we have this big celebration. I love seeing Sid Haig and the other actors get such great attention from it. The funny thing is, ten years becomes a long time. I'll meet someone who's eighteen years old, and that's always been a film that they've loved. It's funny that the film's been around that long to be like that for some people."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/rob-zombie-ventures-back-to-house-of-1-000-corpses-talks-madness-in-halloween-2/7638083|title=Rob Zombie Ventures Back to "House of 1,000 Corpses" Talks Madness in "Halloween 2"|work=ARTISTdirect|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923225614/http://www.artistdirect.com/entertainment-news/article/rob-zombie-ventures-back-to-house-of-1-000-corpses-talks-madness-in-halloween-2/7638083|url-status=deviated|date=September 20, 2010}}</ref> CinemaBlend also wrote of the film's cult status, stating, "While his ''[[Halloween (franchise)|Halloween]]'' films were a mess, Zombie did bring something new with his original films ''House of 1000 Corpses'' and ''The Devil's Rejects'', developing a cult following for his movies on top of the one he earned for his music. Say what you will about him as a director, but there's no denying that he has a unique vision."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cinemablend.com/new/Rob-Zombie-Says-His-Next-Film-Will-Be-Lords-Of-Salem-20783.html|title=Rob Zombie Says His Next Film Will Be Lords Of Salem|work=cinemablend.com|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=October 3, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161003214620/http://www.cinemablend.com/new/Rob-Zombie-Says-His-Next-Film-Will-Be-Lords-Of-Salem-20783.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Zombie has gone on to dismiss the film following its release, saying, "The first film [I directed], which people seems to love, is just a calamitous mess. Well, when it came out it seemed like everyone hated it. Now everyone acts like it's beloved in some way. All I see is flaw, upon flaw, upon flaw&nbsp;... upon flaw."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3279559/a-wonderfully-candid-rob-zombie-says-hes-returning-to-horror/|title=A Wonderfully Candid Rob Zombie Says He's Returning To Horror! -|work=bloody-disgusting.com|date=February 20, 2014|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304220618/http://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3279559/a-wonderfully-candid-rob-zombie-says-hes-returning-to-horror/|url-status=live}}</ref> The success of the film led to two sequels, released in 2005 and 2019 respectively, and later a seasonal haunted house attraction at [[Universal Studios Hollywood]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2010-10-08-zombiehouse08_ST_N.htm|title=Rob Zombie's haunted house: It's a 'living horror movie'|work=USATODAY.com|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=January 28, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130128090645/http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2010-10-08-zombiehouse08_ST_N.htm|url-status=live|date=October 8, 2010|last=della Cava|first=Marco R.}}</ref>

===Home media=== 
The film was released on VHS and DVD on August 12, 2003. For the main menu of the film, Zombie had Sid Haig perform in character as an added bonus.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.eeggs.com/tree/8256.html|title=House of 1000 Corpses Easter Eggs|publisher=eeggs.com|access-date=August 7, 2015|archive-date=September 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150923234230/http://www.eeggs.com/tree/8256.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The Blu-ray edition of the film was released on September 18, 2007.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/House-1000-Corpses-Blu-ray-Haig/dp/B000SQFBZ0|title=Amazon - House of 1000 Corpses (Blu-Ray Edition)|date=September 17, 2007 |publisher=Amazon|access-date=August 31, 2017|archive-date=November 16, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161116134244/https://www.amazon.com/House-1000-Corpses-Blu-ray-Haig/dp/B000SQFBZ0|url-status=live}}</ref> The Blu-ray edition of the film features the added menu content with Haig, as well as the bonus features found on its initial release. The film was released alongside ''[[The Devil's Rejects]]'' (2005) in a combo-pack on January 4, 2011.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Devils-Rejects-Corpses-Two-Pack-Blu-ray/dp/B004AC6PQW/ref=sr_1_2?s=movies-tv&ie=UTF8&qid=1438878198&sr=1-2&keywords=House+of+1000+Corpses|title=The Devil's Rejects / House of 1000 Corpses (Horror Two-Pack) [Blu-ray]|date=January 4, 2011 |publisher=amazon|access-date=August 31, 2017|archive-date=April 11, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230411044150/https://www.amazon.com/Devils-Rejects-Corpses-Two-Pack-Blu-ray/dp/B004AC6PQW/ref=sr_1_2?s=movies-tv&ie=UTF8&qid=1438878198&sr=1-2&keywords=House+of+1000+Corpses|url-status=live}}</ref>
