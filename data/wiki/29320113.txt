'''''Event Horizon''''' is a 1997 [[science fiction horror]] film directed by [[Paul W. S. Anderson|Paul Anderson]]<!-- CREDITED WITHOUT THE "W. S." --> and written by [[Philip Eisner]]. The film stars [[Laurence Fishburne]], [[Sam Neill]], [[Kathleen Quinlan]], and [[Joely Richardson]]. Set in 2047, it follows the crew of the rescue vessel ''Lewis and Clark'', led by Captain Miller (Fishburne), who are sent to investigate the ''Event Horizon'', a spaceship that mysteriously vanished seven years prior during its maiden voyage to [[Proxima Centauri]] and has reappeared in orbit around [[Neptune]]. Accompanied by the ship's designer, Dr. Weir (Neill), they board the derelict ship to search for survivors and determine what happened after its experimental [[Alcubierre drive|gravity drive]], meant for [[faster-than-light]] travel by [[Wormhole|folding space-time]], was activated.

Eisner conceived the story in the early 1990s as a "haunted house in space" and completed a first draft in 1992. Following the commercial success of ''[[Mortal Kombat (1995 film)|Mortal Kombat]]'' (1995), Anderson sought a darker and more visceral project and accepted [[Paramount Pictures]]' offer of Eisner's script. Finding the original script too similar to ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'' (1979), Anderson worked with Eisner to reorient the story, introducing influences from ''[[Solaris (1972 film)|Solaris]]'' (1972) and ''[[Hellraiser]]'' (1987). [[Principal photography]] took place entirely at [[Pinewood Studios]] in England from 18 November 1996 to 14 March 1997 as an American–British co-production, on a budget estimated at $50–70 million. Paramount compressed the schedule after ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]'' was delayed, leaving little time for post-production. Anderson's original 130-minute cut was reduced to 96 minutes at the studio's insistence after test audiences reacted poorly to its graphic content.

''Event Horizon'' premiered in [[Beverly Hills]] on 12 August 1997, and was released in the United States on 15 August and in the United Kingdom one week later. Critical reception was generally unfavourable, with reviewers calling it derivative, shallow, and overly gory, though the performances received some praise. The film was a [[box-office bomb]], grossing $47.1 million worldwide; early in its theatrical run, Paramount's parent company, [[Viacom (1952–2006)|Viacom]], [[write-off|wrote it off]] as a major loss. Commentators have attributed its failure in part to its late-summer release against lighter science-fiction hits such as ''[[Men in Black (1997 film)|Men in Black]]'' and to marketing targeting hardcore science-fiction fans rather than horror enthusiasts.

The film later found a [[cult following]] on home video. The success of its initial DVD release prompted Paramount to discuss a [[director's cut]] with Anderson, but most of the deleted footage had been lost or destroyed. It has since been reappraised, appearing on lists of the best horror films of all time, and influencing many subsequent science-fiction and horror films and video games. In 2025, [[IDW Publishing]] began publishing the comic-book prequel series ''[[Event Horizon: Dark Descent|Dark Descent]]''; a sequel series, ''[[Event Horizon: Inferno|Inferno]]'', followed in 2026.

==Plot==
In 2047, a distress signal is received from the ''Event Horizon'', a spaceship that disappeared during its maiden voyage to [[Proxima Centauri]] seven years earlier. The ship has mysteriously reappeared in orbit around [[Neptune]], prompting the US Aerospace Command to dispatch the ''Lewis and Clark'' rescue vessel to investigate. Its crew members—Captain Miller, second-in-command Lieutenant Starck, pilot Smith, medical technician Peters, engineer Justin, doctor D.J., and rescue technician Cooper—are joined by Dr. William Weir, who designed the ''Event Horizon''. Shortly before arrival at the ''Event Horizon'', Weir briefs them on the ship's experimental [[Alcubierre drive|gravity drive]] with a simple visualization of how it [[Wormhole|folds spacetime]] to instantly transport the ship across vast distances. He then plays them the distress signal, which consists of screams, howls, and what sounds like a voice. D.J. recognizes it as the Latin phrase {{lang|la|"līberāte mē"}}, which he translates as "save me".

Upon boarding the ''Event Horizon'', the crew finds signs of a [[massacre]]. As they search for survivors, the ship's gravity drive activates and briefly pulls Justin into a portal before unleashing a [[shock wave]] that breaches the hull of the ''Lewis and Clark''. The crew is forced to move to the ''Event Horizon'' while Cooper rescues Justin from the portal, finding that he has been reduced to a [[catatonia|catatonic state]]. Smith and Cooper are sent on a [[spacewalk]] to repair the hull of the ''Lewis and Clark'' while the rest of the crew begin to experience hallucinations of their biggest fears and regrets. Miller sees Eddie Corrick, a subordinate from a previous journey whom he left to die in order to save the other crew members. Peters sees her son with his legs covered in bloody lesions, while Weir sees a vision of his late wife, now eyeless, urging him to join her.

Justin suddenly wakes from his coma while the entire ship seems to be shaking and attempts to vent himself from the airlock; he is saved at the last second by Miller, who places a severely injured Justin in [[suspended animation|stasis]]. Shaken, Miller confides in D.J. about his hallucinations, prompting D.J. to reveal that he found a longer phrase in the distress signal. It really says {{lang|la|"libera te tutemet ex inferis<!-- per transcript - see discussion at Talk -->"}}, which he translates as "save {{em|yourself}} from [[Hell]]".<ref>{{Cite web |date=2020-03-09 |title=Libera Te Tutemet Ex Inferis: Event Horizon's Latin Quote Translated & Explained |url=https://screenrant.com/libera-te-tutemet-ex-inferis-event-horizon-latin-quote-translation/ |access-date=2023-06-21 |website=Screen Rant |last=Houseman|first=Andrew}}</ref> D.J. concludes that the ship's drive must have opened a gateway to somewhere beyond the known universe and brought something horrible back with it. His conclusion gains more credibility when a video log is discovered on the ''Event Horizon'', showing the ship's crew members horrifically brutalizing each other after engaging the gravity drive, with their captain chanting in Latin as he holds his own eyeballs in his hands.

Miller immediately orders his crew to speed up their evacuation, ignoring Weir's protests to the contrary. Miller and Smith retrieve CO<sub>2</sub> scrubbers from the ''Event Horizon'' as Peters is lured to her death by a hallucination of her son. Weir finds her body and is flung into a hallucination of his wife's suicide, driving him to gouge out his own eyes and embrace the ship's evil presence. Now corrupted, he uses an explosive to destroy the ''Lewis and Clark'', killing Smith and blasting Cooper into space before killing D.J. by [[Vivisection|vivisecting]] him. Miller confronts Weir on the [[bridge (nautical)|bridge]] but is overpowered. Weir initiates a 10-minute countdown to activate the gravity drive and return the ship to the hellish dimension. Meanwhile, Cooper uses his spacesuit's oxygen supply to propel himself back to the ship and appears at the bridge window. Weir shoots at him, shattering the window and blowing himself into space with the decompression.

Miller, Starck and Cooper survive and manage to seal off the ship's bridge. With their own ship destroyed, Miller plans to destroy the central corridor with explosives, splitting the ''Event Horizon'' in two and using its forward section as a lifeboat. He is attacked by hallucinations which turn out to be the resurrected and even more mutilated Weir. Miller fights him at the gravity drive and detonates the explosives, sacrificing himself to save his remaining crew. The gravity drive activates, pulling the ship's stern into a [[black hole]], returning to the hell dimension. Starck and Cooper enter stasis beside a comatose Justin and wait to be rescued.

Seventy-two days later, the wreckage of the ''Event Horizon'' is boarded by a rescue party who discover the survivors in stasis. Starck hallucinates Weir as one of the rescuers and screams but she quickly awakens from her nightmare. Cooper and the rescue team try to calm the terrified Starck as the doors close.

==Production==
===Conception and writing===
''Event Horizon'' was conceived in the early 1990s by aspiring screenwriter [[Philip Eisner]].<ref name="ibiblio">{{cite web|url=https://www.ibiblio.org/samneill/films/ehpn.txt|title=''Event Horizon''|publisher=[[ibiblio]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010708112800/http://www.ibiblio.org/samneill/films/ehpn.txt|archive-date=July 8, 2001 |url-status=live |access-date=August 8, 2026 }}</ref> Inspired by a rewatching of [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'' (1980), he sought to transpose the haunted-house premise into a science-fiction setting.<ref name=":5">{{Cite web |last=McEvoy |first=Jonathan |date=2016-10-28 |title=[HalloweenFest] Reboarding The Event Horizon - An Interview With Writer Phil Eisner |url=https://www.hidefninja.com/2016/10/28/halloweenfest-reboarding-the-event-horizon-an-interview-with-writer-phil-eisner/ |access-date=2026-08-20 |website=Hi-Def Ninja }}</ref> Eisner also drew from his interest in [[physics]]; he reasoned that exposure to [[Curved spacetime|warped spacetime]] at scales beyond normal human perception would psychologically shatter people.<ref name="OralHistory" /> His other influences included the works of [[H. P. Lovecraft]],<ref name=":17">{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8nJ2O6Wx1bE |title=The Inspiration for Event Horizon Featuring Philip Eisner {{!}} What horror movie inspired Event Horizon |date=2021-06-27 |last=The Successful Screenwriter |access-date=2026-09-05 |via=YouTube}}</ref> [[Robert Wise]]'s ''[[The Haunting (1963 film)|The Haunting]]'' (1963),<ref name="ibiblio" /> Kubrick's ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' (1968),<ref name=":18">{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G1eiGvx3bp0 |title=Philip Eisner on writing Event Horizon |date=2025-05-06 |last=Scriptcake |access-date=2026-09-05 |via=YouTube}}</ref> [[Ridley Scott]]'s [[Alien (film)|''Alien'']] (1979),<ref name=":18" /> the tabletop game ''[[Warhammer 40,000]]'' (starting 1987),<ref name=":17" /> and [[Stephen Hawking]]'s book ''[[A Brief History of Time]]'' (1988).<ref name=":18" />

Eisner first pitched the concept verbally to his [[Employment contract|contract employers]],<ref name="Eisner" />{{Pg|page=00:00:20}} producers [[Lawrence Gordon (producer)|Lawrence Gordon]] and [[Lloyd Levin]], describing it as "a haunted house story in space". Gordon responded favourably and encouraged him to develop a full screenplay.<ref name="ibiblio" />{{sfn|Hughes|1997|pp=30–35}} Eisner began writing in spring 1992,<ref name="ibiblio" /> spending eight months on the first draft<ref name="OralHistory"/> and delivering the initial version of the script by October. Gordon and Levin commissioned the project and advanced it in partnership with [[Paramount Pictures]].<ref name="ibiblio" />

The opening thirty pages remained largely intact through production, aside from the removal of a sequence for budgetary reasons involving the mining vessel ''Lucky Strike'', which would have established the ''Lewis and Clark'' crew working as a team in action, with some elements from it moved to later on in the film when the crew is on ''Event Horizon''. Progress on the remainder of the script was slower, coinciding with personal difficulties for Eisner following the death of his father<ref name=":5" /> in a [[skiing]] accident;<ref name="Eisner" />{{Pg|page=00:00:28}} a studio executive provided support to maintain his momentum.<ref name=":5" /> As the project originated as a pitch, Gordon retained ownership of the material; upon departing [[Largo Entertainment]], he took the ''Event Horizon'' script with him.<ref name=":5" /> The film was subsequently produced by Gordon's new company Golar, whose name is a respelling of Largo.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:00:32–00:00:39}}

===Development and reworking===
[[File:Paul W. S. Anderson by Gage Skidmore.jpg|thumb|upright|Director [[Paul W. S. Anderson|Paul Anderson]] in 2012. He reworked ''Event Horizon'' from an ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]''-style extraterrestrial spaceship story into one about a Hell-possessed ship.]]
After ''[[Mortal Kombat (1995 film)|Mortal Kombat]]'' (1995) was a commercial success in the United States, English director [[Paul W. S. Anderson|Paul Anderson]]<!-- CREDITED WITHOUT THE "W. S." -->{{efn|Anderson was credited as simply "Paul Anderson" on all of his films during the 1990s, including ''Event Horizon''. After his following film, ''[[Soldier (1998 American film)|Soldier]]'' (1998), he adopted "Paul W. S. Anderson", featuring his middle initials, to distinguish himself from [[Paul Thomas Anderson]], as both were registered as "Paul Anderson" in different guilds.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.vulture.com/2014/05/paul-ws-anderson-interview-directing-over-the-top-action-scenes.html |title=Paul W.S. Anderson on Building the Perfect Over-the-Top Action Scene |last=Ebiri |first=Bilge |date=28 May 2014 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |publisher=New York Media |access-date=6 September 2026}}</ref> }} was inundated with screenplay offers, as well as the opportunity to direct the ''Mortal Kombat'' sequel ''[[Mortal Kombat Annihilation]]'' (1997){{sfn|Hughes|1997|pp=30–35}} and the upcoming ''[[X-Men (film)|X-Men]]'' (2000), which he was interested in but withdrew because of its extremely tight production schedule{{sfn|Anderson|Beahm|2021|loc=00:00:36–00:00:48}} or turned it down because he did not want to another [[PG-13]] movie as he had done previously with ''Mortal Kombat''.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:40:28–00:40:55}} He instead sought a darker and more visceral project.<ref>{{cite web |last=Spry |first=Jeff |title=''Event Horizon'' at 25: Director Paul W.S. Anderson on his cult sci-fi film's stylish nightmares (exclusive) |url=https://www.space.com/event-horizon-director-paul-anderson-interview |website=[[Space.com]] |date=15 August 2022 |access-date=5 September 2026}}</ref> He became particularly interested in two science-fiction scripts, ''[[Soldier (1998 American film)|Soldier]]'' (1998) for [[Warner Bros.]] and ''Event Horizon'' for Paramount, and found the choice between them difficult. Anderson initially signed on to direct ''Soldier'', but the project was postponed after its star, [[Kurt Russell]], decided he required a full year of physical training to prepare for the role. Rather than wait, Anderson pivoted back and attached himself to ''Event Horizon'', which at the time still lacked a director.{{sfn|Anderson|Beahm|2021|loc=00:00:51–00:01:25}}<ref name=":1">{{Cite magazine |last=Collis |first=Clark |date=12 August 2022 |title='Event Horizon' director says restoration of long-lost footage would require Snyder Cut efforts |url=https://ew.com/movies/event-horizon-snyder-cut-paul-ws-anderson-interview/ |access-date=2022-12-26 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]}}</ref>

Anderson's initial reaction to the script, which involved the ''Event Horizon'' experiencing a series of hauntings by "tentacular" aliens, having crossed the threshold of their planet or dimension, was that it bore a striking resemblance to ''Alien''. Producer and longtime collaborator [[Jeremy Bolt]] felt it was a "terrific concept" but was "very dense" in terms of length and the storyline got "a bit lost".{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:02:49}} Seeking to avoid direct imitation, Anderson revised the material, reorienting it toward the traditions of classic haunted-house cinema, particularly ''The Haunting'' and ''The Shining'' for their emphasis on unseen evil and ambiguous endings. He also introduced the notion that the vessel itself had become possessed, drawing on the concept of Hell.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:04:03}}<ref name="Hemphill">{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/event-horizon-paul-w-s-anderson-interview-1234749714/|title='Event Horizon' 25 Years Later: Paul W.S. Anderson Looks Back on His Cult Hit|first=Jim|last=Hemphill|date=15 August 2022|website=IndieWire}}</ref> [[Andrew Kevin Walker]], the screenwriter of ''[[Seven (1995 film)|Seven]]'' (1995), was brought in as a [[script doctor]] to perform a major rewrite of the screenplay, developing its hell and damnation concept.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=01:21:58}}<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-08-15 |title=Event Horizon at 25: Paul W.S. Anderson reflects on his misunderstood cult classic |url=https://www.gamesradar.com/event-horizon-paul-w-s-anderson-interview-anniversary/ |access-date=2026-08-09 |website=GamesRadar+ }}</ref> Among his other contributions was a floating tooth (which was filmed but later cut) discovered by the crew upon boarding the ''Event Horizon''.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:04:03}} Walker received no on-screen credit for his work.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lambie |first=Ryan |date=2010-09-12 |title=Looking back at Event Horizon |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/looking-back-at-event-horizon/ |access-date=2026-08-09 |website=Den of Geek }}</ref>

Anderson has stated that the screenplay was also inspired by [[Andrei Tarkovsky]]'s ''[[Solaris (1972 film)|Solaris]]'' (1972), a film of which he is fond of.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Patches |first=Matt |date=21 February 2014 |title=The Other Paul Anderson: The Psychotic Action Vision of 'Pompeii' Director Paul W. S. Anderson |url=https://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/the-other-paul-anderson-the-psychotic-action-vision-of-pompeii-director-paul-w-s-anderson/ |access-date=2022-12-21 |website=[[Grantland]]}}</ref> Bolt believed that the sequence in which rain falls inside the spaceship drew from ''Solaris''. Both Anderson and Bolt recalled that Eisner denied having seen either ''Solaris'' or [[The Walt Disney Company|Disney]]'s ''[[The Black Hole (1979 film)|The Black Hole]]'' (1979), a denial they regarded as untrue. Anderson, however, earlier indicated in the film's audio commentary that he himself had introduced the rain sequence, remarking: "the reason for this coolant... This is something I added to the movie purely ... for the end result which was when they switched the gravity back on the idea that it was raining in the spaceship."{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:27:52–00:28:26}} Anderson also introduced the influence of ''[[Hellraiser]]'' (1987) to the film.<ref name=":17" />

The original script had the crew carrying conventional guns (portrayed more like a space [[Coast guard|coast-guard]] service). Anderson removed them because he saw no logical reason for firearms in space; they were replaced with [[Nail gun|nail guns]].{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:19:00}}

According Eisner, roughly two weeks before principal photography began, Paramount conducted informal surveys at a shopping mall in the [[San Fernando Valley]], asking what a black hole was. The studio was alarmed that few respondents could define the concept and feared the film would not be frightening as a result. This led to pressure to make the ship's connection to Hell more explicit, treating it as a "hell ship." Eisner opposed the changes.<ref name="OralHistory" />

The film was an American–British co-production.<ref name="bfi">{{cite web|title=''Event Horizon'' (1997)|url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/09d0fd47-21df-5425-a162-1ba2908bdc46/event-horizon|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171229215649/http://www.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b7fd8d4ad|url-status=live|archive-date=29 December 2017|website=BFI|publisher=[[British Film Institute]]|access-date=11 February 2018}}</ref><ref name="afi">{{cite web|title=''Event Horizon'' (1997)|url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/60720-EVENT-HORIZON|archive-url=http://web.archive.org/web/20241129011952/https://catalog.afi.com/Film/60720-EVENT-HORIZON|archive-date=29 November 2024|url-status=live|website=AFI|publisher=[[American Film Institute]]|access-date=3 August 2026}}</ref> It was produced by Gordon, Levin and Bolt as a Golar Production in association with Bolt and Anderson's company [[Impact Pictures]], with Nick Gillott serving as executive producer and Paramount Pictures funding and distributing it.<ref name="ibiblio" /> Paramount greenlit the film only 10 weeks before principal photography. Anderson recalled that the roughly 10 months from greenlight to release was "incredibly fast" for such a complicated film.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:14:33–00:15:30}}

===Pre-production===
Pre-production began in May 1996. The decision was made to shoot in England, where Anderson and Bolt were based and where [[Pinewood Studios]] offered the necessary facilities, including the [[007 Stage|large stages associated with the ''James Bond'' films]]. Early hires included cinematographer [[Adrian Biddle]] (as they loved his work on ''[[Aliens (film)|Aliens]]'', ''[[Thelma & Louise]]'', and ''[[1492: Conquest of Paradise]]'') and editor [[Martin Hunter (editor)|Martin Hunter]] (who had collaborated with Anderson on ''Mortal Kombat'').<ref name="ibiblio"/> Biddle was the first to be signed on to work on the film by Anderson; he was hired long before any of the cast. Anderson explained this was because it was "very much a design and look-led movie".{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:10:58}} The production designer was Joseph Bennett, a relatively young designer whose work on ''[[Jude (film)|Jude]]'' (1996) impressed the team; Anderson valued Bennett's enthusiasm and visual flair, believing it would compensate for limited prior experience on large-scale films.<ref name="ibiblio"/>

Visual effects supervisor [[Richard Yuricich]], a veteran of science fiction films whose credits included ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey]]'', ''[[Close Encounters of the Third Kind]]'', and ''[[Blade Runner]]'', was secured early on the strong recommendation of the ''[[Mission: Impossible (film series)|Mission: Impossible]]'' team. Construction manager Harry Metcalfe oversaw the ambitious set-building program. The overall aesthetic was conceived as dark, Gothic, and nightmarish, blending high technology with medieval and visceral human elements. Anderson insisted on a distinctive design that avoided feeling like a lesser ''Alien'' or ''Blade Runner''.<ref name="ibiblio"/>

Sets had already been designed but not built when the short timeline began; according to Anderson construction manager Harry Metcalfe was the only one with that occupation in the United Kingdom willing to attempt the highly detailed and complex builds at the required speed, which he also noted "cost a lot".{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:14:43}} The interior of the ''Lewis and Clark'' was built as a single continuous multi-level set (bridge to gravity tanks), allowing actors to move freely between spaces; continuous [[Steadicam]] shots were used early on to show this.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:07:19}} Portions of the set, including the front half,{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:06:43}} were mounted on [[hydraulics]] so the ship could be physically shaken for turbulence sequences (combined with shaking camera work by Biddle).{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:18:52}} The retracting exit floor was also hydraulic and built over a [[water tank]] for extra depth, so the set extended from the stage rafters down into the tank.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:23:38}}

====Casting====
The filmmakers sought a strong ensemble of skilled actors capable of handling the script's perceived three-dimensional characters rather than relying on a single major star.<ref name="ibiblio" /> Captain Miller was originally envisioned as a white [[Texan]] in concept art.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=The Unseen Event Horizon |date=2021 |orig-date=2005 |time=00:03:35 |via=[[Scream Factory]]}}</ref> After Gordon suggested Laurence Fishburne for the role and the crew strongly agreed, he was cast.<ref name="GTMR"/>{{pg|00:17:24}} Fishburne was hooked by the screenplay's suspense and its focus on character over typical science-fiction spectacle; he was particularly drawn to the dynamic between the experienced astronaut and the ship's designer.<ref name="ibiblio"/> He turned down the role of the judge in [[Francis Ford Coppola]]'s ''[[The Rainmaker (1997 film)|The Rainmaker]]'' (1997) to take the part.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/894213364/|title=Widening horizons|date=10 October 1996|newspaper=[[Cambridge Evening News]]|page=4|via=[[Newspapers.com]]|access-date=19 August 2026|url-access=subscription}}</ref> After [[Gary Sinise]] and [[Jeremy Irons]] declined offers to play Dr. William Weir,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Leo |first=Vincent |date=1998-09-09 |title=''Event Horizon'' (1997) |url=https://qwipster.net/1998/09/09/event-horizon-1997/ |access-date=2026-08-20 |website=Qwipster }}</ref> Neill took on the role instead, intrigued by the story's "air of mystery" and the chance to portray a grieving, troubled scientist whose greatest creation reappears after years of loss.<ref name="ibiblio"/>

Quinlan, whom the crew sought after admiring her work in ''[[Apollo 13 (film)|Apollo 13]]'' (1995), joined as emergency technician Peters, attracted by the ensemble nature of the piece and the opportunity to play an active, professional single mother rather than reacting primarily to special effects. Richardson was cast as navigator Starck, drawn to familiarity with the other cast members as well as the character's sharp edge. Richard T. Jones played Cooper, connecting with the crew's energetic, protective "joker." Jack Noseworthy took the role of engineer Justin, drawn to the script's psychological-thriller quality and its internal, character-driven horror. Jason Isaacs portrayed the taciturn doctor D.J., interested in the roller-coaster intensity of the story and a role that initially appears potentially suspicious. Isaacs was previously in Anderson's debut feature ''[[Shopping (1994 film)|Shopping]]'' (1994). Sean Pertwee was cast as pilot Smith, inspired by the character's superstitious attachment to the ship (echoing the "never get off the boat" philosophy of ''[[Apocalypse Now]]''; Pertwee cited Chef, the character who said that in that film, as an inspiration for his performance in ''Event Horizon'').<ref name="ibiblio"/>

===Filming===
[[File:Pinewood Studios gateway.jpg|alt=A photo of the entrance to Pinewood Studios in England|thumb|''Event Horizon'' was filmed at [[Pinewood Studios]] in England (pictured in 2015).]]

[[Principal photography]] began on 18 November 1996 and wrapped on 14 March 1997, spanning roughly 16 weeks.<ref name="ibiblio"/> The film was shot entirely at [[Pinewood Studios]] in [[Iver Heath]], England,{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:12:21–00:12:41}} using seven sound stages, two silent stages,<ref name="ibiblio"/> and the studio's garden for the only exterior shot set on Earth. This sequence, the first filmed for the production, showed a video of Peter's son's birthday party.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:12:21–00:12:41}} Under Metcalfe's supervision, approximately 20 different sets were constructed, filmed, and struck. The interiors of the ''Event Horizon'' filled much of the available space, including the vast [[007 Stage]].<ref name="ibiblio"/> It was Anderson's largest production yet, with reported budget estimates ranging from {{USD|50 million}}{{efn|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name=":5" /><ref name="Eisner">{{Cite AV media |title=Writer Philip Eisner on Event Horizon |date=2021 |time=00:07:53 |publisher=[[Scream Factory]]}}</ref><ref name="GTMR">{{cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dYAkrtCzOLI |title=329- 'Event Horizon' Screenwriter, Philip Eisner-Movies with Bret from Marcus Theatres |publisher=Geek To Me Radio |date=2022-11-01 |via=YouTube |access-date=2026-09-05 |location=00:07:18}}</ref><ref name="chicagotribune">{{Cite web |date=1997-08-21 |title=New Horizons |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/1997/08/21/new-horizons-10/ |access-date=2026-08-09 |website=[[Chicago Tribune]]}}</ref><ref name="Blair">{{cite news |last=Bliar |first=Iain |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/346483503/ |title=''Event Horizon'' filmed on huge sound stages in England |date=31 July 1997 |newspaper=[[The Sentinel (Pennsylvania)|The Sentinel]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-access=subscription |access-date=30 August 2026 }}</ref><ref name="Mosby">{{cite magazine |url=https://archive.org/details/Total.Film.and.SFX.Present.90s.Movies.The.Ultimate.Celebration.2016.True.PDF/page/n111/mode/2up |title=90's Movies - Visual effects provided by [[Cinesite]] and [[Computer Film Company]]. Using an architectural cam program, Anderson modeled the ''Event Horizon'' ship after [[Notre Dame Cathedral]]. Effects supervisors Yuricich and [[Neil Corbould]] kept most visuals [[In-camera effect|in-camera]], and moving sets were constructed for the gravity drive gyroscope and the revolving tunnel. Because the majority of scenes were filmed in a studio on gothic-inspired sets, Anderson felt the cast experienced a kind of "cabin fever" that better served their performances.<ref name="OralHistory" /><ref name="Surrey">{{Cite web |last=Surrey |first=Miles |date=2020-08-12 |title="Hell Is Only a Word": The Enduring Terror of 'Event Horizon' |url=https://www.theringer.com/movies/2020/8/12/21364035/event-horizon-paul-ws-anderson-retrospective-amazon |access-date=2022-12-21 |website=[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]}}</ref><ref name=":1" /><ref name=":0" /> Joely Richardson called the experience of working on the film "cursed".<ref>{{cite web |last=Fisher |first=Kieran |date=14 August 2019 |title=The Hellish Production of 'Event Horizon' |url=https://filmschoolrejects.com/event-horizon-production/ |website=[[Film School Rejects]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201019162507/https://filmschoolrejects.com/event-horizon-production/|archive-date=19 October 2020}}</ref>

The gravity drive set was filmed on the 007 Stage at Pinewood Studios in London during the Christmas period. The cast and crew faced challenging conditions due to the lack of insulation and the stage's thin metal walls, which made the environment extremely cold; rainfall also produced deafening noise from reverberations off the roof. Neill found the later stages of filming particularly demanding, as he was required to work largely unclothed.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:38:47–00:39:25}}

The effects team minimized the use of [[computer-generated imagery]] (CGI), preferring practical effects and in-camera techniques whenever feasible, in line with Yuricich's insistence on shooting as much as possible practically. CGI was employed mainly as an enhancement rather than for fully synthetic shots. As Anderson noted, the production avoided visual effects where unnecessary, favored traditional methods with greater reliance on models over CGI, and, after discussion, opted to build the gravity drive gyroscope as a practical effect rather than create it digitally.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:37:18–00:38:10}} Many lighting effects were captured in-camera rather than added digitally.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:26:06}}

For zero-gravity sequences, the actors were suspended upside down in harnesses and spun. At the start of filming, the script featured far more such scenes throughout the ''Event Horizon'', but time constraints, the cast's discomfort with wire work, and budget limitations led the introducion of "magnetic boots" instead. Anderson noted that competing with the practical zero-gravity effects of [[Apollo 13 (film)|''Apollo 13'']] (1995), which were done in confined spaces, would have been difficult given the film's larger sets, so the production relied on traditional methods. Most of these scenes were shot on [[VistaVision]] to provide a larger negative for post-production; Anderson used unusual angles and continuous camera movement and rotation to heighten disorientation.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=The Point of No Return - The Filming of Event Horizon |date=2021 |orig-date=2006 |time=00:03:06-0:04:43 |via=[[Scream Factory]]}}</ref>

All shots of the gravity drive gyroscope were filmed in-camera on set. The crew was concerned that production would be severely disrupted if the prop malfunctioned. Although it never fully broke down, pieces occasionally fell off or rattled inside it, requiring most of the on-location dialogue to be looped or re-recorded in post-production. The shot in which Justin first approaches the gyroscope was achieved by having the actor walk backwards out of frame; the footage was then reversed in post-production so that the gyroscope's stop into position, prior to the gateway to hell being opened, appeared smoother.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:29:19–00:30:21}}

During principal photography at Pinewood Studios, a fire broke out on Stage 7 while filming a climax sequence in which Fishburne's character is pursued by a fireball unleashed by the burning man. Bolt stated that real flames and a wind machine ignited [[silk]] set dressings, engulfing the stage within a minute; he called it the production's only major mishap, with no injuries reported and filming resuming after reconstruction.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:42:21}} In the audio commentary, Anderson described a slightly different account: a large [[flamethrower]] used for the fireball effect was knocked over by the force of the blast, set the wooden stage alight, and directed flames toward nearby [[Fuel tank|gas tanks]], prompting an evacuation. The tanks were unharmed, though the stage itself burned.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:24:27}}

The production built a short practical section of the central corridor for the ''Event Horizon''. To create the impression of a far longer passageway without relying on the originally planned extensive visual-effects work for it, the production placed two large mirrors from France facing each other at opposite ends of the set. The resulting reflections made an inexpensive optical illusion of its continuous length. Camera positions had to be carefully controlled to avoid capturing the mirrors or the camera itself in the reflections.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:25:26}} A green room set adjacent to the core was constructed from old green [[Printed circuit board|computer circuit boards]] and shot with [[Wide-angle lens|wide-angle lenses]] to enlarge the space.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:43:05}}

As Anderson was sometimes too busy filming other scenes, second-unit director [[Vadim Jean]] filmed some parts of the "bloody orgy" footage.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=01:24:10}} Real-life amputees were used for special effects scenes where ''Event Horizon'' crew members were mutilated, and [[pornographic film]] actors were hired to make the sex and rape scenes more realistic and graphic.<ref name="Q&A" />

=== Special and visual effects ===
Models supervisor David Sharpe oversaw an extensive miniatures program: roughly 28 models in total (approximately 14 of the ''Event Horizon'', 12 of the ''Lewis and Clark'', and two of the Daylight Space Station), built in multiple scales to allow both wide establishing shots of the enormous vessels and detailed action close-ups. Many models were constructed as "explodables" for destruction sequences.<ref name="ibiblio" />

The opening Daylight Station pull-back (blinds rising, then revealing Weir is on this orbiting station) was a complex composite blending multiple scale-model sizes with a live-action plate. It required dozens of revisions (24 or 25 according to Anderson) at the effects house and was described as extremely difficult; according to Anderson the lead compositor left the industry for a year afterward because of it.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:05:04}} Multiple scales of the ''Lewis and Clark'' were built; the miniature used for its destruction measured approximately 25 feet (7.6 m).{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:12:02}} The main ''Event Horizon'' model was substantially larger, about 70 feet (21 m).{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:12:18}} The ''Lewis and Clark'' destruction combined a practical miniature explosion with CGI (including a spacesuited figure of Cooper) and a stunt performer against a real explosion.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:11:44}}

Floating debris in the [[zero-gravity]] ''Event Horizon'' corridors was computer-generated;{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:24:51}} one object was a bottle of mineral water named after [[Bernd Eichinger]], included as a personal homage as Eichinger was a friend of Anderson and Bolt's.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:24:58}} The airlock sequence involving the veins swelling on Noseworthy's character used practical rubber tubes inflated by an [[air pump]];{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:53:34}} blood from the eyes was done with tubes hidden inside the actor's fingers.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:54:23}}

For the approach sequence, the ''Event Horizon'' was deliberately placed in Neptune's [[upper atmosphere]], surrounded by clouds, to create a classic haunted-house or ghost-ship appearance reminiscent of the ''[[Mary Celeste]]'' or the ''[[Flying Dutchman]]''. Anderson preferred this imagery over a simple orbit, even though it was scientifically unrealistic. The clouds were initially omitted during production because the visual-effects supervisor did not relay Anderson's request; they were added as CG elements only in post-production after Anderson noticed and complained about their absence. At the time, convincing computer-generated clouds were technically challenging, and according to Anderson, the resulting effect created for the film was later reused in other British films for the next five years.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:19:16–00:20:30}}

Other CGI included a digital double of Pertwee's character Smith in a spacesuit walking on the exterior of the ''Lewis and Clark'', noted by Anderson and Bolt as relatively advanced for 1997.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:35:06}} Prosthetics, including Weir's eye sockets, were frequently enhanced with CGI rather than fully replaced.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:12:43}} Holly's (as Claire) eyes in the nightmare sequence were also originally used only white contact lenses; these were later judged unoriginal and not scary enough by Anderson and Bolt and subsequently replaced/enhanced with CGI hollow sockets.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:10:59}} A digital double of Weir exiting through a window was later judged by Anderson not to hold up well, though it was considered cutting-edge at the time by the crew for a figure of that size in frame.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:20:09}} Moving monitor graphics were mostly achieved via green screen; few practical playback monitors were used on set.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:23:12}}

The animation for the film's title credits opening was inspired by the title sequences of the long-running British science-fiction television series ''[[Doctor Who]]''.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:00:45–00:00:51}}<!-- See also: https://vfxblog.com/2017/08/15/event-horizon-vfx-20th-anniversary/
https://web.archive.org/web/20260605210022/https://www.vfxhq.com/1997/event.html -->

===Design===
Anderson and concept artist Rick Buden developed the film's visual style through previsualization in Los Angeles before work relocated to London.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=The Unseen Event Horizon |date=2021 |orig-date=2005 |time=00:03:48-00:04:37 |via=[[Scream Factory]]}}</ref> Bennett and the art department established a "techno-medieval" aesthetic for the ''Event Horizon'': when powered and lit, it resembled a modern vessel in the vein of ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' (1968); when darkened, its forms evoked a dungeon or Gothic cathedral. Inspiration came from particle-accelerator interiors (for their technological yet almost armored, medieval quality), as well as [[Architecture of Tibet|Tibetan]], medieval, and [[Renaissance]] design elements. The ship's overall form was [[cruciform]]; designers scanned elements of [[Notre-Dame Cathedral]] into computers and incorporated towers and other features into the thruster pods and structure, so that from a distance the vessel appeared as a crucifix hanging above Neptune. Areas such as the second and third containments surrounding the gravity drive (literally a contained black hole) received particular attention as the source of the malevolent force.<ref name="ibiblio"/>

In early design concepts for the gravity-drive chamber (the "third containment"), the spikes surrounding the core were intended to be mechanical. When the drive activated and came to a stop, the spikes would extend from the walls and lock into the drive itself. Although the production considered realizing the effect digitally with CGI, the feature was ultimately abandoned due to budgetary constraints.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:04:55}}

By contrast, the ''Lewis and Clark'' was designed as a more familiar, functional vessel—described as a submarine crossed with an ambulance—serving as the crew's relatively safe, lived-in environment.<ref name="ibiblio" /> It drew deliberate inspiration from the ''Nostromo'' in ''Alien'' (1979), aiming for a grubby, realistic, familiar Earth-origin look rather than a pristine future; whites on the interior were deliberately dirtied and smudged.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:07:45}}

Anderson conceived the film's spinning [[Meat Grinder|meat-grinder]]-like containment, inspired by a [[Studio Tour|Universal Studios Tour]] ride from his youth (the ''[[The Six Million Dollar Man|Six Million Dollar Man]]'' attraction, which he believed later became the Earthquake or ''[[Dante's Peak]]'' ride). The ride spun a tube around passengers to disorient them in a haunted-house style, an effect he recreated on a large scale.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:27:14–00:27:42}}

Scenes set aboard the ''Event Horizon'' contain religious and Gothic architectural imagery. Anderson has explained that the ship's overall form is deliberately [[cruciform]], resembling a [[crucifix]] when viewed from above,<ref name=":0" />{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=21:00}} and its medical bay also features Gothic-style pillars of distinctly medieval design.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=26:32}} The gravity drive gyroscope has been compared to the [[ophanim]];<ref>{{Cite web |title=Retrospective: 25 years ago, Event Horizon made space exploration look like a terrible idea |url=https://www.flicks.co.uk/features/retrospective-25-years-ago-event-horizon-made-space-exploration-look-like-a-terrible-idea/ |access-date=2026-09-16 |website=Flicks }}</ref> it was inspired by the [[Lament Configuration]] from the 1987 film ''[[Hellraiser]]'', particularly in the way it realigns and reshapes itself.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:30:22}}

Influences on the overall look and framing included the paintings of [[Hieronymus Bosch]] and [[Pieter Bruegel the Elder|Pieter Bruegel]] as well as the photographs of [[Joel-Peter Witkin]], with the aim of giving the film a painterly quality.{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:56:04}} The film also has visual homages to classic haunted-house and horror films including ''The Haunting'' (1963),{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:49:13}} ''[[Don't Look Now]]'' (1973),{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=1:05:36}} ''[[The Amityville Horror (1979 film)|The Amityville Horror]]'' (1979), and ''The Shining'' (1980).{{Sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=1:23:34}}

As Anderson explained, directors usually have a standard 10-week editing period to produce a film's first cut, as guaranteed by the [[Directors Guild of America]]. However, due to the short production schedule, the rapidly approaching release date, and the fact that principal photography had not finished, Anderson agreed to a six-week editing period, and promised to deliver the film by August 1997, as Paramount wanted a hit film before ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]''{{'s}} planned September release date. When the main unit wrapped, Anderson was supposed to start editing the film, but he had two weeks of shooting left with the [[second unit]], shortening post-production to just four weeks, during which only a rough cut could be assembled. He noted that at two hours and 10 minutes, it was overly long, with weak direction and acting that could have used another editing pass; unfinished special effects; and a poor sound mix.<ref name="Q&A">{{cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TXL_CM4DXZ8 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/TXL_CM4DXZ8 |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live|title=Paul W.S. Anderson Event Horizon Q&A |date=18 December 2011 |publisher=[[YouTube]] |first=Jonny|last=Bunning |access-date=16 July 2018}}</ref>{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=01:17:44}}

[[Martin Hunter (editor)|Martin Hunter]] edited nearly the entire film. [[Terry Rawlings]] assisted for a few weeks, offering guidance and a few edits, most notably the transition from Neil shaving to the space-station window blinds opening, the film's first [[jump scare]]. Anderson regarded this cut as one of his favorites, noting that it heightened audience anticipation and fear thereon.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:04:32–00:05:03}}

[[Test screening]] audiences reacted poorly to the first cut, which had a runtime of 130 minutes.<ref name=":1" /><ref name="NME">{{Cite web |last=Conterio |first=Martyn |date=12 August 2022 |title=''Event Horizon'': how ''Titanic'' sunk Paul W.S. Anderson's cult horror |url=https://www.nme.com/features/film-features/event-horizon-paul-ws-anderson-titanic-horror-3288966 |access-date=24 September 2022 |website=[[NME]] }}</ref> A second assembly, nicknamed "The [[Old Testament]] Cut" by the crew for its more explicit references to hell and damnation, received a similar response.<ref>{{Cite AV media |title=Deleted Scenes - Secrets |date=2006 |last=Anderson |first=Paul |author-link=Paul W. S. Anderson |time=00:08:42}}</ref> Viewers objected to the extreme gore;{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=01:16:51}} Anderson and Bolt later claimed that some audience members fainted.<ref name="Q&A" /> Paramount executives, who had stopped reviewing [[dailies]] before the graphic sequences were filmed, saw the completed film for the first time with the test audience and were shocked by its intensity. They insisted on a shorter running time and reduced gore, and some felt the film was "besmirching" their ''[[Star Trek]]'' franchise. Anderson agreed that while his first cut was too long, Paramount forced him to make one that was instead too short, and that the film would benefit by the restoration of around 10 minutes of footage, including some of the deleted gore.<ref name="Q&A" /><ref name=":2">{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2022/film/news/event-horizon-explained-paul-ws-anderson-anniversary-1235336634/|title='Event Horizon' at 25: Paul W.S. Anderson Recalls Paramount Fearing His Horror Film Slandered 'Star Trek'|first=J. Kim|last=Murphy|date=9 August 2022|website=Variety}}</ref> Over 30 minutes of footage was cut;<ref name=":1" /> the finished film had a runtime of 96 minutes.<ref name="bfi" />

The film's ending was a combination of two unused alternate endings. One did not have a jump scare at the end when another rescue crew finds the last two survivors, and Starck hallucinates that she sees Weir, although there was a similar version of the scene included in this ending where she hears screams of the ''Event Horizon'' crew and screams before Cooper wakes her. This was the film's original ending in the shooting script.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.imsdb.com/scripts/Event-Horizon.html|title=Event Horizon |work=Internet Movie Script Database|access-date=16 July 2018|first=Philip|last=Eisner}}</ref> The second ending had Miller fight the burned man from his visions at the core instead of Weir, but this was changed due to the negative test screening.<ref name="BehindTheScenes">{{cite AV media |people=[[Paul W. S. ===Music===
[[Michael Kamen]] was hired to compose the film's score. Anderson, a fan of hybrid genre music, invited the [[electronic dance music]] duo [[Orbital (band)|Orbital]] to collaborate with Kamen and to provide synthesized sounds for the film's unsettling atmosphere.<ref>{{Cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=sAImDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA4 |title=Contemporary Film Music: Investigating Cinema Narratives and Composition |last1=Coleman |first1=Lindsay |last2=Tillman |first2=Joakim |year=2017 |publisher=Springer |isbn=978-1-13757-375-9 |location=New York|page=4}}</ref><ref name="NME"/> This was also Anderson's first movie to have a [[Classical music|classical]] score.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:02:47–00:02:50}} [[The Prodigy]]'s "[[Funky Shit]]" from their 1997 album ''[[The Fat of the Land]]'' plays over the closing credits; the band visited the set during production and later offered the track shortly before the album topped several charts and brought them fame.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=01:31:19–01:31:46}}

A soundtrack album was released which combined various cues from the score into four tracks of approximately ten minutes.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Event Horizon Soundtrack (1997) |url=https://www.soundtrack.net/album/event-horizon/ |access-date=2022-07-12 |website=Soundtrack.Net}}</ref>

==Release==
''Event Horizon'' was originally slated for a fall 1997 release.<ref name="NME"/> After ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]'' missed its planned summer opening and was postponed to winter, Paramount moved forward ''Event Horizon'' to fill the open summer slot.<ref name="NME"/><ref name=":2" /> In May 1997, ''Event Horizon''{{'}}s release date was scheduled for 1 August; ''[[The Houston Chronicle]]'' wrote that, if Paramount marketed it effectively and audiences responded well, it could become a surprise box-office success.<ref>{{cite newspaper|title=Aug. 1 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/1210220472/ |newspaper=[[The Houston Chronicle]] |date=18 May 1997 |page=348 |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |access-date=20 September 2026 |url-access=subscription }}</ref>

Anderson has repeatedly criticized the rushed marketing campaign that accompanied the accelerated schedule for its August 1997 release. Limited time was available to develop trailers, posters, and promotional materials. He has described the final theatrical poster as particularly unsatisfactory, remarking that "you could throw a plate at the screen and randomly select a better image."<ref name=":2" /> Bolt lobbied hard for a poster of Jack in his spacesuit in the [[Meat grinder|meat-grinder]]-like containment to the gravity drive. Anderson agreed it would have been better than the final poster design.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:26:55–00:27:13}} The campaign, in his view, failed to convey the film's horror intensity and instead positioned it somewhat generically as a space adventure from the studio behind ''[[Star Trek]]''. Anderson later stated that Paramount "almost sold it as a slightly darker version of ''Star Trek'' or something." The film opened against stronger summer competition, including ''[[Air Force One (film)|Air Force One]]'', and was characterized by Anderson as a "feel-bad" movie placed in a "feel-good" season.<ref name=":2" /><ref name=":13">{{Cite web |date=2022-08-15 |title=Event Horizon at 25: Director Says of Course he Talks About Making a Sequel |url=https://www.fandom.com/articles/event-horizon-25th-anniversary-sequel |access-date=2026-08-09 |website=Fandom }}</ref>

An episode of ''[[HBO First Look]]'' featuring behind-the-scenes footage of the film's production first aired on American television in July 1997.<ref>{{cite newspaper |url=https://www.newspapers.com/image/417657655/ |title=TV |newspaper=
Chippewa Herald-Telegram |page=23 |date=10 July 1997 |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |access-date=20 September 2026 |url-access=subscription }}</ref> A [[novelization]] of the film, written by [[Steven Edward McDonald]], was also published on 1 August 1997.{{cn|date=July 2026}}

The film premiered in [[Beverly Hills, California]] on 12 August 1997, with Fishburne, Neill, Quinlan, and Isaacs among those in attendance.<ref name="OralHistory" /><ref name=":6">{{Cite web |date=July 13, 2026 |title=''Jurassic Park'' actor Sam Neill dies. Look back at his career |url=https://www.lohud.com/picture-gallery/news/2026/07/13/sam-neill-of-jurassic-park-dies-look-back-at-his-career/90898482007/ |access-date=August 12, 2026 |website=[[The Journal News]] }}</ref> Three days later, it was released in American theaters.<ref name=":6" /><ref name="BOM" />

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Event Horizon'' grossed $9.5 million during its opening weekend in the United States and Canada, showing in over 2,311 theaters and ranking fourth at the box office behind the debuting ''[[Cop Land]]'' ($13.5 million) and the holdovers ''[[Air Force One (film)|Air Force One]]'' ($12.4 million) and ''[[Conspiracy Theory (film)|Conspiracy Theory]]'' ($12.3 million).<ref name="BOM" /> In the United Kingdom, it earned {{USD|1.7 million}} in its first four days on 207 screens and ranked third behind ''[[Men in Black (1997 film)|Men in Black]]'' and ''[[Bean (film)|Bean]]''; ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' described the opening as "solid but not spectacular".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Groves |first=Don |date=1997-08-27 |title='Event' bows in U.K. with $1.7 mil B.O. |url=https://variety.com/1997/film/box-office/event-bows-in-u-k-with-1-7-mil-b-o-1116677134/ |access-date=2026-08-16 |website=Variety }}</ref> It ultimately ended its run in UK theaters with £3.6 million.<ref name=":3" />

In its second weekend the film fell 54 percent to $4.4 million from 2,328 theaters, dropping to seventh place behind the debuts of ''[[G.I. Jane]]'' and ''[[Money Talks (1997 film)|Money Talks]]'', as well as ''Air Force One'', the new release ''[[Mimic (film)|Mimic]]'', ''Conspiracy Theory'' and ''Cop Land''.<ref name="numbers" /> In its third weekend (the Labor Day frame) it declined a further 29 percent to $3.1 million, falling to eleventh place. The following weekend it dropped to thirteenth with $1.3 million as the theater count was reduced to 1,630. By its fifth weekend it had fallen to fifteenth place with under $1 million, and it left the top 20 by its sixth weekend in mid-September, ultimately finishing its American run with $26.7 million.<ref name="numbers">{{cite web |title= Event Horizon (1997) | url = https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Event-Horizon |website=[[The Numbers (website)|The Numbers]] |access-date = 18 August 2011}}</ref><ref name="BOM">{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0119081/ |title=''Event Horizon'' (1997) |work=[[Box Office Mojo]] |publisher=[[IMDb]] |access-date=16 July 2018 }}</ref><ref name=":4" />

Paramount began a wider international rollout in mid-September. The film opened to $721,809 from 120 screens in Australia and later took $319,777 from 24 screens in Singapore, $302,370 from 64 prints in Italy, and $354,000 in five days on 128 screens in France.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Groves |first=Don |date=October 14, 1997 |title=O'seas accelerates |url=https://variety.com/1997/film/box-office/o-seas-accelerates-111662420/ |work=Variety |access-date=September 11, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=November 4, 1997 |title=Brits hit o'seas |url=https://variety.com/1997/film/box-office/brits-hit-o-seas-111661004/ |work=Variety |access-date=September 11, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Woods |first=Mark |date=January 27, 1998 |title=O'seas B.O. ship-shape |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/o-seas-b-o-ship-shape-1117467101/ |work=Variety |access-date=September 11, 2026}}</ref><ref name="Euro">{{Cite news |last=Groves |first=Don |date=May 12, 1998 |title=Euro B.O. droops |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/euro-b-o-droops-1117470706/ |work=Variety |access-date=September 11, 2026}}</ref> The film's foreign cume was roughly $20.4 million by May 1998.<ref name="Euro"/> A delayed 13 July 1998 release in [[Spain]] sold 366,515 tickets and grossed nearly {{€|1.3 million}}<!--€1,290,396 which was equal to $1,418,145 in July 1998-->; it was distributed there by [[Universal Pictures International]].<ref name="ICAA">{{cite web|url=https://sede.mcu.gob.es/CatalogoICAA/en-us/Peliculas/Detalle?Pelicula=103697|title=''Horizonte Final'' (''Event Horizon'') |work=[[Institute of Cinematography and Audiovisual Arts]] |publisher=[[Ministry of Culture (Spain)|Ministry of Culture]] |language=es |access-date=4 August 2026 }}</ref>

The film was a major [[box-office bomb]].{{efn|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name="Lambie"/><ref name="Surrey"/><ref name="NME"/><ref name="IGNEHessay"/><ref name="Zavvi">{{cite web |last=Ryder |first=Alistair |title=''Event Horizon'' At 25: How This Box Office Flop Became A Cult Classic |website=[[Zavvi]] |date=15 August 2022 |url=https://www.zavvi.com/blog/film/event-horizon-at-25-how-this-box-office-flop-became-a-cult-classic/ |access-date=20 September 2026}}</ref>}} In late September 1997, while it was still in release, [[Viacom (1952–2006)|Viacom]] (Paramount's parent) stock fell 5 percent after [[Wall Street]] analysts cut third-quarter cash-flow forecasts, partly citing a [[write-off]] on ''Event Horizon''; no amount for which was disclosed.<ref>{{cite news |last=Peers |first=Martin |title=Viacom stock slips as Wall St. rethinks |url=https://variety.com/1997/biz/news/viacom-stock-slips-as-wall-st-rethinks-111662528/ |work=Variety |date=September 24, 1997 |access-date=September 11, 2026}}</ref> According to the industry magazine ''[[Boxoffice Pro|BoxOffice]]'', its worldwide theatrical total was $47.1 million.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |title=''Event Horizon'' |url=http://www.boxoffice.com/statistics/movies/event-horizon-1997.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131103024216/http://www.boxoffice.com/statistics/movies/event-horizon-1997.html |archive-date=November 3, 2013 |access-date=August 14, 2026 |website=[[Boxoffice Pro|BoxOffice.com]]}}</ref>

===Critical response===
[[File:Kathleen Quinlan 1975.JPG|thumb|upright|[[Kathleen Quinlan]] (pictured in 1975) was singled out for praise by critics at ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' and ''[[The Washington Post]]'' for her performance. ''The Washington Post'' described her as a "close second" to Fishburne's.<ref name=":9"/><ref name="wp"/>]]
Upon release, ''Event Horizon'' received mostly negative reviews from critics,{{efn|Attributed to multiple references:<ref name=":1" /><ref name="Lambie"/><ref name="Surrey"/><ref name="NME"/><ref name="IGNEHessay"/><ref name="Zavvi"/>}} who often dismissed it as shallow, derivative, and overly gory.<ref name="Lambie" /> {{Rotten Tomatoes prose|score=36|count=96|average=5.4|consensus=Despite a strong opening that promises sci-fi thrills, ''Event Horizon'' quickly devolves into an exercise of style over substance whose flashy effects and gratuitous gore fail to mask its overreliance on horror clichés.|ref=yes |access-date=24 June 2025}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 35 out of 100, based on 20 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Event Horizon'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/event-horizon |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=16 July 2018 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "D+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web |website=[[CinemaScore]] |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= ''Event Horizon'' |access-date= 9 November 2018 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 20 December 2018 |url-status= dead }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film two out of four stars, commending its atmosphere and noting the opening portion as particularly well crafted; however, he felt it never managed to become the intense, thought-provoking experience it wanted to be.<ref name="Ebert">{{cite web | date = 15 August 1997 | author-link = Roger Ebert |first=Roger |last=Ebert |publisher=Ebert Digital LLC |access-date=16 July 2018 | work = Chicago Sun-Times | title = Event Horizon (1997) | url = https://rogerebert.com/reviews/event-horizon-1997 |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> ''[[The Washington Post]]'' critic [[Stephen Hunter]] called the film "pointlessly loud", with more devotion to style than scares and a satisfying explanation of its supernatural experiences.<ref name="wp">{{cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/eventhorizonhunt.htm |title='Event Horizon': Blood Simple|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|first=Stephen |last=Hunter|access-date=16 July 2018|date=15 August 1997}}</ref> In a lukewarm review, ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' magazine wrote: "That the film never fulfils its promise is down to its over reliance on horror vagaries in a precision-built sci-fi milieu, ultimately leaving too many unanswered queries. A sharper script and a more credible solution could have turned this impressive hokum into a force to be reckoned with".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.empireonline.com/reviews/review.asp?FID=13951|title=Empire's Event Horizon Movie Review|work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|access-date=16 July 2018|date=1 January 2000|first=Ian|last=Nathan}}</ref> Critics frequently noted the film's evident influences, often describing it as "''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'' in space"<ref name="gamesradar">{{cite web|url=http://www.gamesradar.com/event-horizon-review/|title=Event Horizon review|author=Total Film|date=22 August 1997|work=[[GamesRadar+]]|access-date=16 July 2018|publisher=Future Publishing}}</ref> or an ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'' knockoff.<ref name="NME"/><ref name=":13"/> Ebert and [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] particularly highlighted the debt to ''Solaris'', with Ebert arguing that the earlier film handled similar themes more effectively.<ref name="Ebert"/><ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/event-horizon/Film?oid=16580962 |title=''Event Horizon'' review |date=1997 |first=Jonathan |last=Rosenbaum |author-link=Jonathan Rosenbaum |newspaper=[[Chicago Reader]] |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190523145653/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/event-horizon/Film?oid=16580962 |archive-date=23 May 2019 }}</ref>

Several critics praised the [[ensemble cast]] even as they faulted the film as a whole. Reviewers in ''The Washington Post'', ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', and the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' highlighted the strong work of Fishburne, Neill, and Quinlan in particular, with Fishburne regarded as the strongest and Quinlan noted as a "close second" by ''The Washington Post'' reviewer.<ref name=":9"/><ref name="wp"/><ref>{{cite news |last=Turan |first=Kenneth |title=''Event Horizon'': Journey to Gory Ghost Ship |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-aug-15-ca-22529-story.html |work=Los Angeles Times |date=August 15, 1997}}</ref> [[James Berardinelli]] remarked that Neill was able to create more of a distinct character than most of the supporting players because he had the most material to work with, while deeming Jones's comic-relief turn as the most memorable performance.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://movie-reviews.colossus.net/movies/e/event.html |title=''Event Horizon'' |last=Berardinelli |first=James |author-link=James Berardinelli |website=ReelViews |date=1997 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20001116231800/http://movie-reviews.colossus.net/movies/e/event.html |archive-date=16 November 2000 |url-status=dead |access-date=28 August 2026}}</ref> ''Variety'', however, also countered the cast worked "far beyond the call of duty, but to little avail" and were sometimes forced to deliver "inadvertently hilarious" lines.<ref name=":9"/> Anderson later noted that critics at the time failed to appreciate the film's sense of humor; certain lines reviewers saw as unintentionally funny had in fact been done intentionally by the filmmakers.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:45:00–00:45:28}}

A few reviewers did, however, defend the film.<ref name="Lambie" /> ''[[Total Film]]'' rated it three out of five stars and praised its special effects and ''Alien''-esque atmosphere.<ref name="gamesradar"/> [[Owen Gleiberman]] gave it a B−, noting that despite its derivative elements, "it unleashes some of the most unsettling horror imagery in years".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://ew.com/article/1997/09/05/event-horizon-2/|title=Event Horizon|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |first=Owen|last=Gleiberman|date=5 September 1997|access-date=25 August 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170314053906/http://ew.com/article/1997/09/05/event-horizon-2/|archive-date=14 March 2017|author-link=Owen Gleiberman}}</ref> ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' found it never dull, crediting its avoidance of standard space-monster tropes and effective use of jump-scare sound design.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.timeout.com/london/film/event-horizon|title=Event Horizon|work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out London]]|author=NKE|date=10 September 2012 |access-date=25 August 2016}}</ref>

==Post-release==

===Performance analysis and aftermath===
Retrospective accounts have tied ''Event Horizon''{{'}}s weak theatrical run to its release date and marketing. As noted by ''[[IndieWire]]'', it arrived a month after the lighthearted science-fiction box-office hits ''[[Men in Black (1997 film)|Men in Black]]'' and ''[[Contact (1997 American film)|Contact]]'', and was jarringly darker by comparison.<ref name="Hemphill"/> Anderson later said the film was not a summer picture and should have opened in fall 1997 nearer Halloween.<ref name=":1" /> He called rushing post-production—at Paramount's request, to fill the slot left by the delayed ''Titanic''—his biggest mistake, and disliked the marketing campaign that sold the film to science-fiction and [[Trekkie|''Star Trek'' fans]] rather than as horror. He recalled an executive telling him, "We're the studio that makes ''Star Trek''!", as if the film were "besmirching" the franchise.<ref name=":2"/> Eisner likewise said Paramount "[didn't know] what they had".<ref name="OralHistory"/>

The failure left Anderson despondent. While preparing ''[[Soldier (1998 American film)|Soldier]]'' (1998), he screened the film for [[Kurt Russell]], who predicted that in twenty years it would be one he was glad he had made.<ref name=":2"/> Anderson has often repeated the remark and has stayed proud of the work, later describing it as having "the best looking stuff [he has] ever shot".{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:12:09}} On the audio commentary, he recalled that Fishburne later contacted him during the production of ''[[The Matrix]]'' (1999) to thank him, stating that the wire-work on ''Event Horizon'' had prepared him for the physical demands of that film and that he "would've died" on ''The Matrix'' without it.{{sfn|Anderson|Bolt|2006|loc=00:43:27}} Anderson also said Richard T. Jones' wife told him it was Jones' favourite film.{{sfn|Narayan|2006|loc=00:28:18}}

Isaacs remembered producer Gordon suggesting they leave an opening-night screening of the film for dinner, and later described "a funny bond" among the cast after the film found an audience.<ref name="OralHistory"/> Neill has called the theatrical version "half an hour too short" and "cut like it was on speed", saying he "always felt cheated" by it and would welcome a [[director's cut]]. He has also noted the film's later fame: "people seem to have seen it and it just sort of sears itself into their imagination."<ref name="George"/> Quinlan recalled that she defended the film during a conversation with ''Titanic'' director [[James Cameron]], who had argued that its failure was because audiences had wanted to see a "manifestation of a creature".<ref name="OralHistory"/>

Bolt, who continued to work with Anderson, later called the production a "baptism of fire" and "a great training ground for ''[[Resident Evil (film series)|Resident Evil]]''", adding that he had always thought the film was "damn good" and found its rediscovery "extremely gratifying".<ref>{{cite web |last=Wixson |first=Heather |date=19 May 2017 |title=Interview: Paul W.S. Anderson and Jeremy Bolt Reflect on the RESIDENT EVIL Film Franchise and EVENT HORIZON |url=https://dailydead.com/interview-paul-w-s-anderson-and-jeremy-bolt-reflect-on-the-resident-evil-film-franchise-and-event-horizon/ |website=Daily Dead |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref>

===Home media===
Although ''Event Horizon'' underperformed theatrically, it became a major success on [[home video]] and developed a [[cult following]] from it.<ref name=":12">{{cite web |last=Collura |first=Scott |date=24 December 2004 |title=''Event Horizon''{{'}}s Special Edition |url=http://www.nowplayingmag.com/content/view/485/2/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070105040059/http://www.nowplayingmag.com/content/view/485/2/ |archive-date=5 January 2007 |access-date=29 August 2026 |website=[[Now Playing Magazine]]}}</ref> This began when Paramount first released it on DVD in December 1998,<ref name="Hemphill"/> as a basic release that included the theatrical trailer as the only special feature.<ref name=":11">{{Cite web |last=Drucker |first=Michael S. |date=14 April 2006 |title=Double Dip Digest: Event Horizon: Special Collector's Edition |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2006/04/14/double-dip-digest-event-horizon-special-collectors-edition |access-date=29 August 2026 |website=[[IGN]] }}</ref> By 2004, strong DVD sales prompted the studio to offer Anderson funding for a director's cut that would reintegrate deleted scenes; Anderson noted that locating the materials after many years would be difficult; alternatives under discussion at the time included separate deleted scenes with commentary or a branching edition.<ref name=":12" /> The excised footage had not been carefully stored, and much of it was missing. The director's cut was therefore abandoned. In its place, Paramount released a special-edition two-DVD set featuring one deleted scene, two extended scenes, and a few shots of deleted material within the [[making-of]] featurette; the recovered footage was of [[videotape]] quality.<ref name="Q&A" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Cotter |first=Padraig |date=2017-10-19 |title=Exploring the Deleted Footage From Event Horizon |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/exploring-the-deleted-footage-from-event-horizon/ |access-date=2023-04-26 |website=[[Den of Geek]]}}</ref>

A two-disc Collector's Edition DVD was released in 2006, featuring an audio commentary by Anderson and Bolt, several making-of featurettes, deleted scenes and other supplements.<ref name=":11" /> Paramount had originally scheduled that DVD release for 2005; the commentary track was recorded in 2004 after an unsuccessful initial attempt by Anderson.<ref name=":12" /> On 23 March 2021, [[Shout! Factory]] issued a new Collector's Edition Blu-ray that included a 4K transfer from the original camera negative, the special features from the 2006 edition, and newly recorded interviews with the cast and crew.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Cranswick |first=Amie |date=2021-02-16 |title=Shout Factory's Event Horizon Collector's Edition Blu-ray details revealed |url=https://www.flickeringmyth.com/shout-factorys-event-horizon-collectors-edition-blu-ray-details-revealed/ |access-date=2026-08-29 }}</ref>

In an ''Event Horizon'' Q&A in 2011, Anderson stated that extra footage would never be made available, explaining that much of it was gone forever.<ref name="Q&A" /> The following year, he announced that producer Levin had located a [[VHS]] tape containing the original rough cut; Anderson planned to view the recovered footage for the first time since assembling the film after completing ''[[Resident Evil: Retribution]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://collider.com/paul-w-s-anderson-resident-evil-5-retribution-interview/ |title=Paul W.S. Anderson Talks RESIDENT EVIL 5 RETRIBUTION, EVENT HORIZON, DEATH RACE: INFERNO |work=[[Collider (website)|Collider]] |publisher=[[Complex (magazine)|Complex Media]] |first=Steve |last=Weintraub |date=17 July 2012 |access-date=4 August 2015 }}</ref> In a January 2017 interview he reiterated that a director's cut would never be released because the footage no longer existed. Regarding the VHS tape, he said neither he nor Levin had yet watched it, as Levin had moved to Spain, though he remained eager to do so at some point.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.sandiegoreader.com/news/2017/jan/25/screen-paul-ws-anderson-puts-end-resident-evil/?page=3 |title=Paul W.S. Anderson puts an end to Resident Evil |work=[[San Diego Reader]] |date=25 January 2017 |first=Scott |last=Marks |access-date=15 February 2017 }}</ref>

===Plagiarism accusations===
In later years, filmmaker [[William Malone (director)|William Malone]] accused ''Event Horizon'' of plagiarising from his unproduced script ''Dead Star'', which had circulated among major Hollywood studios for roughly a decade.<ref name=":15">{{cite magazine |last=Doyle |first=Michael |date=October 2014 |title=Master of Biomechanical Nightmares |magazine=[[Rue Morgue (magazine)|Rue Morgue]] |page=22 |issue=149 |issn=1481-1103}}</ref><ref name=":16">{{Cite web |last=Lambie |first=Ryan |date=29 April 2024 |title=''Dead Star'', and other HR Giger film projects we never got to see |url=https://filmstories.co.uk/news/dead-star-and-other-hr-giger-film-projects-we-never-got-to-see/ |access-date=30 August 2026 |website=Film Stories }}</ref><ref name="Podcast">{{Cite podcast |url=https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/dead-space/id1450737048?i=1000433314232 |title=Dead Space |work=Best Movies Never Made |last1=Scarlata|first=Stephen|last2=Miller|first2=Josh|author-link2=Josh Miller and Patrick Casey |last3=Malone |first3=William |author-link3=William Malone (director)  |date=25 March 2019 |access-date=1 September 2026 |publisher=[[Apple Podcasts]] }}</ref>{{pg|00:15:51}}{{pg|00:22:56}} ''Dead Star''{{'}}s script was set in the distant future and centered on a rescue crew investigating a long-missing spaceship; the crew discovers an alien machine capable of opening a portal to Hell, portrayed as a literal parallel dimension. The device unleashes nightmarish manifestations and ultimately allows Satan to appear and wreak havoc.<ref name="Podcast"/>{{pg|00:08:26}} Malone stated that it "got knocked off by five or six different movies—most notably ''Event Horizon''", adding that "[he has] to imagine that—because ''Event Horizon'' is so similar—major portions of it were lifted from [his] script."<ref name=":15" /> He did not pursue legal action, later explaining that "for [him], life is too short to sue people."<ref name="Podcast"/>{{pg|00:23:26}}

''Dead Star'' later served as the basis for ''[[Supernova (2000 film)|Supernova]]'' (2000), though by then Malone was no longer involved and the finished film differed substantially from his original concept.<ref name=":16" /> Because ''Event Horizon'' had already been released, ''Supernova'' underwent significant reworking to avoid appearing to imitate it and other recent science-fiction films.{{sfn|Laski|1998|p=20}} ''Film Stories'' remarked that [[H. R. Giger]]'s designs for ''Dead Star'' appeared to influence ''Event Horizon'' more strongly than ''Supernova''.<ref name=":16" /> In its review of ''Event Horizon'', ''The Digital Bits'' noted similarities suggesting the production had borrowed heavily from ''Dead Star'', though no one associated with the film has acknowledged this; the review observed that ''Event Horizon'' altered the nature of the portal and made the ship itself the primary source of evil.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bjork |first=Stephen |date=27 March 2023 |title=''Event Horizon'' (4K UHD Review) |url=https://thedigitalbits.com/reviews/item/event-horizon-paramount-2023-uhd |access-date=30 August 2026 |website=The Digital Bits}}</ref> ''Dead Star'' was pitched in the late 1980s as "''Hellraiser'' in space"; a description [[Screen Slate]] also gave ''Event Horizon''.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.screenslate.com/articles/supernova |title=''Supernova'' |last=LaLiberty |first=Justin |publisher=[[Screen Slate]] |date=19 July 2022 |access-date=1 September 2026 }}</ref>

===Financing litigation===
''Event Horizon'' was partly financed by Paramount Pictures through a 1996 insurance-backed slate deal with ICE Insured Finance LLC. and [[JPMorgan]].<ref name="CrossComplaint">{{cite web |title=Cross-Complaint of Paramount Pictures Corporation |url=http://deadline.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/paramount-content-partners-cross-complaint__130607175102.pdf |website=Deadline |date=June 6, 2013 |access-date=September 10, 2026 |format=PDF}}</ref><ref name="BombReport">{{cite web |title=Event Horizon (1997) |url=https://bombreport.com/yearly-breakdowns/1997-2/event-horizon/ |website=Bomb Report |date=15 August 2019 |access-date=10 September 2026 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190916164923/https://bombreport.com/yearly-breakdowns/1997-2/event-horizon/ |archive-date=16 September 2019 }}</ref>  The arrangement was one of several slate financing structures Paramount used in the mid-1990s to share production risk. The ICE-insured slate specifically covered the Paramount releases ''Event Horizon'', ''[[The Saint (1997 film)|The Saint]]'', ''[[Face/Off]]'', and ''[[Twilight (1998 film)|Twilight]]'' (1998). Under the structure, JPMorgan arranged loans to [[special-purpose entity|special-purpose entities]] that purchased profit-participation rights in the films. [[Insurance]] policies were intended to make the loans largely risk-free by covering any shortfalls between loan obligations and film revenues.<ref name="BombReport"/>

Insurers later claimed that the loans connected to the slate, including those related to ''Event Horizon'', had been fraudulently obtained. This left JPMorgan and related banks responsible for the outstanding amounts. Related insurance [[litigation]] arising from the structure was settled around 2004. Following that settlement, JPMorgan obtained control of the participation interests previously held by the special-purpose entities.<ref name="THR2013a">{{cite news |last=Gardner |first=Eriq |title=Paramount Alleges JPMorgan-Led Financial Conspiracy |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-alleges-jpmorgan-led-financial-564708/ |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=7 June 2013 |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref>

In 2010, Content Partners LLC, an [[asset management|asset-management]] firm that had acquired participation interests in the late-1990s Paramount slate of approximately 25 films (including ''Event Horizon''), sued the studio.<ref name="THR2013b">{{cite news |last=Gardner |first=Eriq |title=Paramount Settles Big Film Slate Lawsuit |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-settles-big-film-slate-665363/ |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=12 December 2013 |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref> Content Partners alleged that Paramount had underpaid tens of millions of dollars through improper cost allocations, inflated expenses, and inaccurate revenue reporting on the group of titles.<ref name="Variety2013a">{{cite news |last=Johnson |first=Ted |title=Paramount, Content Partners Escalate Film Slate Profit Dispute |url=https://variety.com/2013/biz/news/paramount-content-partners-escalate-profit-dispute-1200494125/ |work=Variety |date=7 June 2013 |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref> Paramount filed a [[cross-complaint]] accusing Content Partners and JPMorgan of engaging in a [[civil conspiracy]] to transfer rights in the films without the studio's required consent.<ref name="THR2013b"/> After several years of proceedings in the [[Los Angeles Superior Court]], the parties reached a settlement in December 2013.<ref name="Variety2013b">{{cite news |last=Johnson |first=Ted |title=Paramount Settles Film Slate Dispute With Content Partners |url=https://variety.com/2013/film/news/paramount-settles-film-slate-dispute-1200694636/ |work=Variety |date=12 December 2013 |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref> Both sides denied any wrongdoing, the terms of the agreement were not publicly disclosed, and the case was dismissed with prejudice.<ref name="Deadline2013">{{cite news |last=Patten |first=Dominic |title=Paramount Settles Financing Lawsuit |url=https://deadline.com/2013/12/paramount-settles-multi-million-wall-street-financing-lawsuit-651461/ |work=Deadline |date=12 December 2013 |access-date=10 September 2026}}</ref>

==Future==
===Potential films===
In December 2011, Anderson and Bolt stated that there have been ongoing discussions to explore additional movies that would expand the ''Event Horizon'' story, in the form of a prequel and a sequel. Potential stories to be developed include following the first crew aboard the ''Event Horizon'' and their mission that led to their disappearance for seven years, as well as a continuation film detailing the events that followed the rescue of Starck, Cooper, and Justin.<ref name="Q&A"/> By October 2020 however, after years of no development, Anderson stated that he had not returned to the property in any continuation because he did not want to take away from the experience of the original film.<ref name="Future_CB.com">{{cite web|url=https://comicbook.com/movies/news/event-horizon-sequel-tv-series-paul-w-s-anderson-reaction-monster-hunter/|work=ComicBook.com|title=Event Horizon Director Confirms He Has No Interest in Sequel or TV Series|last=Cavanaugh |first=Patrick|date=12 October 2020|access-date=1 February 2023}}</ref>

In August 2019, reports surfaced that a television series based on the film was being developed by [[Paramount Television]] and [[Amazon Studios]]. [[Adam Wingard]] will direct the series, in addition to serving as [[executive producer]] alongside Larry Gordon, Lloyd Levin, both of whom previously had held producer roles on the film, and Jeremy Platt.<ref name="TV_Variety">{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2019/tv/news/event-horizon-series-amazon-1203291883/|title='Event Horizon' Series in Development at Amazon (EXCLUSIVE) |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=5 August 2019 |first=Joe |last=Otterson}}</ref><ref name="Surrey"/> In March 2024, Wingard provided a brief update stating, "It's definitely in the works. I've just been in [[Godzilla (Monsterverse)|Godzilla]] land for so long. I wouldn't say that there's definitive traction in terms of it moving forward, but we have a fucking amazing script. Once this movie's over, it's just about refocusing my attention towards getting that set up."<ref name="Inverse">{{cite web |url=https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/event-horizon-amazon-show-adam-wingard-update|title='Event Horizon' Adaptation Gets a Promising Update From Director Adam Wingard |work=[[Inverse (website)|Inverse]] |date=19 March 2024 |first=Jake|last=Kleinman}}</ref> As of May 2025, the series was still believed to be actively in development.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Spry |first=Jeff |date=13 May 2025 |title=Sci-fi cult classic ''Event Horizon'' scares up a prequel comic series later this year and we can't wait |url=https://www.space.com/entertainment/sci-fi-cult-classic-event-horizon-scares-up-a-prequel-comic-series-later-this-year-and-we-cant-wait |access-date=23 August 2026 |website=[[Space.com]] }}</ref>
