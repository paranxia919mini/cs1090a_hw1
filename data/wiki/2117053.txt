'''''Weekend''''' ({{langx|fr|Week-end}}) is a 1967 [[Postmodernist film|postmodern]] [[black comedy film]]<ref name="metalasylum.com">{{cite web |url=http://www.metalasylum.com/ragingbull/movies/weekend.html |title=Week End |website=Raging Bull Movie Reviews|first=Mike|last=Lorefice|date=15 March 2004|access-date=3 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040504152700/http://www.metalasylum.com/ragingbull/movies/weekend.html|archive-date=4 May 2004}}</ref><ref name="movie-gazette.com">{{cite web |url=http://www.movie-gazette.com/1193/week-end |title=Week End (1967) |access-date=17 September 2010|website=Movie Gazette|date=2 March 2005}}</ref> written and directed by [[Jean-Luc Godard]], based on [[Julio Cortázar]]'s short story "La autopista del Sur".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.unicen.edu.ar/content/week-end-de-godard-en-el-ciclo-de-cine-sobre-cort%C3%A1zar |title=Week End, de Godard, en el ciclo de cine sobre Cortázar |trans-title=Weekend, by Godard, in the film cycle on Cortázar |language=es |publisher=[[National University of Central Buenos Aires|UNICEN]] |date=9 September 2014 |access-date=25 August 2020}}</ref> It stars mainstream French TV stars [[Mireille Darc]] and [[Jean Yanne]]. [[Jean-Pierre Léaud]], star of numerous [[French New Wave]] films, including [[François Truffaut]]'s ''[[The 400 Blows]]'' (1959) and Godard's earlier ''[[Masculin Féminin]]'' (1966), appeared in two roles. [[Raoul Coutard]] served as [[cinematographer]] and [[Suzanne Schiffman]] as [[script supervisor]].

==Plot==
Roland and Corinne Durand are a [[bourgeois]] couple. Each has a secret lover and conspires to murder the other. They drive to Corinne's parents' home in the country to secure her inheritance from her dying father, resolving to resort to murder if necessary. The trip becomes a chaotic journey through a French countryside populated by bizarre characters and punctuated by violent car accidents. After their own [[Facel-Vega]] is destroyed in a collision, they wander through a series of vignettes involving [[class struggle]] and figures from literature and history, such as [[Louis Antoine de Saint-Just]] and [[Emily Brontë]].

In a [[metafictional]] touch, some scenes show the characters in the film being [[self-aware]] such as a driver asking Roland after being flagged down, "Are you in a film or reality?", the film's real actors from the Italian co-production being mentioned during Corinne and Roland's search for a car to Oinville (to which they never specify further as to which Oinville they are referring to), and various [[intertitles]] which are a defining feature to Godard's films.

When Corinne and Roland eventually arrive at her parents' place, they discover that her father has died and her mother refuses to give them a share of the spoils. They kill her and hit the road again, only to fall into the hands of a group of [[hippie]] revolutionaries (calling themselves the [[Seine-et-Oise|Seine and Oise]] Liberation Front) that support themselves through theft and [[Human cannibalism|cannibalism]]. Killed during an escape attempt, Roland is chopped up and cooked.

== Themes and style ==
''Weekend'' has been compared to ''[[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland|Alice in Wonderland]]'', the ''[[James Bond]]'' series, and the works of the [[Marquis de Sade]].<ref name="Hoberman">{{cite news|url=https://www.villagevoice.com/weekend-when-godard-burned-the-movie-house-down/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230907013140/https://www.villagevoice.com/weekend-when-godard-burned-the-movie-house-down/|url-status=dead|archive-date=September 7, 2023|title=Weekend: When Godard Burned the Movie House Down|last=Hoberman|first=J.|author-link=J. Hoberman|newspaper=The Village Voice|date=5 October 2011|access-date=6 September 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1034126-weekend/reviews/|title=Weekend Reviews|website=Rotten Tomatoes|access-date=3 July 2018}}</ref> Tim Brayton described it as a "film that reads itself, tells the viewer what that reading should be, and at the same time tells the viewer that this reading is inaccurate and should be ignored."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.alternateending.com/2008/01/tspdt-199-week-end.html|title=Week End (1967)|last=Brayton|first=Tim|website=Alternate Ending|date=21 January 2008|access-date=15 February 2017}}</ref> In one of the early scenes, Corinne tells her lover about a sexual experience she had. Part of the story she tells is based on the [[Georges Bataille]] novel ''[[Story of the Eye]]'' (''Histoire de l'œil'').<ref name="Hoberman"/>

== Inspiration ==
According to a letter from Argentine writer [[Julio Cortázar]] to his translator [[Suzanne Jill Levine]], the indirect inspiration for the film was Cortázar's short story "La autopista del Sur" ("The Southern Thruway"). Cortázar explained that while a British producer was considering filming his story, a third party had presented the idea to Godard, who was unaware of its true source.{{Citation needed|date=September 2022}}

==Reception==
On Rotten Tomatoes, the film holds an approval rating of 93%, based on 28 reviews, with an average rating of 8.7/10. The website's critics consensus reads "Jean-Luc Godard fixes his considerable ire against French society and the broader human condition in the morbidly funny ''Weekend'', an abstract road trip to damnation that finds the enfant terrible in peak form."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1034126-weekend|title=Weekend|website=Rotten Tomatoes|access-date=6 September 2023}}</ref>

==Animal cruelty==
The film shows a range of gruesome treatments of animals, starting with a motif of a skinned rabbit, which gives way to full-blooded slaughter of animals in the final scene of the film. The animals were actually slaughtered, graphically, and it has been argued that the film fails to condemn the behaviour, even whilst exposing its horrors, through suggesting that it is permissible to kill animals, as animalism, which is also found in bourgeoise society, is subhuman and worthy of sacrifice.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://zooscope.group.shef.ac.uk/weekend-dir-jean-luc-godard-athos-films-1967/|title=Weekend. Dir. Jean-Luc Godard. Athos Films. 1967|website=Zooscope}}</ref>
