'''''Spider-Man: Far From Home''''' is a 2019 American [[superhero film]] based on the [[Marvel Comics]] character [[Spider-Man]]. Produced by [[Columbia Pictures]], [[Marvel Studios]], and [[Pascal Pictures]], and distributed by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]], it is the sequel to ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017) and [[List of Marvel Cinematic Universe films|the 23rd film]] in the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] (MCU). The film was directed by [[Jon Watts]], written by [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]] and [[Erik Sommers]], and stars [[Tom Holland]] as [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]] alongside [[Samuel L. Jackson]], [[Zendaya]], [[Cobie Smulders]], [[Jon Favreau]], [[J. B. Smoove]], [[Jacob Batalon]], [[Martin Starr]], [[Tony Revolori]], [[Marisa Tomei]], and [[Jake Gyllenhaal]]. In the film, Peter is recruited by [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]] (Jackson) and [[Mysterio (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Mysterio]] (Gyllenhaal) to help stop the [[Elementals (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Elementals]] while he is on a school trip to Europe.

Discussions for a sequel to ''Spider-Man: Homecoming'' began by October 2016, and the project was confirmed later that year. Holland, Watts, and the writers were all set to return by the end of 2017. In 2018, Jackson and Gyllenhaal joined the cast as Fury and Mysterio, respectively. Holland revealed the sequel's title ahead of filming, which began that July and took place in England, the Czech Republic, Italy, and the [[New York metropolitan area]]. Production [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] in October 2018. The marketing campaign is one of the most expensive for a film ever and attempted to avoid revealing spoilers for ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' prior to its April 2019 release.

''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' premiered at the [[TCL Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on June 26, 2019, and was theatrically released in the United States on July 2, as the final film in [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three|Phase Three]] of the MCU. The film received positive reviews with praise for its humor, action sequences, visuals, and the performances of Holland and Gyllenhaal. It grossed $1.133 billion worldwide, making it the first [[Spider-Man in film#Reception|Spider-Man film]] to pass the billion-dollar mark, the [[2019 in film#Highest-grossing films|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2019]], and became Sony Pictures' highest-grossing film and the [[List of highest-grossing films#Highest-grossing films|24th<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->-highest-grossing film of all time]]. A sequel, ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'', was released in December 2021.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. Word Count as of March 6, 2025: 636 -->
In [[Ixtenco]], Mexico, [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]] and [[Maria Hill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Maria Hill]] investigate an unnatural storm and encounter the Earth [[Elementals (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Elemental]]. [[Mysterio (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quentin Beck]], a super-powered individual, arrives and defeats the creature. Fury and Hill subsequently recruit him. In New York City, the [[Midtown School of Science and Technology]] completes its year, which was restarted to accommodate the students who disintegrated five years earlier due to [[Thanos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thanos]]'s actions; they had [[The Blip|reappeared un-aged]], thanks to the actions of the [[Avengers (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Avengers]].{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018) and ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' (2019)}} The school organizes a two-week summer [[field trip]] to Europe, where [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker]]—who is still mourning the death of his mentor [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark]]—plans to reveal to classmate [[MJ (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|MJ]]<!--Discuss on talk before making any changes to the character's name--> his attraction to her. [[Happy Hogan (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Happy Hogan]] informs Peter that Fury intends to contact him, but Peter ignores Fury's phone call.

Peter and his classmates travel to [[Venice]], Italy, where the Water Elemental attacks. While Peter helps protect his classmates, Beck arrives and defeats the creature. Fury meets with Peter and gives him [[Tony Stark's glasses|Stark's eyeglasses]]. The glasses enable him to communicate with and take command of the [[artificial intelligence]] [[E.D.I.T.H.]], which has access to [[Stark Industries (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Stark Industries]]' databases and commands a large [[Space weapon|orbital weapons supply]]. Beck claims to have entered from an alternate reality within the [[Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|multiverse]], where the four Elementals killed his family and destroyed his civilization. With only the Fire Elemental left to destroy, Beck predicts it will attack [[Prague]], Czech Republic. Peter declines Fury's invitation to join the fight and returns to his class trip, but Fury secretly changes the class's itinerary to divert the students to Prague.

Peter is forced to help Beck fight the Fire Elemental to again protect his friends. Beck manages to destroy the creature with Peter's help. Fury and Hill invite Peter and Beck to [[Berlin]], Germany to discuss forming a new superhero team, but Peter decides that Beck should go alone and transfers control of E.D.I.T.H. to him. Once Peter leaves, Beck celebrates alongside ex-Stark Industries employees, with whom he had been working to masquerade as a superhero. Beck, who was fired from his position as Stark's holographic-illusions specialist due to his unstable nature, uses [[Augmented reality|advanced projectors]] to simulate his powers and the Elementals. He plans to use E.D.I.T.H.'s orbital weaponized drones to increase the scale of his illusions and fraudulently establish himself as an Avenger-level hero.

After MJ tells Peter she knows he is Spider-Man, they discover that a piece of debris she retrieved during the battle with the Fire Elemental is a projector and the pair realize Beck's deception. Peter travels to Berlin, to warn Fury, but Beck tricks him with an elaborate illusion, forcing him to reveal the names of his friends who know about Beck's plan, and causing Peter to get hit by a train. Beck is unaware that Peter, though injured, has survived. In the Netherlands, Peter contacts Hogan, who flies him to London, England, where his classmates are. Beck uses E.D.I.T.H. to orchestrate a fusion of all the Elementals. He intends to kill Peter's friends during the staged attack, as Beck is concerned that they will expose him with their knowledge. Peter disrupts the illusions and an enraged Beck attacks him with drones. Peter regains control of E.D.I.T.H. and defeats Beck, who is hit by a misfired gunshot from one of the drones. Before he dies, Beck tells his associate [[William Ginter Riva]] to retrieve data from the drones. After returning to New York City, Peter begins a relationship with MJ.

In a [[mid-credits scene]], [[J. Jonah Jameson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|J. Jonah Jameson]] of ''[[TheDailyBugle.net]]'' broadcasts doctored footage of the London incident in which Beck frames Spider-Man for the drone attack and his death before exposing Spider-Man's [[secret identity]] to the world, much to Peter's shock. In a post-credits scene, Fury and Hill are revealed to be the [[Skrulls (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Skrulls]] [[Talos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Talos]] and [[Soren (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Soren]] in disguise, under orders from the real Fury while he is away in space commanding a group of Skrulls.

== Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Tom Holland & Jon Watts (28620388656).jpg|thumb|Actor Tom Holland (L) and director Jon Watts (R) were signed to return for the sequel early in development.]]
In June 2016, [[Sony Pictures]] chairman [[Tom Rothman]] stated that Sony and [[Marvel Studios]] were committed to making future ''Spider-Man'' films after ''[[Spider-Man: Homecoming]]'' (2017).<ref name="RothmanJune2016" /> The following month, Marvel Studios president [[Kevin Feige]] said if additional films were made, the company had an early idea to follow the model of the [[Harry Potter (film series)|''Harry Potter'' film series]], and have the plot of each film cover a new school year,<ref name="FeigeFuture" /> with a second film intended to take place during [[Peter Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Peter Parker / Spider-Man]]'s junior year of high school.<ref name="/FilmSMHSetVisit" /> By October, discussions had begun for a second film, including which villain would be featured, according to Spider-Man actor [[Tom Holland]], who was signed for two more ''Spider-Man'' films after ''Homecoming''.<ref name="HollandSequelOct2016" /><ref name="HollandContract" /> In December, after the successful release of the first ''Homecoming'' trailer, Sony slated a sequel to the film for July 5, 2019.<ref name="DeadlineSequel" /> Sony's insistence that the sequel be released in 2019 complicated Marvel's preference for secrecy regarding their plans for Spider-Man in this film and their other MCU films, since the character would die at the end of ''[[Avengers: Infinity War]]'' (2018), and would not be resurrected before ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'' in April 2019 by which time marketing for this film would have already begun.<ref name="VanityFairDec2018" />

After being able to include [[Robert Downey Jr.]]'s [[Tony Stark (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Tony Stark / Iron Man]] for ''Homecoming'', Sony was revealed in June 2017 to have the use of another Marvel Studios-controlled character for the sequel.<ref name="SonyMarvelTHRJun2017" /> Feige stated that Marvel and Sony were "just starting to solidify our plans" for the film, and felt Spider-Man's appearances in ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame'' would "launch him off into a very new cinematic universe at that point", similar to how ''[[Captain America: Civil War]]'' (2016) "informed everything in ''Homecoming''".<ref name="FeigeJun2017" /> Marvel and Sony both wanted ''Homecoming'' director [[Jon Watts]] returning for the sequel, with Feige saying "that's the intention for sure", and Watts said he was signed for another Spider-Man film.<ref name="ColliderWattsJun2017" /> Feige said the sequel would have a subtitle like ''Homecoming'', and would not feature "2" in the title. He added that filming was expected to begin in April or May 2018.<ref name="ColliderFilmDates" /> As with ''Homecoming'', Feige said the film's villain would be one that had not yet [[List of Spider-Man enemies in other media#Film|been seen in film]].<ref name="NewVillain" /> By July, Watts was in negotiations with the studios regarding his return for the sequel,<ref name="WattsInTalks" /> and [[Marisa Tomei]] expressed interest in returning as [[May Parker (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Aunt May]] from the previous films.<ref name="TomeiVarietyJul2017" /> That same month, Watts expressed interest in including [[Morbius]], [[Blade (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Blade]], or the [[Chameleon (character)|Chameleon]] in the sequel.<ref name="WattsCharacterPossibilities" />

The film is set eight months after the conclusion of ''Endgame''.<ref name="Timeline" /> Watts felt dealing with many of the unanswered questions from ''Endgame'' posed "a fun creative challenge" for the ''Far From Home'' team. They looked to "make a movie that's in that world and deals with those stories but is also still [a] fun Spider-Man movie". For example, Ned, MJ, and Flash all turned to dust during the events of ''Infinity War'' along with Peter, while some of his classmates did not and are now five years older due to the events of ''Endgame''. Watts compared this dynamic to the film ''[[Flight of the Navigator]]'' (1986), calling the situation "really weird{{nbsp}}... but{{nbsp}}... also something you can have a lot of fun with".<ref name="EWWatts2ndTrailer" />

=== Pre-production ===
At the end of August 2017, as the film was entering [[pre-production]],<ref name="ColliderFilmDates" /> [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]] and [[Erik Sommers]] were in final negotiations to return from ''Homecoming'' to write the sequel.<ref name="McKennaSommers" /> In early October, [[Jacob Batalon]] confirmed he would reprise his role of [[Ned Leeds (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Ned Leeds]] in the sequel.<ref name="BatalonReturn" /><ref name="FullNames" /> Feige confirmed that Watts was returning to direct the sequel in December.<ref name="WattsReturn" /> By February 2018, [[Zendaya]] was slated to return for the film, reprising her role as [[MJ (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Michelle "MJ" Jones-Watson]].<ref name="ZendayaReturn" /><ref name="FullNames" /> Location scouting for the film began at the end of the month, as did the pre-visualisation process for the creative team to begin planning action sequences and visual effects for the film.<ref name="LloydGoCreative" /> By late April, Feige stated that filming was scheduled to begin in early July 2018, and would primarily occur in London. This was a change from the first film, which was mostly produced in Atlanta. Feige explained that one of the reasons behind this change in location was because a majority of the sequel would be spent around the world, outside New York City.<ref name="FilmingStart" />

A month later, [[Jake Gyllenhaal]] entered negotiations to play [[Quentin Beck (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Quentin Beck / Mysterio]], while Tomei and [[Michael Keaton]] were confirmed to reprise their respective roles of May and [[Adrian Toomes (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Adrian Toomes / Vulture]]; Gyllenhaal's casting was confirmed a month later,<ref name="GyllenhaalConfirmed" /> but Watts later stated that Keaton and [[Laura Harrier]], who played Toomes' daughter [[Liz (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Liz]] in ''Homecoming'', would not be appearing in the sequel.<ref name="FandangoWatts2ndTrailer" /> Also in May, McKenna and Sommers were also confirmed as the film's screenwriters.<ref name="GyllenhaalTalksVariety" /><ref name="GyllenhaalTalksTHR" /> They began working on the film from a set of notes that Marvel had put together based on different influences and story ideas. This included the directive that the film would be releasing shortly after ''Endgame'' and would need to deal with the death of Stark due to the relationship between that character and Spider-Man that previous films had established. The film also needed to deal with the aftereffects of "[[The Blip]]", the five-year period between ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame'' where half of all life disappeared. Mysterio was chosen to be the film's villain because he is one of Spider-Man's iconic villains that had yet to be featured in a film, and because his history of deception in the comics lent itself to a character who could take advantage of a Spider-Man that is mourning the death of Stark. This also allowed the writers to explore relevant modern themes such as [[fake news]]. Mysterio's plan went through several iterations, including an early suggestion that he could be an alien [[Skrulls (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Skrull]] in disguise.<ref name="WritersColliderJul2019" />

The film reuses two of Spider-Man's costumes from the previous films: his main costume from ''Civil War'' and ''Homecoming'', and the [[Iron Spider (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Iron Spider]] suit from ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame''. It also introduces two new costumes: a black "stealth" costume given to Peter by [[Nick Fury (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Nick Fury]], and a new, upgraded Spider-Man suit that Peter designs for himself at the end of the film.<ref name="CostumesBreakdown" /> Marvel Studios' head of visual development Ryan Meinerding explained that Watts had wanted to include a suit inspired by the [[Spider-Man Noir]] version of the character, which led to the design of the more tactical stealth suit. He added that the suit represents Peter experimenting with being a new kind of superhero. Other tactical costumes from the comic books were looked at when developing this one, but Meinerding felt they looked less practical than the more straightforward Noir inspiration. The costume includes tactical goggles that can be flipped up. For Peter's new self-designed costume, Meinerding originally designed it with the idea that it would be made from Peter's webbing since that is the strongest material he has access to.<ref name="MeinerdingCostumes" /> Practical versions of the costumes were created by Ironhead Studio, who previously worked on [[The Amazing Spider-Man (film series)|''The Amazing Spider-Man'' films]] (2012–2014). For ''Far From Home'', Ironhead developed a skull cap for the costumes that has built-in fans to prevent the goggles from steaming up. They also developed a magnetic bellows system for connecting the goggles to the mask, so they could be easily removed but not fall off during action sequences.<ref name="Ironhead" />

At the end of June 2018, Holland revealed the film's title to be ''Spider-Man: Far From Home''.<ref name="FarFromHome" /> Feige explained that they decided to have Holland reveal the title because they thought it was likely that it would leak after filming began anyway.<ref name="FeigeExplainsLeak" /> He compared the title to ''Homecoming'' in that it is "full of alternate meaning" while continuing the use of "Home",<ref name="FeigeExplainsTitle" /> and revealed the film's premise to be Peter and his friends going to Europe on a summer [[field trip]], away from their home of New York.<ref name="FeigeVacation" /> The creative team had discussions about featuring [[New Asgard]] as one of the destinations for Peter's field trip in the film.<ref name="NewAsgard">{{Cite web |last=Bacon |first=Thomas |date=June 28, 2019 |title=Spider-Man: Far From Home European Trip Almost Visited New Asgard |url=https://screenrant.com/spider-man-far-home-new-asgard-european-trip/ |access-date=September 1, 2024 |website=[[Screen Rant]] |archive-date=January 14, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250114061854/https://screenrant.com/spider-man-far-home-new-asgard-european-trip/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Filming ===

Lloyd served as [[director of photography]], after previously working with Watts on ''[[Cop Car (film)|Cop Car]]'' (2015). Watts had wanted to work with Lloyd on ''Homecoming'', but the cinematographer was working on ''[[Power Rangers (film)|Power Rangers]]'' (2017) at the time.<ref name="LloydPond5" /> Filming moved to London,<ref name="FilmingStart" /> with locations including [[East London]],<ref name="EastLondon" /> and at [[London Stansted Airport]].<ref name="UnitedPartnership" /> Studio work occurred at [[Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden]] near [[Watford]], England, where a [[Venice]] set was created on one of the studio's [[backlot]]s.<ref name="ColliderSetVisit" /><ref name="io9SetVisit" /> Shortly after filming began, set photos revealed [[Hemky Madera]] would return as bodega owner Mr. Delmar,<ref name="Madera" /> while [[J.B. Smoove]] and [[Numan Acar]] joined the cast.<ref name="SmooveCast" /><ref name="Acar" /> By early August, [[Samuel L. Jackson]] and [[Cobie Smulders]] were confirmed to be appearing in the film, reprising their respective roles of Nick Fury and [[Maria Hill (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Maria Hill]] from previous MCU films,<ref name="JacksonSmuldersConfirmed" /> and [[Remy Hii]] joined the cast later in the month.<ref name="Hii" /> Filming took place in [[Prague]] and [[Liberec]] in the Czech Republic in September,<ref name="LiberecShooting" /><ref name="VeniceShooting" /> and moved to Venice by the end of the month.<ref name="VeniceShooting" /> Filming moved to New York City and [[Newark, New Jersey]] in October, where it used the working title ''Bosco''.<ref name="NYCShooting" /><ref name="NYCOnLocationShooting" /> Locations included areas around [[Madison Square Garden]] and [[Pennsylvania Station (New York City)|Penn Station]],<ref name="NYCOnLocationShooting" /> and [[Newark Liberty International Airport]].<ref name="UnitedPartnership" /> An uncredited actor was originally cast as Flash Thompson's father for the airport scene, but [[Tony Revolori]] had [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] to producer Chris Buongiorno about having Flash's butler Gerald show up at the airport instead as a "funnier" option, so the crew shot the scene twice, one version with the actor playing Flash's father and the other one with the butler respectively, with the latter being chosen.<ref name="GeraldsOrigins" /> Filming [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on October 16, 2018.<ref name="FilmingWrapped" />

Lloyd explained that the creative team had approached the film as a road-trip story, and so he wanted to maintain the distinct look and feel of each location visited. This meant giving each stop on the school trip a distinct lighting scheme and color palette. For example, Lloyd visited Venice three times during location scouting to discuss how the scenes there would be filmed, and learned that the city has a "bright, pastel feel, where the light comes through and hits a building. It bathes people in this warm, sandy light". Prague, on other hand, has a "more warm and cool mix, and the beauty of that sort of Baroque, eastern-European architecture." The production took advantage of lighting already on the buildings in Prague, which are often lit for texture in real life. Lloyd felt comfortable working within the aesthetic of the MCU after acting as director of photography on the [[Daredevil season 1|first season]] of the [[Marvel Television]] series ''[[Daredevil (TV series)|Daredevil]]'' (2015–2018) and the miniseries ''[[The Defenders (miniseries)|The Defenders]]'' (2017), as well as working on additional photography for the films ''[[Thor: Ragnarok]]'' (2017) and ''[[Captain Marvel (film)|Captain Marvel]]'' (2019).<ref name="LloydPond5" /> Lloyd originally began planning to use the [[Arri Alexa#Alexa 65|Alexa 65]] cameras that Marvel usually films with, but Sony asked that they focus on optimizing the footage for their 2K releases, so the production switched to [[Alexa Mini]] cameras. This ended up being a lot easier for the camera department to use on location, and for combining live-action shooting with visual effects elements.<ref name="LloydGoCreative" />

name="StarrFarFromHome" /> Following up Revolori's pitch for Flash's family life, the filmmakers added during the E.D.I.T.H. scene the text of Flash texting his parents.<ref name="GeraldsOrigins" /> In April 2019, Sony moved the film's release date up to July 2, 2019.<ref name="NewReleaseDate" /> While promoting ''Endgame'' at a fan event in Shanghai, Feige said ''Far From Home'' would be the final film of the MCU's [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Three|Phase Three]], rather than the start of [[Marvel Cinematic Universe: Phase Four|Phase Four]] as some had believed.<ref name="FFHPhase3" /> Also in April, Jackson returned to do reshoots for the film.<ref name="Reshoots" />

Discussing the film's mid-credits scene, Watts explained that they approached [[J.K. Simmons]] "as late as possible" before the film's release to ask him to reprise his role as [[J. Jonah Jameson (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|J. Jonah Jameson]] from [[Sam Raimi]]'s [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' film trilogy]] (2002–2007), hoping to keep the cameo a surprise for fans.<ref name="SimmonsCollider" /> Watts acknowledged that there was a chance Simmons would not want to reprise the role, but stated that he never considered another actor, saying, "It's gotta be him. Like, if it wasn't him, it wasn't worth doing."<ref name="WattsPostCredits" /> After being pitched the idea for the cameo, Simmons called Raimi and received the director's blessing to return to the role for the new film.<ref name="SimmonsRaimi" /> The MCU version of the character is brand new, not the Raimi version "from another dimension or multiverse".<ref name="FeigeSimmons" /> Simmons' footage was filmed in a Disney conference room in front of a green screen.<ref name="SimmonsCollider" /> Watts noted that Simmons' performance was over-the-top in Raimi's films, but now that same performance has real-world comparisons,<ref name="WattsPostCredits" /> such as [[Alex Jones]]. Watts described ''Far From Home'' as a "con man movie" with "so many layers of deception", and for the post-credits scene he felt that ending the film with "one last twist" was on theme. He felt that he owed the fans a more substantial post-credits scene than the jokey one at the end of ''Homecoming''. The ''Far From Home'' scene depicts Fury on vacation, in a simulation, both of which are also themes in the film, and reveals that the Fury and Hill seen throughout the film are actually the Skrulls [[Talos (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Talos]] and [[Soren (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Soren]] in disguise, with [[Ben Mendelsohn]] and [[Sharon Blynn]] reprising their roles from ''Captain Marvel'', respectively.<ref name="FFHPostCredits" /> Watts felt this twist made sense after Fury's interactions with the Skrulls in ''Captain Marvel''.<ref name="WattsPostCredits" /> Footage of Jackson and Smulders originally intended for the film's opening scene was used in the reveal. Smulders learned about the twist from Feige shortly before the film's release.<ref name="SmuldersTwist" /> Post-production for ''Far From Home'' was completed in June 2019.<ref name="PostWrapped" />

==== Visual effects====
{{external media|float=right|video1=[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hEhdhicsmZI Marvel Studios' Spider-Man: Far From Home – End Credits Main on End Title Sequence] presents the film's main-on-end title sequence, [[YouTube]] video from [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]]'s channel}}
The film's visual effects were provided by [[Framestore]], [[Industrial Light & Magic]], [[Image Engine]], [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], [[Luma Pictures]], [[Rising Sun Pictures]], [[Scanline VFX]], [[Territory Studio]], and [[Method Studios]].<ref name="VFX" /> [[Janek Sirrs]] served as visual effects supervisor on the film, and explained that following the release of the animated film ''[[Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse]]'' (2018) the effects team looked for ways to use similarly inventive visuals in ''Far From Home''. They ultimately did not use added effects to represent Spider-Man's Spider-sense because these did not fit within the live-action world or were too similar to previous effects, such as [[Matt Murdock (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Daredevil]]'s sonar vision. They did try to use Spider-Man's actions and the use of his abilities to show his growth since ''Homecoming'', but to also show his "reach as always exceeding his grasp as he refines his superhero skills".<ref name="SirrsVFXIW" /> The film's main-on-end title sequence was designed by [[Perception (visual effects company)|Perception]].<ref name="Perception" />

Method Studios added Spider-Man to a Venice speedboat scene, using their Spider-Man suit asset from ''Homecoming''.<ref name="VFXAB" /> Image Engine created the Stark jet, drone, and satellite. The jet work included the creation of a new Spider-Man suit,<ref name="VFXAB" /> for which the vendor had to design the interior of the suit based on a scan of a practical costume they were given. They also made a Netherlands tulip field for a scene that was filmed in a grass field in the UK. It required around two million digital tulips.<ref name="AWNImageEngine" /> Rising Sun Pictures created the holograms used by Mysterio when he first explains the Elementals to Spider-Man, which include simulations of black-holes and elements researched from real-world history and mythology. The company aimed for the holograms to have a similar quality to the ones that they had created for ''Captain Marvel''.<ref name="VFXAB" /> Mysterio was designed by Ryan Meinerding, Marvel Studios' head of visual development, to look like a combination of different Marvel heroes such as [[Thor (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Thor]] and [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Doctor Strange]]. His fishbowl helmet is retained from the comics. Sirrs described the character as a "larger-than-life showman at heart" and almost a superhero version of [[Liberace]].<ref name="SirrsVFXIW" /> The digital model for Mysterio was created by Scanline.<ref name="SPIVFX" />

The Elementals consisted of animated figures, often driven by slowed-down motion-capture performances, with environmental effects layered on top. Scanline created Earth and Water, Luma created Fire, and Imageworks created the final amalgamation of the four creatures,<ref name="SirrsVFXIW" /> referred to as the Super Uber Elemental (SUE).<ref name="SPIVFX" /> For the Fire Elemental, Luma created different looks and actions for the character to show Watts and Sirrs potential designs and movements for the character. This included a progression where the creature goes from being upright and humanoid to bigger and more hunched over as it consumes metal throughout the sequence. Watts responded positively to movements that looked more creature-like.<ref name="LumaCB" /> The final battle was created by Imageworks, and consists of over 300 visual effects shots. In addition to creating the SUE—which required extensive effect simulations for the different combined elements such as "water tentacles", rocks, lava, and lightning—the company had to create a digital model of the [[Tower Bridge]] for the fight to take place around. The company attempted to fully render only small sections of the bridge where needed, but ultimately required around 80% of the bridge to be fully detailed due to how much of it is seen throughout the fight. They made use of Image Engine's Stark jet model and Scanline's Mysterio asset for the fight, and created the drone models and new Spider-Man suit that were used by the other vendors. They started with a single drone design that featured missiles, cannons, and Gatling guns, but this became repetitive during the battle and so flamethrower and sonic blaster drones were added. Additionally, the sky and environment needed to be digitally replaced in most of the sequence.<ref name="SPIVFX" />

Framestore handled the "Illusion Battle" sequence in which Mysterio traps Spider-Man within a series of elaborate fear-based illusions. Based on early prototypes of the sequence from Framestore, Marvel decided to extend it to around 150 shots. It was heavily pre-visualized to define which background plates and motion-capture performances would be required; Framestore visual effects supervisor Alexis Wajsbrot compared the motion-capture process for the sequence to [[automated dialogue replacement]], where each short section of the performance was captured individually. The company used existing digital assets from other vendors, including for the different Spider-Man suits, combined with new models such as those for statues of fallen heroes or a "Zombie Iron Man".<ref name="FramestoreIllusions" /><ref name="VFXAB" /> The effect of green smoke that is throughout the sequence was created using [[Houdini (software)|Houdini]]. Framestore took inspiration from the title sequences of the [[List of James Bond films|''James Bond'' films]], especially that of ''[[Spectre (2015 film)|Spectre]]'' (2015) for the graveyard portion of the battle.<ref name="FramestoreIllusions" /> For the transition between the real world and the illusions, Framestore had to match a similar transition from the B.A.R.F. scene in ''Civil War'' which Wajsbrot described as a time-consuming process for each environment in which it was required.<ref name="FramestoreIllusions" /><ref name="VFXAB" /> Sirrs compared the sequence to an elaborate Broadway production with an unlimited budget, and named several other visual influences including the ''[[Looney Tunes]]'' short ''[[Duck Amuck]]'' (1953), ''[[Paprika (2006 film)|Paprika]]'' (2006), ''[[A Nightmare on Elm Street]]'' (1984), and the "[[Pink Elephants on Parade]]" scene from ''[[Dumbo]]'' (1941).<ref name="SirrsVFXIW" />

== Marketing ==
Due to Peter dying at the end of ''Infinity War'' and not being resurrected until ''Endgame'',<ref name="MarketingMay2018" /><ref name="EndgameTitleReveal" /><ref name="VanityFairDec2018" /> Germain Lussier of [[io9]] noted in May 2018 that Sony would either have to begin marketing this film only two months before its release or spoil the fact that Peter is resurrected for general audiences that may not realize that this would happen in ''Endgame''. Lussier suggested the latter approach be taken, while a representative of Sony said the studio would be working with Marvel to "figure out the Spider-Man strategy".<ref name="MarketingMay2018" /> Holland and Gyllenhaal debuted the first trailer for ''Far From Home'' at Sony's [[Comic Con Experience|CCXP Brazil]] panel on December 8, 2018. The footage does not acknowledge the events of ''Infinity War'' or ''Endgame'', with Steven Weintraub of [[Collider (website)|Collider]] describing it as a continuation of "the ''Spider-Man'' universe" only.<ref name="CCXP2018" /> Holland debuted the trailer publicly on his [[Instagram]] account on January 15, 2019.<ref name="FirstTrailerE!News" /> A slightly different "international version" was also released.<ref name="FirstTrailerPolygon" /> Several news outlets commented on Peter's appearance in the trailer following the events of ''Infinity War'',<ref name="FirstTrailerPolygon" /><ref name="FirstTrailerIndieWire" /><ref name="FirstTrailerVox" /> with Zack Straf of [[IndieWire]] pointing out that while the trailer reveals Peter's return, it does not explain how.<ref name="FirstTrailerIndieWire" />

Adam Chitwood of ''Collider'' described the trailer as "cute and fun" just like ''Homecoming'', and approved of the vacation storyline, the addition of Fury, and Mysterio's short appearance. He did think the film looked "the tiniest bit less special" when compared to the acclaimed animated film ''Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse''.<ref name="FirstTrailerCollider" /> A teaser poster was released with the trailer showing Spider-Man's mask covered in travel stickers. Chitwood said the poster was "really fun, leaning into the whole 'summer vacation' vibe" while potentially hinting at the film's locations with the stickers.<ref name="FirstPosterCollider" /> Graeme McMillian of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' felt the trailer presented the threat of the Elementals so audiences would be "thrown off the scent" of Mysterio being the villain.<ref name="FirstTrailerTHRMcMillian" /> McMillian's colleague Richard Newby felt the "main takeaway from the trailer is that ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' is deftly blending old and new school elements of the Spider-Man mythos, for a result that feels surprisingly fresh". Newby was also excited about the inclusion of Fury.<ref name="FirstTrailerTHRNewby" /> ''[[Forbes]]''{{'}}s Scott Mendelson felt the trailer showed "the sheer confidence" of Sony in its Spider-Man films, especially after the successes of ''Homecoming'', ''[[Venom (2018 film)|Venom]]'' (2018), and ''Into the Spider-Verse''.<ref name="FirstTrailerForbes" /> The trailer received 130 million views in 24 hours, surpassing ''Homecoming'' (116 million views) as the most viewed Sony Pictures trailer in that time period.<ref name="TrailerViewsDeadline" />

On May 6, 2019, a second trailer for the film was released. The trailer featured an introduction from Holland warning viewers that it includes spoilers for ''Avengers: Endgame''.<ref name="EWWatts2ndTrailer" /> Watts, who knew the plots of ''Infinity War'' and ''Endgame'' and worked with the Russo brothers on Spider-Man's appearances in those films, was relieved that the trailer's release allowed him to speak more openly about ''Far From Home''.<ref name="EWWatts2ndTrailer" /> The second trailer was viewed 135 million times in 24 hours, surpassing the first ''Far From Home'' trailer as the most viewed Sony Pictures trailer in that time period.<ref name="Trailer2ViewsDeadline" /> Beginning the weekend after the trailer's release, screenings of ''Endgame'' began with a message from Holland telling audiences to stay till the end of the credits, with the trailer played at the end of the film. Marvel previously added a trailer for ''[[The Avengers (2012 film)|The Avengers]]'' (2012) to the end of ''[[Captain America: The First Avenger]]'' (2011).<ref name="TrailerEndgame" /> [[United Airlines]] served as a promotional partner on the film, with one of their [[Boeing 777]] aircraft and several United employees appearing in the film.<ref name="UnitedPartnership" /> As done with previous MCU films, [[Audi]] also sponsored the film, promoting several vehicles such as the [[Audi e-tron (2018)|e-tron SUV]], while having some of their other vehicles appear in the film.<ref name="AudieTron" /> Partnering with several other companies, including [[Dr Pepper]], [[Papa John's Pizza]], and [[Burger King]], the film had a total promotional marketing value of $288 million, the largest for a film ever.<ref name="MediaValue" />

Ahead of the film's home media release, in September 2019, Sony released a "Night Monkey" trailer, featuring footage from the film of Spider-Man in his jokey alter-ego of the same name. Lussier said the trailer was clever, and "in terms of repurposing old footage in fun, funny new ways, you have to give Sony a tip of the cap for this."<ref name="NightMonkeyTrailer" /> Later in the month, Sony created a real version of the fictional TheDailyBugle.net website as part of a [[viral marketing]] campaign to promote the home media release of the film. Inspired by real-world "conspiracy-pushing" websites such as that of Alex Jones, the website features Simmons reprising his role as Jameson in a video where he speaks out against Spider-Man and in support of Mysterio, before adding "Thanks for watching. Don't forget to like and subscribe!"<ref name="DailyBugle" /><ref name="DailyBugle2" /> The website includes testimonials from supposed victims of "the Blip", including one complaining that they disappeared in a dangerous situation and were seriously injured when they reappeared. This contradicts a statement by Feige saying that anyone in such a situation would have reappeared safely.<ref name="DailyBugleContradicts" /> Several days after this was pointed out, the website was updated to say this story was faked for an insurance claim.<ref name="DailyBugleUpdated" />

== Release ==

=== Theatrical ===
''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' had its world premiere at the [[TCL Chinese Theatre]] in [[Hollywood, Los Angeles]], on June 26, 2019.<ref name="WorldPremiere" /> The film was released on June 28, in China and Japan,<ref name="ChinaRelease" /> and in the United States on July 2,<ref name="NewReleaseDate" /> in [[3D film|3D]] and [[IMAX]].<ref name="IMAXDeal" /> In the United States, it opened in 4,634 theaters, which was second all-time behind ''Avengers: Endgame''.<ref name="MondayPreviews" /> It was originally scheduled to be released on July 5.<ref name="FarFromHome" /> The film was re-released on Labor Day weekend, beginning August 29, with four minutes of extra footage.<ref name="ReRelease" /> ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' was the last film released in Phase Three of the MCU, and the conclusion of [[the Infinity Saga]].<ref name="FFHPhase3" />

In March 2024, Sony announced that all of their live-action Spider-Man films would be re-released in theaters as part of Columbia Pictures' 100th anniversary celebration. ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' was re-released on May 27, 2024.<ref name="Columbia100th" />

=== Home media ===
''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' was released by [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]] on [[digital distribution|digital]] on September 17, 2019, and on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]], and [[DVD-Video|DVD]] on October 1.<ref name="HomeRelease" /> The home media release features a short film titled ''Peter's To-Do List'', which uses scenes of Peter retrieving various items for his trip that were cut from the theatrical release of the film.<ref name="Short" /> The short also includes footage featuring [[Hemky Madera]] reprising his ''Homecoming'' role as Mr. Delmar, the owner of a local [[Convenience store|bodega]], which was all cut from ''Far From Home''.<ref name="Madera" /><ref name="Short" />

In April 2021, Sony signed a deal with Disney giving them access to their legacy content, including past Spider-Man films and Marvel content in [[Sony's Spider-Man Universe]], to stream on [[Disney+]] and [[Hulu]] and appear on Disney's linear television networks. Disney's access to Sony's titles would come following their availability on [[Netflix]]. ''Far From Home'' had previously been available on [[Starz]] and [[FX (TV channel)|FX]].<ref name="DisneyStreamingDeadline" /><ref name="DisneyStreamingTHR" /> The film became available on Disney+ in the United States on November 3, 2023.<ref name="Disney+USRelease" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' grossed {{US$|391.3 million|long=no}} in the United States and Canada, and {{US$|741.3 million|long=no}} in other territories, for a worldwide total of {{US$|1.132 billion|long=no}}.<ref name="BOM" /> It was the [[2019 in film#Highest-grossing films|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2019]]<ref name="Highest2019" /> and the [[List of highest-grossing films|<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->24th<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->-highest-grossing film of all time]].<ref name="HighestGrossingAllTime" /> On August 18, 2019, the film surpassed ''[[Skyfall]]'' (2012) to become [[Sony Pictures]]' highest-grossing film worldwide.<ref name="FFHBoxOffice2" /><ref name="FFHBoxOffice1" /> ''Far From Home'' was the first ''[[Spider-Man in film|Spider-Man]]'' film to gross over $1{{nbsp}}billion.<ref name="BillionTheWrap" /> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' calculated the film's net profit as $339{{nbsp}}million, accounting for production budgets, marketing, talent participation, and other costs; box office grosses and home media revenues placed it seventh on their list of 2019's "Most Valuable Blockbusters".<ref name="DeadlineProfitAnalysis" />

Three weeks before its domestic release, official industry tracking had the film grossing around $170 million over its six-day opening frame. Some had the film reaching as high as $200 million, while others were at a conservative $165 million; Sony was predicting a $154 million debut.<ref name="EarlyProjections" /><ref name="Budget" /> By the week of release industry estimates lowered to $140 million, with the studio expecting $120 million, due to the recent underperformance of other sequels.<ref name="DeadlineProjections" /> ''Far From Home'' made a Tuesday-opening record $39.3 million, including an estimated $2.8–3 million from midnight previews at about 1,000 theaters.<ref name="MondayPreviews" /> It then made $27 million on its second day, the best-ever Wednesday gross for an MCU film, and $25.1 million on [[the 4th of July]], the second highest ever total for the holiday behind ''[[Transformers (film)|Transformers]]'' ($29 million in 2007).<ref name="DeadlineOpening" /> In its opening weekend the film made $92.6 million,<ref name="BOMWeekend" /> and a total of $185.1 million over the six-day frame, topping the $180 million made by ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' over its six-day July{{nbsp}}4 opening in 2004; it was the number one film for the weekend.<ref name="DeadlineWeekend" /> In its second weekend, the film made $45.3 million, again topping the box office with a 51% decrease from the first week; lower than ''Homecoming''{{'}}s 62% drop in its second box office weekend.<ref name="Weekend2" /> ''Far From Home'' grossed over $21 million in its third weekend but was dethroned by newcomer ''[[The Lion King (2019 film)|The Lion King]]''.<ref name="Weekend3" /> It completed its box office run as the [[List of American films of 2019#Box office|seventh highest-grossing film of 2019]] in this region.<ref name="American-films" />

''Far From Home'' was projected to gross around $350 million worldwide by the end of its first week of release, and about $500 million over its first 10 days.<ref name="LateProjections" /><ref name="DeadlineProjections" /> In China and Japan, where it was released a week prior to its U.S. debut, the film was expected to gross around a combined $90 million in its opening weekend.<ref name="DeadlineINTpreview" /> In China, where pre-sale tickets were less than ''Homecoming''{{'}}s, the film made $35.5 million on its first day, including $3.4 million from midnight previews (the fourth-best of all time for a superhero film in the country).<ref name="DeadlineINT" /> It ended up slightly over-performing, debuting at $111 million including $98 million in China, the fourth-best-ever superhero opening in the country.<ref name="ChinaOpen" /> ''Far From Home'' ended up grossing $580.1 million worldwide over its first 10 days of release, including $238 million from international territories in its opening weekend. In China, the film had a 10-day total of $167.4 million, and its other biggest debuts were in South Korea ($33.8 million), the United Kingdom ($17.8 million), Mexico ($13.9 million) and Australia ($11.9 million).<ref name="DeadlineINTDebut" />

=== Critical response ===
The [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of 91%, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}, based on 456 reviews. The website's critics' consensus reads, "A breezily unpredictable blend of teen romance and superhero action, ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' stylishly sets the stage for the next era of the MCU."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id={{RT data|rtid|noprefix=y}} |type=m |title=Spider-Man: Far From Home |access-date=June 3, 2025}}{{RT data|edit}}{{cbignore}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a weighted average, assigned the film a score of 69 out of 100 based on reviews from 55 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=spider-man-far-from-home |type=movie |title=Spider-Man: Far From Home |access-date=July 10, 2019}}{{cbignore}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F{{nbsp}}scale,<ref name="CinemaScore" /> while those at [[PostTrak]] gave it a 76% "definite recommend".<ref name="MondayPreviews" />

[[Owen Gleiberman]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised Holland's performance and wrote, "By the end, this Spider-Man really does find his tingle, yet coming after ''Into the Spider-Verse'', with its swirling psychedelic imagery and identity games and trap doors of perception, ''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' touches all the bases of a conventional Marvel movie. It doesn't take you out of this world. But it's good enough to summon the kick—or maybe just the illusion—of consequence."<ref name="GleibermanReview" /> Writing for the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'', [[Richard Roeper]] gave the film 3{{nbsp}}out of 4{{nbsp}}stars, calling it "zesty, sweet and satisfying" and praised the performances of the cast.<ref name="RoeperReview" /> Bernard Boo of ''[[PopMatters]]'' praised the film, commenting, "''Spider-Man: Far From Home'' is technically the final film in Phase [Three] of the MCU, and it's hard to think of a better way to send off the most successful run of one of the highest grossing franchises in history".<ref name="PM" /> Brian Lowry of ''[[CNN]]'' wrote, "Even with a plot that builds off the emotional heft of ''Endgame'', another mediocre villain, heavy dose of [[Disney Channel]]-level romance and too much clunky shtick... made this feel qualitatively more like a middle-weight contender."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cnn.com/2019/07/08/entertainment/marvel-spiderman-slowdown/index.html|title='Spider-Man' flies high, but Marvel should slow its post-'Endgame' roll}}</ref> Adam Graham of ''[[The Detroit News]]'' gave it a "B" grade, stating that "it's the closest to ''[[The Matrix]]'' the MCU has ever come, and ''Far From Home'' is the first Marvel film to hold a mirror up to the audience watching in theaters."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.detroitnews.com/story/entertainment/movies/2019/07/01/review-spider-man-far-home-flies-above-superhero-overkill/1576469001/|title=Review: 'Spider-Man: Far From Home' flies above superhero overkill}}</ref> [[Alonso Duralde]] of ''[[TheWrap]]'' said that the film feels like "a charming teen road-trip comedy that occasionally turns into a superhero movie", which he said was a compliment. He highlighted the cast, including the chemistry between Holland, Zendaya and Batalon, and said that Gyllenhaal "nails his character's earnestness but also clearly enjoys a few moments that let him channel every exasperated-sigh, I-just-want-to-get-this-right male diva director he's ever known."<ref name="TheWrapReview" />

In a 3 out of 5 review, Tara Brady of ''[[The Irish Times]]'' said, "At its best and funniest, Jon Watt's second ''Spider-Man'' film plays like ''[[Sweet Valley High (TV series)|Sweet Valley High]]'' with a powered guest star. But as a Marvel film, it's a little flat and low-key."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/film/spider-man-far-from-home-it-s-flat-low-key-and-the-showdown-is-a-mess-1.3943004|title=Spider-Man: Far From Home: It's flat, low-key and the showdown is a mess}}</ref> Writing for ''[[IndieWire]]'', David Ehrlich gave the film a grade of "C" and, despite complimenting the cast, called the film an "unadventurous bit of superhero housekeeping that only exists to clean up the mess that ''Avengers: Endgame'' left behind". He criticized the character development of Spider-Man, feeling that he does not change throughout the film beyond becoming more confident. He described the action as "plastic", and felt that the film did not give enough attention to its teen elements. Ehrlich said it might be enough to satisfy fans but called it a "failure when compared to the remarkable artistry of ''Into the Spider-Verse'' or the raw [[pathos]] of Sam Raimi's ''[[Spider-Man 2]]'' (2004)".<ref name="IndieWireReview" /> John Anderson of ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'' praised Holland and Zendaya's performances, but described the film as "a visually incoherent, effects-heavy superhero movie", and called the dialogue "dire".<ref name="WSJReview" />

== Sequel ==
{{Main|Spider-Man: No Way Home{{!}}''Spider-Man: No Way Home''}}

In September 2019, Marvel Studios and Sony Pictures announced that they were set to produce a third film, after an impasse between the two companies during negotiations.<ref name="DisneySonySpidey3" /> Watts returned to direct,<ref name="Foxx" /> from a script by McKenna and Sommers.<ref name="FFHSequelWriters" /> Holland, Zendaya, Favreau, Tomei, Batalon,<ref name="NWHVarietyTrailer" /> and Revolori reprise their roles,<ref name="Foxx" /> while [[Benedict Cumberbatch]] and [[Benedict Wong]] reprise their MCU roles as [[Stephen Strange (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Doctor Strange]] and [[Wong (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|Wong]].<ref name="CumberbatchSM3" /><ref name="NWHVarietyTrailer" /> ''Spider-Man: No Way Home'' features several actors reprising their roles from past ''Spider-Man'' films, including [[Tobey Maguire]] and [[Andrew Garfield]] as their versions of Spider-Man from [[Sam Raimi]]'s [[Spider-Man (2002 film series)|''Spider-Man'' trilogy]] (2002–2007) and [[Marc Webb]]'s [[The Amazing Spider-Man (film series)|''The Amazing Spider-Man'' films]] (2012–2014), nicknamed "[[Peter Parker (2002 film series character)|Peter-Two]]" and "[[Peter Parker (The Amazing Spider-Man film series)|Peter-Three]]" respectively,<ref name="NWHMaguireGarfield" /> alongside [[Willem Dafoe]] as [[Norman Osborn (2002 film series character)|Norman Osborn / Green Goblin]], [[Alfred Molina]] as [[Otto Octavius (film character)|Otto Octavius / Doctor Octopus]], and [[Thomas Haden Church]] as [[Flint Marko (Sam Raimi film series)|Flint Marko / Sandman]] from the Raimi trilogy, as well as [[Rhys Ifans]] as [[Lizard (character)|Curt Connors / Lizard]] and [[Jamie Foxx]] as [[Max Dillon (film character)|Max Dillon / Electro]] from the Webb films.<ref name="NWHPastVillains" /> The film was released on December 17, 2021.<ref name="NoWayHome" />
