'''''Superbad''''' is a 2007 American [[coming-of-age story|coming-of-age]] [[teen film|teen]] [[Buddy film|buddy]] [[comedy film]] directed by [[Greg Mottola]], written by [[Seth Rogen]] and [[Evan Goldberg]], and produced by [[Judd Apatow]]. It stars [[Jonah Hill]] and [[Michael Cera]] as Seth and Evan, two teenagers about to graduate from [[high school]]. Before graduating, the boys want to party and lose their [[virginity]], but their plan proves harder than expected. [[Christopher Mintz-Plasse]] (in his film debut), Rogen, [[Bill Hader]], [[Martha MacIsaac]], and [[Emma Stone]] (in her film debut) provide supporting roles.

Rogen and Goldberg wrote the script during their teenage years. It is loosely based on their experience in Grade 12 at [[Point Grey Secondary School]] in [[Vancouver]] during the 1990s. The main characters have the same [[given name]]s as the two writers. Rogen was also initially intended to play Seth, but due to age and physical size this was changed, and Hill went on to portray Seth, while Rogen portrayed the irresponsible Officer Michaels, opposite ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'' star Hader as Officer Slater.

''Superbad'' premiered at [[Grauman's Chinese Theatre]] on August 13, 2007, and was theatrically released in the United States four days later on August 17, 2007. The film received positive reviews, with critics praising the dialogue and the chemistry between the two leads as well as the performances of the supporting cast. The film also proved financially successful, grossing $170.8 million on a $17.5–20 million budget. The film has since become a [[Cult film|cult classic]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Seth and Evan are unpopular [[Nevada]] high school seniors who have been best friends since childhood, and hope to lose their virginities before going to different colleges. Jules, a girl Seth is attracted to, invites him to a party at her house. Seth and Evan's friend Fogell has a fake ID, so Seth promises to buy Jules alcohol. Evan runs into Becca, with whom he is infatuated, and offers to get her alcohol as well.

Fogell attempts to buy the alcohol at a liquor store with his fake ID under the [[mononym]] "McLovin". His attempt seems to work as the clerk accepts his fake ID and starts to ring up the transaction, but mid-transaction, Fogell is knocked down by a robber before he can purchase the alcohol. When police officers Slater and Michaels arrive, Seth and Evan, who are outside, believe that Fogell is being arrested. While waiting outside the liquor store, Seth is hit by a motorist. In exchange for their not reporting him, the motorist promises to take them to another party where they can get alcohol. Meanwhile, after getting his statement, the officers agree to drive Fogell to the party. Despite being on duty, they start drinking. Bonding with Fogell, they engage in various shenanigans.

At the party, Seth plans to steal alcohol while Evan is hesitant to stay. The motorist is kicked out by the party host and a fight ensues; Evan decides to leave despite Seth's insistence he stay. Seth then dances with a drunk Jacinda, who stains his leg with [[Menstruation|menstrual]] blood. Seth is confronted by the party's host for dancing with his fiancée, and a brawl ensues. The police are called, and Seth and Evan reunite outside and escape. Seth chastises Evan for bailing on him, reminding him that they had lifelong plans to attend college together. Evan retorts that Seth is selfish and holds him back. Evan shoves Seth, who is struck by the police cruiser driven by a distracted Slater. The officers plan to blame the crash on Seth and Evan, but as Fogell emerges from the car, all three flee. Reunited, the trio make their way to Jules' party.

At the party, Fogell inadvertently reveals that he and Evan will be living together at college, upsetting Seth. Seth then delivers the alcohol to Jules and begins drinking heavily. Meanwhile, Becca attempts to seduce Evan to take her virginity, but he declines as she is drunk and may regret it. Meanwhile, Fogell impresses classmate Nicola, so she takes him upstairs to have sex. Seth drunkenly attempts to kiss Jules, but she declines because she does not want to kiss him while he is drunk. Believing he has ruined his chance, Seth becomes upset and falls down in a drunken stupor, accidentally headbutting Jules and giving her a black eye.

Slater and Michaels bust the party. Seth wakes up and escapes, carrying an intoxicated Evan away from the house. Slater busts in on Fogell and Nicola, causing her to run off. Slater and Michaels reveal they knew all along that Fogell was underage but played along, wanting to show him that cops can have fun too. To make it up to him, they make a fake spectacle of [[Perp walk|perp-walking]] him to their cruiser in front of his classmates. They then destroy the damaged police cruiser together.

Seth sleeps over at Evan's, where he reveals he discovered weeks earlier that Evan and Fogell planned to live together at college. They apologize to each other and reconcile. The next day, Seth and Evan visit the mall and run into Jules and Becca. Becca and Seth apologize for their drunken behavior, and the boys head their separate ways with their respective love interests.

==Production==

===Development===
''Superbad'' was written by Evan Goldberg and Seth Rogen during their teen years. It is loosely based on their own experience as seniors in Vancouver in the late 1990s, hence the character names Seth and Evan. Other characters and references were influenced by Goldberg and Rogen's adolescence, such as Steven Glanzberg, their peer at Point Grey Secondary School, characterized in the film as a loner.<ref>{{Cite web|date=Aug 17, 2007|title=Seth Rogen's Vancouver high school misadventures hit the big screen|url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/seth-rogen-s-vancouver-high-school-misadventures-hit-the-big-screen-1.668110|website=CBC News}}</ref> According to an interview at an event panel in 2009, Fogell was also a real friend of Rogen and Goldberg. Rogen was initially slated to play Jonah Hill's character Seth. Still, due to his physical size and age, he played one of the police officers.<ref name="OS" /> When asked where he wants to be dropped off, Fogell tells the officers to take him to 13th and Granville, a nod to Rogen and Goldberg's favorite all-you-can-eat sushi restaurant in Vancouver.<ref>{{cite tweet |last1=Rogen |first1=Seth |title=It's also where the all you can eat sushi place that we used to go to all the time in high school was. |user=Sethrogen |number=1143358951045525504 |date=June 24, 2019 |access-date=April 5, 2021}}</ref> ''Superbad'' took seven years to complete from early scripting in 2000 and filming from 2006 to 2007.{{citation needed|date=December 2022}} Among the films that served as inspiration for ''Superbad'' were ''[[Dazed and Confused (film)|Dazed and Confused]]'', ''[[Fast Times at Ridgemont High]]'', and ''[[American Graffiti]]''.<ref name="OS">{{cite news |last1=Moore |first1=Roger |title=If you want to be 'Superbad,' you'd better learn from the best |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-2007-08-17-superguys-story.html |access-date=26 December 2022 |work=Orlando Sentinel |date=August 17, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20221226193133/https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-2007-08-17-superguys-story.html |archive-date=December 26, 2022}}</ref>

===Filming===
The film was primarily shot in Los Angeles in the fall of 2006.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209171524/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad |url-status=dead |archive-date=2013-02-09 |title=Where was Superbad (2007) Filmed |publisher=Wwifdb.com |date=2007-08-17 |access-date=2013-02-27 }}</ref> Exterior shots of the high school were filmed at [[El Segundo High School]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/high-school-exterior-640-main-street-el-segundo-ca |title=Superbad (2007) – High School Exterior |publisher=Wwifdb.com |access-date=2013-02-27 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120918160009/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/high-school-exterior-640-main-street-el-segundo-ca |archive-date=September 18, 2012 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> The mall scenes were shot at the [[Westfield Culver City|Fox Hills Mall]] in [[Culver City, California]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/the-mall-6000-sepulveda-blvd-culver-city-ca-united |title=Superbad (2007) – The Mall |publisher=Wwifdb.com |access-date=2013-02-27 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209152748/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/the-mall-6000-sepulveda-blvd-culver-city-ca-united |archive-date=February 9, 2013 |df=mdy-all }}</ref>

Other filming locations include the convenience store at the beginning of the film, also in Culver City,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/convenience-store-4273-overland-ave-culver-city-ca |title=Superbad (2007) – Convenience Store |publisher=Wwifdb.com |access-date=February 27, 2013|url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209183026/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/convenience-store-4273-overland-ave-culver-city-ca |archive-date=February 9, 2013 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> the liquor store where "McLovin" gets IDed in [[Glendale, California]],<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/mclovin-gets-id-ed-1700-victory-blvd-glendale-ca-u |title=Superbad (2007) – McLovin get's ID-ed |publisher=Wwifdb.com |access-date=February 27, 2013|url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130210101911/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/mclovin-gets-id-ed-1700-victory-blvd-glendale-ca-u |archive-date=February 10, 2013 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> and the bar where the cops take McLovin for a drink is neighboring [[Los Angeles International Airport]] (LAX).<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/cops-take-mclovin-to-a-bar-7415-la-tijera-blvd-wes |title=Cops take McLovin to a bar |publisher=Wwifdb.com |access-date=2013-02-27 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130209190242/http://www.wwifdb.com/movies/superbad/scenes/cops-take-mclovin-to-a-bar-7415-la-tijera-blvd-wes |archive-date=February 9, 2013 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> The scene where McLovin and the cops do donuts in the cop car was filmed in a parking lot on the [[California State University, Northridge]] campus.

Christopher Mintz-Plasse was 17 at the time of filming ''Superbad'', and as a result, his mother was required to be present on set during the filming of his sex scene.<ref>{{cite news|title=Is this it? |url=https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2009/jan/17/1|work=[[The Guardian]] |date=January 16, 2009|access-date=24 July 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/08/superbad-oral-history |title="It's Either Going to Be Really Funny, or They're Going to Be Horrified": An Oral History of Superbad |author=Andrew Buss |publisher=Vanity Fair |date=August 17, 2022 |accessdate=September 2, 2022}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
[[File:Seth Rogen Evan Goldberg Comic Con 2007 perspective corrected.jpg|thumb|[[Seth Rogen]] and [[Evan Goldberg]] at a ''Superbad'' panel at [[San Diego Comic-Con]] in July 2007]]
''Superbad'' opened at number one at the United States box office, grossing US$33,052,411 in its opening weekend from 2,948 theaters for an average of US$11,212 per theater.<ref name="mojoweek">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=superbad.htm |title=Superbad (2007) – Weekend Box Office |access-date=2007-09-24 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]] }}</ref> The film stayed at {{No.|1}} the second week, grossing US$18,044,369.<ref name=mojoweek/>

The film grossed US$121.5 million in the United States and Canada and US$48.4 million in other countries, for a total of US$169.9 million worldwide. Compared to the budget of $17.5–20 million, the film earned a huge financial profit,<ref name="numbers" /><ref name="mojo" /> making it the highest domestic grossing high school comedy at the time (it was surpassed by ''[[21 Jump Street (film)|21 Jump Street]]'', a film also starring Hill, in 2012).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/genres/chart/?id=highschoolcomedy.htm |title=Charts – High School Comedy |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=2013-02-27 |archive-date=July 10, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180710145358/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/genres/chart/?id=highschoolcomedy.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 88% based on reviews from 206 critics, with an average rating of 7.70/10. The website's summary reads: "Deftly balancing vulgarity and sincerity while placing its protagonists in excessive situations, ''Superbad'' is an authentic take on friendship and the overarching awkwardness of the high school experience."<ref>{{cite web |title=Superbad (2007) |url= https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/superbad/ |website= [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date= March 27, 2025 }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a score of 76/100 based on 36 reviews, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.metacritic.com/film/titles/superbad | title = Superbad | website = [[metacritic]] | access-date = 2007-09-03 | archive-date = July 14, 2010 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20100714043544/http://www.metacritic.com/film/titles/superbad | url-status = dead }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a grade A− on scale of A to F.<ref>{{cite web |author1=Gregg Kilday |title=It’s all good for 'Superbad' as comedy beats estimate |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/all-good-superbad-as-comedy-148434/ |website=The Hollywood Reporter |publisher=The Associated Press |date=21 August 2007 |quote=respondents awarded the film an A-minus overall.}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url= https://cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= Cinemascore |url-status=live |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 }}</ref>

Mick LaSalle of the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' called it 2007's most successful comedy.<ref>{{Cite web|last=LaSalle|first=Mick|date=2007-08-16|title=Review: Teens on a mission to buy booze in 'Superbad'|url=https://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/Review-Teens-on-a-mission-to-buy-booze-in-2525884.php|access-date=2021-12-22|website=San Francisco Chronicle|language=en-US}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' had the headline of his review read "McLovin It", and gave the film 3{{1/2}} out of 4 stars, and said: "The movie reminded me a little of ''[[Animal House|National Lampoon's Animal House]]'', except that it's more mature, as all movies are."<ref name=ebert>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/superbad-2007|title=Reviews :: Superbad|first=Roger |last=Ebert |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=2007-08-16 |work=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> Carina Chocano of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' said: "Physically, Hill and Cera recall the classic comic duos—[[Laurel and Hardy]], [[Abbott and Costello]], [[Dan Aykroyd|Aykroyd]] and [[John Belushi|Belushi]]. But they are contemporary kids, sophisticated and sensitive to nuance"; she added: "I hope it's not damning the movie with the wrong kind of praise to say that for a film so deliriously smutty, ''Superbad'' is supercute."<ref name="chocano">{{cite web |url=http://www.calendarlive.com/movies/reviews/cl-et-superbad17aug17,0,711876.story?coll=cl-mreview |title='Superbad's' teen raunch isn't what's shocking; it's the love story |access-date=2007-08-19 |first=Carina |last=Chocano |date=2007-08-17|work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070824051531/http://www.calendarlive.com/movies/reviews/cl-et-superbad17aug17,0,711876.story?coll=cl-mreview |archive-date=2007-08-24 }}</ref> Sean Burns of ''[[Philadelphia Weekly]]'' said "2007: the year Judd Apatow and Seth Rogen saved movie comedy", a reference to ''[[Knocked Up]]'' which was released in June.<ref name=burn>{{cite web |title=Geek Outlook |first=Sean |last=Burns |work=Philadelphia Weekly |date=August 15, 2007 |url-status=dead |url= http://www.philadelphiaweekly.com/articles/15259|archive-url= https://archive.today/20070930020542/http://www.philadelphiaweekly.com/articles/15259 |archive-date=2007-09-30}}</ref> Devin Gordon of ''[[Newsweek]]'' said: "As a ''[[Revenge of the Nerds]]'' redux, ''Superbad'' isn't perfect. But it's super close."<ref>{{cite web |title=Revenge of the Nerds |first=Devin |last=Gordon |date=2007-08-20 |work=Newsweek |url-status=dead |url=http://www.msnbc.msn.com/id/20217418/site/newsweek/ |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070828075852/http://www.msnbc.msn.com/id/20217418/site/newsweek/ |archive-date=2007-08-28}}</ref>

In a more critical vein, Stephen Farber of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'', compared the film to other films with a single-day structure, such as ''[[American Graffiti]]'' and ''[[Dazed and Confused (film)|Dazed and Confused]]'', but said that ''Superbad'' "doesn't have the smarts or the depths of those ensemble comedies."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/film/reviews/article_display.jsp?&rid=9592|title=Superbad|access-date=2007-08-21 |first=Stephen |last=Farber |date=2007-08-07 |work=The Hollywood Reporter |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930235412/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/film/reviews/article_display.jsp?&rid=9592 <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date=2007-09-30}}</ref> ''The Hollywood Reporter'' review was referenced in the film's DVD audio commentary, particularly the review's suggestion that the two main characters have a [[homoeroticism|homoerotic]] experience similar to the film ''[[Y Tu Mamá También]]''.<ref>The DVD audio commentary on the ''Superbad: Unrated Extended Edition'' DVD.</ref> Roger Moore of the ''[[Orlando Sentinel]]'' called the film a "super-derivative, super-raunchy sack of laughs" and a "great vulgarian send-off to the summer of ''Knocked Up''" that plays like "''[[Freaks and Geeks]]: Uncensored''."<ref name="OS2">{{cite news |last1=Moore |first1=Roger |title=It's like an uncensored version of Freaks & Geeks |url=https://www.sun-sentinel.com/news/fl-xpm-2007-08-17-0708151077-story.html |access-date=26 December 2022 |work=Orlando Sentinel |date=August 17, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210702071816/https://www.sun-sentinel.com/news/fl-xpm-2007-08-17-0708151077-story.html |archive-date=July 2, 2021}}</ref> Moore concluded, "The energy flags as it overstays its welcome. Still, there are plenty of those laughs, from the ruder-than-rude opening to the ironic-sentimental ending."<ref name="OS2" /> [[Wesley Morris]] of ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' said the film "has a degree more sophistication than ''Revenge of the Nerds'' and ''[[American Pie (film)|American Pie]]'', and less than the underrated ''[[House Party (1990 film)|House Party]]''." Morris also said, "the few smart observations could have come from an episode of one of Apatow's TV shows" and "I wanted to find this as funny as audiences did."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.boston.com/movies/display?display=movie&id=9508 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080529035519/http://www.boston.com/movies/display?display=movie&id=9508 |url-status=dead |archive-date=May 29, 2008 |title=It's a nerd, he's in pain – it's ''Superbad'' |first=Wesley|last=Morris|date=2007-08-17|work=[[The Boston Globe]] |access-date=2021-08-21 }}</ref>

==Home media==
''Superbad'' was released on [[DVD]], [[Universal Media Disc|UMD]] and [[Blu-ray Disc|Blu-ray]] on December 4, 2007, by [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]].<ref name="numbers" /><ref>{{cite news |last1=Fienberg |first1=Daniel |title=DVD Review: 'Superbad' |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/zap-dvdreview-superbad-story.html |access-date=26 December 2022 |work=Chicago Tribune |date=December 4, 2007}}</ref> The [[special edition]] DVD sparked controversy in [[Hawaii]] due to the inclusion of a replica of the fake McLovin ID used in the film, which heavily resembled the state's real-life driver's license. This resulted in [[Walmart]] removing it from the shelves in the state.<ref>{{cite web | last=Silva | first=Alexandre Da | title=City seeks ban on 'Superbad' over bogus 'McLovin' ID | website=starbulletin.com | date=2007-12-06 | url=https://archives.starbulletin.com/2007/12/06/news/story04.html | access-date=2025-08-13}}</ref><ref>{{cite news | url=https://the.honoluluadvertiser.com/article/2007/Dec/07/ln/hawaii712070359.html | title=Wal-Mart halts 'Superbad' gimmick in Hawaii |newspaper=Honolulu Advertiser }}</ref>

== Legacy ==
Since its release, it has retrospectively garnered acclaim as one of the best comedies of the 2000s and as one of the best high school films of all time. It has been hailed as one of the defining films of the [[Millennials|Millennial generation]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lu |first=Garry |date=Jul 30, 2021 |title=Why 'Superbad' Is The Greatest Millennial Comedy Of All Time |url=https://www.bosshunting.com.au/entertainment/movies/why-superbad-greatest-millennial-comedy-of-all-time |website=Boss Hunting}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |date=July 30, 2014 |title=Readers' Poll: The 10 Funniest Movies of the 2000s |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/readers-poll-the-10-funniest-movies-of-the-2000s-11901/10-the-40-year-old-virgin-222516/ |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]}}</ref>

==Books==
Two tie-in books to the film were published by [[Newmarket Press]]:
* ''Superbad: The Illustrated Moviebook'' ({{ISBN|9781557047984}}) was published on December 4, 2007, to coincide with the release of the film on DVD. This official companion book includes an introduction by producer [[Judd Apatow]]; the complete script by [[Seth Rogen]] and [[Evan Goldberg]]; commentaries by Apatow, Rogen and Goldberg, and journalists from ''[[Rolling Stone]]'', ''[[The New York Times]]'', and ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''; 56 film stills; "Mr. Vagtastic Guide to Buying Porn;" and 24 "phallographic" drawings by David Goldberg that viewers will recognize from the film's end credits.
* ''Superbad: The Drawings'' ({{ISBN|9781557048080}}) was published on February 14, 2008. This gift hardcover art book contains 82 "phallographic" drawings created by David Goldberg ([[Evan Goldberg]]'s brother) for the film.
