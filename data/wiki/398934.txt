'''''10 Things I Hate About You''''' is a 1999 American [[Teen film|teen]] [[romantic comedy]] directed by [[Gil Junger]] (in his directorial debut) from a screenplay by [[Karen McCullah|Karen McCullah Lutz]] and [[Kirsten Smith (writer)|Kirsten Smith]]. Loosely inspired by and based on [[William Shakespeare]]'s comedy ''[[The Taming of the Shrew]]'', it stars [[Julia Stiles]], [[Heath Ledger]], [[Joseph Gordon-Levitt]], [[Larisa Oleynik]], [[Larry Miller (comedian)|Larry Miller]], [[Andrew Keegan]], [[David Krumholtz]], [[Susan May Pratt]], and [[Gabrielle Union]]. 

In the film, high-school student Cameron James (Gordon-Levitt) is unable to date sophomore Bianca Stratford (Oleynik) until her [[asocial]] feminist senior sister Kat (Stiles) dates, leading to Cameron recruiting [[bad boy (archetype)|bad boy]] classmate Patrick Verona (Ledger) to date Kat.

Development on ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' began once Lutz and Smith completed the screenplay in November 1997, with Junger attached to direct soon after. He sought to cast lesser profile actors for the film's lead roles; Stiles joined after several actors declined the role, while Ledger was cast three-and-a-half week prior to the start of [[principal photography]], which took place between June and August 1998 in [[Tacoma, Washington|Tacoma]] and [[Seattle]].

''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' was theatrically released in the United States on 31&nbsp;March 1999, by [[Buena Vista Pictures Distribution]]. It received generally positive reviews from critics, with praise for its screenplay and Stiles', Ledger's and Gordon-Levitt's performances: the film became their [[breakthrough role]]s. It grossed $53.5&nbsp;million worldwide. ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' gained praise in later years for its themes and characters, becoming considered a [[cult film|cult classic]] and as one of the best teen romantic comedies of all time.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Cameron James, a new student at Padua High School in the [[Seattle metropolitan area|Seattle area]], becomes smitten with beautiful and popular sophomore Bianca Stratford. [[Geek]]y Michael Eckman warns him she is vapid and conceited, and that her overprotective single father Walter, an [[obstetrician]] worried about [[teenage pregnancy]], has forbidden her or her shrewish older sister Kat, a senior, to date until they graduate. Frustrated by Bianca's insistence and Kat's rebelliousness, Walter declares that Bianca may date only when Kat does, knowing Kat's unsocial attitude will make it very difficult.

When Cameron asks Bianca out, she informs him of her father's new rule and suggests he find someone willing to date Kat; unbeknownst to Cameron, Bianca wants to date affluent and popular senior Joey Donner. 

Cameron selects Australian "bad boy" Patrick Verona. Michael convinces Joey to pay Patrick to ask out Kat. Patrick agrees, but Kat rebuffs his first few advances. After Michael and Cameron probe Bianca for information on Kat's likes and dislikes, he begins to win Kat's interest. Kat goes to a party with Patrick, enabling Bianca and her best friend Chastity Church to tag along.

At the party, Kat becomes upset upon seeing Bianca with Joey, responding by getting drunk. Patrick attends to her, and after leaving the party, she starts to open up to him, expressing her interest in forming a band. When Kat attempts to kiss him, Patrick pulls away, causing her to depart, infuriated and embarrassed. Meanwhile, Bianca upsets Cameron by ignoring him for Joey, but she soon realizes that Joey is shallow and self-absorbed. When she asks Cameron to drive her home, he calls her selfish for the way she treated him and says his feelings for her are true. Bianca kisses him before leaving.

Joey pays Patrick more money to take Kat to the prom so he can take Bianca. Although Kat is still angry with Patrick, he wins her over by serenading her with a performance of "[[Can't Take My Eyes Off You]]" by [[Frankie Valli]], and she returns the favor by helping him sneak out of detention. Their subsequent date turns romantic and they kiss, but she becomes suspicious when he insists she accompany him to the prom, which she adamantly opposes.

Irritated that Cameron has delayed asking her to the prom, Bianca accepts Joey's invitation, but Walter declines to allow it unless Kat accompanies her. Kat confesses to Bianca that she dated Joey when they were freshmen and, succumbing to peer pressure, had sex with him once and regretted it. After Joey dumped her, she vowed against doing things out of peer pressure, and stays unsocial to protect Bianca. Importantly, Kat's brief relationship with Joey happened close to the time in which Kat and Bianca's estranged mother left them. 

Bianca insists that she should be able to have her own experiences, so Kat decides to go to the prom with Patrick. This lets Bianca go as well, and she attends the prom with Cameron instead of Joey. At the prom, Bianca learns from Chastity that Joey was planning to have sex with Bianca that night, and had made a bet about it with his friends. Angry that Bianca spurned him for Cameron, Joey confronts Patrick, revealing that he had paid Patrick to date Kat and leaving her heartbroken. Joey punches Cameron, but Bianca punches and beats him for hurting her, Kat, and Cameron.

The following day, Bianca reconciles with Kat, and is now officially dating Cameron. For an assignment in which the students were tasked to write their own versions of [[William Shakespeare]]'s [[Sonnet 141]], Kat reads aloud her version, entitled "10 Things I Hate About You", revealing that she still cares for Patrick. He surprises her with a [[Fender Stratocaster]] that he bought with the money Joey paid him, and confesses that he has fallen for her. Kat forgives him, and they reconcile with a kiss.

==Production==
=== Background ===
Writing duo [[Karen McCullah|Karen McCullah Lutz]] and [[Kirsten Smith (writer)|Kirsten Smith]] were inspired to write ''10 Things I Hate About You'' after watching ''[[Clueless]]'' (1995). Being a fan of [[teen film]]s, the pair set out to find a classic play or myth to turn it into a contemporary high-school movie, eventually settling on ''[[The Taming of the Shrew]]'', a [[Shakespearean comedy|comedy]] by [[William Shakespeare]].<ref name="Digital Spy">{{cite web |last1=Mullor |first1=Mireia |title=10 Things I Hate About You: The definitive oral history by the people who made it |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a61420997/10-things-i-hate-about-you-oral-history/ |website=Digital Spy |access-date=January 2, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240709095146/https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a61420997/10-things-i-hate-about-you-oral-history/ |archive-date=July 9, 2024 |date=July 9, 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref> They wanted to write a strong-willed, [[feminism|feminist]] character. Patrick Verona was inspired by [[Judd Nelson]]'s character in ''[[The Breakfast Club]]'' (1985). The script was finalized in November 1997.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Smith |first1=Kirsten |last2=McCullah|first2=Karen|title=Ten Things I Hate About You - Script |date=November 12, 1997}}</ref> The screenplay was eventually picked up by [[The Walt Disney Company]], with [[Gil Junger]] attached to direct.

=== Casting ===
Junger was keen to find unknown actors for the movie. [[Josh Hartnett]] and [[Ashton Kutcher]] were in the running to play Patrick Verona. [[Eliza Dushku]] auditioned for the role of Kat Stratford. [[Katie Holmes]] was also considered for the role. [[Kate Hudson]] was offered the part but her mother, [[Goldie Hawn]], did not like the script, so she passed on the role.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.nytimes.com/2019/03/27/arts/10-things-i-hate-about-you-heath-ledger.html | title='10 Things I Hate About You': When Heath Ledger Was Just Breaking Through | newspaper=The New York Times | date=March 27, 2019 | last1=Kaplan | first1=Ilana | archive-date=August 10, 2022 | access-date=August 10, 2022 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220810154817/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/03/27/arts/10-things-i-hate-about-you-heath-ledger.html | url-status=live }}</ref> Casting director [[Marcia Ross]] contacted Junger and recommended [[Julia Stiles]] for the role.<ref name="Digital Spy" />
Junger met her that evening and was "immediately taken"; after the 10 minute meeting, he gave her the role.<ref name="Digital Spy" /> Joseph Gordon-Levitt auditioned for the roles of Cameron and Michael, despite initial reluctance to act in a "high school film".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Chand |first=Neeraj |date=August 28, 2020 |title=Why Joseph Gordon-Levitt Tried Hard to Escape Doing 10 Things I Hate About You |url=https://movieweb.com/10-things-i-hate-about-you-joseph-gordon-levitt/ |access-date=2025-04-05 |website=MovieWeb |language=en}}</ref> The role of Michael eventually went to David Krumholtz and Gordon-Levitt was then offered the role of Cameron. Casting for Patrick Verona was a long process, with [[Andrew Lazar]] reporting he had seen over 1,000&nbsp;boys for the role.<ref name="Digital Spy"/> Three and a half weeks before filming was due to start, Ross brought [[Heath Ledger]] into a casting session, and Junger was immediately inspired by him.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mohr|first=Jayj |title=Heath Ledger's Audition for Ten Things I Hate About You |url=https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Oc5LCXwcD78|access-date=2025-11-04|website=[[YouTube]] |language=en}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
Filming took place in 1998, between June&nbsp;8 and August&nbsp;6.<ref name="Digital Spy"/> Many of the scenes were filmed on location at [[Stadium High School]] and at a house in the [[North Tacoma, Tacoma, Washington|North End]] of [[Tacoma, Washington]]. The prom sequence was shot over three days in [[Seattle]].<ref name="cinemareview">{{cite web |url=http://www.cinemareview.com/production.asp?prodid=531 |title=10 Things I Hate About You |website=CinemaReview.com |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170913135120/http://www.cinemareview.com/production.asp?prodid=531 |archive-date=September 13, 2017 |access-date=May 24, 2008}}</ref> Ledger's singing scene in the high-school bleachers, regarded as one of the most iconic scenes of the movie,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Midkiff |first1=Sarah |date=30 March 2019 |title=The story behind the iconic ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' singing scene is so sweet |website=Refinery29 |url=https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2019/03/228470/10-things-i-hate-about-you-heath-ledger-bleacher-scene-explained |access-date=2 January 2025 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Burlaza |first1=Angelica |date=22 December 2022 |title=We need more grand serenades like in ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' |website=Collider |url=https://collider.com/10-things-i-hate-about-you-grand-serenade-heath-ledger/ |access-date=January 2, 2025 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Blickley |first1=Leigh |date=29 March 2019 |title=The full story behind Heath Ledger's famous bleacher scene in ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' |website=Huffpost |url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/10-things-i-hate-about-you-bleachers-scene_n_5c990c60e4b057f7330e3190 |access-date=January 2, 2025 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007141030/https://www.huffpost.com/entry/10-things-i-hate-about-you-bleachers-scene_n_5c990c60e4b057f7330e3190 |archive-date=October 7, 2024 }}</ref> had just three [[take]]s. The song for the scene changed numerous times, almost being "[[I Touch Myself]]" by Australian rock band [[Divinyls]], before "[[Can't Take My Eyes Off You]]" by [[Frankie Valli]] was settled on.<ref name="Digital Spy" />

Costume designer Kimberly Tillman designed original dresses for Larisa Oleynik and Julia Stiles, as well as the period outfits for Susan May Pratt and David Krumholtz. Gabrielle Union's snakeskin prom dress is a [[Betsey Johnson]] design. Heath Ledger's and Joseph Gordon-Levitt's vintage [[tuxedo]]s came from Isadora's in Seattle.<ref name="cinemareview"/>

==Reception==
===Box office===
In its opening weekend, the film grossed $8.3&nbsp;million in 2,271&nbsp;theaters domestically, finishing second at the box office, behind ''[[The Matrix]]''. It earned $21&nbsp;million in the first five days of release.<ref>{{cite web |title=Gamechangers in box office history: How ''The Matrix'' and ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' broke the rules |website=IndieWire |date=5 April 2020 |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/gamechangers-box-office-history-matrix-10-things-i-hate-about-you-1202222844/ |url-status=live |access-date=January 3, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230606202024/https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/gamechangers-box-office-history-matrix-10-things-i-hate-about-you-1202222844/ |archive-date=June 6, 2023 }}</ref> The movie grossed a total of $38.2&nbsp;million in the U.S. and Canada, and $15.3 million in other territories, for $53.7&nbsp;million worldwide.<ref name="BOM"/>

Peeples of [[AllMusic]] rated it 3 stars out of 5, calling it "one of the best [[modern rock]] soundtracks of the spring 1999 season".<ref>{{cite web|last=Peeples |first=S. |url= https://www.allmusic.com/album/10-things-i-hate-about-you-original-soundtrack--mw0000237728 |website=[[AllMusic]] |title=10 Things I Hate About You review |access-date=February 18, 2018}}</ref>

===Adaptations===
In June 1999, the [[Scholastic Corporation]] published a [[novelization]] of the story, adapted by [[David Levithan]].<ref>{{cite book| isbn= 0439087309 | title= 10 Things I Hate About You | year= 1999 |publisher= [[Scholastic Corporation|Scholastic]]|location= New York }}</ref> The story is retold as it is in the film, with each chapter written from the point of view of either Bianca, Cameron, Kat, Patrick, or Michael.

In October 2008, [[Freeform (TV channel)|ABC Family]] ordered a [[pilot episode]] of ''[[10 Things I Hate About You (TV series)|10 Things I Hate About You]]'', a half-hour, single-camera comedy series based on the film. Larry Miller is the only actor from the film to reprise his role of Walter Stratford in the TV series. The director of the film, [[Gil Junger]], directed many of the episodes, including the pilot; the film's composer, Richard Gibbs, also returned to do the show's music. The series was adapted and produced by [[Carter Covington]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Nguyen |first= Hanh |url=http://www.zap2it.com/tv/news/zap-pilots10thingsihateaboutyourubyandtherockits,0,2482981.story |title=ABC Family Greenlights '10 Things I Hate,' 'Ruby' Pilots |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081009150350/http://www.zap2it.com/tv/news/zap-pilots10thingsihateaboutyourubyandtherockits,0,2482981.story |archive-date=October 9, 2008 |website=Zap2it |date=October 8, 2008 |access-date=October 8, 2008 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The show premiered on 7&nbsp;July 2009,<ref>{{cite web |title=10 Things I Hate About You |url=http://abcfamily.go.com/abcfamily/path/section_Shows+10-Things-I-Hate-About-You/page_Detail |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090519200819/http://abcfamily.go.com/abcfamily/path/section_Shows+10-Things-I-Hate-About-You/page_Detail |archive-date=May 19, 2009 |access-date=June 17, 2009 |website=ABC Family |url-status=dead}}</ref> and ended on 24&nbsp;May 2010, lasting 20&nbsp;episodes.

===Musical adaptation===
{{Main|10 Things I Hate About You (musical)}}
In April&nbsp;2025, it was announced that [[Lena Dunham]] and Jessica Huang would be writing the book for a musical stage adaptation of ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'', with [[Carly Rae Jepsen]] and [[Ethan Gruska]] writing the score. The creative team will also include [[Christopher Wheeldon]] as director and choreographer and [[Tom Kitt (musician)|Tom Kitt]] as music supervisor, arranger, and orchestrator.<ref>{{cite web |first=Rebecca |last=Milzoff |date=23 April 2025 |title=Carly Rae Jepsen and Lena Dunham team up for ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' broadway musical |website=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |url=https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/carly-rae-jepsen-lena-dunham-10-things-broadway-musical-1235953162/ |access-date=October 26, 2025 }}</ref> It was announced on August 17, 2026, that the musical will begin performances on Broadway on August 17, 2027.<ref>{{cite web |first=Chloe |last=Rabinowitz |title=10 Things I Hate About You to Open on Broadway in 2027 |date=August 17, 2026 |url=https://www.broadwayworld.com/article/10-things-i-hate-about-you-to-open-on-broadway-in-2027 |access-date=August 17, 2026 |website=BroadwayWorld}}</ref>

==Sequels==
On the 10th anniversary home video release, the screenwriters mentioned in an audio commentary that they were floating ideas around for ''11 Things I Hate About You'' after the original film’s release. But ultimately, nothing came from it.<ref>{{cite interview|title=10 Things I Hate About You Commentary|publisher=[[Touchstone Home Entertainment]]|date=September 15, 2026}}</ref>

In May&nbsp;2012, a standalone sequel titled ''[[10 Things I Hate About Life]]'' was announced to be in development with Gil Junger serving as director and screenwriter.<ref name="Sequel_Variety">{{cite magazine |last=McNary |first=Dave |date=9 May 2012 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |title=''10&nbsp;Things'' counts Atwell as star |url=https://variety.com/2012/film/news/10-things-counts-atwell-as-star-1118053704/ |access-date=May 8, 2025 }}</ref> [[Evan Rachel Wood]] and [[Thomas McDonell]] were cast in the lead roles, and while production commenced principal photography halted with delays in continuing the production.<ref name="Sequel_Variety2">{{cite magazine |last=McNary |first=Dave |date=7 November 2013 |title=AFM: Evan Rachel Wood's ''10 Things I Hate About Life'' re-starting |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |url=https://variety.com/2013/film/news/evan-rachel-woods-10-things-i-hate-about-life-1200805517/ |access-date=May 8, 2025 }}</ref> The movie was ultimately shelved indefinitely, resulting in legal action taken by its producers.<ref name="Sequel_TT">{{cite web |last=Spencer |first=Anthony |date=4 February 2021 |title=Why producers sued Evan Rachel Wood for $30&nbsp;million |website=The Things |url=https://www.thethings.com/why-producers-sued-evan-rachel-wood-for-30-million/ |access-date=May 8, 2025}}</ref>

In May&nbsp;2025, a sequel was in development with the title ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About Dating''. Gil Junger will once again serve as director, with the filmmaker co-writing the script with Naya Elle James. Junger stated that similar to the previous movie being a William Shakespeare contemporary retelling, the new project will be based on [[Molière|Jean-Baptiste "Molière" Poquelin]]'s ''[[The Misanthrope|The Misanthrope, or the Cantankerous Lover]]''. The director expressed hopes that cast from the original movie would reprise the roles, in a variety of cameos or as a featured supporting cast alongside the new characters. The movie is intended to be the first in a trilogy of sequels, with the additional projects tentatively titled: ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About Marriage'', and ''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About Kids''. Junger acknowledged that while Ledger cannot appear in the new installment, there will be reference and tribute made to the deceased actor.<ref name="Trilogy_People">{{cite magazine |last=Juneau |first=Jen |date=6 May 2025 |title=''10&nbsp;Things I Hate About You'' director reveals he's working on a follow-up film with a nod to Heath Ledger |magazine=[[People magazine]] |department=Exclusive |url=https://people.com/10-things-i-hate-about-you-new-movie-heath-ledger-nod-director-gil-junger-exclusive-11727082 |access-date=May 8, 2025}}</ref>
