'''''Chez Jolie Coiffure''''' ({{Literal translation|At Jolie Coiffure}}) is a 2018 Belgian–Cameroonian documentary film directed by [[Rosine Mbakam]]. It follows the lives of the staff and clients of a [[Beauty salon|hair salon]] in [[Matongé (Ixelles)|Matongé]], a neighbourhood of [[Ixelles]] known for its large [[Black Belgians|African community]].

== Premise ==
''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' follows the daily working life of Sabine Amiyeme, an undocumented [[Cameroon]]ian woman who fled to [[Belgium]] from [[Lebanon]] seven years earlier, and who treats the hair of other African migrants living in [[Brussels]], including undocumented immigrants. == Production ==
Mbakam stated she had been inspired to make ''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' after observing city tours in Matongé, a neighbourhood known for its large immigrant community, stating that she wanted to portray "the separation of the two worlds" and to give the people being observed the opportunity to speak. She stated her aim was for the film was to reclaim the narrative concerning minorities and immigration.<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":6">{{Cite news |last=Cottam |first=Nina |date=1 July 2020 |title=« Chez Jolie Coiffure », ou l'histoire d'une « invisible » |trans-title="Chez Jolie Coiffure," or the story of an "invisible" woman |url=https://media-animation.be/ressource/chez-jolie-coiffure-ou-l-histoire-d-une-invisible-1300/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260316055927/https://media-animation.be/ressource/chez-jolie-coiffure-ou-l-histoire-d-une-invisible-1300/ |archive-date=16 March 2026 |access-date=2 August 2026 |work=Média Animation |language=fr}}</ref> Mbakam took time to build trust with the community due to their concerns about being undocumented.<ref name=":7">{{Cite news |last=Bauwin |first=Adeline |last2=Dequeker |first2=Camille |date=23 April 2019 |title=Un documentaire intimiste dans un salon de coiffure pour combattre les stéréotypes |trans-title=An intimate documentary filmed in a hair salon to combat stereotypes |url=https://bx1.be/categories/news/un-documentaire-intimiste-dans-un-salon-de-coiffure-pour-combattre-les-stereotypes/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260422233103/https://bx1.be/categories/news/un-documentaire-intimiste-dans-un-salon-de-coiffure-pour-combattre-les-stereotypes/ |archive-date=22 April 2026 |access-date=2 August 2026 |work=BX1 |language=fr}}</ref> Sabine had previously worked as a hairdresser in Cameroon and returned to the work after arriving in Matongé; she and Mbakam, a Cameroonian filmmaker who trained in Belgium, met through a mutual friend.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Tshidimba |first=Karin |date=15 May 2019 |title=« Chez Jolie coiffure » éclaire l'autre visage du quartier Matonge |trans-title="Chez Jolie Coiffure" sheds light on the other side of the Matongé district |url=https://afrique.lalibre.be/36400/chez-jolie-coiffure-eclaire-lautre-visage-du-quartier-matonge/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240304155508/https://afrique.lalibre.be/36400/chez-jolie-coiffure-eclaire-lautre-visage-du-quartier-matonge/ |archive-date=4 March 2024 |access-date=2 August 2026 |work=[[La Libre Belgique|La Libre Afrique]] |language=fr}}</ref>

''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' was Mbakam's second film after her debut, ''[[The Two Faces of a Bamiléké Woman]]'', which premiered in 2017. The film has been described as a piece of [[cinéma vérité]], in which Mbakam positions herself as an observer of the migrant women who work and use the salon, and their discussions of their personal issues as well as geopolitical matters. Mbakam utilises the salon's [[mirror]]s to film the women on a handheld camera in the 8m² size salon.<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":6" /> While she is not seen on camera, at several times during the film Sabine directly addresses Mbakam; at times, Mbakam asks Sabine questions.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":4" /> The women speak in [[French language|French]] and [[Cameroonian Pidgin English]].<ref name=":3" /> The camera shots are often crossed and blocked by hairdressers and clients moving around the salon.<ref name=":4" /> The salon was located in the [[Matongé Gallery]] in Ixelles.<ref name=":7" />

''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' was produced by Mbakam's company Tandor Productions and [[Geoffrey Cernaix]], who was also the film's editor. Mbakam served as the film's cinematographer and sound editor, alongside [[Loïc Villiot]].<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":8">{{Cite news |date=18 March 2021 |title=Quinoa fait son cinéma – 18/03 |trans-title=Quinoa Goes to the Movies – 18/03 |url=https://quinoa.be/quinoa-fait-son-cinema-1803/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250420222014/https://quinoa.be/quinoa-fait-son-cinema-1803/ |archive-date=20 April 2025 |access-date=2 August 2026 |work=Quinoa |language=fr}}</ref> It was filmed over the course of a year, solely inside Sabine's salon in Matongé.<ref name=":3" />

''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' was produced by [[Icarus Films]], in co-production with [[L'Atelier Cinema du Gsara]], [[WIP (film production)|WIP]], and [[Indigo Mood Films]].<ref name=":4" />

Shortly after the film's premiere, Sabine moved to [[Liège]], where she opened another salon, due to frequent immigration police raids in Matongé. In 2019, she received deportation papers from the Belgian government.<ref name=":5" />

== Release ==
The film is 70 minutes long.<ref name=":0" /> It had its world premiere at [[DOK Leipzig]] in 2018.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Icarus Films |url=https://icarusfilms.com/filmmakers?id=34 |access-date=2026-09-19 |website=icarusfilms.com}}</ref> It premiered at the [[Flagey Building]] in Brussels.<ref name=":6" /> It was released in 2019, screening at the True/False and AFI film festivals, as well as the Inncontro Festival.<ref name=":3" /><ref name=":4" /><ref name=":1" /> Its international sales were managed by [[Wallonie Image Production]].<ref name=":4" /> ''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' screened at the 2019 New York African Film Festival.<ref name=":2" />

In 2025, ''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' screened at the [[Brooklyn Academy of Music]]'s "The Black Worker" programme at its 2025 festival.<ref>{{Cite news |date=February 2025 |title=Chez Jolie Coiffure |url=https://www.bam.org/film/2025/the-black-worker-chez-jolie-coiffure |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260313172033/http://www.bam.org/film/2025/the-black-worker-chez-jolie-coiffure |archive-date=13 March 2026 |access-date=2 August 2026 |work=[[Brooklyn Academy of Music]]}}</ref>

== Reception ==
[[Guy Lodge]], writing for the American magazine ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', described ''Chez Jolie Coiffure'' as "bright, receptive and keen-eared", praising Mbakam for her "perceptive" filmmaking, including approaching the salon with "open eyes and ears".<ref name=":4" />

Nina Cottam praised the film for immersing audiences in the world of immigrant women and for proving that "we share more with these women than we could have imagined", as well as for putting names and faces to immigrants and therefore "decolonising" the audience's perspective.<ref name=":6" />

Véronique Fievet for the [[RTBF]] praised the film for showing the "solidarity" at the heart of the Matongé community, comparing it to the salon in the song "[[Chez Jeanne]]" by [[Georges Brassens|Brassens]].<ref name=":5" />

BX1 described the film as an "intimate documentary".<ref name=":7" />

''Quinoa'' called the film "essential" for understanding the immigrant experience "from the inside" and praised it as a "radiant documentary".<ref name=":8" />
