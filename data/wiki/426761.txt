'''''Wet Hot American Summer''''' is a 2001 American [[satirical]] [[comedy film]] directed by [[David Wain]] from a screenplay written by Wain and [[Michael Showalter]]. The film stars an [[ensemble cast]] that includes Showalter, [[Janeane Garofalo]], [[David Hyde Pierce]], [[Molly Shannon]], [[Paul Rudd]], [[Christopher Meloni]], [[Marguerite Moreau]], [[Ken Marino]], [[Michael Ian Black]], [[Zak Orth]], [[A. D. Miles]], [[Amy Poehler]], [[Bradley Cooper]] (in his film debut), [[Marisa Ryan]], [[Kevin Sussman]], [[Joe Lo Truglio]], and [[Elizabeth Banks]].

It takes place during the last full day at a fictional [[summer camp]] in 1981, and spoofs the [[Sex comedy|sex comedies]] aimed at teen audiences of that era.

The film was a critical and commercial failure, but it has since developed a [[Cult film|cult following]],<ref>{{cite web |last=Tobias |first=Scott |url=https://www.avclub.com/the-new-cult-canon-wet-hot-american-summer-1798214174 |title=The New Cult Canon: ''Wet Hot American Summer'' |work=[[The AV Club]] |date=June 11, 2008 |access-date=February 20, 2011 |archive-date=March 1, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110301053106/http://www.avclub.com/articles/the-new-cult-canon-wet-hot-american-summer,2341/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="cantor">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/aug/18/wet-hot-american-summer-film|title='There was a certain hostility': how Wet Hot American Summer went from miss to hit|last=Cantor|first=Matthew|website=[[The Guardian]]|date=August 18, 2026|access-date=August 18, 2026}}</ref> and many of its cast members have gone on to high-profile work. [[Netflix]] revived the [[Wet Hot American Summer (franchise)|franchise]] with the release of ''[[Wet Hot American Summer: First Day of Camp]]'', an eight-episode prequel series starring most of the film's original cast, on July 31, 2015; and ''[[Wet Hot American Summer: Ten Years Later]]'', an eight-episode sequel series, on August 4, 2017.

==Plot==
 <!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->         
The film shows the last day of the 1981 summer camp Camp Firewood, located near [[Waterville, Maine]], concentrating on the experiences of the camp counselors, most of whom are around 16. The interleaved story threads meet in the evening at the camp [[talent show]]:

Cook Gene, a [[Vietnam War]] veteran, clumsily tries to hide his various sexual fetishes. After being encouraged by a talking can of vegetables, he proudly admits them to the campers and counselors in front of the packed food hall, to great applause.

Zealous drama instructors Ben and Susie organize a talent show to be held on the last evening. Ben is in love with counselor McKinley. They have passionate sex and are later symbolically married by Beth in a ceremony by the lake. Beth forces Ben and Susie to give the last slot of their talent show to strange outsider Steve, who surprises everyone when, during his time on stage, an intense storm breaks out inside and outside the venue, created with the power of his mind. He receives a wild ovation.

Counselor Coop is secretly in love with counselor Katie, but she is dating Andy, who is obnoxious and inconsiderate and cheats on her with Lindsay. Katie decides to find a girl for Coop. When they spend time together during the day, Katie develops feelings for him. Eventually they kiss, but Katie returns to Andy and tells Coop they made a mistake.

A devastated Coop is found by Gene, who performs a rigorous training regimen with him. A newly buff Coop impresses Katie at the talent show and she declares her love for him. But when both bid farewell the next morning, she explains that she has returned to Andy again, even though she knows he is a terrible boyfriend, because he is very attractive and she is only interested in sex.

Camp director Beth is attracted to Henry, an associate professor of [[astrophysics]] who vacations near the camp. She asks him to come over and teach the campers about space. At first rudely rejecting her, he changes his mind and does science projects with a group of nerdy children, bonding with them and getting closer to Beth.

Henry and the children discover that a piece of [[Skylab]] will [[atmospheric entry|deorbit]] that evening and will probably impact the camp. They build a contraption to pinpoint the precise location: the talent show venue. Henry thinks he can modify the machine so that it can influence the path of the space debris. After he and the children use the modified machine near the talent show during the storm, the debris lands harmlessly on the grass.

Counselor Victor tries to give the impression that he is a ladies man, but is secretly a virgin. He is promised sex by the promiscuous Abby this night, but is told by Beth to go on a rafting trip with several campers and Neil. He abandons them at the river, steals the van and tries to return to Abby. He crashes the van, but reaches the camp on foot in the evening, where Abby has forgotten about him. When Neil and the children get lost on the river, he abandons them on the raft to look for Victor. He finds him at the camp and brings him back to the river, just in time to save the children.

Arts and crafts instructor Gail has a nervous breakdown during class. The children help her, during which she admits that she is not yet over her ex-husband Ron. With the help of the children, especially Aaron, she finds the courage to reject Ron when he arrives during the talent show and asks her to come back to him. The next morning, Gail and Aaron declare their love for each other.

===Development===
The film's financing took three years to assemble; in a June 2011 interview, Wain revealed the film's budget was $1.8&nbsp;million; he noted that during the 2001 [[Sundance Film Festival]],<ref>{{cite web |url=http://history.sundance.org/films/2216 |series=Archives |title=''Wet Hot American Summer'' |publisher=Sundance Institute |access-date=June 23, 2011 |archive-date=March 12, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120312005631/http://history.sundance.org/films/2216 |url-status=live }}</ref> the film had been promoted as costing $5&nbsp;million, in an attempt to attract a better offer from a distributor.<ref name="QandAGoldsmith" /><!-- 30 minutes into the interview --> Because of the film's relatively small budget, the cast was paid very little; Paul Rudd has stated that he is uncertain that he received any compensation at all for the film.<ref name="Collis 2015">{{cite magazine |url=http://www.ew.com/article/2011/06/24/wet-hot-american-summer-crazy-story-behind-cult-classic |title='Wet Hot American Summer': The crazy story behind the cult classic |first=Clark |last=Collis |magazine=Entertainment Weekly |date=July 30, 2015 |access-date=August 28, 2015 |archive-date=September 5, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150905095255/http://www.ew.com/article/2011/06/24/wet-hot-american-summer-crazy-story-behind-cult-classic |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] lasted 28 days in May 2000, and, according to director David Wain, it rained on every day of shooting.<ref name="QandAGoldsmith" /><!-- About 32 minutes into the interview --> Exterior shots were filmed when possible, sometimes under covers or umbrellas, but some scenes were moved indoors instead. In many interior scenes, rain seen outside turns into sun as soon as characters step outside. Due to the cold, the actors' breath can be seen in some outdoor scenes.<ref name="QandAGoldsmith"/> The film was shot at Camp Towanda in [[Honesdale, Pennsylvania]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.wethotamericansummer.com/credits.html |title=Full Credit List |website=Wet Hot American Summer official website |access-date=February 15, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110718034508/http://www.wethotamericansummer.com/credits.html |archive-date=July 18, 2011 |url-status=dead  }}</ref>

===Music===
As the film is set in the early 1980s, the film's soundtrack features songs from many popular bands of the era, most notably [[Jefferson Starship]], [[Rick Springfield]], [[Loverboy]], and [[Kiss (band)|Kiss]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Ihnat |first=Gwen |date=2015-07-29 |title=‘Wet Hot Annotated Summer’: An exhaustive guide to Camp Firewood |url=https://www.avclub.com/wet-hot-annotated-summer-an-exhaustive-guide-to-camp-f-1798283216 |access-date=21 November 2025 |newspaper=AV Club}}</ref>

==Release==
===Theatrical===
''Wet Hot American Summer'' premiered at the 2001 [[Sundance Film Festival]], where it was screened four times to sold-out crowds,<ref name="wethothist" /> though it failed to attract a distributor.<ref name="QandAGoldsmith" /> Months later, [[USA Films]] offered the filmmakers $100,000 for the film, with virtually no [[Participation (ownership)|participation]] for the filmmakers, an offer the film's investors accepted. It premiered in New York City on July 27, 2001, then received a [[Limited release|limited theatrical release]] in fewer than 30 cities.<ref name="wethothist" />

===Home media===
The film was released in both [[VHS]] and DVD formats on January 15, 2002 by [[USA Home Entertainment]].<ref name="wethothist" /> In 2011, Wain tried to convince [[Universal Studios, Inc.|Universal Studios]] to prepare either a 10th anniversary home video re-release with extra features, or a [[Blu-ray Disc|Blu-ray]] release, but Universal rejected the ideas. The film was released on Blu-ray on May 12, 2015.<ref>{{cite press release |date=April 13, 2015 |title=From Universal Pictures Home Entertainment: Wet Hot American Summer – UNIVERSAL CITY, Calif. |url=http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/from-universal-pictures-home-entertainment-wet-hot-american-summer-300064377.html |access-date=July 27, 2015 |agency=PR Newswire |archive-date=July 2, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150702220124/http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/from-universal-pictures-home-entertainment-wet-hot-american-summer-300064377.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception==
''Wet Hot American Summer'' received mostly negative reviews from critics. [[Rotten Tomatoes]] gives the film a rating of 39%, based on 77 reviews, with an average rating of 4.85/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Wet Hot American Summer''{{'}}s incredibly talented cast is too often outmatched by a deeply silly script that misses its targets at least as often as it skewers them."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/wet_hot_american_summer/?search=Wet%20hot |title=Wet Hot American Summer (2001) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=March 4, 2025|archive-date=December 24, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211224105539/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/wet_hot_american_summer?search=Wet%20hot |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Metacritic]] gives the film a score of 42 out of 100, based on 24 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/wet-hot-american-summer/critic-reviews |title=Wet Hot American Summer (2001) |publisher=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=June 1, 2013 |archive-date=October 11, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171011034000/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/wet-hot-american-summer/critic-reviews |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] rated the film with one star out of four. His review took the form of a tongue-in-cheek parody of [[Allan Sherman]]'s "[[Hello Muddah, Hello Fadduh]]".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/wet-hot-american-summer-2001 |title=''Wet Hot American Summer'' |series=[[Roger Ebert]] Reviews |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |date=August 31, 2001 |access-date=February 15, 2009 |archive-date=February 13, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230213000618/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/wet-hot-american-summer-2001 |url-status=live }}</ref>

In contrast, ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''{{'}}s [[Owen Gleiberman]] awarded the film an "A", and named it as one of the ten best films of the year.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gleiberman |first=Owen |date=July 25, 2001 |title=Wet Hot American Summer |url=https://ew.com/article/2001/07/25/wet-hot-american-summer/ |access-date=October 11, 2022 |website=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |language=en |archive-date=October 11, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221011003415/https://ew.com/article/2001/07/25/wet-hot-american-summer/ |url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[Newsweek]]''{{'}}s [[David Ansen]] also lauded it, calling it a "gloriously silly romp" that "made me laugh harder than any other movie this summer. Make that this year."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.newsweek.com/summer-camp-151629 |title=Summer Camp |last=Ansen |first=David |date=August 26, 2001 |work=[[Newsweek]] |access-date=October 10, 2022 |archive-date=October 10, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221010064102/https://www.newsweek.com/summer-camp-151629 |url-status=live }}</ref> The film has gone on to achieve a [[cult following]].<ref name="Collis 2015" />

Actress [[Kristen Bell]] stated on [[NPR]] on September 2, 2012, that ''Wet Hot American Summer'' was her favorite film, having watched it "hundreds of times."<ref>{{Cite news |date=September 1, 2012 |title=The Movie Kristen Bell Has 'Seen A Million Times' |language=en |work=[[NPR]] |url=https://www.npr.org/2012/09/01/160046980/the-movie-kristen-bell-has-seen-a-million-times |access-date=2022-10-10 |archive-date=October 10, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221010064102/https://www.npr.org/2012/09/01/160046980/the-movie-kristen-bell-has-seen-a-million-times |url-status=live }}</ref> NPR host [[Jesse Thorn]] said on the April 29, 2014 episode of [[Bullseye with Jesse Thorn|''Bullseye'']]: {{cquote|When someone has an open enough heart to accept this silliness{{emdash}}and that's what it's about for me, an open heart{{emdash}}if someone's heart is open to ''Wet Hot American Summer'', they love it. And that's when I know that me and them, we've got an unbreakable bond. Together forever. Like camp counselors.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.maximumfun.org/bullseye/bullseye-jesse-thorn-jim-rash-bob-saget-jessica-st-clair-and-lennon-parham |title=Bullseye with Jesse Thorn: Jim Rash, Bob Saget, Jessica St. Clair and Lennon Parham |website=Maximum Fun |date=April 29, 2014 |access-date=July 27, 2015 |archive-date=September 6, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150906065428/http://www.maximumfun.org/bullseye/bullseye-jesse-thorn-jim-rash-bob-saget-jessica-st-clair-and-lennon-parham |url-status=live }}</ref>}}

In July 2025, it was one of the films voted for the "Readers' Choice" edition of ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century," finishing at number 285.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=[[The New York Times]] |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref>

===Undeveloped TV series===
During a 2015 interview with ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', Wain and Showalter stated that they wrote a pilot for a possible [[Fox Broadcasting Company|Fox]] television series based on the film. Wain described the series as a "22-minute Fox sitcom with commercials and nothing Rated R, so it was a little bit odd."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2015/tv/news/wet-hot-american-summer-netflix-david-wain-michael-showalter-1201549486/|title='Wet Hot American Summer': Oral History Details False Starts, Faking Camp Firewood|first=Julie|last=Seabaugh|date=July 29, 2015|work=Variety|access-date=December 10, 2017|archive-date=May 19, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180519181210/https://variety.com/2015/tv/news/wet-hot-american-summer-netflix-david-wain-michael-showalter-1201549486/|url-status=live}}</ref> The pilot was not picked up for a series.

===Documentary===
Alongside the prequel series, a making-of documentary, ''[[Hurricane of Fun: The Making of Wet Hot]]'', was released on Netflix on July 24, 2015, consisting of behind-the-scenes interviews and footage shot during the filming of the movie.
