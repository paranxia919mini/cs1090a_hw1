'''''Megalopolis'''''{{efn|Also referred to as '''''Francis Ford Coppola's Megalopolis: A Fable'''''<ref name="Chang">{{cite magazine |url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/05/27/megalopolis-movie-review-francis-ford-coppola |title=The Madly Captivating Urban Sprawl of Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' |last=Chang |first=Justin |author-link=Justin Chang |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |date=May 16, 2024 |access-date=July 8, 2024 |url-access=limited |archive-date=July 8, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240708235944/https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/05/27/megalopolis-movie-review-francis-ford-coppola |url-status=live}}</ref>}} is a 2024 American [[Epic film|epic]] [[Science fiction film|science fiction]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] written, directed, and produced by [[Francis Ford Coppola]]. The film features an [[ensemble cast]] including [[Adam Driver]], [[Giancarlo Esposito]], [[Nathalie Emmanuel]], [[Aubrey Plaza]], [[Shia LaBeouf]], [[Jon Voight]], [[Laurence Fishburne]], [[Talia Shire]], [[Jason Schwartzman]], [[Kathryn Hunter]], [[Grace VanderWaal]] in her feature film debut, [[Chloe Fineman]], [[James Remar]], [[D. B. Sweeney]], and [[Dustin Hoffman]]. Set in an [[Alternate history|alternate]] 21st-century [[New York City]] (restyled "New Rome"), the story follows visionary architect Cesar Catilina (Driver) as he clashes with the corrupt Mayor Franklyn Cicero (Esposito), who opposes Catilina's plans to revitalize New Rome by building the futuristic utopia "Megalopolis". The film draws on Roman history, particularly the [[Catilinarian conspiracy]] of 63 BC and the decay of the [[Roman Republic]] into the [[Roman Empire]].

In 1977, Coppola had the idea to make a film drawing parallels between the fall of the Roman Republic and the future of the United States by retelling the Catilinarian conspiracy in modern New York. Although he began plotting the film in 1983, the project spent decades in [[development hell]]. Coppola attempted to produce the film in 1989 and again in 2001, but each time, the studios refused to finance the film, due to Coppola's string of late-career box-office disappointments and the [[September 11 attacks]], respectively. Disillusioned by the studio system, Coppola did not produce ''Megalopolis'' until he built a large fortune in the winemaking business. He spent $120{{nbsp}}million of his own money to make the film. Principal photography took place in [[Georgia (U.S. state)|Georgia]] from November 2022 to March 2023.

The film reunited Coppola with past collaborators, including actors Esposito, Fishburne, Remar, Shire, and Sweeney, cinematographer [[Mihai Mălaimare Jr.]], composer [[Osvaldo Golijov]], and Coppola's son, second-unit director [[Roman Coppola]]. Like several other Coppola films, ''Megalopolis'' had a troubled production. Coppola adopted an experimental style, encouraging his actors to improvise and write certain scenes during the shoot, and adding his own last-minute changes to the script. Members of the art department and visual effects team, among others, left or were fired from the film.

''Megalopolis'' was selected to compete for the [[Palme d'Or]] at the [[77th Cannes Film Festival]], but polarized critics and Hollywood studios. Coppola could not find a studio that would both reimburse his production costs and pay for a large marketing campaign. He opted to pay for an advertising campaign, with [[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]] theatrically releasing the film in the United States. It endured a troubled run-up to release: a trailer was removed for using fabricated [[pull quote]]s, and Coppola sued trade publication ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' for libel after it published allegations of sexual misconduct by him on set. The film premiered at Cannes on May 16, 2024, and was released theatrically on September 27, 2024. It was a [[Box-office bomb|commercial failure]], grossing $14.4{{nbsp}}million against a budget of $120 to $136 million.<ref>Multiple sources:

== Plot ==
In an alternate United States, New Rome is dominated by an elite group of patrician families. Although the Roman elite profess to live by a strict moral code, patricians decadently enjoy forbidden pleasures while ordinary Romans live in poverty.

Patrician architect Cesar Catilina is one of New Rome's leading figures. Cesar wins the [[Nobel Prize]] for inventing the revolutionary building material Megalon. In addition, he can secretly stop time. Despite Cesar's worldly success, he has fallen into alcoholism. Years earlier, his wife disappeared, and District Attorney Franklyn Cicero prosecuted him for murdering her. Although Cesar was acquitted, he remains crushed by guilt, believing his wife took her own life because he was too focused on his work. Cesar pines for his wife, prompting his jealous mistress, TV presenter Wow Platinum, to leave him.

At a televised event, Cesar and now-Mayor Cicero offer different visions for the city's future. Cesar proposes using Megalon to build "Megalopolis", a utopian urbanist community, while Cicero argues that a casino will provide immediate tax revenue. Cesar meets Cicero's well-read but purposeless daughter Julia. Earlier, she watched Cesar stop time while a building was being demolished but was still able to move. Initially, they dislike each other, but Cesar impresses Julia with his vision for Megalopolis, explaining that New Rome needs a grand artistic vision to free itself from its political inertia and unlock its full potential. Later, when Cesar cannot stop time as he once could, Julia encourages him to do so for her. They discover they can stop time when together, and the two become lovers.

Wow marries Cesar's uncle Hamilton Crassus III, the world's richest man. Although Crassus likes his nephew, his mind and health are in decline, and he is easily manipulated by individuals like his other nephew, Clodio Pulcher, who wants to inherit the Crassus bank. Crassus and Wow throw a decadent wedding reception. The headliner is teenage pop star Vesta Sweetwater, who appeals to New Rome's puritanical sensibilities by promising to remain chaste until marriage. To neutralize Cesar, Pulcher leaks a paparazzi video of Cesar having sex with Vesta, prompting Cicero to condemn Cesar. Although Cicero arrests Cesar for statutory rape, Julia exonerates Cesar by discovering that Vesta faked her age and is actually in her twenties.

[[Soviet]] satellite Carthage crashes to Earth, destroying much of New Rome. Cesar begins building Megalopolis in the ruins, financing the project with his family fortune. In a press conference, he makes the case for bold artistic projects that show people that a better world is possible. However, the cost of building Megalopolis contrasts with poverty on the streets. Pulcher becomes a populist politician, encouraging ordinary Romans to oppose Megalopolis as an expensive folly. The allure of power draws Pulcher from populism to fascist demagoguery.

A pregnant Julia tries to broker peace between Cesar and Cicero by taking her father to see the Megalopolis construction site, but Cicero is unimpressed with Cesar's utopianism, and he begs Cesar to leave Julia. In exchange, he offers Cesar valuable blackmail material: a confession that Cicero knew Cesar's wife committed suicide and maliciously prosecuted Cesar anyway. Cesar declines. Pulcher's right-hand man hires an assassin to kill Cesar, but Cesar's doctors use Megalon to rebuild his skull.

Wow tries to force Cesar to leave Julia and marry her by freezing Cesar's account at the Crassus bank. She enlists Pulcher to manipulate Crassus into handing over control of the bank. When Crassus learns of Pulcher's duplicity, he has a stroke and collapses. Pulcher and Wow taunt the bedridden Crassus, but Crassus kills Wow and injures Pulcher with a hidden bow and arrow.

Rioting Pulcher supporters attempt to storm Megalopolis and City Hall. Cesar confronts the rioters, pleading with them to believe in his vision of a better future, while Cicero watches. Cesar's speech inspires the crowd, who go on to hang Pulcher upside down.

With renewed financial support from Crassus, Cesar finally completes Megalopolis. Cicero, holding Julia and Cesar's baby daughter, Sunny Hope, promises to help Cesar build a better future. On New Year's Eve, Julia stops time, but only Sunny seems unaffected.

== Production ==
=== Development ===
[[File:Francis Ford Coppola, Director (05) (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|alt=Filmmaker Francis Ford Coppola in 1973.|Writer, director, and producer [[Francis Ford Coppola]] in 1973]]

Growing up in New York City, [[Francis Ford Coppola]] was fascinated by [[science fiction film]]s, such as [[Fritz Lang]]'s ''[[Metropolis (1927 film)|Metropolis]]'' (1927) and [[William Cameron Menzies]]'s ''[[Things to Come]]'' (1936), and the scientific community's history with dangerous experiments.<ref name="VanityFair-043024">{{cite web |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/megalopolis-francis-ford-coppola-first-look-exclusive |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'': An Exclusive First Look at the Director's Retro-Futurist Epic |last=Breznican |first=Anthony |author-link=Anthony Breznican |website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |date=April 30, 2024 |access-date=April 30, 2024 |url-access=limited |archive-date=April 30, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240430075913/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/megalopolis-francis-ford-coppola-first-look-exclusive |url-status=live}}</ref> His reading of the Roman historian [[Sallust]] and [[William Bolitho Ryall]]'s book ''[[Twelve Against the Gods]]'' (1929) inspired him to make a film about [[Lucius Sergius Catiline]], a populist accused by Roman consul [[Marcus Tullius Cicero]] of conspiring to overthrow the [[Roman Republic]] in 63 BC.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|7}} Coppola conceived the overall idea for ''Megalopolis'' towards the end of filming ''[[Apocalypse Now]]'' (1979) in 1977.<ref name="Romberger"/>{{rp|50}} Sound designer [[Richard Beggs]] described Coppola's vision as an opera screened over four nights, similar to [[Richard Wagner]]'s [[Der Ring des Nibelungen|''Ring'' cycle]] (1876) in [[Bayreuth]], in a "gigantic outdoor purpose-built theatre".<ref name="TheGuardian2024"/> Coppola hoped to have it in "some place as close as possible to the geographical center of the United States",<ref>{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/apocalypsenowboo0000cowi/page/180/mode/2up?view=theater |title=The ''Apocalypse Now'' Book |last=Cowie |first=Peter |author-link=Peter Cowie |publisher=[[Da Capo Press]] |date=April 20, 2001 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=0-306-81046-8 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|181}} like the [[Red Rocks Amphitheatre]] in the state of Colorado.<ref name="TheGuardian2024"/>

Coppola devoted the beginning of 1983 to developing the film; in two months, he assembled four hundred pages of notes and script fragments.<ref name="Schumacher1999">{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/francisfordcoppo0000schu/page/332/mode/2up?view=theater |title=Francis Ford Coppola: A Filmmaker's Life |last=Schumacher |first=Michael |publisher=[[Crown Publishing Group]] |date=October 19, 1999 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=0-517-70445-5 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|333}} Over the next four decades, he collected notes and newspaper [[Clipping (publications)|clippings]] for scrapbooks detailing intriguing subjects he envisioned incorporating into a future screenplay, like political cartoons and different historical subjects, before deciding to make a Roman epic film set in an imagined modern America.<ref name="VanityFair-043024"/><ref name="Empire-Aug29"/> In mid-1983, he described the plot as taking place in one day in New York City with Catiline-era Rome as a backdrop, similar to how [[James Joyce]]'s ''[[Ulysses (novel)|Ulysses]]'' (1922) used [[Homer]] in the context of modern [[Dublin]] and how he updated the setting of [[Joseph Conrad]]'s ''[[Heart of Darkness]]'' (1899) from the late 1800s amid [[Colonisation of Africa|European colonial rule in Africa]] to the 1970s [[Vietnam War]] for ''Apocalypse Now''.<ref name="VanityFair-043024"/><ref>{{cite journal |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/43452924.pdf |title=Idols of the King |last1=Thomson |first1=David |last2=Gray |first2=Lucy |journal=[[Film Comment]] |date=September–October 1983 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |volume=19 |number=5 |pages=61–75 |jstor=43452924}}</ref>{{rp|74}}<ref>{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/hollywoodauteurf0000chow/page/214/mode/2up?view=theater |title=Hollywood Auteur: Francis Coppola |last=Chown |first=Jeffrey |publisher=Praeger |date=May 20, 1988 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=0-275-92910-8 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|215}}

In January 1989, Coppola announced his plans to move to Italy to work on two productions in the next five years, including ''Le Ribellion di Catilina'', a film "so big and complicated it would seem impossible", which biographer Michael Schumacher said "sounded much like what he had in store for ''Megalopolis''".<ref name="Schumacher1999"/>{{rp|409–410}} It was to be shot in [[Cinecittà]], a film studio in Rome, where production designer [[Dean Tavoularis]] and his design team built offices and an art studio for [[drafter]]s to [[storyboard]] the film.<ref name="Clarke2003">{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/coppola0000clar/page/262/mode/2up?view=theater |title=Virgin Film: Coppola |last=Clarke |first=James |author-link=James Clarke (author) |publisher=Virgin Pub |date=January 1, 2003 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=0-7535-0866-4 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|266}}<ref>{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/coppola0000unse/page/234/mode/2up?view=theater |title=Coppola: A Biography |last=Cowie |first=Peter |author-link=Peter Cowie |publisher=[[Da Capo Press]] |date=August 22, 1994 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=978-0-306-80598-1 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|234}} ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' described it as "swing[ing] from the past to the present", merging "the images of Rome{{nbsp}}... with the New York of today". Of it, Coppola said, "I want to be free. I don't want producers around me telling me what to do". Coppola ultimately did not move to Italy, and instead made ''[[The Godfather Part III]]'' (1990).<ref name="Schumacher1999"/>{{rp|410}} Following the 1990–91 [[film awards season]] for that film, Coppola's production company, [[American Zoetrope]], announced several projects in development, including plans to film ''Megalopolis'' in 1991, despite lacking a finished script.<ref name="Schumacher1999"/>{{rp|436}} However, the film was postponed to "no earlier than 1996" after Coppola found himself prioritizing other projects, including ''[[Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992 film)|Bram Stoker's Dracula]]'' (1992), ''[[Jack (1996 film)|Jack]]'' (1996), and ''[[The Rainmaker (1997 film)|The Rainmaker]]'' (1997), to get out of debt accumulated from the box-office failures of ''[[One from the Heart]]'' (1982) and ''[[Tucker: The Man and His Dream]]'' (1988).<ref name="Schumacher1999"/>{{rp|444}}<ref>{{cite book |url=https://archive.org/details/godfatherbook0000cowi/page/110/mode/2up?view=theater |title=The ''Godfather'' Book |last=Cowie |first=Peter |author-link=Peter Cowie |publisher=[[Gardners Books]] |date=January 1, 1997 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |isbn=0-571-19011-1 |url-access=registration}}</ref>{{rp|110}}<ref name="AICN">{{cite web |url=http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/32584 |title=Harry sits down in Austin with Francis Ford Coppola and talks ''Youth Without Youth'', Seventies film, Wine, ''Tetro'', and the Coppolas |last=Knowles |first=Harry |author-link=Harry Knowles |website=[[Ain't It Cool News]] |date=May 8, 2007 |access-date=November 14, 2022 |archive-date=November 14, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221114021855/http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/32584 |url-status=live}}</ref>

In a 1992 diary entry, Coppola figured he should only make films he had "a burning desire to make", preferably in the [[Ingmar Bergman#Style of working|independent-esque manner of Ingmar Bergman]], Production on the film eventually halted. The success of Coppola's [[Francis Ford Coppola#Francis Ford Coppola Presents|winery and resorts]] meant he could produce it with his own money, which his friend [[Wendy Doniger]] said "paralyzed him". "He had no excuse this time if the film was no good", she remarked, "What froze him was having the power to do exactly what he wanted so that his soul was on the line".<ref name="Time">{{cite magazine |url=https://content.time.com/time/arts/article/0,8599,1681652-2,00.html |title=Coppola, Take 2 |last=Keegan |first=Rebecca |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |date=November 7, 2007 |access-date=April 1, 2024 |archive-date=April 1, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240401161534/https://content.time.com/time/arts/article/0,8599,1681652-2,00.html |url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2005, she bestowed him books that she deemed thematically relevant, including [[Mircea Eliade]]'s ''[[Youth Without Youth (novella)|Youth Without Youth]]'' (1976), a novella about a 70-year-old man struggling to complete an ambitious project. The world was attacked and I didn't know how to try to do with that {{sic}}. I tried".<ref name="AICN"/> In 2009, in regards to the likelihood of revisiting the film, he said: "Someday, I'll read what I had on ''Megalopolis'' and maybe I'll think different of it, but it's also a movie that costs a lot of money to make and there's not a patron out there. You see what the studios are making right now".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://movieline.com/2009/06/francis-ford-coppola-to-movieline-godfather-never-should-have-had-more-than-one-movie.php |title=Francis Ford Coppola to Movieline: 'Godfather Never Should Have Had More Than One Movie' |last=Buchanan |first=Kyle |website=[[Movieline]] |date=June 3, 2009 |access-date=March 29, 2024 |archive-date=June 11, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090611150105/http://movieline.com/2009/06/francis-ford-coppola-to-movieline-godfather-never-should-have-had-more-than-one-movie.php |url-status=dead}}</ref>

The film's literary influences included the books ''[[Bullshit Jobs]]'' (2018), ''[[The Dawn of Everything]]'' (2021), and ''[[Debt: The First 5000 Years]]'' (2011) by [[David Graeber]]; ''[[The Chalice and the Blade]]'' (1987) by [[Riane Eisler]]; ''Directors on Directing'' (1963), edited by Toby Cole and [[Helen Krich Chinoy]], particularly an entry by [[Elia Kazan]] on his approach to ''[[A Streetcar Named Desire (1951 film)|A Streetcar Named Desire]]'' (1951); ''[[Dream of the Red Chamber]]'' by [[Cao Xueqin]]; ''[[Elective Affinities]]'' (1809) by [[Johann Wolfgang von Goethe]]; ''[[The Glass Bead Game]]'' (1943) by [[Hermann Hesse]]; ''[[The Origins of Political Order]]'' (2011) by [[Francis Fukuyama]]; ''[[The Swerve]]'' (2011) by [[Stephen Greenblatt]]; ''[[To the Lighthouse]]'' (1927) by [[Virginia Woolf]]; ''Twelve Against the Gods'' (1929) by [[William Bolitho Ryall]]; and ''[[The War Lovers]]'' (2010) by [[Evan Thomas]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.gq.com/story/francis-ford-coppolas-megalopolis-might-actually-come-out-this-year |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' Might Actually Come Out This Year. Based on Catiline, the character of Cesar was renamed at classicist [[Mary Beard (classicist)|Mary Beard]]'s suggestion that [[Julius Caesar]] had ties with Catiline and was more known among audiences. The character was inspired by [[Robert Moses]], as portrayed in [[Robert Caro]]'s biography ''[[The Power Broker]]'' (1974), and architects [[Norman Bel Geddes]], [[Walter Gropius]], [[Raymond Loewy]], and [[Frank Lloyd Wright]]. Coppola researched the [[Claus von Bülow]] murder case, the [[Mary Cunningham Agee|Mary Cunningham]]-[[William Agee]] [[Bendix Corporation]] scandal, the emergence of [[New York Stock Exchange]] reporter [[Maria Bartiromo]], the history of [[Studio 54]], and [[Felix Rohatyn]]'s solution for the [[New York City fiscal crisis of 1975]].<ref name="VanityFair-043024"/> The fictional building material of Megalon was based in part on the work of architect and designer [[Neri Oxman]], who appears as "Dr. Lyra Shir" and was credited as the architectural and scientific advisor. Having worked with Coppola on studies for ''Megalopolis'', her four-part model installation ''Man-Nahāta'' displays a potential future design of Manhattan.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|10; 35}} In line with ''The Godfather'' and ''Dracula'', where he credited [[Mario Puzo]] and [[Bram Stoker]] as the original writers, Coppola branded the film with his name as ''Francis Ford Coppola's Megalopolis''.<ref name="VanityFair-043024"/>

The COVID-19 pandemic delayed the start of production. Before filming began, a week of rehearsals took place with theater-style exercises, much like the one Emmanuel described having in her audition.<ref name="THRMay2024"/> Coppola said he could only rehearse with around a third of the cast, including Plaza and Emmanuel, but notably not Driver, and thus cast [[understudies]] in place of absent members.<ref name="RollingStone-0825"/><ref name="IndieWire-Sept16">{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/francis-ford-coppola-on-megalopolis-1235045897/|title=How Francis Ford Coppola Returned to 'Student' Filmmaking to Pave the Way for ''Megalopolis''|date=September 16, 2024|first=Jim|last=Hemphill|website=[[IndieWire]]|access-date=September 16, 2024|archive-date=October 7, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007030952/https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/francis-ford-coppola-on-megalopolis-1235045897/|url-status=live}}</ref> Fineman, however, recalled rehearsing on [[Halloween]] with Plaza, Driver, Hoffman, and Voight, playing improvisational games with Coppola that encouraged character immersion.<ref name="FinemanCBR"/> Plaza described the workshop-style approach as a collaborative process where actors could improvise and offer input on the script. She recalled working closely with Voight and LaBeouf, writing scenes and passing them to the script supervisor, who would then present them to Coppola. If he approved, the material would sometimes be incorporated into the film.<ref name="DeadlineMay2024"/> One such scene that the actors composed was the part where Plaza's "Wow Platinum" orders LaBeouf's "Clodio Pulcher" to "[[oral sex|orally pleasure]] her as he calls her Auntie Wow", building off an idea of Coppola's.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/29/movies/aubrey-plaza-megalopolis-my-old-ass-agatha-all-along.html|url-access=limited|title=Have We Gotten Aubrey Plaza Wrong This Whole Time?|date=September 29, 2024|first=Kyle|last=Buchanan|website=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=September 30, 2024|archive-date=October 1, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241001195107/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/29/movies/aubrey-plaza-megalopolis-my-old-ass-agatha-all-along.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Coppola described LaBeouf as an actor who intentionally creates tension with directors, often pushing it to an extreme. He found LaBeouf's approach frustrating and irrational, likening it to [[Dennis Hopper]]'s preparations for ''Apocalypse Now''.<ref name="RollingStone-0825"/><ref name="TheTelegraph-Sept13"/>

For the production of ''Megalopolis'', Coppola purchased a [[Days Inn]] drive-in motel in [[Peachtree City, Georgia]], for $4.35{{nbsp}}million to house the crew and his extended family. He renovated the property to include rehearsal and [[post-production]] facilities, which increased the tax incentives available to the project and reportedly reduced the overall production expenditure to $107{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="WSJ-Sept26"/> The site was later opened to the public on July 5, 2024, as the All-Movie Hotel, a hybrid hospitality and filmmaking venue serving as the first U.S. location in Coppola's Hideaway hotel collection and fifth overall. The All-Movie Hotel features 27 suites and rooms personally designed by Coppola, along with a range of production facilities intended for projects of varying scales. These include modular post-production spaces such as two edit suites with laser projection and [[Meyer Sound]] 2.1 monitoring, two additional edit bays, [[Automated dialogue replacement|ADR]] recording capabilities, and a screening room equipped for [[Dolby Atmos]] 9.1.6 sound mixing. The property also houses offices, a wardrobe fitting room, a gym, a swimming pool, and an [[Sound stage|insert stage]]. Coppola's own residential suite from the production of ''Megalopolis'' is available for guests to book, and memorabilia from his past films is displayed throughout the hotel.<ref name="AP-Hotel">{{cite web |url=https://apnews.com/article/coppola-megalopolis-allmovie-hotel-georgia-film-e25192debab6c98c489a5555a2d73d60 |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' wrapped at this Georgia hotel. === Alleged conflicts on set ===
==== Creative differences ====
Plaza spoke about Coppola's spontaneous approach to filmmaking, explaining that he would often come up with new ideas on the spot. At times, this resulted in sudden location changes and unexpected shifts in the day's shooting plans, leaving actors unaware of what to expect as the day unfolded.<ref name="DeadlineMay2024"/> Some crew members described that approach as "exasperating" and "old-school", as Coppola was hesitant to decide how the film would look and would spend work days completing shots practically instead of relying on digital techniques. A crew member recounted that Coppola frequently arrived on set in the mornings before major sequences without a clear plan. Since he refused to let his collaborators establish one, he often spent hours in his trailer, avoiding communication and reportedly smoking marijuana. When he eventually emerged, he would improvise scenes that disregarded prior discussions and the script, leaving the crew to adapt. On Driver's first day on set, Coppola allegedly took six hours to achieve an effect by projecting an image on the side of Driver's head.<ref name="TheGuardian2024"/> Coppola denied smoking marijuana on set, adding that he had been avoiding the substance ever since his weight loss in 2017.<ref name="WSJ-Sept26"/>

On December 9, 2022, Coppola fired most of the [[visual effects]] team, with the rest of the department, including supervisor Mark Russell, soon following. In January 2023, reports indicated the budget ballooned higher than its initial $120{{nbsp}}million, which ''The Hollywood Reporter'' compared to Coppola's history of challenging productions, most notoriously ''Apocalypse Now''. Due to an alleged "unstable filming environment", a claim Coppola and Driver contested, other crew members exited the film, including production designer Beth Mickle and art director David Scott, along with the [[art department]].<ref name="Budget"/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2023/01/francis-ford-coppola-no-truth-to-apocalypse-on-megalopolis-1235216222/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola: No Truth to Apocalypse on ''Megalopolis'' |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=January 10, 2023 |access-date=January 10, 2023 |archive-date=January 10, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230110203401/https://deadline.com/2023/01/francis-ford-coppola-no-truth-to-apocalypse-on-megalopolis-1235216222/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Coppola replaced Russell with his nephew, Jesse James Chisholm, over a dispute over "live special effects", which he completed with his son and second unit director [[Roman Coppola]] as they had with ''Bram Stoker's Dracula''.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|9}}

Coppola further explained that he hired Mickle for her work in recreating 1950s New York for [[Edward Norton]]'s ''[[Motherless Brooklyn]]'' (2019) but that creative differences formed "to a degree that it was decided that the best thing would be if I hired a concept artist and came up with frames that showed what I wanted, which I did".<ref name="Empire-Aug29"/><ref name="WSJ-Sept26"/> Working with concept artist Dean Sherriff to translate his vision through [[keyframe]] concept art, which he described as similar in style to that of a woven mural or tapestry, he permitted minimal input from the art department, whose practices, having recently completed the [[Marvel Cinematic Universe]] film ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3]]'' (2023), he deemed conventional, expensive, and hierarchical.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|8}}<ref name="WSJ-Sept26"/> "The art department was frustrated because they felt I was evolving the look of the picture independently of them", he said, "They wanted giant sets and images. I wanted other elements like costumes and live effects to do some of the work and have it not all be art-department-centric. So, there was disagreement along those lines".<ref name="Empire-Aug29"/> Coppola had originally set $100{{nbsp}}million for the budget and $20{{nbsp}}million as [[Cost contingency|contingency]]. As the budget was at risk of rising to $148{{nbsp}}million (which he said would have "bankrupted me and my family"), he laid off one of five art directors, leading the entire team to resign. Production allegedly wrapped a week ahead of schedule, with the budget close to the planned $120{{nbsp}}million, according to Coppola.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|8–9}}<ref name="RollingStone-0825"/> In contrast, one person involved with the film told ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'' that production costs ultimately reached $136{{nbsp}}million.<ref name="WSJ-Sept26"/>

=== Post-production ===
{{See also|Megalopolis (soundtrack)}}

The son of a classical musician who wrote pieces for ''The Godfather'' and ''Apocalypse Now'', Coppola wanted a non-Hollywood composer. In March 2003, he hand-wrote a letter to Argentinian composer [[Osvaldo Golijov]], and afterward invited him to his home to discuss ''Megalopolis'', asking him to compose a [[symphony]] that would have dictated the film's rhythm.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|20}}<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/features/osvaldo-golijov-composing-the-soundtrack-for-a-coppola-movie-345398.html |title=Osvaldo Golijov: Composing the soundtrack for a Coppola movie |last=Church |first=Michael |website=[[The Independent]] |date=February 14, 2006 |access-date=April 1, 2024 |archive-date=April 1, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240401154233/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/features/osvaldo-golijov-composing-the-soundtrack-for-a-coppola-movie-345398.html |url-status=live}}</ref> They would go on to collaborate on ''Youth Without Youth'', ''Tetro'', and ''Twixt'' before returning to complete ''Megalopolis''. Golijov wanted the score to "blur the line between music and sound design." Given the ambiguity surrounding how the city and music of Rome sounded, he relied on Hollywood portrayals and composed a Roman [[Suite (music)|suite]] inspired by [[Miklós Rózsa]]'s [[Ben-Hur (1959 film)#Musical score|score]] for ''[[Ben-Hur (1959 film)|Ben-Hur]]'' (1959). Coppola also asked Golijov to write a [[Love song|love theme]] in the vein of [[Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky]]'s composition ''[[Romeo and Juliet (Tchaikovsky)|Romeo and Juliet]]'' (1870).<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|20–21}} In February 2024, Coppola recorded the score with the Budapest Art Orchestra in Budapest, Hungary.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://hungarytoday.hu/director-francis-ford-coppola-makes-a-splash-during-his-stay-in-budapest/ |title=Director Francis Ford Coppola Makes a Splash during Stay in Budapest |website=Hungary Today |date=February 18, 2024 |access-date=May 18, 2024 |archive-date=May 18, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240518155557/https://hungarytoday.hu/director-francis-ford-coppola-makes-a-splash-during-his-stay-in-budapest/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The suite was eventually played in concert by the [[Chicago Symphony Orchestra]] conducted by [[Riccardo Muti]] on November 7-9, 2024.<ref>[https://news.wttw.com/2024/11/11/maestro-muti-and-cso-soar-brilliant-world-premiere-and-4-exhilarating-classics-review Maestro Muti and the CSO Soar With a Brilliant World Premiere and 4 Exhilarating Classics: Review|Chicago News|WTTW]</ref><ref>[https://cso.org/experience/article/20579/osvaldo-golijov-eager-to-hear-the-incredible Osvaldo Golijov eager to hear ‘the incredible CSO’ in his Megalopolis Suite – Chicago Symphony Orchestra]</ref>

Post-production, from editing to visual effects, was completed at the All-Movie Hotel, where some reshoots were held.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2024/artisans/news/francis-ford-coppola-all-movie-hotel-georgia-1236062350/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola Opens Hotel for Filmmakers and Public in Georgia With On-Site Post-Production Facilities (Exclusive) |date=July 10, 2024 |first=Jazz |last=Tangcay |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=August 21, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/09/09/is-a-stay-at-francis-ford-coppolas-hotel-an-offer-you-cant-refuse |url-access=limited |title=Is a Stay at Francis Ford Coppola's Hotel an Offer You Can't Refuse? |date=September 2, 2024 |first=Charles |last=Bethea |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |access-date=September 2, 2024 |archive-date=September 24, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924060953/https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/09/09/is-a-stay-at-francis-ford-coppolas-hotel-an-offer-you-cant-refuse |url-status=live}}</ref> Cam McLauchlin and [[Glen Scantlebury]] edited the film, Coppola having contacted McLauchlin after seeing his work on ''[[Nightmare Alley (2021 film)|Nightmare Alley]]'' (2021).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/ontario-boom-hollywood-collaboration-dgc-tiff-1235981761/ |title=Ontario's Behind-the-Camera Talent Boom Sparks Hollywood Collaborations With Global Impact |date=September 7, 2024 |first=Etan |last=Vlessing |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=September 7, 2024|archive-date=September 7, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240907181754/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/ontario-boom-hollywood-collaboration-dgc-tiff-1235981761/|url-status=live}}</ref> McLauchlin defined Coppola's style as theatrical, incorporating theater warm-up techniques. During rehearsal, Cesar and Julia improvised a [[tug-of-war]] with an imaginary rope, a spontaneous moment encouraging them to embrace the script's eccentricity. McLauchlin and Scantlebury were tasked to work on scenes independently but transitioned toward collaboration after realizing they had enough time to keep pace with shooting and experiment with alternate versions. As they edited the film, Coppola was inspired to direct more "unusual takes" of the cast to include.<ref name="Collider-Sept25">{{cite web |url=https://collider.com/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-meaning/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola Reveals the Real Message Behind ''Megalopolis'' |last1=Weisman |first1=Jake |last2=Weintraub |first2=Steven |website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]] |date=September 25, 2024 |access-date=September 26, 2024 |archive-date=October 7, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007031137/https://collider.com/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-meaning/ |url-status=live}}</ref> For a catwalk scene, Coppola mitigated disruptive noise by pre-recording the dialogue and playing it over a loudspeaker for wide shots. As an apparent warm-up exercise, he then had Driver recite the full "[[To be, or not to be]]" soliloquy from Shakespeare's ''[[Hamlet]]'' (1623).<ref name="Empire-Aug29">{{cite web |url=https://gb.readly.com/magazines/empire/2024-08-29/66d07850ff4b9bc5768172af|url-access=limited |title=Building the future |via=[[Readly]] |date=August 29, 2024 |first=Ian |last=Freer|author-link=Ian Freer |website=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|access-date=September 2, 2024|archive-date=September 7, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240907181734/https://gb.readly.com/magazines/empire/2024-08-29/66d07850ff4b9bc5768172af|url-status=live}}</ref> After filming wrapped, Coppola handed McLauchlin the first half of the film and Scantlebury the latter half, allowing them to trade sections to complete the edit. After Scantlebury moved on to another project, McLauchlin and Coppola continued editing for eight more months, during which Coppola suggested including the Shakespeare scene.<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|18–20}} In addition to starring, VanderWaal, a musician who won ''[[America's Got Talent]]'' at age 12 in 2016, wrote two original songs for the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2024/music/news/grace-vanderwaal-new-music-megalopolis-americas-got-talent-1236147242/|title=Grace VanderWaal Grows Up: With New Music and ''Megalopolis'' Role, the ''America{{'}}s Got Talent'' Winner Is Coming Into Her Own — Even if It Makes 'People Really Uncomfortable'|date=September 20, 2024|first=Ellise|last=Shafer|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=September 20, 2024|archive-date=September 20, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240920150202/https://variety.com/2024/music/news/grace-vanderwaal-new-music-megalopolis-americas-got-talent-1236147242/|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Themes ==
=== Rome's example for America ===
[[File:Cicero Denounces Catiline in the Roman Senate by Cesare Maccari.png|thumb|''Megalopolis'' was inspired by the [[Catilinarian conspiracy]]. In [[Cesare Maccari]]'s 1888 painting of the incident, Catiline (right) is depicted sitting alone, deserted by his followers, while Cicero (standing on the left) publicly humiliates him.<ref name="NationalGeographic">{{Cite web |last=Wakeman |first=Gregory |date=October 7, 2024 |title=The real ancient Roman plot that inspired ''Megalopolis'' |url=https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/megalopolis-movie-cicero-catiline-conspiracy |access-date=October 7, 2024 |website=[[National Geographic Society]]}}</ref>|350x350px]]

''Megalopolis'' is both a modern retelling of the [[Catilinarian conspiracy]] and a revisionist look at the source material. Coppola described the film as a commentary on the American political system, reasoning that the Roman analogy was appropriate because the American Founding Fathers borrowed from [[Roman law]] to develop their [[Democracy|democratic government]] without a king.<ref name="VanityFair-043024"/> He specifically noted that America was vulnerable to the same political forces and tensions that destroyed the Roman Republic and created the Empire.<ref name="NationalGeographic"/>

''Megalopolis'' was not the first time Coppola had sought to modernize a historical narrative: he had previously updated the colonial-Africa setting of ''[[Heart of Darkness]]'' (1899) to the [[Vietnam War]] for ''[[Apocalypse Now]]'' (1979).<ref name="Production Notes" />{{rp|4}} Nor was it the first time that an artist had offered a revisionist look at Catiline: [[Henrik Ibsen]]'s play ''[[Catiline (play)|Catiline]]'' reimagined Catiline as a "highly principled [man] pitted against the corruption of the world in which he lived".<ref name=":3">{{Cite news |last=Beard |first=Mary |date=October 2, 2015 |title=Mary Beard: why ancient Rome matters to the modern world |url=https://www.theguardian.com/books/2015/oct/02/mary-beard-why-ancient-rome-matters |access-date=March 17, 2025 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

==== Allusions to Roman historical figures ====
The two male leads in ''Megalopolis'' directly reference the opposing sides in the Catilinarian conspiracy. Protagonist Cesar Catilina is a double reference to populist politician [[Lucius Sergius Catilina]] (anglicized to Catiline), who plotted to overthrow the Roman Republic in 63 BC, and his occasional political ally [[Julius Caesar]].<ref name="VanityFair-043024" /><ref name="NationalGeographic" /> Antagonist Franklyn Cicero references Catiline and Caesar's political rival, [[Marcus Tullius Cicero]], the leader of the Roman conservatives and the [[Roman consul|consul]] (head of the Roman government) during the conspiracy.<ref name="4Columns-Oct07">{{Cite web |last=Pinkerton |first=Nick |title=Megalopolis |url=https://4columns.org/pinkerton-nick/megalopolis |access-date=October 7, 2024 |website=4columns.org}}</ref> Although Caesar was not implicated in the conspiracy, he famously sought to prevent Catiline's execution.<ref name="ScreenRant-Oct07">{{Cite web |last=Hood |first=Cooper |date=September 28, 2024 |title=Megalopolis: The Catilinarian Conspiracy's History & Movie Inspiration Explained |url=https://screenrant.com/megalopolis-catilinarian-conspiracy-history-inspiration-explained/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241006051002/https://screenrant.com/megalopolis-catilinarian-conspiracy-history-inspiration-explained/ |archive-date=October 6, 2024 |access-date=October 7, 2024 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref>

Several supporting characters were also based on Roman notables. Cesar Catilina's mentor Hamilton Crassus references [[Marcus Licinius Crassus]], one of the richest men in Rome and a power broker who facilitated Caesar's rise to the Roman dictatorship.<ref name="4Columns-Oct07" /> Catilina's enemy Clodio Pulcher references [[Publius Clodius Pulcher]], a patrician turned populist demagogue who sponsored a counter-reaction to Cicero's punishment of Catiline's supporters;<ref name="ScreenRant-Oct07" /> in addition, the manner of Clodio's death in the film is a possible allusion to the [[death of Benito Mussolini]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Russo |first=Jude |date=October 3, 2024 |title='Megalopolis' Now |url=https://modernagejournal.com/megalopolis-now/245116/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241203103030/https://modernagejournal.com/megalopolis-now/245116/ |archive-date=December 3, 2024 |access-date=February 18, 2025 |website=Modern Age |language=en-US}}</ref> Pop star Vesta Sweetwater and her chastity pledge reference the [[Vestal Virgins]], Roman priestesses who swore an oath to remain chaste for 30 years. The real-life Catiline was accused of seducing a Vestal Virgin, an offense punishable by death.<ref name="DenofGeek">{{Cite web |last=George |first=Joe |date=September 27, 2024 |title=The Weirdest and Wildest Moments in Megalopolis |url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/the-weirdest-and-wildest-moments-in-megalopolis/ |access-date=October 7, 2024 |website=Den of Geek}}</ref>

Not every character name was an allusion to ancient Rome. Coppola came up with the "Wow Platinum" name after hearing about a woman who was nicknamed "[[wikt:wow|Wow]]" for her great beauty.<ref name="twitter.com"/>

==== Revisionist narrative and historicity ====
In contrast to most academic historians, ''Megalopolis'' portrays its Catiline stand-in as the protagonist and Cicero as the antagonist (albeit a well-meaning one). In 1999, Coppola [[Pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] the film by drawing parallels between the Catilinarian conspiracy and the present-day United States. He warned that Catiline-esque plotters could try to overthrow the American government just as Catiline tried to overthrow Rome, saying, "Rome became a fascist Empire. Is that what we're going to become?"<ref name=":0">{{cite interview |last=Coppola |first=Francis Ford |subject-link=Francis Ford Coppola |interviewer=Ann Nocenti |title=Writing and Directing ''The Conversation'': A Talk with Francis Ford Coppola |url=http://www.scenariomag.com/interviews/coppola.html |access-date=May 12, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20001215150700/http://www.scenariomag.com/interviews/coppola.html |archive-date=December 15, 2000 |url-status=usurped |website=Scenario |number=1 |date=1999 |interviewer-link=Ann Nocenti |volume=5}}</ref> However, in his production notes written a quarter-century later, Coppola explained that at the time, he had not yet written a script and his views reflected the traditional narrative.<ref name="Production Notes" />{{rp|4}} According to that narrative, [[Catiline]] was a populist seditionist who plotted to overthrow the [[Roman Republic]] after losing the election for [[Roman consul|consul]] three times.<ref name="NationalGeographic" /> [[Cicero]], the head of the Roman government, discovered the plot and drove Catiline out of the city,<ref name="Production Notes" />{{rp|7}} after which Catiline was killed at the [[Battle of Pistoria]].<ref name="NationalGeographic" /> Cicero memorialized his actions by publishing the [[Catilinarian orations]]. Although the conspiracy failed, populist politician [[Julius Caesar]] later took advantage of [[Crisis of the Roman Republic|political chaos]] to become [[dictator of Rome]], paving the way for the [[Roman Empire]].<ref name=":2">{{Cite news |last=Oltermann |first=Philip |date=May 17, 2024 |title=Francis Ford Coppola: US politics is at 'the point where we might lose our republic' |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/article/2024/may/17/francis-ford-coppola-us-politics-republic-rome-megalopolis-cannes |access-date=March 16, 2025 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

Coppola rethought the analogy as ''Megalopolis'' languished in [[development hell]]. As early as 1999, he noted that Roman histories of the Catilinarian conspiracy were largely based on Cicero's own retelling of the incident.<ref name=":0" /> In 2008, he said that while the standard narrative of the conspiracy posited that Catiline "was going to take over the city and burn it", he was more interested in the hypothetical of Catiline trying to build "a new society". He said that he wanted to tell a "political fantasy" about "an enlightened [[Robert Moses]], more like an [[Ayn Rand]]", elaborating that "the Ayn Rand character [that is, Howard Roark in ''[[The Fountainhead]]''] wanted to build a city within New York that was the model of what a city could be."<ref name="Film Comment" /> At some point, Coppola began questioning whether Cicero, a political conservative, had simply concocted an excuse to crush the populist policies backed by Catiline and Julius Caesar.<ref name="VanityFair-043024" /> In ''Megalopolis'', Coppola retells two of the real-life Cicero's accusations towards Catiline – that Catiline slept with a Vestal Virgin (a Roman priestess sworn to chastity)<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Cadoux |first=T. J. |date=2005 |title=Catiline and the Vestal Virgins |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/4436764 |url-status=live |journal=Historia: Zeitschrift für Alte Geschichte |volume=54 |issue=2 |pages=166–67 |issn=0018-2311 |jstor=4436764 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007224424/https://www.jstor.org/stable/4436764 |archive-date=October 7, 2024 |access-date=October 7, 2024}}</ref> and that Catiline murdered his first wife<ref>{{Cite news |last=Wood |first=Michael |date=October 14, 2024 |title=Michael Wood · At the Movies: 'Megalopolis' |url=https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v46/n20/michael-wood/at-the-movies |access-date=March 12, 2025 |work=London Review of Books |language=en |volume=46 |issue=20 |issn=0260-9592}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Oratio in Catilinam Prima VI.13-16 Notes |url=https://classics.osu.edu/Undergraduate-Studies/Latin-Program/Catiline/vi13-16/vi13-16-notes |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007224424/https://classics.osu.edu/Undergraduate-Studies/Latin-Program/Catiline/vi13-16/vi13-16-notes |archive-date=October 7, 2024 |access-date=October 7, 2024 |website=The Ohio State University}}</ref> – and acquits Cesar Catilina of both accusations.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Acharya |first=Amartya |date=November 13, 2024 |title=Megalopolis (2024) Movie Ending Explained & Themes Analyzed: What Does Coppola's Magnum Opus Really Want to Say? |url=https://www.highonfilms.com/megalopolis-2024-movie-ending-explained-themes-analysed/ |access-date=March 16, 2025 |website=High On Films |language=en-US}}</ref>

Coppola remained interested in the crisis of Roman republicanism, of which the Catilinarian conspiracy was a part. When promoting the film, he linked its subject matter to what he saw as rising "neo-right, even fascist" tendencies in the United States and abroad, explaining that "what's happening in America, in our republic, in our democracy, is exactly how Rome lost their republic thousands of years ago."<ref name=":2" /> However, in the film, the seditionists plotting to destroy the republic are led by Clodio Pulcher, not Catilina.<ref name="4Columns-Oct07" />

The film's lionization of Catiline diverges from the academic mainstream, although the precise extent is disputed. According to [[Richard Saller]], while the traditional narrative is "Cicero centric", most historians – [[Robin Seager]] being a notable exception – reject the revisionist argument that "Cicero really manufactured the conspiracy".<ref name="NationalGeographic" /> [[Mary Beard (classicist)|Mary Beard]], who received a film credit for her historical advice on the film,<ref name="Production Notes" />{{rp||page=37}} took an intermediate position. In her 2015 book [[SPQR: A History of Ancient Rome|''SPQR'']], she agreed that Catiline was "a disgruntled, bankrupt aristocrat",<ref name="NYTDargis"/> but cautioned that his populist concern for ordinary people may have been genuine.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Mount |first=Ferdinand |date=November 20, 2015 |title=Mary Beard's 'SPQR' and Tom Holland's 'Dynasty' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2015/11/22/books/review/mary-beards-spqr-and-tom-hollands-dynasty.html |access-date=March 17, 2025 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> With respect to Cicero, she conceded that Cicero "probably believed" that Catiline was actually plotting a coup,<ref name=":3" /> but noted that "there is still disagreement about how far the 'reds under the bed' were a figment of Cicero's conservative imagination or paranoia".<ref name=":3" /> She had previously suggested that Cicero needed a major accomplishment as consul to secure his long-term political future and may have been "spoiling for a fight".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Beard |first=Mary |date=August 23, 2001 |title=Lucky City |url=https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v23/n16/mary-beard/lucky-city |access-date=March 16, 2025 |work=London Review of Books |language=en |volume=23 |issue=16 |issn=0260-9592}}</ref>

=== Artistic idealism as antidote to polarization ===
In 2008, Coppola stated that the character of Cesar Catilina would be inspired by the life of urban planner [[Robert Moses]], who built much of the infrastructure of modern-day New York City, but was criticized for favoring wealthy political interests and ignoring the needs of New York's [[ethnic minorities]]. Coppola said that he wanted to imagine an "enlightened" version of Moses that preserved Moses's desire to build a better city.<ref name="Film Comment"/>

''Megalopolis'' argues that for a [[democracy]] to avoid falling into [[fascism]], it needs a bold and inspirational vision for the future, which artists can help provide. In 1999, Coppola said that "Rome didn't fall for a long time. But how does an Empire die? It dies when its people no longer believe in it".<ref name=":0"/> In 2022, Coppola said his film would offer an optimistic look at humanity and the intuitive goodness in people even in a divided climate. He criticized fellow [[New York Military Academy]] alumnus [[Donald Trump]] for calling Mexicans criminal rapists, saying that "dictators and populists stay in power by having you hate someone else. ... [P]eople are in the habit of saying all people are bad ... We're doing...better. We could be proud of being human beings".<ref name=":1">{{cite web |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |date=March 25, 2022 |title=From ''The Godfather'' Trilogy to ''American Graffiti'', ''Patton'', ''The Conversation'' & ''Apocalypse Now'', Francis Ford Coppola Shares His Oscar Memories |url=https://deadline.com/2022/03/francis-ford-coppola-oscars-memories-godfather-patton-apocalypse-now-1234986966/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240508005526/https://deadline.com/2022/03/francis-ford-coppola-oscars-memories-godfather-patton-apocalypse-now-1234986966/ |archive-date=May 8, 2024 |access-date=May 6, 2024 |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref>

The film concludes with Cesar Catilina delivering an impassioned speech that persuades the public to embrace the vision of Megalopolis. Writing for ''[[The New Yorker]]'', [[Justin Chang]] observed that Coppola's struggle to complete the film without studio interference lent the narrative an unintended layer of self-reflection. He described ''Megalopolis'' as an artistic defense of beauty and ambition, arguing that its existence was a statement of hope.<ref name="Chang"/> Coppola himself acknowledged the value of visionary ideas but rejected the notion that Catilina's project should prescribe a specific vision of the future, asserting instead that utopia is not a fixed destination but an ongoing conversation.<ref name=":1"/>

=== Art versus politics ===
In addition to Moses, Coppola based the character of Cesar Catilina and his invention of Megalon on the novels of Ayn Rand.<ref name="Film Comment"/> Catilina specifically resembles two Rand characters who struggle to implement their ideas in the face of the dominant political establishment, which may be either conservative or left-wing. In ''[[The Fountainhead]]'' (1943), modernist architect Howard Roark struggles to be taken seriously by a conservative artistic establishment that insists on aping the architecture of ancient Rome.<ref name="4Columns-Oct07"/> In ''[[Atlas Shrugged]]'' (1957), visionary inventor Hank Rearden creates a high-performance metal alloy, which is subsequently nationalized by a left-wing government. ''Megalopolis'' depicts Cesar Catilina's modernist utopia under simultaneous attack by the left and the right: Cicero's conservatives reject Catilina's bold vision as fanciful, while Pulcher's radicals deride Catilina's ideas as pointlessly extravagant. Writing in ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Ross Douthat]] commented that "the central conflict isn't really left versus right .... Instead, [''Megalopolis''] pits Catilina ... against various forces representing inertia and sterility: the uncomprehending establishment and the ungrateful masses, ... all ranged against the man of genius".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Douthat |first=Ross |date=October 4, 2024 |title="Megalopolis" and the Vexations of Decadence |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/10/04/opinion/coppola-megalopolis-decadence.html |access-date=October 7, 2024 |work=The New York Times |archive-date=October 7, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007224359/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/10/04/opinion/coppola-megalopolis-decadence.html |url-status=live}}</ref>

During the press tour, Coppola said that he was interested in telling a story about a man who "would have brought something wonderful," until "what usually happens happened—the traditional forces subdued the new idea."<ref name=":7"/> He used the character of Mayor Cicero to demonstrate how "a conservative and a classicist" opposes an "enlightened artist",<ref name=":7" /> adding that Cicero was "committed to a regressive status quo" that puts greed and partisan politics over the public good. He concluded that although Cicero "truly wants to save the city", he embodied "despair" instead of hope for a better society, and no longer entertains "transformative ideas".<ref name="Production Notes"/>{{rp|6, 9, 11}} Coppola compared Cicero to several mayors of New York City (mainly [[Ed Koch]], but also [[David Dinkins]] at one point).<ref name="Film Comment"/><ref name=":0"/><ref name=":7" /><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Cowie |first=Peter |date=September 1990 |title=The Family |url=https://archive.org/details/blitz93_sept_1990/page/44/ |journal=[[Blitz (British magazine)|Blitz]] |issue=93 |pages=44–50 |via=Internet Archive}}</ref>

Although Coppola's press statements largely focused on Cicero over Pulcher, ''[[Den of Geek]]'' noted that Pulcher "serves as the [[Trumpism|Trump]] stand-in" and that "it's unclear if Coppola's trying to evoke the opulence of the Roman ruling classes or Trump's obsession with spray tans and hair-pieces or both".<ref name="DenofGeek"/> ''[[The Sweet East]]'' (2023) screenwriter Nick Pinkerton expressed concern that Pulcher's rapid recruitment of working-class populists implied that "the plebeians [are] easily manipulated rubes, a curious omission in a movie that's positioned as a humanist hymn to our species' higher potential".<ref name="4Columns-Oct07"/>

=== Distribution ===
Coppola saw ''Megalopolis'' in full for the first time on an [[IMAX]] screen at the headquarters of [[IMAX Corporation]] in [[Playa Vista, Los Angeles]]. The film used camera technology for certain sequences that could cover an entire IMAX screen.<ref name="Uphill Battle"/> On March 28, 2024, a private screening was presented to distributors at the [[Universal CityWalk#Universal CityWalk Hollywood|Universal CityWalk]] IMAX Theater in Hollywood. Coppola and his longtime attorney Barry Hirsch, a producer on the film, said they would not decide where to debut the film until they secured a [[Film distribution|distribution partner]] and a firm rollout plan.<ref name="Screening">{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2024/03/francis-coppola-megalopolis-first-screening-distributors-citywalk-imax-1235871124/ |title=Francis Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' Screened for First Time Today for Distributors at CityWalk IMAX |last=Fleming |first=Mike Jr. |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=March 28, 2024 |access-date=March 30, 2024 |archive-date=March 30, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240330021515/https://deadline.com/2024/03/francis-coppola-megalopolis-first-screening-distributors-citywalk-imax-1235871124/ |url-status=live}}</ref> However, the "muted" response to the screening made securing a distributor difficult. Studios weighed the return on investment, as Coppola expected a print-and-advertising (P&A) campaign of $80–100{{nbsp}}million and for producers to receive half of the film's revenues.<ref name="Uphill Battle"/><ref name="Distribution Deal">{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-in-talks-french-distribution-deal-le-pacte-1235979859/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' Nears French Distribution Deal with Indie Banner Le Pacte |last=Keslassy |first=Elsa |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=April 23, 2024 |access-date=April 26, 2024 |archive-date=April 24, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240424170703/https://variety.com/2024/film/news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-in-talks-french-distribution-deal-le-pacte-1235979859/ |url-status=live}}</ref> A distribution veteran suggested that "If [Coppola] is willing to put up the P&A or backstop the spend, I think there would be a lot more interested parties".<ref name="Uphill Battle"/>

In Ebert's case, his quote calling a film a "triumph of style over substance" was misattributed from his review for ''[[Batman (1989 film)|Batman]]'' (1989).<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.vulture.com/article/did-the-megalopolis-trailer-make-up-fake-movie-critic-quotes.html|url-access=limited |title=Did the ''Megalopolis'' Trailer Use Fake Movie Critic Quotes? == Reception ==
=== Box office ===
''Megalopolis'' grossed $7.6{{nbsp}}million in the United States and Canada, and $6.8{{nbsp}}million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $14.4{{nbsp}}million.<ref>{{Cite web |title=''Megalopolis'' |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2238742529/ |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=June 9, 2026 |archive-date=September 30, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240930065724/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2238742529/?ref_=bo_we_table_6 |url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' calculated the film lost $75.5 million, when factoring together all expenses and revenues.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2025/04/biggest-box-office-bombs-2024-lowest-grossing-movies-1236381446/|title=Four Fanboy Flicks Bomb Out In Deadline's 2024 Most Valuable Blockbuster Tournament|date=April 30, 2025|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=May 1, 2025}}</ref>

In the United States and Canada, ''Megalopolis'' received a [[wide release]] alongside ''[[The Wild Robot]]''. It was projected to gross $4–8{{nbsp}}million from 1,854 theaters, including an estimated 200 IMAX locations, in its opening weekend.<ref name="BoPro-Aug30">{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficepro.com/long-range-forecast-the-wild-robot-megalopolis-close-out-september/ |title=Long Range Forecast: ''The Wild Robot'', ''Megalopolis'' Close Out September |date=August 30, 2024 |website=[[Boxoffice Pro]]|access-date=August 31, 2024|archive-date=September 7, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240907181641/https://www.boxofficepro.com/long-range-forecast-the-wild-robot-megalopolis-close-out-september/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-tracking-1235992875/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' Tracking for Anemic $5M-Plus Domestic Opening |date=September 5, 2024 |first=Pamela |last=McClintock |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=September 6, 2024|archive-date=September 7, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240907181653/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-tracking-1235992875/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Deadline-BO_Preview"/> ''[[Boxoffice Pro]]'' identified the film's target audience as [[cinephile]]s and said "working against" it were "middling reviews", "a career lull" (Coppola having directed two films in the last 15 years), and "a marketing push marred by controversy", referring to the [[#Allegations of misconduct|misconduct allegations against Coppola]] and the [[#Trailer|trailer]] removed over its use of fake quotes. They mentioned that the film's "blockbuster cast" and IMAX release was "no guarantee of audience interest", given Driver's box-office disappointment in "high-concept sci-fi" with ''[[65 (film)|65]]'' the year prior and that the last Coppola–helmed movie to open above $5{{nbsp}}million was ''[[Supernova (2000 film)|Supernova]]'' (uncredited) in 2000.<ref name="BoPro-Aug30"/> ''Megalopolis'' shared IMAX screens with ''The Wild Robot'' before relinquishing them a week later to ''[[Joker: Folie à Deux]]''; reserved for ''The Wild Robot'' were all showtimes up to primetime, with ''Megalopolis'' having evening IMAX showtimes in "key metropolitan locations".<ref name="Deadline-BO_Preview"/><ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-release-date-lionsgate-divisive-cannes-premiere-1236040069/ |title=Francis Ford Coppola's ''Megalopolis'' Lands at Lionsgate for U.S. Release After Divisive Cannes Premiere |last=Rubin |first=Rebecca |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=June 17, 2024 |access-date=June 18, 2024 |archive-date=June 17, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240617231517/https://variety.com/2024/film/news/francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-release-date-lionsgate-divisive-cannes-premiere-1236040069/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Lionsgate, reportedly "not on the hook" for recovering production or marketing expenses after agreeing to distribute the film for a distribution fee and "some percentage points based on box office", was expected to earn $3–5{{nbsp}}million regardless of box office performance.<ref name="Deadline-BO_Preview">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2024/09/the-wild-robot-megalopolis-box-office-1236098281/|title=''The Wild Robot'' Set To Walk Through Weekend With $20M+ Opening; The film made $1.8{{nbsp}}million on its first day, including an estimated $770,000 from Monday and Thursday previews.<ref name="Deadline-OpeningWk"/> It debuted to $4{{nbsp}}million, finishing sixth at the box office.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2024W39/|title=Domestic 2024 Weekend 39|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=October 1, 2024|archive-date=October 1, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241001182108/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/2024W39/|url-status=live}}</ref> As a result, several publications, including the [[Associated Press]] and ''The Wall Street Journal'', labeled the film a box-office disappointment.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://apnews.com/article/megalopolis-wild-robot-box-office-b086c1f3259c6075915aedd5a1f4d9f9|title=''Megalopolis'' flops, ''The Wild Robot'' soars at box office|date=September 29, 2024|first=Jake|last=Coyle|website=[[Associated Press]]|access-date=October 2, 2024|archive-date=October 1, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241001215230/https://apnews.com/article/megalopolis-wild-robot-box-office-b086c1f3259c6075915aedd5a1f4d9f9|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wsj.com/business/media/francis-ford-coppolas-big-budget-megalopolis-flops-in-debut-weekend-cfa4edf9|url-access=subscription|title=Francis Ford Coppola's Big-Budget ''Megalopolis'' Flops in Debut Weekend|date=September 29, 2024|first=Andrew|last=Tangel|website=[[The Wall Street Journal]]|access-date=October 2, 2024|archive-date=October 2, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241002101843/https://www.wsj.com/business/media/francis-ford-coppolas-big-budget-megalopolis-flops-in-debut-weekend-cfa4edf9|url-status=live}}</ref> IMAX and premium large-format screens accounted for 41% of the gross, with IMAX specifically earning $1.4{{nbsp}}million (35%) of the opening. Audiences were 70% men, with 38% over 35 and 32% 25–34-year-olds, while the demographics were 66% Caucasian, 18% Latino and Hispanic, 7% Asian, and 5% Black. The main reasons given in exit polling for seeing the film were Coppola (61%) and Driver (29%), while 32% bought tickets because they heard it was "entertaining and fun" and 9% because they heard it "was good". AMC Lincoln Square was the film's highest-grossing theater with earnings of $84,000 through Saturday. Anthony D'Alessandro of ''Deadline Hollywood'' compared the opening to other box-office flops, particularly as being more than the overall gross of [[Michael Cimino]]'s ''[[Heaven's Gate (film)|Heaven's Gate]]'' ($3.5{{nbsp}}million in 1980, unadjusted for inflation) but less than [[Kevin Costner]]'s self-financed ''[[Horizon: An American Saga – Chapter 1]]'' ($11{{nbsp}}million opening in 2024). It also made less than the fellow new release ''[[Devara: Part 1]]'' ($5.6{{nbsp}}million), which played in fewer theaters (1,040).<ref name="Deadline-OpeningWk">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2024/09/box-office-megalopolis-the-wild-robot-1236101618/|title=''Wild Robot'' Powers To Third Best September Animated Pic Debut With $35M; ''Megalopolis'' Collapses With $4M & D+ CinemaScore; ''Saturday Night'' Alive In Limited Release – Sunday Box Office Update|date=September 29, 2024|first1=Anthony|last1=D'Alessandro|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=September 29, 2024|archive-date=September 29, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240929001241/https://deadline.com/2024/09/box-office-megalopolis-the-wild-robot-1236101618/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Marc Tracy]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' likened it to [[Joseph L. == Home media and roadshow screenings ==
''Megalopolis'' was released on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]], [[Blu-ray]] and DVD by Lionsgate in Europe following its theatrical run, but did not receive a North American physical home media release.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Plale |first=Mathew |date=March 10, 2025 |title=Why isn't Megalopolis on Blu-ray or streaming? |url=https://www.joblo.com/why-isnt-megalopolis-on-blu-ray-or-streaming/ |access-date=July 24, 2025 |website=JoBlo |language=en-US}}</ref> The film became available for purchase and rental on premium video-on-demand services, including North America, on November 12, 2024, but was pulled from digital platforms in selected markets in early 2025, reportedly because Coppola prefers that the film only be screened in theaters.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Obias |first=Rudie |date=November 13, 2024 |title=Where to Stream 'Megalopolis' Online |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/watch-stream-megalopolis-online-1236060356/ |access-date=July 24, 2025 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Svetkey |first=Benjamin |date=May 7, 2025 |title=Where, Oh Where, Did 'Megalopolis' Go? |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/why-cant-stream-francis-ford-coppola-megalopolis-1236207923/ |access-date=July 24, 2025 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US|last2=Sancton|first2=Julian}}</ref> The film was made available for streaming on [[Mubi (streaming service)|Mubi]] and [[Netflix]] in some European countries in September 2025.<ref>[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WVZd5b--U6w MEGALOPOLIS|Official Trailer|Hand-picked by MUBI on official YouTube channel]</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.justwatch.com/de/Film/megalopolis |title=Francis Ford Coppola's Megalopolis - Eine Fabel - Stream: Online |language=de |access-date=October 12, 2025 |via=www.justwatch.com}}</ref>

Coppola held six [[Roadshow theatrical release|roadshow screenings]] of ''Megalopolis'' in the U.S. from July 20–August 1, 2025. ''The Atlantic'' criticized Coppola's decision to limit the film to theatrical screenings and eschew a home video release, writing, "But rather than allow audiences to organically find ''Megalopolis'', Coppola has made it hard to screen legally. Rather than wait for reevaluations of it to emerge over time, Coppola initiated the conversation from his end. His efforts to reintroduce ''Megalopolis'' to the public demonstrate the challenge of transforming a flop into a cult classic."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Li |first=Shirley |date=August 18, 2025 |title=A Famed Director Tried to Build a Fan Base for His Movie. It Was Awkward. |url=https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2025/08/megalopolis-francis-ford-coppola-cult-classics/683896/ |access-date=November 21, 2025 |website=The Atlantic |language=en}}</ref>
