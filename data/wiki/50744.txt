'''''Return of the Jedi''''' (also known as '''''Star Wars: Episode VI – Return of the Jedi'''''{{space+parenthesis}} <!-- Please do not change this title or the "also known as". It was agreed to in long discussions in January and November 2014. --> is a 1983 American epic [[space opera]] film directed by [[Richard Marquand]] from a screenplay by [[Lawrence Kasdan]] and [[George Lucas]]. The sequel to ''[[The Empire Strikes Back]]'' (1980), it is the third<!-- Please do not add "and final", as that should be obvious for the third installment of a trilogy.--> installment in the [[Star Wars original trilogy|original ''Star Wars'' trilogy]] and the sixth chronological film in the "[[Skywalker Saga]]". The film follows the ongoing struggle between the malevolent [[Galactic Empire (Star Wars)|Galactic Empire]] and the freedom fighters of the [[Rebel Alliance]]. As the Rebels attempt to destroy the Empire's [[second Death Star]], [[Luke Skywalker]] tries to bring his father, [[Darth Vader]], back from the dark side of [[the Force]]. The film stars [[Mark Hamill]], [[Harrison Ford]], [[Carrie Fisher]], [[Billy Dee Williams]], [[Anthony Daniels]], [[David Prowse]], [[Kenny Baker (English actor)|Kenny Baker]], [[Peter Mayhew]], and [[Frank Oz]].

[[Steven Spielberg]], [[David Lynch]], and [[David Cronenberg]] were considered to direct the film before Marquand signed on as director. The production team relied on Lucas' storyboards during pre-production. While writing the shooting script, Lucas, Kasdan, Marquand, and producer [[Howard Kazanjian]] spent two weeks in conference discussing ideas to construct it. Kazanjian's schedule pushed shooting to begin a few weeks early to allow [[Industrial Light & Magic]] more time to work on the film's effects in post-production. Filming took place in England, California, and Arizona from January to {{Start date|1982|5}}.

''Return of the Jedi'' was released in theaters by [[20th Century-Fox]] on May 25, 1983. It grossed $374 million worldwide during its initial theatrical run, becoming the [[1983 in film#Highest-grossing films|highest-grossing film of 1983]]. It received mixed reviews, with critics conflicted on the performances of the actors, the special effects and the action sequences, though [[John Williams]]' score was consistently praised. Later reviews have been more positive, although most critics consider the film to be the weakest of the original trilogy. Several re-releases and [[Changes in Star Wars re-releases#Return of the Jedi|revisions]] to the film have followed over the decades, which have brought its total gross to $482 million. The United States [[Library of Congress]] selected it for preservation in the [[National Film Registry]] in 2021. A [[prequel trilogy]], released between 1999 and 2005, and a [[sequel trilogy]], released between 2015 and 2019, followed.

== Plot ==

One year after [[Han Solo]]'s capture and imprisonment in carbonite,{{efn|As depicted in ''[[The Empire Strikes Back]]'' (1980).<ref name="Ben Travis1">{{Cite web|last=Travis|first=Ben|date=2023-09-08|title=Star Wars Timeline: Every Movie, Series And More|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/star-wars-timeline-chronological-order/|access-date=2024-03-24|website=Empire|archive-date=March 23, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240323122442/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/star-wars-timeline-chronological-order/|url-status=live}}</ref>}} [[C-3PO]] and [[R2-D2]] enter the palace of the crime lord [[Jabba the Hutt]] on [[Tatooine]]. They were sent as a goodwill gift from [[Luke Skywalker]], who hopes to negotiate with Jabba for Han's release. Disguised as a [[bounty hunter]], [[Princess Leia]] infiltrates the palace under the pretense of having captured [[Chewbacca]]. She releases Han from the carbonite but is caught by Jabba and enslaved. Luke arrives to bargain for the release of his friends, but Jabba drops him through a trapdoor to be eaten by a [[rancor]]. After Luke kills the beast, Jabba decrees that he, Han, and Chewbacca will be fed to a<!--"Sarlacc" is capitalized, based on the two references.--> [[Sarlacc]],{{Sfn|Hidalgo|Sansweet|2008|p=127}}<ref name="Sarlacc">{{Cite web |title=Sarlacc |url=https://www.starwars.com/databank/sarlacc |access-date=2024-05-29 |website=StarWars.com}}</ref> a deadly ground-dwelling creature. However, Luke retrieves his new  [[lightsaber]] from {{nowrap|R2-D2}}, and the group of friends battle Jabba's thugs aboard his sail barge. During the chaos, [[Boba Fett]] is swallowed by the Sarlacc, and Leia strangles Jabba to death with her chains. The group escapes as Jabba's sail barge is destroyed.

As the others rendezvous with the Rebel Alliance, Luke returns to [[Dagobah]] to complete his [[Jedi]] training with [[Yoda]], who is dying when he arrives. Before Yoda dies, he confirms that [[Darth Vader]] is Luke's father, the former Jedi Knight Anakin Skywalker, and that there is another Skywalker. [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'s [[Force spirit]] tells Luke that Leia is his twin sister, and that he must face Vader again to finish his training and defeat the Empire.

The Alliance learns that the Empire has been constructing a second Death Star under the supervision of [[Palpatine|the Emperor]]. The station is protected by an energy shield on the forest moon of [[Endor (Star Wars)|Endor]]. Han leads a strike team, including Luke, Leia, and Chewbacca, to destroy its generator. Once on the moon, the team gains the trust of an [[Ewok]] tribe. Luke tells Leia she is his sister, and that Vader is their father. He then surrenders to Imperial troops and is brought before Vader, but fails to convince him to reject the dark side of the Force.

Luke is taken to the Emperor, who intends to turn him to the dark side. He reveals that Luke's friends on Endor and the rebel fleet are heading into a trap. On the forest moon, Han's team is captured by Imperial forces, but the Ewoks counterattack, allowing the Rebels to infiltrate the shield generator. Meanwhile, [[Lando Calrissian]] and [[Admiral Ackbar]] lead the rebel assault on the Death Star, but find its shield still active and the Imperial fleet waiting for them.

== Production ==

=== Development ===
Following the commercial success of ''The Empire Strikes Back'', a second ''Star Wars'' sequel was swiftly put into production.{{sfn|Rinzler|2013|p=2}} As with ''Empire'', Lucas personally financed ''Return of the Jedi'', but chose not to direct the film himself.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams">{{Cite AV media |title=[[Empire of Dreams: The Story of the Star Wars Trilogy]] |date=September 21, 2004 |type=DVD |publisher=20th Century Fox Home Entertainment}}</ref> [[Irvin Kershner]], the director of ''Empire'', declined to return for ''Jedi''.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Mike |first=Ryan |date=2010-10-18 |title=In Hindsight, Empire Strikes Back Director Irvin Kershner Would've Helmed One of the Prequels |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2010/10/irvin-kershner |access-date=2023-11-23 |magazine=Vanity Fair |archive-date=September 25, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230925003441/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2010/10/irvin-kershner |url-status=live}}</ref> Although Lucas' first choice for director was [[Steven Spielberg]], their separate feuds with the [[Directors Guild of America]] led to Spielberg being banned from directing the film.<ref>{{cite web |last=Witzke |first=Sean |date=February 19, 2015 |title=A Look Back at Steven Spielberg at the Height of His Powers |url=https://www.grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/a-look-back-at-steven-spielberg-at-the-height-of-his-powers/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181008174948/http://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/a-look-back-at-steven-spielberg-at-the-height-of-his-powers/ |archive-date=October 8, 2018 |access-date=October 8, 2018 |work=Grantland}}</ref> Lucas approached [[David Lynch]], who had recently been nominated for the [[Academy Award for Best Director]] for ''[[The Elephant Man (1980 film)|The Elephant Man]]'' (1980). Lynch declined, believing that Lucas should direct the film since ''Star Wars'' was his creation.<ref>{{cite web |title=David Lynch Meets George Lucas |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJQ4vCu-S0U |date=February 17, 2010 |access-date=December 1, 2014 |publisher=YouTube |archive-date=September 11, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140911060947/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJQ4vCu-S0U |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Tassi |first=Paul |date=2009-12-09 |title=Lynch talks Jedi |url=https://www.joblo.com/lynch-talks-jedi/ |access-date=2023-11-23 |website=JoBlo |archivedate=November 15, 2023 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20231115155442/https://www.joblo.com/lynch-talks-jedi/ |url-status=live}}</ref>[[File:George Lucas 1986 (cropped).jpg|alt=A photograph of George Lucas|left|thumb|upright|George Lucas (pictured in 1986)]]

Lucas offered [[David Cronenberg]] the chance to direct, but he turned down the offer, as he preferred to direct films he had written himself.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Adler |first=Shawn |date=September 20, 2007 |title=Cronenberg's Aborted Job Offer: Star Wars: Return of the Jedi Director's Chair? |url=http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2007/09/20/cronenbergs-aborted-job-offer-star-wars-return-of-the-jedi-directors-chair/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100317131218/http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2007/09/20/cronenbergs-aborted-job-offer-star-wars-return-of-the-jedi-directors-chair/ |archive-date=March 17, 2010 |access-date=March 23, 2024 |website=MTV Movies Blog}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Keslassy |first=Elsa |date=2022-05-16 |title=David Cronenberg Breaks Down Cannes Walkouts, His New Film's Sexuality, and Why Netflix Turns Him Down |url=https://variety.com/2022/film/news/david-cronenberg-cannes-interview-crimes-of-the-future-walkouts-netflix-1235267464/ |access-date=2023-11-23 |website=Variety |archivedate=January 15, 2024 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20240115170849/https://variety.com/2022/film/news/david-cronenberg-cannes-interview-crimes-of-the-future-walkouts-netflix-1235267464/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Lucas also considered [[Lamont Johnson]], the director of ''[[The Last American Hero]]'' (1973), before finally choosing [[Richard Marquand]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Ryan |first=Joal |date=May 24, 2013 |title=The 'Return of the Jedi' That Could Have Been |url=https://www.yahoo.com/movies/bp/return-jedi-could-202622407.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170223042053/https://www.yahoo.com/movies/bp/return-jedi-could-202622407.html |archive-date=February 23, 2017 |access-date=February 22, 2017 |work=[[Yahoo!]]}}</ref> Lucas was on set frequently because of Marquand's relative inexperience with special effects; Marquand joked that directing ''Jedi'' was like "trying to direct ''[[King Lear]]'' – with [[Shakespeare]] in the next room!"{{efn|Lucas also operated the second camera a few times during filming.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Skywalking: The Life and Films of George Lucas |last=Pollock |first=Dale |publisher=Da Capo |year=1999 |pages=203, 225, 227, 276 |isbn=0-573-60606-4}}</ref>}}<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" /><ref name="hearn">{{Cite book |author=Marcus Hearn |title=The Cinema of George Lucas |publisher=Harry N. Abrams Inc |year=2005 |location=New York City |pages=140–1 |chapter=Cliffhanging |isbn=0-8109-4968-7}}</ref> Lucas praised Marquand's kindness and his ability to work with actors.<ref name="DVDcom">''Star Wars Episode VI: Return of the Jedi'' DVD commentary featuring George Lucas, Ben Burtt, Dennis Muren and Carrie Fisher. [[Fox Home Entertainment]], 2004.</ref>

The screenplay for ''Jedi'' was based on a story by Lucas. The script was written by Lucas and [[Lawrence Kasdan]],{{efn|The screenplay contains uncredited contributions from Marquand and [[David Peoples]].}} who allegedly told Lucas that ''Return of the Jedi'' was a weak title.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" /> Kazanjian echoed Kasdan's concern, and the title was changed to ''Revenge of the Jedi'' soon after.{{Sfn|Rinzler|2013|p=7}}<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=McMillan |first=Graeme |date=May 24, 2013 |title=30 Things You Didn't Know About Return of the Jedi |url=https://www.wired.com/2013/05/return-of-the-jedi-anniversary/ |access-date=2023-11-23 |magazine=Wired |issn=1059-1028 |archive-date=November 6, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231106002027/https://www.wired.com/2013/05/return-of-the-jedi-anniversary/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The screenplay was not finished until late in [[pre-production]], well after a [[production schedule]] and a budget had been created, and after Marquand had been hired, which was unusual for a film. Instead, the [[production team]] relied on Lucas' story and his rough draft of the script to guide their early work. When it came time to write a [[shooting script]], Lucas, Kasdan, Marquand and Kazanjian spent two weeks in conference discussing ideas; Kasdan used tape transcripts of these meetings to construct the script.<ref name="ProdDir3">{{Cite journal |author=Richard Patterson |date=June 1983 |title=Return of the Jedi: Production and Direction, p. 3 |url=http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg3.htm |url-status=live |journal=[[American Cinematographer]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930184715/http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg3.htm |archive-date=September 30, 2007 |access-date=August 19, 2007}}</ref>

The question of whether Harrison Ford would return for ''Return of the Jedi'' arose during pre-production. Unlike Mark Hamill and Carrie Fisher, Ford was not contracted for two sequels, and ''[[Raiders of the Lost Ark]]'' (1981) had made him an even bigger star. Kazanjian said that Han was frozen in carbonite in ''The Empire Strikes Back'' because the filmmakers were unsure whether the character would return. He also claimed that Lucas did not expect Ford to return, and that Han was initially not in the script. However, Kazanjian—who also produced ''Raiders of the Lost Ark—''ultimately convinced Ford to return.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Howard Kazanjian interview {{!}} Producer {{!}} Star Wars {{!}} Indiana Jones |url=http://starwarsinterviews1.blogspot.com/2010/01/howard-kazanjian-interview.html |access-date=2024-03-23 |website=Howard Kazanjian interview {{!}} Producer {{!}} Star Wars {{!}} Indiana Jones |archivedate=February 7, 2016 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20160207142202/http://starwarsinterviews1.blogspot.com/2010/01/howard-kazanjian-interview.html |url-status=live}}</ref>

Ford felt that Han should die through self-sacrifice in ''Jedi''.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Harrison Ford: They should have killed Han Solo | date=November 12, 2010 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mpaR3KDt_no |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20190725025340/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mpaR3KDt_no |archivedate=July 25, 2019 |access-date=March 23, 2024 |publisher=ABC News |via=YouTube}}</ref> Kasdan agreed, and said it should occur near the beginning of the [[Three-act structure|third act]], to make the audience wonder whether the other protagonists will survive.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" /> [[Gary Kurtz]], who produced ''Star Wars'' and ''Empire'' but was replaced by Kazanjian for ''Jedi'', said that Han died in an early version of the script. Kurtz has suggested that one reason Lucas may have rejected the idea of Han dying was that it would not be good for merchandising.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ryan |first=Mike |date=2012-11-09 |title=Harrison Ford's Complicated History With Han Solo |url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/harrison-ford-han-solo_n_2097347 |access-date=2023-11-23 |website=HuffPost |archivedate=November 9, 2023 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20231109023849/https://www.huffpost.com/entry/harrison-ford-han-solo_n_2097347 |url-status=live}}</ref> Kurtz also has claimed that an early script ended with the rebel forces in tatters, Leia grappling with her new duties as a queen, and Luke walking off alone "like [[Clint Eastwood]] in the [[spaghetti Western]]s".<ref name="LostEnding">{{Cite web |last=Boucher |first=Geoff |date=2010-08-12 |title=Did 'Star Wars' become a toy story? Producer Gary Kurtz looks back |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/hero-complex-blog/story/2010-08-12/did-star-wars-become-a-toy-story-producer-gary-kurtz-looks-back |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20240123064813/https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/hero-complex-blog/story/2010-08-12/did-star-wars-become-a-toy-story-producer-gary-kurtz-looks-back |archivedate=January 23, 2024 |access-date=2024-03-23 |website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref>

Yoda was originally not meant to appear in the film, but Marquand strongly felt that returning to Dagobah was essential because audiences would expect it.<ref name="ProdDir3" /> After speaking with a children's psychologist, Lucas was concerned that young viewers might think that Darth Vader had lied about being Luke's father in the previous film, and so he inserted a scene in which Yoda confirms Vader's claim.<ref name="DVDcom" /> There were many other scenes that were changed during writing. At one point the Ewoks were going to be Wookiees, and the ''Millennium Falcon'' was going to land on Endor.<ref>''Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith'' DVD commentary featuring George Lucas, Rick McCallum, Rob Coleman, John Knoll, and Roger Guyett. [[20th Century Fox|Fox Home Entertainment]], 2005.</ref> The ending of the film was going to show Obi-Wan, Yoda and Anakin Skywalker returning to life from their spectral existence in the Force.<ref name="Revengescript">{{cite web |author=George Lucas |date=June 12, 1981 |title=Star Wars&nbsp;— Episode VI: "Revenge of the Jedi" Revised Rough Draft |url=http://www.starwarz.com/starkiller/scripts/revenge_revised_rough_draft.htm |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070203075748/http://www.starwarz.com/starkiller/scripts/revenge_revised_rough_draft.htm |archive-date=February 3, 2007 |access-date=February 22, 2007 |publisher=Starkiller}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
Filming began on January 11, 1982, and lasted through May 20, 1982, a schedule six weeks shorter than ''Empire''. Kazanjian's schedule pushed shooting as early as possible to give [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM) as much time as possible to work on effects, which left some crew members doubtful of their ability to be fully prepared.<ref name="ProdDir4">{{Cite journal |author=Patterson |first=Richard |date=June 1983 |title=Return of the Jedi: Production and Direction, p. 4 |url=http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg4.htm |url-status=live |journal=[[American Cinematographer]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930185150/http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg4.htm |archive-date=September 30, 2007 |access-date=August 19, 2007}}</ref> Working on a budget of $32.5 million,<ref name="ProdDir1">{{Cite journal |author=Richard Patterson |date=June 1983 |title=Return of the Jedi: Production and Direction, p. 1 |url=http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg1.htm |url-status=live |journal=[[American Cinematographer]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930185039/http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/pdir/pg1.htm |archive-date=September 30, 2007 |access-date=August 19, 2007}}</ref> Lucas was determined to avoid going over budget as had happened with ''Empire''. Kazanjian estimated that using ILM (which was owned by Lucasfilm) for special effects saved the production approximately $18 million.<ref name="ProdDir1" /> However, the fact that Lucasfilm was a non-union company made acquiring shooting locations more difficult and more expensive, even though ''Star Wars'' and ''Empire'' had been big hits.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" /> The production of ''Jedi'' was given the [[Working title#Usage as a ruse title|ruse title]] ''Blue Harvest'' with a tagline of "Horror Beyond Imagination." This disguised the production from fans and the press, and prevented [[price gouging]] by service providers.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" />

The first stage of production spanned 78 days at [[Elstree Studios (Shenley Road)|Elstree Studios]] in England, where the film occupied all nine sound stages.<ref name="ProdDir4" /> The shoot commenced with a scene that was later cut from the film, in which the heroes get caught in a sandstorm as they leave Tatooine.{{Efn|The sandstorm scene was the only major sequence cut from the film during editing.}}<ref name="hearn" /><ref name="ProdDir3" /> For the [[rancor]], Lucas wanted to use a [[Suitmation|stunt performer in a suit]] in the style of the [[Toho]] ''[[Godzilla (franchise)|Godzilla]]'' films. The production team made several attempts, but were unable to create an adequate result. Lucas eventually relented and made the rancor a puppet filmed at high speeds.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" />

In April, the crew moved to Arizona's [[Yuma Desert]] for two weeks of Tatooine exteriors.<ref name="hearn" /> Production then moved to northern California, where the crew shot Endor forest scenes in [[Grizzly Creek Redwoods State Park]] on private land near [[Smith River, California|Smith River]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Hesseltine |first1=Cassandra |title=Complete Filmography of Humboldt County |url=http://humboldt.filmhumboldtdelnorte.org/filmography |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20171013013206/http://humboldt.filmhumboldtdelnorte.org/filmography |archivedate=October 13, 2017 |access-date=October 12, 2017 |website=Humboldt Del Norte Film Commission}}</ref><ref name="film">{{cite web |title=Map of the Movies |url=http://filmhumboldtdelnorte.org/sites/default/files/map-of-the-movies_0.pdf |url-status=deviated |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20191210213635/http://filmhumboldtdelnorte.org/sites/default/files/map-of-the-movies_0.pdf |archivedate=December 10, 2019 |access-date=2019-12-10 |publisher=Humboldt – Del Norte Film Commission}}</ref> [[Principal photography]] concluded with about ten days of [[Chroma key|bluescreen]] shots at ILM in [[San Rafael, California|San Rafael]], California. One small post-production unit shot background matte plates for a day in [[Death Valley]], and a [[Steadicam]] unit shot forest backgrounds for the speeder chase on Endor.<ref name="ProdDir4" /><ref name="Steadi3">{{Cite journal |title=Return of the Jedi: Steadicam Plates, p. 3 |url=http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/stcm/pg3.htm |url-status=live |journal=[[American Cinematographer]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928010949/http://www.theasc.com/magazine/starwars/articles/jedi/stcm/pg3.htm |archive-date=September 28, 2007 |access-date=August 19, 2007}}</ref> [[Garrett Brown]], the inventor of the Steadicam, personally filmed the forest shots at less than one [[frame per second]]. By walking at about {{convert|5|mi/h|km/h|abbr=on|0}} and projecting the footage at 24 frames per second, the motion seen in the film appeared as if it were moving at around {{convert|120|mi/h|km/h|abbr=on}}.<ref name="EmpireOfDreams" /> For long-distance shots of the chase, puppets replaced the actors.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Brooks |first=Dan |title=Dennis Muren on 30 Years of Return of the Jedi |url=https://www.starwars.com/news/interview-dennis-muren-on-return-of-the-jedi |access-date=2026-07-03 |website=StarWars.com |language=en}}</ref> Vader's funeral was filmed at [[Skywalker Ranch]].<ref>{{Cite book |title=Star Wars in 100 Scenes |publisher=DK |year=2014 |isbn=9781465420121}}</ref>{{sfn|Rinzler|2013}}

Marquand and Anthony Daniels clashed on set, which led Daniels to record his [[Dubbing (filmmaking)#ADR/post-sync|dialogue]] with Lucas instead.{{sfn|Daniels|2019|pp=145-146}} Fisher also disliked working with Marquand. He often yelled at her, which caused her to burst into tears on one occasion. The tears ruined her makeup so thoroughly that it took an hour to reapply it.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Stern |first=Marlow |date=2015-12-08 |title=Carrie Fisher's Crazy 'Star Wars' Ride: Cocaine, the Rolling Stones, and That Slave Bikini |work=The Daily Beast |url=https://www.thedailybeast.com/articles/2015/12/08/carrie-fisher-s-crazy-star-wars-ride-cocaine-the-rolling-stones-and-that-slave-bikini |access-date=2023-11-23}}</ref>

=== Post-production ===
Lucasfilm encountered problems when it tested the film in a commercial theater. Lucas and his employees could not hear many of the sound effects they had mixed. To make matters worse, the background noise in the theater muffled the majority of the film's sound, which played through the theater's normal commercial sound system. Many theaters at the time had poor room acoustics with [[Monaural sound|mono sound]]. Lucas would later solve this problem by founding a cinema audio certification company, [[THX]], and ensuring that all theaters playing his films used a sound system that THX developed.<ref>{{cite web |date=February 21, 2017 |title=Star Wars, George Lucas & A Disappearing Cello: The Story of THX Cinema Certification |url=https://medium.com/@thxltd/star-wars-george-lucas-a-disappearing-cello-the-story-of-thx-cinema-certification-81f6aeabb640 |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20230301013605/https://medium.com/@thxltd/star-wars-george-lucas-a-disappearing-cello-the-story-of-thx-cinema-certification-81f6aeabb640 |archivedate=March 1, 2023 |access-date=October 1, 2021}}</ref>

Meanwhile, special effects work at ILM quickly stretched the company to its operational limits. While the experience gained from the previous two films allowed for increased efficiency, this was offset by the desire to have the closing film raise the bar set by each of these films.<ref name="ProdDir1" /> A compounding factor was the intention of several departments of ILM to either take on other film work or decrease staff during slow cycles. Instead, as soon as production began, the entire company found it necessary to remain running 20&nbsp;hours a day on six-day weeks in order to meet its goals by April 1, 1983. In total, ILM created about 900 special effects shots for ''Return of the Jedi''.<ref name="ProdDir4" />

== Music ==
{{Main|Return of the Jedi (soundtrack)}}

[[John Williams]] composed the film's musical score and performed it with the [[London Symphony Orchestra]]. Orchestration credits also include [[Thomas Newman]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Greiving |first=Tim |date=October 17, 2015 |title=When John Williams Can't Go, Whom Does Spielberg Call? Thomas Newman |url=https://www.npr.org/2015/10/17/449417429/composer-thomas-newman-teams-with-spielberg-for-bridge-of-spies |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180130092741/https://www.npr.org/2015/10/17/449417429/composer-thomas-newman-teams-with-spielberg-for-bridge-of-spies |archivedate=January 30, 2018 |access-date=April 4, 2018 |work=NPR.org}}</ref> The initial release of the film's soundtrack was on the [[RSO Records]] label in the United States. In 2004, [[Sony Classical Records]] acquired the rights to both the original trilogy scores and to the soundtracks for the prequel films ''[[The Phantom Menace]]'' and ''[[Attack of the Clones]]''. In the same year, Sony Classical re-pressed the 1997 RCA Victor release of ''Return of the Jedi'' along with the other two films in the trilogy. This set was released with new artwork mirroring the first DVD release of the film. Despite the digital re-mastering, which minimally improved the sound heard only on high-end stereos, the 2004 release has been described as essentially the same as the 1997 RCA Victor release.<ref>{{cite web |title=Star Wars / The Empire Strikes Back / Return of the Jedi (Original Soundtracks&nbsp;– 2004 reissue) |url=http://www.musicweb-international.com/film/2004/Sep04/star_wars.html |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20160410025604/http://www.musicweb-international.com/film/2004/Sep04/star_wars.html |archivedate=April 10, 2016 |access-date=January 20, 2007}}</ref>

== Release ==
[[File:RevengeOTJedi.jpg|thumb|right|upright|''Revenge of the Jedi'' teaser poster with artwork by [[Drew Struzan]]]] 
''Return of the Jedi'' was released on May 25, 1983. It was originally slated to open on May 27, but was subsequently changed to coincide with the date of the 1977 release of the original ''[[Star Wars (film)|Star Wars]]'' film.<ref name="ProdDir1" /> It was the first film to be presented with Lucasfilm's new [[THX]] sound system, with four theaters in the U.S. utilizing the system.<ref name="UpgradingSound">{{cite news |last=Grant |first=Lee |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/asbury-park-press-jedi-producer-upgrad/111582137/ |title='Jedi' producer upgrading sound systems in U.S. theaters
|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260531231015/https://www.newspapers.com/article/asbury-park-press-jedi-producer-upgrad/111582137/ |date=May 18, 1983 |access-date=May 31, 2026 |archive-date=May 31, 2026 |page=41 |work=[[Los Angeles Times|The Los Angeles Times]] |publisher=[[Asbury Park Press]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref> With a massive worldwide marketing campaign, illustrator Tim Reamer created the image for the theatrical poster and other advertising. At the time of its release, the film was advertised on posters and merchandise as simply ''Star Wars: Return of the Jedi'', despite its on-screen "Episode VI" distinction. === Title change ===
The original [[Trailer (promotion)|teaser trailer]] for the film carried the name ''Revenge of the Jedi''.<ref>''Revenge of the Jedi'' Trailer from Star Wars Trilogy Box Set DVD Bonus Disc, 2004.</ref> In December 1982, Lucas decided the word "Revenge" was not appropriate because he believed a Jedi should never seek revenge. He returned the film to its original title, but by that time thousands of "Revenge" teaser posters had been printed and distributed. Lucasfilm stopped the shipping of the posters and sold the remaining stock of 6,800 posters to ''Star Wars'' fan club members for $9.50.<ref>{{cite book |last=Sansweet & Vilmur |title=The Star Wars Poster Book |year=2004 |publisher=Chronicle Books |page=124}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Greg Dean Schmitz |author-link=Greg Dean Schmitz |title=Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith&nbsp;— Greg's Preview |url=https://movies.yahoo.com/movie/preview/1808406060 |url-status=dead |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070219022835/http://movies.yahoo.com/movie/preview/1808406060 |archivedate=February 19, 2007 |access-date=March 5, 2007 |publisher=Yahoo! Movies}}</ref>

== Reception ==

=== Critical response ===
The ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' film critic [[Roger Ebert]] gave ''Return of the Jedi'' four out of four stars, calling it "a complete entertainment, a feast for the eyes and a delight for the fancy." He said that Lucas and his team "keep topping themselves."<ref>{{cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=May 25, 1983 |title=Return of the Jedi |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/return-of-the-jedi-1983 |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20130622173225/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/return-of-the-jedi-1983 |archivedate=June 22, 2013 |access-date=March 10, 2007 |publisher=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' also gave the film his highest possible rating, calling it a "childlike delight" which features "every facet of filmmaking at its best."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Siskel |first=Gene |author-link=Gene Siskel |date=May 25, 1983 |title='Return of the Jedi': Another quality toy in 'Star Wars' line |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-gene-siskel-movie-review/80952847/ |access-date=March 23, 2024 |magazine=[[Chicago Tribune]] |page=1 |via=Newspapers.com |archive-date=March 23, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240323162525/https://www.newspapers.com/article/chicago-tribune-gene-siskel-movie-review/80952847/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Writing in ''[[The Washington Post]]'', Gary Arnold called ''Return of the Jedi'' a "feat of mass enchantment" and "an imposing landmark in contemporary popular culture".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Arnold |first=Gary |date=May 22, 1983 |title=Both Magical & Monstrous, the 'Star Wars' Finale Is a Triumph |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/returnofthejediarnold.htm |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181007070047/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/returnofthejediarnold.htm |archive-date=October 7, 2018 |magazine=[[The Washington Post]] |page=G1}}</ref> [[Colin Greenland]] of ''[[Imagine (game magazine)|Imagine]]'' magazine stated, "You would think a series like ''Star Wars'', fuelled by public adoration, coasting along on the hyperdrive of its own hyperboles, would get inexorably worse. It is not. It is getting better."<ref name="Imagine6">{{cite journal |last=Greenland |first=Colin |author-link=Colin Greenland |date=September 1983 |title=Film Review |journal=[[Imagine (AD&D magazine)|Imagine]] |type=review |publisher=TSR Hobbies (UK), Ltd. |issue=6 |pages=35}}</ref>

[[Sheila Benson]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote that the film is fully satisfying, overflows with "new inventiveness", and carries "a weight and a new maturity" without becoming any less fun than the previous films. She likened the characters in ''Return of the Jedi'' to close friends which the viewer must leave behind as the trilogy ends.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Benson |first=Sheila |date=May 25, 1983 |title='Star Wars' Continues with an Inventive 'Jedi' |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/58299004/ |url-status=live |access-date=March 23, 2024 |magazine=[[Los Angeles Times]] |page=1 |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |archivedate=March 23, 2024 |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20240323162524/https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/58299004/}}</ref> Writing for ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine, Gerald Clarke described ''Jedi'' as better and more satisfying than ''The Empire Strikes Back'', but less exciting than ''Star Wars.''<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=May 23, 1983 |title=Star Wars III: Return of the Jedi |url=http://www.time.com/time/covers/0,16641,1101830523,00.html?internalid=AC |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070323005658/http://www.time.com/time/covers/0,16641,1101830523,00.html?internalid=AC |archive-date=March 23, 2007 |access-date=March 10, 2007 |magazine=Time}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |author=Clarke, Gerald |date=May 23, 1983 |title=Great Galloping Galaxies |url=http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,953915,00.html?internalid=ACA |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071124043907/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,953915,00.html?internalid=ACA |archive-date=November 24, 2007 |access-date=March 12, 2007 |magazine=Time}}</ref> James Harwood of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called the film a "visual treat" featuring an impressive array of creatures, but felt that Mark Hamill was not a skilled enough dramatic actor to carry the plot, and that Carrie Fisher and Billy Dee Williams did not perform as well as in previous films. He thought Harrison Ford was not given enough to do.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Harwood |first=James |date=May 18, 1983 |title=Film Reviews: Return Of The Jedi |url=https://variety.com/1983/film/reviews/return-of-the-jedi-1200425674/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190811093221/https://variety.com/1983/film/reviews/return-of-the-jedi-1200425674/ |archive-date=August 11, 2019 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |page=14}}</ref>

The film received negative reviews, as well. [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' called ''Return of the Jedi'' the "dimmest adventure" of the trilogy.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |date=May 25, 1983 |title=Lucas Returns with the 'Jedi' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/library/film/052583jedi.html |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20060424081513/http://www.nytimes.com/library/film/052583jedi.html |archivedate=April 24, 2006 |access-date=March 10, 2008 |work=The New York Times}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |date=May 29, 1983 |title=The Force Is With Them, But the Magic Is Gone |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1983/05/29/movies/film-view-the-force-is-with-them-but-the-magic-is-gone.html |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150524143431/http://www.nytimes.com/1983/05/29/movies/film-view-the-force-is-with-them-but-the-magic-is-gone.html |archivedate=May 24, 2015 |access-date=September 15, 2019 |work=[[The New York Times]] |page=H15}}</ref> [[Pauline Kael]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' felt the film's imagery was lackluster, and called it ''"''an impersonal and rather junky piece of moviemaking."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Kael |first=Pauline |date=May 30, 1983 |title=The Current Cinema: Fun Machines |magazine=[[The New Yorker]] |page=88}}</ref> In his review for ''[[Ares (magazine)|Ares]]'' magazine, Christopher John called ''Jedi'' a failure and "a cheap and tarnished crown for the series which shook the world of film when it started out".<ref name="Ares">{{cite journal | last=John | first=Christopher | title=Film | journal=[[Ares Magazine]] | publisher=[[TSR, Inc.]] | date=Fall 1983| issue=15 | pages=10–11}}</ref>

== Legacy ==
According to the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 84% of critics have given the film a positive review with an average rating of 7.30/10, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews from critics. The website's critics consensus reads: "Though failing to reach the cinematic heights of its predecessors, ''Return of the Jedi'' remains an entertaining sci-fi adventure and a fitting end to the classic trilogy."<ref name="Rotten">{{cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=star_wars_episode_vi_return_of_the_jedi|title=Star Wars: Episode VI -- Return of the Jedi (1983)|type=m|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161011055840/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/star_wars_episode_vi_return_of_the_jedi/|archive-date=October 11, 2016|url-status=live}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> At [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 58 out of 100 based on 24 reviews from mainstream critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite Metacritic |id=star-wars-episode-vi---return-of-the-jedi |title=Star Wars: Episode VI – Return of the Jedi Reviews |type=m |access-date=April 24, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190512174551/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/star-wars-episode-vi---return-of-the-jedi |archive-date=May 12, 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a rare average grade of "A+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web |title=CinemaScore |url=http://www.cinemascore.com/find_a_movie.epl?id=Return_Of_The_Jedi |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20011226161902/http://www.cinemascore.com/find_a_movie.epl?id=Return_Of_The_Jedi |archive-date=December 26, 2001 |access-date= |website=[[CinemaScore]]}}</ref>

In his review of the [[Changes in Star Wars re-releases|1997 Special Edition re-release]], the film critic James Kendrick called ''Return of the Jedi'' the weakest of the three original films, saying it depended "too much on the slick commercialism" of the franchise but that it was still "a magnificent experience in its own right".<ref>{{cite web |last=Kendrick |first=James |title=Star Wars: Episode VI—Return of the Jedi: Special Edition Review of "Return" |url=http://www.qnetwork.com/?page=review&id=669 |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20080207082448/http://www.qnetwork.com/?page=review&id=669 |archivedate=February 7, 2008 |access-date=March 10, 2008 |publisher=Q Network}}</ref> [[James Berardinelli]] described it as the least enjoyable and least innovative film of the trilogy. He felt that the Special Edition was not saved by the alterations, but that it was nevertheless a "must-see" for fans as the conclusion of the trilogy.<ref>{{cite web |last=Berardinelli |first=James |title=Star Wars Episode VI: Return of the Jedi – Reelviews Movie Reviews |url=https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/s/sw3.html |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20210131212821/https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/s/sw3.html |archivedate=January 31, 2021 |access-date=July 10, 2017 |work=Reelviews Movie Reviews}}</ref>

Some fans have questioned the realism of the scene in ''Jedi'' in which the small Ewoks capably fight with primitive weapons against Imperial troops. Lucas has defended the scene, saying that the Ewoks' purpose was to distract the Imperial troops, and they did not really win.<ref name="DVDcom2">''Star Wars Episode VI: Return of the Jedi'' DVD commentary featuring George Lucas, Ben Burtt, Dennis Muren and Carrie Fisher. 20th Century Fox Home Entertainment, 2004.</ref> The Ewok battle scene was inspired by the [[Vietnam War]], during which the [[Viet Cong]] prevailed against the technologically superior [[United States in the Vietnam War|United States]].<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Immerwahr |first1=Daniel |title=21 The Galactic Vietnam: Technology, Modernization, and Empire in George Lucas's Star Wars |journal=Ideology in U.S. Foreign Relations |date=December 31, 2022 |pages=435–451 |doi=10.7312/nich20180-022|isbn=978-0-231-55427-5}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Walsh |first1=Michael |title=The History of ''Star Wars''{{'}} Ewoks |url=https://nerdist.com/article/the-complete-history-of-ewoks-in-star-wars-movies-books-tv-and-more/ |website=[[Nerdist]] |date=August 10, 2023}}</ref>

=== Novelization ===
{{Main|Return of the Jedi (novel)}}

The novelization of ''Return of the Jedi'' was written by [[James Kahn]] and was released on May 12, 1983, thirteen days before the film's release.<ref>{{Cite book |title=Star Wars, Episode VI&nbsp;— Return of the Jedi (Mass Market Paperback) |date=May 12, 1983 |publisher=Amazon.com |isbn=0-345-30767-4}}</ref>

=== Radio drama ===
{{Main|Star Wars (radio)}}
A three-hour radio drama adaptation of the film was written by [[Brian Daley]] with additional material contributed by [[John Whitman (author)|John Whitman]]. It was broadcast on [[National Public Radio]] in 1996, over a decade after the radio adaptations of the first two ''Star Wars'' films. Anthony Daniels reprised his role as C-3PO, but Mark Hamill and Billy Dee Williams were replaced by Joshua Fardon and [[Arye Gross]], respectively. Bernard Behrens and [[Brock Peters]] reprised their roles as Obi-Wan Kenobi and Darth Vader, respectively. [[John Lithgow]] voiced Yoda, and [[Ed Begley, Jr.]] played Boba Fett. [[Ed Asner]] voiced Jabba the Hutt, speaking only in grunts.<ref>{{cite web |title=Return of the Jedi Produced by NPR |url=http://www.highbridgeaudio.com/returnofjedi.html |url-status=deviated |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20061105043650/http://www.highbridgeaudio.com/returnofjedi.html |archivedate=November 5, 2006 |access-date=March 6, 2007 |work=HighBridge Audio}}</ref>

=== Book-and-record set ===
Lucasfilm adapted the plot of the film for a children's book-and-record set. Released in 1983, the 24-page ''Star Wars: Return of the Jedi'' read-along book was accompanied by a {{frac|33|1|3}} rpm {{convert|7|in|cm|adj=on|disp=flip}} [[gramophone record]]. Each page of the book contained a cropped [[Film frame|frame]] from the film with an abridged and condensed version of the story. The record was produced by [[Walt Disney Records|Buena Vista Records]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=November 23, 2013 |title=Star Wars In The UK: Read Along Adventures |url=https://www.starwars.com/news/star-wars-in-the-uk-read-along-adventures |url-status=live |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20180120071755/http://www.starwars.com/news/star-wars-in-the-uk-read-along-adventures |archivedate=January 20, 2018 |access-date=February 26, 2021}}</ref>
