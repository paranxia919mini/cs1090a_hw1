'''''Breakdown''''' is a 1997 American [[action thriller film]] directed by [[Jonathan Mostow]] from the screenplay he wrote with Sam Montgomery. It stars [[Kurt Russell]] as a man whose wife is kidnapped by a truck driver after their car mysteriously breaks down during a cross-country drive, leading him on a desperate search to track her down and confront her captors. 

The film also stars [[J.&nbsp;T. Walsh]] and [[Kathleen Quinlan]]. The original music score was composed and conducted by [[Basil Poledouris]] and performed by the [[Hollywood Studio Symphony]]. The film was produced by [[Dino De Laurentiis]] and [[Martha De Laurentiis]].

''Breakdown'' was released on May 2, 1997, by [[Paramount Pictures]], and is the final film featuring Walsh to be released in his lifetime.

==Plot ==
Jeff and Amy Taylor are driving from [[Boston]] to [[San Diego]] in a new [[Jeep Grand Cherokee]]. In the Arizona desert, they nearly collide with a battered pickup. At a [[Filling station|gas station]], the pickup's driver, Earl, angrily confronts Jeff before departing. On a remote stretch of road, the Jeep stalls. Amy accepts a lift from passing [[Semi-trailer truck|tractor‑trailer]] driver, Red Barr, to reach a nearby diner and seek assistance, while Jeff remains with the vehicle. 

Inspecting the engine, he discovers the battery cables were deliberately loosened. After reconnecting them, he arrives at the diner, where the owner and patrons insist Amy never appeared. Back on the road, Jeff intercepts Red's rig, but Red denies any knowledge. Sheriff Boyd arrives and, searching the cab, finds nothing and releases Red. He directs Jeff to the sheriff's office in Brackett to report Amy's disappearance.

Returning to the diner, Jeff is approached by Billy, a seemingly slow‑witted mechanic, who claims Amy left with several men and warns him to avoid the police. Following Billy's directions, Jeff is ambushed by Earl. He escapes by plunging the Jeep into a river, but is soon captured by Earl and Billy, whose slow-witted nature was an act. They take him to Barr at an isolated hideout. 

Red threateningly demands that Jeff withdraw the $90,000 he says Amy claimed is in their bank account. At the bank in Brackett, Jeff can access only $5,000, so he conceals one‑dollar bills between two hundred‑dollar notes and pockets a letter opener, abandoning an attempt to alert the bank manager when he suspects Red's accomplices are in the bank watching.

Earl collects Jeff, boasts of sabotaging the Jeep, and states that the couple will be murdered regardless of the money. Earl is enraged upon discovering the ruse just as Jeff frees himself with the opener. He stabs Earl, then binds him with duct tape, while forcing him to reveal a truck stop rendezvous point. Sheriff Boyd pulls the truck over and believes Jeff is the perpetrator. Earl shoots and wounds the sheriff but is shot dead in return. After radioing for aid, Jeff races to the truck stop and finds and hides under Red's trailer and is carried to the latter's remote farm.

Inside Red's [[barn]], Jeff sees trophies from earlier tourist robbery-murders. Billy and another accomplice, Al, arrive with a bound Amy and lock her in a cellar freezer. Armed with a found handgun, Jeff confronts Red and the others inside the house. A distraction from Red's young son Deke allows Billy to escape, but Jeff forces Red to open the barn cellar, releasing Amy. He locks Red, his wife, Deke, and Al in the cellar. Billy frees them while Jeff and Amy flee in a pickup. Red and his group are in pursuit. 

Billy's car flips, killing him in an explosion, and Al fatally crashes into Red's detached trailer. On a steel bridge, Red rams the couple's truck into the guard rail, pinning Amy's leg under the dash while leaving both vehicles perilously poised on the ledge. Jeff boards the tractor unit, wrestles with Red and, after a struggle, hurls him over the edge. Red lands on rocks below, seriously injured. Jeff frees Amy who releases the pickup's brake, dropping the dangling semi onto Red, killing him. The couple wait on the bridge for the authorities to arrive.

==Production==
Filming took place between April and June 1996. ''Breakdown'' was filmed on location in [[Sacramento, California]]; [[Victorville, California]]; [[Pyramid Lake (Los Angeles County, California)|Pyramid Lake]]; and [[Moab, Utah]].<ref>{{Cite Q|Q123575108|p=298}}</ref>

==Music==
The score was written by [[Basil Poledouris]], with contributions from Steve Forman, Judd Miller, Eric Colvin, and [[Richard Marvin (composer)|Richard Marvin]].

It was released as a limited edition of 3,000 units by LaLaLand Records in June 2011.<ref>{{cite web|title=Breakdown: Limited Edition (3-CD Set)|url=http://www.lalalandrecords.com/Breakdown.html|website=La La Land Records|access-date=11 July 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130122044351/http://www.lalalandrecords.com/Breakdown.html|archive-date=January 22, 2013}}</ref> The release comprises a 3-CD set: the first CD contains the score as heard in the film, which contains material from additional composers. This is not 100% complete, omitting a few extremely low-key passages from the early scenes, nor is it chronological – some cues have been combined and re-ordered to maintain a listening experience.

The second CD contains an alternative early version of many cues by Poledouris that represent a different, far more orchestral approach to scoring the film. The score in the film stripped away many layers and left mostly percussive and sound design elements for many cues.

The third CD contains further alternates that demonstrate the changing nature of the music as scenes were re-scored.

==Release==
''Breakdown'' was released in the United States on May 2, 1997.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/60674-BREAKDOWN?sid=fc1d7187-4aeb-46d8-8cee-4c6c0505d181&sr=10.346682&cp=1&pos=0|title= Breakdown (1997)|website=[[American Film Institute]]|accessdate=August 15, 2021}}</ref> After initially opening to 2,108 theaters, the film later expanded to 2,348 theaters. 

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Breakdown'' debuted at first place at the box office with $12.3 million.<ref>{{cite news|last=Puig|first=Claudia|title= Weekend Box Office; Box Office Continues Its Breakout|work=Los Angeles Times|date=1997-05-06|url= https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-05-06-ca-56058-story.html|access-date=2010-11-05}}</ref> 
Ultimately, it grossed a total of $50.2 million in the United States and Canada.<ref>{{cite web|title=Breakdown|publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=breakdown.htm|access-date=2010-12-07}}</ref>

===Critical reception===
The review aggregator website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported that 83% of critics gave the film positive reviews based upon a sample of 58, with an [[weighted mean|average]] score of 7.2/10. The site's consensus describes it as "A brainy and suspenseful – if somewhat uneven – thriller".<ref name="rt">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1077077-breakdown/|title=Breakdown (1997)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=2022-09-03}}</ref>  At [[Metacritic]], which assigns a weighted average rating out of 100 to reviews from mainstream critics, the film received an average score of 74 based on 19 reviews.<ref name="mc">{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/breakdown|title=Breakdown|work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=CNET Networks, Inc.|access-date=2010-12-07}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemascore.com|title=Find CinemaScore|format=Type "Breakdown" in the search box|publisher=[[CinemaScore]]|access-date=November 27, 2025}}</ref>

Peter Stack of the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' praised the film, "''Breakdown'' use[s] old-fashioned ingenuity &ndash; plus a compelling star, a fast-paced mystery and a deadpan villain &ndash; to come up with a sizzler."<ref>{{cite news|last=Stack|first=Peter|title=The Call of the Wild Ride|work=San Francisco Chronicle|date=1997-05-02|url=https://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/The-Call-of-the-Wild-Ride-Breakdown-thrills-2841938.php|access-date=2010-12-07}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] gave the film a positive review, calling it "taut, skillful and surgically effective".<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=1997-05-02 |title=Breakdown |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/breakdown-1997 |access-date=2025-12-16 |work=Chicago Sun-Times}}</ref> 

Stephen Hunter of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' criticized Russell for not conveying a desperate husband willing to fight for his missing wife, writing "He does a lot of running around while making desperate faces, but he never projects a sense of deep rage. He never gets dangerous. Thus the movie is shorn of its one primitive gratification: the image of the civilized man who finds the Peruvian commando inside himself and lays waste to louts who have underestimated him."<ref>{{cite news|last=Hunter|first=Stephen|title='Breakdown': Heck on Wheels|newspaper=The Washington Post|date=1997-05-02|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/review97/breakdownhunter.htm|access-date=2010-12-07}}</ref>

===Home media and rights===
[[Paramount Home Entertainment]] released the film on a domestic [[VHS]] in October 1997, and a [[DVD]] followed in 1998.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Breakdown-DVD-by-Kurt-Russell/dp/B01I077IPO|title=Amazon.com}}</ref> It was released on [[LaserDisc]] in the US in October 1997, with a subtitled Hong Kong LaserDisc being released in November 1997, via ERA Home Entertainment.<ref name="ld"/> The following year, it also received LaserDisc releases in France, Germany, Japan and Singapore.<ref name="ld">{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/search.php?search=Breakdown&sort=title|title=LaserDisc Database - Search - Breakdown|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref> The French LaserDisc was handled by PFC Vidéo,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/35709/629935/Breakdown|title=LaserDisc Database - Breakdown [629935] on LD LaserDisc|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref> with the German LaserDisc being handled by Laser Paradise.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/43637/1964-114/Breakdown|title=LaserDisc Database - Breakdown [1964-114] on LD LaserDisc|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref> The French and German LaserDiscs both featured dubbed audio tracks, unlike [[Pioneer LDC]]'s Japanese LaserDisc, which featured the original audio track with Japanese subtitles.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/12095/PILF-2584/Breakdown|title=LaserDisc Database - Breakdown [PILF-2584] on LD LaserDisc|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref>

''Breakdown'' was a co-production between [[Dino De Laurentiis|Dino De Laurentiis Company]] and [[Spelling Films]], with distribution handled by Paramount Pictures. In the credits, Paramount Pictures and Spelling Films were listed as the joint copyright holders. At the time of the film's release, Paramount's parent company [[Viacom (1952–2005)|Viacom]] held a majority stake in Spelling Films, part of [[Spelling Entertainment]]. They acquired their stake in Spelling Entertainment in 1994, through the purchase of [[Blockbuster (retailer)|Blockbuster]], which itself had a 67% stake in Spelling Entertainment since 1993. Viacom bought the remaining stake in Spelling Entertainment in March 1999, which further solidified Paramount's ownership of ''Breakdown''.<ref>{{Cite news |date=1999-03-20 |title=COMPANY NEWS; VIACOM AGREES TO BUY REST OF SPELLING ENTERTAINMENT (Published 1999) |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1999/03/20/business/company-news-viacom-agrees-to-buy-rest-of-spelling-entertainment.html |access-date=2025-11-27 |work=The New York Times |language=en}}</ref>

It received an Australian [[Blu-ray]] release in February 2021, from local distributor Imprint, under license from Paramount. This was the film's first ever Blu-ray release and initially limited to 1,500 copies.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Breakdown-Blu-ray/281480/|title=Breakdown Blu-ray (Imprint #29) (Australia)|via=www.blu-ray.com}}</ref> A US Blu-ray was released by Paramount Home Entertainment in September 2021, under its "Paramount Presents" line.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com.au/Breakdown-Blu-Ray-Kurt-Russell/dp/B08Y7GJ1CV|title=Server Busy}}</ref> The US Blu-ray featured a new [[audio commentary]] with Jonathan Mostow and Kurt Russell, and a new interview with Kathleen Quinlan. It featured an alternate opening of the film and an isolated score.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thedigitalbits.com/reviews/item/breakdown-pp-2021-brd|title=Breakdown (Blu-ray Review)|first=Dennis|last=Seuling|date=October 18, 2021|website=The Digital Bits}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Breakdown-Blu-ray/30457/|title=Breakdown Blu-ray Paramount Presents|date=September 21, 2021|accessdate=August 15, 2021}}</ref> 

Paramount Home Entertainment released it on a [[4K Ultra HD]] Blu-ray in October 2024.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/138461/breakdown4kultrahdblurayparamountscares.html|title=4k Movie, Streaming, Blu-Ray Disc, and Home Theater Product Reviews & News &#124; High Def Digest|website=ultrahd.highdefdigest.com}}</ref> This was part of its ''Paramount Scares'' line of releases (focusing on [[Horror film|horror]] films), even though ''Breakdown'' is generally categorized as a thriller film rather than as a horror film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/breakdown-am7460|title=Breakdown (1997) - Jonathan Mostow &#124; Synopsis, Movie Info, Moods, Themes and Related &#124; AllMovie|via=www.allmovie.com}}</ref>
