'''''Omen''''' ({{langx|fr|'''Augure'''|translation=Augury}}) is a 2023 [[drama film]] written and directed by [[Belgians|Belgian]]-Congolese director [[Baloji (rapper)|Baloji]]. Starring [[Marc Zinga]], [[Lucie Debay]] and [[Eliane Umuhire]],<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://cineuropa.org/en/film/441368/ |title= Omen |last= Baloji |date=19 October 2023|access-date=19 October 2023 |website= [[Cineuropa]] |language=en}}</ref> it premiered at [[2023 Cannes Film Festival|Cannes]] in the [[Un Certain Regard]]  section on 23 May 2023, where it won the New Voice Prize.<ref name="cannes:ver">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2023/film/news/cannes-film-festival-2023-palme-dor-prizes-awards-1235627110/ |title=Cannes Awards: 'Anatomy of a Fall' Takes Palme d'Or, 'The Zone of Interest' and 'The Pot au Feu' Among Winners|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|first=Peter|last=Debruge|date=27 May 2023|access-date=19 October 2023}}</ref>

A co-production between [[Belgium]], [[Democratic Republic of the Congo|Democratic Republic of Congo]], the [[Netherlands]], [[France]], [[Germany]] and [[South Africa]], it was selected as the [[List of Belgian submissions for the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Belgian entry]] for [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]] at the [[96th Academy Awards]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/omen-movie-belgium-2024-oscars-international-feature-1235590847/|title=Oscars: Belgium Picks 'Omen' as International Feature Submission|first=Scott|last=Roxborough|date=September 14, 2023|website=Hollywood Reporter}}</ref>

In 2023/24 movie director Baloji held the exhibition ''Baloji Augurism'' in the [[ModeMuseum Antwerpen|MOMU]] fashion museum in [[Antwerp]], Belgium inspired by the traditional African costumes that he had designed for his film along with Elke Hoste.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wolf |first=Ellen De |date=2023-09-14 |title=Shooting {{!}} Baloji en mots et en photos, pour les tendances Homme de l'automne |url=https://weekend.levif.be/lifestyle/mode/shooting-baloji-en-mots-et-en-photos-pour-les-tendances-homme-de-lautomne/ |access-date=2023-10-18 |website=Weekend |language=fr-BE}}</ref>

== Premise ==
Congolese man Koffi was disowned by his mother in his youth for being a so-called "zabolo", [[Swahili]] for evil sorcerer. Now he returns to Congo from Europe to visit his family, along with his white pregnant girlfriend. The visit does not go well as the ancient rituals and persistent superstitions of the local tribes continue to haunt Koffi.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=2023-05-30 |title="Augure" by Baloji Tshiani Wins the New Voices Award at Cannes |url=https://www.acp-ue-culture.eu/en/blog/augure-by-baloji-tshiani-wins-the-new-voices-award-at-cannes/ |website=ACP-EU Culture}}</ref>

The film is divided into four chapters, loosely corresponding to each of the main characters' storylines: ''Koffi'', ''Paco'', ''Tshala and'' ''Mujila.''<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sajip |first=Arjun |date=2023-05-22 |title=‘Omen’ Review: A Congo Culture Clash Takes a Turn for the Occult |url=https://www.indiewire.com/criticism/movies/omen-review-congo-baloji-1234865316/ |access-date=2025-07-27 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Production ==
The head director and screenwriter is the [[Belgians|Belgian]]-Congolese [[Baloji (rapper)|Baloji]], also known as the music artist of [[Starflam]], among others. The company Wrong Men produced the film. The production budget was 1 million euros. The film received financial support from the [[VRT (broadcaster)|VRT]], the [[RTBF]], the Centre du cinéma de la Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, screen.brussels, the Dutch Audiovisual Fund and the Federal Tax Service, among others. Filming lasted 23 days and took place at 18 different locations. Augure was released in Belgian theaters on November 15, 2023, and is distributed internationally under the name Omen.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=2023-09-14 |title="Augure" selected as Belgian Oscar entry |url=https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/en/2023/09/14/_augure_-selected-as-belgian-oscar-entry/ |access-date=2023-10-18 |website=vrtnws.be |language=en}}</ref>

==Release==

''Omen'' had its premiere at [[2023 Cannes Film Festival]] in '[[Un Certain Regard]]' on 23 May 2023. It was invited to Horizons section of [[57th Karlovy Vary International Film Festival]], where it was screened on 30 June 2023.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.kviff.com/en/programme/film/63/41036-omen |title= 57th Karlovy Vary International Film Festival: Horizons ''Omen'' |date= 30 June 2023|access-date=19 October 2023 |website= Karlovy Vary International Film Festival |language=en}}</ref> It was screened at the [[2023 Festival du nouveau cinéma]] in International Panorama on 8 October,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://nouveaucinema.ca/en/2023/international-panorama |title= International Panorama: ''Omen''|date=2 October 2023|access-date=19 October 2023 |website= Festival du nouveau cinéma|language=en}}</ref> then at [[2023 BFI London Film Festival]] in 'Strand' section under 'Dare' theme on 12 October.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2023/08/london-film-festival-bfi-the-book-of-clarence-1235531461/|title=BFI London Film Festival Full Lineup: 'The Book Of Clarence' World Premiere; Scorsese, Miyazaki, Lanthimos & Fincher Among Headline Galas|website=[[Deadline Hollywood|Deadline]]|first=Zac|last=Ntim|date=31 August 2023|access-date=19 October 2023}}</ref>

''Omen'' was released in Belgium cinemas on 15 November 2023 by Imagine Film.<ref name="rel:sitges">{{Cite web|url=https://www.wbimages.be/en/news/augure-selectionne-a-sitges0/ |title= Omen awarded at Sitges! |date=16 October 2023|access-date=19 October 2023 |website= WB Images|language=en}}</ref>

==Reception==

On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] website, the film has an approval rating of 83% based on 18 reviews, with an average rating of 7.4/10. The critics' consensus reads: "Visually striking and hauntingly surreal, ''Omen'' makes up in mesmerizing imagery and ambition whatever it lacks in narrative legibility."<ref name="score:rotten">{{cite Rotten Tomatoes |title= Omen (2023, Drama) |id= omen_2023 |type=m |access-date=January 2, 2024}}</ref>

[[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film a score of four out of five stars, writing that the film explores and deconstructs the concept of a [[culture clash]]: "culture, heritage, nationality and identity are all shapeshifting concepts here. Perhaps ''Omen'' doesn't completely hang together but it is bold, risky, exciting film-making."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2024/apr/24/omen-augure-review-secrets-and-sorcery-in-congolese-homecoming-baloji|title=Omen (Augure) review – Baloji offers secrets and sorcery in Congolese homecoming|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|author-link=Peter Bradshaw|date=24 April 2024|website=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=6 May 2024}}</ref> ''[[Rue Morgue (magazine)|Rue Morgue]]''{{'}}s Michelle Martin praised the film's performances and visuals, and called it, "[a] stunning exploration of Congolese cultural beliefs posits that things that we deem 'occult' exist on the same plane as that which we view as 'scientific,' and the result is captivating."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://rue-morgue.com/cuff-24-review-omen/|title=CUFF '24 Review: Genre-Blending "Omen" Conjures a Magical Reality|last=Martin|first=Michelle|date=1 May 2024|website=[[Rue Morgue (magazine)|Rue Morgue]]|access-date=6 May 2024}}</ref>

Peyton Robinson of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film three out of four stars, calling it "a visually enthralling piece of [[magical realism]] proposing ideas on pariahs, culture, and individuality in a world with constantly changing rules. But in devoting so much work to the aesthetic, it falls behind in making sense of its phantasmagoric storylines."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/omen-film-review-2024|title=Omen movie review & film summary (2024)|last=Robinson|first=Peyton|date=26 April 2024|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|access-date=6 May 2024}}</ref> Beatrice Loayza of ''[[The New York Times]]'' characterized the film as ambitious, highlighting its "vivid symbolism" and, "In its best moments, a quiet element of absurdity [that] grounds the spectacle", but lamenting that "the film's frenetic world-building eventually becomes numbing, in part because the uneven human dramas—each one offers a vague message about marginalization—lose momentum in all the commotion."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/04/11/movies/omen-review.html|title='Omen' Review: Life in a Different Space-Time Continuum|last=Loayza|first=Beatrice|date=11 April 2024|website=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=6 May 2024}}</ref>
