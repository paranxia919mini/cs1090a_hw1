The [[September 11 attacks]] on the [[World Trade Center (1973–2001)|World Trade Center]] elicited a large response of local emergency and rescue personnel to assist in the evacuation of the two towers, resulting in a large loss of the same personnel when the [[Collapse of the World Trade Center|towers collapsed]]. After the attacks, the media termed the [[World Trade Center site]] "[[Ground zero#World Trade Center|Ground Zero]]", while rescue personnel referred to it as "The Pile".

In the ensuing recovery and cleanup efforts, personnel related to the metalwork and construction professions would descend on the site to offer their services and remained until the site was cleared in May 2002. In the years since, investigations and studies have examined effects upon those who participated, noting a variety of afflictions attributed to the debris and stress.

==Building evacuation==
{{See also|Casualties of the September 11 attacks#Evacuation}}
After [[American Airlines Flight 11]] crashed into the North Tower (1 WTC) of the World Trade Center, a standard announcement was given to tenants in the South Tower (2 WTC) to stay put and that the building was secure. However, many defied those instructions and proceeded to evacuate the South Tower<ref name="Fritsch">{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/12/us/a-day-of-terror-the-response-rescue-workers-rush-in-and-many-do-not-return.html|title=A Day of Terror – The Response: Rescue Workers Rush In, and Many Do Not Return |date=September 12, 2001 |newspaper=New York Times |author=Fritsch, Jane}}</ref> (most notably, [[Rick Rescorla]], Security Director at [[Morgan Stanley]], evacuated 2,687 of the 2,700 Morgan Stanley employees in the building). People evacuating from WTC 2 were ordered up from the lobby level to a door on the mezzanine level that led to a covered footbridge over West Street to a building complex then called [[Brookfield Place (New York City)|the World Financial Center]]. People evacuating from WTC 1 were directed from the lobby level through the WTC shopping mall beneath [[Austin J. Tobin Plaza|the outdoor plaza]]. The firefighters directing evacuees did not want anyone going through the front doors because of falling debris and falling people.

Standard evacuation procedures for fires in the World Trade Center called for evacuating only the floors immediately above and below the fire, as simultaneous evacuation of up to 50,000 workers would be too chaotic.<ref name="Fritsch" />

=== Firefighters ===
[[File:WTC-remnant highres.jpg|thumb|left|upright|A firefighter looks up at the remains of the South Tower. (September 13, 2001)]]
Firefighters from the [[New York City Fire Department]] (FDNY) rushed to the World Trade Center minutes after the first plane struck the North Tower. Chief [[Joseph W. Pfeifer]] and his crew with Battalion 1 were among the first on the scene (Battalion 1 was the first Unit to notify the Manhattan Central Office.). Engine 10 and Ladder 10 were also some of the first on scene because their firehouse was directly across the street from the Towers.<ref>{{cite news |title=Terrorist Attack At New York World Trade Center |url=http://www.firehouse.com/terrorist/911/magazine/harvey.html |author=Eisner, Harvey |publisher=Firehouse Magazine |date=April 2002 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090927183623/http://www.firehouse.com/terrorist/911/magazine/harvey.html |archive-date=September 27, 2009 |access-date=November 24, 2006 }}</ref> At 8:50&nbsp;a.m., an [[Incident Command Post]] was established in the lobby of the North Tower. By 9:00&nbsp;a.m., shortly before [[United Airlines Flight 175]] hit the South Tower, the FDNY chief had arrived and taken over command of the response operations. Concerned about falling debris, he moved the incident command center to a spot located across [[West Side Highway|West Street]], but numerous fire chiefs remained in the lobby which continued to serve as an operations post where alarms, elevators, communications systems, and other equipment were operated. The initial response by the FDNY was on rescue and evacuation of building occupants, which involved sending firefighters up to assist people that were trapped in elevators and elsewhere. Firefighters were also required to ensure all floors were completely evacuated.<ref name="mckinsey-exec">{{cite web |url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/executive_summary.pdf |title=FDNY McKinsey Report – Executive Summary |date=August 2002 |publisher=FDNY / McKinsey & Company |access-date=July 10, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181225172049/http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/executive_summary.pdf%20 |archive-date=December 25, 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Numerous [[staging area]]s were set up near the World Trade Center, where responding fire units could report and get deployment instructions.  However, many firefighters arrived at the World Trade Center without stopping at the staging areas, partly because at 8:48&nbsp;a.m. Battalion 1 transmitted a Third Alarm and ordered third alarm units to the Staging Area and second alarm units to the Towers.  As a result, many chiefs could not keep track of the whereabouts of their units.  Numerous firefighters reported directly to the building lobbies and were ordered by those commanding the operating post to proceed into the building.<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/>

Problems with [[Communication during the September 11 attacks#Radio communications|radio communication]] caused commanders to lose contact with many of the firefighters who went into the buildings.  The [[radio repeater|repeater system]] in the World Trade Center, which was required for portable radio signals to transmit reliably, was malfunctioning after the impact of the planes.<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/>  As a result, firefighters were unable to report to commanders on their progress, and were unable to hear evacuation orders. In addition, many off-duty firefighters arrived to help without their radios.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2002/01/23/nyregion/ground-zero-firefighting-9-11-inspires-call-to-review-response-plan-for-crises.html| title=9/11 Inspires Call to Review Response Plan For Crises |newspaper=The New York Times |date=January 23, 2002 |author1=Lipton, Eric  |author2=James Glanz |name-list-style=amp }}</ref>  FDNY commanders lacked communication with the [[New York City Police Department]] (NYPD), who had helicopters at the scene, or with [[Emergency Medical Services]] (EMS) dispatchers.  The firefighters on the scene also did not have access to television reports or other outside information, which could help in assessing the situation.  When the South Tower collapsed at 9:59&nbsp;a.m., firefighters in the North Tower were not aware of exactly what happened.  The battalion chief in the North Tower lobby immediately issued an order over the radio for firefighters in the tower to evacuate, but many did not hear the order on the faulty radios. Because of this, 342 firefighters died in the collapse of the towers.<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/americas/6294198.stm|title=NY Firefighters attack Giuliani|publisher=BBC News|date=July 12, 2007}}</ref>

The command post located across West Street was taken out when the South Tower collapsed, making command and control even more difficult and disorganized.  When the North Tower collapsed, falling debris killed [[Peter Ganci]], the FDNY chief.<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/>  Following the [[collapse of the World Trade Center]], a command post was set up at a firehouse in [[Greenwich Village]].

The FDNY deployed more than 200 units (about half of all units) to the site,<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/> with more than 400 firefighters on the scene when the buildings collapsed.<ref name="Fritsch"/>  This included a total of 121 engine companies, 62 ladder companies, and other special units.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/x7_fire_apparatus_deployment.pdf |title=Fire Apparatus Deployment on September 11 |publisher=FDNY / McKinsey & Company |date=August 2002 |access-date=July 10, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181225172548/http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/x7_fire_apparatus_deployment.pdf%20 |archive-date=December 25, 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref>  The FDNY also received assistance from fire departments in [[Nassau County, New York|Nassau]], [[Suffolk County, New York|Suffolk]], [[Westchester County, New York|Westchester County]], and other neighboring jurisdictions, but with limited ability to manage and coordinate efforts.<ref name="mckinsey-exec"/>
Besides assisting with recovery operations at Ground Zero, volunteer firefighters from Long Island and Westchester manned numerous firehouses throughout the city to assist with other fire and emergency calls. [[Steve Buscemi]] helped as a volunteer the day after the attacks.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/steve-buscemi-9-11-firefighter-twin-towers-b2409173.html |title=On 9/11, Steve Buscemi returned to his old firefighter job to help search for survivors |newspaper=The Independent |date=September 11, 2023 |author1=O' Connor, Roisin|name-list-style=amp }}</ref>

=== Doctors, EMS, and other medical staff ===
FDNY [[emergency medical technician]]s (EMTs) and [[paramedic]]s, along with 9-1-1 system ambulances operated by voluntary hospitals and volunteer ambulance corps, began arriving at 8:53&nbsp;a.m., and quickly set up a staging area outside the North Tower, at West Street, which was quickly moved over to the corner of Vesey and West Streets.  As more providers responded to the scene, five triage areas were set up around the World Trade Center site. EMS chiefs experienced difficulties communicating via their radios because of the overwhelming volume of radio traffic. At 9:45, an additional [[Dispatch (logistics)|dispatch]] channel was set aside for use by chiefs and supervisors only, but many did not know about this and continued to operate on the other channel. The communication difficulties meant that commanders lacked good [[situational awareness]].<ref name="mckinsey-ems">{{cite web |url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/ems_response.pdf |title=McKinsey Report – Emergency Medical Service response |publisher=FDNY / McKinsey & Company |date=August 9, 2002 |access-date=July 12, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181225172600/http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/ems_response.pdf%20 |archive-date=December 25, 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[Dispatcher]]s at the [[9-1-1]] call center, who coordinate EMS response and assign units, were overwhelmed with incoming calls, as well as communications over the radio system.  Dispatchers were unable to process and make sense of all the incoming information, including information from people trapped in the towers, about conditions on the upper floors.  Overwhelmed dispatchers were unable to effectively give instructions and manage the situation.<ref name="mckinsey-ems"/>

EMS personnel were in disarray after the collapse of the South Tower at 9:59&nbsp;a.m.  Following the collapse of the North Tower at 10:28&nbsp;a.m., EMS commanders regrouped on the North End of [[Battery Park City, Manhattan|Battery Park City]], at the [[Embassy Suites Hotel]].  Around 11:00&nbsp;a.m., EMS triage centers were relocated and consolidated at the [[Chelsea Piers]] and the [[Staten Island Ferry]] Terminal.<ref name="mckinsey-ems"/>  Throughout the early afternoon, the soundstages at the pier were separated into two areas, one for the more seriously injured and one for the walking wounded. On the acute side, multiple makeshift tables, each with a physician, nurse, and other health care workers, and non-emergency service volunteers, were set up for the arrival of mass casualties.

Supplies, including equipment for airway and vascular control, were obtained from neighboring hospitals. Throughout the afternoon, local merchants arrived to donate food. Despite this, few patients arrived for treatment, the earliest at about 5 p.m. around the time that [[7 World Trade Center (1987-2001)#Collapse|Building 7]] collapsed, and were not seriously injured, being limited to smoke inhalation. An announcement was made around 6–7 p.m. that a second shift of providers would cover the evening shift, and that an area was being set up for the day personnel to sleep. Soon after, when it was realized that few would have survived the collapse and be brought to the piers, many decided to leave and the area was closed down.

===Police===
[[File:September 11th NYPD TEMP HQ Burger King WTC New York City.jpg|thumb|right|upright|Temporary NYPD headquarters at the [[Burger King]] at 106 Liberty Street, near the World Trade Center on September 11, 2001.]]
The [[New York City Police Department]] (NYPD) quickly responded with the [[New York City Police Department Emergency Service Unit|Emergency Service Unit]]s (ESU) and other responders after the crash of [[American Airlines Flight 11]] into the North Tower.<ref name="mckinsey-nypd">{{cite web |url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/nypd/pdf/nypdemergency.pdf |title=McKinsey Report – NYPD |date=August 19, 2002 |access-date=July 10, 2007|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070615060709/http://www.nyc.gov/html/nypd/pdf/nypdemergency.pdf <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date = June 15, 2007}}</ref>  The NYPD set up its incident command center at Church Street and Vesey Street, on the opposite side of the World Trade Center from where the FDNY was commanding its operations.<ref>{{cite book |author1=Dwyer, Jim  |author2=Kevin Flynn  |name-list-style=amp |title=102 Minutes |chapter-url=https://archive.org/details/102minutesuntold00dwye  |chapter-url-access=registration  |publisher=Times Books |year=2005 |chapter=Prologue|isbn=978-0805076820 }}</ref>  NYPD helicopters were soon at the scene, reporting on the status of the burning buildings.  When the buildings collapsed, 23 NYPD officers were killed, along with 37 [[Port Authority Police Department]] officers. The NYPD helped facilitate the evacuation of civilians out of Lower Manhattan, including approximately 5,000 civilians evacuated by the Harbor Unit to [[Staten Island]] and to [[New Jersey]].<ref name="mckinsey-nypd"/>  In ensuing days, the police department worked alternating 12-hour shifts to help in the rescue and recovery efforts.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/17/us/after-attacks-safety-police-officers-swiftly-show-inventiveness-during-crisis.html|title=Police Officers Swiftly Show Inventiveness During Crisis |newspaper=The New York Times |date=September 17, 2001 |author=Rashbaum, William K.}}</ref>

===Amateur radio===
{{Main|Communication during the September 11 attacks}}
[[Amateur radio]] played a role in the rescue and clean-up efforts. Amateur radio operators established communications, maintained emergency networks, and formed bucket brigades with hundreds of other volunteer personnel. Approximately 500 amateur radio operators volunteered their services during the disaster and recovery.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nyc-arecs.org/ARRLWeb+ARES-RACES+NYC+Effort+Stands+Down;+REACT+Still+Seeking+Amateur+Operators.htm |title=ARES/RACES NYC Effort Stands Down; REACT Still Seeking Amateur Operators |date=September 25, 2001|access-date=April 8, 2018|url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110727134117/http://www.nyc-arecs.org/ARRLWeb%20ARES-RACES%20NYC%20Effort%20Stands%20Down;%20REACT%20Still%20Seeking%20Amateur%20Operators.htm|archive-date=July 27, 2011}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.arrl.org/arrlletter?issue=2001-09-21|title=ARRL Letter|website=www.arrl.org|access-date=April 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180409043110/http://www.arrl.org/arrlletter?issue=2001-09-21|archive-date=April 9, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Search and rescue efforts==
On the day following the attacks, eleven people were rescued from the rubble, including six [[firefighter]]s and three [[police officer]]s.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://transcripts.cnn.com/TRANSCRIPTS/0109/12/lkl.00.html |title=America Under Attack: The Aftermath, Larry King Live |publisher=CNN |date=September 12, 2001<!-- 21:00 ET--> |access-date=March 22, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080202000951/http://transcripts.cnn.com/TRANSCRIPTS/0109/12/lkl.00.html |archive-date=February 2, 2008 |url-status=live }}</ref> One woman was rescued from the rubble, near where a [[West Side Highway]] pedestrian bridge had been.<ref>{{cite news|author=McFadden, Robert D.|date=September 13, 2001|title=Stunned Rescuers Comb Attack Sites, But Thousands are Presumed Dead|newspaper=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/13/us/after-attacks-overview-stunned-rescuers-comb-attack-sites-but-thousands-are.html|access-date=September 12, 2021|archive-date=September 13, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210913010005/https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/13/us/after-attacks-overview-stunned-rescuers-comb-attack-sites-but-thousands-are.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Two PAPD officers, [[John McLoughlin (9/11 attacks survivor)|John McLoughlin]] and [[Will Jimeno]], were also rescued. Discovered by former U.S. Marines [[Jason Thomas (Marine)|Jason Thomas]] and [[Dave Karnes]], McLoughlin and Jimeno were pulled out alive after spending nearly 24 hours beneath {{convert|30|ft|m|0}} of rubble.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/12/us/a-day-of-terror-the-hopes-survivors-are-found-in-the-rubble.html| title=A Day of Terror: The Hopes; Survivors Are Found In the Rubble |newspaper=The New York Times |date=September 12, 2001 |author=Murphy, Dean E.}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/13/us/after-the-attacks-alive-entombed-for-a-day-then-found.html|title=After the Attacks: Alive; Entombed for a Day, Then Found |newspaper=The New York Times |date=September 13, 2001 |author=Filkins, Dexter}}</ref> Their rescue was later portrayed in the 2006 film ''[[World Trade Center (film)|World Trade Center]]''. In total, twenty survivors were pulled out of the rubble. The final survivor, [[Port Authority of New York and New Jersey|Port Authority]] secretary [[Genelle Guzman-McMillan]], was rescued 27 hours after the collapse of the North Tower.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Shaer|first1=Matthew|title=Survivor, Last Pulled Out|url=https://nymag.com/news/9-11/10th-anniversary/last-survivor/|access-date=September 10, 2016|work=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|date=August 27, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160914173216/http://nymag.com/news/9-11/10th-anniversary/last-survivor/|archive-date=September 14, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|last1=Warrick|first1=Joby|last2=Mekhennet|first2=Souad|title=In the shadow of the towers: Five lives and a world transformed|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/interactive/2021/9-11-attacks-lives-transformed/|date=September 2, 2021|access-date=September 13, 2021|newspaper=Washington Post|language=en|archive-date=September 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210926221927/https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/interactive/2021/9-11-attacks-lives-transformed/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Some firefighters and civilians who survived made cell phone calls from voids beneath the rubble, though the amount of debris made it difficult for rescue workers to get to them.<ref>{{Cite web|date=April 15, 2014|title=Rescuers hear calls from victims trapped in rubble|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/rescuers-hear-calls-victims-trapped-rubble-9260893.html|access-date=September 13, 2021|website=The Independent|language=en|archive-date=September 13, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210913011040/https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/rescuers-hear-calls-victims-trapped-rubble-9260893.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Neilan |first=Terence |date=September 13, 2001 |title=Hopes Are Raised, and Dashed, About Rescue of Firefighters |language=en-US |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/13/nyregion/hopes-are-raised-and-dashed-about-rescue-of-firefighters.html |access-date=October 1, 2023 |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

By Wednesday night, 82 deaths had been confirmed by officials in New York City.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-sep-13-mn-45260-story.html|title=Agonizing Search for Survivors; New York: Rescue efforts continue on thin hopes. Rescue efforts were paused numerous times in the days after the attack because nearby buildings, including [[One Liberty Plaza]], were in danger of collapsing.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2001/09/14/a-fight-vs-steel-despair-weather-rescuers-press-on-valiantly-despite-odds/|title=A fight vs. steel, despair & weather Rescuers press on valiantly, despite odds |newspaper=Daily News (New York) |date=September 14, 2001 |author=Heisler, Bob}}</ref> The last fires at the World Trade Center site were extinguished on December 20, exactly 100 days after the attacks.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2001/dec/20/september11.usa |title=Ground Zero stops burning, after 100 days |website=The Guardian |date=December 20, 2001 |access-date=May 5, 2018 |archive-date=July 13, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180713032752/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2001/dec/20/september11.usa |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Organization===
[[New York City Emergency Management|New York City Office of Emergency Management]] (OEM) was the agency responsible for coordination of the city's response to the attacks. Headed by then-Director [[Richard Sheirer]], the agency was forced to vacate its headquarters, located in 7 World Trade Center, within hours of the attack. The building later collapsed. OEM reestablished operations temporarily at the police academy, where Mayor Giuliani gave many press conferences throughout the afternoon and evening of September 11. By Friday, rescue and reliefs were organized and administered from Pier 92 on the [[Hudson River]].<ref name="cohen">{{cite journal |author=Steven Cohen |author2=William Eimicke |author3=Jessica Horan |name-list-style=amp |title=Catastrophe and the Public Service: A Case Study of the Government Response to the Destruction of the World Trade Center |journal=Public Administration Review |date=September 2002 |volume=62 | page = 24 | doi = 10.1111/1540-6210.62.s1.6}}</ref>

Volunteers quickly descended on Ground Zero to help in the rescue and recovery efforts.  At [[Jacob Javits Convention Center]], thousands showed up to offer help, where they registered with authorities.<ref>{{cite news |author=Stamey, Mark |title=Volunteers are Lining Up by the Thousands |newspaper=The New York Post |date=September 15, 2001}}</ref>  Construction projects around the city came to a halt, as workers walked off the jobs to help at Ground Zero.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/15/us/after-attacks-real-estate-hard-hats-volunteer-rubble-city-s-building-boom-falls.html|title=As Hard Hats Volunteer in Rubble, City's Building Boom Falls Into Doubt |newspaper=The New York Times |date=September 15, 2001 |author1=Blumenthal, Ralph  |author2=Charles V. Bagli |name-list-style=amp }}</ref>  Ironworkers, welders, steel burners, and others with such skills were in high demand.  By the end of the first week, over one thousand ironworkers from across North America had arrived to help, along with countless others.<ref>{{cite news |title=Ironworkers Offer Help at WTC |newspaper=Newsday |date=September 15, 2001 |author=Hetter, Katia}}</ref>

The [[New York City Department of Design & Construction]] oversaw the recovery efforts.  Beginning on September 12, the [[Structural Engineers Association of New York]] (SEAoNY) became involved in the recovery efforts, bringing in experts to review the stability of the rubble, evaluate safety of hundreds of buildings near the site, and designing support for the cranes brought in to clear the debris. The City of New York hired the engineering firm, LZA-[[Thornton Tomasetti]], to oversee the structural engineering operations at the site.<ref name="domel">{{cite web |author=Domel, August Jr. |date=November 2001 |url=http://www.ncsea.com/downloads/wtcseerp.pdf |title=World Trade Center Disaster: Structural Engineers at Ground Zero |access-date=December 12, 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061214075703/http://www.ncsea.com/downloads/wtcseerp.pdf |archive-date=December 14, 2006 |url-status=live }}</ref>

To make the effort more manageable, the World Trade Center site was divided into four quadrants or zones. Each zone was assigned a lead contractor, and a team of three structural engineers, subcontractors, and rescue workers.<ref name="domel"/>
* [[AMEC]] – North Tower along [[West Side Highway|West Street]]
* [[Bovis Lend Lease]] – South Tower along Liberty Street
* Tully Construction Company, Inc. – Eastern portion of the WTC site
* Turner/Plaza Construction Joint Venture – Northern portion and [[7 World Trade Center (1987–2001)|7 World Trade Center]]

The [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]] (FEMA), the [[United States Army Corps of Engineers]], the [[Occupational Safety and Health Administration]] (OSHA), and the New York City Office of Emergency Management (OEM) provided support.<ref name="domel"/> Forestry [[incident management team]]s (IMTs) also provided support beginning in the days after the attacks to help manage operations.<ref name="mckinsey-fireops">{{cite web |url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/fire_operations_response.pdf |title=FDNY McKinsey Report – Fire Operations Response |date=August 2002 |publisher=FDNY / McKinsey & Company |access-date=July 10, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110524092417/http://www.nyc.gov/html/fdny/pdf/mck_report/fire_operations_response.pdf |archive-date=May 24, 2011 |url-status=live }}</ref>

A nearby [[Burger King]] restaurant was used as a center for police operations.<ref name="hamill">{{cite news |author=Hamill, Denis |url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2001/09/16/rescue-workers-keep-up-search-for-signs-of-life-but-instead-of-survivors-more-bodies-are-located/|title=Rescue Workers Keep Up Quest for Signs of Life Ruin All Over, But Not One Unkind Word |newspaper=Daily News (New York) |date=September 16, 2001}}</ref> Given that workers worked at the site, or ''[[World Trade Center site|The Pile]]'', for shifts as long as twelve hours, a specific culture developed at the site, leading to workers developing their own [[jargon]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.groundzeromuseumworkshop.com/lingo.htm|title=Ground Zero Museum Workshop : Gary Marlon Suson|date=January 12, 2007 |access-date=April 8, 2018|url-status=usurped|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070112130645/http://www.groundzeromuseumworkshop.com/lingo.htm|archive-date=January 12, 2007}}</ref>

===Debris removal===
"The Pile" was the term coined by the rescue workers to describe the 1.8 million tons of wreckage left from the collapse of the World Trade Center.<ref name="hamill"/> They avoided the use of "[[ground zero]]", which describes the epicenter of a [[bomb]] explosion.

Numerous volunteers organized to form "bucket brigades", which passed 5-gallon buckets full of debris down a line to investigators, who sifted through the debris in search of evidence and human remains.  Ironworkers helped cut up steel beams into more manageable sizes for removal.  Much of the debris was hauled off to the [[Fresh Kills Landfill]] on [[Staten Island]] where it was further searched and sorted.<ref name="hamill"/>

According to ''The New York Times'', by September 24, 2001, more than 100,000 tons of debris had been removed from the site.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/24/nyregion/nation-challenged-site-scene-random-devastation-most-orderly-mission.html| author=Dan Barry| title=A Nation Challenged: The Site; At the Scene of Random Devastation, a Most Orderly Mission| work=New York Times| date=September 24, 2001| access-date=January 11, 2020| archive-date=September 15, 2019| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190915182022/https://www.nytimes.com/2001/09/24/nyregion/nation-challenged-site-scene-random-devastation-most-orderly-mission.html| url-status=live}}</ref> Some structural engineers have criticized the decision to recycle the steel from the buildings before it could be analyzed as part of the post-collapse investigation.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/12/25/nyregion/a-nation-challenged-the-towers-experts-urging-broader-inquiry-in-towers-fall.html| author=James Glanz and Eric Lipton| title=A Nation Challenged: The Towers; Experts Urging Broader Inquiry In Towers' Fall| work=New York Times| date=December 25, 2001| access-date=January 11, 2020| archive-date=March 19, 2020| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200319201051/https://www.nytimes.com/2001/12/25/nyregion/a-nation-challenged-the-towers-experts-urging-broader-inquiry-in-towers-fall.html| url-status=live}}</ref>

====Vaulted bullion====
In the days following the destruction of the towers, rescuers found scorch marks, likely made by a [[cutting torch]] on a basement doorway underneath 4 WTC; this was thought to be the result of looters. Further exploration of the building's basement revealed that the vault contained large amounts of gold and silver in the form of coins, as well as gold and silver bars.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Dwyer |first1=Jim |title=A Nation Challenged: The Vault; Below Ground Zero, Silver and Gold |website=[[New York Times]] |date=November 1, 2001 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2001/11/01/nyregion/a-nation-challenged-the-vault-below-ground-zero-silver-and-gold.html |access-date=July 21, 2022 |archive-date=July 22, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220722020257/https://www.nytimes.com/2001/11/01/nyregion/a-nation-challenged-the-vault-below-ground-zero-silver-and-gold.html |url-status=live }}</ref> An [[armored truck]] operated by [[New York Mercantile Exchange|COMEX]] was also located below the World Trade Center among the other vehicles, which was fully loaded with gold and silver bars. In order to retrieve the bullion from the vault, electricity had to be supplied to its doors, which had withstood the force of the destruction above them.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gray |first=Geoffrey |date=August 26, 2011 |title=Gold, Recovery of |url=https://nymag.com/news/9-11/10th-anniversary/gold/ |access-date=October 4, 2023 |website=New York Magazine |language=en}}</ref>

Over the subsequent months, much of the bullion was recovered. Approximately 560,000 dollars' worth of coins and bars were stored in the vault by the [[Bank of Nova Scotia]] prior to September 11, 2001, with the bank having stored a total of 379,036 [[Troy weight|troy ounces]] of gold and 29,942,619 troy ounces of silver inside the vault.<ref>{{Cite web |title=CNN.com - Precious metals buried under debris - September 22, 2001 |url=https://edition.cnn.com/2001/US/09/22/rec.buried.treasure/index.html |access-date=March 18, 2024 |website=CNN |archive-date=March 18, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240318214616/https://edition.cnn.com/2001/US/09/22/rec.buried.treasure/index.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> Many of these coins were purchased by Lee S. Minshull of [[Palos Verdes]], California, who then sent them to [[Professional Coin Grading Service|PCGS]] for grading in 2002.<ref>{{cite news |title='Sept. 11' Coins Carry Hefty Markups, Baggage |url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB106012198859689500 |newspaper=[[The Wall Street Journal]] |access-date=October 8, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191008225628/https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB106012198859689500 |archive-date=October 8, 2019 |url-status=live |date=August 6, 2003 }}</ref> These coins were then sold to collectors. Coins salvaged from 4 WTC's vault included [[American Silver Eagle]]s,<ref>{{cite web |title=2001 $1 Silver Eagle 9-11-01 WTC Ground Zero Recovery PCGS MS69 |url=https://www.pcgs.com/auctionprices/item/2001-1-silver-eagle-wtc/9954/-2482785246210447058 |website=[[Professional Coin Grading Service|PCGS]] |access-date=October 8, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191008225632/https://www.pcgs.com/auctionprices/item/2001-1-silver-eagle-wtc/9954/-2482785246210447058 |archive-date=October 8, 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Canadian Gold Maple Leaf]]s, South African [[Krugerrand]]s and British [[Gold Britannia]]s.

====Hazards====
Hazards at the World Trade Center site included a [[diesel fuel]] tank buried seven stories below.<ref name="osha"/>  Approximately 2,000 [[automobile]]s that had been in the [[parking garage]] also presented a risk, with each containing, on average, at least {{convert|5|usgal|L}} of gasoline.  Once recovery workers reached down to the parking garage level, they found some cars that had exploded and burned.<ref name="osha"/>  The [[United States Customs Service]], which was housed in [[6 World Trade Center (1974–2001)|6 World Trade Center]], had 1.2 million rounds of ammunition and weapons in storage in a third-floor vault, to support their [[firing range]].<ref name="osha"/>

===Morale===
[[File:Remarks from Ground Zero September 14, 2001.webm|thumb|left|President [[George W. Bush]] speaking at Ground Zero with [[Bob Beckwith]] beside him]]
In the hours immediately after the attacks on the World Trade Center, [[Raising the Flag at Ground Zero|three firefighters raised an American flag over the rubble]]. The flag was taken from a yacht, and the moment, which was captured on a well-known photograph, evoked comparisons to the iconic [[Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima|Iwo Jima photograph from 1945]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Mcphee|first=Michele| url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2004/03/06/sue-city-in-9-11-flag-flap/ | location=New York | work=Daily News | title=Sue City In 9-11 Flag Flap | date=March 6, 2004 }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Chen|first=David W.|journal=New York Times|title=With 9/11 Flag, a Mystery Unfurls|date=September 4, 2002|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2002/09/04/nyregion/with-9-11-flag-a-mystery-unfurls.html|access-date=August 4, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140106090451/http://www.nytimes.com/2002/09/04/nyregion/with-9-11-flag-a-mystery-unfurls.html|archive-date=January 6, 2014|url-status=live}}</ref> Morale of rescue workers was boosted on September 14, 2001, when President [[George W. Bush]] paid a visit to Ground Zero. Standing with retired firefighter [[Bob Beckwith]], Bush [[Bullhorn Address|addressed]] the firefighters and rescue workers with a bullhorn and thanked them.<ref name = "NYDN1">{{cite news|url=http://articles.nydailynews.com/2002-02-26/news/18186914_1_president-bush-fire-truck-bullhorn|title=Bush is given WTC megaphone|first1=Kenneth|last1=Bazinet|date=February 26, 2002|access-date=September 5, 2011|newspaper=[[New York Daily News]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120614052821/http://articles.nydailynews.com/2002-02-26/news/18186914_1_president-bush-fire-truck-bullhorn|archive-date=June 14, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=President tours disaster scene; Bush calls New York rescue workers; heroes in the eyes of American public |newspaper=The Globe and Mail |date=September 15, 2001}}</ref>  Bush later remarked, "I'm shocked at the size of the devastation, It's hard to describe what it's like to see the gnarled steel and broken glass and twisted buildings silhouetted against the smoke. The rest of the world hears you. And the people who knocked these buildings down will hear all of us soon!"<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/bush-visits-ground-zero-in-new-york-1.263318 | work=CBC News | title=Bush visits 'ground zero' in New York | date=September 15, 2001 | access-date=March 25, 2009 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090505154020/https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/bush-visits-ground-zero-in-new-york-1.263318 | archive-date=May 5, 2009 | url-status=live }}</ref>

At some point, rescue workers realized that they were not going to find any more survivors.  After a couple of weeks, the conditions at Ground Zero remained harsh, with lingering [[odor]]s of decaying human remains and smoke.  Morale among workers was boosted by letters they received from children around the United States and the world, as well as support from thousands of neighbors in [[TriBeCa]] and other Lower Manhattan neighborhoods.<ref>{{cite video |title=Up From Zero |date=2003 |publisher=Production Link LLC |people=Sutherland, Ann DeStefano and Tim Baney}}</ref>

This support continued to spread and eventually led to the founding of over 250 non-profit organizations of which raised almost $700 million within their first two years of operation.<ref name="morale">{{cite news|author=Inside Staff|title=Public Administration Experts Track 9-11 Nonprofits|url=http://www.binghamton.edu/inside/index.php/inside/story/public-administration-expert-tracks-9-11-nonprofits|access-date=November 27, 2012|newspaper=Inside Bingham University|date=August 26, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120324213239/http://www.binghamton.edu/inside/index.php/inside/story/public-administration-expert-tracks-9-11-nonprofits|archive-date=March 24, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>  One of the nonprofits included One Day's Pay, later changed to MyGoodDeed, which championed the effort to designate [[September 11 National Day of Service|September 11 as an official National Day of Service]] (9/11 Day).

By 2012, many of the more than 250 organizations had disbanded for lack of funding. Of the ones that remain, a handful remained functioning for those who remain in need. One of these organizations, Tuesday's Children, was founded the day after September 11 in hopes of supporting the children immediately affected by the attacks. The founder of this non-profit, [[David Weild|David Weild IV]],  now calls them one of the "last men standing" in that they are now one of the few remaining organizations who "provide direct services for what social-service groups and survivors of the attacks call the '9-11 Community.'"<ref>{{cite journal|last=Berkman|first=Jacob|title=As Anniversary Nears, 9/11 Charities Struggle to Raise Money|journal=Chronicle of Philanthropy|date=August 25, 2011|volume=23|issue=17|pages=25|url=http://philanthropy.com/article/As-Anniversary-Nears-9-11/128761/|access-date=November 27, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110918114335/http://philanthropy.com/article/As-Anniversary-Nears-9-11/128761/|archive-date=September 18, 2011|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Military support==

=== Civil Air Patrol ===

Immediately following the attacks, members of the [[Civil Air Patrol]] (CAP) were called up to help respond.  Northeast Region placed its personnel and assets on alert moments after learning of the attack.  With the exception of CAP, civilian flights were grounded by the [[Federal Aviation Administration]].  CAP flew [[Reconnaissance#Reconnaissance from air and space vehicles|aerial reconnaissance]] missions over Ground Zero in order to provide detailed analysis of the wreckage and aid in recovery efforts, including transportation of blood donations.

=== National Guard ===
[[Image:National Guard at WTC.jpg|thumb|left|upright|Two members of the [[New York Army National Guard]] standing at the World Trade Center site]]

[[File:442nd Military Police Company - WTC Ground Zero.jpg|thumb|Military Police officers at Ground Zero]]

Elements of the New York Army National Guard's 1-101st Cavalry (Staten Island), 258th Field Artillery, 442nd Military Police Company, and [[69th Infantry Regiment (New York)|69th Infantry Regiment]] based in Manhattan were the first military force to secure Ground Zero on September 11. The 69th Infantry's armory on Lexington Avenue became the Family Information Center to assist persons in locating missing family members.<ref>Flynn, Sean. ''The Fighting 69th: One Remarkable National Guard Unit's Journey from Ground Zero to Baghdad'', Penguin Books, 2007</ref>

The National Guard supplemented the NYPD and FDNY, with 2,250 guard members on the scene by the next morning.<ref name="cohen"/> Eventually thousands of New York Army and [[Air National Guardsmen]] participated in the rescue/recovery efforts. They conducted site security at the WTC, and at other locations. They provided the NYPD with support for traffic control, and they participated directly in recovery operations providing manpower in the form of "bucket brigades" sorting through the debris by hand.

Additionally service members provided security at a variety of location throughout the city and New York State to deter further attacks and reassure the public.

Members of the Air National Guard's 109th Airlift Wing out of Scotia, and Syracuse's 174th Fighter Wing immediately responded to New York City, setting up camp at places such as Fort Hamilton.  Mostly civil engineers, firefighters and military police, they greatly aided in the clean-up effort.  F-16s from the 174th Fighter Wing also ramped up their flying sorties and patrolled the skies.

The [[New Jersey National Guard]] assisted the New York National Guard's efforts following the attacks.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nj.gov/military/publications/guardlife/volume34no1/6.html|title=Guardlife – The Magazine of the New Jersey National Guard|website=www.nj.gov|access-date=April 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160708182739/http://www.nj.gov/military/publications/guardlife/volume34no1/6.html|archive-date=July 8, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== U.S. Marine Corps ===
[[File:USMC Jason Thomas, September 11, 2001.JPG|thumb|upright|Marine [[Jason Thomas (Marine)|Sgt. Jason Thomas]] standing at the site of the ruined World Trade Center, several days after the attacks]]

[[United States Marine Corps|U.S. Marines]] were also present to assist in the rescue efforts. Films such as 2006 docudrama ''[[World Trade Center (film)|World Trade Center]]'' and the 2021 documentary ''[[9/11: One Day in America]]'' talked of two Marines who rescued two trapped police officers in the rubble. U.S. Marines were headquartered at the 340 Westside Hwy Bloomberg News Building. The commanding officer was Navy Commander Hardy, and executive officer was Maj. Priester. These two oversaw 110 military personnel of various branches, various police departments and EMTs.{{citation needed|date=August 2013}}

Comfort's 24-hour galley also provided 30,000 meals. Its medical resources were also used to provide first-aid and sick call services to nearly 600 people. ==Handling of cleanup procedure==
{{See also|United States Environmental Protection Agency September 11 attacks pollution controversy}}
A May 14, 2007, ''New York Times'' article, "Ground Zero Illness Clouding Giuliani's Legacy", gave the interpretation that thousands of workers at Ground Zero have become sick and that "regard Mr. Giuliani's triumph of leadership as having come with a human cost". The article reported that the mayor seized control of the cleanup of Ground Zero, taking control away from established federal agencies, such as the [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]], the [[United States Army Corps of Engineers|U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] and the [[Occupational Safety and Health Administration]].  He instead handed over responsibility to the "largely unknown" city Department of Design and Construction.  Documents indicate that the Giuliani administration never enforced federal requirements requiring the wearing of [[respirators]].  Concurrently, the administration threatened companies with dismissal if cleanup work slowed.<ref>{{cite news |author=Anthony DePalma |title=Ground Zero Illness Clouding Giuliani's Legacy |work=New York Times |date=May 14, 2007 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/05/14/nyregion/14giuliani.html?hp |access-date=April 21, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180922015117/https://www.nytimes.com/2007/05/14/nyregion/14giuliani.html?hp |archive-date=September 22, 2018 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Workers at the Ground Zero pit worked without proper respirators.  They wore painters' masks or no facial covering.  Specialists claim that the only effective protection against toxins, such as airborne asbestos, is a special respirator.  New York Committee for Occupational Safety and Health industrial hygienist David Newman said, "I was down there watching people working without respirators." He continued, "Others took off their respirators to eat.  It was a surreal, ridiculous, unacceptable situation."<ref name="autogenerated1">{{Cite magazine|last=Mason|first=Michael|url=http://discovermagazine.com/2007/oct/the-9-11-cover-up|title=The 9/11 Cover-Up|magazine=[[Discover (magazine)|Discover]]|date=October 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070925065531/http://discovermagazine.com/2007/oct/the-9-11-cover-up |archive-date=September 25, 2007 }}</ref>

The local EPA office sidelined the regional EPA office.  Dr. [[Cate Jenkins]], a whistle-blower EPA scientist, said that on September 12, 2001, a regional EPA office offered to dispatch 30 to 40 electron microscopes to the WTC pit to test bulk dust samples for the presence of [[asbestos]] fibers.  Instead, the local office chose the less effective polarized light microscopy testing method. Dr. Jenkins alleged that the local office refused, and said, "We don't want you fucking cowboys here. The best thing they could do is reassign you to [[Alaska]]."<ref name="autogenerated1" />

===Health effects===
{{Main|Health effects arising from the September 11 attacks}}
[[File:EPA WTC 2001-10-10.jpg|thumb|right|upright|An [[United States Environmental Protection Agency|EPA]] employee checks one of the many air sampling locations set up around the World Trade Center site.]]

There were many health problems caused by the toxins. 99% of exposed firefighters reported at least one new respiratory problem while working at the World Trade Center site that they had not experienced before.<ref>{{cite web |title=9/11 Health|url=http://www.nyc.gov/html/doh/wtc/html/rescue/know.shtml|website=nyc.gov|access-date=December 3, 2012 |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130128042040/http://www.nyc.gov/html/doh/wtc/html/rescue/know.shtml|archive-date=January 28, 2013}}</ref> Chronic airway disease is the main lung injury among firefighters who were exposed to toxins during 9/11.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Weiden MD|author2=Ferrier N.|author3=Nolan A|journal=Chest|year=2010|volume=137|issue=3|pages=566–574|url=http://journal.publications.chestnet.org/article.aspx?articleid=1086333&issueno=3|access-date=December 3, 2012|doi=10.1378/chest.09-1580|title=Obstructive Airways Disease With Air Trapping Among Firefighters Exposed to World Trade Center Dust|pmc=2832867|pmid=19820077|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121108142817/http://journal.publications.chestnet.org/article.aspx?articleid=1086333&issueno=3|archive-date=November 8, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref> Six years after the attacks, among those who never smoked, approximately 13% of firefighters and 22% of EMS had lungs that did not function as well as others around the same age. Steep declines in pulmonary lung function has been a problem since first detected among firefighters and EMS within a year of 9/11 have persisted.<ref>{{cite journal|last=Aldrich|first=TK|author2=Gustave, Hall |journal= New England Journal of Medicine|year=2010|volume=362|issue=14|pages=1263–1272|pmid=20375403|doi=10.1056/NEJMoa0910087|title=Lung function in rescue workers at the World Trade Center after 7 years.|pmc=4940972}}</ref>
Increasing numbers of Ground Zero workers are getting [[Health effects arising from the September 11 attacks|illness]]es, such as cancer. Between September 11, 2001, through 2008, there were 263 new cases of cancer found in 8,927 male firefighters who responded to 9/11 attacks. This number is 25 times more than what is expected from men from a similar age group and race. There is a 19% increase in cancer overall, between firefighters who responded to the attacks and those who were not exposed to toxins from responding to the attacks on September 11.<ref>{{cite web|title=Rescue and recovery workers}}</ref>

On January 30, 2007, Ground Zero workers and groups such as [[Sierra Club]] and Unsung Heroes Helping Heroes met at the Ground Zero site and urged President [[George W. Bush|George Bush]] to spend more money on aid for sick Ground Zero workers.  They said that the $25 million that Bush promised for the ill workers was inadequate.  A Long Island iron-worker, John Sferazo, at the protest rally said, "Why has it taken you five and a half years to meet with us, Mr. President?"<ref>Emi Endo, "Sick 9/11 workers protest at Ground Zero" ''Newsday'', January 31, 2007, [http://www.newsday.com/news/nationworld/nation/am-groundzero0201,0,1528416.story?coll=ny-main-bigpix]{{dead link|date=May 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>

Firefighters, police and their unions, have criticized Mayor [[Rudy Giuliani]] over the issue of protective equipment and illnesses after the attacks.<ref name="smith">{{Cite news|last=Smith|first=Ben|url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2006/09/18/rudys-black-cloud-wtc-health-risks-may-hurt-prez-bid/|title=Rudy's Black Cloud|publisher=New York Daily News|date=September 18, 2006}}</ref> A study by the National Institute of Environmental Safety and Health said that cleanup workers lacked adequate protective gear.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.democracynow.org/article.pl?sid=06/09/08/1349248|title= New Yorkers Tell Federal Officials To Stop Ignoring 9/11's Health Effects|website=democracynow.org|date=September 8, 2006|access-date=April 8, 2018|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071115002115/http://www.democracynow.org/article.pl?sid=06%2F09%2F08%2F1349248|archive-date=November 15, 2007}}</ref> The Executive Director of the National Fraternal Order of Police reportedly said of Giuliani: "Everybody likes a Churchillian kind of leader who jumps up when the ashes are still falling and takes over. But two or three good days don't expunge an eight-year record."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://deseretnews.com/dn/view/0,1249,660203115,00.html|title=Giuliani will miss firefighters event|date=March 14, 2007|website=[[Deseret News]]|access-date=April 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071011224205/http://deseretnews.com/dn/view/0,1249,660203115,00.html|archive-date=October 11, 2007|url-status=dead}}</ref>  [[Sally Regenhard]], said, "There's a large and growing number of both FDNY families, FDNY members, former and current, and civilian families who want to expose the true failures of the Giuliani administration when it comes to 9/11."  She told the ''New York Daily News'' that she intends to "[[Swiftboating|Swift Boat]]" Giuliani.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Smith|first=Ben|url=https://www.nydailynews.com/2006/09/18/rudys-black-cloud-wtc-health-risks-may-hurt-prez-bid/|title=Rudy's Black Cloud: WTC Health Risks may hurt Prez Bid|publisher=New York Daily News|date=September 18, 2006}}</ref>

Various health programs arose after the attacks to provide treatment for 9/11-related illnesses among responders, recovery workers, and other survivors. When the [[James Zadroga 9/11 Health and Compensation Act]] became federal law in January 2011, these programs were replaced by the [[World Trade Center Health Program]].<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.cnn.com/2010/POLITICS/12/22/911.bill/index.html | work=CNN | title=Bloomberg urges passage of 9/11 health bill | date=December 20, 2010 | url-status=live | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120327152416/http://articles.cnn.com/2010-12-22/politics/911.bill_1_first-responders-funding-votes-house-members?_s=PM%3APOLITICS | archive-date=March 27, 2012 }}</ref><ref name="WTCHP faq">{{cite web|title=World Trade Center Health Program FAQ|url=https://www.cdc.gov/wtc/faq.html|publisher=Centers for Disease Control and Prevention|access-date=July 2, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120619013029/http://www.cdc.gov/wtc/faq.html|archive-date=June 19, 2012|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Investigations ==
Soon after the attacks, [[Government of New York City|New York City]] commissioned [[McKinsey & Company]] to investigate the response of both the [[New York City Fire Department]] and [[New York City Police Department]] and make recommendations on how to respond more effectively to such large-scale emergencies in the future.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://home2.nyc.gov/html/om/html/2002b/pr223-02.html |title=Mayor Michael R. Bloomberg, Police Commissioner Raymond W. Kelly, and Fire Commissioner Nicholas Scoppetta Release McKinsey & Company Studies of NYPD's and FDNY's Responses to World Trade Center Attack |date=August 19, 2002 |publisher=The City of New York |access-date=July 10, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071014003450/http://home2.nyc.gov/html/om/html/2002b/pr223-02.html |archive-date=October 14, 2007 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Officials with the [[International Association of Fire Fighters]] have also criticized [[Rudy Giuliani]] for failing to support modernized radios that might have spared the lives of more firefighters. Some firefighters never heard the evacuation orders and died in the collapse of the towers.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2007-apr-08-na-giuliani8-story.html | work=Los Angeles Times | title=Giuliani foes plan to use 9/11 against him | first=Peter | last=Wallsten | date=April 8, 2007 | access-date=September 28, 2010 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121105212402/http://www.latimes.com/news/politics/la-na-firefighters8apr08,0,4483360,full.story | archive-date=November 5, 2012 | url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reconstruction==
{{Main|World Trade Center (2001–present)}}
Plans for the World Trade Center rebuilding started in July 2002, which was headed by the Lower Manhattan Development Corporation. There were rounds of proposals on how to rebuild the World Trade Center; however, many of the early schemes were criticized for lacking creativity.  There was division among members of the public, architects, and political leadership as to what an appropriate new World Trade Center would look like.  Several architects were chosen and replaced throughout the planning and design process; there were issues with implementing the early designs. By 2006 all architects for the site had been chosen and designs were largely finalized in 2007.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2006/05/03/arts/design/03towe.html |title=Richard Rogers to Design Tower at Ground Zero |author=Pogrebin, Robin |date=May 3, 2006 |work=The New York Times |access-date=November 19, 2008 |archive-date=August 30, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210830224005/https://www.nytimes.com/2006/05/03/arts/design/03towe.html |url-status=live }}</ref>  The date of completion for the World Trade Center was scheduled for 2016.<ref>{{cite news|last=Suddath|first=Claire|title=What Took So Long? Why The World Trade Center Is Still Not Finished.|url=http://www.time.com/time/nation/article/0,8599,2092503,00.html|access-date=November 28, 2012|newspaper=Time|date=September 10, 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121129134500/http://www.time.com/time/nation/article/0,8599,2092503,00.html|archive-date=November 29, 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Feiden|first=Douglas|title=Why is Ground Zero rebuilding taking so long? Mayor Bloomberg pins blame on Port Authority.|url=http://www.nydailynews.com/new-york/ground-zero-rebuilding-long-mayor-bloomberg-pins-blame-port-authority-article-1.394170|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150510015005/http://www.nydailynews.com/new-york/ground-zero-rebuilding-long-mayor-bloomberg-pins-blame-port-authority-article-1.394170|url-status=dead|date=August 4, 2009|archive-date=May 10, 2015|access-date=November 28, 2012|newspaper=New York Daily News}}</ref> {{As of|2018|6|11|df=us}}, four of seven planned buildings were completed,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2018/06/10/3-world-trade-center-open-after-years-delays-twin-towers-site/689035002/|title=New World Trade Center to open after years of delays|last=Matthews|first=Karen|date=June 10, 2018|website=USA Today|agency=Associated Press|access-date=June 11, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180612214637/https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2018/06/10/3-world-trade-center-open-after-years-delays-twin-towers-site/689035002/|archive-date=June 12, 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref> as were the [[World Trade Center Transportation Hub|transportation hub]], [[National September 11 Memorial & Museum|9/11 Memorial]], and [[Liberty Park]].<ref>{{cite web | last=Bindelglass | first=Evan | title=The status of the World Trade Center complex, 16 years later | website=Curbed NY | date=September 11, 2017 | url=https://ny.curbed.com/2017/9/11/16284798/world-trade-center-nyc-construction-update | access-date=February 25, 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180210010950/https://ny.curbed.com/2017/9/11/16284798/world-trade-center-nyc-construction-update | archive-date=February 10, 2018 | url-status=dead }}</ref>
