'''''9{{Nbsp}}to{{Nbsp}}5''''' (also titled '''''Nine to Five''''') is a 1980 American [[comedy film]] directed by [[Colin Higgins]] from a screenplay by Higgins and [[Patricia Resnick]]. It stars [[Jane Fonda]], [[Lily Tomlin]], [[Dolly Parton]] (in her film debut), [[Dabney Coleman]], [[Elizabeth Wilson]], and [[Sterling Hayden]]. In the film, three female office employees band together to seek revenge against their sexist and autocratic male boss.

Development on ''9 to 5'' began after Fonda formed the production company IPC Films, and was inspired by 1940s-era films which featured multiple female leads. The initial screenplay was written by Resnick as a drama, but after discussions between Tomlin and Fonda, Higgins was hired and adapted ''9 to 5'' from a drama to a comedy. Production was paused during rewrites, during which, the rest of the main cast was rounded out: ''9 to 5'' was a [[star vehicle]] for Parton, who was a successful musician prior to acting in the film. [[Principal photography]] took place primarily in [[Los Angeles]]. The film's [[9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)|theme song]], performed by Parton, was released in November 1980.

''9 to 5'' was first theatrically released in the United States on December 19, 1980, by [[20th Century Fox]]. Upon release, the film received mixed reviews from critics, with praise for Parton's performance but criticism for its plot. Its theme song was nominated for an [[Academy Award for Best Original Song|Academy Award]] and a [[Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song|Golden Globe]], while Parton received two Golden Globe nominations, including for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy|Best Actress]]. ''9 to 5'' has since received retrospective praise for its comedy and commentary on workplace sexism and women's labor.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=AFI's 100 YEARS...100 LAUGHS The 100 Funniest American Movies Of All Time |url=https://www.afi.com/afis-100-years-100-laughs/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210301072345/https://www.afi.com/afis-100-years-100-laughs/ |archive-date=March 1, 2021 |access-date=August 15, 2022 |website=[[American Film Institute|AFI]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Murtha |first=Tara |date=December 18, 2015 |title='9 to 5' Turns 35, and It's Still Radical Today |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/feature/9-to-5-turns-35-and-its-still-radical-today-50499/ |access-date=April 18, 2023 |magazine=Rolling Stone |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Kale |first=Neha |date=February 10, 2022 |title=Dolly Parton's 9 to 5: the office comedy with serious undertones |url=https://www.smh.com.au/culture/musicals/dolly-parton-s-9-to-5-the-office-comedy-with-serious-undertones-20220204-p59tt7.html |access-date=April 18, 2023 |website=The Sydney Morning Herald |language=en}}</ref> It grossed $103.3 million worldwide,<ref name="Mojo"/> and has also been adapted into both a [[9 to 5 (TV series)|television series]] and a [[9 to 5 (musical)|musical]]. In April 2024, it was reported that a remake, produced by [[Jennifer Aniston]], is in development.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Housewife Judy Bernly starts work as a secretary at Consolidated Companies after her divorce and is placed under the supervision of sharp-tongued widow Violet Newstead. Both work under president Mr. Hinkle and egotistical, sexist vice president Franklin Hart.

When Hart turns down Violet for a promotion in favor of the less-qualified Bob Enright, Violet confronts him in his office when his secretary, Doralee Rhodes, follows Violet and subsequently learns about the rumor that she's having an affair with Hart. Discovering the rumor is a lie, both women hit a bar to drink away their frustration. Meanwhile, Hart fires Maria Delgado after learning from his loyal assistant, Roz, that Maria discussed her salary with a co-worker. Disgusted by Hart's cruelty, Judy joins Violet and Doralee at the bar.

They spend the evening [[Cannabis smoking|smoking marijuana]] at Doralee's house and fantasizing about how they would get revenge on Hart: Judy would hunt him with a gun and mount his head on a wall, as he does to animals on his hunting trips; Doralee would turn the tables on sexual harassment, [[hogtie]] him rodeo style, then roast him over a fire; and Violet, in a fairytale-like sequence, would poison his coffee and throw him out the window. The next day, a distracted Violet accidentally puts rat poison in Hart's coffee, mistaking the box for the nearly identical box of sweetener. Before he can drink it, his desk chair malfunctions and he blacks out after hitting his head on a [[credenza]].

Violet realizes her mistake and thinks the poisoned coffee caused Hart to black out. She and Judy meet Doralee at the hospital just in time to overhear a doctor pronounce a man dead from poisoning. Thinking the dead man is Hart, Violet steals the body to prevent an autopsy, but while arguing with Judy and Doralee, she crashes her car, damaging a fender. When Doralee retrieves a tire iron from the trunk to fix the fender, she discovers the body is not Hart so they return it to the hospital.

The next morning, Hart shocks the women when he arrives for work as usual. In the ladies’ room, Doralee explains that Hart hit his head but did not drink the coffee. Relieved that nothing will come out of the night's events, the ladies agree to meet for happy hour at the end of the day. However, Roz overhears their conversation and reports back to Hart.

Hart summons Doralee to his office and offers her a choice: if she spends the night with him, he will not report her, Judy, and Violet for attempted murder. Doralee refuses, and when Hart will not hear her out, she hogties him and stuffs a scarf in his mouth to keep him quiet. He tricks Judy into loosening the binds, which leads her to shoot at him with Doralee's handgun, nearly committing murder.

The women discover that Hart has been selling Consolidated inventory and pocketing the proceeds, so they decide to blackmail him into keeping quiet. When they are told that invoices Violet ordered to prove the crimes will not arrive for 4 to 6 weeks, they kidnap Hart and hold him prisoner in his bedroom.

While Hart is out of the office, Doralee forges his signature and in his name, they implement several programs that are popular with the workers, including an in-office daycare center, equal pay for everyone, flexible hours, and a job-sharing program allowing employees to work part-time.

Days before the invoices arrive, Hart's wife Missy returns from a cruise and frees him, giving him the time to buy back the inventory he sold. Before Hart can report Judy, Doralee, and Violet to the police, the chairman of the board Russell Tinsworthy arrives and congratulates Hart on his improvements to the office, which have resulted in a 20 percent increase in productivity. Tinsworthy invites Hart to join him on a multiyear project in Brazil, which he reluctantly accepts. 

A postscript states that Violet is promoted to vice president. Judy marries the Xerox representative. Doralee leaves Consolidated to become a [[Country music|country & western]] singer. Hart is abducted by a tribe of [[Amazons]] in the Brazilian jungle and is "never heard from again".

==Production==
The film was based on an idea by Jane Fonda, who had recently formed her own production company IPC Films:

<blockquote>My ideas for films always come from things that I hear and perceive in my daily life ... [[Karen Nussbaum|A very old friend of mine]] had started an organization in Boston called "[[9to5|Nine to Five]]", which was an association of women office workers. I heard them talking about their work and they had some great stories. And I've always been attracted to those 1940s films with three female stars.<ref name="fonda"/></blockquote>

Fonda said the film was at first going to be a drama, but "any way we did it, it seemed too preachy, too much of a feminist line. I'd wanted to work with Lily [Tomlin] for some time, and it suddenly occurred to [her producing partner] Bruce and me that we should make it a comedy."<ref name="fonda">{{cite news |url=http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article125651063 |title=LIFE STYLE. |newspaper=[[The Canberra Times]] |date=February 6, 1981 |access-date=February 28, 2015 |page=17 |via=National Library of Australia |archive-date=April 30, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210430230929/https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/125651063 |url-status=live}}</ref> Patricia Resnick wrote the first draft drama, and Fonda cast herself, Lily Tomlin, and Dolly Parton in the leads, Parton in her first film role.<ref name="aww">{{cite news |url=http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article56325277 |title=Dolly Parton: first footlights, now films. |newspaper=[[The Australian Women's Weekly]] |date=May 28, 1980 |access-date=February 28, 2015 |page=145 Supplement: FREE Your TV Magazine |via=National Library of Australia |archive-date=April 30, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210430230845/https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/56325277 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Colin Higgins then came on board to direct and rewrite the script; part of his job was to make room for all three in the script. Higgins said Fonda was a very encouraging producer, who allowed him to push back production while the script was being rewritten.<ref>{{cite news |last=Goldstein |first=Patrick |title=Higgins: Writer-director on hot streak |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=January 24, 1981 |page=b15}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title='9 to 5' a real grind for writer|author=Beck, Marilyn|work=Chicago Tribune|date=October 23, 1979|page=a12}}</ref> "He's a very nice, quiet, low-key guy", said Parton of Higgins. "I don't know what I would have done if I'd had one of those mean directors on my first film."<ref name="aww"/> Higgins admitted that he "expected some tension" from working with three stars, "but they were totally professional, great fun, and a joy to work with. I just wish everything would be as easy."<ref>{{cite news |url=http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article51771755 |title=John Michael Howson's Hollywood |newspaper=[[The Australian Women's Weekly]] |date=March 25, 1981 |access-date=February 28, 2015 |page=47 Supplement: TV WORLD |via=National Library of Australia |archive-date=April 30, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210430230912/https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/51771755 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Fonda stated that the resulting film "remains a 'labour film{{'"}}, but that she hoped it to be "of a new kind, different from the ''[[The Grapes of Wrath (film)|Grapes of Wrath]]'' or ''[[Salt of the Earth (1954 film)|Salt of the Earth]]''{{-"}}. "We took out a lot of stuff that was filmed, even stuff the director, Colin Higgins, thought worked but which I asked to have taken out. I'm just super-sensitive to anything that smacks of the soapbox or lecturing the audience".<ref name="fonda"/>

Fonda said she did a lot of research, focusing on women who had begun work late in life due to divorce or being widowed.

<blockquote>What I found was that secretaries know the work they do is important, is skilled, but they also know they're not treated with respect. They call themselves "office wives". They have to put gas in the boss's car, get his coffee, buy the presents for his wife and mistress. So when we came to do the film, we said to Colin [Higgins], OK, what you have to do is write a screenplay which shows you can run an office without a boss, but you can't run an office without the secretaries!<ref name="fonda"/></blockquote>

===Filming locations===
The home of Franklin Hart is located at 10431 Bellagio Road in [[Bel Air, Los Angeles]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Nine To Five |url=https://www.itsfilmedthere.com/2013/03/nine-to-five.html |access-date=July 30, 2022 |website=itsfilmedthere.com |language=en}}</ref> According to commentary included in the DVD release of the film, the home was, at the time, owned by the Chandler family, publishers of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]''.<ref>20th Century Studios DVD release, ''9 to 5 (Sexist, Egotistical, Lying Hypocritical Bigot Edition - Widescreen'') ([[Audio commentary]]). April 4, 2006.</ref> The Consolidated offices were presumably in the Pacific Financial Center located at 800 W 6th Street, at South Flower Street in Los Angeles. Although the story appears to be set in Los Angeles, the opening credit montage, set to the title song, is mostly composed of shots from downtown [[San Francisco]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=February 19, 2017 |title=Nine To Five (1980) |url=https://www.set-jetter.com/ontheset/ninetofive |access-date=July 30, 2022 |website=Set-Jetter |language=en-US}}</ref> These shots include an electric [[San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency|Muni]] bus fitted with a KFOG 104.5 FM advertisement, the Ferry Building clock, and a brief glimpse of the San Francisco twins, [[Marian and Vivian Brown]].

===Theme song===
{{main|9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)}}
The film's theme song, "[[9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)|9 to 5]]", written and recorded by Parton, became one of her biggest hits of the decade. While filming the ''9 to 5'' movie, Parton found she could use her long acrylic fingernails to simulate the sound of a typewriter. She wrote the song on set by clicking her nails together and forming the beat. The song went to number one for two weeks on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], as well as the U.S. country singles charts, and was nominated for several awards, including the [[Academy Award for Best Original Song]]. It won the 1981 [[People's Choice Awards|People's Choice Award]] for "Favorite Motion Picture Song", and two 1982 [[Grammy Award]]s: for "[[Grammy Award for Best Country Song|Country Song of the Year]]" and "[[Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance|Female Country Vocal of the Year]]" (it was nominated for four Grammys). Additionally, it was certified [[Platinum single|platinum]] by the [[RIAA]].

==Reception==
[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film 3 stars out of 4 and called it "pleasant entertainment, and I liked it, despite its uneven qualities and a plot that's almost too preposterous for the material." Ebert singled out Dolly Parton as "a natural-born movie star" who "contains so much energy, so much life and unstudied natural exuberance that watching her do anything in this movie is a pleasure."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/nine-to-five-1980 |title=Nine To Five |last=Ebert |first=Roger |website=[[RogerEbert.com]] |access-date=April 24, 2019 |archive-date=April 24, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190424052544/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/nine-to-five-1980 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' was less enthusiastic, writing, "It's clearly a movie that began as someone's bright idea, which then went into production before anyone had time to give it a well-defined personality."<ref>Canby, Vincent (December 19, 1980). "[https://www.nytimes.com/1980/12/19/archives/screen-nine-to-five-office-comedy-revolt-of-the-women.html Screen: 'Nine to Five,' Office Comedy—Revolt of the Women] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191020193318/https://www.nytimes.com/1980/12/19/archives/screen-nine-to-five-office-comedy-revolt-of-the-women.html |date=October 20, 2019}}". ''[[The New York Times]]''. C20.</ref>

[[Gene Siskel]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' gave the film 2.5 stars out of 4 and wrote, "The most pleasant surprise is the appearance of Dolly Parton, who with this one film establishes herself as a thoroughly engaging movie star. The biggest disappointment is that this Jane Fonda comedy about a trio of secretaries out to get their boss doesn't have more bite ... Instead of getting darker and darker, 'Nine to Five' gets lighter and lighter until it loses most of the energy it established so well early on."<ref>[[Siskel, Gene]] (December 19, 1980). "'Nine to Five': Sugar, no spice". ''[[Chicago Tribune]]''. Section 2, p. 1.</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' stated, "Although it can probably be argued that Patricia Resnick and director Colin Higgins' script at times borders on the inane, the bottom line is that this picture is a lot of fun."<ref>{{cite news|title=Film Reviews: Nine To Five |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=December 31, 1979 |page=17 |url=https://variety.com/1979/film/reviews/nine-to-five-1200424692/}}</ref> [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote that the film "appears to be an audience pleaser that never misses an intended laugh. However, it strays so far from reality for so long that it threatens to become mired in overly complicated silliness and to lose sight of the serious satirical points it wants to make. Happily, it does pull together for a finish that's as strong as it is funny."<ref>Thomas, Kevin (December 19, 1980). "[https://www.newspapers.com/clip/37552867/ Scoring Points in the '9 to 5' Game] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210430230913/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/37552867/los-angeles-times-movie-reviewnine/ |date=April 30, 2021}}". ''Los Angeles Times''. Part VI, p. 20.</ref>

Gary Arnold of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' thought the film "runs a merely weak comic premise into the ground with coarse, laborious execution". He thought that Dolly Parton was the film's "only reassuring aspect", as she seemed "an instantly likable natural on the movie screen, too".<ref>{{cite news |last=Arnold |first=Gary |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1980/12/19/squandered-stars-in-nine-to-five/775cb75d-e577-4ba4-a53c-36b614ac4e9a/ |title=Squandered Stars In 'Nine to Five' |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=December 19, 1980 |pages=E1, E10}}</ref> [[David Ansen]] of ''[[Newsweek]]'' called the film "a disappointment ... It's not wild or dark enough to qualify as a truly disturbing farce and it's too fanciful and silly to succeed as realistic [[satire]]. Politically and esthetically, it's harmless—a mildly amusing romp that tends to get swallowed up by its own overly intricate plot."<ref>{{cite news |last=Ansen |first=David |title=Get the Boss |url=https://archive.org/details/sim_newsweek_1980-12-22_96_25/page/40/mode/2up |work=[[Newsweek]] |date=December 22, 1980 |page=72}}</ref>

[[Ronald Reagan]] wrote in his presidential diary that he and his wife [[Nancy Reagan|Nancy]] watched the film on Valentine's Day 1981. He wrote, "Funny—but one scene made me mad. A truly funny scene if the 3 gals had played getting drunk but no they had to get [[Cannabis (drug)|stoned on pot]]. It was an endorsement of Pot smoking for any young person who sees the picture."<ref>{{cite web |last=Reagan |first=Ronald |author-link=Ronald Reagan |title=White House Diaries |url=https://www.reaganfoundation.org/ronald-reagan/white-house-diaries/diary-entry-02141981/ |url-status=live |publisher=The Reagan Foundation |access-date=April 11, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200929054934/https://www.reaganfoundation.org/ronald-reagan/white-house-diaries/diary-entry-02141981/ |archive-date=September 29, 2020}}</ref>

==Legacy==
===Television series===
{{main|9 to 5 (TV series)}}
The film inspired a sitcom, which aired from 1982 to 1983 and from 1986 to 1988, on [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] (1982–83) and in first-run syndication (1986–88). It featured Parton's younger sister, [[Rachel Dennison]], in Parton's role; with [[Rita Moreno]] and [[Valerie Curtin]], respectively, taking over Tomlin's and Fonda's roles. In the syndicated era, [[Sally Struthers]] replaced Moreno. A total of 85 episodes were filmed.

===Stage adaptation===
{{main|9 to 5 (musical)}}
In an interview aired on September 30, 2005, on ''[[Larry King Live]]'', Parton revealed that she was writing the songs for a [[Musical theater|musical]] stage adaptation of the film.<ref>{{cite web |format=transcript |title=Dolly Parton's Anniversary; Walter Cronkite on Peter Jennings |url=http://transcripts.cnn.com/TRANSCRIPTS/0509/30/lkl.01.html |work=[[Larry King Live]] |publisher=CNN |date=September 30, 2005 |access-date=March 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130911050009/http://transcripts.cnn.com/TRANSCRIPTS/0509/30/lkl.01.html |archive-date=September 11, 2013}}</ref> A private reading of the musical took place on January 19, 2007.<ref>{{cite web |last=Jones |first=Kenneth |url=http://www.playbill.com/news/article/104796.html |title=Ullman, Ripley, Hilty, Kudisch, Lewis Will Read ''Nine to Five'' Musical |publisher=playbill.com |date=January 11, 2007 |access-date=March 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081206032038/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/104796.html |archive-date=December 6, 2008}}</ref> Further private presentations were held in New York City in summer 2007.

In early March 2008, [[Center Theatre Group]] artistic director Michael Ritchie announced that ''9 to 5'' would have its pre-Broadway run at the center's [[Ahmanson Theatre]] in Los Angeles beginning September 21, 2008, with [[Allison Janney]] starring as Violet, joined by [[Stephanie J. Block]] as Judy, [[Megan Hilty]] as Doralee, and [[Marc Kudisch]] as Franklin Hart Jr. The libretto was written by Patricia Resnick, who co-authored the film. Andy Blankenbuehler choreographed the show, and [[Joe Mantello]] directed.<ref>{{cite web |title=CTG'S 42nd Ahmanson Theatre Season Announced |url=https://www.centertheatregroup.org/tickets/content.aspx?id=5994 |publisher=Center Theater Group |access-date=March 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120229172627/https://www.centertheatregroup.org/tickets/content.aspx?id=5994 |archive-date=February 29, 2012}}</ref>

According to Playbill, the musical opened on [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]] at the [[Marquis Theatre]] in previews on April 7, 2009, and officially on April 30, 2009.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.playbill.com/news/article/119466.html |title=Hello, Dolly! ''9 to 5'' Books Broadway's Marquis; Full Casting Announced |author=Kenneth Jones |date=July 15, 2008 |publisher=playbill.com |access-date=March 24, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090506095050/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/119466.html |archive-date=May 6, 2009}}</ref> However, due to low ticket sales and gross, the production closed on September 6, 2009. A national tour began in September 2010.

===Possible sequel===
[[File:Dolly Parton Lily Tomlin Jane Fonda (48591893841).jpg|thumb|Parton, Tomlin, and Fonda in 2000]]
In the 1980s, Universal developed a sequel with Higgins. [[Tom Mankiewicz]] worked on it for a while and says that while Parton was enthusiastic, Fonda was not and Higgins' heart was not in it.<ref>{{Cite book |last1=Mankiewicz |first1=Tom |title=My Life as a Mankiewicz: An Insider's Journey Through Hollywood V |last2=Crane |first2=Robert |publisher=University Press of Kentucky |year=2015 |isbn=978-0-8131-6123-5 |pages=278 |language=en |author-link=Tom Mankiewicz}}</ref>

In a 2005 [[BBC One]] interview broadcast, Fonda, Tomlin, and Parton all expressed interest in a sequel. Fonda said if the right script was written she would definitely do it, suggesting a suitable name for a 21st-century sequel would be ''24/7''. Parton suggested they had better hurry up before they reach [[retirement]] age. In the DVD commentary, the three reiterate their enthusiasm; Fonda suggests a sequel should cover outsourcing and they agree Hart would have to return as their nemesis.

In 2018, Parton announced that a sequel was in the works to bring the story into a modern-day setting.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.etonline.com/dolly-parton-says-original-9-to-5-cast-is-all-in-for-a-sequel-wed-love-to-do-it-97438|title=Dolly Parton Says Original '9 to 5' Cast Is All in for a Sequel: 'We'd Love to Do It'|author=Zach Seemayer|date=March 1, 2018|publisher=etonline.com|access-date=March 11, 2018|archive-date=March 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180308082517/http://www.etonline.com/dolly-parton-says-original-9-to-5-cast-is-all-in-for-a-sequel-wed-love-to-do-it-97438|url-status=live}}</ref> In July 2018, Jane Fonda also confirmed that a sequel was in the works with herself, Tomlin and Parton returning to their roles as mentors to a new generation of women. Fonda revealed that she is also an executive producer on the project.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/9-5-sequel-jane-fonda-spills-script-casting-sexual-harassment-1129804| title=Jane Fonda says '9 to 5' Sequel is Moving Forward with Original Cast| author=Michael O'Connell| work=Hollywood Reporter| date=July 25, 2018| access-date=July 25, 2018| archive-date=July 25, 2018| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180725205641/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/9-5-sequel-jane-fonda-spills-script-casting-sexual-harassment-1129804| url-status=live}}</ref> [[Rashida Jones]] and Pat Resnick have been attached to write a script.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://deadline.com/2018/02/9-to-5-reboot-rashida-jones-pat-resnick-dolly-parton-jane-fonda-lily-tomlin-1202304453/| title='9 To 5' Reboot Punching In: Rashida Jones To Script With Pat Resnick; Dolly Parton, Jane Fonda, Lily Tomlin All Circling| author=Mike Fleming Jr| work=Hollywood Deadline| date=February 28, 2018| access-date=January 6, 2019| archive-date=January 7, 2019| archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190107181012/https://deadline.com/2018/02/9-to-5-reboot-rashida-jones-pat-resnick-dolly-parton-jane-fonda-lily-tomlin-1202304453/| url-status=live}}</ref> On October 23, 2018, Fonda reiterated news about the development of a sequel on ''[[GMA Day]]''. On October 30, 2019, Parton announced the sequel had been dropped.<ref>{{cite news |author=Viktoria Ristanovic |date=October 30, 2019 |title=Dolly Parton says Jane Fonda, Lily Tomlin have 'dropped' the idea of a '9 to 5' sequel |publisher=Fox News |url=https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/dolly-parton-9-to-5-sequel-dropped |url-status=live |access-date=October 30, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191030200137/https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/dolly-parton-9-to-5-sequel-dropped |archive-date=October 30, 2019}}</ref>

In 2022, though, all three actresses made a full reunion of the ''9 to 5'' cast, after Parton appeared in a guest starring role in the final episode of the [[Netflix]] comedy streaming TV series ''[[Grace and Frankie]]'', which starred Fonda and Tomlin for seven seasons.<ref>{{cite web |date=November 5, 2021 |title=Dolly Parton Joins 'Grace and Frankie's' Final Season (TV Roundup) |url=https://variety.com/2021/tv/news/dolly-parton-grace-and-frankie-final-season-tv-news-roundup-1235105875/#! |access-date=November 13, 2021 |website=Variety}}</ref>

===Remake===
In April 2024, [[Variety (magazine)|''Variety'']] revealed that [[Jennifer Aniston]] and her [[Echo Films]] partner [[Kristin Hahn]] are producing a ''9 to 5'' reimagining for [[20th Century Studios]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/jennifer-aniston-reimagine-9-to-5-20th-century-1235981575/|title=Jennifer Aniston to Produce '9 to 5' Reimagining for 20th Century Studios|last=Zee|first=Michaela|date=April 24, 2024|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=April 28, 2024}}</ref> In January 2025, it was reported that Aniston was considering casting [[Ariana Grande]], [[Sydney Sweeney]] and [[Zendaya]] in the lead roles.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.lifeandstylemag.com/posts/jennifer-anistons-eyeing-3-hollywood-starlets-for-9-to-5-remake-excl/|title=Jennifer Aniston's Eyeing 3 Hollywood Starlets for 9 to 5 Remake (Excl)|first=Jessica|last=Stopper|date=January 20, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://tribune.com.pk/story/2523770/jennifer-anistons-dream-cast-for-9-to-5-remake-sydney-sweeney-zendaya-and-ariana-grande|title=Jennifer Aniston's dream cast for '9 to 5' remake: Sydney Sweeney, Zendaya, and Ariana Grande|first=Pop Culture &|last=Art|date=January 22, 2025|website=The Express Tribune}}</ref>

===Turkish adaptation===
There is an adaptation film titled ''[[:tr:Patron Duymasın|Patron Duymasın]]'' (''Don't Let the Boss Find Out''), which was made in Turkey in 1985. Except for the fact that the two main oppressed characters are both men (the boss is also male), the script is almost identical.<ref>[https://gurkankilicaslan.com/patron-duymasin-zeki-alasya-1985/ Patron Duymasın – Zeki Alasya (1985)] - Written by: Gürkan Kılıçaslan (a comparative review of two films) (Turkish)</ref> It has been performed by famous actors in Turkey and is occasionally broadcast on television.

==Home media==
In 1981, the film was released on [[VHS]] and [[LaserDisc]] by [[Magnetic Video Corporation]]. It would later be rereleased by its successor, [[CBS/Fox Video]] (a joint venture between [[CBS]] and 20th Century Fox) later that decade.<ref name="class">{{cite web | title=Nine to five | website=Australian Classification | date=August 30, 2019 | url=https://www.classification.gov.au/titles/nine-five-2 | access-date=August 28, 2025}}</ref><ref name="ld">{{Cite web|url=https://www.lddb.com/search.php?search=9+to+5&sort=title|title=LaserDisc Database - Search - 9 to 5|website=www.lddb.com}}</ref> The CBS/Fox Video release covered several countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, the Netherlands, Japan and Australia.<ref name="ld"/><ref name="class"/> [[20th Century Fox Home Entertainment]] later released the film on [[DVD]] in the United States on April 17, 2001.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com.au/Nine-To-Five/dp/B000EBIOQC|title=2001 9 to 5 DVD at Amazon}}</ref> In Australia, it was released on DVD in 2003 by 20th Century Fox Home Entertainment South Pacific,<ref>Copyright notice on Region 4 DVD packaging for ''9 to 5'', 2003.</ref> while in the United Kingdom it was released on DVD on March 6, 2006.<ref>{{cite web | title=9 to 5, Elevation Sales - Shop Online for Movies, DVDS in Australia | url=https://www.fishpond.com.au/Movies/9-to-5-Jane-Fonda/5039036025973?srsltid=AfmBOor_UhmAr_3s6xiHOrfE5SfFaMAvXOLKnuo-QdMZcDNAK0xBX1Ey }}</ref> During the 2000s, the film was in rotation on the [[FX Movie Channel|Fox Movie Channel]], a channel dedicated to 20th Century Fox Films.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CjfU6hi65j4|title=Fox Movie Channel: Trailers & Commercials: October 2004|date=January 1, 2024|via=YouTube}}</ref>

On March 20, 2019, [[Rupert Murdoch]] sold most of [[21st Century Fox]]'s film and television assets to [[The Walt Disney Company]],<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.cnn.com/2019/03/12/media/disney-fox-deal|title=Disney says its Fox takeover will take effect on March 20 &#124; CNN Business|first=Brian|last=Stelter|date=March 12, 2019|website=CNN}}</ref> and ''9 to 5'' was one of the films included in the deal.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://parkcircus.com/film/115768-9-to-5|title=9 to 5|website=Park Circus}}</ref> ''9 to 5'' was made available on Disney's streaming service [[Disney+]], which launched in 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.flicks.com.au/features/the-25-best-comedies-on-disney/|title=The best comedy movies on Disney+ Australia|website=Flicks.com.au}}</ref>
