'''''Lenny''''' is a 1974 American [[Biographical film|biographical]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama]] film about the comedian [[Lenny Bruce]], starring [[Dustin Hoffman]] and directed by [[Bob Fosse]]. The screenplay by [[Julian Barry]] is based on his play of the same name.

==Plot==
The film jumps between various sections of Lenny Bruce's life, including scenes of when he was in his prime, and the burned-out, strung-out performer who, in the twilight of his life, used his nightclub act to pour out his personal frustrations. Up-and-coming Bruce courts his "[[Shiksa]] goddess", a stripper named Honey. With family responsibilities, Lenny is encouraged to do a "safe" act, but he cannot do it. Constantly in trouble for flouting obscenity laws, Lenny develops a near-messianic complex that fuels both his comedy genius and his talent for self-destruction. Worn out by a lifetime of tilting at establishment windmills, Lenny Bruce dies of a [[morphine]] overdose in 1966.

==Reception==
===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 88% based on 34 reviews. The critical consensus reads: "Dustin Hoffman inhabits Lenny Bruce with nervy energy in Bob Fosse's richly stylized telling of the pioneering comedian's career and downfall."<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/lenny/ |title= ''Lenny'' Movie Reviews, Pictures |website= [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date= May 31, 2026}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 61 out of 100, based on 9 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |title=Lenny |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/lenny |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date= 2020-07-01 }}</ref>

[[Pauline Kael]] for ''[[The New Yorker]]'' wrote that the film was "for audiences who want to believe that [[Lenny Bruce]] was a saintly gadfly who was martyred for having lived before their time" and mentions it's "well made" but complains that it takes itself so "insufferably seriously." Kael finishes with the acknowledgement that the movie is "black and white earnest" and that the "youth-culture saintliness laid on Lenny Bruce are the ultimate in modern showbiz sentimentality."<ref>{{Cite book |last=Kael |first=Pauline |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_Age_of_Movies_Selected_Writings_of_P/tkShTL84MrcC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=the+age+of+movies+selected+writings+pauline+kael&printsec=frontcover |title=The Age of Movies: Selected Writings of Pauline Kael: A Library of America Special Publication |date=2011-10-27 |publisher=Library of America |isbn=978-1-59853-171-8 |pages=432–439 |language=en}}</ref>

One of the less enthusiastic reviews came from [[Roger Ebert]], stating, "Unless we go in convinced that Lenny Bruce was an important performer, the movie doesn't convince us."<ref>{{cite web |last= Ebert |first= Roger |author-link= Roger Ebert |title= Lenny movie review & film summary (1974) |url= https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/lenny-1974 |website= [[Chicago Sun-Times]] }}</ref>

In 2012, British film critic [[Mark Kermode]] put Hoffman's performance as Lenny Bruce at number eight in a top-ten video of Hoffman's best performances.<ref>Archived at [https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211211/6tsOsSZrEB4 Ghostarchive]{{cbignore}} and the [https://web.archive.org/web/20121210061000/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6tsOsSZrEB4&gl=US&hl=en Wayback Machine]{{cbignore}}: {{Cite AV media |author= kermodeandmayo |title= Kermode Uncut: Hoffman Top Ten |date= 2012-12-04 |url= https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6tsOsSZrEB4 |via= [[YouTube]] |access-date= 2017-10-25 }}{{cbignore}}</ref>
