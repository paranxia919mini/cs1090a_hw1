'''''Purple Rain''''' is a 1984 American [[Romance film|romantic]] [[rock musical]] [[drama film]] directed by [[Albert Magnoli]] from a screenplay co-written by Magnoli and [[William Blinn]]. It stars [[Prince (musician)|Prince]] (in his feature film debut), [[Apollonia Kotero]], [[Morris Day]], [[Olga Karlatos]], and [[Clarence Williams III]]. In the film, the Kid (Prince), a rising musician, grapples with his turbulent personal life as he competes with rival local musician Morris (Day).

Development on ''Purple Rain'' began in 1982, stemming from Prince's desire to star in a film: his manager, Robert Cavallo, commissioned Blinn to adapt plot points written by Prince into a screenplay. Magnoli was hired as director and changed several elements of Blinn's initial screenplay. Casting for ''Purple Rain'' was extensive, with studio executives attempting to replace Prince as the film's lead. [[Principal photography]] began in October 1983 and lasted until that December, with filming primarily taking place across [[Minneapolis]]. Production faced delays or schedule alterations due to a feud between Day and Prince.

''Purple Rain'' was theatrically released in the United States on July 27, 1984, by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]]. It grossed $70.3 million worldwide and received mixed reviews from critics, with praise for Prince's score and its [[Purple Rain (album)|soundtrack]] but criticism for its depiction of its female characters. The film won for [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]] at the [[57th Academy Awards]] and its songs "[[When Doves Cry]]", "[[Let's Go Crazy]]", and "[[Purple Rain (song)|Purple Rain]]" went platinum in the United States. In 2019, the film was selected for preservation in the U.S. [[National Film Registry]] by the [[Library of Congress]] for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".<ref name="Chow">{{cite magazine |last=Chow |first=Andrew R. |date=December 11, 2019 |title=See the 25 New Additions to the National Film Registry, From Purple Rain to Clerks |url=https://time.com/5747503/national-film-registry-2019-additions/ |magazine=Time |location=New York, NY |access-date=December 11, 2019 |archive-date=December 11, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191211082207/https://time.com/5747503/national-film-registry-2019-additions/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/moviesnow/la-et-mn-prince-purple-rain-songs-movie-20160421-story.html|title=Prince's movie legacy: Will there ever be another like 'Purple Rain'?|date=2016-04-21|website=Los Angeles Times|language=en-US|access-date=2019-12-15|archive-date=August 6, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190806222656/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/moviesnow/la-et-mn-prince-purple-rain-songs-movie-20160421-story.html|url-status=live|last=Zeitchik|first=Steven}}</ref>

==Plot==
The Kid is the talented but troubled [[frontman]] of his [[Minneapolis]]-based band [[The Revolution (band)|The Revolution]]. To escape his abusive home life, The Kid spends his days rehearsing and nights performing at the [[First Avenue (nightclub)|First Avenue]] nightclub.

The Revolution, the flashy [[Morris Day]] and his group [[The Time (band)|the Time]], and [[Dez Dickerson]] and his group the Modernaires hold the nightclub's three [[house band]] slots. Morris, aware that the Revolution's guitarist Wendy and keyboardist Lisa are frustrated that The Kid is unwilling to play their compositions, lobbies Billy Sparks, the club's owner, to replace The Revolution with a [[girl group]] which Morris is already forming. Morris targets The Kid's girlfriend Apollonia, an aspiring singer and new arrival to Minneapolis, to lead his group, and tries to persuade her that The Kid is too self-centered to help her. She eventually joins Morris's group, which Morris names [[Apollonia 6]]. When Apollonia reveals her newfound partnership to The Kid, he becomes furious and slaps her.

At the club, The Kid responds to the band's internal strife, the pressure to draw larger crowds, and his strained private life with the uncomfortably personal "[[Darling Nikki]]". His performance publicly humiliates Apollonia, who runs off in tears, and angers both Morris and Billy, worsening his situation. Billy confronts The Kid, castigating him for bringing his emotional baggage onto the stage, before warning him that he is wasting his musical talent as his father did. Apollonia 6 successfully debuts, and Billy warns The Kid that his First Avenue slot is at risk.

The Kid seizes Apollonia from a drunken Morris, leading them to argue and fight; Apollonia then abandons him. Returning home, The Kid finds the house in tatters, with his mother nowhere to be found. His abusive father, who had been lurking in the basement with a loaded handgun, attempts suicide with a gunshot to his head. Frenzied after a night of torment, The Kid tears apart the basement to release his frustration, only to find a large box of his father's musical compositions. The next morning, The Kid picks up a [[cassette tape]] of one of Wendy and Lisa's compositions, a rhythm track named "Slow Groove", and begins to compose.

That night at First Avenue, all is quiet in the Revolution's dressing room until the Time stops by to taunt The Kid. Once on stage, The Kid announces that he will be playing "a song the girls in the band wrote", dedicated to his father, revealed to be "[[Purple Rain (song)|Purple Rain]]". As the song ends, The Kid rushes from the stage and out the back door of the club, intending to ride away on his motorcycle; before he can, however, The Kid realizes that "Purple Rain" has thrilled the crowd.

The Kid returns to the club, with his fellow musicians greeting him with approval and a teary-eyed Apollonia embracing him. The Kid returns to the stage for two encores with the Revolution ("[[I Would Die 4 U]]" and "[[Baby I'm a Star]]") to the wild approval of the crowd and even Morris. Overlaid scenes show The Kid visiting his parents in the hospital and sorting his father's compositions in the basement, accompanied by Apollonia. A montage of all the songs plays as the credits roll.

==Production==
===Development===
After the success of his album ''[[1999 (Prince album)|1999]]'', [[Prince (musician)|Prince]] confronted his then-manager Robert Cavallo and told him he would not renew his contract with him unless he got to star in a studio film. Every studio they had met with rejected the premise of a musician-led film, leading Cavallo to produce the film himself.<ref name="Gilchrist">{{cite web|last=Gilchrist|first=Todd|url=https://variety.com/2019/film/news/prince-purple-rain-anniversary-director-gets-deep-1203274953/|title=''Purple Rain'' Director Gets Deep About Working With Prince: 'How Is It You Just Told My Life Story?'|date=July 26, 2019|website=Variety|access-date=August 29, 2020|archive-date=October 25, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201025001924/https://variety.com/2019/film/news/prince-purple-rain-anniversary-director-gets-deep-1203274953/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[David Geffen]] and [[Richard Pryor]] were among those who passed on the film.<ref>{{cite web|last=Gilchrist|first=Todd|url=https://www.moviefone.com/2019/07/27/purple-rain-trivia-prince/|title=Baby, I'm A Star: 19 Things You Didn't Know About Prince's ''Purple Rain''|date=July 27, 2019|website=Movie Fone|access-date=April 5, 2022}}</ref> Prince spent months writing down the basic plot points of the film.<ref name="Gilchrist"/> Impressed with his work on ''[[Fame (1982 TV series)|Fame]]'', Cavallo commissioned [[William Blinn]] to write the script.<ref>{{cite web|last=Haring|first=Bruce|url=https://deadline.com/2020/10/william-blinn-dies-emmy-winning-screenwriter-purple-rain-starskyhutch-obituarary-1234602939/|title=William Blinn Dies: Emmy-Winning Screenwriter For ''Brian's Song'' And ''Roots'' Was 83|date=October 24, 2020|website=Deadline|access-date=April 6, 2022}}</ref> Blinn's original script, titled ''Dreams'', was much darker.<ref name="Gilchrist"/>

''[[Reckless (1984 film)|Reckless]]'' director [[James Foley (director)|James Foley]] was offered to direct the film, but was not interested and instead passed it on to his editor [[Albert Magnoli]], who disliked Blinn's script for lacking "truth", and was then hired to direct and edit after delivering a pitch on the spot to Cavallo.<ref name="Gilchrist"/><ref>{{cite web|author=O'Connor|url=https://timeline.com/prince-purple-rain-movie-147e38d30f78|title=Prince Was the Only Person Who Thought ''Purple Rain'' Would Be a Box-Office Hit|date=April 20, 2017|website=Timeline|access-date=August 29, 2020|archive-date=May 31, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170531084017/https://timeline.com/prince-purple-rain-movie-147e38d30f78|url-status=dead|first=Josh}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Cain|first=Cody|url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/the-director-of-purple-rain-prince-memories_b_9853060?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAJCP8LMN1_l_xv6S1Fuj8UIG2LCLWqtAzlO-DQbPhvpPfSVD9qhfJk_fxyN1yTzonr8tClkhfme_nbVHmOh2T744YcbdAPIxjxBt2k6Xeh84c_Gf0u1UWFzsYk6fzMogKJGiZy9ALPlrmyAVlMSFLe2_6_LB6EXkRx7UKwaZeSZ1|title=The Director of ''Purple Rain'', Albert Magnoli, Honors Prince With Memories|date=May 7, 2017|website=HuffPost|access-date=August 29, 2020|archive-date=January 16, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210116124702/https://www.huffpost.com/entry/the-director-of-purple-rain-prince-memories_b_9853060?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAJCP8LMN1_l_xv6S1Fuj8UIG2LCLWqtAzlO-DQbPhvpPfSVD9qhfJk_fxyN1yTzonr8tClkhfme_nbVHmOh2T744YcbdAPIxjxBt2k6Xeh84c_Gf0u1UWFzsYk6fzMogKJGiZy9ALPlrmyAVlMSFLe2_6_LB6EXkRx7UKwaZeSZ1|url-status=live}}</ref> Allegedly during the first meeting with [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]], the studio executives asked Cavallo if [[John Travolta]] could replace Prince as the film's lead.<ref>{{cite web|last=Grubb|first=Brian|url=https://uproxx.com/movies/purple-rain-travolta-prince/|title=Warner Bros.' First Choice For Prince's Role In ''Purple Rain''? John Travolta!|date=April 29, 2016|website=Uproxx|access-date=April 5, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160501212520/https://uproxx.com/movies/purple-rain-travolta-prince/|archive-date=May 1, 2016}}</ref>

Prince intended to cast [[Vanity (singer)|Vanity]], leader of the girl group [[Vanity 6]], but she left the group before filming began. Her role was initially offered to [[Jennifer Beals]] (who turned it down because she wanted to concentrate on college) before going to Apollonia Kotero, who was then virtually unknown. Prince had seen her appearance on the February 1983 episode of ''[[Tales of the Gold Monkey]]''.{{sfn|Hahn|2004|p = 118}} [[Lorraine Bracco]] at on point was considered for the role  but the producers found out that she couldn’t sing so they rejected her. Excluding Prince and his onscreen parents, almost every character in the movie is named after the actor who plays them. Despite what was depicted in the film, both of Prince's real parents were Black. Reportedly, Prince's mother was upset that a white woman was cast to play his mother. Kotero was the last to audition and caught the eye of Magnoli. Kotero had no background in singing and was doubled by [[Lisa Coleman (musician)|Lisa Coleman]] in her vocals on "[[Take Me With U]]".<ref>{{cite web|last=Raftery|first=Brian|url=https://www.spin.com/2016/04/prince-the-oral-history-of-purple-rain-brian-raftery/|title=Prince: The Oral History Of ''Purple Rain''|date=April 22, 2016|website=Spin|access-date=April 6, 2022}}</ref>

After the character change from Vanity to Apollonia, the script was drastically revised, and many dark scenes were cut. Some of these scenes include Prince and Apollonia having sex in a barn (a concept which was the story behind the 1985 song "[[Raspberry Beret]]"); Prince going to Apollonia 6's rehearsal and physically fighting with the members of The Time; and a scene which featured Prince's mother talking to him about her shaky relationship with his father. In addition, many scenes such as the "Lake Minnetonka" scene, Apollonia first meeting Morris, and the railyard scene were cut down because of time constraints. Many clips from these scenes were featured, however, in the trailer for the movie as well as the "When Doves Cry" and "Let's Go Crazy" montage. Prince required the other musicians in the film to take acting classes prior to filming. [[Morris Day]] was supposedly kicked out of the classes for "clowning around".<ref name="Getlen">{{cite web|last=Getlen|first=Larry|url=https://nypost.com/2014/11/29/inside-the-making-of-princes-classic-purple-rain/|title=Inside the Unlikely Making of ''Purple Rain''|date=November 29, 2014|website=New York Post|access-date=April 5, 2022}}</ref>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] began on October 31, 1983, in [[Minneapolis, Minnesota]], and spanned 42 days.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Browne|first=David|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-news/purple-rain-director-talks-princes-weird-wonderful-masterpiece-75944/|title=''Purple Rain'' Director Talks Prince's Weird, Wonderful Masterpiece|date=April 29, 2016|magazine=Rolling Stone|access-date=April 6, 2022}}</ref> The film features many local landmarks, including the Crystal Court of the [[IDS Center]] (also shown in segments of the opening credits to ''[[The Mary Tyler Moore Show]]'') and the [[First Avenue (nightclub)|First Avenue]] nightclub, which was paid $100,000 for usage during filming and was closed for 25 days.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://discussions.mnhs.org/collections/2011/11/purple-rain-document/|title=Purple Rain/First Avenue Agreement|website=Discussions.mnhs.org|access-date=November 30, 2011|archive-date=March 19, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180319151553/http://discussions.mnhs.org/collections/2011/11/purple-rain-document/|url-status=dead|date=November 28, 2011|last=Williamson|first=Lori}}</ref> According to [[Alan Leeds]], several days of shooting were altered due to Day refusing to show up to set due to a rivalry with Prince.<ref name="Getlen"/>

The Huntington Hotel, where Apollonia stayed, is located on Main Street in downtown [[Los Angeles]]. In the film, it is supposed to be across the street from First Avenue. In the film, Prince rides a customized [[Hondamatic]] [[Honda CM400|Honda CM400A]] motorcycle.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025 |title=Remembering the Purple One |url=https://hennepinarts.org/remembering-the-purple-one-motorcycle |access-date=2026-08-04 |website=Hennepin Arts |language=en}}</ref> The road north from [[Henderson Station, Minnesota]], along the Minnesota River was the setting for the motorcycle ride scenes.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fischenich |first=Mark |date=2016-04-27 |title=Road near Henderson was setting for indelible 'Purple Rain' scenes |url=https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/local_news/road-near-henderson-was-setting-for-indelible-purple-rain-scenes/article_b1c0fb56-0c1a-11e6-811e-77e7a4fdb18b.html |access-date=2026-08-04 |website=Mankato Free Press |language=en |quote=Not-Lake Minnetonka was actually the Minnesota River in famous movie scene}}</ref> Some of the First Avenue scenes were shot at [[The Wiltern]].

During production, Magnoli asked Prince to write one more song that touched on the themes of the film. The following day, Prince already had the song, "[[When Doves Cry]]", fully produced.<ref>{{cite web|last=Cataldo|first=Jennie|url=https://theworld.org/stories/2019/05/16/when-doves-cry-35|title='When Doves Cry' at 35|date=May 16, 2019|website=The World|access-date=August 29, 2020|archive-date=September 17, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200917060051/https://www.pri.org/stories/2019-05-16/when-doves-cry-35|url-status=live}}</ref>

Although [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] considered the film "outrageous" at the time, it was accepted for distribution after an internal debate. Music industry publicist [[Howard Bloom]] had advocated for the film to be released and said that "killing ''Purple Rain'' would be a sin against art!".<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.brooklynpaper.com/stories/32/30/32_30_jk_real_prince.html|title=The Park Slope Man Who Saved ''Purple Rain''!|last=Kleinman|first=Jacob|work=The Brooklyn Paper|date=July 28, 2009|access-date=August 9, 2009|archive-date=September 8, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170908141635/http://www.brooklynpaper.com/stories/32/30/32_30_jk_real_prince.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Music==
{{Main|Purple Rain (album)}}
The film is tied into the [[Purple Rain (album)|album of the same name]], which spawned two chart-topping singles, "[[When Doves Cry]]" and the opening number "[[Let's Go Crazy]]", as well as "[[Purple Rain (song)|Purple Rain]]", which reached number two. The film won an [[Academy Award for Best Original Song Score]]. The soundtrack sold over 15 million copies in the United States alone, and 25 million worldwide.<ref name=":1">{{cite news | url= http://www.hindustantimes.com/StoryPage/StoryPage.aspx?sectionName=NLetter&id=40e182fc-061c-43d5-832e-ff930aa4a508&Headline=Those+chart+busters | work= The Hindustan Times | title= Those chart busters | url-status= dead | archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20090521191026/http://www.hindustantimes.com/StoryPage/StoryPage.aspx?sectionName=NLetter&id=40e182fc-061c-43d5-832e-ff930aa4a508&Headline=Those+chart+busters | archive-date= May 21, 2009 | df= mdy-all |last=Taneja|first=Nikhil|date=December 9, 2008}}</ref> The film also coincided with [[Spin-off (media)|spin-off]] albums by [[The Time (band)|The Time]] (''[[Ice Cream Castle]]'') and Apollonia 6 ([[Apollonia 6 (album)|their self-titled album]]).

''Purple Rain'' became Prince's first album to reach number one on the [[Billboard 200|''Billboard'' 200]]. The album spent 24 consecutive weeks atop on the ''Billboard'' 200 and was present on the chart for a total of 167 weeks. "When Doves Cry" and "[[Let's Go Crazy]]" reached number one on the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]], while "[[Purple Rain (song)|Purple Rain]]" peaked at number two and "[[I Would Die 4&nbsp;U]]" peaked at number eight. In May 1996, the album was certified [[RIAA certification|13× Platinum]] by the [[Recording Industry Association of America]] (RIAA). It is Prince's commercial peak, with total sales standing at 25 million copies worldwide, making it one of the [[List of best-selling albums|best-selling albums of all time]]. Prince and the Revolution won [[27th Annual Grammy Awards|Grammy Awards]] for [[Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal]] and [[Best Score Soundtrack for Visual Media]], while Prince also won the [[Academy Award for Best Original Song Score]] for the film ''Purple Rain''.

Music critics noted the innovative and experimental aspects of the soundtrack's music, most famously on the spare, bass-less "When Doves Cry". Other aspects of the music, especially its synthesis of electronic elements with organic instrumentation and full-band performances along with its consolidation of rock and R&B, were identified by critics as distinguishing, even experimental factors. ''Purple Rain'' is regularly ranked among the greatest albums of all time. ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' ranked the album number eight on its list of the [[Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time|500 Greatest Albums of All Time]]. It was inducted into the [[Grammy Hall of Fame]] and added to the [[Library of Congress]]' [[National Recording Registry]] list of sound recordings that are "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

==Release==
''Purple Rain'' was released on July 27, 1984, by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]].

===Home media===
[[Warner Home Video]] released ''Purple Rain'' on [[VHS]], [[Betamax]] and [[LaserDisc]] in 1984 while the film was still in theaters. At the time, it was one of the first major releases to be sold at the lower listed price of $29.95 in the United States.<ref name=hv>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=1|title=Par Home Vid Cutting Prices on 'Trek' Tapes|date=December 21, 1984}}</ref> It shipped 435,000 units.<ref name=hv/> It was released on [[DVD]] in 1999.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/0790731533/|title=Purple Rain [DVD]|date=February 3, 2004 |publisher=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=December 1, 2016|archive-date=March 7, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140307164034/http://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/0790731533|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was released on [[Blu-ray]] and [[HD DVD]] on July 24, 2007.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/B000R208LC/ref=pd_aw_sbs_74_3 |title=Purple Rain [Blu-ray]|date=July 24, 2007 |publisher=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=December 1, 2016}}</ref> On October 4, 2016, a remastered Blu-ray with higher-quality [[Advanced Video Coding|AVC]] encoded video in the film's original 1.85:1 aspect ratio (compared to the 2007 Blu-ray's [[VC-1]] encode and 16:9 aspect ratio) was released as part of the Prince Movie Collection in a purple [[keep case]] (as opposed to the normal blue).<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/B01JT8MSXU/ |title=Purple Rain / Graffiti Bridge / Under the Cherry Moon (BD) (3pk) [Blu-ray]|date=October 4, 2016 |publisher=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=December 1, 2016}}</ref> To celebrate the 40th anniversary of its theatrical release, ''Purple Rain'' was released on [[4K Blu-ray]] on June 25, 2024.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Purple-Rain-4K-Blu-ray/357839/ |website=Blu-ray.com |access-date=31 May 2024 |title=Purple Rain 4K Blu-ray (40th Anniversary Edition) }}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film was commercially successful, grossing $68,392,977 in the United States.<ref name="mojo">{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=purplerain.htm |title=Purple Rain (1984) |website=Box Office Mojo |access-date=April 21, 2017 |archive-date=August 1, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170801025625/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=purplerain.htm |url-status=live }}</ref> It grossed $70.3 million worldwide against its $7.2 million budget.<ref name="Rockhall" />

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has a 73% rating based on 59 reviews and an average rating of 6.50/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "''Purple Rain'' makes for undeniably uneven cinema, but it's held together by its star's singular charisma – not to mention a slew of classic songs."<ref>{{rotten-tomatoes|purple_rain|Purple Rain}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 55 out of 100 based on 14 reviews, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{Metacritic film|title=Purple Rain}}</ref> Prince famously said after the movie opened that he had a nightmare that [[Roger Ebert]] and [[Gene Siskel]] hated the movie and tore it apart on their TV show; in fact, both critics loved ''Purple Rain'' and put it on their Top 10 lists of the best films in 1984.<ref>{{cite episode |url= | last=Siskel |first=Gene |series=At the Movies |season=3 |number=15 |title=The best of 1984 |website=IMDB | date=January 5, 1985}}</ref> Roger Ebert would go on to call it "one of the greatest rock movies of all time".<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert | first=Roger | url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/sign-o-the-times-1987 |title=Sign O' the Times| newspaper=Chicago Sun Times |date=November 20, 1987|via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref>

In recent years, some have criticised the film as [[misogynistic]], especially the scene in which Jerome throws a woman into a dumpster at Morris's behest. David Alm in ''[[Forbes]]'' wrote that the female characters are treated "quite literally like garbage."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Alm |first1=David |title=Re-watching 'Purple Rain' After 30 Years |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidalm/2016/04/22/re-watching-purple-rain-after-30-years/ |access-date=10 July 2024 |work=Forbes |date=22 April 2016}}</ref> Tom Leatham of ''Far Out'' wrote that the film "(explores) eroticism and sexuality with a complete disregard for the respect of women (and) shocking misogyny" with "little redemption", going so far as to call for the film to be removed from the United States [[National Film Registry]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Leatham |first1=Tom |title=The overt misogyny in Prince's 'Purple Rain' movie |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/misogyny-prince-purple-rain-movie/ |access-date=10 July 2024 |work=Far Out |date=31 August 2022}}</ref><!-- Since Far Out magazine is considered to engage in Churnalism, should it be cited here? -->

Others have perceived the film's treatment of women as a criticism of misogyny, rather than an endorsement of it. "Prince saw fit to hide in plain sight behind celluloid and glamour", wrote Ben Philpott of ''Drowned in Sound''. "The conflict between commercialism, integrity, sex, misogyny and morality is clear and nobody has done it better since."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Philpott |first1=Ben |title=Prince's Purple Rain, Sex and Misogyny: A Contemporary Perspective |url=https://drownedinsound.com/in_depth/4146103-princes-purple-rain-sex-and-misogyny--a-contemporary-perspective |access-date=10 July 2024 |work=Drowned in Sound |date=7 March 2013}}</ref> Elwin Cotman of ''Bright Wall/Dark Room'' also wrote that the film paved the way for later films by [[Spike Lee]], as a then-revolutionary depiction of flawed, complicated Black men:<blockquote>''Purple Rain'' is a story about misogynists, and about misogyny itself; hatred of women undergirds the film’s motif wherein Black men treat white women like trash, sometimes literally. There is deep-seated racial resentment to all the beatings, pranks, gaslighting, and insults. And unlike most Hollywood films, where actors ranging from [[Leonardo DiCaprio]] to [[Woody Allen]] portray misogyny as heroic, ''Purple Rain'' dares to make it a sign of weakness and disintegration, rooted in trauma... Magnoli presents The Kid’s treatment of women as a denial of his feminine side. Such in-depth psychology for a Black male character was uncommon at the time.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Cotman |first1=Elwin |date=9 June 2021 |title=Is He Okay? |url=https://www.brightwalldarkroom.com/2021/06/09/is-he-okay/ |access-date=10 July 2024 |work=Bright Wall/Dark Room}}</ref></blockquote>

==Legacy==
In 1986, Prince followed up ''Purple Rain'' with ''[[Under the Cherry Moon]]'', a [[black and white]] [[screwball comedy]] shot on location in the south of France in an [[Old Hollywood]] style. Prince starred as the lead character and also directed the film himself, while Benton returned as his sidekick. While the ''[[Parade (Prince album)|Parade]]'' soundtrack album was a hit, initial critical reception of the film was largely negative with many thrown off by the completely different tone from ''Purple Rain''. Later years saw critical re-evaluation of ''Under the Cherry Moon'' in a more positive light, and the development of a [[cult following]].

In 1987, Prince directed ''[[Sign o' the Times (film)|Sign o' the Times]]'', a [[concert film]] based on the [[Sign o' the Times Tour]]. He returned to narrative filmmaking with 1990's ''[[Graffiti Bridge (film)|Graffiti Bridge]]'', a direct sequel to ''Purple Rain'' which follows up on the rivalry between Morris Day and The Kid. It was largely negatively received, but again the [[Graffiti Bridge (album)|soundtrack album]] was a success.

[[Eminem]]'s loosely autobiographical 2002 feature film ''[[8 Mile (film)|8 Mile]]'' is often compared to ''Purple Rain'' and has been even called "identical" in plot: "Young musician character in a grungy upper Midwest city first fails and then succeeds in musical battle against a hated rival musician."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Parrish |first1=Geov |title=8 Mile's Lie |url=https://www.seattleweekly.com/news/8-miles-lie/ |access-date=11 July 2024 |work=Seattle Weekly |date=9 October 2006}}</ref>

In 2015, a [[Tuareg language|Tuareg-language]]-remake of ''Purple Rain'' starring the Nigerien guitarist [[Mdou Moctar]] entitled ''[[Akounak Tedalat Taha Tazoughai]]'' ("Rain the Color of Blue with a Little Red In It") was released. That same year, Prince purchased the house on Snelling Avenue in the [[Longfellow, Minneapolis|Longfellow community]] that was used for exterior scenes of The Kid's family home in the original.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.startribune.com/nothing-to-kid-about-prince-bought-the-purple-rain-house-last-summer/387497501/|title=Nothing to Kid about: Prince bought the 'Purple Rain' house last summer|last=Riemenschneider|first=Chris|date=July 20, 2016|website=Star Tribune|language=en|access-date=January 25, 2020|archive-date=January 25, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200125012733/http://www.startribune.com/nothing-to-kid-about-prince-bought-the-purple-rain-house-last-summer/387497501/|url-status=live}}</ref> It remains the property of his estate and is now a popular tourist destination.
