'''''Take Me Out to the Ball Game''''' (released in the United Kingdom as '''''Everybody's Cheering''''') is a 1949 American [[Technicolor]] [[Musical film|musical]] [[romantic comedy]] film directed by [[Busby Berkeley]] and produced in the [[Arthur Freed]] unit of [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM]]. It stars [[Frank Sinatra]], [[Esther Williams]], [[Gene Kelly]], [[Betty Garrett]], [[Edward Arnold (actor)|Edward Arnold]], and [[Jules Munshin]]. The title and nominal theme is taken from the unofficial anthem of American [[baseball]], "[[Take Me Out to the Ball Game]]".

==Plot==
In the early 1900s, world champion baseball team, the Wolves, is preparing for spring training but is missing star players Dennis Ryan and Eddie O'Brien, who are late returning from their off-season jobs as [[vaudeville]] performers. While Eddie prefers performing, Dennis is eager to return to baseball. When they finally arrive in [[Sarasota, Florida]], they discover the team's owner has died and bequeathed it to a distant relative, K.C. Higgins, who is actually a woman named Katherine. She impresses the team with her baseball knowledge and skills, except for Eddie, who acts condescending toward her.

Katherine imposes a strict curfew on the team, with fines for breaking it. Eddie, who wants to go out for the evening, notices Katherine swimming, and he and fellow player Nat Goldberg encourage shy Dennis to talk to her, hoping to arrange a triple date to avoid curfew. Dennis sings to her, but Katherine is not interested and politely ends the conversation. Undeterred, Eddie climbs to her balcony to impress her, but is sent away and receives a fine. During the opening game, Eddie, Dennis, and Nat entertain the crowd with a skit, and one fan, Shirley Delwyn, becomes infatuated with Dennis. Tensions rise when Katherine scolds Eddie for heckling the [[Umpire (baseball)|umpire]], only to lose her own temper later. The Wolves lose, and Shirley pursues Dennis throughout the stadium, determined to win his affection.

During a mid-season break at a clambake, Katherine and Eddie share a kiss, but Katherine feels humiliated when she learns it was part of a bet. Confused about her feelings, she kisses Dennis in front of Eddie, but neither feels a spark, leading Dennis to realize his feelings for Shirley. Meanwhile, mobster Joe Lorgan, who bet a large sum against the team, sees Eddie perform and offers him a nightclub gig that conflicts with his baseball schedule. Eddie decides to secretly rehearse at night, but starts to struggle in games due to exhaustion. Mike Gilhuly, the Wolves' manager, misinterprets Eddie's decline as love troubles, suggesting Katherine spend time with him.

Eddie and Katherine privately discuss their feelings for each other when Joe interrupts and tells Katherine about Eddie's nighttime rehearsals, leading her to kick him off the team. The Wolves' chances of winning the pennant plummet without Eddie. In a bid to save the team, Eddie encourages a group of children to chant for him at the championship game, leading to a stadium-wide rally. The team joyously welcomes him back, and Katherine agrees to let him join the game. However, Shirley overhears Joe order his henchmen to ensure Eddie does not play and warns Dennis, who chooses to keep Eddie safe by knocking him out with a real baseball during their skit.

Eddie is taken to the locker room, where Joe's men, posing as doctors, knock him out every time he wakes up. Shirley recognizes the men and, with Katherine's help, gets the Wolves to throw them out. When Eddie comes to, he learns that Dennis knocked him out on purpose and furiously hits a home run before chasing Dennis around the bases, which wins the pennant for the Wolves. The police arrest Joe and his gang as the crowd lifts Eddie and Dennis into the air in celebration.

==Production==
The film was announced in May 1948. It was based on a story by Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen, with a script by Harry Tugend.<ref>{{cite news |last=Brady |first=Thomas F. |title=Kelly, Sinatra set for Baseball Film |date=May 18, 1948 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |id={{ProQuest|108194628}}}}</ref> The female lead of club owner K.C. Higgins was originally to be played by [[Ginger Rogers]], but she withdrew a month before filming and [[Esther Williams]] replaced her.<ref>{{cite news |last=Brady |first=Thomas F. |title=Beatrice Pearson set for Film Role |date=May 29, 1948 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |id={{ProQuest|108194242}}}}</ref> Williams claimed that [[Judy Garland]] was originally slated to star but was replaced because of [[substance abuse]] issues.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Williams |first1=Esther |author-link1=Esther Williams |last2=Diehl |first2=Digby |url=https://archive.org/details/milliondollarmer00will/page/162/mode/2up |title=The Million Dollar Mermaid: An Autobiography |location=San Diego |publisher=[[Harcourt Brace]] |year=1999 |page=162 |isbn=978-0-15-601135-8 |url-access=registration}}</ref> Sinatra's role of Dennis Ryan was originally intended for professional baseball manager (and former player) [[Leo Durocher]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Erickson |first=Hal |url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/take-me-out-to-the-ball-game-v48429 |title=Take Me Out to the Ball Game (1949) – Busby Berkeley |website=[[AllMovie]] |access-date=March 15, 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190323213349/https://www.allmovie.com/movie/take-me-out-to-the-ball-game-v48429 |archive-date=March 23, 2019 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

According to [[TCM Movies|TCM]]'s [[Alicia Malone]], Williams maintained a positive relationship with Sinatra but did not enjoy making the film because of the exhausting directorial demands set by Kelly. Although Busby Berkeley was hired as director by producer Arthur Freed, Berkeley withdrew and much of the film was directed by Kelly and Stanley Donen. Though the reason provided for Berkeley's departure was exhaustion, his exit may have been necessitated by his chronic alcoholism and depression. However, his touch can be seen in Williams's pool sequence.<ref>{{cite book |last=O'Brien |first=Daniel |url=https://archive.org/details/franksinatrafilm0000obri/page/40/mode/2up |title=The Frank Sinatra Film Guide |location=London |publisher=[[BT Batsford]] |year=1998 |page=41 |isbn=978-0-7134-8418-2 |url-access=registration}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Take Me Out to the Ball Game'' was a box-office success, earning $2,987,000 in the United States and Canada and $978,000 overseas, resulting in a profit of $675,000.<ref name="Mannix"/><ref name="top">{{cite magazine|url=https://archive.org/stream/variety177-1950-01#page/n58/mode/1up|title=Top Grossers of 1949|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=4 January 1950|page=59}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On March 13, 1949, [[Bob Thomas (reporter)|Bob Thomas]] of the [[Associated Press]] wrote, "''Take Me Out to the Ball Game'' is a sure cure for anybody's blues. Seldom has there been a film loaded with such fine entertainment. Bright young people like Frank Sinatra, Gene Kelly, Esther Williams, Betty Garrett and Jules Munshin make even the contrived plot likable."<ref>{{cite news |last=Thomas |first=Bob |author-link=Bob Thomas (reporter) |url=https://cdnc.ucr.edu/?a=d&d=SBS19490313.1.4&srpos=1&e=------194-en--20--1--txt-txIN--------1 |title=Capsule review: 'Take Me Out to the Ball Game' (M.G.M.) |newspaper=[[San Bernardino Sun-Telegram]] |volume=1 |issue=98 |date=March 13, 1949 |page=4 |via=[[California Digital Newspaper Collection]]}}</ref>

[[The New York Times]]' [[Bosley Crowther]] gave the film a mixed review, concluding, "For all its high spots, however, the show lacks consistent style and pace, and the stars are forced to clown and grimace much more than becomes their speed. Actually, the plotted humor is conspicuously bush-league stuff. Don't be surprised if you see people getting up for a seventh-inning stretch."<ref>{{cite news |last=Crowther |first=Bosley |author-link=Bosley Crowther |title=Frank Sinatra and Gene Kelly in 'Take Me Out to the Ball Game,' at Loew's State |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=1949-03-10 |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9B06E2D61F38E23BBC4852DFB5668382659EDE |access-date=2008-05-19}}</ref>

On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 95% based on 19 reviews, with an average rating of 8/10.<ref>{{cite web |title=Take Me Out to the Ball Game |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/take_me_out_to_the_ball_game |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=2024-06-12}}</ref>

===Accolades===
Harry Tugend and [[George Wells (screenwriter)|George Wells]] were nominated for the 1950 [[Writers Guild of America Award]] in the category of Best Written American Musical. They lost to [[Betty Comden]] and [[Adolph Green]] for ''[[On the Town (film)|On the Town]]'', another [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM]] musical comedy also produced by [[Arthur Freed]] and also starring [[Gene Kelly]], [[Frank Sinatra]], [[Betty Garrett]], and [[Jules Munshin]], which was released four months after the premiere of ''Take Me Out to the Ball Game''.
