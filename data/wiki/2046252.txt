'''''Le Samouraï''''' ({{IPA|fr|lə sa.mu.ʁa.i}}; {{literal translation|The Samurai}}) is a 1967  [[neo-noir]] [[crime thriller film]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/v20089|title=Le Samourai (1967) - Jean-Pierre Melville|website=[[AllMovie]]}}</ref> written and directed by [[Jean-Pierre Melville]] and starring [[Alain Delon]], [[François Périer]], [[Nathalie Delon]], and [[Cathy Rosier]]. A Franco-Italian production, it depicts the intersecting paths of a professional [[hitman]] (Delon) trying to find out who hired him for a job and then tried to have him killed, and the Parisian ''[[Commissaire de police|commissaire]]'' (Périer) trying to catch him.

The film was released on 25 October 1967, and it sold over 1.9 million tickets in France.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=http://www.boxofficestory.com/le-samourai-alain-delon-box-office-1967-a125233144|title=LE SAMOURAI - ALAIN DELON BOX OFFICE 1967|website=BOX OFFICE STORY|access-date=2019-11-08}}</ref> It received positive reviews, with particular praise given to Melville's screenwriting and atmospheric direction, and Delon's performance. An English-[[Dubbing|dubbed]] version of the film was released in the U.S. in 1972 as '''''The Godson'''''.

The film has come to be regarded as widely influential, particularly among the crime thriller genre,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Debruge |first=Peter |date=9 April 2024 |title=Critic's Notebook: Sharper Than Ever, French Crime Classic 'Le Samouraï' Might Be the Coolest Film Ever Made |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/columns/le-samourai-the-coolest-film-ever-made-jean-pierre-melville-alain-delon-4k-restoration-1235964766/ |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Yegulalp |first=Serdar |title=Le samouraï (Infinimata Press) |url=https://www.infinimata.com/2003/10/le-samoura.html |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=www.infinimata.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Dean |first=Remy |date=4 December 2021 |title=LE SAMOURAI (1967) • Frame Rated |url=https://www.framerated.co.uk/le-samourai-1967/ |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=Frame Rated |language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Le Samouraï (1967) - Classic Cinemas |url=https://www.classiccinemas.com.au/movies/le-samoura-1967 |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=www.classiccinemas.com.au |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=The Untold Story Of 'Le Samouraï' |url=https://www.thebeverlytheater.com/news/the-untold-story-of-le-samourai |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=www.thebeverlytheater.com}}</ref><ref name="auto4">{{Cite web |title=Disciples of the Blade: 6 Films Influenced By Le Samouraï – Establishing Shot |url=https://blogs.iu.edu/establishingshot/2017/09/12/disciples-of-the-blade-6-films-influenced-by-le-samourai/ |access-date=21 October 2024 |website=blogs.iu.edu}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Dee |first=Jake |date=2024-08-22 |title=Why Was Alain Delon So Famous? ==Plot==
Impassive hitman Jef Costello lives in a spartan single-room Paris apartment and keeps a small bird in a cage as a pet. His methodical [[modus operandi]] involves creating airtight alibis, including ones provided by his lover, Jane. After carrying out a contract killing on Martey, a nightclub owner, Jef is clearly seen leaving the scene by the club's piano player, Valérie, and glimpsed by several other witnesses. The police bring Jef and other suspects in for an identity parade but the witnesses, including Valérie, fail to positively identify him.

Jef is released, but the commissaire suspects him and has him followed. Jef loses the tail and goes to collect his fee for the hit. Instead of paying him, the man he meets tries to kill him, shooting him in the arm. Jef realises that his unknown employers now view him as a liability because he is suspected in Martey's murder. After treating his wound, Jef returns to the nightclub. While he is out, two police officers [[Covert listening device|bug]] his room, agitating the bird in its cage.

After the nightclub closes, Jef has Valérie take him to her home, reasoning that she did not identify him as the killer because his employer had told her not to. He asks her who hired him, and she tells him to call her in two hours. The police search Jane's apartment and offer to leave her alone if she recants her alibi for Jef. She flatly rejects the offer.

Back at his apartment, Jef notices some loose feathers scattered around his bird's cage and suspects it was agitated by an intrusion. He finds the police bug and deactivates it and then goes to a public phone to call Valérie but she does not answer. When he returns home, he is ambushed by the man who shot him. Holding Jef at gunpoint, the man offers a fresh start. He pays Jef for the hit on Martey, as well as for an upcoming one. Jef overpowers the man and forces him to disclose the identity of his boss: Olivier Rey.

Dozens of undercover police attempt to tail Jef in the Paris Métro but he evades them. He visits Jane and assures her everything will work out, then drives to Rey's house, which is the same home Valérie took him to earlier in the film, though she is not there. Jef kills Rey and returns to the nightclub, this time making no attempt to avoid being seen. He checks his hat, but leaves his hat-check ticket on the counter, and puts on white gloves, which he wears when carrying out his hits, in full view of everyone. He approaches the stage where Valérie is performing. She quietly advises him to leave but he points his gun at her. Strangely unafraid, she asks him why he is doing this and he responds he was paid to. Suddenly, Jef is shot four times by policemen who had been waiting for him. When the Commissaire inspects Jef's gun, he discovers it was unloaded.

==Production==
Melville wrote the film for Delon.<ref>A Samurai in Paris Nogueira, Rui; TRUCHAUD, FRANCOIS. Sight and Sound; London Vol. 37, Iss. 3,  (Summer 1968): 118.</ref> It was the first film for Delon's wife, Nathalie. He filed for divorce after the film wrapped, but they terminated the divorce proceedings a few days later, though they separated in June 1968 and their divorce became official in February 1969, with Nathalie being granted custody of their son, Anthony.<ref>''Orlando Evening Star''. Feb. 14, 1969</ref> [[François Périer]], who played the Commissaire, was a comedian cast against type.

Studios Jenner, Melville's private film studio, was destroyed by a fire on June 29, 1967, while ''Le Samouraï'' was in production. Melville, who termed the blaze "suspicious", finished the shoot at another studio.<ref>I"Fire Razes Melville's Studio Jenner in Paris."
''Variety''. July 12, 1967.</ref>

==Alternative ending==
In an interview with Rui Nogueira, Melville said he originally filmed Jef Costello meeting his death with a picture-perfect grin, but he modified the scene after discovering Delon had a smiling death scene in another of his films. Production stills of the smiling death exist.

==Release and reception==
===Box office===
''Le Samouraï'' was released in France on 25 October 1967. It sold over 1.9 million tickets in France, and over 797,011 tickets in Spain.<ref name=":0" /> First released in theaters in the United States in 1972, it grossed $39,481 from a 1997 re-release.<ref name=Numbers/>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has a 92% approval rating based on 51 reviews, and an average score of 8.7/10; the site's "critics consensus" reads: "''Le Samouraï'' makes the most of its spare aesthetic, using stylish – and influential – direction, solid performances, and thick atmosphere to weave an absorbing story."<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/le_samourai/ |title= Le Samouraï |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |date= 12 July 1972 |access-date=18 March 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 90 out of 100, based on 21 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Le Samouraï'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/ |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date= |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Le Samouraï |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/le-samourai/ |access-date=18 September 2025 |website=Metacritic}}</ref>

A 1967 review in ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called the film "a curious hybrid" that "appears a bit too solemn to inject all the suspense, action and characterization [Melville] seeks", and said it "almost seems to be an American film dubbed into French" and "could be cut a bit".<ref>''Variety'', Nov 8, 1967. p. 24.</ref>

[[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' called the original film "immaculate", but criticized the dubbing of the 1972 version released in the U.S. as ''The Godson'' as "disorienting" and "dreadful".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Canby |first1=Vincent |author1-link=Vincent Canby |title='The Godson' (No Relation to 'Godfather') Opens |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1972/07/13/archives/the-godson-no-relation-to-godfather-opens.html |website=[[The New York Times]] |date=13 July 1972}}</ref>

In a 1997 review of the film that later appeared in his first ''[[The Great Movies]]'' collection of essays, [[Roger Ebert]] gave the film four out of four stars, writing: "Like a painter or a musician, a filmmaker can suggest complete mastery with just a few strokes. [[Jean-Pierre Melville]] involves us in the spell of ''Le Samourai'' (1967) before a word is spoken. He does it with light: a cold light, like dawn on an ugly day. And color: grays and blues. And actions that speak in place of words."<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-le-samourai-1967|title=Le Samourai movie review & film summary (1967) |last=Ebert|first=Roger |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]  |access-date=2019-11-08}}</ref>

In 2010, the film was ranked No. 39 on ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Films of World Cinema".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.empireonline.com/features/100-greatest-world-cinema-films/default.asp?film=39 |title=The 100 Best Films Of World Cinema – 39. Le Samourai |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.--> |year=2010 |website=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |publisher=[[Bauer Media Group]] |access-date=18 June 2013 |archive-date=3 October 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131003054540/http://www.empireonline.com/features/100-greatest-world-cinema-films/default.asp?film=39 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Writing in ''[[Le Figaro]]'' of the Delons' performances after Nathalie's death in 2021, Bertrand Guyard noted that the husband and wife are both nearly silent in the film, but "their gazes, fraught with meaning, are enough to make the camera quiver", and the director was able to create from their portrayals "a mythical couple of the seventh art."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-01-21 |title=Nathalie Delon, éternelle complice du Samouraï |url=https://www.lefigaro.fr/cinema/nathalie-delon-eternelle-complice-du-samourai-20210121 |access-date=2022-03-21 |website=LEFIGARO |language=fr}}</ref>

==Influence and legacy==
Stephen Teo calls ''Le Samouraï'' "possibly the most influential French crime thriller ever made, a mixture of a police procedural (''le film policier'') and a suspenseful action thriller concentrated on a professional hit man, Jef (spelled with one "f") Costello, played by Alain Delon, giving the definitive performance of his career".<ref name="Teo2019">{{cite book |last1=Teo |first1=Stephen |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5AaZDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA103 |title=Eastern Approaches to Western Film: Asian Reception and Aesthetics in Cinema |publisher=Bloomsbury Publishing |year=2019 |isbn=978-1-350-11331-2 |page=103}}</ref>

The film has influenced numerous other works and directors:

* The German film ''[[Love Is Colder Than Death (film)|Liebe ist kälter als der Tod]]'' pays tribute to French cinema masters such as [[Claude Chabrol]] and [[Éric Rohmer]]. Furthermore, the directing style is inspired by Melville's ''Le Samouraï'', while the artwork on the poster resembles the silhouette of Alain Delon in ''Le Samouraï''.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Chiesi |first1=Roberto |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=bmtAqVGyzS4C |title=Alain Delon |date=2003 |publisher=Gremese Editore |isbn=978-88-7301-498-0 |page=39 |language=fr |access-date=2025-03-24}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> The character Bruno ([[Ulli Lommel]]) is also inspired by the character portrayed by Delon.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0064588/movieconnections/#references |title=Love Is Colder Than Death (1969) - Connections - IMDb |language=en-US |access-date=2025-03-24 |via=www.imdb.com}}</ref>
* The character of Jef Costello, portrayed by Delon in ''Le Samouraï'' (1967), had a major impact on [[Fernando Di Leo]] and his ''Milieu Trilogy'' (''[[Caliber 9]], [[The Italian Connection]], [[Il Boss]])''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Wilkins |first=Budd |date=2023-06-14 |title='Caliber 9' Blu-ray Review: Raro Video |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/caliber-9-blu-ray-review-fernando-di-leo/ |access-date=2024-07-08 |website=Slant Magazine |language=en-US}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Dellamorte |first=Andre |date=2011-03-21 |title=FERNANDO DI LEO: THE ITALIAN CRIME COLLECTION DVD Review |url=https://collider.com/fernando-di-leo-italian-crime-collection-dvd-review/ |access-date=2024-07-08 |website=Collider |language=en}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=SensCritique |title=Le Boss (1973) - SB17 |url=https://www.senscritique.com/film/le_boss/critique/227456722 |access-date=2024-07-08 |website=SensCritique |language=fr}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref> 
* [[Luc Besson]] drew inspiration from Jef Costello (played by Delon in ''Le Samouraï'') to create Léon, the titular character embodied by Jean Reno in ''[[Léon: The Professional]]''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Dean |first=Remy |date=2021-12-04 |title=LE SAMOURAI (1967). Frame Rated |url=https://www.framerated.co.uk/le-samourai-1967/ |access-date=2024-10-24 |website=Frame Rated |language=en-GB}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator --></ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Heskins |first=Andrew |date=2011-04-04 |title=Un amour asiatique de film {{!}} easternkicks.com |url=https://www.easternkicks.com/features/un-amour-de-asiatique-film/ |access-date=2024-07-25 |website=[[EasternKicks]] |language=en-GB}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref>
* [[Richard Ayoade]] cites ''The Samurai'' as a major inspiration for his films. In ''[[Submarine (2010 film)|Submarine]],'' [[Submarine (2010 film)|Oliver Tate]]'s character wears the same costume as Delon in ''The Samurai'' and displays a poster of the film in his room, out of admiration for the character he plays.<ref>{{cite AV media |url=https://www.allocine.fr/film/fichefilm-196629/secrets-tournage/ |title=Les secrets de tournage du film The Double |author=AlloCine |language=fr |access-date=2025-02-20}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Schmidlin |first=Charlie |date=2014-05-08 |title=Director Richard Ayoade Shares The 5 Films That Influenced 'The Double' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/director-richard-ayoade-shares-the-5-films-that-influenced-the-double-86262/ |access-date=2025-02-20 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator --></ref> Some scenes in the film are directly inspired by the French classic.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Ayoade |first1=Richard |date=2011-03-12 |title=Richard Ayoade reveals the inspirations behind his directorial debut Submarine |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2011/mar/12/richard-ayoade-submarine-antiheroes |journal=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077 |access-date=2025-02-20}}</ref>
* [[Fabio Grassadonia and Antonio Piazza]], in ''[[Salvo (film)|Salvo]]'', sought to recreate the aura of French film noir.<ref>{{Cite web |last=ondacinema.it |title=Fabio Grassadonia, Antonio Piazza - Speciale Salvo {{!}} Speciali |url=https://www.ondacinema.it/speciali/scheda/fabio_grassadonia_antonio_piazza_speciale_salvo.html |access-date=2025-03-07 |website=www.ondacinema.it}}</ref> Their choice of [[Saleh Bakri]] to play Salvo, a Sicilian hitman, stemmed from this vision: “We wanted a strong physical presence that would dominate the screen with charisma, thinking of Jean-Pierre Melville, French film noir, and actors like Alain Delon.” <ref>{{cite web |last=Cinebel |title=Vu à Cannes : "Salvo" de Fabio Grassadonia et Antonio Piazza |url=https://cinebel.dhnet.be/fr/actualite/6610/Vu%20%C3%A0%20Cannes%20:%20%22Salvo%22%20de%20%20Fabio%20Grassadonia%20et%20Antonio%20Piazza |access-date=2024-07-31 |website=Cinebel |language=fr}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2013-05-18 |title=Fabio Grassadonia and Antonio Piazza. Directors |url=https://cineuropa.org/en/interview/238608/ |access-date=2024-07-31 |website=[[Cineuropa]] |language=en}}<!-- auto-translated from French by Module:CS1 translator -->.</ref>
