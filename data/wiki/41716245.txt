'''''The Babadook''''' is a 2014 Australian [[psychological horror film]] written and directed by [[Jennifer Kent]] in her [[list of directorial debuts|feature directorial debut]], based on her 2005 short film ''Monster''. Starring [[Essie Davis]], Noah Wiseman, [[Daniel Henshall]], [[Hayley McElhinney]], Barbara West, and [[Ben Winspear]], the film follows a widowed single mother who, with her son, must confront a mysterious humanoid monster in their home.

Kent began writing the screenplay in 2009, intending to explore parenting, grief, and the fear of madness. Financing was secured through [[Film and television financing in Australia|Australian government grants]] and partly through [[crowdfunding]]. Filming took place in [[Adelaide]], where Kent drew from her experiences as a production assistant on [[Lars von Trier]]'s ''[[Dogville]]'' (2003). During filming, the production team worked to ensure six-year-old Wiseman was [[Child protection|protected]] from the film's disturbing subject matter.

''The Babadook'' premiered at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] on 17 January 2014 and was given a limited release in Australian cinemas on 22 May 2014, initially failing to become a commercial success in its native country. However, it generated wider attention internationally, grossing $10 million against its $2 million budget. The film was praised for the cast's performances, creature design, premise, and themes. At the [[4th AACTA Awards]], it won for [[AACTA Award for Best Film|Best Film]], and Kent won for [[AACTA Award for Best Direction|Best Direction]] and [[AACTA Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]], respectively. In the years since its release, ''The Babadook'' has become a [[Cult following|cult classic]].

==Plot==
Amelia Vanek is a troubled, exhausted widow and single mother of six-year-old Sam living in [[Adelaide]]. Her husband, Oskar, was killed in a car accident while driving her to the hospital during labour. Sam begins displaying erratic behaviour, causing problems at school and in their social life, and becomes preoccupied with an imaginary monster, which he has built weapons to fight. One night, he asks his mother to read a [[Pop-up book|pop-up fairy tale book]], titled ''Mister Babadook''. It describes a monster with a [[top hat]] wearing a suit, which grows more sinister and menacing with each page turned. Amelia is disturbed by the book and its mysterious appearance, while Sam grows frightened and paranoid, convinced that the Babadook is real.

Strange events begin to occur, such as Amelia finding glass shards in her food. She attributes the events to Sam, but he blames the Babadook. Amelia subsequently rips up the book and disposes of it. At her birthday party, Sam's cousin, Ruby, bullies him, and he pushes her out of her tree house, inadvertently breaking her nose. Amelia's sister and Ruby's mother, Claire, is furious at her nephew for what he did to her. Claire admonishes her sister, stating that even Amelia resents her son for his behavior. On the ride home, Sam has a vision of the Babadook and suffers a violent [[seizure]], after which Amelia brings him to therapy and receives [[sedatives]] for him.

Amelia finds the ''Mister Babadook'' book reassembled at their front door. New words in it taunt her; the book now contains pop-ups of her killing their dog Bugsy, Sam, then herself. Terrified, she burns the book and runs to the police station after a disturbing phone call. However, she has no proof, and leaves when she sees what looks like the Babadook's suit hung up behind the front desk.

That night, the Babadook attacks her. After the attack, Amelia becomes more isolated and impatient, angrily shouting at Samuel and having more visions of the Babadook. She also exhibits violent behaviour, including cutting the [[phone line]] with a knife, and has disturbing [[hallucination]]s in which she sees herself murdering Sam.

An apparition of Oskar appears, offering to return if she "brings the boy" to him. Realising he is a creation of the Babadook, she flees, but the Babadook finally [[Spirit possession|possesses]] her. Under its influence, she kills Bugsy and attempts to kill Sam. Eventually luring her into the basement, Sam knocks her out with his contraptions. Amelia awakens and tries to strangle Sam. When he lovingly caresses her face, she regurgitates a black substance, which seemingly expels the Babadook. However, Sam reminds her that "you can't get rid of the Babadook", and an unseen force drags him into her bedroom. She saves him but is forced to see a vision of her husband's death. Furious, she confronts the Babadook, making it retreat into the basement, locking the door in the process.

Amelia and Sam manage to recover. She is attentive and caring toward him, encouraging him to build his weapons and being impressed by his magic tricks. In the garden, they gather worms in a bowl, which Amelia takes to the basement. The Babadook tries to attack her, but she soothes it and it retreats to the corner, taking the bowl of worms with it. She returns to the yard to celebrate Sam's birthday.

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Jennifer Kent, Paris Cinéma 2014 (cropped).jpg|thumb|Writer and director [[Jennifer Kent]]]]
[[Jennifer Kent]] attended the [[National Institute of Dramatic Art]] (NIDA), where she studied acting alongside [[Essie Davis]] and graduated in 1991.<ref name="Lui"/> She then worked primarily as an actor in the film industry for over two decades. She eventually lost her passion for acting by the end of the 1990s and sent a written proposal to Danish filmmaker [[Lars von Trier]], asking if she could assist on the film set of his 2003 drama film ''[[Dogville]] ''to learn from him. He accepted her proposal, and she considers the experience her "film school", citing the importance of stubbornness as the key lesson she learned.<ref name="Den"/><ref name="Paul">{{cite news|last=MacInnes|first=Paul|title=The Babadook: 'I wanted to talk about the need to face darkness in ourselves'|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/oct/18/the-babadook-jennifer-kent|access-date=21 November 2014|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=18 October 2014}}</ref>

Prior to ''The'' ''Babadook'', Kent completed a short film ''Monster'' and an episode of the television series ''[[Two Twisted]]''. She explained in May 2014 that the origins of ''The Babadook'' could be found in ''Monster'', which she calls "baby ''Babadook''".<ref name="San">{{cite web|last=George|first=Sandy |title=How Jennifer Kent made The Babadook|url=http://www.sbs.com.au/movies/article/2014/05/21/how-jennifer-kent-made-babadook|publisher=[[Special Broadcasting Service|SBS]]|access-date=21 November 2014|date=21 May 2014}}</ref>

She began writing the screenplay in around 2009. She said she sought to tell a story about facing up to the darkness within ourselves, the "fear of going mad", and an exploration of [[parenting]] from a "real perspective". Regarding parenting, she further explained in October 2014: "Now, I'm not saying we all want to go and kill our kids, but a lot of women struggle. And it is a very taboo subject, to say that motherhood is anything but a perfect experience for women."<ref name="Den">{{cite web|last=Lamble|first=Ryan|title=Jennifer Kent interview: directing The Babadook|url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/jennifer-kent-interview-directing-the-babadook/|website=[[Den of Geek]]|access-date=21 November 2014|date=13 October 2014}}</ref> About the characters, she said: "It was really important for me that they were loving, and loveable people. I don't mean likeable—I mean that we really felt for them".<ref name="Den"/> The term ''babadook'' is an invention of Kent's. It is improvised from "{{Lang|sh|[[babaroga]]}}", the [[Serbo-Croatian]] word for the [[boogeyman]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Adams |first=Sam | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/boogeyman-nights-the-story-behind-this-years-horror-hit-the-babadook-49141/ | title=The Story Behind This Year's Horror Hit 'The Babadook' | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=27 November 2014 |url-access=subscription |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230327160722/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/boogeyman-nights-the-story-behind-this-years-horror-hit-the-babadook-49141/ |archive-date=27 March 2023 |url-status=live}}</ref> She completed five drafts of the script.<ref name="Alt">{{cite magazine |last=Alter |first=Ethan |date=14 November 2014 |title=Parental descent: Jennifer Kent's 'The Babadook' is a spooky tale of a mother in crisis |url=http://www.filmjournal.com/filmjournal/content_display/news-and-features/features/movies/e3icebb717e84f08bc21ecace8c6a04a7b0 |magazine=[[Film Journal International]] |access-date=21 November 2014 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141129081412/http://www.filmjournal.com/filmjournal/content_display/news-and-features/features/movies/e3icebb717e84f08bc21ecace8c6a04a7b0 |archive-date=29 November 2014}}</ref>

Kent drew from her experience on the set of ''Dogville'' for the assembling of her production team, as she observed that von Trier was surrounded by a well-known "family of people". Kent sought her own "family of collaborators to work with for the long term." Unable to find all of the suitable people within the Australian film industry, Kent hired Polish director of photography (DOP) Radek Ladczuk, for whom ''The Babadook'' was his first-ever English language film, and American illustrator Alexander Juhasz.<ref name="San"/> In terms of influences, Kent cited [[1960s in film|1960s]], [[1970s in film|'70s]] and [[1980s in film|'80s]] horror—including ''[[Eyes Without a Face (film)|Eyes Without a Face]]'' (1960), ''[[Carnival of Souls]]'' (1962), ''[[The Texas Chain Saw Massacre]]'' (1974), ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' (1978), ''[[The Thing (1982 film)|The Thing]]'' (1982) and ''[[The Shining (film)|The Shining]]'' (1980)—as well as ''[[Vampyr]]'' (1932), ''[[Nosferatu]]'' (1922) and ''[[Let the Right One In (film)|Let the Right One In]]'' (2008).<ref name="Emp"/>

Although the process was challenging and she was forced to reduce their total budget, producer Kristina Ceyton managed to secure funding of around [[Australian dollar|A$]]2.5 million from government bodies [[Screen Australia]] and the [[South Australian Film Corporation|SAFC]]; however, they still required an additional budget for the construction of the film sets. To attain the funds for the sets, Kent and Causeway Films producer Kristina Ceyton launched a [[Kickstarter]] crowdfunding campaign in June 2012, with a target of [[United States dollar|US$]]30,000. Their funding goal was reached on 27 September 2012 through pledges from 259 backers raising $30,071.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Kent|first1=Jennifer|last2=Ceyton|first2=Kristina|title=Realise the vision of The Babadook|url=https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/thebabadook/realise-the-vision-of-the-babadook|website=[[Kickstarter]]|access-date=21 November 2014|date=June 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=Kent|first1=Jennifer|last2=Ceyton|first2=Kristina|title=The Babadook – Profile|url=https://www.kickstarter.com/profile/thebabadook|website=[[Kickstarter]]|access-date=21 November 2014|date=June 2012}}</ref> Kent said that the crowdfunding closed a crucial gap in which to cover design and special effects expenses to build a "special visual world".<ref name="Erickson"/>

Casting the [[Child actor|child lead]] for the film involved casting director Nikki Barrett viewing around 500 audition tapes of young boys, before selecting smaller groups and individuals for in-person improvisation.<ref name="Erickson"/> Six-year old Noah Wiseman was selected as a standout, with Kent saying he had a certain innocence about him that older boys did not have, possibly as he is the son of a child psychologist.<ref name="Erickson"/><ref name="Gunter">{{cite web |last=Gunter |first=Gary |date=26 October 2018 |title=20 Wild Details Behind The Making Of The Babadook |url=https://screenrant.com/the-babadook-behind-scenes-making-of-hidden-trivia/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref>

===Filming===
Filming began in September 2012.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://if.com.au/the-babadook-starring-essie-davis-begins-filming-in-sa/|title=The Babadook, starring Essie Davis, begins filming in SA|website=If|date=September 3, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.australianageingagenda.com.au/noticeboard/movie-making-magic-in-adelaide/|title=Movie making magic in Adelaide|website=Australian Ageing Agenda|date=September 18, 2012}}</ref> It was primarily shot in [[Adelaide]], [[South Australia]], with most of the interior shots filmed on a sound stage in the city; as funding was from the [[Government of South Australia|South Australian state government]], this was a requirement that Kent needed to meet.<ref name="Lui"/> However, Kent explained to ''[[Den of Geek]]'' that she is not patriotic and did not want the film to be "particularly Australian".<ref name="Den"/>

{{quote box|width=35%|quote=I wanted to create a myth in a domestic setting. And even though it happened to be in some strange suburb in Australia somewhere, it could have been anywhere. I guess part of that is creating a world that wasn't particularly Australian ... I'm very happy, actually, that it doesn't feel particularly Australian.|source=Director Jennifer Kent on her desire to avoid the clichéd "[[Australian culture|Australian feel]]" of the film<ref name="Den"/>}}

Kent claimed in an interview that to contribute to the universality of the film's appearance, a Victorian terrace-style house was specifically built for the film, as there are very few houses designed in such a style in Adelaide.<ref name="Den"/> However exterior shots were of an existing terraced house in [[North Adelaide]]. A script reading was not done since Noah Wiseman was only six years old at the time, and Kent focused instead on bonding, playing games and lots of time spent with the actors so they could become more familiar with one another.<ref name="Seibold">{{cite web |last=Seibold |first=Witney |date=22 March 2022 |title=The Age Of The Babadook's Star Presented A Unique Problem |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/807216/the-age-of-the-babadooks-star-presented-a-unique-problem/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |website=[[/Film]]}}</ref> Pre-production occurred in Adelaide and lasted three weeks and, during this time, Kent conveyed a "kiddie" version of the narrative to Wiseman, in which young Samuel is the hero.<ref name="Alt"/><ref name="Seibold"/> Kent took Wiseman to [[Adelaide Zoo]] to explain the story, and said Wiseman was aware it was a scary film and that he "knew how important his role was".<ref name="Erickson">{{cite web |last=Erickson |first=Steve |date=25 November 2014 |title=Jennifer Kent on What it Took to Make the Psych-Horror ''Babadook'' |url=https://www.studiodaily.com/2014/11/jennifer-kent-took-make-psych-horror-film-babadook/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |website=Studio Daily |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150408003516/http://www.studiodaily.com/2014/11/jennifer-kent-took-make-psych-horror-film-babadook/ |archive-date=8 April 2015}}</ref>

Kent originally wanted to film solely in [[Black and white|black-and-white]], as she wanted to create a "heightened feel" that is still believable. She was also influenced by [[History of horror films|pre-1950s]] [[B movie|B-grade horror films]], as they were "very theatrical", in addition to being "visually beautiful and terrifying". Kent later lost interest in the black-and-white idea and worked closely with production designer Alex Holmes and Radek to create a "very cool", "very claustrophobic" interior environment with "meticulously designed" sets.<ref name="Den"/><ref name="San"/> The film's final colour scheme was achieved without the use of gels on the camera lenses or any alterations during the post-filming stage.<ref name="Paul"/> Kent cited [[David Lynch]] and [[Roman Polanski]] as key influences during the filming stage.<ref name="Alt"/>

Kent described the filming process as "stressful" because of Wiseman's age. She explained, "So I really had to be focused. We needed double the time we had." Wiseman's mother was on set and a "very protective, loving environment" was created.<ref name="Den"/> Kent explained after the release of the film that Wiseman was [[Child protection|protected]] throughout the entire project: "During the reverse shots where Amelia was abusing Sam verbally, we had Essie [Davis] yell at an adult stand-in on his knees. I didn't want to destroy a childhood to make this film—that wouldn't be fair."<ref name="Alt"/> Kent's friendship with Davis was a boon during filming and Kent praised her former classmate in the media: "To her credit, she's [Davis] very receptive, likes to be directed and is a joy to work with."<ref name="San"/>
[[File:Lon Chaney London After Midnight Vampire Bat Cape.jpg|thumb|The monster design for the Babadook was inspired by the Man in the Beaver Hat in ''[[London After Midnight (film)|London After Midnight]]'' (1927).]]
In terms of the Babadook monster and the scary effects of the film, Kent was adamant from the outset of production that a low-fi and handmade approach would be used. She cites the influence of [[Georges Méliès]], [[Jean Epstein]]'s ''[[The Fall of the House of Usher (1928 French film)|The Fall of the House of Usher]]'' (1928) and ''[[Häxan]]'' (1922).<ref name="Gunter"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Ulaby |first=Neda |title=Beware The 'Babadook,' The Monster Of Your Own Making |publisher=[[NPR]] |url=https://www.npr.org/2014/11/27/366622062/beware-the-babadook-the-monster-of-your-own-making |date=27 November 2014 |access-date=24 September 2022}}</ref><ref name="Gary">{{cite web|last=Collinson|first=Gary|title=Interview with Jennifer Kent, director of The Babadook|url=http://www.flickeringmyth.com/2014/11/interview-jennifer-kent-director-babadook.html|website=Flickering Myth|access-date=21 November 2014|date=13 November 2014}}</ref> Kent used [[Stop motion|stop-motion]] effects for the monster and a large amount of smoothing was completed in post-production. Kent explained to  ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'': "There's been some criticism of the lo-fi approach of the effects, and that makes me laugh because it was always intentional. I wanted the film to be all in camera."<ref name="Emp">{{cite magazine|last=O'Hara|first=Helen|title=The Scariest Film Of The Year? Jennifer Kent On The Babadook|url=https://empireonline.com/interviews/interview.asp?IID=1950|magazine=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|access-date=21 November 2014|date=2014|archive-date=1 November 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141101192828/http://www.empireonline.com/interviews/interview.asp?IID=1950|url-status=dead}}</ref> She has also said that the Man in the Beaver Hat from the lost 1927 film ''[[London After Midnight (film)|London After Midnight]]'' was an inspiration for the design of the Babadook.<ref>{{cite news|last=Hanke|first=Ken|url=https://mountainx.com/movies/interview-with-the-babadooks-writer-director-jennifer-kent/|title=Interview with The Babadook's Writer-Director Jennifer Kent|newspaper=[[Mountain Xpress]]|date=9 December 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190710193421/https://mountainx.com/movies/interview-with-the-babadooks-writer-director-jennifer-kent/|archive-date=10 July 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Release==
The film's global premiere was in January 2014 at the [[2014 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite magazine | url=http://www.filmink.com.au/news/two-aussie-features-selected-for-sundance/ | magazine=[[FilmInk]] | first=Eden | last=Caceda | title=Two Aussie Features Selected For Sundance | date=9 December 2013 | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150627074856/http://www.filmink.com.au/news/two-aussie-features-selected-for-sundance/ | archive-date=27 June 2015}}</ref> The film then received a [[limited theatrical release]] in Australia in May 2014,<ref name="Alt"/> following a screening in April 2014 at the [[Stanley Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Gupta|first=Shipra |title=Stanley Film Festival Announces Full Lineup |url=https://indiewire.com/article/stanley-film-festival-announces-full-lineup-including-special-screening-of-open-windows-and-jemaine-clements-what-we-do-in-the-shadows-as-closing-night-film |website=[[IndieWire]] |access-date=21 November 2014|date=3 April 2014}}</ref>

In Singapore, the film was released on 25 September 2014.<ref name="Lui">{{cite news|last=Lui|first=John|title=Director Jennifer Kent's debut feature The Babadook is a horror movie without gore or cheap screams|url=http://www.straitstimes.com/lifestyle/movies/story/director-jennifer-kents-debut-feature-the-babadook-horror-movie-without-gore-|access-date=21 November 2014|newspaper=[[The Straits Times]]|date=24 September 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141009055345/http://www.straitstimes.com/lifestyle/movies/story/director-jennifer-kents-debut-feature-the-babadook-horror-movie-without-gore-|archive-date=9 October 2014}}</ref> The film opened in the United Kingdom for general release on 17 October 2014, and in the United States on 28 November 2014.<ref name="Alt"/> In 2020, amid [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on cinema|cinema closures due to the COVID-19 pandemic]], ''The Babadook'' was one of the films made available for free for screenings by independent cinemas by [[IFC Films]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Sneider |first=Jeff |date=21 April 2020 |title=The Babadook Among Films Offered to Indie Theaters Once They Reopen |url=https://collider.com/the-babadook-ifc-films-library-indie-theaters/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]}}</ref>

A 10th anniversary screening of the film was announced in December 2023 to take place at the [[2024 Sundance Film Festival]] on January 25, 2024 to commemorate the festival's 40th anniversary. On the announcement, Jennifer Kent remarked "I definitely have some distance on ''Babadook'' now, after ten years. The film feels like an old friend, one that changed my life in many ways ... Sundance was such a huge part of that change. I look forward to 'coming home' to the place where it all began, and to the festival that has given me and 'Mister B' so much."<ref>{{cite web |last=Bergeson |first=Samantha | url=https://www.indiewire.com/news/breaking-news/sundance-2024-shorts-lineup-anniversary-programming-1234934592/ | title=Sundance Unveils Shorts and 40th Anniversary Programming |website=[[IndieWire]] | date=12 December 2023}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Babadook'' grossed $10.7 million worldwide against an estimated production budget of $2 million.<ref name="Numbers"/><ref name="BOM"/> The film opened in Australia on 22 May 2014 in 13 cinemas on a [[limited release]], eventually grossing a total of $258,000.<ref name="Hardie">{{cite news|last=Hardie|first=Giles|url=https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/entertainment/2014/12/03/babadook|title=Why was 'The Babadook' kept from Australians|newspaper=[[The New Daily]]|date=3 December 2014|access-date=12 May 2015}}</ref> The film fared much better internationally than it did in its native country. In North America, ''The Babadook'' opened on a limited release basis in three theaters and grossed US$30,007 on its opening weekend, with an average of $10,002 per theater.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl340100609/weekend/|title=The Babadook – Domestic Weekend|website=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=15 October 2025}}</ref> It generated $633,000 in the United Kingdom in its opening weekend (surpassing its entire Australian run), and made over $1.09 million in France and $335,000 in Thailand.<ref name="Tan">{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/australia-culture-blog/2014/oct/29/the-babadooks-monster-uk-box-office-success-highlights-problems-at-home|title=The Babadook's monster UK box office success highlights problems at home|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|first=Monica|last=Tan|date=29 October 2014|access-date=14 March 2016}}</ref> In France, it opened at number 11 in the local box office, which producer Kristian Molière credited to [[Wild Bunch (company)|Wild Bunch]]'s promoting, and contrasted this with Australian promoters that declined to book the film slots in [[Multiplex (movie theater)|multiplexes]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2014-10-11/sa-horror-film-the-babadook-may-make-a-profit-in-france-uk-us/5806648|title=SA film to make a profit thanks to overseas success|date=11 October 2014|first=Matthew|last=Smith|publisher=[[ABC News (Australia)]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=7 August 2014 |title=French audiences, exhibitors embrace The Babadook|first=Don|last=Groves |url=https://if.com.au/french-audiences-exhibitors-embrace-the-babadook/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |magazine=[[IF Magazine]]}}</ref> Its success overseas re-generated interest in Australia ahead of DVD releases and television screenings.<ref name="Hardie"/><ref name="Tan"/>

===Critical response===
[[File:Essie Davis-67964.jpg|thumb|[[Essie Davis]], who plays Amelia]]<!---do not add uncited content to photo captions--->
On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 98% based on 248 reviews, with an average rating of 8.2/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "''The Babadook'' relies on real horror rather than cheap jump scares – and boasts a heartfelt, genuinely moving story to boot."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/the_babadook/ |title=The Babadook |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=4 September 2021}}</ref> It was ranked the best reviewed [[horror film]] and third best-reviewed film of 2014 on the site.<ref>{{cite web |title=Best Horror Movie 2014 |url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/best-movies-2014-horror/ |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=24 September 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Top 100 Movies of 2014 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |url=https://rtv2-production-2-6.rottentomatoes.com/top/bestofrt/?year=2014 |access-date=2 August 2022 |archive-date=26 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220726004024/https://rtv2-production-2-6.rottentomatoes.com/top/bestofrt/?year=2014 |url-status=dead}}</ref> {{as of|October 2025}}, Rotten Tomatoes ranks ''The Babadook'' the 5th best horror film of all time.<ref>{{cite web | last=Vo | first=Alex | title=200 Best Horror Movies of All Time | website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] | date=15 October 2025 | url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/best-horror-movies-of-all-time/ | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251015064126/https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/best-horror-movies-of-all-time/ | archive-date=15 October 2025 | url-status=live | access-date=15 October 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], which assigns a [[weighted average]] score out of 100 to reviews from mainstream critics, the film received an average score of 86 based on 34 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |title=The Babadook |url=https://metacritic.com/movie/the-babadook |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=14 December 2014}}</ref>

[[Glenn Kenny]], writing for ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'', called the film "the finest and most genuinely provocative horror movie to emerge in this still very-new century."<ref>{{cite web |last=Kenny |first=Glenn |date=28 November 2014 |title=The Babadook Movie Review [Stanley Film Festival] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-babadook-2014 |website=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> Dan Schindel from ''Movie Mezzanine'' said that "''The Babadook'' is the best genre creature creation since the big black wolf-dog aliens from ''[[Attack the Block]]''."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://moviemezzanine.com/sundance-review-the-babadook/ | title =Sundance Review: ''The Babadook'' Scares and Surprises| last=Schindel | first=Dan | date= 22 January 2014 | website=Movie Mezzanine | access-date=8 March 2014}}</ref> In ''[[The Guardian]]'', [[Peter Bradshaw]] described the film as a "[[Freudian]] thriller", giving it 4 out of 5 stars and praised the performances, themes and Kent's direction. Bradshaw said that "Kent exerts a masterly control over this tense situation and the sound design is terrifically good: creating a haunted, insidiously whispery intimacy that never relies on sudden volume hikes for the scares."<ref>{{cite news |last=Bradshaw |first=Peter |author-link=Peter Bradshaw |date=23 October 2014 |title=The Babadook review – a superbly acted, chilling Freudian thriller |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/oct/23/the-babadook-review-chilling-freudian-thriller |access-date=24 September 2022 |newspaper=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref> In ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', Scott Foundas commended the production design and direction, saying that the film "manages to deliver real, seat-grabbing jolts while also touching on more serious themes of loss, grief and other demons that can not be so easily vanquished".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Foundas |first=Scott |date=6 February 2014 |title=Film Review: 'The Babadook' |url=https://variety.com/2014/film/reviews/film-review-the-babadook-1201088892/ |access-date=24 September 2022 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>
 
On 30 November 2014, [[William Friedkin]], director of ''[[The Exorcist]]'' (1973) stated on his [[Twitter]] profile, "''[[Psycho (1960 film)|Psycho]]'', ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'', ''[[Les Diaboliques (film)|Diabolique]]'', and now ''THE BABADOOK''."<ref>{{cite tweet |last=Friedkin |first=William |author-link=William Friedkin |user=WilliamFriedkin |date=30 November 2014 |number=539244895236390912 |title=Psycho, Alien, Diabolique, and now THE BABADOOK. |access-date=1 December 2014 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20141201024554/https://twitter.com/WilliamFriedkin/status/539244895236390912 |archive-date=1 December 2014}}</ref> Friedkin also added, "I've never seen a more terrifying film. It will scare the hell out of you as it did me."<ref>{{cite tweet |last=Friedkin |first=William |author-link=William Friedkin |user=WilliamFriedkin |date=30 November 2014 |number=539241120236961792 |title=I've never seen a more terrifying film than THE BABADOOK. It will scare the hell out of you as it did me. |access-date=1 December 2014}}</ref> Prominent British film critic [[Mark Kermode]] named ''The Babadook'' his favourite film of 2014<ref>{{cite AV media|title=My Top Ten Films of 2014 – Part 2|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pCWtjAN5cVo | archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211117/pCWtjAN5cVo| archive-date=17 November 2021 | url-status=live|via=[[YouTube]]|access-date=30 December 2014|date=30 December 2014}}{{cbignore}}</ref> and in 2018 listed it his eighth favourite film of the decade.<ref>{{cite AV media|title=Kermode Uncut: My Top Ten Films Of The Last Ten Years – Part One|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G9DpyPQ8Qnw| archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211117/G9DpyPQ8Qnw| archive-date=17 November 2021 | url-status=live|access-date=12 June 2021|via=[[YouTube]]}}{{cbignore}}</ref> In 2022, Samuel Murrian declared it the "best horror movie so far this century" in ''[[Parade (magazine)|Parade]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Murrian |first=Samuel R. |date=12 June 2022 |title=Celebrate Pride Month With Our In-Depth Look at The Babadook, The Best Horror Movie So Far This Century |url=https://parade.com/897935/samuelmurrian/the-babadook-jennifer-kent-analysis/ |magazine=[[Parade (magazine)|Parade]] |access-date=14 June 2022}}</ref>

In subsequent years, ''The Babadook'' has been listed as a modern [[cult film]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Davis |first=Gwyneth |date=11 September 2018 |title=10 Must-See Modern Cult Classics |url=https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/973005-10-must-see-modern-cult-classics |access-date=3 July 2022 |website=ComingSoon.net}}</ref> In ''[[The Guardian]]'', Luke Buckmaster listed it as one of the best Australian films of the [[2010s in film|2010s]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Buckmaster |first=Luke |date=10 December 2019 |title=From Animal Kingdom to The Babadook: the best Australian films of the decade |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/dec/10/from-animal-kingdom-to-the-babadook-the-best-australian-films-of-the-decade |access-date=24 September 2022 |newspaper=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref> Film scholar Amanda Howell argues that part of the film's critical success can be attributed to many film critics having discussed the film within the context of [[Art horror|art-horror]] rather than purely as a horror film. Howell discussed the film as part of an international cycle of contemporary art-horror films alongside ''[[Pan's Labyrinth]]'' (2006), ''[[Let the Right One In]]'' (2008), and ''[[Antichrist (film)|Antichrist]]'' (2009) that negotiate and blur the boundaries between art and horror.<ref>{{cite book |last=Howell |first=Amanda |url=https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-48299-6 |title=Australian Screen in the 2000s |publisher=Palgrave Macmillan |year=2017 |editor-last=Ryan |editor-first=Mark David |location=Switzerland |pages=119–139 |chapter=Haunted Art House: The Babadook and International Art Cinema Horror. |doi=10.1007/978-3-319-48299-6 |isbn=978-3-319-48298-9 |editor-last2=Goldsmith |editor-first2=Ben}}</ref> In 2025, it ranked number four on ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]''{{'}}s list of the "25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century".<ref name="Horror">{{cite magazine |last=Rooney |first=David |date=July 23, 2025 |title=25 Best Horror Movies of the 21st Century, Ranked |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/25-best-horror-movies-of-the-21st-century-ranked/the-babadook-2014/ |magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date=15 October 2025}}</ref>

===LGBTQ community===
''The Babadook'' has gained popularity as an [[internet meme]], with the titular monster being ironically adopted as a [[queer icon]].<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/jun/11/the-babadook-how-horror-movie-monster-became-a-gay-icon|title=The Babadook: how the horror movie monster became a gay icon|first=Elle|last=Hunt|work=[[The Guardian]]|date=11 June 2017|accessdate=14 October 2024}}</ref> In October 2016, a [[Tumblr]] user joked that the Babadook is [[coming out|openly gay]]; in December 2016, another Tumblr user posted a viral screenshot showing the film classified by [[Netflix]] as an [[LGBTQ film]].<ref name="vanityfair"/><ref name=":0" /> Despite the absence of overt references to [[LGBTQ culture]] in the film, fans and journalists generated interpretations of [[queer]] subtext in the film (dubbed "Babadiscourse"<ref name=":0" />) that were often [[tongue-in-cheek]], but occasionally more serious, highlighting the character's dramatic persona, grotesque costume, and chaotic effect within a traditional family structure. In June 2017, ''The Babadook'' [[Twitter trends|trended on Twitter]] and was displayed as a symbol during that year's [[Pride Month]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Seavers|first=Kris|title=The Babadook is this year's Pride Month's unofficial mascot|url=https://www.dailydot.com/unclick/babadook-gay-memes/|access-date=8 June 2017|website=[[The Daily Dot]]|date=7 June 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Abad-Santos |first=Alex |date=9 June 2017 |title=How the Babadook became the LGBTQ icon we didn't know we needed |url=https://www.vox.com/explainers/2017/6/9/15757964/gay-babadook-lgbtq |website=[[Vox (website)|Vox]] |access-date=14 June 2022 |url-access=limited |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220321105219/https://www.vox.com/explainers/2017/6/9/15757964/gay-babadook-lgbtq |archive-date=21 March 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref> The social media response became so strong that theatres in Los Angeles took the opportunity to hold screenings of the film for charity.<ref>{{cite web |last=Rife |first=Katie |date=June 19, 2017 |title=The Babadook to raise bababucks at special LGBT charity screenings |url=https://www.avclub.com/the-babadook-to-raise-bababucks-at-special-lgbt-charity-1798263127 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |access-date=24 August 2020 |archive-date=9 August 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200809040825/https://news.avclub.com/the-babadook-to-raise-bababucks-at-special-lgbt-charity-1798263127 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Michael Bronski]] said to the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'': "In this moment, who better than the Babadook to represent not only queer desire, but queer antagonism, queer in-your-faceness, queer queerness?", and drew comparisons to historic [[LGBTQ themes in horror fiction|connections between queerness and horror fiction]] such as ''[[Frankenstein]]'' and ''[[Dracula]]''.<ref>{{cite news |last=Roy |first=Jessica |date=2017-06-09 |title=The Babadook as an LGBT icon makes sense. No, really |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-et-mn-babadook-gay-icon-lgbt-history-20170609-story.html |access-date=2022-11-01 |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref>

Kent said that she "loved" the [[Internet meme|meme]], saying that "I think it's crazy and [the meme] just kept him alive. I thought ah, you bastard. He doesn't want to die so he's finding ways to become relevant."<ref>{{cite web|first=Fred|last=Topel|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/interviews/3543658/sundance-2019-jennifer-kent-says-lgbtq-babadook-memes-kept-bastard-alive-exlcusive/|title=Director Jennifer Kent comments on those LGBT 'Babadook' memes [interview]|website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|date=30 January 2019}}</ref> In 2019, IFC Films, the film's US distributor, released a limited Pride edition of the film on Blu-ray.<ref name=":0" />

==Themes and symbolism==
''The Babadook'' is a [[psychological horror film]].<ref name =nfsa>{{cite web |title=The Babadook |website=[[National Film and Sound Archive of Australia]] |url=https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/99195-babadook |access-date=22 October 2025}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[The Daily Beast]]'', Tim Teeman contends that [[grief]] is the "real monster" in ''The Babadook'', and that the film is "about the aftermath of death; how its remnants destroy long after the dead body has been buried or burned". Teeman writes that he was "gripped" by the "metaphorical imperative" of Kent's film, with the Babadook monster representing "the shape of grief: all-enveloping, shape-shifting, black". Teeman states that the film's ending "underscored the thrum of grief and loss at the movie's heart", and concludes that it informs the audience that grief has its place and the best that humans can do is "marshal it".<ref>{{cite web|first=Tim|last=Teeman|title=Grief: The Real Monster in The Babadook|url=http://www.thedailybeast.com/articles/2014/12/19/grief-the-real-monster-in-the-babadook.html|website=[[The Daily Beast]]|access-date=6 February 2015|date=19 December 2014}}</ref>

''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]'' also proposed that the monster "seems to [[Symbolism (arts)|symbolize]] Amelia and Sam's shared grief/trauma over losing Oskar" and that Amelia's efforts to suppress this led to it becoming stronger.<ref name="collidergrief">{{cite web |date=6 January 2022 |title=How Grief and Trauma Can Manifest as 'The Babadook' |url=https://collider.com/the-babadook-ending-explained/ |access-date=14 June 2022 |website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]}}</ref> The writers suggest that "healing from serious traumas in real life does not happen overnight, but takes a lot of mental and emotional processing. ''The Babadook'' warns of the dangers of trying to ignore or 'stuff' our traumas below the surface: this is the most dangerous place to put them because that's where we lose control of them and they gain control over us."<ref name="collidergrief"/>
