'''''The Warriors''''' is a 1979 American [[action thriller film]] directed by [[Walter Hill (director)|Walter Hill]] from a screenplay by Hill and [[David Shaber]] and based on the [[The Warriors (Yurick novel)|1965 novel of the same name]] by [[Sol Yurick]], in-turn inspired by [[Xenophon]]'s [[Anabasis (Xenophon)|''Anabasis'']]. The film features an [[ensemble cast]] which includes [[Michael Beck]], [[James Remar]], [[Deborah Van Valkenburgh]], [[Marcelino Sánchez]], [[David Harris (American actor)|David Harris]], Tom McKitterick, Brian Tyler, [[Dorsey Wright]], [[Terry Michos]], [[David Patrick Kelly]], [[Roger Hill (actor)|Roger Hill]], Edward Sewer, and [[Lynne Thigpen]]. In the film, a street gang must escape from [[the Bronx]] back to their home turf on [[Coney Island]] after being framed for the murder of a respected gang leader.

Development on ''The Warriors'' initially began in 1969 after [[American International Pictures]] purchased the film rights to Yurick's novel, but production stalled until producer [[Lawrence Gordon (producer)|Lawrence Gordon]] obtained the rights and commissioned Shaber and Hill to work on the screenplay. Originally envisioned as a [[western film|western]], the novel was ultimately adapted as a stylized action thriller. Casting was extensive and production was troubled, with Hill clashing with some of the actors and going over budget. [[Principal photography]] took place in 1978 on location across [[New York City]], including at [[Astoria Studios]].

''The Warriors'' was theatrically released in the United States on February 9, 1979, by [[Paramount Pictures]]. It grossed $22.5 million worldwide and received negative reviews from critics for its lack of realism and dialogue. Initial screenings of the film were linked to vandalism and murders, prompting Paramount to halt advertising or release theaters from their obligation to screen the film. ''The Warriors'' has been reappraised by critics and is considered a [[cult film]]. It has since launched a franchise which spawned several spinoffs, including video games and a comic book series.

==Plot==
Cyrus, charismatic leader of the [[Gramercy Park|Gramercy]] Riffs, the most powerful gang in [[New York City]], requests that 100 of the city's gangs each send nine unarmed delegates to [[Van Cortlandt Park]] for a midnight summit. The Warriors, a gang from [[Coney Island]], send a delegation consisting of "warlord" (leader) Cleon; "war chief" (second-in-command) Swan; enforcer Ajax; scout Fox; graffiti artist Rembrandt; music-man Snow; bearer Vermin and soldiers Cowboy and Cochise. Cyrus proposes a citywide truce and alliance to the assembled crowd, allowing the gangs to control the city together since they collectively outnumber the police by three to one.

Most of the gang members applaud this idea, but Luther, the unbalanced and sadistic leader of the Rogues, shoots Cyrus dead as the [[NYPD]] arrive to raid the summit. In the chaos, Luther realizes that Fox witnessed his actions; after failing to shoot him, he falsely accuses the Warriors of responsibility. Cleon denies the assassination, however he is viciously attacked by the vengeful Riffs, killing him. Meanwhile, the other Warriors escape to [[Woodlawn Cemetery (Bronx, New York)|Woodlawn Cemetery]], unaware that they have been implicated in Cyrus's killing. Masai, Cyrus's second-in-command in the Riffs, puts out a "dead or alive" bounty on the Warriors through a radio [[DJ]]. To Ajax's disappointment, Swan takes charge of the group as they try to get home.

The Turnbull ACs spot the Warriors and try to run them down with a modified school bus, but they escape and board the [[MTA New York City Subway]]. On the ride to Coney Island, the train is stopped by a building fire alongside the tracks, stranding the Warriors in [[Tremont, Bronx|Tremont]]. Setting out on foot, they encounter the Orphans, who are insecure about their lowly status as they were excluded from Cyrus's meeting. After Mercy, the girlfriend of the Orphans' leader Sully, instigates a confrontation, Swan throws a [[Molotov cocktail]], and the Warriors run to the nearest subway station. Impressed and desperate to escape her depressing neighborhood, Mercy follows the Warriors.

When the group arrives at the [[96th Street and Broadway station]] in [[Manhattan]], they are pursued by cops and separated. Vermin, Cochise, and Rembrandt escape by boarding a subway car. Fox, struggling with a police officer, is thrown onto the tracks and killed by a passing train as Mercy flees the scene. The Baseball Furies chase Swan, Ajax, Snow, and Cowboy into [[Riverside Park (Manhattan)|Riverside Park]], but they are defeated in a brawl. After the fight, Ajax sees a lone woman sitting on a park bench and leaves the group despite Swan's objections. When Ajax becomes sexually aggressive, the woman reveals herself as an [[undercover cop]] and arrests him with the help of uniformed officers.

Upon arriving at [[14th Street–Union Square station|Union Square station]], Vermin, Cochise, and Rembrandt are seduced by an all-female gang called the Lizzies and invited into their hideout. They narrowly escape the Lizzies' subsequent attack, learning in the process that the gangland community and the police believe the Warriors murdered Cyrus. As a lone scout, Swan returns to the 96th Street station, where Mercy joins him (although he spurns her advances). After reaching the Union Square station by walking along the tracks, they reunite with the remaining Warriors and fight with an [[overalls]]-wearing [[roller skating]] gang, the Punks, which allows Mercy to prove herself in combat. Meanwhile, an unidentified gang member visits the Riffs and tells them that he saw Luther shoot Cyrus.

At dawn, the Warriors finally reach Coney Island, only to find Luther and the Rogues waiting for them. Swan asks Luther why he killed Cyrus, to which Luther replies, "No reason... I just like doing things like that!" Swan challenges Luther to single combat, but Luther pulls a gun instead. Swan dodges his shot and throws a switchblade into Luther's forearm, disarming him. The Riffs arrive, acknowledging the Warriors' innocence of Cyrus' murder while saluting their courage and skill. The Riffs let the Warriors leave before descending on the Rogues and Luther, who screams in anguish at his imminent demise. The radio DJ announces that the bounty on the Warriors has been cancelled and salutes them with a song, "[[In the City (Joe Walsh song)|In the City]]." The film ends with Swan, Mercy, and the rest of the gang walking down a Coney Island beach illuminated by the rising sun.

==Production==
===Development===
<div style="float:right; margin:0 0 1em 1em; width:200px;">
  [[File:Walter Hill 1137 (cropped).jpg|right|thumb|180 px|alt=Walter Hill|Walter Hill, director and co-writer of ''The Warriors'' (pictured in 2014)]]
</div>
The film is based on [[Sol Yurick]]'s 1965 novel ''[[The Warriors (Yurick novel)|The Warriors]]'', which was, in turn, based on [[Xenophon]]'s ''[[Anabasis (Xenophon)|Anabasis]]''.<ref>{{cite web |first=Erik |last=Brown |title=One book inspired Alexander the Great and 'The Warriors' |website=Lessons from History |date=June 27, 2020 |url=https://medium.com/lessons-from-history/one-book-inspired-alexander-the-great-and-the-warriors-9035ad965766 |access-date=February 22, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |first=Steve |last=Coates |title=The Original Warriors |work=ArtsBeat |publisher=New York Times |date=March 24, 2008 |url=https://artsbeat.blogs.nytimes.com/2008/03/24/the-original-warriors/ |access-date=February 22, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |first=Eran |last=Almagor |chapter=Going Home: Xenophon's ''Anabasis'' in Sol Yurick's ''The Warriors'' (1965) |title=Rewriting the Ancient World: Greeks, Romans, Jews and Christians in modern popular fiction |publisher=Brill |location=Leiden |year=2017 |isbn=9789004346383 |pages=85–113 |url=https://brill.com/view/book/edcoll/9789004346383/B9789004346383_006.xml}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |first=Eric |last=Homberger |title=Sol Yurick obituary |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=January 7, 2013 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/books/2013/jan/07/sol-yurick |access-date=February 22, 2021}}</ref> Film rights were bought in 1969 by [[American International Pictures]] but no film resulted.<ref>{{Cite news|title=MOVIE CALL SHEET: 'New Leaf' Next for Weston|author=Martin, Betty|date=April 25, 1969|work=Los Angeles Times|page=i12}}</ref>

Rights were then obtained by producer [[Lawrence Gordon (producer)|Lawrence Gordon]] who commissioned [[David Shaber]] to write a script. Gordon had made ''Hard Times'' (1975) and ''The Driver'' (1978) with Walter Hill; he sent the script to Hill with a copy of Sol Yurick's novel. Hill recalls, "I said 'Larry, I would love to do this, but nobody will let us do it.' It was going to be too extreme and too weird."<ref name="hill5">{{Cite web |last=Markowitz |first=Robert |title=Interview with Walter Hill – Chapter 5 |url=https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Walter-Hill.aspx?Filter=Full+Interview |access-date=June 12, 2014 |website=[[Directors Guild of America|DGA]] |date=April 7, 2011 }}</ref><ref name="ducker">{{Cite news
 | last = Ducker
 | first = Eric
 | title = New York Mythology
 | work = Fader
 | date = October 3, 2005
 | url = http://www.thefader.com/blog/articles/2005/10/03/new-york-mythology
 | access-date = September 28, 2008}}</ref>

Gordon and Hill were originally going to make a western but when the financing on the project failed to materialize, they took ''The Warriors'' to [[Paramount Pictures]] because they were interested in youth films at the time and succeeded in getting the project financed. Hill remembers "it came together very quickly. Larry had a special relationship with Paramount and we promised to make the movie very cheaply, which we did. So it came together within a matter of weeks. I think we got the green light in April or May 1978 and we were in theaters in February 1979. So it was a very accelerated process."<ref name="esquire"/>

Hill was drawn to the "extreme narrative simplicity and stripped down quality of the script".<ref name="ducker"/> The script, as written, was a realistic take on street gangs but Hill was a huge fan of comic books and wanted to divide the film into chapters and then have each chapter "come to life starting with a splash panel".<ref name="ducker"/> However, Hill was working on a low budget and a tight post-production schedule because of a fixed release date as the studio wanted to release ''The Warriors'' before a rival gang picture called ''[[The Wanderers (1979 film)|The Wanderers]]''. Hill was finally able to include this type of scene transition in the Ultimate Director's Cut released for home video in 2005.<ref name="ducker"/>

===Casting===
[[File:Thomas G. Waites.jpg|thumb|upright|Thomas G. Waites, who portrayed Fox before being fired during production (pictured in 2022)<ref>{{Cite web |last=Connor |first=Jackson |date=2015-09-08 |title=Remember the Warriors: Behind the Chaotic, Drug-Fueled, and Often Terrifying Making of a Cult Classic |url=https://www.villagevoice.com/remember-the-warriors-behind-the-chaotic-drug-fueled-and-often-terrifying-making-of-a-cult-classic/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230818070148/https://www.villagevoice.com/remember-the-warriors-behind-the-chaotic-drug-fueled-and-often-terrifying-making-of-a-cult-classic/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=August 18, 2023 |access-date=2025-08-11 |website=The Village Voice |language=en-US}}</ref>]]
The filmmakers did extensive casting in New York City.<ref name="ducker"/> Hill was considering hiring [[Sigourney Weaver]] from ''[[Alien (film)|Alien]]'' and watched a movie she had filmed in [[Israel]] called ''Madman'' where the male lead opposite Weaver was played by Michael Beck. The director was impressed with Beck's performance and cast him in ''The Warriors''. Hill initially wanted a Puerto Rican actress for the role of Mercy, but Deborah Van Valkenburgh's agent convinced the film's casting directors to see her and she was eventually cast. The filmmakers wanted to cast [[Tony Danza]] in the role of Vermin but he was cast in the sitcom ''[[Taxi (TV series)|Taxi]]'' and Terry Michos was cast instead. While there were white characters in Yurick's book, none of the central characters or protagonists were white: according to Hill, Paramount did not want an all-black cast for "commercial reasons".<ref name="ducker"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Lee |first=Theodore |date=2024-02-12 |title=Whatever Happened to Terry Michos, Vermin From 'The Warriors'? |url=https://cavemancircus.com/2024/02/12/terry-michos/ |access-date=2025-09-05 |website=Caveman Circus |language=en-US}}</ref>

Hill saw [[Thomas G. Waites]] as the next [[James Dean]], and the director "invited the young actor to the Gulf and Western to watch movies like ''[[Rebel Without a Cause]]'' and ''[[East of Eden (film)|East of Eden]]'' for inspiration."<ref name=Remember>{{cite news |last=Connor |first=Jackson |title=Remember the Warriors: Behind the Chaotic, Drug-Fueled, and Often Terrifying Making of a Cult Classic |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/news/remember-the-warriors-behind-the-chaotic-drug-fueled-and-often-terrifying-making-of-a-cult-classic-7607043 |newspaper=[[The Village Voice]] |date=September 8, 2015 |access-date=April 2, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150910104117/https://www.villagevoice.com/news/remember-the-warriors-behind-the-chaotic-drug-fueled-and-often-terrifying-making-of-a-cult-classic-7607043|archive-date=September 10, 2015}}</ref> During the screening, Hill offered Waites a drink, which Waites refused, resulting in a rift between the two that grew worse during the grueling summer shoot. At one point, Waites threatened to report the working conditions to the [[Screen Actors Guild]], forcing [[Paramount Pictures|Paramount]] to provide a second trailer for the eight Warriors to share.<ref name=Remember/>

Finally, eight weeks into principal photography, when the tension on set between Waites and Hill reached the breaking point, Hill demanded that stunt coordinator Craig Baxley improvise a stunt scene in which Waites' character would be killed. "Stunned, Baxley demurred. Such a critical scene would take careful planning. But Hill was insistent. 'I don't give a shit how you kill him,' Baxley recalls the director saying. 'Kill him.'" Baxley found a crew member who resembled Waites and staged a scene in which the character is thrown off a subway platform in front of an approaching train. "It was like someone cut my soul out and left a shell", Waites remembers. He would later demand that his name be removed from the cast altogether; he remains uncredited to this day.<ref name=Remember/> Both Waites and Hill have expressed regret over how the situation was handled and since made up.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Abramovitch |first1=Seth |title=Director Walter Hill Regrets Cutting Gay Gang the Dingos From 1979's 'The Warriors' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/walter-hill-regrets-cutting-gay-gang-dingos-the-warriors-1235239468/ |website=The Hollywood Reporter |date=October 18, 2022 |access-date=19 October 2024}}</ref>

===Filming===
[[File:Coney Island beach and amusement parks (June 2016).jpg|thumb|Scenes from the film were shot in [[Coney Island]] (pictured in 2016).]]
Stunt coordinator [[Craig R. Baxley]] put the cast through stunt school because Hill wanted realistic fights depicted in the film.<ref name="ducker"/> In preparation for his role, [[James Remar]] hung out at Coney Island to find a model for his character. The entire film was shot on the streets in New York City<ref name="bbc">{{cite news |last=Wakeman |first=Gregory |title=The Warriors: The 1979 cult hit that shows an ultra-violent NY |url=https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20240209-the-warriors-the-1979-cult-hit-that-shows-an-ultra-violent-ny |access-date=9 February 2024 |website=BBC Culture}}</ref> with some interior scenes done at [[Astoria Studios]]. They would shoot from sundown to sunrise. The film quickly fell behind schedule and went over budget. Although the Conclave scene at the beginning was supposed to be in The Bronx, it was actually filmed in [[Riverside Park (Manhattan)|Riverside Park]] in Manhattan.<ref name="bbc"/> The bathroom fight scene against The Punks was shot in a studio. The entire movie was only filmed in Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens. Actor Joel Weiss remembers that filming of his scene on [[Avenue A (Manhattan)|Avenue A]], in Manhattan's notorious [[Alphabet City, Manhattan#20th century|Alphabet City]], was canceled because there was a double homicide nearby. For the big meeting at the beginning of the film, Hill wanted real gang members in the scene with off duty police officers also in the crowd so that there would be no trouble.<ref name="ducker"/>

The studio would not allow Baxley to bring any stunt men from [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]] and he needed someone to double for the character of Cyrus so he did the stunt himself dressed as the character.<ref name="ducker"/> Real gang members sometimes approached the set to challenge the cast members but were stopped, sometimes violently, by production security. The actors playing the Warriors bonded early in the shoot, on and off the set. Originally, the character of Fox was supposed to end up with Mercy, while Swan was captured by a rival, homosexual gang known as the Dingos, only to escape later. However, Hill watched the dailies and felt that Beck and Van Valkenburgh had great chemistry; the script was rewritten so that their characters ended up together.<ref name="ducker"/>

The Rogues' car in the Coney Island confrontation was a 1955 Cadillac [[hearse]].<ref>{{cite web |author=Time to create page: 0.196 seconds |url=http://www.v8tvshow.com/forum/index.php?action=printpage;topic=9768.0 |title=V8tvshow.com |publisher=V8tvshow.com |access-date=January 26, 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140103135431/http://www.v8tvshow.com/forum/index.php?action=printpage;topic=9768.0 |archive-date=January 3, 2014}}</ref> Hill was underwhelmed by the script's depiction of Luther taunting the Warriors, and asked actor [[David Patrick Kelly]] for ideas. Kelly first proposed using two dead pigeons, which was rejected as Hill was unconvinced that it would work.<ref name="ducker"/> While Hill left to adjust some cameras, Kelly went under the boardwalk and emerged with some discarded beer bottles: when Hill returned to the car to ask what he had come up with, Kelly clinked the bottles together with his fingers and delivered the now-iconic line, "Warriors, come out to play." Hill immediately declared, "Go with that. Don't change it. Let's shoot."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-warriors-director-explains-the-origin-of-the-iconic-line/|title="Come out to play-yay": 'The Warriors' director explains the origin of the iconic line|last=Russell|first=Calum|date=October 24, 2022|website=Far Out|access-date=March 19, 2024}}</ref> Kelly recalls his improvisation was inspired by something said to him by his then-next door neighbor, a man he suspects of having been a gangster and whose voice he also drew from for the line.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Waller |first1=Sam |title=An Interview with David Patrick Kelly |url=https://romancandlemagazine.com/2020/03/21/an-interview-with-david-patrick-kelly/ |website=Roman Candle Magazine |access-date=11 July 2026 |date=21 March 2020}}</ref>

Hill wanted [[Orson Welles]] to do a narrated introduction about [[Ten Thousand|Greek themes]] but the studio did not like this idea and refused to pay for it.<ref name="ducker"/> However this sequence was finally included in the 2005 Ultimate Director's Cut, with Hill providing the narration himself. "I wanted to take it into a fantasy element, but at the same time add some contemporary flash", said Hill. "Those were some of the hard ideas we had to get the studio to understand. But we did not get along very well with our parent company. After the movie came out and it did well, everybody was sort of friends. But up until then there was a lot of misunderstanding. They thought it was going to be ''[[Saturday Night Fever]]'' or something."<ref name="esquire">{{cite web|last=Wood |first=Jennifer M. |url=http://www.esquire.com/entertainment/movies/interviews/a27499/the-warriors-walter-hill-interview/ |title='Can You Dig It?' The Warriors, 35 Years Later |publisher=Esquire.com |date=February 19, 2014 |access-date=February 7, 2017}}</ref>

===Controversy===
The following weekend, the film was linked to sporadic outbreaks of vandalism and three killings—two in [[Southern California]] and one in [[Boston]]—involving moviegoers on their way to or from showings.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1979/02/23/archives/ads-resumed-for-a-gang-movie-after-sporadic-violence-at-theaters.html |title=Ads Resumed for a Gang Movie After Sporadic Violence at Theaters |last=Herman |first=Robin |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=1979-02-23 |page=A18}}</ref>

Paramount was prompted to remove advertisements from radio and television completely and display ads in the press were reduced to the film's title, rating and participating theaters.<ref name="arnold"/> As a reaction, 200 theaters across the country added security personnel. Due to safety concerns, theater owners were relieved of their contractual obligations if they did not want to show the film, and Paramount offered to pay costs for additional security and damages due to [[vandalism]].<ref name="TimeMag">{{Cite magazine
 | title = The Flick of Violence
 | magazine = [[Time (magazine)|Time]]
 | date = 1979-03-19
 | url = http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,947013,00.html
 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20071023033632/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,947013,00.html
 | url-status = dead
 | archive-date = 2007-10-23
 | access-date = 2008-09-23}}</ref>

Hill later remembered: {{blockquote|"I think the reason why there were some violent incidents is really very simple: The movie was very popular with the [[street gangs]], especially young men, a lot of whom had very strong feelings about each other. And suddenly they all went to the movies together! They looked across the aisle and there were the guys they didn't like, so there were a lot of incidents. And also, the movie itself is [[wikt:rambunctious|rambunctious]]—I would certainly say that."<ref name="esquire"/>}}

===Home media===
The film was first released on VHS in 1980, LaserDisc in 1981, and DVD in 2001. <ref>{{Cite web |title=The Warriors (DVD, 2001) |url=https://www.ebay.com/p/3346863 |access-date=2026-08-28 |website=eBay |language=en-us}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title= Warriors, The (1979) |url=https://www.lddb.com/laserdisc/23175/LV-1122/Warriors-The |website=LaserDisc Database |isbn=}}</ref> The DVD contained the theatrical cut unrestored; this release has since fallen out of print. Then, in 2005, [[Paramount Home Entertainment]] released the "Ultimate Director's Cut" DVD of ''The Warriors''. In addition to remastered picture quality and a new 5.1 surround remixed soundtrack, the film was re-edited with a new introduction and comic book-style sequences between scenes.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/dvd/review/the-warriors/775 |title=''The Warriors'' – DVD Review |author=Henderson, Eric |publisher=[[Slant (magazine)|Slant]] magazine |date=October 18, 2005 |access-date=September 29, 2011}}</ref> In July 2007, the "Ultimate Director's Cut" was released on Blu-ray and has since been available for online streaming rentals and purchases through Amazon, iTunes, Google Play, Vudu and YouTube.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/The-Warriors-Blu-ray/418/ |title=The Warriors Blu-ray United States Ultimate Director's Cut |publisher=blu-ray.com|access-date=November 19, 2013}}</ref>

The original theatrical cut is available to stream in HD on those same services and was released as a Manufactured On Demand DVD in the U.S. in March 2020 by Paramount. In May 2022, ''The Warriors'' received another release through [imprint] on Blu-ray in Australia, which included not only the "Ultimate Director's Cut", but also the theatrical version, which had not seen a Blu-ray release before. In December 2023, ''The Warriors'' was released for the first time on 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray by [[Arrow Films|Arrow Video]] with both theatrical and director's cut included.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Warriors 4K UHD |url=https://www.arrowvideo.com/p/the-warriors-4k-uhd/15244566/ |access-date=2025-08-12 |website=Arrow Video |language=en}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
After two weeks free of incidents, the studio expanded the display ads to take advantage of reviews from reputable critics including [[Pauline Kael]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]''. She wrote, "''The Warriors'' is a real moviemaker's movie: it has in visual terms the kind of impact that '[[Rock Around the Clock]]' did behind the titles of ''[[Blackboard Jungle]]''. ''The Warriors'' is like visual rock."<ref name="barra"/> At Seattle's Grand Illusion Cinema, programmer Zack Carlson remembers, "people were squeezed in, lying on the floor, cheering."<ref>{{cite web | last=Wood | first=Jennifer M | title=Midnight Movie Madness | work=Moviemaker | url=http://www.moviemaker.com/directing/article/midnight_movie_madness_2923/ | date=June 20, 2004 | access-date=February 13, 2009 | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080513023613/http://www.moviemaker.com/directing/article/midnight_movie_madness_2923 | archive-date=May 13, 2008 | df=mdy-all}}</ref> By its sixth week, ''The Warriors'' had grossed $16.4 million, well above its estimated $4 million<ref name="la"/> to $7 million budget.<ref name="arnold"/><ref>{{Cite web |last=Gareth |first=Jones |title=The Making of The Warriors |url=http://warriorsmovie.co.uk/production |access-date=August 16, 2013 |website=The Warriors Movie Site}}</ref>

Walter Hill reflected:
<blockquote>What made it a success with young people... is that for the first time somebody made a film within Hollywood, big distribution, that took the gang situation and did not present it as a social problem. Presented them as a neutral or positive aspect of their lives. As soon as you said in the old days "gang movies", it was "how do we cure the pestilence and how do we fix the social waste. We want to take these kids, make sure they go to college..." This was just a movie that conceptually was different. Accepted the idea of the gang, didn't question it. That was their lives, they functioned within that context. And the social problem wasn't "were they going to college", but "were they going to survive". It's the great Hawksian dictum, where is the drama? Will he live or die? That's the drama.<ref name="hill5"/></blockquote>

"Hollywood forgives a lot when you have a hit," he added. "I don't know what to say about it, other than the fact that it was just a gift in terms of getting it. The studio hated it, and didn't even want to release it. There was a lot of friction with management at the time. Some of it might have been my fault."<ref name="rolling">{{cite news|last=Fear|first=David|date=September 16, 2016|title=Walter Hill on Controversial Revenge Thriller '(Re)Assignment'|newspaper=[[Rolling Stone]]|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/features/walter-hill-on-controversial-wtf-thriller-reassignment-w439981|access-date=October 8, 2016}}</ref>

===Critical reception===
[[File:Gene Siskel 1982 ad (cropped) (cropped).jpg|thumb|160px|alt=Gene Siskel|Gene Siskel,(pictured in 1982)]]
''The Warriors'' received negative reviews from contemporary critics, who derided its lack of realism and found its dialogue stilted.<ref>{{cite book|last=Mulholland|first=Garry|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=YzkIVy5IxIkC&pg=PP80|access-date=August 21, 2013|page=80|quote=Presumably, Hill was stung by some of the bad reviews at the time, which sneered at the film's lack of realism and stilted dialogue...|title=Stranded at the Drive-In: The 100 Best Teen Movies|publisher=[[Hachette (publisher)|Hachette UK]]|year=2011|isbn=978-1409122517}}</ref> In his review for the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'', [[Roger Ebert]] gave it two out of four stars and wrote that, despite Hill's cinematic skill, the film is implausible in a mannerist style that deprives the characters of depth and spontaneity: "No matter what impression the ads give, this isn't even remotely intended as an action film. It's a set piece. It's a ballet of stylized male violence."<ref>{{cite news|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|date=February 13, 1979|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-warriors-1979|title=The Warriors Movie Review & Film Summary (1979)|newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|access-date=August 11, 2013}}</ref>

However, Ebert later wrote during a review of Hill's film ''[[Southern Comfort (1981 film)|Southern Comfort]]'' that he felt he overlooked some positive qualities in ''The Warriors'' out of his dislike for Hill's general approach to broad characterizations.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/southern-comfort-1981|title=Southern Comfort|last=Ebert|first=Roger|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|date=January 1, 1981|access-date=August 15, 2015}}</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] gave the film one star out of four, likening the dialogue to that of "[[Harvey Lembeck]] in those silly '60s motorcycle pictures" and concluding, "You would think after watching 'The Warriors' that gang membership was a victimless crime, save for the occasional sadist who pops up as comic relief. This entire film is a romantic lie."<ref>[[Gene Siskel|Siskel, Gene]] (February 13, 1979). "The gang cliches are all here in a weak 'Warriors'". ''[[Chicago Tribune]]''. Section 2, p. 6.</ref>

Linda Gross of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' called the film "an insightful, stylized and shallow portrayal of gang warfare that panders to angry youthful audiences."<ref>Gross, Linda (February 14, 1979). "Gangs Vs. Gangs in ''The Warriors''". ''[[Los Angeles Times]]''. Part IV, p. 8.</ref> Gary Arnold of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' wrote, "None of Hill's dynamism will save ''The Warriors'' from impressing most neutral observers as a ghastly folly."<ref>{{Cite news|last=Arnold|first=Gary|date=February 10, 1979|title=Abstracted Epic of Gang Warfare|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]}}</ref> In his review for ''[[Newsweek]]'', [[David Ansen]] wrote, "Another problem arises when the gang members open their mouths: their banal dialogue is jarringly at odds with Hill's hyperbolic visual scheme."<ref>{{Cite news|last = Ansen| first = David |title = Gang War| work = [[Newsweek]]|date = February 26, 1979}}</ref>

Frank Rich of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' wrote, "unfortunately, sheer visual zip is not enough to carry the film; it drags from one scuffle to the next ... ''The Warriors'' is not lively enough to be cheap fun or thoughtful enough to be serious."<ref>{{Cite magazine|last = Rich|first = Frank| title = Dead End|magazine = [[Time (magazine)|Time]]| date = February 26, 1979| url = http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,920188,00.html| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20071024121516/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,920188,00.html| url-status = dead| archive-date = October 24, 2007| access-date = September 23, 2008}}</ref> Yurick expressed his disappointment and speculated that it scared some people because "it appeals to the fear of a demonic uprising by lumpen youth", appealing to many teenagers because it "hits a series of collective fantasies."<ref name="arnold"/> President [[Ronald Reagan]] was a fan of the film, even calling lead actor Michael Beck to tell him he had screened it at [[Camp David]] and enjoyed it.<ref name="barra">{{Cite news| last = Barra| first = Allen | title = ''The Warriors'' Fights On | url=http://www.salon.com/2005/11/28/warriors_2/ | work = Salon.com|date = November 28, 2005}}</ref>

=== Censorship ===
In France, the movie was [[X-rated]] for incitement to violence on June 25, 1979, by the Movies Control Board (''<span dir="ltr" lang="fr">[[:fr:Commission de contrôle des films cinématographiques|Commission de contrôle des films cinématographiques]]</span>'', the official organization tasked with classifying films) which deemed that the work gave "a very realistic vision of the [[urban guerrilla]] warfare that gangs can develop to conquer a city." In order to allow the movie to be released in normal theaters, 10 minutes were cut, including the speech by Cleon. In 1980, the movie was finally rerated as banned for viewers under 13 and the missing scenes were finally readded.<ref group="N">{{Text and translation|Dans la mesure où il schématise sans souci de vérité psychologique une guerre de bandes de voyous à New York, le film a cependant paru accéder à une dimension de vérité, et par elle à une portée incitative dans la mesure où il donne une vision très réaliste de la guérilla urbaine que des gangs peuvent développer pour conquérir une ville.|To the extent that it schematizes a [[gang war]] between thugs in New York City without concern for psychological truth, the film nevertheless seemed to access a dimension of truth, and through it an inciting scope to the extent that it gives a very realistic vision of the urban guerrilla warfare that gangs can develop to conquer a city.|''Commision de contrôle''}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Triollet |first=Christophe |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=nAkHEAAAQBAJ&dq=%22Les+Guerriers+de+la+nuit%22+%22incitation+%C3%A0+la+violence%22&pg=PA65 |title=Darkness 6 (Censure & cinéma en France) |date=2020-11-04 |publisher=LettMotif |isbn=978-2-36716-329-1 |pages=65 |language=fr}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Triollet |first=Christophe |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=C9osDwAAQBAJ&dq=%22Les+Guerriers+de+la+nuit%22+%22incitation+%C3%A0+la+violence%22&pg=PA286 |title=Darkness, censure et cinéma (1. Gore & violence) |date=2017-07-15 |publisher=LettMotif |isbn=978-2-36716-089-4 |pages=288–289 |language=fr}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=mKAoAQAAIAAJ&q=%22guerilla+urbaine%22+%22guerriers+de+la+nuit%22 |title=L'Express |date=1980 |publisher=Presse-Union |pages=14 |language=fr}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |date=June 29, 1979 |title=Arrêtés des 19 et 25 juin 1979 relatifs au classement de films pornographiques ou d'incitation à la violence |trans-title=Orders of 19 and 25 June 1979 relating to the classification of pornographic or violence-inciting films |url=https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/jo/period/?datePubli=29%2F06%2F1979+%3E+29%2F06%2F1979 |journal=[[Journal officiel de la République française]] |language=fr |pages=5480 |via=Légifrance}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |date=June 20, 1980 |title=Arrêté du 13 juin 1980 abrogeant un précédent arrêté relatif au classement d'un film d'incitation à la violence |trans-title=Order of June 13, 1980 repealing a previous order relating to the classification of a violence-inciting film |url=https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/jo/period/?datePubli=20%2F06%2F1980+%3E+20%2F06%2F1980 |journal=[[Journal officiel de la République française]] |pages=5352 |via=Légifrance}}</ref>

===Retrospective===
[[File:Warriors Grafitti in Long Island City.jpg|275px|thumb|right|A building in [[Long Island City]], [[New York (state)|New York]], with a wall covered in graffiti with the names of the fictional gangs from the movie. This rooftop location appears in the film during the confrontation between the Warriors and the Orphans.]]
''The Warriors'' has become a [[cult film]], and some film critics have since re-examined it. As of December 2023, the film garnered an 88% approval rating at Rotten Tomatoes, based on 51 reviews. The critical consensus reads: "As violent as it is stylish, ''The Warriors'' is a thrilling piece of pulp filmmaking."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1023205-warriors/|title=The Warriors (1979)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=June 11, 2018}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 65 out of 100, based on 16 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''The Warriors'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-warriors |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=August 11, 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=The Warriors Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-warriors/ |access-date=2025-08-11 |website=www.metacritic.com |language=en}}</ref>

In 2003, ''The New York Times'' placed the film on its "Best 1,000 Movies Ever Made".<ref>{{Cite web |date=April 29, 2003 |title=The Best 1,000 Movies Ever Made |url=https://www.nytimes.com/ref/movies/1000best.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080612032429/https://www.nytimes.com/ref/movies/1000best.html |archive-date=June 12, 2008 |access-date=June 12, 2008 |website=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> ''Entertainment Weekly'' named it the 16th-greatest cult film on its 2003 "Top 50 Greatest Cult Films" list,<ref>{{cite web | last=Dirks | first=Tim | title=The Top 50 Cult Movies | work=Filmsite.org | url=http://www.filmsite.org/cultfilmsew.html}}</ref> and ranked it 14th in its 2008 list of the "25 Most Controversial Movies Ever".<ref>{{cite magazine | title=25 Most Controversial Movies Ever | url=https://www.ew.com/gallery/25-most-controversial-movies-ever | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] | date=August 27, 2008 | access-date=August 27, 2008}}</ref>

Hill reflected in 2016:
<blockquote>I love the fact that people still enjoy something I did what, 37 years ago? It makes an old man happy. I'm surprised by it. But I loved working with my cameraman Andy Laszlo in shooting it, and I loved working with my cast, who were incredibly trusting of this crazy old fucker that was making the movie. They didn't get it, I don't think—costumed gangs running around New York?—but they just went with it.<ref name="rolling"/></blockquote>

In 2018, Dante James wrote for ''Film Threat'' that few who see ''The Warriors'' can explain its longevity—other than saying, “It's just fucking cool, man!” He noted the film's enduring appeal is grounded in its gritty charm and cult energy.<ref>{{cite web |last=James |first=Dante |title=The Warriors |website=Film Threat |date=August 14, 2018 |url=https://filmthreat.com/reviews/the-warriors/ |access-date=August 11, 2025}}</ref>

In 2024, Weslee Taylor of ''The Scarlet'' wrote that the film was “a thrilling, gritty tale of brotherhood and survival,” noting that despite its early negative reviews, it had “developed a massive cult following” and was later included in ''The New York Times''' list of the “Best 1,000 Movies Ever Made.”<ref>{{cite web |last=Taylor |first=Weslee |title=Review: "Warriors" Delivers an Innovative Reimagining of a Cult Classic |website=The Scarlet |date=October 25, 2024 |url=https://thescarlet.org/20074/larts/review-warriors-delivers-an-innovative-reimagining-of-a-cult-classic/ |access-date=August 11, 2025}}</ref>

In modern retrospectives, ''The Warriors'' has been revisited by critics who praise its rhythmic visuals and cult legacy as a stylized urban fable.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Movie Review: The Warriors (1979) |url=https://boxreview.com/movie-review-the-warriors-1979 |access-date=Oct 9, 2025 |website=BoxReview.com}}</ref>

===Merchandise===
In 2005, [[Mezco Toyz]] released several action figures based on characters from the film, including Swan, Cleon, Cochise, Ajax, Luther, and a Baseball Fury.<ref>[http://www.mezcotoyz.com/products.asp?pline=war Mezco Toyz | Movie, Television and Proprietary Action Figures & Collectibles] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061213174848/http://www.mezcotoyz.com/products.asp?pline=war |date=December 13, 2006}}</ref>

=== Video games ===
A [[beat em up]] [[The Warriors (video game)|video game of the same name]] based on the film was released by [[Rockstar Games]] in October 2005. The game expands upon the story of the film, featuring 13 levels that take place before the film's events and depict the Warriors' rise to power. The final five levels directly adapt the events of the film, with only a few changes. Several of the actors from the film returned to reprise their roles. In 2005, [[Roger Hill (actor)|Roger Hill]] (who portrayed Cyrus in the film) sued Rockstar Games and [[Take-Two Interactive]] for royalty fees, claiming the video game used his voice and depiction of his likeness without his consent or paying him royalties. Take-Two asserted its claims that the voice and likeness of Cyrus were a component of its licensing agreement for the film. Roger Hill died in 2014 with the case unresolved.<ref>{{cite web|last=Dagan|first=Carmel|date=February 25, 2014|title=Roger Hill, Who Played Cyrus in 'The Warriors,' Dies at 65|url=https://variety.com/2014/film/obituaries-people-news/roger-hill-who-played-cyrus-in-the-warriors-dies-at-65-1201120971/|access-date=April 20, 2018|work=Variety}}</ref>

In 2009, [[Paramount Digital Entertainment]] released a [[beat 'em up]] scroller game based on the film, titled ''[[The Warriors: Street Brawl]]''.

In the same year, [[Dynamite Entertainment]] also published ''The Warriors: Jail Break'', a four-issue mini series which takes place several months after the events portrayed in the film and involve the gang's attempt to break Ajax from prison.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://splashpage.mtv.com/2009/06/02/exclusive-the-warriors-jailbreak-begins-where-the-movie-ends-and-heres-your-first-look/|title=Exclusive: 'The Warriors: Jailbreak' Begins Where The Movie Ends -- And Here's Your First Look!|last=Marshall|first=Rick|date=February 6, 2009|access-date=December 24, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090606151756/http://splashpage.mtv.com/2009/06/02/exclusive-the-warriors-jailbreak-begins-where-the-movie-ends-and-heres-your-first-look/|archive-date=June 6, 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref>

=== Stage musical and concept album ===
{{Main|Warriors (Lin-Manuel Miranda and Eisa Davis album)}}
In August 2023, it was reported that [[Lin-Manuel Miranda]] had begun work on a stage musical gender-swapped adaptation of [[The Warriors (Yurick novel)|the Sol Yurick novel]] that provided the basis for the film.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gans |first=Andrew |date=2023-08-04 |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda Penning Stage Musical Adaptation of The Warriors |url=https://playbill.com/article/lin-manuel-miranda-penning-stage-musical-adaptation-of-the-warriors |website=Playbill.com}}</ref> In August 2024 it was reported that the project was in fact to produce a [[concept album]] with [[Eisa Davis]], adapted from the film. The album, ''[[Warriors (Lin-Manuel Miranda and Eisa Davis album)|Warriors]]'', was released on 18 October 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-01 |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda and Eisa Davis' 'Warriors' will be a concept album, not a stage musical |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/story/2024-08-01/lin-manuel-miranda-warriors-album-eisa-davis |access-date=2024-09-27 |website=Los Angeles Times}}</ref> Original film cast members James Remar and David Patrick Kelly contributed vocals to the album, with Remar voicing the cop who arrests Ajax, and Kelly voicing the officer who kills Fox. In December 2024, Lin-Manuel Miranda officially announced via an interview<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bahr |first=Sarah |date=2024-12-22 |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda on 'Mufasa' and the Secret to a Disney Hit|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/12/22/arts/music/lin-manuel-miranda-mufasa.html|website=The New York Times}}</ref> with ''[[The New York Times]]'', that he, alongside Eisa Davis would be adapting the well-received concept album into a full stage show. "People really like it, but because we're such a visual culture, everyone said to me and Eisa 'OK, when can we see it?'" he noted, "So I think at the top of the year, Eisa and I will just start having conversations about how to adapt that to the stage."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wild |first=Stephi |date=2024-12-26 |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda Reveals WARRIORS Will Come to the Stage|url=https://www.broadwayworld.com/article/Lin-Manuel-Miranda-Reveals-WARRIORS-Will-Come-to-the-Stage-20241226|website=Broadway World}}</ref> In June 2026, it was reported that the musical, now described as being based on both the novel and the movie, would begin performances at [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]]'s [[Lunt-Fontanne Theatre]] in March 2027.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gans |first=Andrew |title=Lin-Manuel Miranda and Eisa Davis' Warriors Musical Will Open on Broadway in 2027 |url=https://playbill.com/article/lin-manuel-miranda-and-eisa-davis-warriors-musical-will-open-on-broadway-in-2027}}</ref>

==Proposed remake==
In 2005, director [[Tony Scott]] announced plans for a remake of ''The Warriors'', relocating the story to modern-day [[Los Angeles]]. Scott stated that the film would not feature the stylized gangs of the original, such as the Baseball Furies and Hi-Hats.<ref>{{cite web|last=Miller|first=Daniel|date=August 21, 2012|title=Tony Scott Update: Director Had Personal Connection to San Pedro Bridge|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/tony-scott-death-san-pedro-bridge-unstoppable-364517|access-date=April 1, 2015|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=Carroll|first1=Larry|date=September 19, 2005|title=Warriors' Remake Without The Baseball Furies And Those Bad-Ass Mimes? Oh Yes|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1509875/warriors-remake-without-the-baseball-furies-and-those-bad-ass-mimes-oh-yes/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140514000923/http://www.mtv.com/news/1509875/warriors-remake-without-the-baseball-furies-and-those-bad-ass-mimes-oh-yes/|url-status=dead|archive-date=May 14, 2014|access-date=January 20, 2016|publisher=MTV}}</ref> Following Scott's death in 2012, filmmaker [[Mark Neveldine]] expressed interest in taking over the project, though no remake has materialized.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Singer|first1=Matt|date=July 21, 2015|title=Mark Neveldine on 'The Vatican Tapes,' Remaking 'The Warriors,' and the Future of Neveldine/Taylor|url=https://screencrush.com/mark-neveldine-vatican-tapes-interview/?trackback=tsmclip|access-date=January 20, 2016|publisher=ScreenCrush}}</ref>
