'''''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle''''' is a 2017 American [[action-adventure]] film directed by [[Jake Kasdan]] and written by [[Chris McKenna (writer)|Chris McKenna]], [[Erik Sommers]], [[Scott Rosenberg]], and [[Jeff Pinkner]]. The film is the third installment in the [[Jumanji|''Jumanji'' film series]], a sequel to ''[[Jumanji (film)|Jumanji]]'' (1995) and its spin-off film ''[[Zathura: A Space Adventure]]'' (2005). It stars [[Dwayne Johnson]], [[Jack Black]], [[Kevin Hart]], and [[Karen Gillan]], with [[Nick Jonas]], [[Rhys Darby]], and [[Bobby Cannavale]] in supporting roles.

The story focuses on a group of teenagers who come across ''Jumanji'', now transformed into a video game nearly thirty years after the original film's events. They find themselves trapped inside the game as a set of adult [[Avatar (computing)|avatars]], seeking to complete a treacherous quest alongside another player who has been trapped in it since recovering and playing it in 1996. [[Principal photography]] began in Honolulu in September 2016 and ended in Atlanta in December.

''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' premiered at the [[Grand Rex]] in [[Paris]] on December 5, 2017, and was released in the United States on December 20 by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]]. The film received mostly positive reviews from critics, and grossed $963{{nbsp}}million, becoming the [[2017 in film#Highest-grossing films|fifth-highest-grossing film of 2017]]. A sequel, ''[[Jumanji: The Next Level]]'', was released in 2019.

== Plot ==
<!--Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. See "Wikipedia:How to write a plot summary" (WP:PLOTSUM) for general guidelines.-->
In 1996, Brantford, [[New Hampshire]], teenager Alex Vreeke receives ''Jumanji'', which was previously disposed of by Alan Parrish and Sarah Whittle in 1969,{{efn|As depicted in the 1995 film ''[[Jumanji (film)|Jumanji]]''.}} from his father, who found it on the coast. Only interested in video games, he sets the boardgame aside. Later that night, Alex finds it transformed into a [[ROM cartridge|video game cartridge]]. Opting to play, he is sucked inside the game, disappearing from the real world as time continues to pass, thus leaving his parents and their house in a disheveled state.
	
Twenty-one years later, four Brantford High School students, awkward Spencer Gilpin, athletic Anthony "Fridge" Johnson, ditzy Bethany Walker, and cynical Martha Kaply are given detention – Fridge and Spencer for cheating on their assignments, Bethany for video calling during class, and Martha for insulting the [[Physical education|gym]] teacher. After finding Alex's discarded video game system and starting it up, the group is sucked into ''Jumanji'', landing in a jungle as their chosen avatars – Spencer as muscular [[archaeologist]] Dr. Xander "Smolder" Bravestone, Fridge as diminutive [[zoologist]] Franklin "Mouse" Finbar, Bethany as male, overweight [[cartography|cartographer]] and [[paleontology|paleontologist]] Professor Sheldon "Shelly" Oberon, and Martha as sultry [[martial arts]] expert Ruby Roundhouse. Three marks on their arms denote their lives in the game, making them afraid that if they lose all three, they will actually die. 

The group's goal is to end a curse on ''Jumanji'', brought about by a corrupt archaeologist, Professor Russell Van Pelt after he stole a magical jewel called the "Jaguar's Eye" and gained control of the jungle's animals. They must return the jewel to the shrine and call out "Jumanji" to lift the curse and leave the game.

Coping with their avatars, their "weaknesses", Van Pelt's men, and their own group dynamics as they begin their journey, they encounter Alex in the form of a fifth avatar – [[pilot]] Jefferson "Seaplane" McDonough – who takes them to a treehouse that Alan built during his tenure in Jumanji. Alex thinks he has only been stuck in the game for a couple of months; everyone, including him, is shocked to realize he has been there for two decades. The group coordinate with each other's strengths and work together to overcome the game's obstacles. They manage to return the jewel to the statue and break the curse, defeating Van Pelt and therefore completing the game before returning to the real world.

Back in the present, the group finds the Vreeke home restored and lively from its previous state after Alex's disappearance. They meet Alex, now an adult, who explains that he returned to 1996, allowing him to live his life. He remained in Brantford and now leads a married life with two children; his daughter is named after Bethany to honor her after she had saved him by sacrificing one of her own lives in the game.

The four students form an unlikely friendship after their experiences in the game, having bonded and become better people, including Spencer and Martha beginning a relationship. Soon, after hearing drumbeat sounds coming from the game, they destroy the game to prevent it from endangering anyone else.

===Development===
Plans for a ''[[Jumanji (film)|Jumanji]]'' sequel were put forward by [[Sony Pictures|Sony Pictures Entertainment]] in the late 1990s. As reported by [[Ain't It Cool News]], a [[stand-alone sequel]] entitled ''Jumanji 2'' was in development in 1999. The plot of the story involved John Cooper, the [[President of the United States]], buying Jumanji from an old antique store in [[Europe]] and bringing it to the [[White House]] to play it with his children (one of whom, Butch, just wants a dad, not a President for a father). Cooper then gets sucked into the world of Jumanji, paving the way for his evil [[Vice President of the United States|Vice President]], who was supposed to be played by [[Steve Buscemi]], to rise to power as Cooper's replacement.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/3998|title=Who's playing the 'evil Vice-President' in JUMANJI 2'|work=[[Ain't It Cool News]]|access-date=February 25, 2019|date=July 20, 1999}}</ref> Inside the game, Cooper would have teamed up with [[hybrid animal]]s, which were going to be animated with [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]]; Sony Pictures Consumer Products executive VP of worldwide consumer products Peter Dang revealed prototype drawings of animals that might have appeared in the film, all designed by [[Ken Ralston]], who served as visual effects supervisor in the original film and was planned to make his [[directorial debut]] with ''Jumanji 2'', slated for a [[Christmas]] 2000 release date.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://kidscreen.com/1999/08/01/26272-19990801/|title=Wild hybrids for Jumanji 2|work=Kidscreen|last=Robertson|first=Virginia|access-date=February 25, 2019|date=August 1, 1999}}</ref> The first film's co-writer, [[Jonathan Hensleigh]], had written the initial draft. Ralston eventually stepped down and the project stalled, albeit the DVD commentary of the first film still references a sequel directed by Ralston.<ref name="DVDcom">''[[Jumanji (film)|Jumanji]]'' DVD commentary (dvd). The film's complete title, ''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'', was confirmed in Cinemacon 2017 held in March. Its plot involved teenagers cleaning out a school's basement, who find a [[Retrogaming|vintage video-game]] version of ''Jumanji'' and are sucked into the first film's jungle setting. Although fans debated whether the film was a sequel or a [[Reboot (fiction)|reboot]], the second trailer (released that September) indicated that the sequel is set 21 years after the first. Dwayne Johnson noted that the film was inspired by classic [[1990s in video gaming|video games of the 1990s]].<ref>{{cite news |title=Jumanji Inspired By 1990s Video Games |url=https://screenrant.com/jumanji-tone-video-games-1990s/ |work=[[Screen Rant]] |date=13 October 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=How Video Games From The 1990s Are Influencing Jumanji, According To The Rock |url=https://www.cinemablend.com/news/1713130/how-video-games-from-the-1990s-are-influencing-jumanji-according-to-the-rock |work=Cinema Blend |date=October 12, 2017 |access-date=2018-11-15 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171223044825/https://www.cinemablend.com/news/1713130/how-video-games-from-the-1990s-are-influencing-jumanji-according-to-the-rock |archive-date=2017-12-23 |url-status=live}}</ref> The film had used the working title "Jumanji" and the final title and the use of the song "Welcome to the Jungle" was suggested by Jack Black.<ref name="Nemiroff">{{cite web |date=7 April 2020 |last=Nemiroff |first=Perri |title=Director Jake Kasdan Talks the Status of 'Jumanji 4' & Tricky Franchise Time Travel |url=https://collider.com/jumanji-4-update-jake-kasdan-interview/ |website=Collider}}</ref>

==Release==
===Theatrical===
Sony initially gave the film a release date of December 25, 2016.<ref>{{cite news|last=Kroll|first=Justin|title=Sony Dates 16 Films Including Two More 'Bad Boys' Sequels, 'Jumanji' Remake|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/news/sony-films-bad-boys-jennifer-lawrence-passengers-1201557242/|access-date=October 25, 2015|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=August 5, 2015|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151013062027/http://variety.com/2015/film/news/sony-films-bad-boys-jennifer-lawrence-passengers-1201557242/|archive-date=October 13, 2015|df=mdy-all}}</ref> Since filming did not begin until September 2016, the release was pushed back to July 28 and then to December 20, 2017.<ref name="insight"/>

[[Amazon Prime]] members in the United States could access to tickets for a December 8 screening of the film at select [[Regal Entertainment Group|Regal]], [[National Amusements]], [[ArcLight Hollywood|ArcLight Cinemas]] and [[AMC Theatres|AMC]] theaters.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2017/11/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-amazon-prime-early-screenings-marketing-1202216329/|title='Jumanji: Welcome To The Jungle' To Swing In Early For Amazon Prime Members|first=Nancy|last=Tartaglione|work=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|date=November 29, 2017|access-date=November 29, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171129143833/http://deadline.com/2017/11/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-amazon-prime-early-screenings-marketing-1202216329/|archive-date=2017-11-29|url-status=live}}</ref> The screenings sold out at 1,200 theaters and earned $1.9 million.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2017/12/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-amazon-prime-sneak-box-office-1202227292/|title='Jumanji: Welcome To The Jungle' Amazon Prime Sneaks Sell Out Grossing Near $2M|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|work=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|date=December 14, 2017|access-date=December 15, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171214235449/http://deadline.com/2017/12/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-amazon-prime-sneak-box-office-1202227292/|archive-date=2017-12-14|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was released on [[IMAX]] 2D on January 12, 2018.

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' grossed $405 million in the United States and Canada and $558 million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $963{{nbsp}}million<ref name="mojo"/> It was the [[2017 in film#Highest-grossing films|fifth-highest-grossing film of 2017]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=2017 Worldwide Box Office |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/world/2017/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210211165008/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/world/2017/ |archive-date=February 11, 2021 |access-date=August 19, 2023 |website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> On April 10, 2018, the film passed ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' ($403.7 million) to become Sony's highest-grossing film domestically.<ref>{{cite web|last=Domanico|first=Michael|title=Jumanji Overtakes Spider-Man as Sony's Biggest Domestic Box Office Release Ever|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2018/04/11/jumanji-overtakes-spider-man-as-sonys-biggest-domestic-box-office-release-ever|publisher=IGN|access-date=March 19, 2022|date=April 11, 2018}}</ref> On December 25, 2021, ''[[Spider-Man: No Way Home]]'' surpassed the film at $405 million to become Sony's highest-grossing film domestically. ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' calculated its net profit as $305.7 million when factoring all expenses and revenues, making it 2017's fourth-most-profitable release.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2018/03/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-box-office-profit-1202351461/|title=No. 4 'Jumanji: Welcome To The Jungle' Box Office Profits – 2017 Most Valuable Blockbuster Tournament|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|work=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|date=March 26, 2018|access-date=March 26, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180327005923/http://deadline.com/2018/03/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-box-office-profit-1202351461/|archive-date=2018-03-27|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 77% based on 235 reviews, and an average rating of 6.1/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' uses a charming cast and a humorous twist to offer an undemanding yet solidly entertaining update on its source material."<ref>{{cite web |title=Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle (2017) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/jumanji_welcome_to_the_jungle |access-date=May 22, 2025 |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 58 out of 100, based on 44 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.metacritic.com/movie/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle |title=Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle Reviews|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=October 29, 2018}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale, while those at [[PostTrak]] gave it an 84% positive score.<ref name="opening"/>

Dave White of ''[[TheWrap]]'' praised the cast and called the film a pleasant surprise: "''Jumanji: Welcome to The Jungle'' is the Christmas tentpole release that aims to please and succeeds, a funny family entertainment product that subverts more expectations than it was obligated to contractually".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-film-review-dwayne-johnson-kevin-hart-jack-black/|title='Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle' Film Review: A Mainstream Moviegoing Miracle|work=[[TheWrap]]|date=December 19, 2017|access-date=December 19, 2017|last=White|first=David|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171220150617/https://www.thewrap.com/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-film-review-dwayne-johnson-kevin-hart-jack-black/|archive-date=2017-12-20|url-status=live}}</ref> Peter Travers of ''[[Rolling Stone (magazine)|Rolling Stone]]'' writes "enough star power and comic zest to deliver a fun time at the movies ... barely" and praises the cast, particularly Jack Black as hilarious and for finding the "vulnerable heart" of the character. Travers gives the film 2.5 stars out of 4.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-reviews/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-review-sequel-barely-squeaks-by-on-charm-199023/|title='Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle' Review: Sequel Squeaks by on Charm|first1=Peter|last1=Travers|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=19 December 2017}}</ref> Peter Bradshaw of ''[[The Guardian]]'' newspaper gives the film 3 out of 5 stars. Bradshaw praises Johnson for his "endearing performance" and calls it an "amiable effort" which can be expected to go down well on home viewings.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/dec/09/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-review-dwayne-the-rock-johnson-jack-black-karen-gillan|title=Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle review – fantasy romp likably upgraded for gamer generation|first=Peter|last=Bradshaw|newspaper=The Guardian |date=9 December 2017|via=www.theguardian.com}}</ref>

David Ehrlich of ''[[IndieWire]]'' gave the film a C grade, calling it unnecessary, but mildly amusing: "''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' is further proof that even the stalest whiff of brand recognition has become preferable to originality. Only part of the blame for that belongs to the studios, but after cannibalizing themselves for much of the last 20 years, Hollywood has clearly eaten their way down to the crumbs".<ref>{{cite web |first=David |last=Ehrlich |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2017/12/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-review-dwayne-johnson-jack-black-kevin-hart-karen-gillan-1201904969/ |title='Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle' Review: A Robin Williams Relic Is Reborn as a Mildly Amusing Video Game Movie |work=[[IndieWire]] |publisher=[[Penske Business Media]] |date=December 8, 2017 |access-date=December 9, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171209072600/http://www.indiewire.com/2017/12/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-review-dwayne-johnson-jack-black-kevin-hart-karen-gillan-1201904969/ |archive-date=2017-12-09 |url-status=live}}</ref> For ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', [[Owen Gleiberman]] wrote: "Excitement! Suspense! Childlike innocence! Ingeniously staged action set pieces! These are a few of the things you will not find, anywhere, in ''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' ... It's supposed to be a board game come to life but really, it's just a bored game."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2017/film/reviews/jumanji-welcome-to-the-jungle-review-dwayne-johnson-kevin-hart-1202633910/|title=Film Review: 'Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=December 8, 2017 |access-date=December 9, 2017 |publisher=[[Penske Business Media]] |first=Owen |last=Gleiberman}}</ref>

A [[virtual reality|virtual reality experience]] titled ''Jumanji: The VR Adventure'', developed by MWM Immersive and published by Sony Pictures Virtual Reality, was released on [[Steam (service)|Steam]] for [[HTC Vive]] on January 17, 2018.<ref name="JumanjiVR">{{Cite web|url=https://steamdb.info/app/780590/history/|title=Jumanji: The VR Adventure|website=SteamDB|access-date=July 4, 2019}}</ref> Although it was announced that the experience would be released on [[Oculus Rift]] and [[PlayStation VR]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sonypictures.com/corp/press_releases/2018/01_18/011818_jumanjivr.html|title=Sony Pictures Virtual Reality Partners With Vr Leader Survios To Expand Jumanji Experience To Vr Audiences Worldwide|publisher=Sony Pictures Entertainment|date=January 18, 2018|access-date=July 4, 2019}}</ref> the releases were canceled, as the game was panned by critics for its poor graphics and hardware performance.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://uploadvr.com/2018/01/18/jumanji-vr-adventure-graphics/|title=Sony's Jumanji: The VR Adventure Is Drawing Attention For The Wrong Reasons|last=Feltham|first=Jamie|publisher=[[UploadVR]]|date=January 18, 2018|access-date=July 4, 2019|archive-date=July 4, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190704101501/https://uploadvr.com/2018/01/18/jumanji-vr-adventure-graphics/|url-status=dead}}</ref> It was delisted from Steam on February 9, 2018.<ref name="JumanjiVR" />

A video game titled ''Jumanji: The Video Game'', developed by Funsolve and published by Outright Games, was released on November 8, 2019, for [[PlayStation 4]], [[Xbox One]], [[Nintendo Switch]], and [[Microsoft Windows]]. It is based on ''Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle'' and ''[[Jumanji: The Next Level]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/jumanji/news/the-jungle-awaits-new-video-game-based-the-successful-jumanji-film-franchise-launches|title=The jungle awaits as new video game based on the successful Jumanji film franchise launches November 15, 2019|publisher=Bandai Namco Entertainment Europe|date=June 4, 2019|access-date=July 4, 2019}}</ref>
