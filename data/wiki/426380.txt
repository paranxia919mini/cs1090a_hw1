{{nihongo|'''Kiyoshi Kurosawa'''{{efn|{{IPAc-en|k|ʊər|ə|ˈ|s|ɑː|w|ə}}}}|黒沢 清|Kurosawa Kiyoshi|born July 19, 1955}} is a Japanese filmmaker, critic, author, actor, and a former professor at [[Tokyo University of the Arts]] (2005–23).

Noted for his [[Psychological fiction|psychological]] films that often focus on ambiguous narratives and on their characters' inner turmoils and quests for meaning and connections, he is best known for his contributions to [[psychological horror]] and [[Japanese horror]], notably his acclaimed 1997 film ''[[Cure (film)|Cure]]'', although he has also worked in a variety of other genres. While most of his work has been in [[Japanese language|Japanese]], two of his films, ''[[Daguerrotype (film)|Daguerrotype]]'' (2016) and ''[[Serpent's Path (2024 film)|Serpent's Path]]'' (2024; a remake of his own [[Serpent's Path (1998 film)|1998 film of the same name]]), were in [[French language|French]]. 

Kurosawa received the [[Medals of Honor (Japan)|Medal with Purple Ribbon]] for his contributions to Japanese cinema, in 2021.<ref name=":5">{{cite web |url= https://www.yomiuri.co.jp/national/20211101-OYT1T50299/|title= 秋の褒章、808人・22団体...紫綬褒章はソフト「金」の上野由岐子さんら最多90人|access-date= November 2, 2021|work= Yomiuri Shimbun|date= 2 November 2021}}</ref> ''[[The New York Times]]'' stated that Kurosawa "is to psychological fright what [[David Cronenberg]] is to body horror."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/07/19/movies/kiyoshi-kurosawa-primer.html|title=The Kurosawa You May Never Have Heard Of|first=Carlos|last=Aguilar|date=July 19, 2025|work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref>

==Early life and education ==
Born in [[Kobe]] on July 19, 1955,<ref name=schneider>{{cite book|title=501 Movie Directors|last=Schneider|first=Steven Jay|year=2007|publisher=ABC Books|location=New York City, New York|page=563|isbn=978-0-733-32052-1}}</ref> Kiyoshi Kurosawa started making films about his life in high school.<ref name =ign/> He studied at [[Rikkyo University]] in [[Tokyo]] under the guidance of prominent film critic [[Shigehiko Hasumi]],<ref>{{cite book|title=Opening Bazin|publisher=Oxford University Press|editor-first=Dudley|editor-last=Andrew|first=Kan|last=Nozaki|year=2011|pages=327}}</ref> where he began making [[8mm film]]s.<ref name="ign">{{cite web |url=http://www.ign.com/articles/2001/08/23/interview-with-director-kiyoshi-kurosawa|title=Interview with Director Kiyoshi Kurosawa|publisher=IGN|first=Spencer|last=D.|date=23 August 2001}}</ref>

Though they share a surname, he is not related to filmmaker [[Akira Kurosawa]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Richie |first=Donald |author-link=Donald Richie |title=A Hundred Years of Japanese Film: A Concise History |year=2001 |publisher=Kodansha International |location=Tokyo |page=214 |isbn=4-7700-2682-X}}</ref>

== Filmmaking career ==
Kurosawa began directing commercially in the 1980s, working on [[pink film]]s<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/books/the-films-of-kiyoshi-kurosawa-master-of-fear/|title=Midnight Eye book review: The Films of Kiyoshi Kurosawa: Master of Fear|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Nicholas|last=Rucka|date=9 March 2009}}</ref> and low-budget [[V-Cinema]] (direct-to-video) productions such as formula [[yakuza film]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/serpents-path/|title=Midnight Eye review: Serpent's Path|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=14 November 2001}}</ref> In 1981, his 8mm film ''Shigarami Gakuen'' (しがらみ学園) was nominated for the Oshima Prize at the PFF ([[Pia Film Festival]]). In 1983, after he worked with [[Shinji Sōmai|Shinji Soumai]], he released his first feature film ''[[Kandagawa Pervert Wars]]'' (1983). He became popular after ''The Excitement of the Do-Re-Mi-Fa Girl'' (1985) and ''[[The Guard from Underground]]'' (1992).<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=黒沢清|url=https://eiga.com/person/29025/|website=映画.com|language=ja|access-date=2020-05-01}}</ref>

In the early 1990s, Kurosawa won a scholarship to the [[Sundance Institute]] by submitting his original screen play ''[[Charisma (film)|Charisma]].'' Then, he was able to study filmmaking in the United States, although he had been directing for nearly ten years professionally.<ref name="charisma">{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/charisma/|title=Midnight Eye review: Charisma|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=20 March 2001}}</ref>

Kurosawa first achieved international acclaim with his 1997 crime thriller film ''[[Cure (film)|Cure]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/cure/|title=Midnight Eye review: Cure|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=20 March 2001}}</ref> A year later, he completed two thrillers back-to-back, ''Serpent's Path'' and ''Eyes of the Spider'', both of which shared the same premise (a father taking revenge for his child's murder) and lead actor ([[Show Aikawa]]) but spun entirely different stories.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jonathanrosenbaum.net/?p=6295|title=Three films by Kiyoshi Kurosawa|publisher=JonathanRosenbaum.net|first=Jonathan|last=Rosenbaum|author-link=Jonathan Rosenbaum|date=17 August 2001}}</ref> In March 1999, the Hong Kong International Film Festival presented his first retrospective, a five-title-program including ''The Excitement of the Do-re-mi fa Girls'', ''The Guard from Underground'', ''Serpent's Path'', ''Eyes of the Spider'', and ''License to Live''.

Kurosawa followed up ''Cure'' with a semi-sequel in 1999 with ''[[Charisma (film)|Charisma]]'', a detective film starring [[Kōji Yakusho]].<ref name="charisma"/> In 2000, ''[[Séance (2001 film)|Seance]]'', Kurosawa's adaptation of the novel ''Seance on a Wet Afternoon'' by Mark McShane, premiered on [[Kansai Telecasting Corporation|Kansai TV]]. It also starred Yakusho, as well as [[Jun Fubuki]] (the two had appeared together in ''Charisma'' as well). In 2001, he directed the horror film ''[[Pulse (2001 film)|Pulse]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/pulse/|title=Midnight Eye review: Pulse|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=21 June 2001}}</ref> Kurosawa released ''[[Bright Future (film)|Bright Future]]'', starring [[Tadanobu Asano]], [[Joe Odagiri]] and [[Tatsuya Fuji]], in 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/bright-future/|title=Midnight Eye review: Bright Future|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Michael|last=Arnold|date=20 August 2003}}</ref> He followed this with another digital feature, ''[[Doppelganger (2003 film)|Doppelganger]]'', later the same year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://twitchfilm.com/2005/01/kiyoshi-kurosawas-doppelganger-review.html|title=Kiyoshi Kurosawa's Doppelganger Review|publisher=Twitch Film|first=Todd|last=Brown|date=January 23, 2005|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141129232729/http://twitchfilm.com/2005/01/kiyoshi-kurosawas-doppelganger-review.html|archive-date=November 29, 2014}}</ref> Both ''Bright Future and Doppelganger'' have nominated for the [[Cannes Film Festival]]s<ref name=":0" />
[[File:Kiyoshi Kurosawa (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|Kurosawa in 2008]]
In 2005, Kurosawa returned with ''[[Loft (2005 film)|Loft]]'', his first love story since ''Seance''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cahiersducinema.com/Critique-Loft-by-Kiyoshi-Kurosawa.html|title=Critique. Loft by Kiyoshi Kurosawa|publisher=Cahiers du Cinéma|first=Jean-Philippe|last=Tesse|date=January 2007|access-date=2012-11-01|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130619034708/http://www.cahiersducinema.com/Critique-Loft-by-Kiyoshi-Kurosawa.html|archive-date=2013-06-19|url-status=dead}}</ref> Another horror film, ''[[Retribution (2006 film)|Retribution]]'', followed in the next year.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.slantmagazine.com/house/2006/12/j-horror-mash-up-kiyoshi-kurosawas-retribution/|title=J-horror Mash-Up: Kiyoshi Kurosawa's Retribution|publisher=Slant Magazine|first=Travis Mackenzie|last=Hoover|date=6 December 2006|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130202224905/http://www.slantmagazine.com/house/2006/12/j-horror-mash-up-kiyoshi-kurosawas-retribution/|archive-date=2 February 2013}}</ref> With his 2008 film, ''[[Tokyo Sonata]]'', Kurosawa was considered to step "out of his usual horror genre and into family drama".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2009-mar-22-ca-indieeye22-story.html|title=Kiyoshi Kurosawa provides domestic chills in 'Tokyo Sonata'|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|first=Susan|last=King|date=22 March 2009}}</ref>

He has written a novelization of his own film ''Pulse'', as well as a history of horror cinema with [[Makoto Shinozaki]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/books/mon-effroyable-histoire-du-cinema/|title=Midnight Eye book review: Mon effroyable histoire du cinéma|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=9 March 2009}}</ref>

In September 2012, it was announced that he would direct ''[[1905 (film)|1905]]'', a film starring [[Tony Leung Chiu-Wai]], [[Shota Matsuda]] and [[Atsuko Maeda]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.screendaily.com/news/asia-pacific/kurosawa-to-direct-japan-china-co-production-starring-leung/5046442.article|title=Kurosawa to direct Japan-China co-production starring Leung|publisher=Screen International|first=Jason|last=Gray|date=September 11, 2012}}</ref> In February 2013, it was announced that production of the film had been cancelled before filming could start.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/production-company-prenom-h-bankrupted-424512|title=Production Company Bankrupted by China-Japan Island Dispute Fallout|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media|Prometheus Global Media, LLC]]|first=Gavin J.|last=Blair|date=26 February 2013}}</ref>

Kurosawa directed a 2012 five-part television drama ''[[Penance (Japanese TV series)|Penance]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.screendaily.com/reviews/the-latest/penance/5045813.article|title=Penance - Review - Screen|publisher=Screen International|first=Dan|last=Fainaru|date=29 August 2012}}</ref> ''Beautiful 2013'', an anthology film featuring Kurosawa's ''Beautiful New Bay Area Project'', screened at the [[Hong Kong International Film Festival]] in 2013.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/beautiful-2013-hong-kong-review-431276|title=Beautiful 2013: Hong Kong Review - The Hollywood Reporter|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media|Prometheus Global Media, LLC]]|first=Elizabeth|last=Kerr|date=27 March 2013}}</ref>

Kurosawa's next feature film ''[[Real (2013 film)|Real]]'', which stars [[Takeru Sato]] and [[Haruka Ayase]], was released in 2013.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2013/film/reviews/real-review-kiyoshi-kurosawa-locarno-1200576666/|title=Locarno Film Review: 'Real'|publisher=Variety|first=Maggie|last=Lee|date=9 August 2013}}</ref> He won the Best Director award at the 8th [[Rome Film Festival]] for ''[[Seventh Code]]'' later that year.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/japanese-director-kiyoshi-kurosawa-very-656864|title=Japanese Director Kiyoshi Kurosawa 'Very Surprised' About Two Wins at Rome Film Fest|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media|Prometheus Global Media, LLC]]|first=Gavin J.|last=Blair|date=18 November 2013}}</ref>

His 2015 film ''[[Journey to the Shore]]'' was screened in the [[Un Certain Regard]] section at the [[2015 Cannes Film Festival]] where he won the prize for Best Director.<ref name="2015Cannes">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/article/61306.html|title=2015 Official Selection|access-date=16 April 2015|work=[[Cannes Film Festival|Festival de Cannes]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418084150/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/article/61306.html|archive-date=18 April 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/cannes-2015-regard-winners-announced-797333|title=Cannes: 'Rams' Wins Un Certain Regard Prize|author=Rebeccas Ford|date=23 May 2015|access-date=23 May 2015|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media|Prometheus Global Media, LLC]]}}</ref>

In 2016, his thriller ''[[Creepy (film)|Creepy]]'' premiered at the [[66th Berlin International Film Festival]].<ref name="Shackleton">{{cite web|url=http://www.screendaily.com/territories/asia-pacific/hkiff-to-open-with-trivisa-chongqing-hotpot/5100786.article|title=HKIFF to open with Trivisa, Chongqing Hotpot|first=Liz|last=Shackleton|date=24 February 2016|access-date=25 February 2016|work=[[Screen Daily]]}}</ref> The film marked Kurosawa's first cinematic return to the horror genre since 2006.

His 2017 film ''[[Before We Vanish]]'' was screened in the Un Certain Regard category at the Cannes Film Festival.

His 2019 film ''[[To the Ends of the Earth (2019 film)|To the Ends of the Earth]]'' was screened as the closing film in the Piazza Grande program of the [[72nd Locarno Film Festival]].

In 2020, Kurosawa won the [[Silver Lion#Silver Lion for Best Direction (1990–present)|Silver Lion for Best Direction]] at the [[77th Venice International Film Festival]] for his film ''[[Wife of a Spy]]''.<ref name=japantimes>{{citation |title=Kiyoshi Kurosawa wins best director award at Venice for 'Wife of a Spy' | newspaper=[[The Japan Times]] |date=September 13, 2020 |url=https://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2020/09/13/films/kiyoshi-kurosawa-best-director-award-venice/}}</ref>

Kurosawa directed three films in 2024: the short film ''[[Chime (film)|Chime]]'', a [[Serpent's Path (2024 film)|French-language remake]] of ''Serpent's Path'', and the thriller ''[[Cloud (film)|Cloud]]''. The latter was nominated for Best Film at that year's [[Sitges Film Festival]].

His first [[jidaigeki]] (period drama) ''[[The Samurai and the Prisoner]]'' is scheduled to premiere in June 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-03-10 |title=New Trailer for Kiyoshi Kurosawa’s Kokurojo: The Samurai and the Prisoner and Chime / Serpent’s Path Double Feature |url=https://thefilmstage.com/new-trailer-for-kiyoshi-kurosawas-kokurojo-the-samurai-and-the-prisoner-premiering-this-june/ |access-date=2026-03-25 |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Academic career ==
From 2005 to 2023, Kurosawa was a Professor at the [[Tokyo University of the Arts]]'s Graduate School of Film and New Media.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Nippon Connection |url=https://db.nipponconnection.com/en/person/111/kiyoshi-kurosawa |access-date=2026-03-25 |website=db.nipponconnection.com |language=en}}</ref>

== Style and influences ==
[[File:Yasujiro Ozu 01.jpg|thumb|Yasujiro Ozu]]
Kurosawa's filmmaking style has been compared to those of [[Stanley Kubrick]] and [[Andrei Tarkovsky]], though he has never expressly listed those directors as influences.<ref>''Cure'' DVD. "Interview with Kiyoshi Kurosawa." New York: Home Vision Entertainment/Janus Films, 2001.</ref>  In an interview, he claimed that [[Alfred Hitchcock]] and [[Yasujirō Ozu]] contributed to shaping his personal vision of the medium.<ref>{{cite web|first=Giuseppe|last=Sedia|url=http://www.asiaexpress.it/interviste/interviste-cinema/66-kurosawa-kiyoshi.html|title=Interview with Kiyoshi Kurosawa|publisher=Asia Express|language=it|date=October 2006|access-date=2011-06-07|archive-date=2021-04-23|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210423122605/http://www.asiaexpress.it/interviste/interviste-cinema/66-kurosawa-kiyoshi.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> He has also expressed admiration for American film directors such as [[Don Siegel]], [[Sam Peckinpah]], [[Robert Aldrich]], [[Richard Fleischer]],<ref>{{cite web|url=http://twitchfilm.com/2008/08/kiyoshi-kurosawa-blogathoncure-confusion-and-sophistication.html|title=KIYOSHI KUROSAWA BLOGATHON—CURE: Confusion and Sophistication|publisher=Twitch Film|first=Michael|last=Guillen|date=13 August 2008|access-date=5 November 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304065712/http://twitchfilm.com/2008/08/kiyoshi-kurosawa-blogathoncure-confusion-and-sophistication.html|archive-date=4 March 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref> and [[Tobe Hooper]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.midnighteye.com/reviews/sweet-home/|title=Midnight Eye review: Sweet Home|publisher=[[Midnight Eye]]|first=Tom|last=Mes|date=31 October 2001}}</ref> In a 2000 interview with ''Time'', Kurosawa noted his appreciation for [[John Cassavetes]], stating, "When I watch a Cassavetes film, I am awed by the understanding that people can subtly change in the course of a simple conversation."<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Grant |first=Ed |date=2001-08-02 |title=The Other Kurosawa |url=http://www.time.com/time/sampler/article/0%2C8599%2C169908%2C00.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20011006171546/http://www.time.com/time/sampler/article/0,8599,169908,00.html |archive-date=2001-10-06 |access-date=2025-10-17 |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]}}</ref> Through his association with mentor Shigehiko Hasumi, Kurosawa was also influenced by [[Jean-Luc Godard]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Stephens |first=Chuck |date=2001-07-24 |title=Kiyoshi Kurosawa Begins at the End |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2001-07-24/film/kiyoshi-kurosawa-begins-at-the-end/full/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150321102153/http://www.villagevoice.com/2001-07-24/film/kiyoshi-kurosawa-begins-at-the-end/full/ |archive-date=2015-03-21 |access-date=2025-10-17 |work=[[The Village Voice]]}}</ref>

In a 2009 interview with [[IFC (U.S. TV channel)|IFC]], Kurosawa talked about the reason why he has cast the actor [[Kōji Yakusho]] in many of his films: "He has similar values and sensitivities. We're from the same generation. That's a big reason why I enjoy working with him on the set."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ifc.com/fix/2009/03/kiyoshi-kurosawa|title=Kiyoshi Kurosawa Composes "Tokyo Sonata"|publisher=IFC|first=Steve|last=Erickson|date=12 March 2009}}</ref>

According to Tim Palmer, Kurosawa's films occupy a peculiar position between the materials of mass genre, on the one hand, and esoteric or intellectual abstraction, on the other. They also clearly engage with issues of environmental critique, given Kurosawa's preference for shooting in decaying open spaces, abandoned (and often condemned) buildings, and in places rife with toxins, pestilence and entropy.<ref>{{cite book|last=Palmer|first=Tim|chapter=The Rules of the World: Japanese Ecocinema and Kiyoshi Kurosawa|editor-last=Willoquet-Maricondi|editor-first=Paula|title=Framing the World: Explorations in Ecocriticism and Film|publisher=University of Virginia Press|year=2010|isbn=978-0-8139-3006-0}}</ref>

In an interview with the Tokyo Art University, where he is a professor, Kurosawa talks about not wanting his directorial style to be too fixed.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web|title=東京藝術大学 {{!}} 第六回 黒沢 清 大学院映像研究科映画専攻教授|url=https://www.geidai.ac.jp/container/column/closeup_006|website=www.geidai.ac.jp|access-date=2020-05-01}}</ref> The interviewer makes reference to Kurosawa's versatility when they talk about [[Clint Eastwood]]; Kurosawa says he admires people who can do many things and that he doesn't box himself into one style or one theme. When asked what he wants to try next, he answered: "The next thing I want to do is something I have never done." Kurosawa also mentions that he has seen many films since he was young, and that he knows there are many great films from around the world. Those films motivate him to be a better filmmaker; he always asks himself how to make films that will be memorable for a long time.

In the same article by the Tokyo Art University, Kurosawa names film critic Hasumi Shigehiko as a mentor and early influence in his filmmaking career. Much of Hasumi's influence would go on to shape the core of Kurosawa's filmography. Kurosawa met Hasumi in University, where he was one of the few students to finish his course, and credits Hasumi with teaching him that film is worth dedicating your entire life to. Hasumi and Kurosawa believe that every element of the film matters and should be meticulously planned. Kurosawa has also stated that one of his goals as a filmmaker is to share Hasumi's teachings.

==Personal life==

=== Political activities ===
In December 2023, alongside 50 other filmmakers, Kurosawa signed an open letter published in ''[[Libération]]'' demanding a ceasefire and an end to the killing of civilians amid the [[Israeli invasion of the Gaza Strip (2023–present)|2023 Israeli invasion of the Gaza Strip]], and for a humanitarian corridor into Gaza to be established for humanitarian aid, and the release of hostages.<ref>{{cite news|date=28 December 2023|title=Gaza : des cinéastes du monde entier demandent un cessez-le-feu immédiat|url=https://www.liberation.fr/idees-et-debats/tribunes/gaza-des-cineastes-du-monde-entier-demandent-un-cessez-le-feu-immediat-20231228_WMAUSVJVLFEBNK4ME4XU3ZRU3M/|newspaper=[[Libération]]|language=fr|access-date=24 January 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://thefilmstage.com/claire-denis-ryusuke-hamaguchi-kiyoshi-kurosawa-christian-petzold-apichatpong-weerasethakul-more-sign-demand-for-ceasefire-in-gaza/|title=Claire Denis, Ryusuke Hamaguchi, Kiyoshi Kurosawa, Christian Petzold, Apichatpong Weerasethakul & More Sign Demand for Ceasefire in Gaza|last=Newman|first=Nick|date=29 December 2023|website=The Film Stage|access-date=24 January 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|date=31 December 2023|title=Directors of cinema sign petition for immediate ceasefire|url=https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-780176|newspaper=[[The Jerusalem Post]]|access-date=24 January 2024}}</ref>
