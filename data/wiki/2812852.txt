'''''Love Jones''''' (stylized as '''''love jones''''') is a 1997 American [[Romance film|romantic]] [[comedy-drama]] film written and directed by [[Theodore Witcher]], in his [[directorial debut|feature film directing debut]]. The film stars [[Larenz Tate]], [[Nia Long]], [[Isaiah Washington]], [[Bill Bellamy]], and [[Lisa Nicole Carson]].

''Love Jones'' had its world premiere at the [[Sundance Film Festival]] on January 17, 1997, where it tied with Hurricane Street for the Audience Award.<ref name="LAT2017">{{cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-et-mn-love-jones-oral-history-20170313-htmlstory.html |title=With 'Love Jones,' black love took center stage: An oral history |newspaper=Los Angeles Times |access-date=November 14, 2022}}</ref> It was released in the United States on March 14, 1997, by [[New Line Cinema]]. Although the film received favorable critical reviews, it was not a financial success. It is Theodore Witcher's only directorial work to date.<ref name="latimes">{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/la-et-mn-love-jones-witcher-19970320-story.html |title='Love Jones' director Theodore Witcher hopes his departure film of the black experience opens Hollywood's eyes |date=20 March 1997 |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] }}</ref>

==Plot==
{{More plot|date=March 2022}}
In Chicago, Darius Lovehall, a writer and poet, meets photographer Nina Mosley at the Sanctuary, a nightclub where jazz and spoken-word are performed. After an awkward first meeting at the bar, Darius is called to the stage and performs a poem that he dedicates to Nina. The two are attracted to each other, but Nina is hesitant because she has recently ended an engagement with Marvin. Darius sees Nina again at the record store and asks her out. After she declines, he obtains her address through her friend Sheila and later arrives at her apartment with a compact disc she had been searching for. Nina eventually agrees to spend time with him, and the two begin a romantic relationship, Although they become close, both avoid clearly expressing what they want from each other. Nina remains uncertain about her unresolved feelings for Marvin, while Darius is reluctant to admit the depth of his feelings for her.

Their relationship becomes strained when Marvin asks Nina to come to New York to discuss their future. Nina tells Darius that she has unfinished business with Marvin and leaves Chicago. Darius is hurt by he decision and later becomes involved with another women. When Nina returns and learns about it, she distances herself from him and begins spending time with Hollywood, one of Darius's friend. This leads to tension between Darius and Hollywood, later Darius and Nina briefly reconcile. Despite their reconciliation, Darius and Nina continue to struggle with mistrust, pride, and poor communication. Nina eventually accepts an opportunity in New York and prepares to leave Chicago again.

Darius realizes that he wants to be with her and tries to reach her before she departs, but he arrives at Union Station too late and misses her train. After time apart, Nina returns to Chicago and sees Darius again. The two acknowledge that their feelings for each other remain unresolved. Darius tells Nina that he still loves her, and the film ends with them considering whether to continue their relationship despite the distance between Chicago and New York.

==Production==
{{Expand section|date=March 2022}}
The producers of ''Love Jones'' stated that they wanted to make a modern film about Black American life that did not use violence and recreational drugs as elements in the story.<ref>Seavor, Jim. "'love jones' is a fresh look at an oft-told tale." ''[[The Providence Journal]]''. March 14, 1997. E03. Retrieved on February 11, 2012. "The people behind love jones say they wanted to make a contemporary film about African-American life that did not deal with guns and drugs"</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Honor |first=Billy Michael |date=March 8, 2017 |title=Reflecting on Love Jones 20 years Later |url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/reflecting-on-love-jones-20-years-later_b_58bf6093e4b070e55af9e920 |access-date=2022-11-14 |website=[[HuffPost]] |language=en}}</ref> Witcher developed the film from his interest in Chicago's underground poetry scene. In a 2017 oral history, he said that a Chicago poetry club called Spices provided the setting that he used as the background for a story about a relationship between twentysomethings. With encouragement from New Line executive Helena Echegoyen, Witcher wrote the screenplay over about nine months.<ref name="latimes.com">{{Cite web |date=2017-03-14 |title=With 'Love Jones,' black love took center stage: An oral history |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-et-mn-love-jones-oral-history-20170313-htmlstory.html |access-date=2026-05-19 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref>

Two of the [[poem]]s recited by Nia Long's character, Nina, were written by [[Sonia Sanchez]] and are included in her book ''Like the Singing Coming Off the Drums: Love Poems''.<ref>{{Cite news |date=March 30, 1997 |title=Setting, Characters Make 'Love Jones' a Bit Different |work=Orlando Sentinel |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1997-03-30-9704040822-story.html |url-status=dead |access-date=14 November 2022 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20221114211252/https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1997-03-30-9704040822-story.html |archive-date=14 November 2022}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Yohannes |first=Neyat |date=December 15, 2016 |title=Five Reasons to Love Love Jones |url=http://cleojournal.com/2016/12/15/five-reasons-to-love-love-jones/ |access-date=2022-11-14 |website=cléo |language=en-US}}</ref>

The film was shot in Chicago. According to the Los Angeles Times, cold weather and persistent rain affected the production, and rain was incorporated into the film's visual style and story.<ref name="latimes.com"/>

==Soundtrack==
The soundtrack included songs by artists such as Maxwell, Lauryn Hill, Groove Theory, and Cassandra Wilson. A 2022 essay published by the Criterion Collection stated that the soundtrack's reception helped renew interest in the film after its initial theatrical underperformance.<ref>{{Cite web |title=love jones: Sweet Home Chicago |url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/7741-love-jones-sweet-home-chicago |access-date=2026-05-19 |website=The Criterion Collection |language=en}}</ref>{{Main|Love Jones (soundtrack)}}

==Reception==
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds a 76% approval rating based on reviews from 29 critics, with an average rating of 6.7/10. The consensus summarizes: "An endearing romance that puts a fairly fresh perspective on familiar framework, ''Love Jones'' is worth falling for."<ref name="tomatoes">{{cite web |title=Love Jones |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/love_jones |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] it has a score of 65% based on reviews from 22 critics.<ref>{{cite web |title=Love Jones |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/love-jones |website=[[Metacritic]] }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a grade "A" on scale of A to F.<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= LOVE JONES (1997) A  |work= [[CinemaScore]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 }}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film a score of three out of four stars, and expressed the view that "There is also a bow to the unconventional in the ending of his film. Many love stories contrive to get their characters together at the end. This one contrives, not to keep them apart, but to bring them to a bittersweet awareness that is above simple love. Some audience members would probably prefer a romantic embrace in the sunset, as the music swells. But ''Love Jones'' is too smart for that." He also noted on the acting: "It's hard to believe that Tate--so smooth, literate and attractive here--played the savage killer O-Dog in ''[[Menace II Society]]''. Nia Long was Brandi, one of the girl friends, in ''[[Boyz n the Hood]]''. ''Love Jones'' extends their range, to put it mildly".<ref>{{cite news |author=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=March 14, 1997 |title=Love Jones |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/love-jones-1997 |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref>

[[James Berardinelli]], writing for ReelReviews, also awarded the film three out of four stars, writing that "There are several reasons why this film works better than the common, garden-variety love story. To begin with, the setting and texture are much different than that of most mainstream romances. The culture, in which post-college African Americans mingle while pursuing careers and relationships, represents a significant change from what we're used to. The Sanctuary, the intimate Chicago nightclub where Darius and Nina meet, is rich in its eclectic, [[blues]]y atmosphere. And ''Love Jones''{{'}}s dialogue is rarely trite. When the characters open their mouths, it usually is because they have something intelligent to say, not because they're trying to fill up dead air with meaningless words".<ref>{{cite web |last=Berardinelli |first=James |title=Review: Love Jones |url=http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/l/love_jones.html |website=Reelviews.net}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=March 14, 2020 |title=11 Quotes From 'Love Jones' That Are Still Relevant 20 Years Later |url=https://www.bet.com/article/twbl4u/11-quotes-from-love-jones-that-are-still-relevant |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241204175235/https://www.bet.com/article/twbl4u/11-quotes-from-love-jones-that-are-still-relevant |url-status=dead |archive-date=December 4, 2024 |access-date=November 14, 2022 |website=[[BET Networks|BET.com]]}}</ref>

==Home media==
The film was released on [[VHS]] by [[New Line Home Entertainment]].<ref>{{cite book|title=Love Jones [VHS]|isbn=0780619439 }}</ref> It later received a standalone [[DVD]] release,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Love-Jones-Jay-Stern/dp/B00000JGHO/|title=Love Jones (DVD)|website=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=March 7, 2022}}</ref> as well as a New Line "double feature" DVD release, the latter of which also included the 1996 film ''[[A Thin Line Between Love and Hate]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Love-Jones-Thin-Line-Between/dp/B001K98MGE|title=Love Jones/Thin Line Between Love and Hate (DBFE) (DVD)|website=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=March 7, 2022}}</ref>

A [[4K resolution|4K]] restoration of the film was released on [[Blu-ray]] by [[the Criterion Collection]] on March 29, 2022.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.criterion.com/films/31501-love-jones|title=love jones (1997)|publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]]|access-date=March 7, 2022}}</ref>

The Criterion Collection released a director-approved Blu-ray edition on March 29, 2022. The release included a new 4K digital restoration supervised by Theodore Witcher, a 5.1 surround DTS-HD Master Audio soundtrack, an audio commentary by Witcher new interviews, a panel discussion with members of the cast and crew, and an essay by Danielle Amir Jackson.<ref>{{Cite web |title=love jones |url=https://www.criterion.com/films/31501-love-jones |access-date=2026-05-19 |website=The Criterion Collection |language=en}}</ref>

==Legacy==
In a 2017 oral history, the Los Angeles Times described Love Jones as part of a group of late- 1990s and early-2000s films, including Soul Food, The Wood, and Brown Sugar, that broadened screen depiction of Black life, love, and relationships.<ref name="latimes.com"/>

In 2025, the American Black Film Festival announced that Nia Long and Larenz Tate would serve as ambassadors for that year's festival and participate in "ABFF Remembers: Love Jones," s conversation and musical tribute focused on the film.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Miami |first=ABFF |date=2025-01-30 |title=The 2025 American Black Film Festival Announces Nia Long and Larenz Tate as Ambassadors – American Black Film Festival Miami |url=https://www.abff.com/miami/the-2025-abff-announces-nia-long-and-larenz-tate-as-ambassadors/ |access-date=2026-05-19 |language=en-US}}</ref>
