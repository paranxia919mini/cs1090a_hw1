'''''Amrum''''' is a 2025 German [[Historical drama|historical drama film]] directed by [[Fatih Akin]], and co-written by Akin with [[Hark Bohm]], based on Bohm's childhood on the German island of [[Amrum]] during the last months of [[World War II]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=AMRUM |url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/amrum/ |access-date=2025-05-08 |website=Festival de Cannes |language=en-US |archive-date=8 May 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250508171516/https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/amrum/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="filmportal">{{cite web |title=Amrum |url=https://www.filmportal.de/film/amrum_8606befa4f5b4dd8b2a2c670bc563346 |access-date=8 May 2025 |website=[[filmportal.de]] |language=de}}</ref> It stars Jasper Billerbeck, Kian Köppke, [[Laura Tonke]], [[Diane Kruger]], [[Detlev Buck]] and [[Matthias Schweighöfer]].

The film had its world premiere at the Cannes Premiere section of the [[2025 Cannes Film Festival]], on 15 May,<ref name="Screenings Guide">{{cite web|url=https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2025/the-screenings-guide-of-the-78th-festival-de-cannes/|title=The Screenings Guide of the 78th Festival de Cannes|date=8 May 2025|website=Festival de Cannes|access-date=8 May 2025|archive-date=8 May 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250508200015/https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2025/the-screenings-guide-of-the-78th-festival-de-cannes/|url-status=live}}</ref> where it was met with positive reviews from critics.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Laffly |first=Tomris |date=2025-05-16 |title=‘Amrum’ Review: Fatih Akin’s Understated Coming-of-Age Tale Is Generous, Classical and Soul-Stirring |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/reviews/amrum-review-fatih-akin-cannes-1236400275/ |access-date=2026-02-14 |website=Variety |language=en-US |archive-date=16 February 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260216162337/https://variety.com/2025/film/reviews/amrum-review-fatih-akin-cannes-1236400275/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Hammond |first=Pete |date=2025-05-22 |title=‘Amrum’ Review: Fatih Akin’s German Coming-Of-Age Tale Set During Final Days Of WWII Is ‘Stand By Me’ In Shadow Of Fading Nazi Regime – Cannes Film Festival |url=https://deadline.com/2025/05/amrum-review-fatih-akin-movie-1236408216/ |access-date=2026-02-14 |website=Deadline |language=en-US |archive-date=13 February 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260213171433/https://deadline.com/2025/05/amrum-review-fatih-akin-movie-1236408216/ |url-status=live }}</ref> It was theatrically released in Germany on 9 October by [[Warner Bros. Bohm died two months after the film's theatrical release in Germany.<ref>[https://www.sat1regional.de/newsticker/hamburger-filmemacher-hark-bohm-ist-tot/ Filmemacher Hark Bohm stirbt im Alter von 86 Jahren] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251114210710/https://www.sat1regional.de/newsticker/hamburger-filmemacher-hark-bohm-ist-tot/ |date=14 November 2025 }} {{in lang|de}}</ref>

==Plot==
1945, in the final weeks of World War II, in a small village on the island of [[Amrum]] off Germany's [[North Sea]] coast. Twelve-year-old [[Hark Bohm|Nanning Bohm]], the eldest child in his family, works in the potato fields or gathers driftwood for firewood to help his mother feed the family. She is a staunch Nazi and in the later stages of pregnancy. He, his aunt Ena, and his two younger siblings had to flee to the island from bombed-out [[Hamburg]]. 

Nanning's father is an [[SS-Obersturmbannführer]] (lieutenant colonel) and is away at war; his wife is left to fend for herself on Amrum, while the villagers secretly listen to proscribed [[jazz]] on the radio. 

As the war draws to a close, Nanning faces new challenges. Since the birth of his sister and the death of Adolf Hitler, his mother has fallen into a deep depression and refuses to eat. Nanning tries to find creative ways to fulfill her craving for white bread with butter and honey, something he continues to struggle with throughout the movie, as the war has left the island with a severe shortage of all materials. Through bartering, he tries to obtain the coveted ingredients like butter and sugar. 

The boy gets to know the island, its inhabitants, and the [[North Frisian language]] spoken there further. However, while helping with seal hunting and gutting a rabbit, he often experiences close encounters with death. He learns that his uncle Theo, who gave him a whaling knife at birth, was forced to stay in America because of the Nazis, and that they murdered his fiancée, Ruth. Theo appears to him in a dream and confronts him: "You are not to blame, but you are still involved." As the younger of the two, he bears no personal guilt for his parents' Nazi past, but he cannot escape their legacy and the questions it raises.

==Production==
The film was first announced in 2022, and is based on the childhood memories of German director and screenwriter [[Hark Bohm]], a long-standing friend of director [[Fatih Akin]]. It is produced by Bombero International and Warner Bros. Film Productions Germany, in co-production with [[Rialto Film]].<ref>{{cite web|website=Deadline Hollywood|accessdate=25 March 2025|url=https://deadline.com/2024/04/fatih-akin-1945-drama-amrum-principal-photography-laura-tonke-diane-kruger-1235891373/|title=Fatih Akin's 1945 Drama 'Amrum' Kicks Off Principal Photography With Jasper Billerbeck, Laura Tonke & Diane Kruger|first=Melanie|last=Goodfellow|date=April 22, 2024|archive-date=22 April 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240422115629/https://deadline.com/2024/04/fatih-akin-1945-drama-amrum-principal-photography-laura-tonke-diane-kruger-1235891373/|url-status=live}}</ref> Bohm had initially planned to direct the film himself before handing directorial duties to Akin, who co-wrote the screenplay.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/diane-kruger-fatih-akin-film-amrum-1235878756/|website=Hollywood Reporter|accessdate=25 March 2025|title= Diane Kruger, Fatih Akin Reunite for 'Amrum'|first=Scott|last=Roxborough|date=22 April 2024}}</ref>

The cast is led by child actors Jasper Billerbeck and Kian Köppke, and also includes [[Laura Tonke]], [[Diane Kruger]], Lisa Hagmeister, [[Detlev Buck]], [[Matthias Schweighöfer]] and Lars Jessen.<ref name="lewis">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/global/fatih-akin-amrum-beta-cinema-1235977853/|website=Variety|accessdate=25 March 2025|date=Apr 22, 2024|title=Fatih Akin's 'Amrum' to Be Sold Internationally by Beta Cinema|first=Leo|last=Barraclough|archive-date=4 January 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250104150436/https://variety.com/2024/film/global/fatih-akin-amrum-beta-cinema-1235977853/|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Principal photography]] took place in [[Hamburg]], Germany, as well as in Denmark and the island of [[Amrum]], and began in April 2024.<ref>{{cite web|website=Europa.org|accessdate=25 March 2025|url=https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/461473/|title=Fatih Akin's new movie, Amrum, now shooting in Hamburg|first=Davide|last=Abbatescianni|date=24 April 2024|archive-date=19 February 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250219131740/https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/461473|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Release==
The film had its world premiere at the Cannes Premiere section of the [[2025 Cannes Film Festival]], on 15 May. It marked Akin's return to Cannes' Official Selection after 8 years.<ref name="Screenings Guide" /> It was also selected for the International competition section of the [[56th International Film Festival of India]] in November 2025.<ref name="ic:variety">{{Cite news |author=Naman Ramachandran |date=8 November 2025 |title=Miike Takashi’s ‘Sham,’ Rajkumar Periasamy’s ‘Amaran’ to Compete for Golden Peacock at India’s IFFI |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/festivals/miike-takashi-sham-golden-peacock-india-iffi-1236573068/ |access-date=9 November 2025 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |language=en |archive-date=21 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251221030008/https://variety.com/2025/film/festivals/miike-takashi-sham-golden-peacock-india-iffi-1236573068/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

It was released in German theaters on 9 October 2025 by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]].<ref name="lewis" />

[[Kino Lorber]] acquired the U.S. rights; the film was screened at Quad Cinema in New York City April 17–June 4, 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Goldsmith |first=Jill |date=2026-02-13 |title=Kino Lorber Sets Diane Kruger-Starring ‘Amrum’ For April Theatrical Release |url=https://deadline.com/2026/02/kino-lorber-sets-amrum-april-theatrical-release-1236717923/ |access-date=2026-02-14 |website=Deadline |language=en-US |archive-date=14 February 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260214175937/https://deadline.com/2026/02/kino-lorber-sets-amrum-april-theatrical-release-1236717923/ |url-status=live }}</ref> 

== Reception ==
===Box office===
It grossed $8 million at the German box office. Grossing $9.3 million worldwide.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Amrum |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt22507216/ |access-date=2026-02-14 |website=Box Office Mojo}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 98% based on 46 reviews, with an average rating of 7.4/10. The website's critical consensus states: "All the more emotionally powerful because of its dramatic restraint, ''Amrum'' is a painterly coming-of-age picture that thoughtfully expands filmmaker Fatih Akin's career-spanning exploration of German identity".<ref>https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/amrum</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average score]] of 76 out of 100, based on 14 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>https://www.metacritic.com/movie/amrum/?ftag=MCD-06-10aaa1c</ref>
