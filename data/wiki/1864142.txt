'''''The Beyond''''' ({{langx|it|…E tu vivrai nel terrore! L'aldilà|4=...And you will live in terror! The afterlife}}) is a 1981 <!-- Please source language tag. --> Italian [[supernatural horror]] film directed by [[Lucio Fulci]]. The [[Southern Gothic]]<ref name = "Gibron"/><ref>{{cite web|last1=Gibron|first1=Bill|title=Lucio Fulci's The Beyond (1981)|url=https://popmatters.com/post/lucio-fulcis-the-beyond-1981/|website=PopMatters|date=15 October 2007|access-date=26 July 2015}}</ref> work is based on an original story by [[Dardano Sacchetti]], starring [[Catriona MacColl]] and [[David Warbeck]]. Its plot follows a woman who inherits a hotel in rural [[Louisiana]] that was once the site of a horrific murder, and which may be a gateway to [[hell]]. It is the second film in Fulci's [[Gates of Hell trilogy|''Gates of Hell'' trilogy]] after ''[[City of the Living Dead]]'' (1980), and was followed by ''[[The House by the Cemetery]]'' (1981).{{sfn|Fonseca|2014|p=105}}

Filmed on location in and around [[New Orleans]] and at De Paolis Studios in Rome, in late 1980. Released theatrically in Italy in the spring of 1981, ''The Beyond'' did not see a North American release until late 1983 through Aquarius Releasing, which released an edited version of the film titled '''''7 Doors of Death'''''; this version featured an entirely different musical score and ran several minutes shorter than Fulci's original cut, and was branded a "[[video nasty]]" immediately upon its release in the UK. The original version of the film saw its first US release in September 1998 through a distribution partnership between [[Rolling Thunder Pictures]], [[Grindhouse Releasing]], and [[Noah Cowan#Cowboy Booking International and Cowboy Pictures|Cowboy Booking International]].

Following its release, the reception of ''The Beyond'' was polarized. Contemporary and retrospective critics have praised the film for its [[surrealism|surrealistic]] qualities, special effects, musical score, and cinematography, but note its narrative inconsistencies; horror filmmakers and surrealists have interpreted these inconsistencies as intentionally disorienting, supplementing the atmospheric tone and direction. ''The Beyond'' is ranked among Fulci's most celebrated films, and has gained an international [[cult following]] over the ensuing decades.<ref name=Biodrowski>{{cite web|url=http://cinefantastiqueonline.com/2009/02/beyond-the-1981-dvd-review|author=Biodrowski, Steve|title=Beyond, The (1981)- DVD Review|work=[[Cinefantastique|Cinfefantastique Online]]|date=20 February 2009|access-date=4 July 2015}}</ref>

==Plot==
In 1927, artist and [[warlock]] Schweick works on a painting in Room 36 of the Seven Doors Hotel in [[New Orleans]]. He protects one of the seven gates of [[hell]], which, if opened, will bring about the [[apocalypse|end of the world]] and the death of mankind. A [[lynching|lynch mob]] drags Schweick from his room and into the hotel's basement, killing him for practicing [[black magic]]. Meanwhile, a white-eyed woman reads from the ancient tome "[[List of Cthulhu Mythos books#Book of Eibon|Eibon]]", prophesizing the opening of one of the seven gates.

Decades later, in 1981, Liza Merrill inherits the hotel and moves from [[New York City|NYC]] to refurbish and reopen it. A worker glimpses a white-eyed woman through a window and falls off his scaffolding. Local doctor John McCabe takes the injured man to the hospital. The bell for Room 36 rings, but Liza dismisses it as a malfunction, as the hotel has yet to open. A plumber named Joe investigates the lack of running water. In the flooded basement, he uncovers a bricked-off area and accidentally opens the gate to hell. He is attacked by a ghoul, blinded, and killed. The bodies of both Joe and Schweick are discovered by a maid, Martha, and are taken to the local morgue.

While driving into town, Liza encounters a blind woman named Emily and her guide dog, Dicky. Emily warns Liza that reopening the hotel would be a mistake and that she should return to NYC. At a bar, John urges Liza to give up on the hotel project. However, she refuses, thinking it is her only chance at financial success.

Later, Emily tells Liza about Schweick and warns her not to enter Room 36. Upon examining Schweick's painting, she states it was part of his [[seance]], and his own death counted as a sacrifice to curse the land. After Liza scoffs at the warnings, Emily's hands begin to bleed, causing her to run with Dicky from the hotel in terror. Liza noticed that neither of them made audible footsteps as they left. Despite Emily's warning, Liza enters Room 36 and discovers the Eibon as well as Schweick's corpse nailed to the bathroom wall. She returns to the room with John, but the Eibon and the corpse are gone. Liza tells John about her encounter with Emily, but he insists that no blind woman lives in town. Furthermore, he says that the house where Liza claims Emily lives has been abandoned for years.

Liza's architect, Martin, visits the town library to inspect the hotel's [[blueprints]], which reveal an ample, unexplained space in the basement. Upon discovering this, he is knocked off his ladder by an unseen force, breaks his neck, and is [[paralysis|paralyzed]]. As he lies helpless on the floor, [[tarantula]]s eat him alive, and the blueprints gradually disappear from the paper, rendering it blank. Back at the hotel, Martha is cleaning the bathroom in Room 36 when Joe's corpse emerges from the bathtub water and kills her. John breaks into the old house where Emily is supposed to live. It is abandoned, but he finds the Eibon and begins to read it, learning that the hotel is one of the seven gates to hell.

Emily is confronted in her home by the animated corpses of Schweick and the other recently deceased. She commands Dicky to attack. Although the dog chases away the undead, he next turns upon Emily, killing her. Meanwhile, Liza returns to the basement and is attacked by an undead worker. In her escape, she runs into John at the entrance. Upon investigation, there is no sign of the undead worker, and Liza begins to question her sanity. The two drive to the hospital and find it deserted except for Dr. Harris, Joe's daughter, Jill, and a horde of the undead. Harris is killed by flying glass, and John dispatches Jill when she transforms and attacks Liza.

John and Liza escape down a staircase but discover they have again arrived in the basement. They proceed through the flooded labyrinth and stumble into a wasteland—the same landscape in Schweick's painting. No matter where they turn, they return to their starting point. They are ultimately blinded, just like Emily, and disappear.

==Pictorialist interpretations and themes==
John Thonen of ''[[Cinefantastique]]'' wrote that ''The Beyond'' has "a story structure akin to that of an advanced fever dream".<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Cinefantastique]]|page=120|year=1998|publisher=Frederick S. Clarke|author=Thonen, John|title=The Beyond: Resurrecting|issn=0145-6032}}</ref> Film scholar Wheeler Dixon similarly wrote that the "slight framing narrative is merely the excuse for Fulci to stage a series of macabre, distressing set pieces".{{sfn|Dixon|2000|p=74}} Writer Bill Gibron suggested that the film has a subtext of "slavery, witchcraft, mob justice—and perhaps the key to almost all Fulci narratives—revenge" at its core.<ref name = "Gibron">{{cite web|work=[[PopMatters]]|title=More than Just Gore: The Macabre Moral Compass of Lucio Fulci|author=Gibron, Bill|date=19 May 2010|url=https://www.popmatters.com/125846-more-than-just-gore-the-macabre-moral-compass-of-lucio-fulci-2496191887.html|access-date=30 August 2018}}</ref>

The concept of "the beyond", which the characters Liza and John enter in the film's final sequence, is interpreted by film writer Meagan Navarro as a statement on "the Catholic concept of purgatory".<ref name=butcher>{{cite web|work=Bloody Disgusting|author=Navarro, Meagan|date=24 February 2018|access-date=9 September 2018|title=Godfather of Gore Lucio Fulci's 'The Beyond'|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3485142/butcher-block-godfather-gore-lucio-fulcis-beyond/}}</ref> Fulci himself was a Catholic, and previous films of his dealt with aspects of his faith that troubled him, such as ''[[Don't Torture a Duckling]]'' (1972), which touched on corruption among clergy.{{sfn|O'Brien|2011|p=257}}

A prominent theme, according to Phillip L. Simpson, is that of [[visual impairment|blindness]] as a result of exposure to evil, specifically tied to the Book of Eibon: "The book, like many other (in)famous 'evil' books found in literature and cinema, is a physical, written record of valuable occult knowledge that attempts to codify—accompanied by dire warnings that careless or ignorant deployment of that power will result in horrific consequences—what is otherwise usually represented as literally 'unseeable'."{{sfn|Simpson|2018|p=245}} Simpson interprets the film's "pervasive images of blindness and eye mutilation" as being directly consequential to characters' exposure to the book.{{sfn|Simpson|2018|p=245}} Simpson points out that only Schweick, the warlock lynched in the film's 1927 prologue, and Emily, a "[[Oracle|seeress]] who transcends temporality", possess the "necessary sight" to interpret the book's content.{{sfn|Simpson|2018|p=249}}

==Production==
===Development and pre-production===
Producer [[Fabrizio De Angelis]] said that on his previous collaboration with Fulci, ''[[Zombi 2]]'' (1979), they had aspired to make "a comic book movie…&nbsp;that is, instead of being scared, people would laugh when they saw these zombies".<ref name=looking>{{cite interview |last1=Salvati |first1=Sergio |last2=Sacchetti |first2=Dardano |last3=De Angelis |first3=Fabrizio |author3-link=Fabrizio De Angelis |last4=Fulci |first4=Antonella |display-authors=etal |title=Looking Back: The Making of ''The Beyond'' |publisher=Grindhouse Releasing |medium=Blu-ray |series=Disc&nbsp;2 |year=2015 |oclc=959418475 |work=The Beyond}}</ref> Instead, audiences largely responded with fear, prompting them to make a straightforward horror film.<ref name=looking/> After making ''[[City of the Living Dead]]'' (1980), Fulci sought to make a follow-up film as part of a [[trilogy]] with the "Gates of Hell" being a unifying theme.<ref name=looking/> Simpson describes the trilogy as being loosely "connected by the trope of hapless mortals literally living on top of an entrance to Hell and then inadvertently falling into it".{{sfn|Simpson|2018|pages=246–247}}

The base concept for ''The Beyond'' was devised by Fulci, [[Giorgio Mariuzzo]], and [[Dardano Sacchetti]], with Sacchetti creating the film's original story.<ref name=looking/> A poster designed by [[Enzo Sciotti]] was produced based solely on the [[film treatment|treatment]] in hopes of selling the film to international markets, and the film's title was chosen based on a conversation between Fulci and De Angelis.<ref name=looking/> De Angelis recalled Fulci discussing the film's concept with him: "So he's telling me this story about a couple moving into a house, where underneath is hell. And I was like, 'What does this mean?'… there weren't any dead people, maybe killed… No, there was hell under that house!" And he said 'the beyond'. And when I heard 'The Beyond', that was already the title."<ref name=looking/> After receiving De Angelis's approval, Fulci requested that Sacchetti begin writing a full screenplay based on the brief treatment they had completed.<ref name=looking/> According to De Angelis, much of the plot was devised based on vague ideas Fulci had for various death scenes, as well as several key words that he felt unified his vision.<ref name=looking/> Some elements of the screenplay were derived in an arbitrary manner, such as the design of the Eibon symbol, which Fulci based on the shape of a trivial amateur tattoo his daughter had gotten on her arm.<ref name=looking/>

Sacchetti's conception of the "beyond" was based on his own ruminations on death, and the "suffering of being born condemned to death… [of being] born to be erased".<ref name=looking/> Sacchetti sought to depict the beyond as a hell full of dead souls, an otherworld existing "outside of [[Euclidean geometry]]".<ref name=looking/> Originally, the film's final sequence in which the characters enter the "beyond" was meant to take place at an amusement park, where the two main characters, now dead, are able to enjoy themselves in a "great amusement park of life".<ref name=looking/> Due to logistical restrictions, however, this was unable to be filmed, and the existing final sequence—in which the characters enter a vast desert landscape full of corpses—was devised by Sacchetti "on the spur of the moment".<ref name=looking/> In an interview, Fulci gave the film's budget as 580 million [[Italian lire]], half the amount that De Angelis would declare it cost.{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}}

===Casting===
Although Fulci originally intended to cast ''Zombi 2'' star [[Tisa Farrow]] as Liza Merrill,{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} English actress [[Catriona MacColl]] was chosen after having worked with him previously on ''City of the Living Dead''. She would go on to appear in the subsequent final film of Fulci's "Gates of Hell" trilogy, ''The House by the Cemetery'', as well.<ref name=back/> MacColl would later deem ''The Beyond'' to be her personal favorite film of the trilogy, stating that she remains drawn to the "decadent Italian macabre poetry" that she feels the film exudes.<ref name=intro>{{cite interview|last1=MacColl|first1=Catriona|display-authors=etal |title=Introduction by Catriona MacColl|publisher=Grindhouse Releasing|medium=Blu-ray|series=Disc 1|year=2015|oclc=959418475|work=The Beyond}}</ref>

[[David Warbeck]] was cast as Dr. John McCabe after he and Fulci became friends during the making of ''[[The Black Cat (1981 film)|The Black Cat]]'' (1981).{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} Originally, the roles of Joe and Professor Harris were to be played by [[Venantino Venantini]] and [[Ivan Rassimov]], respectively, but they were replaced by two of Fulci's friends, stage actor Tonino Pulci and ''Zombi 2'' co-star [[Al Cliver]], whom the director affectionately nicknamed "Tufus".{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} Partway during the shoot, French actor [[Antoine Saint-John]], who played the doomed painter Schweick, was replaced by Giovanni De Nava, who played the "zombified" version of the character;{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} De Nava is sometimes mistakenly credited for playing Joe.<ref name=looking/>

For the role of the ghostly Emily, [[Stefania Casini]] was originally considered, but Casini turned down the role as she did not want to wear the blinding [[contact lenses]] she would have been required to wear.{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} Fulci instead cast actress and model [[Cinzia Monreale]], who had previously starred in his [[Spaghetti Western]] ''[[Silver Saddle]]'' (1978).<ref name=emily>{{cite interview|last=Monreale|first=Cinzia|title=See Emily Play|publisher=Grindhouse Releasing|medium=Blu-ray|series=Disc 2|year=2015|oclc=959418475|work=The Beyond}}</ref> Monreale was drawn to the film as she felt Fulci's concept was "well written and full of mystery," and she had enjoyed her time working with him previously.<ref name=emily/>

===Filming===

The majority of ''The Beyond'' was filmed on location in late 1980 in [[New Orleans]], as well as the outlying cities of [[Metairie, Louisiana|Metairie]], [[Monroe, Louisiana|Monroe]], and [[Madisonville, Louisiana|Madisonville]]. Larry Ray, a New Orleans resident and member of the Louisiana Film Commission, was hired to help Fulci scout locations.<ref name=connection/> While scouting, Fulci took a liking to Ray and hired him as a production manager and producer's assistant.<ref name=connection/> Within New Orleans proper, filming was completed in the ''[[French Quarter|Vieux Carré]]'' district, as well as on the campus of [[Dillard University]].<ref name=fr/> The funeral sequence in the graveyard was shot at the [[Saint Louis Cemetery|Saint Louis Cemetery no.&nbsp;1]].<ref name=fr>{{cite web |work=LucioFulci.fr |url=http://www.luciofulci.fr/en/interviews/larry-ray-acteurassistant-production-lau-dela |title=Larry Ray – actor/assistant to the producer (The Beyond) |date=3 November 2012 |author=Grenier, Lionel |translator=Stefan Rousseau |access-date=10 September 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161007084920/http://www.luciofulci.fr/en/interviews/larry-ray-acteurassistant-production-lau-dela |archive-date=7 October 2016 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The historic Otis House near [[Lake Pontchartrain]], located within the [[Fairview-Riverside State Park]], served as the Seven Doors Hotel.<ref name=ray>{{cite web|work=LarryRay.com|url=http://www.larryray.com/the-beyond---behind-the.html|title= My Stunt Fall to a Bloody Death, First to Die From the Deadly Zombie Gaze - Rare Production Photos|last=Ray|first=Larry|publisher=Larry Ray|date=2014|access-date=10 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171206072706/http://www.larryray.com/the-beyond---behind-the.html|archive-date=6 December 2017}}</ref> During filming, the production designers aged the home's exteriors by spraying the siding with water and dark dye, as well as throwing cement and sand on the floors to make it appear dusty and dilapidated.<ref name=fr/> According to Ray, the sand damaged the original wood floors, and the production had to pay the State of Louisiana to restore them once filming wrapped.<ref name=fr/> The sequence in which Liza meets Emily on the bridge was shot on the [[Lake Pontchartrain Causeway]],<ref name=fr/> while Emily's house was the same New Orleans residence used in [[Louis Malle]]'s ''[[Pretty Baby (1978 film)|Pretty Baby]]'' (1978).<ref name=back>{{cite interview|title=Beyond and Back: Memories of Lucio Fulci|last=MacColl|first=Catriona|publisher=Grindhouse Releasing|year=2015|medium=[[Blu-ray]]|series=Disc 2|oclc=959418475|work=The Beyond}}</ref>

To achieve the film's stark visual style, cinematographer [[Sergio Salvati]] photographed the New Orleans exteriors using "warm colors" in hope of capturing the "sun, the heat, [and] the jazz" of the city.<ref name=looking/> Salvati contrasted the urban New Orleans settings with the "cooler" interiors of the hotel, which often feature cool blue, orange, and violet lighting.<ref name=looking/>

According to Ray, Fulci had no official shooting script present while filming ''The Beyond'',<ref name=connection/> only a treatment that ran three pages in length:<ref name=fr/>
{{blockquote|
The early concept script outline was only three pages with a story quite different from the one of the final movie. Fulci was always finding new ideas. Let me give you an example. {{convert|100|mi}} from the Otis House that we used as the exterior of the Hotel of the story there was a long and narrow lake. We had been given the authorisation to shoot the exterior as well as interior of this historic house. Lucio Fulci or Sergio Salvati had the idea to use the lake for the scene with the boats loaded with the angry mob with torches. This produced beautiful reflections on the water and smoke machines provided the perfect atmosphere. In the end, lots of these scenes were dreamed up on the spot and added as a combined work between Fulci, David Pash and the crew.
}}

The majority of the cast were English speakers, while Fulci spoke [[Italian language|Italian]].<ref name=fr/> Due to the language barrier, much of Fulci's direction was done with "miming, making faces and moving his body in order to make the actors understand what he wanted of them."<ref name=fr/> As he was a fluent Italian speaker, Ray served as Fulci's translator and interpreter throughout the shoot.<ref name=connection>{{cite interview |last=Ray |first=Larry |title=The New Orleans Connection |year=2015 |medium=Blu-ray |series=Disc&nbsp;2 |work=The Beyond |publisher=Grindhouse Releasing |oclc=959418475}}</ref>

After filming had completed in the United States, additional photography took place at De Paolis Studios in [[Rome]], primarily for the special-effects-intensive scenes. Among these were the interior shots of the film's opening sequence featuring the mob murder,<ref name=fr/> as well as interior replicas of Emily's home.<ref name=emily/> Some additional sequences featuring Emily's dog were also shot in Rome, which required the production to find a lookalike [[German Shepherd]] in Italy.<ref name=fr/> According to MacColl, the film's end sequence, in which she and Warbeck's characters enter "the beyond", was completed on their last day of shooting, on 22&nbsp;December 1980.<ref name=back/> The sequence was shot on an empty soundstage, which had leftover sand from a previous film,<ref name=looking/> and random civilians were hired off the street to appear as inanimate corpses lying in the sand.<ref name=back/> The sand was dampened with water, which, through evaporation, resulted in the natural fog effect present in the film.<ref name=looking/>

===Special effects===
''The Beyond'''s special effects were achieved via practical methods<ref name=real/> and have been noted by scholars as Fulci's "signature violent set pieces".{{sfn|Simpson|2018|p=257}} The majority of the special effects-intensive scenes were filmed on sound stages in Italy.<ref name=real/> Monreale recalled her time spent on set during the effects preparation as the most "intense" due to elaborate setups and hours spent properly achieving Fulci's desired visual outcome.<ref name=emily/> Among these for Monreale was her character's violent death sequence, in which she is viciously mauled by her dog.<ref name=emily/> To achieve the effect, a fake dog head was crafted, as well as a layered prosthetic neck which the dog tears open with its teeth.<ref name=emily/> This sequence alone took around three days to complete, with the dummy dog being manually [[puppet]]eered.<ref name=emily/> On set, Fulci jokingly referred to the fake dog head as "Puppola".<ref name=real/>

[[File:Cinzea Monreale in The Beyond trailer.jpg|thumb|The white contact lenses worn by several of the actors (Cinzia Monreale pictured) completely blocked their vision.]]
The white contact lenses worn by several actors in the film were made of glass and hand-painted with multiple shades of [[Enamel paint|white enamel]].<ref name=real/> They were described by both MacColl and Warbeck as being very uncomfortable; MacColl recalled that after completing the film's finale sequence, both she and Warbeck had difficulty removing them from their eyes.<ref name=back/> The lenses also obscured the wearer's vision, rendering them completely or nearly-completely blind.<ref name=emily/> Monreale expressed that wearing the lenses was extremely difficult for her; all of her scenes except one required her to wear them, and she had to rely on the film's makeup artist, Maurizio Trani, for assistance navigating the sets.<ref name=emily/> To minimize discomfort, Trani would regularly remove the lenses from the actors' eyes and disinfect them between takes.<ref name=real/>

One of the film's more elaborate special-effects sequences was the death scene of Martin Avery, who is attacked by a horde of tarantulas.<ref name=real>{{cite interview |last1=DeRossi |first1=Giannetto |last2=Trani |first2=Maurizio |year=2015 |title=Making it Real: The Special Effects of ''The Beyond'' |publisher=Grindhouse Releasing |medium=Blu-ray |series=Disc&nbsp;2 |oclc=959418475 |work=The Beyond}}</ref> According to Trani, crafting the sequence was "complicated" but "proved to be simpler than expected".<ref name=real/> Special effects designer Giannetto DeRossi created half of a prosthetic mouth for the actor, Michele Mirabella, from latex and a [[dental impression|dental cast]] he obtained from his dentist.<ref name=real/> While some of the spiders were real, the ones filmed biting at Avery's mouth and face were fake and controlled with handheld clamps.<ref name=real/> Close-up shots of Mirabella's real mouth were intercut with ones of his false mouth (including close-ups of a fake tongue) as it is bitten by the spiders, resulting in a seamless image of the attack.<ref name=real/>

The special effects during the film's first morgue scene in which a bottle of [[acid]] is poured over the face of Mary-Anne were created by effects artist Germano Natali.<ref name=real/> To achieve the melting effect of the face, real [[sulfuric acid]] was dumped over a cast of the actress's face, which was made of a mixture of wax and [[clay]]; sulfuric acid dissolves the latter substance.<ref name=real/> A similar dummy head was created to achieve the effect of the character of Joe's eyeball being gouged.<ref name=real/>

The [[crucifixion]] of Schweick, shown in the opening sequence, was also achieved via practical methods: holes were cut for Saint-John to conceal his forearms behind the cross, while dummy forearms filled with fake blood reservoirs were attached to the front of the cross.<ref name=real/> Two additional holes were cut at the ends of the dummy forearms, through which DeRossi inserted his own hands.<ref name=real/> The result appears as two seamless arms and allowed the crucifixion to look authentic, as the hands were motile and could writhe in reaction.<ref name=real/> Trani's hands appear onscreen as the nailer who completes the crucifixion.<ref name=real/>

===Music===
Fulci commissioned composer [[Fabio Frizzi]] to complete a musical score for the film.<ref name=rss>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|title=35 Greatest Horror Soundtracks|date=26 October 2016|author=Weingarten, Christopher R. |display-authors=etal |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/35-greatest-horror-soundtracks-modern-masters-gatekeepers-choose-126190/the-beyond-fabio-frizzi-1981-129006/|access-date=2 September 2018}}</ref> Commenting on writing the score, Frizzi said: "The distinctive aim of the film's soundtrack was to achieve an old goal of mine. I wanted to combine two different instrumental forms I had always loved: the band and the orchestra. When I started writing music some years before, I had learned to combine these two sounds, but for many reasons, the roles of strings and wind instruments were mainly created by keyboards. This time I decided to get serious."<ref name=rss/> The score features various instruments, including [[Mellotron]] and [[bass guitar|bass]], as well as orchestral and choral arrangements.<ref name=rss/> ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' reviewers noted that as the film progresses toward its conclusion, "Frizzi's score also darkens, growing heavy, underlining the inescapable fate of the characters."<ref name=rss/>

In 2016, ''Rolling Stone'' ranked Frizzi's score the 11th-best horror film score of all time.<ref name=rss/> Chris Alexander of [[ComingSoon.net]] also ranked the film's main theme, "Voci Dal Nulla" ({{langx|en|"Voices from the Void"}}), one of the greatest horror film themes ever composed.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[ComingSoon.net]]|author=Alexander, Chris|title=Frizzi 2 Fulci: A Look at Composer Fabio Frizzi's Work with Director Lucio Fulci|date=27 October 2015|url=https://comingsoon.net/horror/news/747349-frizzi-2-fulci-look-composer-fabio-frizzis-work-director-lucio-fulci|access-date=2 September 2018}}</ref> In 1981, the score was released in Italy on [[LP record|vinyl]] through Beat Records Company.<ref>{{cite AV media|title=L'Aldila|medium=Vinyl LP|year=1981|publisher=Beat Records Company|id= LPF 052}}</ref> On October 30, 2015, the independent record label Death Waltz issued the remastered score on vinyl.<ref>{{cite AV media|title=The Beyond: Original Motion Picture Score|publisher=Death Waltz|medium=Vinyl LP|year=2015|asin=B0170KG1HS|id=DW031LP}}</ref> A [[compact disc|CD]] version had been released previously in 2001 by Dagored Records.<ref>{{cite AV media|title=The Beyond: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack|asin= B00005N84Q|year=2001|publisher=Dagored Records|medium=CD}}</ref> Grindhouse Releasing now includes the soundtrack with the Blu-ray release in the USA.

On October 17, 2019, Eibon Press announced that Grindhouse Releasing had discovered the original recordings of the score for the US ''7 Doors of Death'' cut, and they had collaborated to release a limited-edition CD with a [[comic book]] adaptation of the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://eibonpress.com/blogs/news/the-beyond-issue-1-extras-revealed-on-sale-next-friday-oct-25th|title=The Beyond issue 1 Extras Revealed on sale Next Friday Oct 25th|date=17 October 2019|access-date=18 October 2019|archive-date=19 October 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191019004954/https://eibonpress.com/blogs/news/the-beyond-issue-1-extras-revealed-on-sale-next-friday-oct-25th|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Reception and legacy==
===Theatrical distribution===
''The Beyond'' was released theatrically in Italy on 29 April 1981,<ref name="am-arc">{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130829024513/https://allmovie.com/movie/the-beyond-v43842|url=https://allmovie.com:80/movie/the-beyond-v43842|title=The Beyond|access-date=August 14, 2017|last=Firsching|first=Robert|archive-date=August 29, 2013|work=[[AllMovie]]|url-status=dead}}</ref> where it grossed 747,615,662 lire on its initial run;{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=64}} film historian Roberto Curti described this as "OK business", noting that while these takings were less than that of several of Fulci's earlier horror films, the film was widely distributed in other countries, and was especially successful in Spain.{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=71}} In England, the film had difficulty with censors.{{sfn|Howarth|2015|p=225}} The [[British Board of Film Classification|BBFC]] passed it with an [[X rating]] demanding several cuts and subsequently it was considered a [[Video nasty]].{{sfn|Howarth|2015|p=225}} It would not be released in the United Kingdom uncut until 2001 on home video.{{sfn|Howarth|2015|p=225}} In Germany, the film was released under the title ''Die Geisterstadt der Zombies'' ({{translation|The Ghost Town of Zombies}}).{{sfn|Stine|2003|p=20}} The German theatrical version was the only version of the film in which the pre-credit sequence was printed in color; in the Italian and international versions, the sequence was printed in [[Photographic print toning#Sepia toning|sepia]].{{sfn|Curti|2019|p=72}}

''The Beyond'' did not see a U.S. release until 1983, when it was acquired for theatrical distribution by Terry Levine of Aquarius Releasing,{{sfn|Weldon|1996|p=493}} a New York City-based distributor who had previously handled regional distribution for [[John Carpenter]]'s ''[[Halloween (1978 film)|Halloween]]'' (1978) and ''[[Faces of Death]]''.{{sfn|Muir|2012b|p=15}}<ref name=beyondit>{{cite interview|publisher=Grindhouse Releasing|title=Beyond Italy: Louis Fuller and the Seven Doors of Death|year=2015|medium=Blu-ray|series=Disc 2|work=The Beyond|oclc=959418475|last=Levene|first=Terry}}</ref> Levine purchased the U.S. distribution rights for around $35,000,<ref name=beyondit/> and spent an additional $10,000 on additional post-production work, which included replacing Frizzi's music with a new score by Mitch and Ira Yuspeh,{{sfn|Fonseca|2014|p=105}}{{sfn|Grant|1999|p=29}} as well as truncating it by several minutes to achieve an R-rating. While Levine was reluctant to trim the gory scenes, he had Frizzi's score replaced due to the limitations of its mono recording, as he wanted to release the film with a prestigious [[Dolby Surround]] mix.{{sfn|Weldon|1996|p=493}} Aquarius released this alternate version of the film in the USA on 11 November 1983 under the title ''7 Doors of Death''.<ref>{{cite news|work=[[New York Daily News]]|title=Movies|date=11 November 1983|page=119|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/23663221/daily_news/|via=Newspapers.com|location=New York City}} {{open access}}</ref> The title change was due to Levine's belief that the original title was too nebulous, and that the film's "Seven Doors" plot device was a more interesting narrative hook that would intrigue audiences.<ref name=beyondit/> To promote this release, Levine screened the film for [[Kim Henkel]] and [[Tobe Hooper]], the respective co-writer and director of ''[[The Texas Chain Saw Massacre]]'' (1974), who granted laudatory quotes about the film to be used in TV and print advertisements.<ref name=beyondit/> Most of the cast and crew names for this version were also "[[Anglicisation|anglicised]]" to appeal to U.S. audiences, with Fulci being credited as "Louis Fuller".<ref name=beyondit/> ''7 Doors of Death'' was a commercial success, grossing $455,652 during its first week of release;<ref name=gallery>{{cite video|publisher=Grindhouse Releasing|title=Images from ''The Beyond''|year=2015|medium=Blu-ray|series=Disc 1|work=The Beyond|oclc=959418475}}</ref> Levine estimated that the film turned him a profit of roughly $700,000.<ref name=beyondit/>

The uncut version of ''The Beyond'' was not made commercially available in the U.S. until after Fulci's death in 1996;{{sfn|Howarth|2015|p=225}} the North American distribution rights to the film were acquired by [[Bob Murawski]] and [[Sage Stallone]] for their company [[Grindhouse Releasing]], which partnered with [[Quentin Tarantino]] and Jerry Martinez's [[Rolling Thunder Pictures]] and [[Noah Cowan]]'s Cowboy Booking International to re-release the film through a series of [[Midnight movie|midnight screenings]] that began in [[Los Angeles|L.A.]], [[New York City|NYC]] and five other locations on 12 June 1998, followed by a national roll-out. Remarking on Rolling Thunder's stake in the film, Cowboy VP John Vanco noted that "Fulci is one of [Tarantino and Martinez's] favorite directors", and the pair "wanted to have someone with a little more kind of midnight movie specialized kind of background [to help distribute the film]".<ref>{{cite web|work=[[IndieWire]]|url=https://www.indiewire.com/1998/04/rolling-thunder-grindhouse-and-cowboy-go-beyond-dinner-and-a-movie-to-midnight-82944/|title=Rolling Thunder, Grindhouse and Cowboy Go 'Beyond' Dinner and a Movie, To Midnight|access-date=14 March 2020|date=8 April 1998|author=Rabinowitz, Mark}}</ref> By July 26, the re-release had made $123,843 at the box office.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0082307/?ref_=bo_se_r_1|title=The Beyond (1981)|access-date=14 March 2020}}</ref> Murawski's ownership of ''The Beyond'''s U.S. rights proved useful while editing [[Spider-Man|Peter Parker]]'s transformation scene in [[Sam Raimi]]'s ''[[Spider-Man (2002 film)|Spider-Man]]'' (2002): due to a lack of money to create original footage, he inserted a brief [[close-up]] shot of a spider from Martin's death scene into the sequence, along with footage from Raimi's ''[[Darkman]]'' (1990).<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3433204/shot-fulcis-beyond-raimis-spider-man/|title=There Was a Shot from Fulci's 'The Beyond' in Raimi's 'Spider-Man'?!|access-date=14 March 2020|date=19 April 2017|author=Squires, John}}</ref> Grindhouse gave the film a second theatrical re-release in North America to celebrate its 24th anniversary, starting on 9 February 2015 at the [[Alamo Drafthouse Cinema]] in [[Yonkers, New York|Yonkers]], and ending on 27 March 2015 in the [[Music Box Theatre (Chicago)|Music Box Theatre]] in [[Chicago]].<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Dread Central]]|url=https://dreadcentral.com/news/87400/the-beyonds-coast-to-coast-trek-rolls-on-new-york-city-january-30-31-coast-coast-trek-rolls/|title=The Beyond's Coast-to-Coast Trek Rolls On|access-date=9 September 2018|date=29 January 2015|author=Barton, Steve|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151025010627/https://dreadcentral.com/news/87400/the-beyonds-coast-to-coast-trek-rolls-on-new-york-city-january-30-31-coast-coast-trek-rolls/|archive-date=25 October 2015}}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
====Contemporaneous====
{{Quote box
| width = 20em
| quote = People who blame ''The Beyond'' for its lack of story have not understood that it's a film of images, which must be received without any reflection. They say it is very difficult to interpret such a film, but it is very easy to interpret a film with threads: Any idiot can understand Molinaro's ''[[La Cage aux Folles (film)|La Cage aux Folles]]'', or even Carpenter's ''[[Escape from New York]]'', while ''The Beyond'' or Argento's ''[[Inferno (1980 film)|Inferno]]'' are absolute films.
| source = Lucio Fulci on ''The Beyond''{{'}}s reception{{sfn|Howarth|2015|p=223}}
}}

Upon the film's 1983 release in the United States as ''7 Doors of Death'', critic [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times|LA Times]]'' deemed the film visually "elegant" but noted: "as a thriller of the occult it's overly familiar, just another rotting-flesh ghoul parade".<ref>{{cite news |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=1 May 1984 |author=Thomas, Kevin |author-link=Kevin Thomas (film critic) |title='Fire' and 'Death' a Pair of Silly Scarers |page=3 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/23663237/the_los_angeles_times/ |location=Los Angeles, California |via=Newspapers.com}} {{open access}}</ref> Bill Kelley of the ''[[Sun-Sentinel]]'' similarly praised the film's visual elements, including the sepia prologue, but added: "The problem is, whenever someone in the film is trying to act, the camera is recording something that's really not worth seeing", ultimately classing it as a "Z-grade horror movie".<ref>{{cite news |author=Kelley, Bill |page=6D |date=24 January 1984 |work=[[Sun-Sentinel]] |location=Fort Lauderdale, Florida |title='Doors of Death' should be under lock and key |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/23663256/south_florida_sun_sentinel/ |via=Newspapers.com}} {{open access}}</ref>

The ''[[Akron Beacon Journal]]''{{'}}s Bill O'Connor criticized the plot for a lack of coherence, writing: "People get killed all over the hotel. Then, after they're killed, they get ugly.&nbsp;... We never know why they get killed or why they get ugly, which leads me to suspect that maybe this is an art film. At the end of the movie, the dead walk.&nbsp;... Then the people leave the movie theater. They look just like the dead people who walked out of the morgue. Maybe this is not an art movie. Maybe this is a documentary."<ref>{{cite news |work=[[Akron Beacon Journal]] |location=Akron, Ohio |title=Film 'Seven Doors of Death' just isn't worth your time |author=O'Connor, Bill |date=16 March 1985 |page=A8 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/23663277/the_akron_beacon_journal/ |via=Newspapers.com}} {{open access}}</ref>

Tim Pulleine (''[[The Monthly Film Bulletin]]'') stated that the film allows for "two or three visually striking passages-and granting that, from [[Mario Bava|Bava]] onwards, narrative concision has not been the strong suit for Italian horror movies—the film is still completely undone by its wildly disorganized plot".<ref name="mfb">{{cite magazine |magazine=[[Monthly Film Bulletin]] |title=…E tu vivrai nel terrore! L'Aldila (The Beyond) |page=243 |volume=48 |issue=564 |year=1981 |publisher=[[British Film Institute]] |location=London |last=Pulleine |first=Tim}}</ref> The review also critiqued the dub, noting its "sheer ineptitude".<ref name="mfb"/>

====Retrospective====
Upon its 1998 re-release, critic [[Roger Ebert]] deemed it a film "filled with bad dialogue" and criticized it for having an incoherent plot.<ref>{{cite web|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-beyond-1998|title=The Beyond|authorlink=Roger Ebert|author=Ebert, Roger|date=July 3, 1998}}</ref>{{sfn|Ebert|2000|pages=41–42}} In 2000, he included the film in a book of his "most-hated" movies.{{sfn|Ebert|2000|pages=41–42}}

On the [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''The Beyond'' holds a 75% approval rating based on 24 critic reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/beyond/ |title=E tu vivrai nel terrore - L'aldilà (The Beyond) (1981) |publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=5 December 2025}}</ref>

[[AllMovie]] called the film a "surreal and bloody horror epic" and labeled it "Italian horror at its nightmarish extreme."<ref name=guarisco>{{cite web |url=https://allmovie.com/movie/the-beyond-v43842/review |title=The Beyond (1981) |first=Donald| last=Guarisco |publisher=[[AllMovie]] |access-date=25 June 2012}}</ref>

[[DVD Talk]] said, "The likeable main characters, the sense of place (and anti-place) make it work for me -- and I thought I had plenty of sales resistance to modern guts 'n' gore horror."<ref name=DT>{{cite web|date=May 2, 2015|archivedate=May 6, 2015|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s4796beyo.html|title=The Beyond|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150506111044/https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s4796beyo.html|publisher=[[DVD Talk]]|author=Erickson, Glenn}}</ref>

[[Time Out (magazine)|''Time Out'' London]], alternatively, called it "a shamelessly artless horror movie whose senseless story—a girl inherits a spooky, seedy hotel which just happens to have one of the seven doors of Hell in its cellar—is merely an excuse for a poorly connected series of sadistic tableaux of torture and gore."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://timeout.com/film/reviews/67705/the_beyond.html |title=The Beyond (1981) |work=Time Out London|access-date=25 June 2012 |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131109111308/https://timeout.com/london/film/the-beyond|archive-date=9 November 2013}}</ref>

Critic [[John Kenneth Muir]] wrote in ''Horror Films of 1980s'': "Fulci's films may be dread-filled excursions into surrealism and dream imagery, but in the real world, they don't hang together, and ''The Beyond'' is Exhibit A."{{sfn|Muir|2012a|pages=351–352}} 

Bill Gibron of ''[[PopMatters]]'', wrote in 2007:

{{blockquote|''The Beyond'' is an incoherent, chaotic combination of Italian terror and monster movie grave robbing that is almost saved by its bleak, atmospheric ending. It is a wretched gore fest sprinkled with wonderfully evocative touches. It has more potential than dozens of past and present Hollywood horror films, yet finds ways to squander and squelch each and every golden gruesome opportunity. It's a movie that gets better with multiple viewings, familiarity lessening the startling goofiness of some of the dialogue and dubbing. It is a film that is far more effective in recollection than it is as an actual viewing experience. It would probably work best as a silent movie, stripped of the illogical scripting, stupendously redundant [[Goblin (band)|Goblin]]-in-training soundtrack drones, and obtuse aural cues.<ref name=gibron2007/>}}

In the years since its release, ''The Beyond'' has acquired a [[cult following]].<ref name=gibron2007>{{cite web|work=[[PopMatters]]|url=https://www.popmatters.com/lucio-fulcis-the-beyond-1981-2496205726.html|author=Gibron, Bill|title=Lucio Fulci's The Beyond (1981)|access-date=13 September 2018|date=15 October 2007}}</ref> ''Time Out'' London conducted a poll with several authors, directors, actors and critics who have worked within the horror genre to vote for their top horror films. {{As of|2019|October|1}}, ''The Beyond'' placed at number 64 on ''Time Out'' London's top 100 list.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.timeout.com/london/film/best-horror-films|work=Time Out London|access-date=2019-10-19|title=The 100 best horror films - the scariest movies ranked by experts|author=Tom Huddleston |author2=Cath Clarke |author3=Dave Calhoun |author4=Nigel Floyd |author5=Alim Kheraj |author6=Phil de Semlyen|date=2019-10-01|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191019093031/https://www.timeout.com/london/film/best-horror-films|archive-date=2019-10-19}}<br />{{cite web|url=https://timeout.com/london/film/best-horror-films?pageNumber=6|work=Time Out London|access-date=13 April 2014|title=The 100 best horror films|author=Tom Huddleston |author2=Cath Clarke |author3=Dave Calhoun |author4=Nigel Floyd|date=2016-09-19|quote=The best horror films and movies of all time, voted for by over 100 experts including Simon Pegg, Stephen King and Alice Cooper|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161214033243/https://www.timeout.com/london/film/best-horror-films?pageNumber=6|archive-date=14 December 2016}}</ref>

Film critic Steven Jay Schneider ranked the film number 71 in his book ''101 Horror Movies You Must See Before You Die'' (2009).{{sfn|Schneider|2009|pages=97–98}}

===Home video===
On 10 October 2000, [[Grindhouse Releasing]] co-distributed the film in collaboration with [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]] on [[DVD-Video|DVD]] in both a limited-edition tin-box set, and a standard DVD.<ref name=ab/> Both releases featured the fully uncut 89-minute version of the film.<ref name=ab1>{{cite AV media|publisher=Anchor Bay Entertainment|year=2000|medium=DVD|title=The Beyond|oclc= 55591908}}</ref> There were only 20,000 limited-edition sets released for purchase. The limited-edition set was packaged in a tin box with alternative cover artwork, including an informative booklet on the film's production as well as various miniature poster replications.<ref name=ab>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/1113/beyond-the/|title=The Beyond|last=Tyner|first=Adam|work=[[DVD Talk]]|date=3 October 2000|access-date=10 July 2015}}</ref> The same year, Diamond Entertainment released a DVD edition bearing the ''7 Doors of Death'', which in spite of its usage of the US release title, is uncut at 89 minutes and sourced from the film's laserdisc.<ref name=diamond>{{cite AV media|title=Seven Doors of Death|year=2000|publisher=Diamond Entertainment|oclc=74454943|medium=DVD}}</ref>

A [[Blu-ray]] version of the film was released in Australia on 20 November 2013.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.ezydvd.com.au/blu-ray/cinema-cult-the-beyond/dp/6144560|title=Cinema Cult: The Beyond at EzyDVD|work=[[EzyDVD]]|access-date=25 August 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924052234/http://www.ezydvd.com.au/blu-ray/cinema-cult-the-beyond/dp/6144560|archive-date=24 September 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref> Grindhouse Releasing, the film's North American distributor, released the film in 2015 on Blu-ray in the USA, featuring two Blu-ray discs as well as a [[CD]] soundtrack. The distributors also wanted to restore the "7 Doors" version, but the original print of that version was presumed lost; trailers and TV spots for it, along with an interview with Aquarius owner Terry Levene explaining the "Americanization" process, were included as special features instead. Levine also stated that he is content with the original version being praised and preserved, and admitted that he only made the ''7 Doors'' version as a way to gain American interest.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/84585/grindhouse-releasings-beyond-blu-ray-detailed/|author=Barton, Steve|date=7 January 2015|title=Grindhouse Releasing's The Beyond Blu-ray Detailed|access-date=13 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170810003838/https://dreadcentral.com/news/84585/grindhouse-releasings-beyond-blu-ray-detailed/|archive-date=10 August 2017|work=Dread Central}}</ref> {{as of|2018}}, a cut version of the film available for streaming via [[Amazon Video]] under the ''7 Doors of Death'' title runs approximately 84 minutes.<ref name=amazon>{{cite web|work=Amazon Video|url=https://www.amazon.com/gp/video/detail/B075KLHRQ9|title=Seven Doors of Death|access-date=10 September 2018}}</ref>

In 2020, Shameless Screen Entertainment released ''The Beyond'' on Blu-ray for the UK market, sourced from a [[2K resolution|2K]] scan of the original elements, including the film's original color prologue. Via [[seamless branching]] with the film or in a separate comparison video, four versions of the prologue are presented on the disc: the standard sepia version, the color version, a [[black and white]] version, and a variant of the color version featuring a digitally-created yellow tint, which is intended to replicate Salvati's original vision for the sequence ― that of "an old, yellowed photograph, something outside time, establishing a gap between the prologue and the present day as though they were two separate films, divided by an infinite arc of time indicating the Dimension of the Afterwards". Aside from two previously released [[Audio commentary|audio commentaries]] — one with MacColl and Warbeck, and another with Salvati — the disc's special features include new interviews with Monreale, Mirabella, and Mariuzzo, and behind-the-scenes footage of Fulci from the filming of ''[[Demonia (film)|Demonia]]''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.shameless-films.com/product/the-beyond-blu-ray/|title=The Beyond Blu-Ray - Shameless Films|work=Shameless Screen Entertainment|access-date=31 March 2021}}</ref>

In 2025, a 6-disc UHD box set was released in North America by Grindhouse Releasing, which included a 4K color-graded restoration of the film and a newer "Composers Cut" by Fabio Frizzi, with the color intro as in the German release. Frizzi was able to redo his intended score with a larger orchestra and choir, and a CD of the OST is in the box set as well. A DVD of the "7 Doors of Death" Americanized cut, along with interviews by Terry Levine, was included, after the original one-inch analog video master was rediscovered. This boxset included a case resembling the "Book of Eibon", some limited editions came with an "Eye of Eibon" prop that was given away in the 1998 re-release, and exclusive editions had an action figure of the character "Joe the Plumber".<ref>{{cite web|access-date=29 April 2025 |title=The Beyond (1981) - UHD Deluxe Edition (USA - Canada Only) |url=https://shop.grindhousereleasing.com/products/the-beyond-1981-uhd-deluxe-edition-1 |work=Grindhouse Releasing}}</ref>
