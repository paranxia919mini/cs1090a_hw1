'''''Swimming to Cambodia''''', also known as '''''Spalding Gray's Swimming to Cambodia''''', is a 1987 American performance monologue written by and starring [[Spalding Gray]], and directed by [[Jonathan Demme]].<ref name=NYT>{{cite web|work=[[The New York Times]]|title=FILM: SPALDING GRAY'S 'SWIMMING TO CAMBODIA'|authorlink=Janet Maslin|first=Janet|last=Maslin|date=March 13, 1987|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20131216020620/http://www.nytimes.com/1987/03/13/movies/film-spalding-gray-s-swimming-to-cambodia.html?pagewanted=2|archivedate=December 16, 2013|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1987/03/13/movies/film-spalding-gray-s-swimming-to-cambodia.html}}</ref> The performance film is of Gray's play and monologue, which centered on such themes as his trip to [[Southeast Asia]] to create the role of the [[List of diplomatic missions of the United States|U.S. Ambassador's aide]] in the film ''[[The Killing Fields (film)|The Killing Fields]]'', the [[Cold War]], [[Cambodia]] [[Year Zero (political notion)|Year Zero]], and his search for his "perfect moment."<ref name=NYT/>  It was nominated for Best Feature at the 1988 [[3rd Independent Spirit Awards|Independent Spirit Awards]]<ref name=LAT>{{cite web|author=Voland, John|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-02-15-ca-28845-story.html|title=MOVIES - Feb. 15, 1988|date=February 15, 1988|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=March 14, 2025}}</ref> along with Best Director, Best Actor and Best Screenplay.

==Background==
Over a period of two years, Gray originally developed ''Swimming to Cambodia'' as a theatre performance piece.  The original running time of the performance was four hours, and it was presented over two nights. Gray received a Special Citation for this work at the 1985 [[Obie Award]]s.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.obieawards.com/?s=spalding|title=Spalding Gray|website=obieawards.com}}</ref>

In 2001, Gray revived ''Swimming to Cambodia,'' performing it on stage in [[Los Angeles]],<ref>{{cite web|newspaper=Los Angeles Times|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2002-feb-21-wk-shirley21-story.html|author=Shirley, Don|title=Sinking Feeling in 'Swimming to Cambodia' Revival|date=February 21, 2002}}</ref> [[Chicago]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/2002/03/12/spalding-grays-new-reality/|newspaper=[[Chicago Tribune]]|title=Spalding Gray's new reality|date=August 20, 2021}}</ref> [[San Francisco]],<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Playbill]]|title=After New York, Gray Is Swimming to Cambodia in San Francisco Dec. 26-31|date=December 26, 2001|author=Ehren, Christine|url=https://playbill.com/article/after-new-york-gray-is-swimming-to-cambodia-in-san-francisco-dec-26-31-com-100496}}</ref> and [[Albany, New York]].<ref>{{cite web|author=Lefkowitz, David|work=Playbill|url=https://playbill.com/article/spalding-gray-goes-back-to-swimming-to-cambodia-nov-14-dec-12-oob-com-99340|title=Spalding Gray Goes Back to Swimming to Cambodia|date=October 24, 2001}}</ref>

==Content==
The opening shots of the film show Gray walking toward the [[Performing Garage]] in New York City. He enters, and after passing the audience, takes a seat behind a table. On the table are a glass of water, a microphone, and a notebook, the latter brought by Gray. Behind him are two pull-down maps. One is a map of Southeast Asia and the other is a diagram of the bombing of Cambodia, which Gray explains was called [[Operation Menu#Breakfast|Operation Breakfast]]. There is also a back-lit projection screen showing a photograph of a beach.

Gray's monologue describes his experiences filming a small role in the film ''[[The Killing Fields (film)|The Killing Fields]]'', and the then-recent history of Cambodia up through the rise to power of the [[Khmer Rouge]] and the [[Cambodian genocide]]. Three scenes from ''The Killing Fields'' that feature Gray are shown at various points in the film.

''The Killing Fields{{'}}'' [[second unit]] cinematographer [[Ivan Strasburg]] and Gray's then girlfriend [[Renée Shafransky]] are featured characters that Gray discusses throughout the film.<ref>{{cite web|publisher=[[IFC (American TV channel)|IFC]]|url=https://www.ifc.com/blogs/parker-gail-swimming-to-cambodia--1000616|title=Everything You Need to Know About the Movie That Inspired "Parker Gail's Location is Everything"|date=September 27, 2016}}</ref>

==Production==
The soundtrack for the film was composed and performed by [[Laurie Anderson (performance artist)|Laurie Anderson]],<ref name=NYT/> who would also score Gray's follow-up film, ''[[Monster in a Box]].''<ref>{{cite web|newspaper=The New York Times|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/06/05/movies/review-film-a-monster-dense-with-meaning.html|title=Review/Film; A 'Monster' Dense With Meaning|authorlink=Vincent Canby|author=Canby, Vincent|date=June 5, 1992}}</ref>

Gray later provided the voice of a TV interviewer for Anderson's 1986 short film, ''[[What You Mean We?]]''<ref>{{cite web|publisher=[[Paley Center for Media]]|date=September 1, 1986|url=https://www.paleycenter.org/collection/item?q=first&p=415&item=T91%3A0120|title=ALIVE FROM OFF CENTER: WHAT YOU MEAN "WE"? (TV)}}</ref> No soundtrack album was released; Anderson later reused music from the film for a series of "Personal Service Announcements" which she produced in 1989 to promote her album, ''[[Strange Angels (Laurie Anderson album)|Strange Angels]].''

While [[Sam Waterston]] and Ira Wheeler are credited as cast in this film, they appear only in clips used from ''[[The Killing Fields (film)|The Killing Fields]].''

==Critical reception==
[[Janet Maslin]] writing for ''[[The New York Times]]'', "Mr. Gray's feature-length monologue brings people, places and things so vibrantly to life that they're very nearly visible on the screen."<ref name=NYT/>

Sean Burns writing for [[WBUR]], "Whenever current events get too insane and overwhelming, I take solace in the film and Gray’s quixotic search for beauty and the sublime in a world where unspeakable horrors and atrocities happen every day. As you might imagine, it’s been in pretty heavy rotation lately."<ref name=W>{{cite web|title=Spalding Gray's 'Swimming to Cambodia' is back at the Brattle 40 years later|date=August 12, 2025|author=Burns, Sean|publisher=[[WBUR-FM|WBUR]]|url=https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/08/12/spalding-grays-swimming-to-cambodia-is-back-at-the-brattle-40-years-later}}</ref>
