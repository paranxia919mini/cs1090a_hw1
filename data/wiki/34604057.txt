'''''Inside Llewyn Davis''''' ({{IPAc-en|ˈ|l|uː|ɪ|n}}) is a 2013 [[period drama|period]] [[black comedy]] [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] written, directed, produced, and edited by [[Coen brothers|Joel and Ethan Coen]]. Set in 1961, the film follows one week in the life of [[Llewyn Davis]], played by [[Oscar Isaac]] in his breakthrough role, a folk singer struggling to achieve musical success while keeping his life in order. The supporting cast includes [[Carey Mulligan]], [[John Goodman]], [[Garrett Hedlund]], [[F. Murray Abraham]], [[Justin Timberlake]] and [[Adam Driver]].

Though Davis is a fictional character, the story was partly inspired by folk singer [[Dave Van Ronk]]'s autobiography.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.insidellewyndavis.com/about/before-the-flood |title=Before the Flood: Llewyn Davis, David Van Ronk, and the Village Folk Scene of 1961 |last=Wald |first=Elija |website=Inside Llewyn Davis (official site) |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140209033657/http://www.insidellewyndavis.com/about/before-the-flood |archive-date=February 9, 2014 |url-status=dead |access-date=August 26, 2018}}</ref> Most of the folk songs performed in the film are sung in full and recorded live. [[T Bone Burnett]] was the executive music producer. [[Principal photography]] took place in early 2012, primarily in [[New York City]]. The film, an [[international co-production]] among companies in France, the United Kingdom and the United States, was financed by [[StudioCanal]] before it received an American [[film distributor|distributor]].

''Inside Llewyn Davis'' premiered at the [[2013 Cannes Film Festival]] on May 19, 2013, where it won the [[Grand Prix (Cannes Film Festival)|Grand Prix]]. The film was theatrically released in France on November 6, 2013, and in the United Kingdom on January 24, 2014, by StudioCanal. It was given a [[limited release]] by [[CBS Films]] in the United States on December 6, 2013, before opening in a wide release on January 10, 2014. The film received critical acclaim and was nominated for two [[Academy Awards]] ([[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] and [[Academy Award for Best Sound Mixing|Best Sound Mixing]]) and three [[Golden Globe Awards]] for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy|Best Motion Picture – Comedy or Musical]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy|Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy]] (Isaac), and [[Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song|Best Original Song]]. ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' has been held in high critical esteem since its release as one of the [[greatest films ever made]], being voted the 11th-best film of the 21st century in both a [[BBC's 100 Greatest Films of the 21st Century|2016 BBC poll]] and a 2017 ''[[The New York Times]]'' list.<ref name="bbc.com">{{cite web|title=The 21st Century's 100 greatest films|url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films|website=bbc.com|date=August 23, 2016 |access-date=August 31, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last1=Dargis|first1=Manohla|last2=Scott|first2=A.O.|title=The 25 Best Films of the 21st Century...So Far|url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2017/06/09/movies/the-25-best-films-of-the-21st-century.html|website=The New York Times|date=June 9, 2017 |access-date=July 8, 2017}}</ref> The film was ranked 14 on ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' magazine's list of "50 Best Movies of the 2010s" and 2 on ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]''{{'}}s list of the "50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far)."<ref>{{cite magazine|title=50 Best Movies of the 2010s|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-lists/best-movies-decade-2010s-910770/cold-war-2018-910867/|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|date=December 18, 2019 }}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Frosch|first1=Jon|last2=Rooney|first2=David|last3=Linden|first3=Sheri|last4=Gyarkye|first4=Lovia|last5=Felperin|first5=Leslie|last6=Mintzer|first6=Jordan|date=April 6, 2023|title=Hollywood Reporter Critics Pick the 50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far)|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-movies-21st-century/weekend-2011/|magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=November 16, 2023}}</ref>

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries should be 400-700 words for feature films -->
In February 1961, [[Llewyn Davis]] is a struggling folk singer in New York City's [[Greenwich Village]]. His solo album ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' is not selling; he is penniless and sleeps on acquaintances' couches. After playing [[The Gaslight Café]] one night, he is beaten up in the alley behind the café by a man in a suit.
	
Llewyn awakens in the apartment of two friends, the Gorfeins. As he leaves, their ginger cat escapes and is locked out. He takes it to the apartment of Jim and Jean Berkey, where Jean reluctantly allows Llewyn to stay the night. Jean tells Llewyn that she is pregnant, and that he could be the father. The next morning, Llewyn opens a window and the Gorfeins' cat escapes. Later, Jean asks Llewyn to pay for an abortion, though she is upset it may be Jim's child she is terminating.

Llewyn visits his sister to borrow money. Instead, she gives him a box of his belongings, which he tells her to discard. She mentions that he could make money by returning to the [[United States Merchant Marine|Merchant Marine]]. On Jim's invitation, Llewyn records a space travel-themed [[novelty song]] with Jim and Al Cody. Needing money for the abortion, Llewyn turns down royalties in favor of an immediate $200. He makes an appointment for the abortion, only to learn that payment will not be necessary. He already paid for the same procedure two years earlier on behalf of another woman who decided to keep the child.

Llewyn captures what he believes to be the Gorfeins' ginger cat and returns it to them that evening. Asked to perform after dinner, he reluctantly plays "[[Dink's Song|Fare Thee Well]]", a song he had recorded with his old partner Mike Timlin. When Mrs. Gorfein sings Mike's harmony, Llewyn angrily tells her not to. She leaves the table crying, then returns with the cat, which is not theirs. Llewyn leaves with the cat.

Llewyn drives to Chicago with two musicians: [[beat poet]] Johnny Five and jazz musician Roland Turner. During the trip, he discloses that Mike died by suicide.

At a roadside restaurant, Roland collapses from a heroin overdose. The three stop on the side of the highway to rest. When a police officer tells them to move on, he suspects that Johnny is drunk and orders him out of the car. Johnny resists and is arrested. Without the keys, Llewyn abandons the car, leaving the cat and the unconscious Roland behind. In Chicago, Llewyn auditions for Bud Grossman, who offers a place in a trio he is forming. Rejecting the offer, Llewyn hitchhikes back to New York. Driving while the car owner sleeps, he hits a ginger cat; it limps into the woods as Llewyn watches from afar.

In New York, Llewyn uses his last $148 for back dues to rejoin the Merchant Marine union. He searches for his seaman's license so he can ship out, but it had been in the box he told his sister to trash. Llewyn returns to the Union Hall to replace it, but cannot afford the $85 fee. He visits Jean, who tells him she got him a gig at the Gaslight.

At the Gaslight, Llewyn learns that Pappi, the manager, also had sex with Jean. Disillusioned, Llewyn gets drunk and heckles a woman singing on stage. He visits the Gorfeins, who graciously welcome him. He learns that the novelty song is likely to be a major hit with massive royalties, which he will see no part of. Llewyn is amazed to see that their actual cat, Ulysses, found his way home.

The opening scene is revealed to be the ending. Llewyn performs at the Gaslight. Pappi teases him for heckling the previous evening’s singer and says that a friend of his is waiting in the alley. As Llewyn walks outside, a young [[Bob Dylan]] starts performing on stage. Behind the Gaslight, Llewyn is beaten by the suited man for having cruelly heckled his wife, the previous night’s performer. He watches as the man leaves in a taxi, bidding him "Au revoir".

Well before writing the script, the Coens began with a single idea, of [[Dave Van Ronk]] being beaten up outside of [[Gerde's Folk City]] in the [[West Village|Village]]. They employed the image in the opening scenes, then periodically returned to the project over the next couple of years to expand the story using a fictional character.<ref name="NPR Coen Bros transcript">{{cite web |title=The Coen Bros. According to the book's co-author, [[Elijah Wald]], the Coens mined the work "for local color and a few scenes".<ref name="Wald: Historical Context essay">{{cite web |url=http://www.insidellewyndavis.com/us/historical-context |title=The World of Llewyn Davis |last=Wald |first=Elija |website=Inside Llewyn Davis official site |publisher=CBS Films |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131021051038/http://www.insidellewyndavis.com/us/historical-context |archive-date=October 21, 2013 |url-status=dead |access-date=August 26, 2018}}</ref><ref name="NYT JCoen interview" /><ref>{{cite news |author-first1=Russ |author-last1=Fischer |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/coen-bros-film-based-folk-musician-dave-van-ronk/#more-106431 |title=The Coen Bros. ==Production==
Shooting was complicated by an early New York spring, which interfered with the bleak winter atmosphere that prevails throughout the film,<ref name="NYFF50 presser">{{cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lri3xYFS3fM |title=NYFF51: ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' Press Conference |via=YouTube |publisher=Film at Lincoln Center|date=September 29, 2013}}</ref> and by the difficulty of filming several cats, which, unlike dogs, ignore filmmakers' directions. On an animal trainer's advice, the Coens put out a casting call for an orange [[tabby cat]], since they are sufficiently common that several could play one part. Individual cats were then selected for each scene based on what they were disposed to do on their own.<ref name="NPR Coen Bros transcript" /> After a chance run-in with a cabdriver who lived at their old address, the Coens used the apartment they'd rented several decades earlier.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/blog/2018/12/3/when-i-met-the-coen-brothers-and-the-secret-behind-the-filming-of-inside-llewyn-davis|title=When I Met The Coen Brothers And a Secret Behind the Filming of Inside Llewyn Davis|website=Select Software|language=en-US|access-date=December 31, 2018|date=5 July 2023|author-first1=Phil|author-last1=Strazzulla}}</ref>

Producer [[Scott Rudin]], who worked with the Coens on ''[[No Country for Old Men (film)|No Country for Old Men]]'' and ''[[True Grit (2010 film)|True Grit]]'', collaborated on the project. [[StudioCanal]] helped finance it without an American distributor in place. "After shooting in New York City and elsewhere last year ... the brothers finished the movie at their own pace," wrote Michael Cieply in a January 2013 ''[[The New York Times|New York Times]]'' interview with Joel Coen ahead of a private, pre-Grammys screening in Los Angeles. "They could have rushed it into the Oscar season but didn't."<ref name="NYT JCoen interview">{{cite news |last=Cieply |first=Michael |title=Macdougal Street Homesick Blues |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/01/28/movies/joel-coen-on-inside-llewyn-davis.html |access-date=February 20, 2013 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=January 27, 2013}}</ref> On February 19, [[CBS Films]] announced it had picked up the U.S. domestic distribution rights for about $4 million. StudioCanal has rights to international distribution and foreign sales.<ref name="Billboard: CBS to distribute">{{cite news |title=CBS Films Pays $4 Million for Coen Bros.' ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |access-date=February 20, 2013 |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/risky-business/cbs-films-pays-4-million-422333 |newspaper=Billboard |agency=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=February 19, 2013}}</ref><ref name="CBS distribution release">{{cite press release |title=CBS Films Acquires The Coen Brothers' ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |url=http://www.cbsfilms.com/news/cbs_films_acquires_the_coen_brothers_inside_llewyn_davis |access-date=February 20, 2013 |publisher=CBS Films |date=February 19, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130223054720/http://www.cbsfilms.com/news/cbs_films_acquires_the_coen_brothers_inside_llewyn_davis |archive-date=February 23, 2013}}</ref>

===Music===
{{See also|Inside Llewyn Davis (soundtrack){{!}}''Inside Llewyn Davis'' (soundtrack)}}

[[File:Oscar Isaac.JPG|thumb|upright|Isaac and other cast members performed the music live, and did not use lip-synching.]]

Dave Van Ronk's music served as the Coens' starting point for the script, and many of the songs first designated for the film were his. Van Ronk biographer Elijah Wald said that Llewyn Davis "is not at all Dave, but the music is".<ref>{{cite web |last=Haglund |first=David |title=The People Who Inspired ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |url=http://www.slate.com/blogs/browbeat/2013/12/02/llewyn_davis_real_person_true_story_behind_coen_brothers_movie_with_oscar.html |work=Slate |date=December 2, 2013 |access-date=January 4, 2014}}</ref> (The cover of Davis's solo album, ''Inside Llewyn Davis'', resembles that of ''[[Inside Dave Van Ronk]]''. Both feature the artist in a doorway, wearing a [[tweed (cloth)|tweed]] jacket and smoking a cigarette.<ref name="Rolling Stone covers">{{cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/pictures/whats-real-in-inside-llewyn-davis-20131205 |title=What's Real in ''Inside Llewyn Davis'': Llewyn Davis' Album Cover Looks Very Familiar |last=Golden |first=Zara |date=December 5, 2013 |magazine=Rolling Stone}}</ref> One difference between the two covers is that there is a cat in the doorway on the cover of ''Inside Dave Van Ronk.'') Other songs emerged in conversations between the Coens and [[T Bone Burnett]], who produced the music in association with [[Marcus Mumford]].<ref name="NYT JCoen interview" /><ref name="NYFF50 presser" /><ref>{{cite web |title=Coens' folk revival 'Llewyn' serenades Cannes |url=http://newsok.com/coens-folk-revival-llewyn-serenades-cannes/article/feed/543273 |work=The Oklahoman |agency=Associated Press |date=May 19, 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130926120119/http://newsok.com/coens-folk-revival-llewyn-serenades-cannes/article/feed/543273|archive-date=26 September 2013}}</ref> Burnett previously worked with the Coens on the soundtracks for ''[[The Big Lebowski]]'' and ''[[O Brother, Where Art Thou?]]'', the latter of which sold about 8 million copies in the United States.<ref name="WSJ: Music needle">{{cite news |last=Jurgensen |first=John |title=T Bone Burnett and the Coen Brothers Move the Music Needle |url=https://online.wsj.com/news/articles/SB10001424052702303653004579210423619570510 |newspaper=Wall Street Journal |date=November 21, 2013}}</ref> The Coens viewed the music in ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' as a direct descendant of the music in ''O Brother.''<ref name="NPR Coen Bros transcript" />

The humorous [[novelty song]] "Please Mr. Kennedy", a plea from a reluctant astronaut, appears to be a fourth-generation derivative of the 1960 song "[[Mr. Custer]]", also known as "Please Mr. Custer", about the [[Battle of the Little Bighorn]], sung by [[Larry Verne]] and written by [[Al De Lory]], Fred Darian, and Joseph Van Winkle. A [[Tamla Motown|Tamla-Motown]] single followed in 1961: "Please Mr. Kennedy (I Don't Want to Go)", a plea from a reluctant [[Vietnam War]] draftee, sung by Mickey Woods and credited to [[Berry Gordy]], Loucye Wakefield and Ronald Wakefield. In 1962, using a similar theme, The Goldcoast Singers recorded "Please Mr. Kennedy" on their album ''Here They Are'', with writing credits to Ed Rush and [[George Cromarty]]. The Llewyn Davis version credits Rush, Cromarty, Burnett, Timberlake, and the Coens.<ref name="NPR Coen Bros transcript" /><ref name="Hollywood Reporter: Mr. Kennedy">{{cite news |last=Myers |first=Mitch |title=''Inside Llewyn Davis'': From General Custer to Mr. Kennedy, the Genesis of a Novelty Song |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/earshot/inside-llewyn-davis-general-custer-661912 |newspaper=The Hollywood Reporter |date=December 3, 2013}}</ref><ref name="Nonesuch soundtrack listing">{{cite web |title=''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |date=June 18, 2013 |url=https://www.nonesuch.com/albums/inside-llewyn-davis-original-soundtrack-recording|publisher=Nonesuch (track listing)}}</ref>

Isaac, Timberlake, Mulligan, Driver and others performed the music live.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/news/the-lost-world-of-llewyn-davis-christgau-on-the-coen-brothers-20131204 |title=The Lost World of ''Llewyn Davis'': Christgau on the Coen Brothers |last=Christgau |first=Robert |date=December 4, 2013 |magazine=Rolling Stone}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=http://soundandpicture.com/2014/02/inside-llewyn-davis-with-production-mixer-peter-f-kurland |title=''Inside Llewyn Davis'' with Production Mixer Peter F. Kurkland |date=February 3, 2014 |work=Sound & Picture |archive-date=September 11, 2014 |access-date=May 15, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140911120438/http://soundandpicture.com/2014/02/inside-llewyn-davis-with-production-mixer-peter-f-kurland/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The exception was "[[The Auld Triangle]]", which was lip-synced, with Timberlake singing bass. (Timberlake's vocal range was on display in the film. Critic [[Janet Maslin]], listening to the soundtrack, mistook Timberlake's voice for Mulligan's, which she thought resembled that of [[Mary Travers]]).<ref name="NPR Coen Bros transcript" />

It opened in 133 additional theaters on December 20 and wide on January 10, 2014.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2013/film/news/coen-brothers-inside-llewyn-davis-release-date-1200448664 |title=Coen Brothers' ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' Dated For Dec. 6 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |publisher=[[Reed Business Information]] |date=May 3, 2013 |access-date=May 7, 2013 |last=McNary |first=Dave}}</ref><ref name="DeadlineHollywood: 3rd week">{{cite news |last=Brooks |first=Brian |title=Specialty Box Office: ''Her'' Nabs $42K Per-Screen; Bollywood's ''Dhoom 3'' Sizzles; ''The Past'' Coasts In Opening; ''Llewyn Davis'' Still Faring Well |url=https://deadline.com/2013/12/specialty-box-office-bollywood-dhoom-the-past-inside-llewyn-davis-personal-trainer-655834/ |newspaper=Deadline Hollywood |date=December 22, 2013}}</ref>

===Critical response===
[[File:Oscar Isaac 2013.jpg|thumb|upright|Isaac's performance received widespread acclaim from critics.]]

[[Rotten Tomatoes]] gives the film a rating of 92%, based on 295 reviews, with an average score of 8.5/10. The critical consensus states: "Smart, funny, and profoundly melancholy, ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' finds the Coen brothers in fine form."<ref>{{cite web |title=Inside Llewyn Davis (2013) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/inside_llewyn_davis_2013 |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=November 5, 2021}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] gives the film a score of 93 out of 100, based on reviews from 52 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |title=Inside Llewyn Davis Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/inside-llewyn-davis |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |work=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=November 4, 2013}}</ref>

[[A. O. Scott]] noted similarities between Davis and Van Ronk but wrote it's "not a biopic, it's a Coen brothers movie, which is to say a brilliant magpie's nest of surrealism, period detail and pop-culture scholarship. To put it another way, it's a folk tale...An important character is named Ulysses, whose ancient wanderings inspired ''[[O Brother, Where Art Thou?]]'', the Coens' earlier venture (also in the company of music supervisor T Bone Burnett) into American vernacular musical traditions...But since this is a Coen brothers film, it is predestined that Llewyn will fail and that we will laugh both at and with him as he deals with a world full of cretins, including the one he sees reflected in the windows of the subway train...I won't speculate further on what ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' might mean. But at least one of its lessons seems clear to me, after several viewings, as clear and bright as a G major chord. We are, as a species, ridiculous: vain, ugly, selfish and self-deluding. But somehow, some of our attempts to take stock of this condition—our songs and stories and moving pictures old and new—manage to be beautiful, even sublime."<ref>{{cite news| author-link1=A. O. Scott|author-first1=A. O. |author-last1=Scott| title=Melancholy Odyssey Through the Folk Scene| url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/12/06/movies/inside-llewyn-davis-directed-by-joel-and-ethan-coen.html| work=[[The New York Times]]| date=December 5, 2013}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[The Village Voice]]'', Alan Scherstuhl praised the film: "While often funny and alive with winning performances, ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' finds the brothers in a dark mood, exploring the near-inevitable disappointment that faces artists too sincere to compromise—disappointments that the Coens, to their credit, have made a career out of dodging. The result is their most affecting film since the masterful ''[[A Serious Man]]''."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2013-09-25/film/inside-llewyn-davis-nyff-review|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130930135230/http://www.villagevoice.com/2013-09-25/film/inside-llewyn-davis-nyff-review/|url-status=dead |archive-date=September 30, 2013 |title=''Inside Llewyn Davis'': The Coen Brothers' Most Moving Film Since ''A Serious Man''|author-first1=Alan|author-last1=Scherstuhl|work=[[The Village Voice]] |date=September 27, 2013 |access-date=September 27, 2013}}</ref> [[Todd McCarthy]] of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' called the film "an outstanding fictional take on the early 1960s folk music scene", praising the "fresh, resonant folk soundtrack", and said Isaac's performance "deftly manages the task of making Llewyn compulsively watchable".<ref>{{cite web |date=May 18, 2013 |last=McCarthy |first=Todd |title=''Inside Llewyn Davis'': Cannes Review |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movie/inside-llewyn-davis/review/524956 |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]] |access-date=November 11, 2013}}</ref> Leigh Singer of ''[[IGN]]'' gave the film a 10 out of 10 'Masterpiece' score, saying "Don't be fooled by the seemingly minor key ... this is one of the finest works by—let's just call it—the most consistently innovative, versatile and thrilling American filmmakers of the last quarter-century."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2013/05/21/inside-llewyn-davis-review|title=Inside Llewyn Davis Review|website=IGN|author-first1=Leigh |author-last1=Singer|date=May 21, 2013|access-date=February 13, 2016}}</ref>

Folk singers have criticized the film for representing the Village folk scene of the time as a less friendly place than it actually was. Terri Thal, Dave Van Ronk's ex-wife, said, "I didn't expect it to be almost unrecognizable as the folk-music world of the early 1960s."<ref>{{cite web |author-first1=Terri |author-last1=Thal |url=http://blogs.villagevoice.com/music/2013/12/dave_van_ronk_inside_llewyn_davis.php |title=Dave Van Ronk's Ex-Wife Takes Us Inside ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |work=Village Voice |date=December 13, 2013 |access-date=February 25, 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150225225745/http://blogs.villagevoice.com/music/2013/12/dave_van_ronk_inside_llewyn_davis.php |archive-date=February 25, 2015}}</ref> [[Suzanne Vega]] said, "I feel they took a vibrant, crackling, competitive, romantic, communal, crazy, drunken, brawling scene and crumpled it into a slow brown sad movie."<ref name="Reader">{{cite web |author-first1=Aimee |author-last1=Levitt |url=http://www.chicagoreader.com/Bleader/archives/2014/01/17/the-folk-song-armys-attack-on-inside-llewyn-davis |title=The folk-song army's attack on ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |work=Chicago Reader |date=January 17, 2014 |access-date=January 19, 2014}}</ref> The film was also criticized because, though to some extent based on Van Ronk's memoir, it portrayed a character very different from Van Ronk, who, in contrast with the character Llewyn Davis, is usually described as a "nice guy".<ref name="Reader" /><ref>{{cite magazine |author-first1=David |author-last1=Browne |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/meet-the-folk-singer-who-inspired-inside-llewyn-davis-20131202 |title=Meet the Folk Singer Who Inspired ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' |magazine=Rolling Stone |date=December 2, 2014 |access-date=January 19, 2014 |archive-date=January 22, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140122191418/http://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/meet-the-folk-singer-who-inspired-inside-llewyn-davis-20131202 |url-status=dead }}</ref> At a press interview before the premiere at Cannes, the Coens said Davis was an original creation, and that the music was the major influence they had drawn from Van Ronk.<ref>{{cite web |date=May 19, 2013 |title=Interview of ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' by Ethan Coen, Joel Coen |url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/13025.html |work=[[Cannes Film Festival]] |access-date=February 24, 2014 |archive-date=March 1, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140301180424/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/mediaPlayer/13025.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

It was voted the 11th greatest film of the 21st century in a [[BBC's 100 Greatest Films of the 21st Century|2016 BBC poll]].<ref name="bbc.com"/> It also ranked 11th on ''[[The New York Times]]'''s list of the Best Films of the 21st Century So Far.<ref>{{cite web| work=Nonesuch Records| title=''NY Times'' Names ''There Will Be Blood'', ''Boyhood'', ''Inside Llewyn Davis'' Among 25 Best Films of the 21st Century| date=June 9, 2017| url=https://www.nonesuch.com/journal/ny-times-there-will-be-blood-boyhood-inside-llewyn-davis-25-best-films-21st-century-2017-06-09}}</ref> In 2021, members of [[Writers Guild of America West]] (WGAW) and [[Writers Guild of America, East]] (WGAE) voted its screenplay 72nd in WGA’s 101 Greatest Screenplays of the 21st Century (so far).<ref>{{Cite web |title=101 Greatest Screenplays of the 21st Century (*so far) |url=https://www.wga.org/writers-room/101-best-lists/101-best-screenplays-of-the-21st-century-so-far/list |access-date=2025-12-25 |website=www.wga.org}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Pedersen |first=Erik |date=2021-12-06 |title=101 Greatest Screenplays Of The 21st Century: Horror Pic Tops Writers Guild’s List |url=https://deadline.com/2021/12/greatest-screenplays-of-21st-century-list-writers-guild-get-out-1234885622/ |access-date=2025-12-26 |website=Deadline |language=en-US}}</ref>

In February 2025, ''[[The Washington Post]]'' ranked it at number 8 on its list of "The 25 best movie musicals of the 21st century," with Ty Burr writing "The best way in 2025 to come at the Coen brothers' fond evocation of the 1960s Greenwich Village folk scene is to think of it as ''[[A Complete Unknown]]'' with an unmagnetic jerk at its center. Oscar Isaac plays the purist folkie of the title without a whisper of self-pity, and the film is full of music old and new, lovingly curated by T-Bone Burnett and as faithful to its period as Burnett's work for ''[[O Brother, Where Art Thou?]]'' What's inside Llewyn Davis? A hole that he fills with music that's never quite his."<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Burr |first1=Ty |last2=Kumar |first2=Naveen |date=2025-02-27 |title=The 25 best movie musicals of the 21st century |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/entertainment/movies/2025/02/27/best-musical-movies/ |access-date=2025-04-12 |newspaper=The Washington Post}}</ref> In June 2025, it ranked number 83 on ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century" and number 95 on the "Readers' Choice" edition of the list.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |date=July 2, 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref><ref name="Times">{{cite web | url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html | title=The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | work=The New York Times | date=June 23, 2025 }}</ref> In July 2025, it ranked number 18 on ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century."<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-movies-of-the-21st-century-1235200512/ | title=100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=July 1, 2025 }}</ref>
