'''''Purple Noon''''' ({{langx|fr|'''Plein soleil'''}}; {{langx|it|'''Delitto in pieno sole'''}}; also known as '''''Full Sun''''', '''''Blazing Sun''''', '''''Lust for Evil''''', and '''''Talented Mr. Ripley''''')<ref name="PS">{{cite book |title=Histoire du Cinéma Français 1956–1960 |editor1=Maurice Bessy |editor2=Raymond Chirat |editor3=André Bernard |isbn=978-2857043799 |publisher=Pygmalion |author=Maurice Bessy|year=1992 }}</ref> is a 1960 [[crime thriller film]] directed by [[René Clément]], based on the 1955 novel ''[[The Talented Mr. Ripley]]'' by [[Patricia Highsmith]].   

''Purple Noon'' stars [[Alain Delon]] (in his first major role), alongside [[Marie Laforêt]] and [[Maurice Ronet]]; [[Romy Schneider]], Delon's girlfriend at the time, makes a [[cameo appearance|cameo]]. Delon plays [[Tom Ripley]], a young American sent to Italy to convince a wealthy playboy, Philippe Greenleaf (Ronet), to return home. As Tom becomes obsessed with Philippe's luxurious lifestyle, he devises a plan to take over Philippe's life.  

''Purple Noon'' received positive reviews and made Delon a star. A second adaptation of the novel, ''[[The Talented Mr. Ripley (film)|The Talented Mr. Ripley]],'' was released in 1999.

==Plot==
The young American [[Tom Ripley]] has been sent to Italy by the father of a wealthy playboy, Philippe Greenleaf, to persuade him to return to San Francisco and take over the family business. However, Philippe has no intention of doing so.

Tom becomes fixated on Philippe and his fiancée, Marge, and covets their life of luxury in the seaside town of [[Mongibello]]. Philippe grows bored with Tom and becomes cruel and abusive to him. During a yachting trip, Philippe strands Tom in the dinghy and accidentally leaves him to drift for hours in the sun. 

Tom devises a plan to kill Philippe and steal his identity. He leaves evidence of Philippe's philandering for Marge to find. Finding that Tom has obtained his bank records, Philippe seeks to draw him out. After Marge goes ashore following an argument with Philippe, Philippe confronts Tom, who admits his plan. Philippe offers Tom a substantial sum to leave him and Marge alone, but Tom rebuffs it, saying he is interested in far more.

Tom stabs Philippe, wraps his corpse in canvas and weighs it with an anchor. He is preparing to dump it overboard when he is hit by the ship's [[Boom (sailing)|boom]] and knocked into the sea, taking Philippe's corpse with him. Tom makes it back onto the ship.

Returning to Mongibello alone, Tom informs Marge that Philippe has decided to stay away. He goes to Rome, skillfully replaces Philippe's picture with his own in Philippe's passport, masters forging Philippe's signature, and takes over his wealth, identity, and lifestyle.

When Philippe's friend Freddy Miles tracks down "Philippe's" hideaway, he is surprised to only find Tom there. Feeling that Freddy is beginning to suspect the truth, Tom impulsively murders him and dumps the body outside town. Freddy's body is soon found, and the Italian police become involved, suspecting "Philippe" in the murder. Tom continues his charade, switching between his own identity and Philippe's to give the illusion that Philippe is still alive and on the run. 

Tom survives a string of close calls, throwing the Italian police off his trail and seemingly outwitting everyone. After forging Philippe's suicide note and a will that leaves Philippe's entire fortune to Marge, Tom thinks he is finally in the clear. He returns to Mongibello and then seduces Marge, in Philippe's home.

Philippe's father arrives in Mongibello to settle the transfer of Philippe's estate and the sale of his yacht. Marge leaves Tom at the beach to go meet Philippe's father, while Tom goes to a seaside café and celebrates the success of his gambit by ordering the best drink in the house. As Philippe's boat is being pulled out of the water so a potential buyer can inspect it, Marge is horrified to see Philippe's canvas-wrapped body dragged up the [[slipway]] behind it, the loose end of the lashing having become wrapped around the sailboat's propeller. A police inspector from Rome who has been watching Tom goes to the café, where he has the waitress tell Tom, who is lounging in the sun, that there is a phone call for him. Tom smirks and, unsuspectingly, walks into a trap.

==Production==
Delon was cast after Clément saw him in ''[[Women Are Weak]]'' (1959).<ref name="la">"New Dream for Alain Delon", [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Thomas, Kevin]]. ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', 18 December 1965, p. a12.</ref> [[Billy Kearns]] was an expatriate American actor well-liked in France.<ref>{{Cite news |date=1992-12-04 |title=Bill Kearns, 69, Actor Seen in French Films |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/12/04/obituaries/bill-kearns-69-actor-seen-in-french-films.html |access-date=2026-05-14 |work=The New York Times |page=D19 |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> Screenwriter [[Paul Gégauff]] wrote a variation on the same story in 1968 when he worked on ''[[Les Biches (film)|Les biches]]'' with [[Claude Chabrol]].

==Reception==
''Purple Noon'' was lauded by critics and made Delon a star.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Thomas |first=Kevin |date=1996-06-28 |title='Noon' Reemerges as a Compelling, Suspenseful Classic |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-06-28-ca-19219-story.html |access-date=2024-03-15 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref> In 1962, Clément and Gégauff won an [[Edgar Award]] from the [[Mystery Writers of America]] for Best Foreign Film Screenplay.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Category List – Best Foreign film |url=https://edgarawards.com/category-list-best-foreign-film/ |access-date=2024-03-15 |website=Edgar Awards Info & Database}}</ref> {{Rotten Tomatoes prose|92|8.2|39|consensus=The talented Tom Ripley is brought to enigmatic life on the screen in ''Purple Noon'', an exquisite thriller wherein the murderous social climber is embodied by an unnerving Alain Delon.}}<ref>{{Cite web |title=Purple Noon |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/purple_noon_1961 |access-date=20 September 2025 |website=Rotten Tomatoes}}</ref> The film enjoys a loyal [[cult following]], with fans including film director [[Martin Scorsese]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sandor |first=Samuel |date=2022-09-05 |title=Alain Delon's 'Purple Noon': The Half-Forgotten French Precursor to 'The Talented Mr. Ripley' Starring Matt Damon & Jude Law - Hollywood Insider |url=https://www.hollywoodinsider.com/purple-noon-review/ |access-date=2024-03-15 |language=en-US}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three stars (compared to the four-star review he gave to 1999's ''[[The Talented Mr. Ripley (film)|The Talented Mr. Ripley]]''<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=24 December 1999 |title=The Talented Mr. Ripley |url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19991224/REVIEWS/912240305/1023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20051129043352/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19991224/REVIEWS/912240305/1023 |archive-date=2005-11-29 |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=Chicago Sun-Times |via=rogerebert.suntimes.com}}</ref>), writing that "the best thing about the film is the way the plot devises a way for Ripley to create a perfect cover-up", while criticizing the "less than satisfactory ending", about which he wrote: "''Purple Noon'' ends as it does only because Clement doesn't have Highsmith's iron nerve."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=3 July 1996 |title=Purple Noon |url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19960703/REVIEWS/607030303/1023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090824233740/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19960703/REVIEWS/607030303/1023 |archive-date=2009-08-24 |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=Chicago Sun-Times |via=rogerebert.suntimes.com}}</ref>

[[James Berardinelli]] rated ''Purple Noon'' higher than ''The Talented Mr. Ripley'', giving it a four-star review (compared to two-and-a-half stars for ''The Talented Mr. Ripley'').<ref>{{Cite web |last=Berardinelli |first=James |title=Review: The Talented Mr. Ripley |url=https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/t/talented.html |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=preview.reelviews.net}}</ref> Berardinelli praised Delon's acting, writing that "Tom is fascinating because Delon makes him so", and also complimented the film for "expert camerawork and crisp direction".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Berardinelli |first=James |title=Review: Purple Noon (Plein Soleil) |url=https://preview.reelviews.net/movies/p/purple.html |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=preview.reelviews.net}}</ref> In the entry for ''Purple Noon'' on Berardinelli's All-Time Top 100 list, he compared it to the 1999 film, saying: "The remake went back to the source material, Patricia Highsmith's ''The Talented Mr. Ripley''. The result, while arguably truer to the events of Highsmith's book, is vastly inferior. To say it suffers by comparison to ''Purple Noon'' is an understatement. Almost every aspect of Rene Clement's 1960 motion picture is superior to that of [[Anthony Minghella|Minghella]]'s 1999 version, from the cinematography to the acting to the screenplay. [[Matt Damon]] might make a credible Tom Ripley but only for those who never experienced Alain Delon's portrayal."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Berardinelli |first=James |title=#86: Purple Noon |url=https://preview.reelviews.net/top100/86.html |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=preview.reelviews.net}}</ref>

Nandini Ramnath, writing for Scroll.in, said: "The definitive portrayal of crime novelist Patricia Highsmith's most enduring creation was as early as 1960. Damon and [[Dennis Hopper|Hopper]]{{efn|[[Dennis Hopper]] portrayed Tom Ripley in [[Wim Wenders]]' 1977 film ''[[The American Friend]]''.}} come close to conveying the ruthlessness and ambition of Tom Ripley, but Delon effortlessly captures his mystique."<ref>{{cite web | url=http://thereel.scroll.in/811069/five-star-cinema-rene-clements-plein-soleil | title=Five-star cinema: René Clément's ''Plein Soleil''|website=Scroll.in| date=3 July 2016 | access-date=26 July 2016 | author=Nandini Ramnath|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160706055943/http://thereel.scroll.in/811069/five-star-cinema-rene-clements-plein-soleil|archive-date=6 July 2016}}</ref>

Highsmith's opinion of the film was mixed. She felt that Alain Delon was "excellent" in the role of Tom Ripley<ref name="peary">{{Cite magazine |last=Peary |first=Gerald |date=1988 |title=Patricia Highsmith |url=http://www.geraldpeary.com/interviews/ghi/highsmith.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303173347/http://www.geraldpeary.com/interviews/ghi/highsmith.html |archive-date=3 March 2016 |magazine=Sight and Sound |pages=104-105 |via=GeraldPeary.com |volume=75 |issue=2}}</ref> and described the film overall as "very beautiful to the eye and interesting for the intellect",<ref name="Telegraph">{{cite web |author=Wilson, Andrew |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/3595207/Ripleys-enduring-allure.html |title=Ripley's enduring allure |work=[[The Daily Telegraph]]|location=London|date=2003-05-24 |access-date=2010-12-30}}</ref> but criticized the ending (in which it is implied that Ripley is to be caught by the police): "[I]t was a terrible concession to so-called public morality that the criminal had to be caught."<ref name="Telegraph" /> The Japanese filmmaker [[Akira Kurosawa]] cited ''Purple Noon'' as one of his 100 favorite films.<ref name="farout">{{cite web |last1=Thomas-Mason |first1=Lee |title=From Stanley Kubrick to Martin Scorsese: Akira Kurosawa once named his top 100 favourite films of all time |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/akira-kurosawa-100-favourite-films-list/ |website=Far Out Magazine |date=12 January 2021 |access-date=23 January 2023}}</ref>{{better source needed|date=May 2026}}

==Restoration and re-release==
In 2012, [[StudioCanal]] funded a [[art restoration|restoration]] of the film by the {{Lang|it|Immagine Ritrovata|italic=no}} laboratory, to be shown at the [[2013 Cannes Film Festival]] as part of a [[Homage (arts)|homage]] to Delon's career prior to a theatrical re-release in (at least) France.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.lefigaro.fr/flash-actu/2013/05/07/97001-20130507FILWWW00306-cannes-va-rendre-hommage-a-delon.php |title=Cannes va rendre hommage à Delon |work=[[Le Figaro]]|language=fr|date=2013-05-07 |access-date=2013-05-07}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.screendaily.com/news/cannes-classics-2013-line-up/5054402.article?blocktitle=Latest-News&contentID=1846|title=Cannes Classics 2013 line-up unveiled |work=Screen Daily|access-date=2013-04-30|date=29 April 2013|last=Rosser|first=Michael|last2=Wiseman|first2=Andreas}}</ref>

On 4 December 2012, [[The Criterion Collection]] released the high-definition digital restoration of ''Purple Noon'' on Blu-ray and DVD. Special features include an interview with René Clément scholar and author Denitza Bantcheva, archival interviews with Alain Delon and Patricia Highsmith, the film's original English-language trailer, and a booklet featuring an essay by film critic [[Geoffrey O'Brien]] and excerpts from a 1981 interview with Clément.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Purple Noon |url=https://www.criterion.com/films/27810-purple-noon |access-date=2026-05-14 |website=The Criterion Collection |language=en}}</ref> The film has also been released on Blu-ray in the UK and Germany (by StudioCanal in 2013), and Japan (by Kinokuniya in 2011).
