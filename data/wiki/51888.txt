'''''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade''''' is a 1989 American [[action-adventure film]] directed by [[Steven Spielberg]] from a screenplay by [[Jeffrey Boam]], based on a story by [[George Lucas]] and [[Menno Meyjes]]. It is the third installment in the [[Indiana Jones|''Indiana Jones'' film series]] and the narrative sequel to ''[[Raiders of the Lost Ark]]'' (1981)<!-- Temple of Doom occurs in 1935, so it is a prequel to Raiders, which occurs in 1936. Last Crusade occurs primarily in 1938 and is a sequel to Raiders, not Temple of Doom. -->. [[Harrison Ford]] reprises his role as [[Indiana Jones (character)|the titular character]], while [[Sean Connery]] co-stars as [[Henry Jones, Sr.|his father]]. Other cast members featured include [[Alison Doody]], [[Denholm Elliott]], [[Julian Glover]], [[River Phoenix]] and [[John Rhys-Davies]]. In the film, set in 1938, Indiana Jones searches for his father, a [[Holy Grail]] scholar, who has been kidnapped and held hostage by the [[Nazis]] while on a journey to find the Holy Grail.

After the criticism that ''[[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom]]'' (1984) received, Spielberg chose to make a more lighthearted film for the next installment, as well as bringing back several elements from ''Raiders of the Lost Ark''. During the five years between ''Temple of Doom'' and ''The Last Crusade'', he and executive producer Lucas reviewed several scripts before accepting Jeffrey Boam's. Filming locations included Spain, Italy, West Germany, Jordan, the United Kingdom, and the United States.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67198 |publisher=[[American Film Institute]] |title=''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade'' |access-date=September 21, 2018 |archive-date=August 29, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190829211114/https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67198 |url-status=live }}</ref>

''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade'' was released in the United States on May 24, 1989, by [[Paramount Pictures]]. The film received positive reviews and was a financial success, earning $474.2 million worldwide, making it the [[1989 in film|highest-grossing film of 1989]]. It also won the [[Academy Awards|Academy Award]] for [[Academy Award for Best Sound Editing|Best Sound Effects Editing]] and was nominated for [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]] and [[Academy Award for Best Sound|Best Sound]] at the [[62nd Academy Awards]]. Although Spielberg and Lucas originally intended for ''The Last Crusade'' to be the end of the series, a sequel, ''[[Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull]]'', followed in May 2008, while a fifth and final film, ''[[Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny]]'', was released in June 2023.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. -->

In 1912, [[Scouts BSA|Boy Scout]] [[Indiana Jones (character)|Indiana Jones]] lives with his father [[Henry Jones Sr.]] in [[Moab, Utah]]. One day, while exploring caves on an excursion with his scout troop, Indy takes a [[crucifix]] owned by [[Francisco Vázquez de Coronado]] from a group of [[graverobber|graverobbers]] led by a man named Garth, and after a brief chase on horseback and aboard a circus train, Indy flees to his home, where Garth and his men find him and confiscate the crucifix with the help of the local sheriff. Garth, however, admits his respect for the scrappy young man, and gives him his [[fedora]] before leaving.

In 1938, Indy successfully takes back the crucifix from the [[List of Indiana Jones characters#Panama Hat|employer of the graverobbers]] off the [[Estado Novo (Portugal)|Portuguese]] coast. After returning to his university classroom in the United States, Indy learns his father has disappeared while searching for the [[Holy Grail]] and Henry's home was ransacked. [[List of Indiana Jones characters#Walter Donovan|Walter Donovan]], his father's financial backer, tasks Indy with finding both Henry and the Grail. Indy receives a package containing Henry's diary, which includes his research on the Grail, and travels to [[Venice]] alongside [[List of Indiana Jones characters#Marcus Brody|Marcus Brody]] to meet Henry's associate [[List of Indiana Jones characters#Elsa Schneider|Dr. Elsa Schneider]]. Beneath the library where Henry was last seen, Indy and Elsa discover a [[Catacombs|catacomb]] containing an inscribed shield which reveals that the path to the Grail begins in [[İskenderun|Alexandretta]]. The two are subsequently attacked by a mysterious group who reveal themselves to be the secret Brotherhood of the Cruciform Sword, dedicated to protecting the Grail. After saving the group's leader Kazim, Indy learns that Henry is being held at Brunwald Castle in [[Austria within Nazi Germany|Austria]]. Indy entrusts Marcus with a map from the diary detailing a route to the Grail and sends him to Alexandretta to rendezvous with their old friend [[Sallah]]. Discovering their rooms have been ransacked, Indy reveals the diary's existence to Elsa before they sleep together.

In Austria, Indy and Elsa infiltrate the castle, discovering it to be under [[Nazi Germany|Nazi]] control commanded by Standartenführer Ernst Vogel. Indy finds Henry and tries to escape, but surrenders after Vogel holds Elsa at gunpoint. She reveals herself and Donovan to be Nazi collaborators and takes the diary. After arriving in Alexandretta, Marcus is captured by the Nazis as well. Elsa returns to Germany, while Indy and Henry escape the castle before traveling to [[Berlin]], where Indy retrieves the diary during a [[Book burnings in Nazi Germany|book burning rally]] hosted by [[Adolf Hitler]]. After recovering it from Elsa, Indy and Henry flee on a [[Zeppelin]]. Soon the zeppelin reroutes back to Germany, forcing Indy and his father to commandeer a spare plane before evading two [[Luftwaffe]] planes pursuing them.

Once Indy and Henry arrive in [[Hatay State|Hatay]], Sallah informs them that the Nazis have also traveled there using the map. While they are following the trail, the Nazis are ambushed by Kazim and the Brotherhood, but the Nazis manage to kill them all. Henry takes advantage of the distraction to try to rescue Marcus but is captured; Indy attacks the Nazi convoy in response and is eventually able to destroy it with help from Henry and Marcus while sending Vogel and his tank over the cliff. Indy, Henry, Marcus, and Sallah proceed to a temple containing the Grail, where they observe the Nazis attempting to overcome the temple's traps before being captured. Donovan forces Indy to find safe passage for them by mortally wounding Henry; a drink from the Grail can heal him. With the help of the diary, Indy overcomes the traps and finds a room with many cups and an [[List of Indiana Jones characters#Grail Knight|ancient knight]], who explains that only one cup is the true Grail. Donovan and Elsa enter the room, and Elsa deliberately gives him the wrong cup, killing Donovan by rapid aging after he drinks from it. Indy identifies the true Grail and saves Henry's life with a warning from the knight that the Grail can never cross the great seal. Elsa ignores the warning, setting off a cataclysm in the temple; she falls to her death trying to take the Grail, and Indy nearly suffers the same fate before Henry convinces him to let it go. The Grail falls into an abyss as Indy and his companions escape and ride off into the sunset.

==Cast==
{{See also|List of Indiana Jones characters}}
* [[Harrison Ford]] as [[Indiana Jones (character)|Henry "Indiana" Jones, Jr.]]: An archaeologist, professor and adventurer who seeks to rescue his father and find the Holy Grail. Ford said he loved the idea of introducing Indiana's father because it allowed him to explore another side to Indiana's personality: "These are men who have never made any accommodation to each other. Indy behaves differently in his father's presence. Who else would dare call Indy 'junior'?"<ref name=hattrick/>
** [[River Phoenix]] as a younger Indiana Jones. Phoenix had portrayed the son of Ford's character in ''[[The Mosquito Coast (film)|The Mosquito Coast]]'' (1986). Ford recommended Phoenix for the part; he said that of the young actors working at the time, Phoenix looked the most like him when he was around that age.<ref name=making/>
* [[Sean Connery]] as [[Henry Jones, Sr.]]: Indiana's father, a professor of [[medieval literature]] who cared more about looking for the Grail than raising his son. Spielberg had Connery in mind when he suggested introducing Indiana's father, though he did not tell Lucas at first. Consequently, Lucas wrote the role as "a crazy, eccentric" professor resembling [[Laurence Olivier]], whose relationship with Indiana is "strict schoolmaster and student rather than a father and son".<ref name=monkeyking/><ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://ew.com/article/2011/12/02/steven-spielberg-ew-interview/ | title=Steven Spielberg: The EW interview | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] }}</ref> Spielberg had been a fan of Connery's work as [[James Bond]] and felt that no one else could perform the role as well.<ref name=time/> Spielberg biographer [[Joseph McBride (writer)|Joseph McBride]] wrote, "Connery was already the father of Indiana Jones since the series had sprung from the desire of Lucas and Spielberg to rival (and outdo) Connery's [[Production of the James Bond films|''James Bond'' films]]."<ref name=faber>McBride, "An Awfully Big Adventure", p. 379–413</ref> Connery, who had eschewed major franchise films since his work on the James Bond series, as he found those roles dull and wanted to avoid paparazzi attention, initially turned the role down (as he was only twelve years older than Ford) but eventually relented. Connery—a student of history—began to reshape the character, and revisions were made to the script to address his concerns. "I wanted to play Henry Jones as a kind of [[Richard Francis Burton]]," Connery commented. "I was bound to have fun with the role of a gruff, Victorian Scottish father."<ref name=time>{{cite magazine |author1=Richard Corliss |author2=Elaine Dutka |author3=Denise Worrell |author4=Jane Walker |url=http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,957848-1,00.html |title=What's Old Is Gold: A Triumph for Indy |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |date=May 29, 1989 |access-date=January 6, 2009 |author1-link=Richard Corliss |archive-date=May 24, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100524110419/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,957848-1,00.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> Connery believed Henry should be a match for his son, telling Spielberg that "whatever Indy'd done my character has done and my character has done it better".<ref name=making>{{cite AV media |title=Indiana Jones: Making the Trilogy |medium=DVD |publisher=[[Paramount Pictures]] |date=2003}}</ref> Connery signed to the film on March 25, 1988.<ref name=monkeyking/> He improvised the line, "She talks in her sleep", which was left in because it made everyone laugh;<ref name=twenty-five/> in Boam's scripts, Henry telling Indiana that he slept with Elsa occurs later.<ref name=monkeyking/>
** [[Alex Hyde-White]] plays Henry in the film's prologue, though his face is never shown and his lines were dubbed by Connery.
* [[Denholm Elliott]] as [[Marcus Brody]]: Indiana's bumbling English colleague, revealed to also be an old friend of Henry. Elliott returned after Spielberg sought to recapture the tone of ''[[Raiders of the Lost Ark]]'' (1981), following the actor's absence in the darker ''[[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom]]'' (1984).<ref name=making/>
* [[Alison Doody]] as [[Elsa Schneider]]: An [[Austrians|Austrian]] art professor and Indy's love interest, who is in league with the [[Nazis]]. She seduces the Joneses to trick them, but appears to have genuine feelings for Indy. While the character of Elsa is in her 30s during the film, Doody was 21 when she auditioned and was one of the first actresses who met for the part.<ref name=monkeyking/> [[Amanda Redman]] was offered the role, but declined.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.shortlist.com/news/15-things-you-probably-didnt-know-about-indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade|title=15 Things You (Probably) Didn't Know About Indiana Jones And The Last Crusade|date=December 30, 2015|website=Shortlist.com|access-date=November 8, 2021|archive-date=May 9, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190509020002/https://www.shortlist.com/news/15-things-you-probably-didnt-know-about-indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade|url-status=live}}</ref> 
* [[John Rhys-Davies]] as [[Sallah]]: A friend of Indiana and a professional [[excavation (archaeology)|excavator]] living in [[Cairo]]. Like Elliott's, Rhys-Davies's return was also an attempt to recapture the spirit of ''Raiders of the Lost Ark''.<ref name=making/>
* [[Julian Glover]] as [[Walter Donovan]]: A wealthy American businessman and grail hunter who sends the Joneses on their quest for the Holy Grail. Later revealed to be secretly working with the Nazis, he is motivated by a desire to achieve [[immortality]]. He originally auditioned for the role of Vogel. Glover, who is English, adopted an American accent for the film,<ref name=Hearn/> but was dissatisfied with the result.<ref name=making/>
* [[Michael Byrne (actor)|Michael Byrne]] as [[Standartenführer]] Ernst Vogel: A brutal ''[[Schutzstaffel|SS]]'' officer. Byrne and Ford had previously starred in ''[[Force 10 from Navarone (film)|Force 10 from Navarone]]'' (1978), in which they also respectively played a German and an American.<ref name=castingcrusaders/>
* [[Stefan Kalipha]] as Hatay Tank Gunner.

[[Kevork Malikyan]] portrays Kazim, the leader of the Brotherhood of the Cruciform Sword, an organization that protects the Holy Grail through the centuries. Malikyan had impressed Spielberg with his performance in ''[[Midnight Express (film)|Midnight Express]]'' (1978) and would have auditioned for the role of Sallah in ''Raiders of the Lost Ark'' had a traffic jam not delayed his meeting with the director.<ref name=castingcrusaders>{{cite web |url=http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_2_casting.php |title=Casting the Crusaders |publisher=TheRaider.net |access-date=February 6, 2009 |archive-date=January 21, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090121055841/http://theraider.net/films/crusade/making_2_casting.php |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Robert Eddison]] appears as the Grail Knight, the guardian of the Grail who drank from the cup of Christ during the Crusades. Eddison was a stage and television veteran only appearing in a few films since the 1930s. Glover recalled Eddison was excited and nervous for his return to film, often asking if he had performed correctly.<ref name=pro/> [[Laurence Olivier]] was originally considered to play the Grail Knight,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.moviefone.com/2014/05/24/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-facts/ |title=25 Things You (Probably) Didn't Know About 'Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade' |first=Sharon |last=Knolle |date=May 24, 2014 |access-date=March 31, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170331205319/https://www.moviefone.com/2014/05/24/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-facts/|archive-date=March 31, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> but he was too ill and died the same year in which the film was released.

[[Michael Sheard]] briefly appears as [[Adolf Hitler]], whom Jones encounters at a book-burning rally in Berlin. Although a non-speaking role, Sheard could speak German and had already portrayed Hitler three times during his career. He had also appeared as the [[U-boat]] commander Oskar Schomburg in ''Raiders of the Lost Ark''. In the same scene, [[Ronald Lacey]], who played [[Sicherheitsdienst|SD]] agent [[Arnold Toht]] in ''Raiders of the Lost Ark'', cameos as [[Heinrich Himmler]]. [[Alexei Sayle]] played the fictional sultan of Hatay (the real [[Hatay State]] was a republic). [[Paul Maxwell]] portrayed "the man with the Panama Hat" who took possession of the Cross of Coronado. Wrestler and stuntman [[Pat Roach]], who played three roles in the previous two films (notably the musclebound German soldier who gets into a fistfight with Jones in ''Raiders of the Lost Ark''), made a short [[Cameo appearance|cameo]] as the Nazi who accompanies Vogel to the Zeppelin. Roach was set to film a fight with Ford, but it was cut. In a deleted scene, Roach's agent boards the second biplane on the Zeppelin with a World War I flying ace (played by [[Frederick Jaeger]]), only for the pair to fall to their deaths after the flying ace makes an error. [[Richard Young (actor)|Richard Young]] played Garth, the leader of the tomb robbers who chased young Indiana Jones and then gives him his hat. [[Eugene Lipinski]] portrayed the mysterious agent G-Man, while [[Vernon Dobtcheff]] appeared as the butler of Castle Brunwald.

==Production==
===Development===
[[George Lucas]] and [[Steven Spielberg]] had intended to make a trilogy of ''[[Indiana Jones]]'' films since Lucas had first [[pitch (filmmaking)|pitched]] ''[[Raiders of the Lost Ark]]'' to Spielberg in 1977,<ref name=Pre/> though they signed for five films with [[Paramount Pictures]] by 1979.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Rinzler |last2=Bouzereau |first1=J.W. |first2=Laurent |author-link1=J.W. Rinzler |author-link2=Laurent Bouzereau |date=May 20, 2008 |title=''The Complete Making of Indiana Jones'', Chapter 1: "The Name is Indiana: 1968 to February 1980", p. 36 |url=http://www.randomhouse.com/catalog/display.pperl?isbn=9780345501295 |location=[[New York City]], [[United States of America]] |publisher=[[Penguin Random House]] |page=36 |isbn=978-0-345-50129-5 |access-date=December 26, 2022 |archive-date=February 24, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210224163647/http://www.randomhouse.com/catalog/display.pperl?isbn=9780345501295 |url-status=live }}</ref> After the mixed critical and public reaction to ''[[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom]]'', Spielberg decided to complete the trilogy to fulfill his promise to Lucas, with the intent to imbue the film with the spirit and tone of ''Raiders of the Lost Ark''.<ref>{{cite news |author=Nancy Griffin |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |work=[[Premiere (magazine)|Premiere]] |date=June 1988}}</ref> ''Temple of Doom'' writers [[Willard Huyck]] and [[Gloria Katz]] chose not to return due to both having other commitments and feeling satisfied with their work in the second film.<ref>{{cite web |last=Vespe |first=Eric |title=FORTUNE AND GLORY: Writers of Doom! Quint interviews Willard Huyck and Gloria Katz! |url=http://www.aintitcool.com/node/67450 |website=Ain't Cool News |access-date=December 13, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190211082543/http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/67450 |archive-date=February 11, 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Throughout the film's development and [[pre-production]], Spielberg admitted he was "consciously regressing" in making the film.<ref name="faber"/> Due to his commitment to the film, the director had to drop out of directing ''[[Big (film)|Big]]'' and ''[[Rain Man]]''.<ref name=Pre/>

[[File:Hollywood Museum - Indiana Jones' Grail Diary (7659583966).jpg|thumb|upright|left|Grail diary of [[Henry Jones Sr.]] as seen in the film]]
Lucas initially suggested making the film "a [[haunted house|haunted mansion]] movie", for which ''[[Romancing the Stone]]'' writer [[Diane Thomas]] wrote a script. Spielberg rejected the idea because of the similarity to ''[[Poltergeist (1982 film)|Poltergeist]]'', which he had co-written and produced.<ref name=faber/> Lucas first introduced the [[Holy Grail]] in an idea for the film's prologue, which was to be set in Scotland. He intended the Grail to have a [[pagan]] basis, with the rest of the film revolving around a separate Christian artifact in Africa. Spielberg did not care for the Grail idea, which he found too esoteric,<ref name=hattrick>{{cite web |url=http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_1_thehattrick.php |title=The Hat Trick |publisher=TheRaider.net |access-date=February 6, 2009 |archive-date=April 21, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130421022440/http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_1_thehattrick.php |url-status=live }}</ref> even after Lucas suggested giving it healing powers and the ability to grant immortality (much like the fictional magical power given to the Ark in the first film of the trilogy). In September 1984, Lucas completed an eight-page treatment titled ''Indiana Jones and the [[Sun Wukong|Monkey King]]'', which he soon followed with an 11-page outline. The story saw Indiana battling a ghost in Scotland before finding the [[Fountain of Youth]] in Africa.<ref name=monkeyking>Rinzler, Bouzereau, "The Monkey King: July 1984 to May 1988", p. 184–203.</ref>

[[Chris Columbus (filmmaker)|Chris Columbus]]—who had written the Spielberg-produced ''[[Gremlins]]'', ''[[The Goonies]]'', and ''[[Young Sherlock Holmes]]''—was hired to write the script. His first draft, dated May 3, 1985, changed the main plot device to a [[Peaches of Immortality|Garden of Immortal Peaches]]. It begins in 1937, with Indiana battling the murderous ghost of Baron Seamus Seagrove III in Scotland. Indiana travels to [[Mozambique]] to aid Dr. Clare Clarke (a [[Katharine Hepburn]]-type according to Lucas), who has found a 200-year-old [[pygmies|pygmy]]. The pygmy is kidnapped by the Nazis during a boat chase, and Indiana, Clare and Scraggy Brier—an old friend of Indiana—travel up the [[Zambezi]] river to rescue him. Indiana is killed in the climactic battle but is resurrected by the Monkey King. Other characters include a cannibalistic African tribe; Nazi Sergeant Gutterbuhg, who has a mechanical arm; Betsy, a stowaway student who is suicidally in love with Indiana; and a pirate leader named Kezure (described as a [[Toshiro Mifune]]-type), who dies eating a peach because he is not pure of heart.<ref name=monkeyking/>

Columbus's second draft, dated August 6, 1985, removed Betsy and featured Dash—an [[expatriate]] bar owner for whom the Nazis work—and the Monkey King as villains. The Monkey King forces Indiana and Dash to play chess with real people and disintegrates each person who is [[Capture (chess)|captured]]. Indiana subsequently battles the undead, destroys [[Ruyi Jingu Bang|the Monkey King's rod]], and marries Clare.<ref name=monkeyking/> [[Location scouting]] commenced in Africa but Spielberg and Lucas abandoned ''Monkey King'' because of its negative depiction of African natives,<ref>McBride, p.318</ref> and because the script was too unrealistic.<ref name=monkeyking/> Spielberg acknowledged that it made him "... feel very old, too old to direct it."<ref name=hattrick/> Columbus's script was leaked onto the Internet in 1997, and many believed it was an early draft for [[Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull|the fourth film]] because it was mistakenly dated to 1995.<ref>{{cite news |author=David Hughes |title=The Long Strange Journey of Indiana Jones IV |work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |date=November 2005 |page=131}}</ref>

Dissatisfied, Spielberg suggested introducing Indiana's father, [[Henry Jones, Sr.]] Lucas was dubious, believing the Grail should be the story's focus, but Spielberg convinced him that the father–son relationship would serve as a great metaphor in Indiana's search for the artifact.<ref name=faber/> Spielberg hired [[Menno Meyjes]], who had worked on Spielberg's ''[[The Color Purple (1985 film)|The Color Purple]]'' and ''[[Empire of the Sun (film)|Empire of the Sun]]'', to begin a new script on January 1, 1986. Meyjes completed his script ten months later. It depicted Indiana searching for his father in [[Montségur]], where he meets a nun named Chantal. Indiana travels to [[Venice]], takes the ''[[Orient Express]]'' to [[Istanbul]], and continues by train to [[Petra]], where he meets [[Sallah]] and reunites with his father. Together they find the grail. At the climax, a Nazi villain touches the Grail and explodes; when Henry touches it, he ascends a stairway to [[Heaven]]. Chantal chooses to stay on Earth because of her love for Indiana. In a revised draft dated two months later, Indiana finds his father in [[Krak des Chevaliers]], the Nazi leader is a woman named Greta von Grimm, and Indiana battles a demon at the Grail site, which he defeats with a dagger inscribed with "God is King". The prologue in both drafts has Indiana in Mexico battling for possession of [[Moctezuma II|Montezuma]]'s [[death mask]] with a man who owns gorillas as pets.<ref name=monkeyking/>

[[File:Indiana Jones and the Cross of Coronado.jpg|left|thumb|[[Indiana Jones]] ([[River Phoenix]]) finds the Cross of Coronado as a 13-year-old Boy Scout. Spielberg suggested making Indiana a Boy Scout as both he and Harrison Ford were former Scouts.]]
Spielberg suggested ''[[Innerspace]]'' writer [[Jeffrey Boam]] perform the next rewrite. Boam spent two weeks reworking the story with Lucas, which yielded a treatment that is largely similar to the final film.<ref name=monkeyking/> Boam told Lucas that Indiana should find his father in the middle of the story. "Given the fact that it's the third film in the series, you couldn't just end with them obtaining the object. That's how the first two films ended," he said, "So I thought, let them ''lose'' the Grail, and let the father–son relationship be the main point. It's an archaeological search for Indy's own identity and coming to accept his father is more what it's about [than the quest for the Grail]."<ref name=faber/> Boam said he felt there was not enough character development in the previous films.<ref name=hattrick/> In Boam's first draft, dated September 1987, the film is set in 1939. The prologue has adult Indiana retrieving an Aztec relic for a museum curator in Mexico and features the circus train. Henry and Elsa (who is described as having dark hair) were searching for the Grail on behalf of the Chandler Foundation, before Henry went missing. The character of Kazim is here named Kemal, and is an agent of the Republic of Hatay, which seeks the grail for its own. Kemal shoots Henry and dies drinking from the wrong chalice. The Grail Knight battles Indiana on horseback, while Vogel is crushed by a boulder while attempting to steal the Grail.<ref name=monkeyking/>

[[File:The Organ at Arches National Park Utah Corrected.jpg|thumb|left|[[The Organ (Arches National Park)|The Organ]] in the [[Arches National Park]] in [[Utah]], United States]]
Boam's February 23, 1988, rewrite used many of Connery's comic suggestions. It included the prologue that was eventually filmed; because of the mixed response to ''Empire of the Sun'', which was about a young boy, Lucas had to convince Spielberg to show Indiana as a boy.<ref name=hattrick/> Spielberg—who was later awarded the [[Distinguished Eagle Scout Award]]—had the idea of making Indiana a [[Scouts BSA (Boy Scouts of America)|Boy Scout]].<ref name=Pre>{{cite news |author=Susan Royal |title=''Always'': An Interview with Steven Spielberg |work=[[Premiere (magazine)|Premiere]] |pages=45–56 |date=December 1989}}</ref> The 1912 prologue as seen in the film refers to events in the lives of Indiana's creators. When Indiana cracks the bullwhip to defend himself against a lion, he accidentally lashes and scars his chin. Ford gained this scar in a car accident as a young man.<ref name=making/> Indiana taking his nickname from his pet [[Alaskan Malamute]] is a reference to the character being named after Lucas's dog.<ref name=viewingguide/> The train carriage Indiana enters is named "Doctor Fantasy's Magic Caboose", which was the name producer [[Frank Marshall (producer)|Frank Marshall]] used when performing magic tricks. Spielberg suggested the idea, Marshall came up with the false-bottomed box through which Indiana escapes,<ref name=salvo>{{cite news |title=Last Crusade Opening Salvo |work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |date=October 2006 |pages=98–99}}</ref> and production designer Elliott Scott suggested the trick be done in a single, uninterrupted shot.<ref name=familybonds/> Spielberg intended the shot of Henry with his umbrella—after he causes the bird strike on the German plane—to evoke ''[[Ryan's Daughter]]''.<ref name=viewingguide/> [[List of characters in the Indiana Jones series#Anna|Indiana's mother]], named Margaret in this version, dismisses Indiana when he returns home with the Cross of Coronado, while his father is on a long-distance call. Walter Chandler of the Chandler Foundation features, but is not the main villain; he plunges to his death in the tank. Elsa introduces Indiana and Brody to a large Venetian family that knows Henry. [[Leni Riefenstahl]] appears at the Nazi rally in Berlin. Vogel is beheaded by the traps guarding the Grail. Kemal tries to blow up the Grail Temple during a comic fight in which gunpowder is repeatedly lit and extinguished. Elsa shoots Henry, then dies drinking from the wrong Grail, and Indiana rescues his father from falling into the chasm while grasping for the Grail. Boam's revision of March 1 showed Henry causing the seagulls to strike the plane, and has Henry saving Indiana at the end.<ref name=monkeyking/><ref>{{cite web |last1=Boam |first1=Jeffrey |title=Indy III |url=http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/multimedia/lastcrusade.pdf |website=The Raider |access-date=January 8, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160910084824/http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/multimedia/lastcrusade.pdf|archive-date=September 10, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

During an undated "[[Amblin Entertainment]]" revision and a rewrite by [[Tom Stoppard]] (under the pen name Barry Watson) dated May 8, 1988,<ref name=monkeyking/> further changes were made. Stoppard polished most of the dialogue,<ref name="twenty-five">{{cite news |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade: An Oral History |work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |date=May 8, 2008 |url=https://www.empireonline.com/indy/day17/default.asp |access-date=May 8, 2008 |archive-date=November 19, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081119033800/http://www.empireonline.com/indy/day17/default.asp |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Koski |first1=Genevieve |title=Raiders of the Lost Ark |url=https://www.avclub.com/article/iraiders-of-the-lost-arki-2302 |website=The Onion AV Club |date=May 16, 2008 |access-date=January 8, 2016 |archive-date=March 26, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160326204128/http://www.avclub.com/article/iraiders-of-the-lost-arki-2302 |url-status=live }}</ref> and created the "Panama Hat" character to link the prologue's segments featuring the young and adult Indianas. The Venetian family is cut. Kemal is renamed Kazim and now wants to protect the grail rather than find it. Chandler is renamed Donovan. The scene of Brody being captured is added. Vogel now dies in the tank, while Donovan shoots Henry and then drinks from the false grail, and Elsa falls into the chasm. The Grail trials are expanded to include the stone-stepping and leap of faith.<ref name=monkeyking/><ref>{{cite web |last1=Fitzgerald |first1=Mike |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade: Learning from Stoppard |url=http://creativescreenwriting.com/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-learning-from-stoppard/ |website=Creative Screenwriting Magazine |access-date=March 30, 2016 |archive-date=March 31, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160331122150/http://creativescreenwriting.com/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-learning-from-stoppard/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Filming===
[[File:Chiesa di San Barnaba - Venezia.jpg|thumb|left|[[San Barnaba di Venezia]] in [[Venice]], whose exterior served as a library]]
[[Principal photography]] began on May 16, 1988, in the [[Tabernas Desert]] in [[Spain]]'s [[Almería (province)|Almería province]]. Spielberg originally had planned the chase to be a short sequence shot over two days, but he drew up storyboards to make the scene an action-packed centerpiece.<ref name=making/> Thinking he would not surpass the truck pursuit from ''Raiders of the Lost Ark'' (because the truck was much faster than the tank), he felt this sequence should be more story-based and needed to show Indiana and Henry helping each other. He later said he had more fun storyboarding the sequence than filming it.<ref name=familybonds>{{cite web |title=Filming Family Bonds |publisher=TheRaider.net |url=http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_3_production.php |access-date=February 6, 2009 |archive-date=May 2, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130502003500/http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_3_production.php |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[second unit]] had begun filming two weeks before.<ref name=pro/> After approximately ten days, the production moved to the [[:es:Escuela de Arte de Almería|Escuela de Arte de Almería]], to film the scenes set in the Sultan of Hatay's palace. [[Cabo de Gata-Níjar Natural Park]] was used for the road, tunnel and beach sequence (at [[Playa de Mónsul]]) in which birds strike the plane. The shoot's Spanish portion wrapped on June 2, 1988, in [[Guadix]], [[Granada (province)|Granada]], with filming of Brody's capture at Alexandretta train station.<ref name=pro/> The filmmakers built a mosque near the station for atmosphere, rather than adding it as a visual effect.<ref name=familybonds/>

[[Image:Buerresheim 1.jpg|thumb|left|[[Bürresheim Castle]], near [[Mayen]], in the then-[[West Germany|West German]] state of [[Rhineland-Palatinate]], served as "Brunwald Castle" in Austria near the German border.]]
The exteriors of "Brunwald Castle" were filmed at [[Bürresheim Castle]] in West Germany. In the movie the image of the castle has been flipped and the building was expanded with a [[Matte painting]] depicting a copy of the right facade to the left of the main tower. Filming for the castle interiors took place in the [[United Kingdom]] from June 5 to&nbsp;10, 1988, at [[Elstree Studios (Shenley Road)|Elstree Studios]] in [[Borehamwood]], [[England]]. On June 16, [[Lawrence Hall, London]], was used as the interior of [[Berlin Tempelhof Airport]]. Filming returned to Elstree the next day to capture the motorcycle escape, continuing at the studio for interior scenes until July 18. One day was spent at [[North Weald Airfield]] on June 29 to film Indiana leaving for Venice.<ref name=pro/> Ford and Connery acted much of the Zeppelin table conversation without trousers on because of the overheated set.<ref name=viewingguide>{{cite news |title=Crusade: Viewing Guide |work=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |page=101 |date=October 2006}}</ref> Spielberg, Marshall and Kennedy interrupted the shoot to make a plea to the [[Parliament of the United Kingdom]] to support the economically "depressed" British studios. July&nbsp;20–22 was spent filming the temple interiors. The temple set, which took six weeks to build, was supported on {{convert|80|feet}} of hydraulics and ten [[gimbal]]s for use during the earthquake scene. Resetting between takes took twenty minutes while the hydraulics were put to their starting positions and the cracks filled with plaster. The shot of the Grail falling to the temple floor—causing the first crack to appear—was attempted on the full-size set, but proved too difficult. Instead, crews built a separate floor section that incorporated a pre-scored crack sealed with plaster. It took several takes to throw the Grail from {{convert|6|feet}} onto the right part of the crack.<ref name=familybonds/> July&nbsp;25–26 was spent on night shoots at [[Stowe School]], [[Stowe, Buckinghamshire]], for the Nazi rally.<ref name=pro/>

Filming resumed two days later at Elstree, where Spielberg swiftly filmed the library, Portuguese freighter, and catacombs sequences.<ref name=pro/> The steamship fight in the prologue's 1938 portion was filmed in three days on a sixty-by-forty-feet (eighteen-by-twelve-meter) deck built on gimbals at Elstree. A dozen dump tanks – each holding three hundred imperial gallons (360 U.S. gallons; 3000&nbsp;lb.; 1363 liters) of water – were used in the scene.<ref name=familybonds/> Henry's house was filmed at [[Mill Hill]], London. Indiana and Kazim's fight in Venice in front of a ship's propeller was filmed in a water tank at Elstree. Spielberg used a [[long focus lens]] to make it appear the actors were closer to the propeller than they really were.<ref name=pro/> Two days later, on August 4, another portion of the boat chase using [[Hacker Craft]] sport boats, was filmed at [[Port of Tilbury|Tilbury Docks]] in [[Essex]].<ref name=pro/> The shot of the boats passing between two ships was achieved by first cabling the ships off so they would be safe. The ships were moved together while the boats passed between, close enough that one of the boats scraped the sides of the ships. An empty speedboat containing dummies was launched from a floating platform between the ships amid fire and smoke that helped obscure the platform. The stunt was performed twice because the boat landed too short of the camera in the first attempt.<ref name=familybonds/> The following day, filming in England wrapped at the [[Royal Masonic School]] in [[Rickmansworth]], which doubled for Indiana's college (as it had in ''Raiders of the Lost Ark'').<ref name=pro/>
[[File:Küste bei Monsul14.jpg|thumb|[[Playa de Mónsul]] in [[Cabo de Gata-Níjar Natural Park]] in [[Almería]], [[Spain]]]]
Shooting in Venice took place on August 8.<ref name=pro/> For scenes such as Indiana and Brody greeting Elsa, shots of the boat chase, and Kazim telling Indiana where his father is,<ref name=familybonds/> [[Robert Watts]] gained control of the [[Grand Canal (Venice)|Grand Canal]] from 7&nbsp;am to 1&nbsp;pm, sealing off tourists for as long as possible. Cinematographer [[Douglas Slocombe]] positioned the camera to ensure no satellite dishes would be visible.<ref name=pro/> [[San Barnaba di Venezia]] served as the library's exterior.<ref name=making/> The next day, filming moved to the ancient city of [[Petra]], [[Jordan]], where [[Al Khazneh]] (The Treasury) stood in for the temple housing the Grail. The cast and crew became guests of [[Hussein of Jordan|King Hussein]] and [[Queen Noor of Jordan|Queen Noor]]. The Treasury had previously appeared in ''[[Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger]]''. The main cast completed their scenes that week, after 63 days of filming.<ref name=pro/>

The second unit filmed part of the prologue's 1912 segment from August 29 to September 3. The main unit began two days later with the circus train sequence at [[Alamosa, Colorado]], on the [[Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad]]. They filmed at [[Pagosa Springs, Colorado|Pagosa Springs]] on September 7, and then at [[Cortez, Colorado|Cortez]] on September 10. From September 14 to&nbsp;16, filming of Indiana falling into the train carriages took place in Los Angeles. The production then moved to Utah's [[Arches National Park]] to shoot more of the opening. A house in Antonito, Colorado was used for the Jones family home.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WMGPRR_Indiana_Jones_House_Indiana_Jones_and_the_Last_Crusade_Antonito_CO |title=Indiana Jones House - "Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade" - Antonito, CO - Movie Locations on Waymarking.com |work=waymarking.com |access-date=December 27, 2016 |archive-date=January 6, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170106160530/http://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WMGPRR_Indiana_Jones_House_Indiana_Jones_and_the_Last_Crusade_Antonito_CO |url-status=live }}</ref> The production had intended to film at [[Mesa Verde National Park]], but [[Native Americans in the United States|Native American]] representatives had religious objections to its use.<ref name=familybonds/> When Spielberg and editor [[Michael Kahn (film editor)|Michael Kahn]] viewed a [[rough cut]] of the film in late 1988, they felt it suffered from a lack of action. The motorcycle chase was shot during [[post-production]] at [[Mount Tamalpais]] and [[Fairfax, California|Fairfax]] near [[Skywalker Ranch]]. The closing shot of Indiana, Henry, Sallah and Brody riding into the sunset was filmed in [[Amarillo, Texas]] in early 1989 by the second unit, directed by Frank Marshall.<ref name=pro/><ref name=Hearn>{{cite book |author=Marcus Hearn |title=The Cinema of George Lucas |publisher=[[Harry N. Abrams, Inc.|Harry N. Abrams Inc]] |year=2005 |pages=159–165 |location=New York City |isbn=0-8109-4968-7}}</ref> Filming ended on September 16, 1988, after 123 days of filming.

===Design===
[[Image:LastCrusadeTank.jpg|thumb|Composite photograph of the tank on location]]
Mechanical effects supervisor [[George Gibbs (special effects artist)|George Gibbs]] said the film was the most difficult one of his career.<ref name=familybonds/> He visited a museum to negotiate renting a small French [[Tanks in World War I|World War I tank]], but decided he wanted to make one.<ref name=pro/> The tank was based on the [[Tank Mark VIII]], which was {{convert|36|ft|disp=flip}} long and weighed {{convert|28|ST}}. However, some liberties were taken with the design.<ref>[https://tanks-encyclopedia.com/fictional-tanks-hatay-heavy-tank/ Hatay Heavy Tank (Fictional Tank)]</ref> Gibbs built the tank over the framework of a {{convert|28|ST|adj=on}} [[excavator]] and added {{convert|7|ST|adj=on}} [[Continuous track|tracks]] that were driven by two automatic [[hydraulic pump]]s, each connected to a [[Range Rover]] [[Rover V8 engine|V8 engine]]. Gibbs built the tank from steel rather than aluminum or fiberglass because it would allow the realistically [[suspension (vehicle)|suspensionless]] vehicle to endure the rocky surfaces. Unlike its historic counterpart, which had only the two side guns, the tank had a turret gun added as well. It took four months to build and was transported to Almería on a [[Short Belfast]] plane and then a low loader truck.<ref name=familybonds/>

The tank broke down twice. The [[distributor]]'s rotor arm broke and a replacement had to be sourced from [[Madrid]]. Then two of the device's valves used to cool the oil exploded, due to [[solder]] melting and mixing with the oil. It was very hot in the tank, despite the installation of ten fans, and the lack of suspension meant the driver was unable to stop shaking during filming breaks.<ref name=familybonds/> The tank only moved at {{convert|10|to|12|mph}}, which [[Vic Armstrong]] said made it difficult to film Indiana riding a horse against the tank while making it appear faster.<ref name=pro>Rinzler, Bouzereau, "The Professionals: May 1988 to May 1989", p. 204–229.</ref> A smaller section of the tank's top made from aluminum and which used rubber tracks was used for close-ups. It was built from a searchlight trailer, weighed eight tons, and was towed by a four-wheel drive truck. It had safety nets on each end to prevent injury to those falling off.<ref name=familybonds/> A quarter-scale model by Gibbs was driven over a {{convert|50|ft|m|adj=on|disp=flip}} cliff on location; [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM) created further shots of the tank's destruction with models and miniatures.<ref name=completion/>

[[File:Almeria 02.jpg|thumb|left|The tank chase was filmed in the [[Tabernas Desert]] in Almería, Spain.]]
[[Michael Lantieri]], mechanical effects supervisor for the 1912 scenes, noted the difficulty in shooting the train sequence. "You can't just stop a train," he said, "If it misses its mark, it takes blocks and blocks to stop it and back up." Lantieri hid handles for the actors and stuntmen to grab onto when leaping from carriage to carriage. The carriage interiors shot at [[Universal Studios Hollywood]] were built on tubes that inflated and deflated to create a rocking motion.<ref name=familybonds/> For the close-up of the rhinoceros that strikes at (and misses) Indiana, a foam and fiberglass animatronic was made in London. When Spielberg decided he wanted it to move, the prop was sent to [[John Carl Buechler]] in Los Angeles, who resculpted it over three days to blink, snarl, snort and wiggle its ears. The giraffes were also created in London. Because steam locomotives are very loud, Lantieri's crew would respond to [[assistant director#Sub-roles|first assistant director]] [[David Tomblin]]'s radioed directions by making the giraffes nod or shake their heads to his questions, which amused the crew.<ref name=completion>{{cite web |title=A Quest's Completion |publisher=TheRaider.net |url=http://www.theraider.net/films/crusade/making_4_postproduction.php |access-date=February 6, 2009 |archive-date=February 20, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090220124058/http://theraider.net/films/crusade/making_4_postproduction.php |url-status=live }}</ref> For the villains' cars, Lantieri selected a 1914 [[Ford Model T]], a 1919 [[Ford Model T]] truck and a 1916 [[Saxon (automobile)|Saxon]] Model 14, fitting each with a [[Ford Pinto]] [[V6 engine]]. Sacks of dust were hung under the cars to create a dustier environment.<ref name=familybonds/>

[[File:Escuela de Arte de Almería, patio 02.jpg|thumb|The courtyard of the [[Almería Art School]], served as the palace of the fictional "Sultan of Hatay".]]
Spielberg used doves for the seagulls that Henry scares into striking the German plane because the real gulls used in the first take did not fly.<ref name=making/> In December 1988, Lucasfilm ordered 1,000 disease-free gray rats for the catacombs scenes from the company that supplied the snakes and bugs for the previous films. Within five months, 5,000 rats had been bred for the sequence;<ref name=making/> 1,000 mechanical rats stood in for those that were set on fire. Several thousand snakes of five breeds—including a [[boa constrictor]]—were used for the train scene, in addition to rubber ones onto which Phoenix could fall. The snakes would slither from their crates, requiring the crew to dig through sawdust after filming to find and return them. Two lions were used, which became nervous because of the rocking motion and flickering lights.<ref name=familybonds/>
[[File:Al Khazneh Petra edit 2.jpg|thumb|upright|[[Al-Khazneh|Al Khazneh]] (The Treasury) at Petra, Jordan was used for the entrance to the temple housing the Holy Grail.]]
Costume designer [[Anthony Powell (designer)|Anthony Powell]] found it a challenge to create Connery's costume because the script required the character to wear the same clothes throughout. Powell thought about his own grandfather and incorporated [[tweed (cloth)|tweed]] suits and fishing hats. Powell felt it necessary for Henry to wear glasses, but did not want to hide Connery's eyes, so chose rimless ones. He could not find any suitable, so he had them specially made. The Nazi costumes were genuine and were found in Eastern Europe by Powell's co-designer Joanna Johnston, to whom he gave research pictures and drawings for reference.<ref name=pro/>
The motorcycles used in the chase from the castle were a mixed bag: the scout model with sidecar in which Indy and Henry escape was an original [[Dnepr (motorcycle)|Dnepr]], complete with machine gun [[Pintle mount#Pintle|pintle]] on the sidecar, while the pursuing vehicles were more modern machines dressed up with equipment and logos to make them resemble German army models.
Gibbs used two Swiss [[Pilatus P-2]] air force training planes standing in as pursuing Luftwaffe [[Arado Ar 96]]s for the Zeppelin biplane escape sequence. He built a device based on an [[internal combustion engine]] to simulate gunfire, which was safer and less expensive than firing [[Blank (cartridge)|blanks]].<ref name=completion/> Baking soda was applied to Connery to create Henry's bullet wound. Vinegar was applied to create the foaming effect as the water from the Grail washes it away.<ref name=completion/>
At least one reproduction [[Volkswagen Kübelwagen|Kübelwagen]] was used during filming despite the film being set two years prior to manufacture of said vehicles.{{citation needed|date=September 2016}}

===Visual effects===
[[Industrial Light & Magic]] built an {{convert|8|foot|adj=on}} foam model of the Zeppelin to complement shots of Ford and Connery climbing into the [[Gotha Go 145C]] biplane. A biplane model with a {{convert|2|foot|adj=on}} wingspan was used for the shot of the biplane detaching. [[Stop-motion]] animation was used for the shot of the German fighter's wings breaking off as it crashes through the tunnel. The tunnel was a {{convert|210|feet}} model that occupied 14 of ILM's parking spaces for two months. It was built in eight-foot sections, with hinges allowing each section to be opened to film through. Ford and Connery were filmed against [[Chroma key|bluescreen]]; the sequence required their car to have a dirty windscreen, but to make the integration easier this was removed and later [[compositing|composited]] into the shot. Dust and shadows were animated onto shots of the plane miniature to make it appear as if it disturbed rocks and dirt before it exploded. Several hundred [[tim-bird]]s were used in the background shots of the seagulls striking the other plane; for the closer shots, ILM dropped feather-coated crosses onto the camera. These only looked convincing because the scene's quick cuts merely required shapes that suggested gulls.<ref name=completion/> ILM's [[Wes Takahashi]] supervised the film's visual effects sequences.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.agni-animation.com/fullerton/halloffame/Wes_Ford_Takahashi.html |title=Subject: Wes Ford Takahashi |publisher=Animators' Hall of Fame |access-date=July 12, 2016 |archive-date=August 12, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160812020742/http://www.agni-animation.com/fullerton/halloffame/Wes_Ford_Takahashi.html |url-status=usurped }}</ref>

[[File:Leapoffaith.jpg|thumb|left|Indiana discovers a bridge hidden by [[camouflage]]. Ford was filmed in front of a bluescreen for the scene, which was completed by a model of the bridge filmed against a matte painting.]]
Spielberg devised the three trials that guard the Grail.<ref name=hattrick/> For the first, the blades under which Indiana ducks like a penitent man were a mix of practical and miniature blades created by Gibbs and ILM. For the second trial, in which Indiana spells "[[Jehovah|Iehova]]" on stable stepping stones, it was intended to have a tarantula crawl up Indiana after he mistakenly steps on "J". This was filmed and deemed unsatisfactory, so ILM filmed a stuntman hanging through a hole that appears in the floor, {{convert|30|feet}} above a cavern. As this was dark, it did not matter that the matte painting and models were rushed late in production. The third trial, the leap of faith that Indiana makes over an apparently impassable ravine after discovering a bridge hidden by [[forced perspective]], was created with a model bridge and painted backgrounds. This was cheaper than building a full-size set. A puppet of Ford was used to create a shadow on the {{convert|9|ft|m|adj=mid|-tall}} by {{convert|13|ft|m|adj=mid|-wide}} model because Ford had filmed the scene against bluescreen, which did not incorporate the shaft of light from the entrance.<ref name=completion/>

Spielberg wanted Donovan's death shown in one shot, so it would not look like an actor having makeup applied between takes. Inflatable pads were applied to [[Julian Glover]]'s forehead and cheeks by [[Nick Dudman]] that made his eyes seem to recede during the character's initial decomposition, as well as a mechanical wig that grew his hair. The shot of Donovan's death was the first all-digital composite scene in a movie,<ref>[https://www.ilm.com/vfx/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade/ Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade | Industrial Light & Magic]</ref> and was created over three months by [[morphing]] together three puppets of Donovan created by [[Stephan Dupuis]] in separate stages of decay, a technique ILM mastered on ''[[Willow (1988 film)|Willow]]'' (1988).<ref name=viewingguide/> A fourth puppet was used for the decaying clothes, because the puppet's torso mechanics had been exposed. Complications arose because [[Alison Doody]]'s double had not been filmed for the scene's latter two elements, so the background and hair from the first shot had to be used throughout, with the other faces [[Texture mapping|mapped]] over it. Donovan's skeleton was hung on wires like a [[marionette]]; it required several takes to film it crashing against the wall because not all the pieces released upon impact.<ref name=completion/>

[[Ben Burtt]] designed the sound effects. He recorded chickens for the sounds of the rats,<ref name=pro/> and digitally manipulated the noise made by a Styrofoam cup for the castle fire. He rode in a biplane to record the sounds for the dogfight sequence, and visited the demolition of a [[wind turbine]] for the plane crashes.<ref name=completion/> Burtt wanted an echoing gunshot for Donovan wounding Henry, so he fired a [[.357 Magnum]] in [[Skywalker Ranch]]'s underground car park, just as Lucas drove in.<ref name=pro/> A rubber balloon was used for the earthquake tremors at the temple.<ref>(2003). ''The Sound of Indiana Jones'' (DVD). [[Paramount Pictures]].</ref> The film was released in select theaters in the 70&nbsp;mm Full-Field Sound format, which allowed sounds to not only move from side to side, but also from the theater's [[Quadraphonic sound|front to its rear]].<ref name=completion/>
[[File:Treasure Island Administration Building (2), August 2021.jpg|thumb|The [[Administration Building, Treasure Island|Administration Building]], on [[Treasure Island, California|Treasure Island]], in [[San Francisco]], [[California]], served as the "Berlin Airport".]]
[[Matte painting]]s of the Austrian castle and the Berlin airport were based on real buildings; the Austrian castle "Schloss Brunwald" is [[Bürresheim Castle]] near [[Mayen]] in [[Rhineland-Palatinate]], West Germany, that was made to look larger. Rain was created by filming granulated [[Borax]] soap against black at high speed. It was only lightly [[Multiple exposure#Double exposure|double exposed]] into the shots so it would not resemble snow. The lightning was animated. The [[Administration Building, Treasure Island|Administration Building]] at San Francisco's [[Treasure Island (California)|Treasure Island]] was used as the exterior of [[Berlin Tempelhof Airport]]. The structure already had period-appropriate [[art deco]] architecture, as it had been constructed in 1938 for planned use as an airport terminal. ILM added a control tower, Nazi banners, vintage automobiles and a sign stating "Berlin Flughafen". The establishing shot of the Hatayan city at dusk was created by filming silhouetted cutouts that were backlit and obscured by smoke. Matte paintings were used for the sky and to give the appearance of [[fill light]] in the shadows and [[Backlighting (lighting design)|rim light]] on the edges of the buildings.<ref name=completion/>

==Themes==
A son's relationship with his estranged father is a common theme in Spielberg's films, including ''[[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial]]'' and ''[[Hook (film)|Hook]]''.<ref name=faber/> <!-- Spielberg biographer Douglas Brode notes that apart from Henry, Indiana has two other father figures, [[Marcus Brody]], and the grave robber known as Fedora. Henry seeks spiritual "illumination" while searching for the Holy Grail. In the prolog, Henry's face is not shown on screen, symbolizing that his relationship with his son has disappeared on an emotional level. Fedora is a mercenary and a villain but he is friendly towards Indiana. Both men inspired Indiana's dual personality as an adult: on the one hand, Indiana honors his father by teaching archaeology, but this is a social rather than spiritual way of examining artifacts, and further rejects Henry by dressing and adventuring like Fedora, the exciting father Indiana wished he had. Brody is far less self-important than Henry, and had he been Indiana's father then there would have been no need for him to invent the rebellious persona of the hatted explorer.-->

The film's exploration of fathers and sons coupled with its use of religious imagery is comparable to two other 1989 films, ''[[Star Trek V: The Final Frontier]]'' and ''[[Field of Dreams]]''. Writing for ''[[The New York Times]]'', Caryn James felt the combination in these films reflected [[New Age]] concerns, where the worship of God was equated to searching for fathers. James felt that neither Indiana nor his father is preoccupied with finding the Grail or defeating the Nazis, but that, rather, both seek professional respect for one another in their [[Boy's Own Paper|boys' own]] adventure. James contrasted the temple's biblically epic destruction with the more effective and quiet conversation between the Joneses at the film's end. James noted that Indiana's mother does not appear in the prologue, being portrayed as already having died before the film's events began.<ref>{{cite news |author=Caryn James |title=It's a New Age For Father–Son Relationships |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=July 9, 1989 |url=https://query.nytimes.com/gst/fullpage.html?res=950DEEDE143AF93AA35754C0A96F948260 |access-date=February 18, 2009 |archive-date=May 7, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210507082546/https://www.nytimes.com/1989/07/09/movies/film-view-it-s-a-new-age-for-father-son-relationships.html |url-status=live}}</ref>

The film's [[teaser trailer]] debuted in November 1988 with ''[[Scrooged]]'' and ''[[The Naked Gun: From the Files of Police Squad!|The Naked Gun]]''.<ref>{{cite news |author=Aljean Harmetz |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1989/01/18/movies/makers-of-jones-sequel-offer-teasers-and-tidbits.html |title=Makers of 'Jones' Sequel Offer Teasers and Tidbits |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=January 18, 1989 |access-date=January 7, 2009 |archive-date=May 25, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150525122653/http://www.nytimes.com/1989/01/18/movies/makers-of-jones-sequel-offer-teasers-and-tidbits.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Rob MacGregor wrote the [[tie-in]] novelization that was released in June 1989;<ref>{{cite book |author=Rob MacGregor |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |publisher=[[Ballantine Books]] |date=September 1989 |url=http://www.randomhouse.com/rhpg/catalog/display.pperl?isbn=9780345361615 |isbn=978-0-345-36161-5 |access-date=July 5, 2008 |archive-date=May 7, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210507082554/https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/105355/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-by-rob-macgregor/9780345361615 |url-status=live }}</ref> it sold enough copies to be included on the [[New York Times Best Seller list|''New York Times'' Best Seller list]].<ref>{{cite news |author=Staff |title=Paperback Best Sellers: June 11, 1989 |date=June 11, 1989 |work=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> MacGregor went on to write the first six [[Indiana Jones#Novels|''Indiana Jones'' prequel novels]] during the 1990s. [[Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade (1991 video game)|A third game]] was produced by [[Taito]] and released in 1991 for the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]]. [[Ryder Windham]] wrote another novelization, released in April 2008 by [[Scholastic Corporation|Scholastic]], to coincide with the release of ''[[Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull]]''. [[Hasbro]] released toys based on ''The Last Crusade'' in July 2008.<ref>{{cite news |author=Edward Douglas |title=Hasbro Previews G.I. ==Home media==
The film was first released on VHS on February 1, 1990.  In 2003 it was made available along with its two predecessors as part of a trilogy [[DVD]] box set. It contained extra materials and a special documentary covering the production of the films. ==Reception==

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade'' has an approval rating of {{RT data|score}} based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average rating of 8/10. The site's critics consensus reads, "Lighter and more comedic than its predecessor, ''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade'' returns the series to the brisk serial adventure of ''Raiders'', while adding a dynamite double act between Harrison Ford and Sean Connery."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/indiana_jones_and_the_last_crusade|title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade (1989)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201031110119/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/indiana_jones_and_the_last_crusade|archive-date=October 31, 2020|url-status=live}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] calculated a weighted average score of 65 out of 100 based on 14 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=April 20, 2019 |archive-date=July 15, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190715101430/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade |url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web|date=December 20, 2018|title=Cinemascore :: Movie Title Search|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|url-status=dead|archive-date=December 20, 2018|access-date=July 27, 2020}}</ref>

[[File:Sean Connery (1983).jpg|thumb|upright|[[Sean Connery]] received Golden Globe and BAFTA nominations for his performance.]]
Jay Boyar of the ''[[Orlando Sentinel]]'' said that while the film "lacks the novelty of ''Raiders'', and the breathless pacing of ''Temple of Doom'', it was an entertaining capper to the trilogy."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1989-05-28-8905280320-story.html |title='CRUSADE' FITS NEATLY INTO INDY TRILOGY |date=May 28, 1989 |last=Boyar |first=Jay |access-date=June 20, 2020 |work=[[Orlando Sentinel]] |archive-date=June 5, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200605143627/https://www.orlandosentinel.com/news/os-xpm-1989-05-28-8905280320-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone (magazine)|Rolling Stone]]'' remarked the film was "the wildest and wittiest Indy of them all". [[Richard Corliss]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' and [[David Ansen]] of ''[[Newsweek]]'' praised it, as did [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]''.<ref name=pro/> "Though it seems to have the manner of some magically reconstituted B-movie of an earlier era, ''The Last Crusade'' is an endearing original," Canby wrote, calling the revelation that Jones had a father who was not proud of him to be a "comic surprise". Canby believed that while the film did not match the previous two in its pacing, it still had "hilariously off-the-wall sequences" such as the circus train chase. He also said that Spielberg was maturing by focusing on the father–son relationship,<ref>{{cite news |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/mem/movies/review.html?res=950DEFDB1139F93BA25755C0A96F948260 |author=Vincent Canby |title=Spielberg's Elixir Shows Signs Of Mature Magic |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=June 18, 1989 |access-date=February 5, 2009 |author-link=Vincent Canby}}{{Dead link|date=October 2018 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> a call echoed by McBride in ''Variety''.<ref name=variety>{{cite news |author=Joseph McBride |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |url=https://www.variety.com/review/VE1117791934.html?categoryid=31&cs=1 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=May 24, 1989 |access-date=February 23, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090721004330/http://www.variety.com/review/VE1117791934.html?categoryid=31&cs=1 |archive-date=July 21, 2009 |url-status=dead }}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of The Chicago-Sun Times gave the film a positive review, saying it was familiar if well made. Ebert praised the open sequence depicting Jones as a [[Scouts BSA (Boy Scouts of America)|Boy Scout]] with the Cross of Coronado as the most original and effective part, comparing it to the "style of illustration that appeared in the boys' adventure magazines of the 1940s". He said that Spielberg "must have been paging through his old issues of ''[[Boys' Life]]'' magazine...the feeling that you can stumble over astounding adventures just by going on a hike with your Scout troop. Spielberg lights the scene in the strong, basic colors of old pulp magazines."<ref>{{cite news |author=Roger Ebert |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/indiana-jones-and-the-last-crusade-1989 |date=May 24, 1989 |access-date=February 6, 2023 |author-link=Roger Ebert }}</ref> ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' felt Connery and Ford deserved Academy Award nominations.<ref name=pro/>

It was panned by [[Andrew Sarris]] in ''[[The New York Observer]]'', [[David Denby (film critic)|David Denby]] in ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' magazine, [[Stanley Kauffmann]] in ''[[The New Republic]]'' and Georgia Brown in ''[[The Village Voice]]''.<ref name=pro/> [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' called the film "soulless".<ref>{{cite news |author=Jonathan Rosenbaum |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |url=http://onfilm.chicagoreader.com/movies/capsules/4529_INDIANA_JONES_AND_THE_LAST_CRUSADE |work=[[Chicago Reader]] |access-date=January 7, 2009 |author-link=Jonathan Rosenbaum |archive-date=May 7, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210507082550/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/film/Section?oid=846987&path= |url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[The Washington Post]]'' reviewed the film twice; Hal Hinson's review on the day of the film's release was negative, describing it as "nearly all chases and dull exposition". Although he praised Ford and Connery, he felt the film's exploration of Jones's character took away his mystery and that Spielberg should not have tried to mature his storytelling.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/indianajonesandthelastcrusadepg13hinson_a0a93b.htm |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |author=Hal Hinson |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=May 24, 1989 |access-date=February 5, 2009 |archive-date=November 10, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121110102901/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/indianajonesandthelastcrusadepg13hinson_a0a93b.htm |url-status=live }}</ref> Two days later, [[Desson Thomson]] published a positive review praising the film's adventure and action, as well as the father–son relationship's thematic depth.<ref>{{cite news |author=Desson Thomson |title=Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/indianajonesandthelastcrusadepg13howe_a0b214.htm |date=May 26, 1989 |access-date=February 5, 2009 |author-link=Desson Thomson |archive-date=July 24, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080724134715/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/indianajonesandthelastcrusadepg13howe_a0b214.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 2025, ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' listed ''Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade'' as having the best stunts of 1989.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Doherty |first=Thomas |date=June 7, 2025 |title=The Best Stunts of All Time, Over Nearly 100 Years of the Oscars |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-stunts-all-time-movies-oscars-stunt-design-100-years/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250720043450/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-stunts-all-time-movies-oscars-stunt-design-100-years/ |archive-date=July 20, 2025}}</ref>

The prologue depicting Jones in his youth inspired Lucas to create ''[[The Young Indiana Jones Chronicles]]'' television series, which featured [[Sean Patrick Flanery]] as the young adult Indiana and [[Corey Carrier]] as the 8- to 10-year-old Indiana.<ref name=Hearn/> According to [[Dark Horse Comics]] author [[Lee Marrs]], Lucasfilm considered for a while to make a continuation to the film series starring Phoenix as a younger Jones, but these plans were dropped after his untimely death.<ref>{{Cite podcast |url= https://www.theindycast.com/indycast-episode-330/ |title= INDYCAST: EPISODE 330 |website= IndyCast |publisher= IndyCast |host= Ed Dolista |date= February 20, 2023 |time= |access-date= May 12, 2023}}</ref> The 13-year-old incarnation played by Phoenix in the film was the focus of a ''Young Indiana Jones'' series of young adult novels that began in 1990;<ref>{{cite book |author=William McCay |title=Young Indiana Jones and the Plantation Treasure |publisher=[[Random House]] |year=1990 |isbn=0-679-80579-6 |url=https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780679805793}}</ref> by the ninth novel, the series had become a tie-in to the television show.<ref>{{cite book |author=Les Martin |title=Young Indiana Jones and the Titanic Adventure |publisher=[[Random House]] |year=1993 |isbn=0-679-84925-4 |author-link=Les Martin}}</ref> German author [[Wolfgang Hohlbein]] revisited the prologue in one of his novels, in which Jones encounters the lead grave robber—whom Hohlbein christens Jake—in 1943.<ref>{{cite book |author=Wolfgang Hohlbein |title=Indiana Jones und das Verschwundene Volk |publisher=[[Goldmann (publisher)|Goldmann Verlag]] |year=1991 |isbn=3-442-41028-2 |author-link=Wolfgang Hohlbein}}</ref> The film's ending begins the 1995 comic series ''[[Indiana Jones and the Spear of Destiny]]'', which moves forward to depict Jones and his father searching for the [[Holy Lance]] in Ireland in 1945.<ref>{{Cite comic |writer=[[Elaine Lee]] |penciller=[[Dan Spiegle]] |title=[[Indiana Jones and the Spear of Destiny]] |issue=4 |date=April to July 1995 |publisher=[[Dark Horse Comics]]}}</ref> Spielberg intended to have Connery cameo as Henry in ''[[Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull]]'' (2008), but Connery turned it down as he had retired.<ref>{{Cite news |author=Lucasfilm |title=The Indiana Jones Cast Expands |publisher=IndianaJones.com |date=June 7, 2007 |url=http://www.indianajones.com/site/index.html?deeplink=news/n13 |access-date=February 15, 2009 |author-link=Lucasfilm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090215124713/http://www.indianajones.com/site/index.html?deeplink=news%2Fn13 |archive-date=February 15, 2009}}</ref>

Petra's use for the movie's climactic scenes greatly contributed to its popularity as an international tourist destination. Before the film's release, only a few thousand visitors per year made the trip; since then it has grown to almost a million annually.<ref name="Petra book">{{cite book |last=Comer |first=Douglas |title=Tourism and Archaeological Heritage Management at Petra: Driver to Development Or Destruction? |date=2011 |publisher=Springer |isbn=9781461414803 |pages=5–6 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=DwrArFQGm0YC&pg=PA5 |access-date=February 24, 2014 |archive-date=June 28, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140628102151/http://books.google.com/books?id=DwrArFQGm0YC&pg=PA5 |url-status=live }}</ref> Shops and hotels near the site play up the connection, and it is mentioned prominently in itineraries of locations used in the film series.<ref name="NBC News page">{{cite news |last=Bulger |first=Adam |title=Indiana Jones' Favorite Destinations |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna24619007 |newspaper=[[NBC News]] |date=May 21, 2008 |access-date=February 24, 2014 |archive-date=February 28, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140228031626/http://www.nbcnews.com/id/24619007/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Jordan's tourism board mentions the connection on its website.<ref name="Jordan tourist board website">{{cite web |title=petra |url=http://www.visitjordan.com/default.aspx?tabid=63 |publisher=Jordan Tourism Board |date=2010 |access-date=February 24, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161104133425/http://visitjordan.com/Default.aspx?tabid=63 |archive-date=November 4, 2016 |url-status=dead }}</ref> In 2012, the satirical news site [[The Pan-Arabia Enquirer]] ran a mock story claiming that the board had officially renamed Petra "That Place from ''Indiana Jones''" to reflect how the world more commonly refers to it.<ref name="Pan-Arabia Enquirer">{{cite web |title=Petra to be renamed 'That Place From Indiana Jones' |url=http://www.panarabiaenquirer.com/wordpress/petra-to-be-renamed-that-place-from-indiana-jones/ |publisher=The Pan-Arabia Enquirer |date=May 18, 2012 |access-date=February 24, 2014 |archive-date=February 28, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140228034913/http://www.panarabiaenquirer.com/wordpress/petra-to-be-renamed-that-place-from-indiana-jones/ |url-status=usurped }}</ref>
