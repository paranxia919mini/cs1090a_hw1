'''''The Unbelievable Truth''''' is a 1989 American [[comedy-drama]] film written and directed by [[Hal Hartley]]<ref>[https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8230-the-criterion-channels-september-2023-lineup The Criterion Channel’s September 2023 Lineup|Current|The Criterion Collection]</ref> and starring [[Adrienne Shelly]] in her film debut, [[Robert John Burke|Robert Burke]], Christopher Cooke, Julia McNeal, Gary Sauer, Mark Bailey, David Healy, Katherine Mayfield, [[Edie Falco]] and [[Matt Malloy]]. It tells the story of Audry, who dumps her high-school boyfriend and becomes a successful fashion model, but all along is in love with a mysterious man called Josh, released after conviction for manslaughter. He, after his experiences, is uncomfortable with relationships, but learns that he cannot stay an observer of life and must fight to win her. The film was nominated for a Grand Jury Prize in 1990 at the [[Sundance Film Festival]]. Along with ''[[Trust (1990 film)|Trust]]'' (1990) and ''[[Simple Men]]'' (1992), it is part of the "Long Island trilogy."<ref>[https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/35556/1/2011ManleySCPhD.pdf The Cinema of Hal Hartley: Place, Cultural Identity and Indie Authorship - Sebastian Christopher Manley - Thesis submitted for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy University of East Anglia, School of Film and Television Studies - June 2011]</ref>

==Plot==
Back from prison to a small community on Long Island comes Josh, a sober young man whose crimes most cannot remember exactly and finds a job at Vic's auto repair shop. Vic's daughter Audry falls instantly in love with him, only to be rejected when she declares her feelings because he is not ready for such a relationship and fears Vic's reaction. In revenge, she gives up the place she has won at Harvard and goes off to New York to be a photographic model, appearing first in lingerie and then nude.

Horrified, her parents send the reliable Josh off to the city to reclaim her, but he gives up in disgust when he finds she is living with her agent. Returning home, he meets the daughter of the man he is supposed to have killed in a struggle, who says she can testify that he is innocent. Audry, overjoyed that Josh has re-entered her life, gives her money-obsessed father all her earnings from modeling and again offers herself to Josh, who this time is overjoyed too.

==Production==
For the making of this film, his [[feature directorial debut]], Hal Hartley said he was influenced by European art films, particularly those of [[Jean-Luc Godard]], as well as by [[Howard Hawks]] and [[Preston Sturges]].<ref>{{cite book |last1=Wood |first1=Jason |title=100 American Independent Films |date=2004 |publisher=BFI |location=London |isbn=978-1-84-457005-8 |pages=229–231 |url=https://archive.org/details/100americanindep0000wood/page/228/mode/2up |access-date=20 December 2022}}</ref>

==Reception==
''The Unbelievable Truth'' received critical acclaim. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], it has a 100% rating based on reviews from 11 critics.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Unbelievable Truth (1990) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/unbelievable_truth |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=February 29, 2020}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 67 out of 100.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Unbelievable Truth Review |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-unbelievable-truth |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=February 29, 2020}}</ref> 
[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film three out of four stars.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ebert |first1=Roger |title=The Unbelievable Truth movie review |date=August 3, 1990 |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-unbelievable-truth-1990 |website=RogerEbert.com |access-date=29 February 2020}}</ref>

===Box office===
The film grossed $546,541 on a budget of an estimated $75,000.
