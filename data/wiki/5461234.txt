'''''The Apple''''' (also called '''''Star Rock''''') is a 1980 [[musical film|musical]] [[science fiction film]] written and directed by [[Menahem Golan]]. It stars [[Catherine Mary Stewart]] as a young singer named Bibi, who, in a futuristic 1994, signs to an evil label named Boogalow International Music. It deals with themes of conformity versus rebellion, and makes use of biblical allegory<ref name="Flavorwire">{{Cite web |last=Bailey |first=Jason |date=2017-07-11 |title=Bad Movie Night: The Distinctive Disco Cheese of 'The Apple' |url=https://www.flavorwire.com/607226/bad-movie-night-the-distinctive-disco-cheese-of-the-apple |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210123014250/https://www.flavorwire.com/607226/bad-movie-night-the-distinctive-disco-cheese-of-the-apple |archive-date=2021-01-23 |access-date=2017-12-09 |website=[[Flavorwire]] |language=en}}</ref> including the story of [[Adam and Eve]].

Principal photography took place in late 1979 in [[West Berlin]]. The film was panned by critics and has been considered to be [[List of 20th century films considered the worst#The Apple (1980)|one of the worst films ever made]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Brownfield |first=Troy |date=7 August 2020 |title=The Worst Movie Musicals Ever |url=https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2020/08/the-worst-movie-musicals-ever/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220111170729/https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2020/08/the-worst-movie-musicals-ever/ |archive-date=2022-01-11 |access-date=2022-01-11 |website=[[The Saturday Evening Post]]}}</ref><ref name="PhiladelphiaWeekly">{{Cite news |last=Burns |first=Sean |date=July 12, 2006 |title=Summer Camp |work=[[Philadelphia Weekly]]}}</ref>

==Plot==
In a cut<ref name="ProjectionBooth">{{Cite podcast |url=http://www.blogtalkradio.com/projectionbooth/2012/01/18/episode-46-the-apple-1980 |title=Episode 46: The Apple (1980) |website=[[The Projection Booth]] |publisher=[[BlogTalkRadio]] |last=White |first=Mike |date=18 January 2012 |access-date=14 November 2021}}</ref>{{rp|at=44:53–44:59|q="Paradise was [supposed to be] a large portion of the movie. Paradise never made the movie."}}<ref>{{Cite podcast |url=https://www.flophousepodcast.com/2018/02/episode-250-the-apple/ |title=Episode #250 – The Apple |website=[[The Flop House]] |publisher=[[Maximum Fun]] |last=McCoy |first=Dan |date=3 February 2018 |time=1:04:54–1:05:55 |access-date=14 November 2021}}</ref> two-song prologue of the film named "Paradise Day,"<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=41:06–41:13}}<ref name="LocationsTheApple" /> Mr. Topps ([[Joss Ackland]]) creates heaven and carves the first human, Alphie, out of a rock, sending Alphie to Earth to meet Bibi.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=41:10–41:49}}<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=42:31–42:36}}

The two take part in the 1994 Worldvision Song Festival. Despite being the most talented performers, they are beaten by BIM (Boogalow International Music) and its leader, Mr. Boogalow, who use underhanded tactics to secure a victory. The duo is approached by Mr. Boogalow to sign to his music label, but they soon discover the darker side of the music industry. Bibi is caught up in the wild lifestyle BIM offers, while Alphie risks his life to free her from the company's evil clutches. Alphie eventually convinces Bibi to run away with him, and the pair live as [[hippy|hippies]] for a year (and produce a child) before being located by Mr. Boogalow who insists Bibi owes him $10 million. Alphie and Bibi are saved by the [[Rapture]], and all good souls are taken away by Mr. Topps who arrives on the scene in a flying apparition of a Rolls-Royce.

==Production==
===Development===
====Conception====
In 1975, Coby Recht, a successful Israeli [[rock music|rock]] producer, was signed to a major label for the first time, that being [[Barclay Records]], which was founded and led by French producer [[Eddie Barclay]].<ref name = "SlashFilm1">Harris, Blake (5 February 2016). [http://www.slashfilm.com/the-apple-oral-history/1/ "How Did This Get Made: The Apple (An Oral History)"]. ''[[Slash Film|<nowiki>/</nowiki>Film]]''. p. 1. Retrieved 6 December 2017.</ref><ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=17:35–17:39}} As Recht described working with Barclay, "he really believed in me. But there was something there that I couldn't trust. I don't know why, but the guy looked to me like a villain."<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> His experience with Barclay, as well as being a "moral guy" who never liked what went on in show business, inspired the story of a musical Recht and his wife Iris Yotvat were conceiving for six weeks while in Paris,<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=41:10–41:49}}<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=17:27–18:36}}<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> and the antagonist Mr. Boogalow was based on Barclay.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Recht explained that it was "supposed to be ''[[Nineteen Eighty-Four|1984]]'', but with music."<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/>

[[File:Menahem Golan 034 edited.jpg|thumb|right|[[Menahem Golan]], writer, director and producer of ''The Apple'', made several revisions to the story of the musical that "frustrated" Recht and Yotvat.]]
Writing the musical in 1977,<ref name="FastRewindBehindtheScenes" /> Recht and Yotvat had 17 demos recorded before the first draft was even submitted to a company.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=20:23–20:24}} It was originally planned by Recht and Yotvat to be a Hebrew stage production, but the show "was so expensive that nobody could really raise it up for the stage," Recht explained.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> He was then informed by a friend that filmmaker [[Menahem Golan]] was having a brief visit in Israel.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Recht had known Golan since he was nine, performing in shows at an Israeli-based children's theater where Golan directed.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Recht called Golan on a Friday for a face-to-face meeting about ''The Apple'', which involved Golan spending four hours listening to demos of songs from the musical.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Golan finally decided to direct and produce a film version of ''The Apple'', instructing Recht to be in Los Angeles immediately.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Yotvat said, "That was marvelous. That was just fantastic to think that it was going to be a movie all of the sudden. It was just amazing."<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/>

====Rewrites for the film and song translation====
In 1979, Recht and Yotvat went to Los Angeles, where they resided in a villa that Golan offered,<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=20:48–20:58}} while Recht occasionally went to Golan's house in [[Cheviot Hills, Los Angeles|Cheviot Hills]] to have meetings about the film.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=21:44–22:00}} Golan spent three weeks writing six screenplay drafts of ''The Apple''.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=22:04–22:15}} According to Recht, Golan rewrote the story and all of the songs to the point where they were completely different from what Recht and Yotvat originally wrote,<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=21:10–21:24}} which "frustrated" the two.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Golan's version of the plot was more comical and contradicted the [[Orwellian]] tone of the musical Recht originally envisioned.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=25:30–26:06}} Yotvat said that Golan was turning the script into "something that was kind of corny,"<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> and Recht stated Golan was making the story "out of touch" and "out of date."<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=21:08–21:09}} He also disliked Golan's criticisms of Recht and Yotvat's plot not having any "action."<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Recht said in a retrospective interview, "It's a small movie. It's rock and roll. And then he said to me, 'Eh…Rockin' Horse, Rock n' Roll.' So what can you do?"<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/>

[[George S. Clinton]], who had a previous career as a songwriter and recording artist, joined the project to translate the lyrics from Hebrew to English after being recommended for the job by Golan's secretary Kathy Doyle.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=22:59–23:27}} Clinton worked with Golan, Recht and Yotvat on the translations at Golan's house on afternoons.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=24:14–24:19}} Recht and Yotvat had a much more favorable time working with Clinton than with Golan, who translated the Hebrew lyrics into English.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> As Recht explained, "working with George was great, I loved it because we were both lyricists."<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> After the translations were finished, the songs were tracked on a tape recorder and taken to Golan's home studio for the demos to be recorded, which Clinton sang on.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=24:20–24:32}}

===Auditions===
[[File:Catherine Mary Stewart 2012.jpg|thumb|left|[[Catherine Mary Stewart]] originally went to auditions for ''The Apple'' as a dancer, but was chosen by Recht for playing the lead role of Bibi.]]
Approximately 50% of the demos for the English versions of the songs were finished by the time work on ''The Apple'' moved to London,<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> where two months worth of auditions, rehearsals, and recording and mixing of the score took place.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /><ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=25:15–25:26}} The auditions for ''The Apple'' were held at [[Turnham Green]]<ref name="FastRewindBehindtheScenes">{{Cite web |title=Behind the Scenes of The Apple Movie |url=http://www.fast-rewind.com/making_theapple.htm |access-date=2 August 2010 |website=Fast Rewind}}</ref> for about a week, with around 1,000 people auditioning.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=29:12–29:18}} As Clinton described them, "We had auditioned a lot of different people, and it was crazy. I mean, people came from all over [the world] to be there, and there were so many people."<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=17:19–27:28}} In fact, one of the people who auditioned for the film, who was from Wales, became so impatient while waiting that he yelled at Clinton and the other people auditioning to "finish the god damn song."<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=27:49–28:00}}

Around 200 people attended the dancer auditions, one of them [[Catherine Mary Stewart]], a performing arts school attendee who was informed by the other students about the auditions.<ref name="SlashFilm2">{{Cite web |last=Harris |first=Blake |date=2016-02-05 |title=The Apple Oral History: How Did This Get Made |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/542502/the-apple-oral-history/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220112025341/https://www.slashfilm.com/542502/the-apple-oral-history/ |archive-date=2022-01-12 |access-date=2017-12-06 |website=[[/Film]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Recht took particular notice of Stewart.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> He recalled, "all of the sudden I saw her and I said to myself: This is Bibi. This is exactly what I had in mind when I wrote the script."<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Stewart explained in a 2012 interview that while she had vocal training in her performing arts school, she didn't have the same "professional" singing skills of other members of the film's cast.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=30:46–31:14}} Despite this, Recht wanted her for the part of Bibi, and thus, she was cast for the role.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Recht described Stewart as "shocked" and "trembling all over" when she got the part.<ref name="SlashFilm2" />

Recht lied to Golan that Stewart "was a great singer" to convince Golan to cast her Bibi.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Golan eventually noticed her poor singing, and he, as Stewart explained, "sent me to a voice coach who actually thought I was fine for the movie, but [the producers] kind of got cold feet."<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Thus, Mary Hylan, a "terrific professional singer from L.A." as Stewart described her, served as Bibi's singing voice for the film.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Clinton first noticed Hylan singing at a Christmas party in 1978, and "she just blew me away," he recalled.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=32:32–32:41}}

===Filming===
[[File:ICC & Funkturm.jpg|thumb|right|[[Internationales Congress Centrum Berlin]] was the location for the opening concert scene of ''The Apple''.]]
According to Recht, he and Yotvat initially planned ''The Apple'' to cost $4 million to produce, "but when we were in London, Menahem [Golan] used to go like every week to Berlin and come back with another million. And then we got to $10 million."<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> This led Golan to shoot scenes with settings that looked bigger than what Recht wanted, which he was dissatisfied with.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Principal photography took place from September to December 1979 in [[West Berlin]], where the production was moved in exchange for government subsidies from [[West Germany]].<ref name="LocationsTheApple">{{Cite web |title=The Apple Movie Filming Locations |url=http://www.fast-rewind.com/locations_theapple.htm |access-date=2 August 2010 |website=Fast Rewind}}</ref><ref name="afi" /> ''The Apple'' editor Alain Jakubowicz claimed that, as with Golan's previous works, the original footage was around 1 million feet long, or four hours worth, and "five to six" cameras were used to shoot the musical sequences.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> The opening concert sequence was shot at the main hall of [[Internationales Congress Centrum Berlin]]<ref name="LocationsTheApple" /> and was filmed for five days in a row.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=55:59–56:11}} The sequence for the track "Speed" was filmed at the [[Metropol (Berlin)|Metropol]] nightclub, which held the [[Guinness World Record]] for biggest indoor laser show.<ref name="LocationsTheApple" /> The outside hippie scenes were filmed at Schloss-Park, close to Königstraße.<ref name="LocationsTheApple" /> Some scenes in the film were shot in a factory that formerly produced poison gas during World War II.<ref name="YahooMusic">{{Cite web |last=Parker |first=Lyndsey |date=2015-11-16 |title=Nigel Lythgoe on His Disco Cult Movie, 'The Apple': 'The Best Part of Making It Was Finishing It' |url=https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/nigel-lythgoe-on-his-disco-cult-movie-the-014657840.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220131072436/https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/nigel-lythgoe-on-his-disco-cult-movie-the-014657840.html |archive-date=2022-01-31 |access-date=2017-12-19 |website=[[Yahoo! Music]] |language=en-US}}</ref>
[[File:Nigel Lythgoe.jpg|thumb|left|[[Nigel Lythgoe]], who later became notable for his work on the singing competition show ''[[American Idol]]'', choreographed ''The Apple''.]]
A majority of the extras for ''The Apple'' were students at Berlin American High School.<ref name="FastRewindBehindtheScenes" /> [[Ray Shell]], who portrayed Shake and described making the film as "big fun," was replaced by a stunt double for his dancing shoots four times, and a scene for a song titled "Slip and Slide" was cut from the final product due to Shell's poor dancing.<ref name="FastRewindBehindtheScenes" /> [[Nigel Lythgoe]] and [[Ken Warwick]] served as choreographers for ''The Apple'', Warwick featured in the film dancing in the sequence for "Coming."<ref name="YahooMusic" /> Lythgoe described making ''The Apple'' as "really, really depressing on some days" and "very, very stressful," adding that most of the cast and crew "didn't really like the script. I mean, we really didn't."<ref name="YahooMusic" /> Lythgoe and Warwick later both started working in singing competition shows like ''[[American Idol]]'', which led to [[conspiracy theories]] by multiple people that ''The Apple'' predicted their later work.<ref name="YahooMusic" /> Lythgoe shrugged these off: "There were a lot of singing competitions going on at the time. So it never really hit me that 'the search for talent' was ''Popstars'' and ''Pop Idol'' and ''American Idol'', as much as just thinking that ''The Apple'' was kind of a Eurovision-style song competition.<ref name="YahooMusic" />

Conflicts between Golan and Recht took place when filming started; Recht was still in London mixing the songs, and Golan constantly made phone calls demanding him to be at the shoots in Berlin.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> When he went to Berlin to go to the shooting location, the first thing he saw was Golan filming the uncut "Paradise Day" sequence<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=41:27–41:49}} that took a million dollars alone to make:<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> "He was shooting this part that never ended up on the screen because it was terrible. It was terrible. There was like fifteen dinosaurs on the set. I couldn't believe my eyes."<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> The scene was filmed at an exterior location at [[CCC Film]]'s [[Spandau Studios]], a studio which was also used as the location for Boogalow's penthouse.<ref name="LocationsTheApple" /> Joss Ackland, who portrayed Mr. Topps, recalled that the scene consisted of a combination of both real and "phony" animals<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=43:55–44:00}} and the Mr. Topps character was in a crevice making Alphie and singing the song "Creation."<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=44:00–44:15}} Multiple problems occurred shooting the sequence, such as the dinosaur pieces falling down and a tiger running from the set.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=44:18–44:23}} The uncut scene also involved both Mr. Topps and the devil character Mr. Boogalow doing a dance together, which involved Boogalow actor Vladek Sheybal falling on a river.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=44:38–44:44}}

===Post-production===
Dov Hoenig was an editor for Golan's previous films and was initially planned to also be the editor of ''The Apple''.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> He also was on set with Golan on shooting days for the film.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> Recht described him as his "spiritual partner," reasoning that he was the only person working on the movie that agreed with Recht and Yotvat's stylistic and production ideas.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> However, Golan and Hoenig always had arguments with each other during the making of Golan's other works, and when working on ''The Apple'', they nearly got into a fistfight.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> It was by the time Hoenig suggested to Golan that a shot was "out of focus" that he fired him from the project.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> This and the shooting of what Recht and Yotvat considered the film's "horrible" ending sequence is what persuaded the two to leave Berlin and go back to Israel.<ref name="SlashFilm2" />

Jakubowicz replaced Hoenig's position and claimed in a retrospective that he enjoyed editing ''The Apple'', especially the film's musical scenes.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> However, he also described it as a "weird experience" due to the film's over-the-top nature and kitsch plot.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> ''The Apple'' is edited in a fast-paced [[music video]]-esque manner.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=58:10–58:20}} According to Ackland, "Heaven was three quarters of the script. It was the whole point of the movie."<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=58:36–58:48}} Cutting out most of the story's heaven aspects, including the "Paradise Day" scene, were Golan's methods of not going too "extreme" with the religious undertones in order for the film to be more "acceptable" and less "formidable" for audiences.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=45:17–45:48}} Yotvat suggested Golan's added comical elements of the story was also due to his focus on the film's accessibility.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=48:13–48:30}} Jakubowicz later edited another musical film Golan worked on, an adaptation of ''[[The Threepenny Opera]]'' (1928) named ''[[Mack the Knife (1989 film)|Mack the Knife]]'' (1989).<ref name="SlashFilm2" />

==Music==
The recording and mixing of ''The Apple''<nowiki>'</nowiki>s score took place at The Music Centre, a studio in Wembley,<ref name="Linernotes">''The Apple: The Original Soundtrack Of The Musical Film'' (1980) (Back cover). Various Artists. Cannon. C1001.</ref> and cost an "amazing budget" to complete, Recht said.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=33:10–33:14}} Richard Goldblatt served as the audio engineer.<ref name="Linernotes" /> The tracks were remixed to Dolby Stereo on a [[Neve console]] for the film by [[The Village Recorder]], the only studio Recht could find that had a Neve.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=35:04–35:58}} The studio charged $7,000 a day for remixing the tracks, and Golan sent a messenger to give the studio money.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=35:34–35:46}} ''The Apple''<nowiki>'</nowiki>s soundtrack features sounds and production techniques that were never heard on any other record before, such as the use of [[pink noise]] on the snare drums.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=36:06–36:16}} The [[London Philharmonic Orchestra]] performed the orchestral arrangements for both the songs and score for the film.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=36:18–36:40}} Clinton served as the conductor and was initially "scared shitless" as it was his first time conducting a full professional orchestra.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=36:40–36:54}} Instead of a baton, he conducted the orchestra with chopsticks he received from a Chinese restaurant that was two buildings away from the studio where the soundtrack was recorded.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=36:55–37:07}}

Recht recalled one of the sessions having more than 200 people, including several musicians and a choir consisting of 120 vocalists.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=33:25–33:48}} Recht said recording the film's [[reggae music|reggae]] number "How To Be a Master" was complicated due to actual Jamaican musicians performing on the song and not being able to "read the charts" that were in English.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=33:52–34:14}} According to Clinton, [[Grace Kennedy (singer)|Grace Kennedy]], who played Pandi, had an uncomfortable time singing "Coming," so Clinton had to tell her that it was her character singing the lyrics and not Kennedy.<ref name="ProjectionBooth" />{{rp|at=37:29–34:44}}

==Themes==
Described by Francis Rizzo of ''DVD Talk'' as the [[Faust legend]] told through the lens of the music industry,<ref name="DVDTalk">{{Cite web |last=Rizzo |first=Francis |date=2017-06-20 |title=The Apple (Blu-Ray) |url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/72162/apple-the/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190305185404/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/72162/apple-the/ |archive-date=2019-03-05 |access-date=2017-12-09 |website=[[DVD Talk]]}}</ref> ''The Apple'' is about a conflict between good and evil, a battle between the [[pacifistic]] [[hippie]]s and show business people who only care about wealth and power.<ref name = "SlashFilm1"/> Journalist Richard Harland Smith {{who|date=May 2021}}, likening ''The Apple'' to a "[[Christian film|Christian scare film]]," compared the story to [[Adolf Hitler]]'s ''[[Mein Kampf]]'' (1925) and its ending to the [[Nazi]]'s [[Final Solution]];<ref name="TCM" /> Smith wrote that the film involves a homophobic protagonist Alphie who is trying to save his "normal" relationship with Bibi from what the film considers satanic, which includes [[electronic music]], glitter, homosexuals, and drag queens.<ref name="TCM">{{Cite web |last=Harland Smith |first=Richard |title=The Apple (1980) |url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/67529/the-apple#articles-reviews?articleId=181351 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201019042739/https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/67529/the-apple/#articles-reviews?articleId=181351 |archive-date=2020-10-19 |access-date=2017-12-16 |website=[[Turner Classic Movies]]}}</ref>

==Release and reception==
Stewart described how Golan predicted the success of ''The Apple'':

{{blockquote|Menahem was very passionate about what he was doing. He had very lofty ideas about the project. He thought this was going to break him into the American film industry. It had, you know, all the elements that he thought were necessary at that time. It was the early eighties and there were a lot of musicals. And Menahem thought that was his ticket into the American film industry.<ref name="SlashFilm2" />}}

''The Apple'' made its premiere as the opener of the 1980 [[Montreal World Film Festival]], where its attendees received [[vinyl records]] of music from the film; the festival's president [[Serge Losique]]’s son recommended he screen the movie.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> According to Jakubowicz, there were "bad reviews almost everywhere."<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> The movie received a largely negative audience at the festival, some watchers throwing the vinyl records at the screen.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> According to Stewart, Golan was so distraught from this that after the festival, he considered killing himself by jumping off a hotel balcony.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> As Golan once said, "It's impossible that I'm so wrong about it. I cannot be that wrong about the movie. They just don't understand what I was trying to do."<ref name="SlashFilm2" />

Common criticism from both reviews that appeared in trade publications and major news outlets and the audience were a lack of originality,<ref name="afi" /> a weak script,<ref name="afi" /> uninspired music,<ref name="afi" /> poor execution,<ref name="afi" /> and Golan's inexperienced take on the 1960s hippie movement.<ref name="SlashFilm2" /> ''Variety'' gave a negative review of the film, stating that "the characters and story are flimsy and seem intended as mere pegs on which to hang the musical numbers" and that "the choreography generates a lot of energy, but its frantic tempo doesn't always compensate for lack of imagination." However, the review also complimented the film's technical aspects, particularly that "David Gurfinkel's camera work profusely [uses] every trick in the business, every filter and lighting device, to hold attention up at all times."{{citation needed|date=February 2022}} The ''[[Monthly Film Bulletin]]'' described ''The Apple'' as a "cut-price extravaganza [that] plummets to a new low in opportunistic inanity," and said that the "sole saving grace is an enthusiastically camp performance by Vladek Skeybal."<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Pulleine |first=Tim |year=1982 |title=Star-Rock |magazine=[[Monthly Film Bulletin]] |location=London |publisher=British Film Institute |volume=49 |issue=576 |page=137}}</ref> ''[[The Ottawa Citizen]]'' described ''The Apple'' (along with Golan's earlier film ''[[The Magician of Lublin (film)|The Magician of Lublin]]'') as containing "remarkable feats of ineptitude".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Labonte |first=Richard |date=November 28, 1981 |title=Kung Fu Film Flops |page=39 |work=The Ottawa Citizen}}</ref> On Rotten Tomatoes, the film has a 27% score based on 11 reviews, with an average rating of 4/10.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Apple (1980) |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1001131-apple/ |access-date=December 4, 2021 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]]}}</ref> On Metacritic, the film has a [[weighted mean|weighted average score]] of 39 out of 100, based on 5 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable reviews".<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Apple (1980) reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-apple |access-date=April 4, 2020 |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]}}</ref>

At the 1980 Stinkers Bad Movie Awards, the film was nominated for Worst Picture. According to Stinkers founder Mike Lancaster, ''The Apple'' was technically the worst movie of the year—and the worst he had ever seen—but its profile was too low to really warrant winning the award. Instead, Worst Picture went to ''[[Popeye (1980 film)|Popeye]]''. The ballot was revised and re-released in 2006, where the Worst Picture results remained the same. In addition, the film received six more nominations, two of which were wins: Worst Director (Golan) and Least “Special” Special Effects. Its other four nominations were for Worst Screenplay, Worst Song (both “B.I.M.” and “Universal Melody”), and Most Intrusive Musical Score.<ref>{{Cite web |date=August 15, 2007 |title=Past Winners Database |url=http://theenvelope.latimes.com/extras/lostmind/year/1980/1980st.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070815004414/http://theenvelope.latimes.com/extras/lostmind/year/1980/1980st.htm |archive-date=August 15, 2007}}</ref>

From retrospective reviews, Eric Henderson of ''Slant'' magazine gave ''The Apple'' one star out of four and said "every song in the goddamned movie sucks" and added that the film's "relentless bad taste is sure to appeal to the same audience that won't even realize they're being slapped in the face".<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=August 24, 2004 |title=''The Apple'' Review |url=http://www.slantmagazine.com/film/review/the-apple |magazine=Slant |access-date=January 29, 2015}}</ref> ''[[TV Guide]]'' stated "''The Apple'' clearly was designed to duplicate the success of ''[[The Rocky Horror Picture Show]]'' (1975) and failed dismally, in large part because the music is so stupendously banal...The lesson: Making a cult hit is harder than it looks."<ref>{{Cite magazine |title=The Apple |url=https://www.tvguide.com/movies/the-apple/2000273527/ |magazine=[[TV Guide]] |language=en |access-date=2022-02-13}}</ref> Sean Burns in the ''Philadelphia Weekly'' gave the film a scathing review: "''The Apple'' isn't just the worst disco musical ever made; it could very well be the worst movie ever made, period."<ref name="PhiladelphiaWeekly" /> Bill Gibron of [[DVD Verdict]] described ''The Apple'' as "a gamy glitterdome of outrageous kitsch passing itself off as a futuristic fable."<ref name="dv">{{Cite web |last=Gibron |first=Bill |title="The Apple" Review |url=http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/theapple.php |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040921064403/http://www.dvdverdict.com/reviews/theapple.php |archive-date=September 21, 2004 |access-date=11 February 2015 |website=DVD Verdict |df=mdy-all}}</ref> Gibron strongly criticised the film's music, saying "Lines fail to rhyme, emotions are so spelled out that inbred invertebrates can figure out the meaning, and everything feels like it was produced by [[Giorgio Moroder|Georgio Moroder]]'s {{sic}} insane brother...''The Apple'' should be a celebration of all that is camp. Instead, it's just seriously disturbed."<ref name="dv" />

However, articles about the film's 2017 Blu-ray release were much more favorable towards ''The Apple''. Austin Trunick honored it as "the perfect sort of cult film – unintentionally campy, with an insane premise, great production value, and memorable songs – that most people, once they've seen it, spend the rest of their movie-loving lives trying to get it in front of as many other people as possible."<ref name="UndertheRadar">{{Cite web |last=Trunick |first=Austin |date=2017-06-27 |title=The Apple |url=https://www.undertheradarmag.com/reviews/the_apple |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190414200357/http://www.undertheradarmag.com/reviews/the_apple/ |archive-date=2019-04-14 |access-date=2017-12-09 |website=[[Under the Radar (magazine)|Under the Radar]] |language=en}}</ref> Rizzo praised it as an example of "a filmmaker trying to do too much and attempting to punch above their weight, rather than doing the lazy, same-old thing."<ref name="DVDTalk" /> He concluded, "It may not be a great (or even good) movie, but ''The Apple'' is certainly an experience, one that any fan of wondrously bad cinema or over-the-top musicals will suffer gladly, luxuriating in its wacky vision of the then-future and idiosyncratic tale of good versus evil."<ref name="DVDTalk" /> ''Flavorwire'' claimed "True bad movies carve out their own worlds. ''The Apple'' seems to have come from another universe entirely – and God bless it for that."<ref name="Flavorwire" /> They reasoned, "all through The Apple, everyone is just going for it, full-throated, even though the songs are shit and the sets are cheap and the effects are worse. But you get the sense that you couldn't have told them any of that. Whatever Golan had, it was something everyone involved was snorting (literally or figuratively)."<ref name="Flavorwire" /> On the web-show [[Red_Letter_Media#Best_of_the_Worst_(2013–present)|Best of the Worst]] RLM's Rich Evans stated "Seeing this is just kind of like a revelation to me. Like, oh my God, there's a point in time where they [The Cannon Group] tried. There's a lot of effort in this movie. It doesn't always work, but god damn do you see the effort in every [[Oh Brother, Where Art Thou?|Homer Simpson car]]."<ref>{{Citation |title=Best of the Worst: Star Slammer, Talos, and The Apple | date=July 20, 2025 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AV95LmBqFJU |language=en |access-date=2025-07-20}}</ref>

==Alternate version==
In 2008, there was a mix-up booking the print for a screening at The Silent Movie Theatre in Los Angeles, so MGM sent over uninspected reels marked "Screening Print."<ref name="Idolator">{{Cite web |last=Jensen |first=Lucas |date=December 11, 2008 |title="The Apple": The Lost Footage, Revealed |url=https://www.idolator.com/5107678/the-apple-the-lost-footage-revealed?view-all |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20220213025249/https://www.idolator.com/5107678/the-apple-the-lost-footage-revealed?chrome=1 |archive-date=February 13, 2022 |access-date=2022-02-13 |website=[[Idolator (website)|Idolator]] }}</ref> Presumably this was an original preview print, as it included additional scenes that were cut out of the widely released version (including the complete ''Coming'' and ''Child of Love'' musical sequences which had been truncated in the final print), a simpler entrance for Mr. Topps at the end (instead of exiting from a Rolls-Royce, he merely transforms from the previously seen hippie leader), and the closing credits were presented in a different font and layout.<ref name=Idolator/> This version was screened a few times at The Silent Movie Theatre,<ref name=Idolator/> and it ran at [[Alamo Drafthouse Cinema]] in August 2008.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Austin - The Apple |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/austin-the-apple/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120918102041/http://www.viceland.com/blogs/en/2008/08/14/austin-the-apple/ |archive-date=2012-09-18 |access-date=2022-02-13 |website=[[Vice (magazine)|Vice]] |date=August 14, 2008 |language=en}}</ref>

==Home media==
''The Apple'' was released on a Region 1 DVD by [[MGM Home Entertainment]] on August 24, 2004. Scorpion Releasing under license from MGM, released the film onto Blu-Ray in 2017 which featured a brand new 2016 HD master, audio commentary by star Catherine Mary Stewart moderated by film historian Nathaniel Thompson, On Camera Interview with star Catherine Mary Stewart, and Original Theatrical Trailer. On April 17, 2013, the film was released as a video on demand from RiffTrax. This edition of the film features a satirical commentary done by the former stars of ''[[Mystery Science Theater 3000]]'' - [[Michael J. Nelson]], [[Kevin Murphy (actor)|Kevin Murphy]], and [[Bill Corbett]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2013-04-16 |title=Now Available on VOD from RiffTrax… |url=http://www.mst3kinfo.com/?p=15217 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180926112703/http://www.mst3kinfo.com/?p=15217 |archive-date=2018-09-26 |access-date=2022-02-13 |website=Satellite News}}</ref>
