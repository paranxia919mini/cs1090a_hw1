'''''Green Room''''' is a 2015 American [[horror film|horror]]-[[thriller film]]<ref>{{cite web | title=Green Room | url=https://a24films.com/films/green-room }}</ref><ref name=VarietyReview /> written and directed by [[Jeremy Saulnier]], and produced by [[Neil Kopp]], Victor Moyers and Anish Savjani. Starring [[Anton Yelchin]], [[Imogen Poots]], [[Alia Shawkat]], [[Joe Cole (actor)|Joe Cole]], [[Callum Turner]] and [[Patrick Stewart]], the film focuses on a punk band who find themselves attacked by [[White power skinhead|neo-Nazi skinheads]] after witnessing a murder at a remote club in the [[Pacific Northwest]].

Principal photography took place during October 2014 in [[Portland, Oregon]]. The film was financed and produced by [[Broad Green Pictures]]. ''Green Room'' was screened in the [[Directors' Fortnight]] section at the [[2015 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="Quinzaine">{{cite web|url=http://www.quinzaine-realisateurs.com/the-directors-fortnight-2015-selection-p487.html |title=The Directors' Fortnight 2015 selection! |access-date=21 April 2015 |work=Quinzaine des Réalisateurs |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150423224901/http://www.quinzaine-realisateurs.com/the-directors-fortnight-2015-selection-p487.html |archive-date=April 23, 2015 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> At the [[2015 Toronto International Film Festival]], the film finished third in the balloting for the Grolsch People's Choice Midnight Madness Award.<ref>{{cite press release |date=2015-09-20 |access-date=2015-09-21 |title=Toronto International Film Festival Announces 2015 Award Winners |url=https://s3.amazonaws.com/presscontent.tiff.net/docs/48vk20_Festival_Awards_2015__8877337_1442771061.pdf |publisher=TIFF |archive-date=March 3, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303175312/https://s3.amazonaws.com/presscontent.tiff.net/docs/48vk20_Festival_Awards_2015__8877337_1442771061.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> The film began a [[limited release]] on April 15, 2016, before being [[Wide release|widely released]] on May 13 through [[A24]].<ref name="Jaugernath">{{cite web|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/watch-arm-snapping-punk-rock-red-band-trailer-for-jeremy-saulniers-green-room-starring-patrick-stewart-20160202|title=Watch: Arm-Snapping, Punk Rock, Red-Band Trailer For Jeremy Saulnier's 'Green Room' Starring Patrick Stewart|publisher=[[Indiewire.com]]|first=Kevin|last=Jaugernath|date=February 2, 2016|access-date=February 12, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160205193641/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/watch-arm-snapping-punk-rock-red-band-trailer-for-jeremy-saulniers-green-room-starring-patrick-stewart-20160202|archive-date=February 5, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref> It appeared on many critics' lists as one of the best films of 2016 and received a 2017 [[Empire Award]] nomination for [[Empire Award for Best Horror|Best Horror]], and grossed $3.8 million against a budget of $5 million. This was Anton Yelchin's final film released during his lifetime.

==Plot summary==
Pat, Sam, Reece and Tiger are members of a punk band, the Ain't Rights, traveling through the [[Pacific Northwest]]. After their gig is cancelled, a local radio host, Tad, arranges a show through his cousin, Daniel, at a [[neo-Nazi skinhead]] bar in the woods outside [[Portland, Oregon|Portland]], opening for the [[Nazi metal]] band Cowcatcher.

After the show, Pat returns to the [[green room]] to retrieve Sam's phone. He sees the body of a woman, Emily, who has been stabbed to death by Werm, a member of Cowcatcher. Pat calls the police, but the bar employees Gabe and Big Justin confiscate the band's phones and hold them captive in the green room with Emily's friend Amber. Gabe pays a skinhead to stab another to create a cover story for the police who respond to the call. He consults with the bar owner and skinhead leader, Darcy, who decides to gift Cowcatcher with poisoned drugs and kill the band to eliminate witnesses.

The band overpowers Big Justin and holds him hostage, taking a box cutter from his pocket and his pistol. They negotiate through the door with Darcy, who asks them to surrender the pistol. Pat agrees, but as he opens the door, Amber realizes it is a trap. Darcy and his men slash at Pat's arm and partially sever his hand with machetes until he drops the gun, but he closes the door. Big Justin attacks the band, but Reece chokes him into unconsciousness. When Big Justin surprises Reece by awaking again, Reece chokes Big Justin again and Amber cuts him open to ensure that he is dead.

Searching for a way out, the band discovers an underground drug lab, but the only exit is locked from the outside. Arming themselves with improvised weapons, they exit the green room into the empty club.  A skinhead, Clark, unleashes an attack dog, which kills Tiger. Amber and Pat drive the dog away with microphone feedback. Reece flees through a window, but is murdered by a skinhead.

Pat, Amber, and Sam retreat to the green room. Darcy sends Daniel into the club to kill the band, who Darcy claims murdered Emily. Amber tells Daniel that Werm murdered Emily after discovering Daniel and Emily planned to leave the skinheads. Daniel agrees to help them escape and leads the band into the club, where he is shot dead by the bartender. The group kills the bartender and takes his shotgun, but Clark's dog kills Sam. Darcy's men wound Amber, who retreats to the green room with Pat.

As Pat and Amber come up with a survival plan, Darcy sends skinheads Jonathan and Kyle with Gabe to kill Pat and Amber, and he leaves with the bodies, planning to stage their deaths to appear as though they were killed while trespassing. Pat lures Jonathan into the drug lab while Kyle remains in the green room, where Amber ambushes and kills him. As Pat fights Jonathan, Amber sneaks up behind the latter and shoots him. Gabe then surrenders to Pat and Amber, fearing prison time for his involvement.

Holding Gabe at gunpoint, the group trek through the woods. When Pat hears Darcy and his men staging the deaths, he and Amber go after them. Gabe volunteers to go into a farm and call the police. Pat and Amber kill Clark and another skinhead. Fleeing, Darcy pulls a revolver from his jacket but is shot dead by Pat and Amber. The two sit on the side of the road and wait for the police.

==Production==
The film came from Saulnier's desire to create a thriller set in a green room, calling the idea "an obsession".<ref name="slashfilmfirstpage"/>  Saulnier created a short film set in one as part of a 48-hour film challenge in 2007 which involved the [[supernatural]] and according to Saulnier was "Really kind of fun and hammy." However, he still wanted a chance to do his green room movie "the right way".<ref name="slashfilmfirstpage">{{cite web|last1=Hall|first1=Jacob|title=Interview: 'Green Room' Director Jeremy Saulnier on Patrick Stewart, Inept Heroes, and "Full-Frontal Gore"|url=http://www.slashfilm.com/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview/|website=Slashfilm|date=April 13, 2016|access-date=26 July 2016|page=1|archive-date=July 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160718073056/http://www.slashfilm.com/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview/|url-status=live}}</ref> Although the film features a large amount of violence and what Saulnier calls "full frontal gore", he has gone on record as stating that it is not "sadistic", and that every act of violence apart from the initiating incident is done with a reason.<ref name="slashfilmpagetwo"/>  As such Saulnier made sure that there were no "gratuitous close ups" of recently deceased characters.<ref name="slashfilmpagetwo">{{cite web|last1=Hall|first1=Jacob|title=Interview: 'Green Room' Director Jeremy Saulnier on Patrick Stewart, Inept Heroes, and "Full-Frontal Gore"|url=http://www.slashfilm.com/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview/2/|website=Slashfilm|date=April 13, 2016|access-date=26 July 2016|archive-date=July 18, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160718071639/http://www.slashfilm.com/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview/2/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Music===
Saulnier, who used to play in a [[hardcore punk]] band called No Turn on Fred,<ref>{{cite news |last1=Eisenberg |first1=Ophira |title=Alia Shawkat And Jeremy Saulnier: In The 'Green Room' |url=https://www.npr.org/2016/04/19/476154880/alia-shawkat-and-jeremy-saulnier-in-the-green-room |access-date=March 3, 2021 |work=[[Ask Me Another (radio program)|Ask Me Another]] |publisher=[[NPR]] |date=April 29, 2016 |archive-date=April 2, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220402210344/https://www.npr.org/2016/04/19/476154880/alia-shawkat-and-jeremy-saulnier-in-the-green-room |url-status=live }}</ref> wanted the film to "stand the test of real musicians scrutinizing every frame".<ref>{{cite news |last1=McDermott |first1=Patrick D. |title=''Green Room'' Is Punk Because It Doesn't Really Try To Be |url=https://www.thefader.com/2016/04/26/green-room-movie-jeremy-saulnier-interview |access-date=March 3, 2021 |work=[[The Fader]] |date=April 26, 2016 |archive-date=September 26, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240926212817/https://www.thefader.com/2016/04/26/green-room-movie-jeremy-saulnier-interview |url-status=live }}</ref> He enlisted [[Hutch Harris]] of American [[indie rock]] band [[The Thermals]] to teach the actors the musical parts that they would be performing onscreen.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Harris |first1=Hutch |title=The Ain't Rights are All Right: Four Days in the Green Room |url=https://www.talkhouse.com/aint-rights-right-four-days-green-room/ |access-date=March 3, 2021 |work=[[Talkhouse]] |date=June 7, 2016 |archive-date=September 26, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240926212819/https://www.talkhouse.com/aint-rights-right-four-days-green-room/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The film's soundtrack is largely populated by [[Heavy metal music|heavy metal]] artists like Midnight rather than [[White power music|white nationalist bands]]. Saulnier says that he wanted the club to have more of a [[Motörhead]]-like atmosphere, and that he had no intention of financially supporting white nationalist artists.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Yanick |first1=Joe |title=Director Jeremy Saulnier Talks About Getting the Punks-Versus-Skins Dynamic Right in 'Green Room' |url=https://www.vice.com/en/article/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview/ |access-date=March 3, 2021 |work=Noisey |publisher=VICE |date=April 11, 2016 |archive-date=December 7, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221207061214/https://www.vice.com/en/article/rgpeg7/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-interview |url-status=live }}</ref>

On October 29, 2014, WestEnd Films acquired the international rights to the film.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Barraclough|first1=Leo|title=AFM: WestEnd Films Adds 'Green Room' to Slate|url=https://variety.com/2014/film/news/afm-westend-films-adds-green-room-to-slate-1201342200/|access-date=October 30, 2014|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=October 29, 2014|archive-date=September 26, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240926212818/https://variety.com/2014/film/news/afm-westend-films-adds-green-room-to-slate-1201342200/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film had its world premiere at the [[Cannes Film Festival]] on May 17, 2015.<ref name=VarietyReview /> Shortly after, it was announced [[A24]] had acquired distribution rights to the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/news/green-room-a24-patrick-stewart-1201573676/|title='Green Room':A24 Buys Patrick Stewart Thriller|work=Variety|first=Dave|last=McNary|date=August 19, 2015|access-date=October 10, 2015|archive-date=October 6, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151006114837/http://variety.com/2015/film/news/green-room-a24-patrick-stewart-1201573676/|url-status=live}}</ref> The film screened on opening night of the [[2015 Toronto International Film Festival]], on September 10, 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/news/toronto-film-fest-docs-midnight-slate-green-room-charlie-hebdo-1201567389/|title=Toronto Film Fest Documentary, Midnight Movies Lineup Revealed|work=Variety|first=Jennie|last=Punter|date=August 11, 2015|access-date=October 10, 2015|archive-date=September 26, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240926212819/https://variety.com/2015/film/news/toronto-film-fest-docs-midnight-slate-green-room-charlie-hebdo-1201567389/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film was originally to open in a [[limited release]] on April 1, 2016, before opening in a [[wide release]] on April 15, 2016.<ref name=release>{{cite web|url=https://www.thewrap.com/a24-to-release-anton-yelchins-neo-nazi-thriller-green-room-on-april-fools-day/|title=A24 to release Anton Yelchin's Neo-Nazi Thriller 'Green Room' on April Fools Day|work=The Wrap|first=Jeff|last=Sneider|date=October 5, 2015|access-date=October 10, 2015|archive-date=October 3, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231003175606/https://www.thewrap.com/a24-to-release-anton-yelchins-neo-nazi-thriller-green-room-on-april-fools-day/|url-status=live}}</ref> However, it was moved to April 15, in limited release, and May 13 wide.<ref name="Jaugernath"/>

===Home media===
Lionsgate, as the home media distributor of A24 releases, released ''Green Room'' on [[Blu-ray]] and [[DVD]] on July 12, 2016.<ref>{{cite web|work=[[DVD Talk]]|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/70876/green-room/|title=Green Room (Blu-ray)|author=Jane, Ian|date=June 25, 2016|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190930012433/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/70876/green-room/|archive-date=September 30, 2019|url-status=live|access-date=September 30, 2019}}</ref> The end credits of the film's home media and subsequent releases feature an addended dedication to the memory of star Yelchin, who died on June 19, 2016.<ref>{{cite AV media|title=Green Room|publisher=Broad Green Pictures|medium=[[Blu-ray]]|year=2016}} End credits read: "''In loving memory of our friend, Anton.''"</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Green Room'' opened at #30 in its limited release, premiering in 3 theaters, earning $87,984. In its official wide release, the film premiered at 777 theaters, taking the #16 rank on opening weekend, and grossing $547,031 in North America.<ref name="mojo" /> It grossed a total of $3.8 million worldwide.<ref name="numbers">{{cite web |title=Green Room (2016) – Financial Information |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Green-Room-(2015)#tab=summary |website=The Numbers }}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] the film holds an approval rating of 90% based on 250 reviews, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The site's critical consensus reads: "''Green Room'' delivers unapologetic genre thrills with uncommon intelligence and powerfully acted élan."<ref name="GreenRottenTomatoes">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/green_room_2016/|title=''Green Room (2016)''|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=January 23, 2022|archive-date=September 26, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240926214916/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/green_room_2016|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] gave it a weighted average score of 79 out of 100, based on 42 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/green-room|title=''Green Room''|work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=October 24, 2019|archive-date=February 19, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219181239/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/green-room|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Richard Roeper]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' praised Patrick Stewart, Imogen Poots, Alia Shawkat and Macon Blair's performances and called the film "a wonderfully nasty, gruesome, jagged-edge gem of a horror film" that has "first-rate" cinematography, set design, soundtrack, and editing.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://chicago.suntimes.com/news/green-room-punk-bands-gig-turns-truly-hardcore-in-horror-gem/|title='Green Room': Punk band's gig turns truly hardcore in horror gem|last=Roeper|first=Richard|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|date=April 18, 2016|access-date=February 5, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206022501/http://chicago.suntimes.com/news/green-room-punk-bands-gig-turns-truly-hardcore-in-horror-gem/|archive-date=February 6, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> Barry Hertz of ''[[The Globe and Mail]]'' awarded it a full four stars and wrote, "Jeremy Saulnier (Murder Party, Blue Ruin) continues one of the best streaks in independent horror with this terrifying and inventive thriller."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/film/film-reviews/green-room-is-a-delightfully-cruel-work-of-high-tension/article29786494/|title=Green Room is a delightfully cruel work of high tension|last=Hertz|first=Barry|work=[[The Globe and Mail]]|date=April 29, 2016|access-date=February 6, 2017|archive-date=February 6, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206105638/http://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/film/film-reviews/green-room-is-a-delightfully-cruel-work-of-high-tension/article29786494/|url-status=live}}</ref> Lenika Cruz of ''[[The Atlantic]]'' said it's "a tense gore-fest, one that’s as grimy and claustrophobic as the titular room. But scrape off the scum, and you’ll find Green Room full of visual artistry, dark humor, smart writing, and glints of humanity".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2016/04/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-review/479244/|title=Green Room: Escape From the Nazi Punks|last=Cruz|first=Lenika|work=[[The Atlantic]]|date=April 27, 2016|access-date=February 6, 2017|archive-date=February 6, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206104648/https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2016/04/green-room-jeremy-saulnier-review/479244/|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[IGN]]'' awarded it a score of 9 out of 10, saying, "This follow-up to the brilliant ''[[Blue Ruin]]'' pits a rock band against white supremacists with ace, ultra-violent results."<ref>Singer, Leigh (May 18, 2015).  [https://uk.ign.com/articles/2015/05/18/green-room-review "Green Room Review."] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191113053657/https://uk.ign.com/articles/2015/05/18/green-room-review |date=November 13, 2019 }}  ''[[IGN]]''.  Retrieved February 2, 2016.</ref>

Jeffrey Bloomer of ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' favorably compared the film's "genre maturity", "amoral survivalism and malleable sense of good and evil", "brutal efficiency" and "weary humor" to [[John Carpenter]]'s ''[[Assault on Precinct 13 (1976 film)|Assault on Precinct 13]]'' and praised the cast, writing "If the world knows any justice[...] then the Screen Actors Guild will remember this cast when it doles out its awards next year".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.slate.com/articles/arts/movies/2016/04/green_room_reviewed_a_bloody_funny_ensemble_thriller.html|title=Green Room reviewed: A bloody, funny ensemble thriller.|last=Bloomer|first=Jeffrey|work=[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]|date=April 15, 2016|access-date=February 7, 2017|archive-date=February 6, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206142952/http://www.slate.com/articles/arts/movies/2016/04/green_room_reviewed_a_bloody_funny_ensemble_thriller.html|url-status=live}}</ref> [[James Berardinelli]] concludes the film is "for anyone who enjoys sitting through 90 tense minutes and feeling the attendant adrenaline rush. It’s like a well-constructed horror movie" that's "As intimate as it is unnerving".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.reelviews.net/reelviews/green-room|title=Green Room (United States, 2016)|last=Berardinelli|first=James|work=reelviews.net|date=April 28, 2016|access-date=February 6, 2017|archive-date=February 6, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206110851/http://www.reelviews.net/reelviews/green-room|url-status=live}}</ref> Guy Lodge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it "a technically sharp backwoods horror-thriller that lacks a human element".<ref name=VarietyReview>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/festivals/green-room-review-1201498852/|title=Cannes Film Review: 'Green Room'|last=Lodge|first=Guy|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=May 17, 2015|access-date=June 23, 2015|archive-date=May 17, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150517190120/https://variety.com/2015/film/festivals/green-room-review-1201498852/|url-status=live}}</ref>  Leslie Felperin of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' wrote that it's entertaining but "less disciplined, less original and less memorable work than ''[[Blue Ruin]]''".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://hollywoodreporter.com/review/green-room-cannes-review-796288|title='Green Room': Cannes Review|last=Felperin|first=Leslie|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=May 17, 2015|access-date=June 23, 2015|archive-date=June 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150624071203/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/green-room-cannes-review-796288|url-status=live}}</ref>
