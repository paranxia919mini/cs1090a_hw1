'''''Big Time''''' is a 1988 American musical film directed by Chris Blum.

==Summary==
A [[concert film]] centering on singer [[Tom Waits]] featuring songs from the albums ''[[Swordfishtrombones]]'', ''[[Rain Dogs]]'' and ''[[Franks Wild Years]]''.<ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=qa5_AQAAQBAJ&dq=big+time+tom+waits+1988+vhs&pg=PT292 TLA Film and Video Guide 2000-2001 - Google Books (pg.56)]</ref>

==Production==
Filming took place in [[Los Angeles]] and [[San Francisco, California]].<ref>[https://www.roxie.com/ai1ec_event/big-time-35mm/?instance_id=23609 Roxie]</ref>

There were no known existing 35mm prints of this concert film until an archival one appeared in the late 2010s.<ref>[https://filmstreams.org/films/big-time Film Streams]</ref><ref>[https://www.standbyformindcontrol.com/2017/10/tom-waitss-big-time-is-beautiful-and-im-sorry-you-cant-watch-it/ Tom Waits's Big Time Is Beautiful, And I'm Sorry You Can't Watch It|Stand By For Mind Control]</ref>

==Critical response==
[[Jon Pareles]] wrote a negative review in ''[[The New York Times]]'', saying even fans of Waits would find it "frustrating and off-putting" and that it "turns Mr. Waits's performance into a freak show."<ref>{{cite news|last1=Pareles|first1=Jon|authorlink1=Jon Pareles|title=Review/Film - 'Big Time,' a Look at the Rock Star Tom Waits|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1988/09/30/movies/review-film-big-time-a-look-at-the-rock-star-tom-waits.html|accessdate=November 25, 2017|work=[[The New York Times]]|date=September 30, 1988}}</ref> Richard Harrington wrote a negative review in ''[[The Washington Post]]'', describing the film as "More an indulgence than a concert" and the songs as "often intriguing" but "only rarely [...] listenable".<ref>{{cite news|last1=Harrington|first1=Richard|title='Big Time'|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/bigtimepgharrington_a0aa97.htm|accessdate=November 25, 2017|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|date=October 1, 1988}}</ref> Jeffrey M. Anderson, in a mixed review for ''Combustible Celluloid'', described the film as "a treat" for fans of Tom Waits and "one hell of a show."<ref>{{cite web|last1=Anderson|first1=Jeffrey M.|title=Big Time (1988): Tom Waits for No Man|url=http://www.combustiblecelluloid.com/bigtime.shtml|website=Combustible Celluloid|accessdate=November 25, 2017|date=September 15, 1988}}</ref> [[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out magazine]] called it a "magnificent movie" and "A concert film unlike any other".<ref>{{cite magazine|author1=MC|title=Big Time|url=https://www.timeout.com/london/film/big-time-1988|accessdate=November 25, 2017|magazine=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|publisher=Time Out Group Plc}}</ref> [[TV Guide]] gave the film 3 out of 5 stars, calling it "more performance than music" and a "work that demands to be taken on its own terms."<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Big Time|url=http://movies.tvguide.com/big-time/review/132765|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120320010432/http://movies.tvguide.com/big-time/review/132765|url-status=dead|archive-date=March 20, 2012|accessdate=25 November 2017|magazine=[[TV Guide]]}}</ref>

==Formats==
''Big Time'' was issued on [[LaserDisc]], [[VHS]] in Japan, UK and Yugoslavia, but was only issued on VHS in North America.<ref>[https://www.discogs.com/release/6990074-Tom-Waits-Big-Time Discogs]</ref> No official [[DVD]]<ref>[http://www.tasteofcinema.com/2016/tom-tapes-the-cinematic-appearances-of-tom-waits-and-his-music/2/ Tom Tapes: The Cinematic Appearances of Tom Waits and His Music - Page 2 - Taste of Cinema]</ref> or [[Blu-ray]] edition has yet been released, though the film was made available for streaming on the [[Amazon Prime]] platform on September 1, 2020.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Archer|first1=John|title= Amazon Video Announces New Films And TV Shows For September (2020)|url= https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnarcher/2020/08/30/amazon-video-announces-new-films-and-tv-shows-for-september-2020/|accessdate=September 9, 2020|work=[[Forbes]]}}</ref>
