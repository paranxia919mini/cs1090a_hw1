'''''Parasite''''' ({{Korean|hangul=기생충}}) is a 2019 South Korean [[black comedy]] [[thriller film]]<ref name="vulture edelstein">{{cite web |last1=Edelstein |first1=David |title=Bong Joon-ho's Parasite Is an Acid-Black Comedy That Eats at the Mind |url=https://www.vulture.com/2019/10/parasite-movie-review-bong-joon-hos-latest-film.html |website=Vulture |date=11 October 2019 |publisher=Vox Media |access-date=3 February 2024}}</ref> directed by [[Bong Joon Ho]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Han Jin-won]]. It stars [[Song Kang-ho]], [[Lee Sun-kyun]], [[Cho Yeo-jeong]], [[Choi Woo-shik]], [[Park So-dam]], [[Jang Hye-jin (actress)|Jang Hye-jin]], [[Park Myung-hoon]], and [[Lee Jung-eun (actress)|Lee Jung-eun]]. The film follows a poor family who infiltrate the home and life of a wealthy family.

The script is based on a play Bong wrote in 2013.<ref name=press>{{cite web |title=''Parasite'' international press kit |url=https://cdn-media.festival-cannes.com/film_film/0001/66/72f026493fedd9576b0ebc4b7837fd67b8cc95a3.pdf |publisher=[[CJ Entertainment]] |access-date=1 January 2020 |date=2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200110043736/https://cdn-media.festival-cannes.com/film_film/0001/66/72f026493fedd9576b0ebc4b7837fd67b8cc95a3.pdf |archive-date=10 January 2020 |url-status=dead}}</ref> He later adapted it into a 15-page [[Film treatment|film draft]], which was split into three different drafts by Han. Bong said he was inspired by the Korean film ''[[The Housemaid (1960 film)|The Housemaid]]'' (1960) and the 1930s [[Christine and Léa Papin]] incident. Filming ran from May to September 2018 and included cinematographer [[Hong Kyung-pyo]],<ref>{{cite web |title=BONG Joon-ho's ''PARASITE'' Claims Early Sales |url=https://www.koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/news.jsp?pageIndex=1&blbdComCd=601006&seq=5013&mode=VIEW |website=Korean Film Biz Zone |access-date=3 February 2019 |language=en |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190204013817/https://www.koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/news.jsp?pageIndex=1&blbdComCd=601006&seq=5013&mode=VIEW |archive-date=4 February 2019 |url-status=dead}}</ref> editor [[Yang Jin-mo]], and composer [[Jung Jae-il]]. ''Parasite'' premiered on 21 May 2019 at the [[2019 Cannes Film Festival|Cannes Film Festival]], where it became the first Korean film to win the [[Palme d'Or]]. It was praised for its direction, screenplay, performances, editing, and production design. It was released in South Korea by [[CJ Entertainment]] on 30 May, grossing $258 million worldwide<ref name="BOM" /> on an $11.4 million budget.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.hankyung.com/entertainment/article/2019060326461 |date=3 June 2019|script-title=ko:영화 '기생충' 흥행 질주...손익분기점 400만명 눈앞 |title=|access-date=26 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190626173250/https://www.hankyung.com/entertainment/article/2019060326461|archive-date=26 June 2019|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=DeadlineProfit/>

Among its [[List of accolades received by Parasite|numerous accolades]], ''Parasite'' won the [[Academy Award for Best Picture]] at the [[92nd Academy Awards]], becoming the [[List of foreign-language films nominated for Academy Awards#Best Picture|first non-English-language film to do so]].{{refn|group=note|Although ''Parasite'' was the first film with a non-English script to win Best Picture at the Oscars, it is not to be confused with the first ''foreign'' film{{emdash}}produced by a studio from a country that does not use English as its primary language{{emdash}}to win Best Picture, which was achieved by the French production ''[[The Artist (film)|The Artist]]'' (2012). However, this film was largely silent with French [[intertitle]]s and contained a few spoken lines in English.<ref name="guardian clarification">{{cite web |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/feb/10/parasite-best-picture-new-era-for-oscars |title=Parasite's best picture Oscar could kickstart a new era of internationalism |first=Phil |last=Hoad |date=10 February 2020 |access-date=11 June 2020 |work=[[The Guardian]] | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200618003809/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/feb/10/parasite-best-picture-new-era-for-oscars | archive-date = 18 June 2020 | url-status = live}}</ref> The Academy dictates foreign language as the main qualification for international film, hence ''The Artist'' did not qualify.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.oscars.org/oscars/rules-eligibility |title=Rules & Eligibility |date=28 July 2014 |website=Oscars.org {{!}} Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences|language=en|access-date=10 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200108163338/https://www.oscars.org/oscars/rules-eligibility|archive-date=8 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> Further, while prior winners ''[[The Last Emperor]]'' (1987) and ''[[Slumdog Millionaire]]'' (2008) include significant amounts of non-English dialogue, they were considered products of the Hollywood system.<ref name="guardian clarification"/>}} It won an additional three Oscars, for [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]], [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]], and [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]]. It is the first [[List of Academy Award winners and nominees from South Korea|South Korean film to receive any Academy Award recognition]], and one of only four films overall to win both the Palme d'Or and the Academy Award for Best Picture, the first such achievement in over 60 years.{{refn|group=note|By ''Parasite''{{'s}} 2019 release, the previous two films to win both awards were ''[[The Lost Weekend (film)|The Lost Weekend]]'' (1945) and ''[[Marty (film)|Marty]]'' (1955). The next to attain such achievement was ''[[Anora]]'' (2024).}} It won the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Non-English Language]] and the [[BAFTA Award for Best Film Not in the English Language]], and became the first non-English-language film to win the [[Actor Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture]]. 

Since its release, ''Parasite'' has been regarded as Bong's ''[[magnum opus]]'' and was ranked in the 2022 ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' poll as the 90th [[best films|best film of all time]].<ref name="BFI" /> In 2025, ''[[The New York Times]]'' ranked it as the [[NYT's 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century (June 2025)|best movie of the 21st century so far]] in a list based on a vote by readers, actors, and directors.<ref name="NYT">{{cite web |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html#movie-1 |website=The New York Times |date=23 June 2025 |access-date=27 June 2025}}</ref> As of February 2025, an [[HBO]] limited television series based on the film is in early development.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be set between 400 to 700 words only. -->
Kim Ki-taek and Chung-sook live in a semi-basement flat ({{transliteration|ko|banjiha}}) in [[Seoul]] with their daughter Ki-jung and son Ki-woo. As the family has financial struggles and low-income jobs, family friend Min-hyuk gives them a [[Suseok|scholar's rock]] meant to promise wealth. Leaving to study abroad, he suggests that Ki-woo lie about his qualifications to take over Min-hyuk's job as an [[English as a second or foreign language|English tutor]] for Da-hye, the daughter of the wealthy but naïve Park family. Min-hyuk trusts Ki-woo because he likes Da-hye and wants to ask her out when he returns. After Ki-jung helps forge a certificate for him, Ki-woo, posing as a [[Yonsei University]] student named "Kevin", goes to the Parks' home, where he is hired by the mother Mrs Park by the end of his first lesson.

The Kims' scheme is to secure jobs for each family member within the Park household while concealing their true identities. Ki-woo recommends "Jessica," actually Ki-jung, as an [[Art therapy|art therapist]] for the Parks' young son, Da-song, who has been traumatised from having a [[seizure]] after seeing a "ghost" in their kitchen. Ki-jung then frames Yoon, the Parks' chauffeur, by making it appear as though he had a sexual encounter in the car, leading to Ki-taek being hired as his replacement. The Kims exploit the [[Peach#Korea|peach]] allergy of the Parks' longtime housekeeper, Moon-gwang, to convince Mrs Park that Moon-gwang has [[tuberculosis]], and Chung-sook is hired as her replacement. Meanwhile, Ki-woo begins a secret romantic relationship with Da-hye.

While the Parks are away on a camping trip, the Kims revel in the luxuries of the house when Moon-gwang suddenly appears at the door, claiming to have forgotten something in the basement. She goes through a hidden entrance to an underground bunker created by the house's architect and previous owner, who had kept its existence secret from the Parks. There, Moon-gwang's husband, Geun-sae, has been hiding from loan sharks and is revealed to be the "ghost" Da-song saw. Moon-gwang begs Chung-sook to allow Geun-sae to continue living there in exchange for regular payments, but the three other Kims, who are eavesdropping, accidentally reveal their true identities. Moon-gwang films them and threatens to expose their deception to the Parks.

After the Parks call to inform the Kims that they are returning early due to a sudden severe rainstorm, the Kims quickly destroy all evidence of their ruse and subdue Moon-gwang and Geun-sae in the bunker, although Moon-gwang is injured during the struggle. The Kims manage to escape, but the torrential rain floods their flat with sewage water, forcing them to take shelter in a gymnasium alongside other displaced people.

The next day, Mrs Park organises an impromptu garden party for Da-song's birthday, with the elder Kims assisting while the younger Kims are invited as guests. Ki-woo enters the bunker with his scholar's rock, with intentions unknown. Moon-gwang is already dead from injuries sustained in the previous day's brawl; Geun-sae bludgeons Ki-woo with the rock, leaving him unconscious. Geun-sae then leaves the bunker and stabs Ki-jung with a kitchen knife in front of horrified party guests. Da-song seizes upon seeing the "ghost" again. Chung-sook impales Geun-sae with a barbecue skewer after a struggle. While Ki-taek tends to Ki-jung, Mr Park orders him to drive Da-song to the hospital. Ki-taek, gradually enraged by the Parks' lack of empathy or awareness of their own privilege, stabs him with Geun-sae's knife and flees.

Weeks later, Ki-woo is recovering from brain surgery, and he and Chung-sook have been convicted of fraud and placed on probation. Ki-jung has died from her injuries, and Ki-taek – now a wanted fugitive – has disappeared. Geun-sae is presumed to have been a random homeless man, and the motives for the murders remain unsolved. Ki-woo spies on the Parks' former home, now owned by a foreign family, and notices a [[Morse code]] message in a light visible from outside. Ki-taek, hiding in the bunker, has buried Moon-gwang in the garden and sends daily messages, hoping Ki-woo will see them. Still living in a semi-basement flat with his mother, Ki-woo writes a letter to Ki-taek, vowing to earn enough money one day to buy the house and free him.

== Production ==

=== Development ===
The idea for ''Parasite'' originated in 2013. While working on ''[[Snowpiercer]]'', Bong was encouraged by a theatre actor friend to write a play. He had been a tutor for the son of a wealthy family in Seoul in his early 20s and considered turning his experience into a stage production.<ref name ="THRBrzeski">{{cite news |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/features/making-parasite-how-bong-joon-hos-real-life-inspired-a-plot-twisty-tale-rich-poor-1252015 |title=Making of 'Parasite': How Bong Joon Ho's Real Life Inspired a Plot-Twisty Tale of Rich vs. Poor |last=Brzeski |first=Patrick |date=8 November 2019 |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=18 January 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200113115829/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/features/making-parasite-how-bong-joon-hos-real-life-inspired-a-plot-twisty-tale-rich-poor-1252015|archive-date=13 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> The film's title, ''Parasite'', was selected by Bong as it served a double meaning, and he had to convince the film's marketing group to use it. He said: "Because the story is about the poor family infiltrating and creeping into the rich house, it seems very obvious that ''Parasite'' refers to the poor family, and I think that's why the marketing team was a little hesitant. But if you look at it the other way, you can say that rich family, they're also parasites in terms of labor. They can't even wash dishes, they can't drive themselves, so they leech off the poor family's labor. So both are parasites."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/parasite-bong-joon-ho-reveals-the-meaning-behind-the-title-of-the-oscar-nominated-film |title=Parasite: Bong Joon-ho Reveals the Meaning Behind the Title of the Oscar-Nominated Film |first=Adele |last=Ankers |date=31 January 2020 |access-date=31 January 2020 |work=[[IGN]] | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200131150546/https://www.ign.com/articles/parasite-bong-joon-ho-reveals-the-meaning-behind-the-title-of-the-oscar-nominated-film | archive-date = 31 January 2020 | url-status = live}}</ref>

=== Writing ===
After completing ''Snowpiercer'', Bong wrote a 15-page [[film treatment]] for the first half of ''Parasite'', which his production assistant on ''Snowpiercer'', Han Jin-won, turned into three different drafts of the screenplay.<ref name ="THRBrzeski" /> After finishing ''[[Okja]]'', Bong returned to the project and finished the script. Han is credited as a co-writer.<ref name ="THRBrzeski" />

Bong said the film was influenced by the 1960 Korean "domestic Gothic" film ''[[The Housemaid (1960 film)|The Housemaid]]'', in which a middle-class family's stability is threatened by a disruptive interloper in the form of household help.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ulaby |first=Neda |url=https://www.npr.org/2019/12/10/785090210/parasite-director-bong-joon-ho-wanted-to-reflect-the-truth-of-current-times |title='Parasite' Director Bong Joon-ho 'Wanted To Reflect The Truth Of Current Times |date=10 December 2019 |work=[[NPR]] |access-date=12 February 2020 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191212221503/https://www.npr.org/2019/12/10/785090210/parasite-director-bong-joon-ho-wanted-to-reflect-the-truth-of-current-times |archive-date=12 December 2019}}</ref> Bong further referenced the design of the wealthy protagonist's house in [[Akira Kurosawa|Kurosawa]]'s 1963 film ''[[High and Low (1963 film)|High and Low]]'' as an important visual influence, as was the incident of [[Christine and Léa Papin]] to the story—an event wherein two live-in maids murdered their employers in 1930s France.<ref>{{Cite web |last=O'Falt |first=Chris |date=29 October 2019|title=Building the 'Parasite' House: How Bong Joon Ho and His Team Made the Year's Best Set |url=https://www.indiewire.com/awards/industry/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250106082321/https://www.indiewire.com/awards/industry/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/ |archive-date=6 January 2025 |access-date=28 November 2024|website=IndieWire }}</ref><ref name="JungEA">{{Cite web |url=https://www.vulture.com/2020/01/parasite-ending-explained-by-bong-joon-ho.html |title=Bong Joon Ho on Why He Wanted ''Parasite'' to End With a 'Surefire Kill' |last=Jung, E. Alex |date=14 January 2020 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |url-access=limited |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200115024246/https://www.vulture.com/2020/01/parasite-ending-explained-by-bong-joon-ho.html |archive-date=15 January 2020 |access-date=23 January 2020}}</ref> Bong had also tutored for a rich family himself. He said: "I got this feeling that I was infiltrating the private lives of complete strangers. Every week I would go into their house, and I thought how fun it would be if I could get all my friends to infiltrate the house one by one."<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2019/10/bong-joon-ho-parasite-interview/600007/ |title=How Bong Joon Ho Invented the Weird World of Parasite |last=Sims |first=David |date=15 October 2019 |website=[[The Atlantic]] |access-date=16 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200217011351/https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2019/10/bong-joon-ho-parasite-interview/600007/ |url-access=limited |archive-date=17 February 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref> Additionally, Moon-gwang's allergy to peaches was inspired by one of Bong's university friends having this allergy.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2491028/parasites-peach-scene-was-based-on-a-real-thing-that-happened-to-bong-joon-hos-friend|title=Parasite's Peach Scene Was Based On A Real Thing That Happened To Bong Joon-Ho's Friend|date=27 February 2020|first=Sarah|last=El-Mahmoud|website=CinemaBlend|access-date=15 April 2021|archive-date=15 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210415171743/https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2491028/parasites-peach-scene-was-based-on-a-real-thing-that-happened-to-bong-joon-hos-friend|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Darcy Paquet]], an American residing in South Korea, translated the English subtitles, writing directly with Bong.<ref name=Hanweaving>{{cite web |author=Han, Karen |url=https://www.polygon.com/2019/10/14/20906430/parasite-bong-joon-ho-interview-rock-peach-spoilers |title=Bong Joon-ho on weaving his personal memories into Parasite |work=[[Polygon (magazine)|Polygon]] |date=14 October 2019 |access-date=9 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191226230904/https://www.polygon.com/2019/10/14/20906430/parasite-bong-joon-ho-interview-rock-peach-spoilers|archive-date=26 December 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Paquet rendered [[jjapaguri]] or chapaguri, a dish cooked by a character in the film, as ''ram-don'', meaning [[ramen]]-[[udon]]. It is a mix of [[Chapagetti]] and [[Neoguri (instant noodle)|Neoguri]] produced by [[Nongshim]].<ref name="Rochlin"/> The English version of the film shows packages labelled in English "[[ramyeon]]" and "udon" to highlight to English speakers how the name was created. Paquet believed the word ''ram-don'' did not previously exist as he found no results on Google.<ref name=LeeHanaParasub>{{cite web |author=Lee, Hana |url=http://m.korea.net/english/NewsFocus/People/view?articleId=171974&page=1 |title='Parasite' subtitle translator: Comedies are a fun challenge |work=[[Korea.net]] |date=19 June 2019 |access-date=8 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200208155256/http://m.korea.net/english/NewsFocus/People/view?articleId=171974&page=1|archive-date=8 February 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> On one occasion, Paquet used [[Oxford University]] as a reference instead of [[Seoul National University]], and in another, used [[WhatsApp]] as the messaging application instead of [[KakaoTalk]].<ref name=Hanweaving /> Paquet chose Oxford over [[Harvard University|Harvard]] because of Bong's affinity for the United Kingdom, and because Paquet believed using Harvard would be "too obvious".<ref name=LeeHanaParasub /> Paquet wrote, "In order for humor to work, people need to understand it immediately. With an unfamiliar word, the humor is lost."<ref name=LeeHanaParasub />

[[File:Parasite house before and after VFX.jpg|thumb|right|The house was constructed on a set and everything above the first floor was added in post-production.]]
The Parks' house was a specially constructed set. The ground floor and the garden were constructed on an empty outdoor lot, while the basement and first floor were constructed on set.<ref name="archdigest"/> "We built the main floor of the house in a backlot and for the second floor it was all green screen outside", explained editor [[Yang Jin-mo]]. "When we shot toward the outside from inside, everything beyond the garden was all [[VFX]]."<ref>{{cite web |title=How Parasite Uses Brilliant Design and Invisible VFX to Transcend Language |website=Frame.io Insider |date=13 January 2020 |url=https://blog.frame.io/2020/01/13/parasite-design-and-vfx/ | access-date=9 April 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200330140958/https://blog.frame.io/2020/01/13/parasite-design-and-vfx/ | archive-date=30 March 2020 | url-status=live}}</ref> As part of the scripting, Bong also designed the home's basic layout. "It's like its own universe inside this film. Each character and each team has spaces that they take over, that they can infiltrate, and also secret spaces that they don't know."<ref name="IndieWire 2019" /> A fictional architect, Namgoong Hyeonja, was created as the home's designer and previous owner, and production designer Lee Ha-jun considered the house's form and function based on how Namgoong would design it.<ref name="archdigest"/> It was designed and constructed to be not only beautiful but "a stage that served the precise needs of his camera, compositions, and characters, while embodying his film's rich themes".<ref>{{Cite web |last=O'Falt |first=Chris |date=29 October 2019 |title=Building the 'Parasite' House: How Bong Joon Ho and His Team Made the Year's Best Set |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2019/10/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/|access-date=23 June 2020 |website=IndieWire |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191104232715/https://www.indiewire.com/2019/10/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/|archive-date=4 November 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Lee said, <blockquote>Since Mr Park's house is built by an architect in the story, it wasn't easy finding the right approach to designing the house...I'm not an architect, and I think there's a difference in how an architect envisions a space and how a production designer does. We prioritize blocking and camera angles while architects build spaces for people to actually live in and thus design around people. So I think the approach is very different.<ref name="IndieWire 2019" /></blockquote> For example, Ha-jun established that Namgoong would have used the first floor's living room to appreciate the garden, so it was built with a single wide window and only spartan seating options for this function.<ref name="archdigest"/> Some of the interior artwork in the house sets were by South Korean artist Seung-mo Park, including existing artwork of hers and some created for the film.<ref name="archdigest"/> The team designed the home and interiors to make the set amenable for filming at the 2.35:1 aspect ratio, favouring wide and deep rooms rather than height.<ref name="IndieWire 2019" />

Lee said the sun was an important factor when building the outdoor set. "The sun's direction was a crucial point of consideration while we were searching for outdoor lots", he said. "We had to remember the sun's position during our desired time frame and determine the positions and sizes of the windows accordingly. In terms of practical lighting, the DP [Hong Kyung-pyo] had specific requests regarding the color. He wanted sophisticated indirect lighting and the warmth from [[Incandescent light bulb|tungsten light sources]]. Before building the set, the DP and I visited the lot several times to check the sun's movement each time, and we decided on the set's location together".<ref name="IndieWire 2019">{{cite web |title=How Bong Joon Ho Designed the House in ''Parasite'' |website=[[IndieWire]] |date=29 October 2019 |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2019/10/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/ |access-date=6 November 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191104232715/https://www.indiewire.com/2019/10/parasite-house-set-design-bong-joon-ho-1202185829/ |archive-date=4 November 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The Kims' semi-basement flat and its street were also built on set, partially out of necessity for the flooding scenes.<ref name="IndieWire 2019" /> Lee Ha-jun visited and photographed several abandoned villages and towns in South Korea scheduled to be torn down to help inform the set design. He also created stories for the Kims' neighbours and added details of those residents along the street to improve the authenticity of the street's appearance.<ref name="archdigest">{{cite web |url=https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/bong-joon-ho-parasite-movie-set-design-interview |title=Inside the House From Bong Joon Ho's Parasite |first=Rachel |last=Wallace |date=31 October 2019 |access-date=16 February 2020 |work=[[Architectural Digest]] | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191106032043/https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/bong-joon-ho-parasite-movie-set-design-interview | archive-date = 6 November 2019 | url-status = live}}</ref>

=== Editing ===
According to editor Yang Jin-mo, Bong chose to shoot the film without traditional [[Camera coverage|coverage]]. To give them more editing options with limited shots, they sometimes stitched together different takes of the same shot.<ref>{{cite web |last=Buder |first=Emily |title='Parasite' Editor Jinmo Yang Teaches Us How to Edit Without Coverage |url=https://nofilmschool.com/parasite-editor-interview |website=No Film School |date=6 December 2019 |access-date=17 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200118035230/https://nofilmschool.com/parasite-editor-interview |archive-date=18 January 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref> Yang edited the film using [[Final Cut Pro|Final Cut Pro 7]], a program not updated since 2011.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Janney|first=Robbie|date=7 February 2020|title=Academy Award-Nominated Editor Jin-mo Yang Shares His Secrets|url=https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/parasite-editor-jinmo-yang/|access-date=1 October 2020|website=The Beat: A Blog by PremiumBeat|language=en-US|archive-date=27 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200927110626/https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/parasite-editor-jinmo-yang/|url-status=live}}</ref>

While the film was produced for release in colour, a [[black and white]] version predated the world premiere in Cannes. The monochrome edition debuted on 26 January 2020 at the [[International Film Festival Rotterdam]], was re-screened from 29 to 31 January, and received a limited release in some countries.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://iffr.com/en/2020/films/parasite-bw-version |title=Parasite (B&W Version) |work=IFFR.com |access-date=13 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200209090234/https://iffr.com/en/2020/films/parasite-bw-version |archive-date=9 February 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/bong-joon-ho-black-white-parasite-cut-1274862 |title=Bong Joon Ho Talks Genesis of New Black-and-White 'Parasite' Cut |work=The Hollywood Reporter |first=Alex |last=Ritman |date=30 January 2020 |access-date=13 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200213155204/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/bong-joon-ho-black-white-parasite-cut-1274862 |archive-date=13 February 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref>

== Themes and interpretations ==
{{See also|Economic inequality in South Korea}}
The main themes of ''Parasite'' are [[class conflict]], [[social inequality]] and [[Economic inequality|wealth disparity]].<ref name="classconflict">{{cite web |url=https://www.npr.org/2019/11/05/776388423/the-hit-movie-parasite-puts-basement-structures-in-structural-inequality |title=The Hit Movie 'Parasite' Puts Basement Structures In Structural Inequality |date=5 November 2019 |access-date=3 February 2020 |last=Ulaby |first=Neda |publisher=[[NPR]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200116002438/https://www.npr.org/2019/11/05/776388423/the-hit-movie-parasite-puts-basement-structures-in-structural-inequality|archive-date=16 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://ew.com/movies/2019/10/15/parasite-2019-cinematic-class-conflict/ |access-date=3 February 2020 |last=Holub |first=Christian |date=15 October 2019 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |title=From Parasite to Hustlers: How 2019 became the year of cinematic class conflict|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200203170810/https://ew.com/movies/2019/10/15/parasite-2019-cinematic-class-conflict/|archive-date=3 February 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Nulman-2021">{{Cite book|last=Nulman|first=Eugene|title=Coronavirus Capitalism Goes to the Cinema|publisher=[[Routledge]]|year=2021|isbn=9781003173472|location=London}}</ref> Film critics and Bong Joon Ho himself have considered the film as a reflection of [[late-stage capitalism]],<ref name="Bean">{{cite web |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/travisbean/2020/01/30/capitalism-gone-wild-the-ending-of-parasite-explained/#2a97c8df6dbd |title=Capitalism Gone Wild: The Ending of 'Parasite' Explained |last=Bean |first=Travis |date=30 January 2020 |access-date=3 February 2020 |work=[[Forbes]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200131150417/https://www.forbes.com/sites/travisbean/2020/01/30/capitalism-gone-wild-the-ending-of-parasite-explained/#2a97c8df6dbd|archive-date=31 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.gq.com/story/parasite-director-bong-joon-ho-interview |title=Parasite Director Bong Joon-ho on the Art of Class Warfare |date=8 October 2019 |access-date=3 February 2020 |last=Paiella |first=Gabriella |work=[[GQ]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191219211642/https://www.gq.com/story/parasite-director-bong-joon-ho-interview|archive-date=19 December 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> and some have associated it with the term "[[Hell Joseon]]" ({{Korean|hangul=헬조선|labels=no}}), a satirical phrase that posits that living in hell would be akin to living in modern South Korea. This term came about due to high rates of youth unemployment, the intense demands of pursuing [[higher education]], the crisis of home affordability, and the increasing socioeconomic gap between the wealthy and poor.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Kim |first1=Min-soo |access-date=7 February 2020 |title=Why "Parasite" Is Bong Joon-ho's Biggest Hit and Darkest Film Yet |url=https://www.cinemaescapist.com/2019/10/parasite-korean-movie-review/ |date=11 October 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200111215042/https://www.cinemaescapist.com/2019/10/parasite-korean-movie-review/|archive-date=11 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Opinion: For South Korean youth, Parasite burrows close to home |url=https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-for-south-korean-youth-parasite-burrows-close-to-home/ |newspaper=The Globe and Mail |date=3 January 2020 |access-date=7 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200110024738/https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-for-south-korean-youth-parasite-burrows-close-to-home/|archive-date=10 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Kim |first=E. Tammy |date=13 January 2020 |title=UPSTAIRS, DOWNSTAIRS, The politics of Bong Joon-ho's Parasite |magazine=[[The Nation]] |pages=44–45 }}</ref> In ''Coronavirus Capitalism Goes to the Cinema'', Nulman writes that the word "parasite" originally referred to a "person who eats at the table of another", which occurs in one scene of the film.<ref name="Nulman-2021" /> Nulman also notes the connection between parasites and the [[Karl Marx]] quote: <blockquote>The [[capitalism|capitalist]]... is only capital personified. His soul is the soul of capital. But capital has one sole driving force, the drive to valorize itself, to create [[surplus-value]], to make its constant part, the means of production, absorb the greatest possible amount of [[surplus labor]]. Capital is dead labor which, vampire-like, lives only by sucking living labor, and lives the more, the more labor it sucks.<ref name="Nulman-2021" /> </blockquote>The film also analyses the use of connections and qualifications to get ahead, for rich and poor families alike.<ref name="WBFO">{{cite web |last=Kasulis |first=Kelly |title=Oscar-nominated 'Parasite' speaks to a growing divide in South Korea |url=https://news.wbfo.org/post/oscar-nominated-parasite-speaks-growing-divide-south-korea |website=news.wbfo.org |access-date=7 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200207131837/https://news.wbfo.org/post/oscar-nominated-parasite-speaks-growing-divide-south-korea |archive-date=7 February 2020 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Some argue that its discussion of class relates to [[Pierre Bourdieu]]'s concept of [[Habitus (sociology)|habitus]].<ref name="Nulman-2021" />

Bong has referred to ''Parasite'' as an upstairs/downstairs or "stairway movie",<ref name="JungEA" /> in which staircases are used as a motif to represent the positions of the families in the homes of the Kims and the Parks, as well as the basement bunker.<ref>{{cite news |last=Rao |first=Sonia |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/arts-entertainment/2019/10/24/unpacking-metaphorical-ending-parasite/?outputType=amp |title=Unpacking the 'metaphorical' ending of 'Parasite' |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=24 October 2019 |access-date=23 January 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200107173908/https://www.washingtonpost.com/arts-entertainment/2019/10/24/unpacking-metaphorical-ending-parasite/?outputType=amp|archive-date=7 January 2020|url-status=live}}</ref> The Kims' semi-basement apartment is typical for poorer Seoul residents due to its lower rent, despite having issues such as mould and increased risk of disease.<ref name="classconflict" /> [[East Asian Monsoon|Monsoon]] floods such as the one depicted in the film commonly damage these types of residences the most.<ref name="WBFO" /> The film presents class in spatial terms that speak to hierarchy, according to Nulman.<ref name="Nulman-2021" /><ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Farahbakhsh |first1=Alireza |last2=Ebrahimi |first2=Ramtin |date=14 July 2021 |title=The Social Implications of Metaphor in Bong Joon-ho's Parasite |url=http://cinej.pitt.edu/ojs/index.php/cinej/article/view/291 |journal=CINEJ Cinema Journal |volume=9 |issue=1 |pages=87–116 |doi=10.5195/cinej.2021.291 |s2cid=237775092 |issn=2158-8724 |doi-access=free |access-date=4 November 2022 |archive-date=4 November 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221104021737/https://cinej.pitt.edu/ojs/index.php/cinej/article/view/291 |url-status=live }}</ref>

According to Bong, the ending implies that Ki-woo will not be able to earn the funds needed to buy the house, as the final shot shows him still in the basement flat and recalls the first scene; he described this shot as a "surefire kill" ({{Korean|hangul=확인사살|labels=no}}), referring to a [[coup de grace]] to ensure death.<ref name="JungEA" /> The ending song refers to Ki-woo working to make money to get the house. Choi Woo-shik estimated that it would take approximately 564 years for Ki-woo to earn enough money to purchase the house. Nevertheless, he was optimistic: "I'm pretty sure Ki-woo is one of those bright kids. He'll come up with some idea, and he would just go into the German family's house, and I think he will rescue his father".<ref name="HarrisChoiInt" /> But on many interpretations, this dream subscribes to a [[bootstrapping]] mentality and is unlikely to be achieved;<ref name="JungEA" /><ref name="Bean" /><ref name="HarrisChoiInt" /> furthermore, "it does not address the fundamental problem at hand. Even in this fantasy scenario, Ki-taek would still be contained in the house by a legal system that would seek his prosecution and imprisonment. The forces that created and upheld the Kim family's separation would not be undone, merely adapted to".<ref name="Park-2020">{{Cite web |last=Park |first=Ju-Hyun |date=17 February 2020 |title=Reading Colonialism in "Parasite" |url=https://tropicsofmeta.com/2020/02/17/reading-colonialism-in-parasite/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200218055749/https://tropicsofmeta.com/2020/02/17/reading-colonialism-in-parasite/ |archive-date=18 February 2020 |access-date=18 February 2020 |website=Tropics of Meta |language=en}}</ref>

Park Da-song is obsessed with "[[Indigenous peoples of the Americas|Indians]]" and owns Native American-themed toys and inauthentic replicas.<ref>{{cite web |last=Chu |first=Li-Wei |title=Interview: Dissecting the hidden motifs of 'Parasite' with director Bong Joon-Ho |url=https://fromtheintercom.com/interview-director-bong-joon-ho/ |website=From the Intercom |date=12 October 2019 |access-date=12 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200212063116/https://fromtheintercom.com/interview-director-bong-joon-ho/ |archive-date=12 February 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Cea |first=Max |title=''Parasite''{{'}}s Wild Ending, Broken Down |url=https://www.gq.com/story/bong-joon-ho-breaks-down-parasites-wild-ending |website=GQ |date=4 November 2019 |access-date=12 February 2020 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200212200908/https://www.gq.com/story/bong-joon-ho-breaks-down-parasites-wild-ending |archive-date=12 February 2020}}</ref><ref name="Nulman-2021" /> Nulman makes the link between the "native" Park family and the invaders: the Kims, who bring with them deadly parasites to which the natives have no immunity. Nulman points to the [[miasma theory]] of scent carrying disease: it was thought that natives could catch diseases just by smelling the noxious air carried by colonising Spaniards. This connects to the film's theme around the class distinction of smell.<ref name="Nulman-2021" /> Bong has noted: "I wouldn't go so far as to say it's a commentary on what happened in the United States, but it's related in the sense that this family starts infiltrating the house and they already find a family living there. So you could say it's a joke in that context. But at the same time, the Native Americans have a very complicated and long, deep history. But in this family, that story is reduced to a young boy's hobby and decoration. The boy's mother mentions the tent as a US imported good, and I think it's like the [[Che Guevara]] T-shirts that people wear. They don't know the life of the revolutionary figure, they just think it's a cool T-shirt. That's what happens in our current time: the context and meaning behind these actual things only exists as a surface-level thing".<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://ew.com/movies/2019/10/23/parasite-bong-joon-ho-ending-explained/ |title=''Parasite'' director Bong Joon Ho discusses the film's twisty ending |last=Holub |first=Christian |date=23 October 2019 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=12 February 2020|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200213063431/https://ew.com/movies/2019/10/23/parasite-bong-joon-ho-ending-explained/|archive-date=13 February 2020}}</ref>

Some critics note the importance of working-class solidarity as presented in the film. The Kims' problems are a result of lack of class solidarity with the other poor family, Geun-sae and Moon-gwang. At the film's climax, Ki-taek becomes aware of his class identity when Mr Park is disgusted by Geun-sae's smell.<ref name="Nulman-2021" /> Others said ''Parasite'' revealed the misfortunes of poor, powerless victims of an indifferent world who are transformed into liberation through the comical effect of mass slaughter.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Beck |first=Bernard |date=2 April 2020 |title=The Last Laugh: Parasite, Joker, Dark Waters and the Outcasts' Revenge |url=https://doi.org/10.1080/15210960.2020.1748995 |journal=Multicultural Perspectives |volume=22 |issue=2 |pages=79–81 |doi=10.1080/15210960.2020.1748995 |s2cid=219440739 |issn=1521-0960|url-access=subscription }}</ref>
