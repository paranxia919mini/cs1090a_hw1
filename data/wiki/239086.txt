'''''Amarcord''''' ({{IPA|it|amarˈkɔrd|lang}}) is a 1973 [[comedy-drama]] film directed by [[Federico Fellini]], a semi-[[autobiographical]] tale about Titta, an adolescent boy growing up among an eccentric cast of characters in the village of Borgo San Giuliano (situated near the ancient walls of [[Rimini]])<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://archivio.federicofellini.it/en/node/2611|title=Borgo San Giuliano|publisher=Fondazione Federico Fellini|access-date=19 August 2021|archive-date=19 August 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210819144948/http://archivio.federicofellini.it/en/node/2611|url-status=live}}</ref> in 1930s [[Fascist Italy (1922–1943)|Fascist Italy]].

The film's title is a [[univerbation]] (multiple words combined to form a single word) of the [[Romagnol language|Romagnol]] phrase {{lang|rgn|a m'arcôrd}} ({{IPA|rgn|a marˈkoːrd|pron}}), 'I remember'.{{sfn|Pettigrew|2003|loc=p. 76}} Fellini elaborated further by suggesting that the Italian words {{lang|it|amare}} ('to love'), {{lang|it|cuore}} ('heart'), {{lang|it|ricordare}} ('to remember') and {{lang|it|amaro}} ('bitter') could be expressed simultaneously through the Romagnol word. The title then became a neologism of the Italian language, with the meaning of 'nostalgic evocation', 'fond memory'.<ref>{{Cite web |title=amarcord in Vocabolario |url=https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/amarcord |access-date=2022-04-24 |work=[[Treccani]] |language=it-IT |archive-date=24 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220424005248/https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/amarcord |url-status=live }}</ref>

The central role of Titta is based on Fellini's childhood friend from Rimini, Luigi Titta Benzi. Benzi became a lawyer and remained in close contact with Fellini throughout his life.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.criterionchannel.com/amarcord/videos/fellini-s-homecoming|title=Fellini's Homecoming – Amarcord|website=[[The Criterion Channel]]}}</ref> Titta's sentimental education is emblematic of Italy's "lapse of conscience".{{sfn|Bondanella|1978|pp=20–21}} Fellini skewers Mussolini's ludicrous posturings and those of a [[Catholic Church]] that "imprisoned Italians in a perpetual adolescence"{{sfn|Bondanella|1978|loc=p. 20. For other discussions of Fellini and fascism, see Bondanella's ''The Cinema of Federico Fellini'' and ''[[I'm a Born Liar: A Fellini Lexicon]]''}} by mocking himself and his fellow villagers in comic scenes that underline their incapacity to adopt genuine moral responsibility or outgrow foolish sexual fantasies.

The film won the [[47th Academy Awards|Academy Award]] for [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]], and was nominated for two more [[48th Academy Awards|Academy Awards]]: [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] and [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]] the following year.

In 2008, the film was included on the [[Ministry of Cultural Heritage and Activities and Tourism|Italian Ministry of Cultural Heritage]]’s [[100 film italiani da salvare|100 Italian films to be saved]], a list of 100 films that "have changed the collective memory of the country between 1942 and 1978."<ref>{{Cite web|title=Ecco i cento film italiani da salvare Corriere della Sera|url=https://www.corriere.it/spettacoli/08_febbraio_28/elenco_cento_film_d83cacd8-e5ce-11dc-ab61-0003ba99c667.shtml|access-date=2021-03-11|website=www.corriere.it|archive-date=11 July 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230711194044/https://www.corriere.it/spettacoli/08_febbraio_28/elenco_cento_film_d83cacd8-e5ce-11dc-ab61-0003ba99c667.shtml|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
In Borgo San Giuliano, a village near [[Rimini]], the arrival of fluffy [[Populus nigra|poplar]] seeds floating on the wind heralds the arrival of spring. As night falls, the inhabitants make their way to the village square for the {{Lang|it|[[fogheraccia]]}}, a traditional bonfire in which the {{Interlanguage link|segavecchia|it|Sega la vecchia|italic=y}}, an effigy of an old woman, is ritually burned.<ref name=":3">{{Cite web |date=15 March 2023 |title=Fogheraccia 2023, dove andare in Romagna per i falò di San Giuseppe |trans-title=Fogheraccia 2023: Where to go in Romagna for the bonfires of St Joseph |url=https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/emilia-romagna/cosa-fare/fogheraccia-2023-san-giuseppe-broi7v2p |access-date=8 April 2024 |website=[[Il Resto del Carlino]] |language=it |archive-date=8 April 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240408143155/https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/emilia-romagna/cosa-fare/fogheraccia-2023-san-giuseppe-broi7v2p |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":5">{{Cite web |last=Lazzari |first=Martina |date=17 March 2024 |title=Fogheraccia: origine del falò che "incendia" la notte di San Giuseppe |trans-title=Fogheraccia: Origin of the bonfire that "alights" St Joseph's night |url=https://www.riminitoday.it/social/fogheraccia-focarina-origini-san-giuseppe-18-marzo-informazioni.html |access-date=8 April 2024 |website=RiminiToday |language=it}}</ref> The townspeople play pranks on one another, explode fireworks, cavort with loose women and make lewd noises when the civically minded lawyer lectures on the history of the region.

School under Fascism is a tedious cavalcade of dry facts recited by instructors of varying levels of engagement and skill. All Titta and his fellow students can do is goof off or skip class when not called upon to solve math problems or identify obscure historical details. When Titta goes to confession, he manages to avoid telling Don Balosa about his masturbatory activities and attempt to seduce Gradisca, a glamorous older woman, because the priest is more concerned with floral arrangements.

Fascist officials come for a tour, and the schoolchildren are required to perform athletic routines for their approval. Titta's friend Ciccio daydreams about being married to his crush, Aldina, by a giant [[papier-mâché]] rendering of Mussolini's face. Surreptitiously wired into the bell tower of the town church, a gramophone plays a recording of "[[The Internationale]]", but it is soon shot at and destroyed by Fascists. Owing to his anarchist past, Titta's father Aurelio is brought in for questioning and forced to drink [[castor oil]]. He limps home in a nauseous state to be washed by his wife, Miranda.

One summer afternoon, the family visits Uncle Teo, Aurelio's brother, confined to an insane asylum. They take him out for a day in the country, but he escapes into a tree, repeatedly yelling, "''Voglio una donna!''" ("I want a woman!"). All attempts to bring him down are met with stones that Teo carries in his pockets. A dwarf nun and two orderlies finally arrive on the scene. Marching up the ladder, the nun reprimands Teo, who obediently agrees to return to the asylum.

Fall arrives. The town's inhabitants embark in small boats to meet the passage of the [[SS Rex|SS ''Rex'']], the regime's proudest technological achievement. By midnight they have fallen asleep waiting for its arrival. Awakened by a foghorn, they watch in awe as the liner sails past, capsizing their boats in its wake. Titta's grandfather wanders lost in a disorienting fog so thick it seems to smother the house and the autumnal landscape. Walking out to the town's Grand Hotel, Titta and his friends find it boarded up. Like zombies, they waltz on the terrace with imaginary female partners enveloped in the fog.

The annual car race provides the occasion for Titta to daydream of winning the grand prize, Gradisca. One evening he visits the buxom tobacconist at closing time. She becomes aroused when he demonstrates he is strong enough to lift her, but is annoyed when he becomes overwhelmed as she presses her breasts into his face. She gives him a cigarette then coldly sends him home.

Winter brings with it record snowfall. Miranda nurses a sick Titta to health, then as spring arrives again, dies of an illness herself. Titta is devastated. Later, the village attends the reception for Gradisca's marriage to a ''[[Carabinieri|Carabiniere]]''. As Gradisca drives off with her new husband, someone notices that Titta has gone too.

==Deleted scene==
A scene was shot that was later cut from the film by Fellini. The scene was shot without sound. It is described, however, in the novelization published by Rizzoli in 1973 and involves the contessa's loss of a diamond ring down her toilet. Carlini, or "Eau de cologne", the man who empties the town's cesspits, is called to retrieve it. This scene is available on the Criterion release of the film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.criterion.com/films/208-amarcord|title=Amarcord|publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]]|access-date=13 April 2021|archive-date=13 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210413160046/https://www.criterion.com/films/208-amarcord|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://criterionchannel.vhx.tv/videos/deleted-scene-7|title=AMARCORD Deleted Scene|website=The Criterion Channel}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Europe===
Released in Italy on 18 December 1973, ''Amarcord'' was an "unmitigated success".{{sfn|Kezich|2006|p=314}} Critic Giovanni Grazzini, reviewing for the Italian newspaper ''[[Corriere della Sera]]'', described Fellini as "an artist at his peak" and the film as the work of a mature, more refined director whose "autobiographical content shows greater insight into historical fact and the reality of a generation. Almost all of ''Amarcord'' is a macabre dance against a cheerful background".{{sfn|Fava|Vigano|1990|loc=p. 157. Grazzini's review was first published in ''[[Corriere della Sera]]'', 19 December 1973}}

The film screened at the [[1974 Cannes Film Festival]], but was not entered into the main competition.<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web |url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/2214/year/1974.html |title=Festival de Cannes: Amarcord |access-date=27 April 2009 |publisher=[[Cannes Film Festival]] |archive-date=19 January 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120119023004/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/2214/year/1974.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

[[Russell Davies]], British film critic and later a [[BBC]] radio host, compared the film to the work of [[Thornton Wilder]] and [[Dylan Thomas]]: "The pattern is cyclic&nbsp;... A year in the life of a coastal village, with due emphasis on the seasons, and the births, marriages and deaths. It is an ''[[Our Town]]'' or ''[[Under Milk Wood]]'' of the Adriatic seaboard, concocted and displayed in the Roman film studios with the latter-day Fellini's distaste for real stone and wind and sky. The people, however, are real, and the many non-actors among them come in all the shapes and sizes one cares to imagine without plunging too deep into [[Tod Browning]] freak territory."{{sfn|Fava|Vigano|1990|loc=p. 158. Davies' review first published in ''[[The London Observer]]'', 29 September 1974}}

Rapidly picked up for international distribution after winning an Oscar for Best Foreign Film in 1975, the film was destined to be Fellini's "last major commercial success".<ref>{{cite book |last=Bondanella |first=Peter |url=https://archive.org/details/cinemaoffederico0000bond |title=The Cinema of Federico Fellini |publisher=[[Princeton University Press]] |year=1992 |page=265 |isbn=978-0-6910-0875-2 |url-access=registration}}</ref> In 2008, the film was voted at number 50 on the list of the "100 Greatest Films" by the French magazine ''[[Cahiers du cinéma]]''.<ref>{{cite web|last=Heron|first=Ambrose|url=https://www.filmdetail.com/2008/11/23/cahiers-du-cinemas-100-greatest-films/|title=Cahiers du cinéma's 100 Greatest Films|website=FILMdetail|date=23 November 2008|access-date=4 December 2022|archive-date=23 July 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230723191403/http://www.filmdetail.com/2008/11/23/cahiers-du-cinemas-100-greatest-films/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===United States===
On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Amarcord'' holds an approval rating of 88%, based on 48 reviews, with an average rating of 8.2/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "Ribald, sweet, and sentimental, ''Amarcord'' is a larger-than-life journey through a seaside village and its colorful citizens."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/amarcord|title=Amarcord|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=2016-03-08}}</ref>

When ''Amarcord'' opened in New York City, critic [[Vincent Canby]] lauded it as possibly "Fellini's most marvelous film&nbsp;... It's an extravagantly funny, sometimes dreamlike evocation of a year in the life of a small Italian coastal town in the nineteen-thirties, not as it literally was, perhaps, but as it is recalled by a director with a superstar's access to the resources of the Italian film industry and a piper's command over our imaginations.  When Mr. Fellini is working in peak condition, as he is in ''Amarcord'' (the vernacular for 'I remember' in [[Romagna]]), he somehow brings out the best in us. We become more humane, less stuffy, more appreciative of the profound importance of attitudes that in other circumstances would seem merely eccentric if not lunatic."<ref>{{cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |author-link=Vincent Canby |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9A03E0DF163FE13BBC4851DFBF66838F669EDE |title=Movie Review – Amarcord – Funny, Marvelous Fellini 'Amarcord' |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=20 September 1974 |access-date=22 February 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110519231815/http://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9A03E0DF163FE13BBC4851DFBF66838F669EDE |archive-date=19 May 2011 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Rating the film four out of four stars, critic [[Roger Ebert]] discussed Fellini's value as a director: "It's also absolutely breathtaking filmmaking. Fellini has ranked for a long time among the five or six greatest directors in the world, and of them all, he's the natural. [[Ingmar Bergman]] achieves his greatness through thought and soul-searching, [[Alfred Hitchcock]] built his films with meticulous craftsmanship, and [[Luis Buñuel]] used his fetishes and fantasies to construct barbed jokes about humanity. But Fellini&nbsp;... well, moviemaking for him seems almost effortless, like breathing, and he can orchestrate the most complicated scenes with purity and ease. He's the [[Willie Mays]] of movies."<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/amarcord-1974 |title=Amarcord |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |date=19 September 1974 |access-date=4 December 2022 |via=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> Ebert ranked the film fourth on his "10 Best Films of 1973" list.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20041215/COMMENTARY/41215001/1023 |title=Ebert's 10 Best Lists: 1967–present |newspaper=Chicago Sun-Times |date=15 December 2004 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060908200137/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F20041215%2FCOMMENTARY%2F41215001%2F1023 |archive-date=8 September 2006 |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> He later included the film on his ''[[Great Movies]]'' list.<ref>{{cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=4 January 2004 |title=Great Movies: Amarcord |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-amarcord-1974 |website=Chicago Sun-Times |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> [[Jay Cocks]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine considered it "some of the finest work Fellini has ever done—which also means it stands with the best that anyone in film has ever achieved."{{sfn|Alpert|1988|p=248}}

In the [[British Film Institute]]'s 2012 ''[[Sight & Sound]]'' polls of [[The Sight & Sound Greatest Films of All Time 2012|the greatest films ever made]], ''Amarcord'' was ranked 30th among directors.

==Home media==
In 1984, ''Amarcord'' became the first film released for home video fully [[Letterboxing (filming)|letterboxed]], as implemented by [[RCA Corporation|RCA]] for their [[Capacitance Electronic Disc]] videodisc format.<ref name="haines">{{cite book |last=Haines |first=Richard W. |author-link=Richard W. Haines |title=The Moviegoing Experience, 1968–2001 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=qe-myrvrzOUC&pg=PA139 |location=Jefferson, North Carolina |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |year=2003 |page=139 |isbn=978-0-7864-1361-4}}</ref> The film was later released on DVD twice by [[The Criterion Collection]], first in 1998, then re-released in 2006 with an [[anamorphic widescreen]] transfer and additional supplements. Criterion re-issued the 2006 release on Blu-ray Disc in 2011.  The film was also released as part of Criterion's "Essential Fellini" boxset in 2020.
