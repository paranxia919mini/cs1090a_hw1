'''''The Mastermind''''' is a 2025 [[heist film]] written and directed by [[Kelly Reichardt]]. It stars [[Josh O'Connor]], [[Alana Haim]], [[Hope Davis]], [[John Magaro]], [[Gaby Hoffmann]] and [[Bill Camp]]. Set in 1970, the film follows a struggling family man who plots to steal art from a suburban museum while the US rages over the [[Vietnam War]]. Reichardt described it as a struggle between the allure of individualism and the necessity of collective action. The film was inspired by a mixture of real-life events and classic films, including the [[1972 Worcester Art Museum robbery|1972 robbery of the Worcester Art Museum]] and the films of [[Jean-Pierre Melville]].

The film had its world premiere at the [[2025 Cannes Film Festival]] on May 23, 2025, where it competed for the [[Palme d'Or]], and was released in the United States by [[Mubi (streaming service)|Mubi]] on October 17, 2025.

==Plot==
In 1970 [[Framingham]], [[Massachusetts]], an unemployed carpenter, James Blaine "JB" Mooney, plots to steal four [[Arthur Dove]] paintings from the local art museum. He borrows money from his mother under the pretense of using it for work so he can pay three men to pull off the heist. The getaway driver, Larry Duffy, provides a stolen car but backs out of the heist. JB drives the two thieves, Guy Hickey and Ronnie Gibson, to the museum, where they steal the paintings. During the heist, Gibson pulls a gun on a student and beats up a security guard.

JB hides the paintings in a barn and returns home to find the [[FBI]] questioning his family. The FBI inform him that Ronnie was arrested after robbing a bank and named JB as the mastermind of the heist. JB denies this and asks his furious wife, Terri, to take their sons Carl and Tommy to his parents' house. Tommy at first refuses to go with her.

Guy calls JB to press him for more money. When JB takes Tommy along to meet him, an [[organized crime]] outfit abducts JB and forces him to take them to the paintings. As his role in the robbery is made public, JB goes into hiding with Fred and Maude, his friends from art school. While Fred is excited by JB's story, Maude privately confronts him, having deduced that JB's plan was to use their former professor, an admirer of Dove, as a [[Fence (criminal)|fence]] to sell the paintings. She asks him to leave. JB rejects Fred's suggestion to hide out at his brother's [[Toronto]] [[Agricultural commune|commune]] in favor of traveling to [[Cleveland]] to stay with friends. There, he learns that they have left town and hitchhikes to [[Cincinnati]].

JB reads that the paintings had been recovered. He calls Terri and attempts to rationalize his actions, but she hangs up when he asks for money. Unable to afford the bus fare to Toronto, he steals an elderly woman's pocketbook and escapes into a crowd of [[Opposition to United States involvement in the Vietnam War|anti-Vietnam War protesters]]. The police violently break up the rally and arrest JB along with several protesters.

=== Inspirations ===
Reichardt had a long-running fascination with art thefts, and had been collecting newspaper clippings about heists for years. She remarked that in the 1970s, it was relatively easy to steal art, and that the [[Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum heist]] happened because there were "[[LSD|acid heads]] running the security."<ref>{{Cite web |date=October 21, 2025 |title=Kelly Reichardt on The Mastermind: "Question Why You Want the Things You Want" |url=https://thefilmstage.com/kelly-reichardt-on-the-mastermind-question-why-you-want-the-things-you-want/ |access-date=October 28, 2025 |language=en-US}}</ref> She was specifically inspired by the [[1972 Worcester Art Museum robbery|1972 robbery of the Worcester Art Museum in Massachusetts]], in which two [[Paul Gauguin|Gauguins]], a [[Pablo Picasso|Picasso]], and a [[Rembrandt]] were stolen.<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Andriotis |first=Mary Elizabeth |date=October 20, 2025 |title=The Mastermind Takes Inspiration from a Real-Life 1970s Art Museum Heist |url=https://galeriemagazine.com/the-mastermind-inspired-by-1970s-art-museum-heist/ |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=Galerie Magazine |language=en-US}}</ref> She started working on the movie after reading about the 50th anniversary of the Worcester heist. She explained that "I'm just fascinated with people stealing art, and the idea of taking something from a public space to enjoy on your own, like the people that had the [[Willem de Kooning|de Kooning]] in their bedroom. Instead of everyone enjoying this painting, it'll just be you behind your bedroom door."<ref name=":3">{{Cite web |last=Jenkins |first=David |date=October 22, 2025 |title=Kelly Reichardt: 'I'm fascinated with people stealing art' |url=https://lwlies.com/in-conversation/kelly-reichardt-the-mastermind |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=Little White Lies |language=en-GB}}</ref> Although many Reichardt films are shot in the Pacific Northwest, Reichardt set the film in Massachusetts because she went to art school in the state and felt that the script "makes sense [as] a Massachusetts story."<ref name=":4" />

The film's setting highlights the decline of 1960s counterculture. Reichardt said that she moved the date of the heist from 1972 to 1970 because the characters needed to be "figuring out what's next" as a country, because at the end of the 1960s, "[t]his whole freewheeling thing's not working out."<ref name=":3" /> In addition, the film's narrative parallels "the unconventional narratives of [1970s] [[New Hollywood]] [cinema] that were populated by offbeat antiheroes."<ref name="grierson" /> Living in a comfortable suburb, and with no risk of being drafted,<ref name=":7">{{Cite web |last=Roxborough |first=Scott |date=May 23, 2025 |title=Kelly Reichardt on 'The Mastermind,' Josh O'Connor and What the '70s Have to Teach Us Today |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/kelly-reichardt-the-mastermind-josh-oconnor-cannes-1236227650/ |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref> JB has the luxury of not being "tuned in to the political climate," although reality remains "just on the edges of the frame."<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |last=Shaffer |first=Marshall |date=October 16, 2025 |title=Kelly Reichardt on 'The Mastermind' and the Art of the Aftermath |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/kelly-reichardt-interview-the-mastermind/ |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=Slant Magazine |language=en-US}}</ref> She noted that JB was "rebelling against his middle-class life" without "a clear idea of what the alternative would be," and "mindlessly[] ... take[s] advantage of his privilege whenever he is in trouble or whenever he needs to."<ref name=":5">{{Cite web |last=Beaupre |first=Mitchell |date=October 24, 2025 |title=A Man Escaped: Kelly Reichardt on observance and disruption in The Mastermind • Journal • A Letterboxd Magazine |url=https://letterboxd.com/journal/kelly-reichardt-the-mastermind-interview/ |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=letterboxd.com |language=en}}</ref> 

JB's unfocused rebellion ties into a common Reichardt theme, "the individual versus the person in a community."<ref name=":7" /> Like [[Robert Bresson]]'s ''[[Pickpocket (film)|Pickpocket]]'' (1959), the film is structured as an "exploration of a sinner, why he sins, and the consequences of those sins."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gorham |first=Luke |date=October 7, 2025 |title=The Mastermind — Kelly Reichardt [Review] |url=https://inreviewonline.com/2025/09/26/the-mastermind-review/ |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=In Review Online |language=en-US}}</ref> Reichardt said that the film was in part about the "cost" of "personal freedom," explaining that "being able to be the outlaw is a privilege" and that JB relies on the women in his life to "carr[y] the weight" of his antics.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Balanescu |first=Miriam |date=October 17, 2025 |title='Paintings were suddenly seen as money': The reason art heists exploded in the 1970s |url=https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20251016-the-reason-art-heists-exploded-in-the-1970s |access-date=October 27, 2025 |website=www.bbc.com |language=en-GB}}</ref> She added that the film questions whether a person can "really remain separate from what's going on around [them]."<ref name=":7" />

JB's choice of what art to steal reflects his own personal tastes, rather than profit maximization. O'Connor said that "there's a sort of ego there ... ''<nowiki/>'I'll steal the artists that only real artists know about.'''"<ref>{{Cite news |last=Simon |first=Scott |date=October 25, 2025 |title=Josh O'Connor talks about his leading role in the art === Style ===
The film plays with genre expectations and has been described as an "anti-heist movie." Reichardt explained while she was heavily inspired by the heist films of [[Jean-Pierre Melville]], she did not want to focus on the process of the heist as much as Melville would. She was more interested in the "unwinding" of JB's plan than the plan itself, and said that "if you went to this film thinking it was a heist film, you might be pissed."<ref name=":3" /> However, she noted that the heist film formula existed for a reason and that scripting a film to defy genre conventions required "a lot of trial and error."<ref name=":5" />

Reichardt scripted the character of JB like a [[Robert Bresson|Bresson]] actor, "a sort of blank character that you can project onto." She praised Josh O'Connor for "set[ting] aside ego" to play the part.<ref name=":3" /> [[Manohla Dargis]] explained that "[o]ne of the trickier things about JB as a character is that he isn't obviously likable or dislikable, which makes it hard to get a handle on him."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Dargis |first=Manohla |date=October 16, 2025 |title='The Mastermind' Review: A Robbery and a Life Unraveling |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/10/16/movies/the-mastermind-review.html |access-date=October 27, 2025 |work=The New York Times |language=en}}</ref> Sarah Belmont agreed that "figuring out who [JB] is and what drives him becomes almost as important as knowing if he will get away with his master plan."<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Belmont |first=Sarah |date=June 24, 2025 |title='The Mastermind' Presents an Art Heist That's In on the Joke |url=https://www.artnews.com/art-news/reviews/the-mastermind-film-review-kelly-reichardt-josh-oconnor-1234746055/ |access-date=October 9, 2025 |website=ARTnews.com |language=en-US}}</ref>
