'''''Best in Show''''' is a 2000 American [[mockumentary]] [[comedy film]] co-written by [[Christopher Guest]] and [[Eugene Levy]] and directed by Guest. The film follows five entrants in a prestigious [[conformation show|dog show]] as they travel to and compete at the show, and stars Guest and Levy alongside [[Jennifer Coolidge]], [[John Michael Higgins]], [[Michael Hitchcock]], [[Jane Lynch]], [[Michael McKean]], [[Catherine O'Hara]], and [[Parker Posey]]. Much of the dialogue was improvised. Many of the comic actors were also involved in Guest's other films, including ''[[Waiting for Guffman]]'', ''[[A Mighty Wind]]'', ''[[For Your Consideration (film)|For Your Consideration]]'', and ''[[Mascots (2016 film)|Mascots]]''. The film's score was composed by C. J. Vanston.

==Plot==
Five dogs and their owners, trainers, and handlers travel to [[Philadelphia]] to compete in the Mayflower Kennel Club Dog Show.

Gerry and Cookie Fleck are a middle-class couple from [[Florida]] who arrive at the Taft Hotel with their [[Norwich Terrier]] Winky. After having forgotten to pay their credit card bill and short of cash, they are forced to sleep in the hotel's storage room. While traveling to the show, they encounter several of Cookie's former lovers who try to seduce her again, to Gerry's chagrin.

Meg and Hamilton Swan, a stereotypical [[yuppie]] couple from a [[Chicago]] suburb, arrive with their [[weimaraner]] Beatrice. Their constant doting and neurotic behavior toward Beatrice—for example, taking her to a psychotherapist after she sees them having sex in a position they learned from the ''[[Kama Sutra]]''—confuses and upsets her.

Harlan Pepper, the affable [[Southern United States|Southern]] owner of a fishing goods store and an aspiring [[ventriloquist]], arrives with his [[bloodhound]] Hubert. The Pepper family has raised a variety of hounds for generations.

Sherri Ann Cabot is the socially inept, overly glamorous [[trophy wife]] of her elderly [[age disparity in sexual relationships|sugar daddy]] Leslie Ward Cabot. A former two-time winner of the show, she receives help with her [[Standard Poodle]] Rhapsody in White, also known as Butch, from her taskmaster trainer Christy Cummings. An extremely competitive [[Butch (lesbian slang)|butch]] [[lesbian]], Christy rigorously trains Butch for the show and rejects Sherri Ann's offers of a beauty [[makeover]]. Leslie is oblivious to Christy and Sherri Ann's romantic involvement, as well as everything else happening around him.

Scott Donlan and Stefan Vanderhoof are a [[camp (style)|campy]] [[gay]] couple who take great pride in their [[Shih Tzu]] Miss Agnes and are confident that she will win the competition. They share a love of old movies and enjoy making fun of Christy Cummings but are friendly to the other competitors, especially the Flecks.

The dog show is hosted by dog expert Trevor Beckwith and oblivious "color" commentator Buck Laughlin, whose inane banter is lost on Beckwith. During the first round, Beatrice is disqualified when she becomes aggressive and Hamilton cannot control her, which the Swans chalk up to a dog toy of Beatrice's that they had lost prior to the show. The other four dogs advance to the final round. Just before the finals, Cookie dislocates her knee and insists that Gerry take over for her despite his literal two left feet (the result of a birth defect). Though Gerry is nervous, Winky takes Best in Show.

After the competition, Gerry and Cookie return home to Florida and enjoy brief fame. While in a studio recording [[novelty song]]s about terriers, they discover that the recording engineer is yet another of Cookie's ex-lovers, to Gerry's  frustration. Christy and Sherri Ann, now openly in a relationship (Leslie's fate is unclear), publish ''American Bitch'', a magazine for lesbian owners of purebred dogs. After spending weeks on a [[kibbutz]], Harlan fulfills his dream of being a ventriloquist, entertaining sparse crowds with a [[honky tonk]] song-and-dance number. Stefan and Scott design a calendar featuring Shih Tzu dogs in costume appearing in scenes from classic films such as ''[[Casablanca (film)|Casablanca]]'' and ''[[Gone with the Wind (film)|Gone with the Wind]]''. Meg and Hamilton are visibly happier together after replacing Beatrice with a [[pug]] named Kipper, whom they claim enjoys watching them have sex.

===Dogs===
The starring dogs listed are denoted by their [[Breed registry|registered name]]s. All have earned the title ''Ch.'', indicating they have qualified for a championship at conformation shows, with most qualifying for the [[Canadian Kennel Club]] Championship—hence the prefix ''Can''. The kennel prefix of one or more breeders precedes each dog's registered name; e. g. in "Arokat Echobar Take Me Dancing", the first breeder is "Arokat" and the second is "Echobar" while the name is "Take Me Dancing". ==Production==
Filming took place in Vancouver and Los Angeles. In a 2000 interview, Christopher Guest reflected, "Originally, we thought it would be easier to go to an actual dog show and film there, but nobody would let us do that."<ref name="notes">{{Cite web|url=https://www.cinema.com/articles/159/best-in-show-production-notes.phtml|title=Best in Show : Production Notes|website=Cinema.com|access-date=September 9, 2021|archive-date=September 9, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210909015453/https://www.cinema.com/articles/159/best-in-show-production-notes.phtml|url-status=live}}</ref> Eugene Levy adds that, "We actually had to stage our own dog show. And that's where the nightmare started. We literally had to put everything together from scratch, get somebody to organize the whole show, get the dogs in, find trainers and so forth."<ref name="notes"/>

The greater part of the film was [[improvisation|improvised]] by the actors, with little to no planning. Sixty hours of footage were filmed, as was the case with Guest's previous mockumentary ''[[Waiting for Guffman]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://variety.com/2001/digital/features/best-in-show-5-1200468847/|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|title=Best In Show|date=June 8, 2001|last=Koehler|first=Robert|access-date=June 21, 2019|archive-date=October 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201004120538/https://variety.com/2001/digital/features/best-in-show-5-1200468847/|url-status=live}}</ref> Levy said, "There is a much wider appeal for this movie than for ''Waiting for Guffman'', which was a funny movie and a funny premise but not as accessible as this film, which is more mainstream. People just love dogs."<ref name="notes"/> ''Waiting for Guffman'' premiered on the festival circuit, and a few months later in early 1997, played to just roughly 50 theaters in the United States. It was distributed by [[Sony Pictures Entertainment|Sony]]'s [[arthouse film|arthouse]] division [[Sony Pictures Classics]], while this film was distributed by the more commercial [[Warner Bros. Pictures]], and played in 497 theaters.<ref>{{cite web | title=Weekend Box Office: Audiences Bewitched by Parents | date=October 30, 2000 | url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2000/10/30/weekend-box-office-audiences-bewitched-by-parents }}</ref>

==Reception==
''Best in Show'' received critical acclaim. The film has a 93% approval rating on [[Rotten Tomatoes]], based on 118 reviews with an average rating of 7.5/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "A fine example of writer/director/star Christopher Guest's gift for improvisation comedy, ''Best in Show'' boasts an appealingly quirky premise and a brilliantly talented cast."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/best_in_show/ |title=Best In Show (2000) |access-date=2021-06-17 |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |archive-date=June 11, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240611183837/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/best_in_show/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The film also has a score of 78 out of 100 on [[Metacritic]], based on 33 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/best-in-show |title=Best In Show Reviews |access-date=2018-03-06 |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |work=[[Metacritic]] |archive-date=February 24, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150224204459/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/best-in-show |url-status=live }}</ref> It won [[American Comedy Awards|American]], [[British Comedy Awards|British]], and [[Canadian Comedy Awards]]. The film is number 38 on [[Bravo (U.S. TV network)|Bravo's]] "100 Funniest Movies". A 2022 ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' review of the best comedy films of the 21st century ranked this as first.<ref name="best">{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/greatest-comedies-of-the-21st-century-630244/best-in-show-2000-628162/ |title=70 Greatest Comedies of the 21st Century |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=2022-10-06 |accessdate=2022-06-10 |lang=en-US |last=Hynes |first=Eric |archive-date=October 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221001172854/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/greatest-comedies-of-the-21st-century-630244/best-in-show-2000-628162/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In June 2025, the film ranked number 57 on ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century" and was one of the films voted for the "Readers' Choice" edition of the list, finishing at number 117.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |date=July 2, 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html | title=The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | work=The New York Times | date=June 23, 2025 }}</ref> In July 2025, it ranked number 80 on ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century."<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-movies-of-the-21st-century-1235200512/ | title=100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=July 1, 2025 }}</ref>

The film opened to a weekend gross of $413,436 to 13 theatres with an average of $31,802 per theater. After opening to a total of 497 theaters, the film ended its run with a domestic total of $18,715,392. The foreign gross of $2,074,164 brought its total gross revenue to $20,789,556.<ref name=Mojo/>

==Legacy==
''Best in Show'' was the inspiration for the broadcast of the [[National Dog Show]], which has aired each [[Thanksgiving (United States)|Thanksgiving]] on NBC since 2002.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.phillymag.com/news/2018/11/10/national-dog-show-thanksgiving/ |title=How the National Dog Show Won Thanksgiving |website=Philly Mag |date=2018-11-10 |access-date=July 16, 2020 |archive-date=August 8, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200808031836/https://www.phillymag.com/news/2018/11/10/national-dog-show-thanksgiving/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

To celebrate Halloween 2022, former ''[[Victorious]]'' castmates [[Ariana Grande]] and [[Elizabeth Gillies]] paid homage to ''Best in Show'' by recreating several scenes from the movie in which they each dressed up as multiple characters. After shooting in May, the results were then posted to Instagram on October 28 and drew praise from Jennifer Coolidge.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/movies/ariana-grande-best-in-show-halloween-costume-jennifer-coolidge/|title=Ariana Grande wins blue ribbon from Jennifer Coolidge for Best in Show costume: 'F---ing great'|first=Lauren|last=Huff|magazine=Entertainment Weekly|date=2022-10-28|accessdate=2022-10-31|archive-date=October 30, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221030202945/https://ew.com/movies/ariana-grande-best-in-show-halloween-costume-jennifer-coolidge/|url-status=live}}</ref>
