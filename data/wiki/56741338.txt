{{nihongo|'''''The Boy and the Heron'''''|君たちはどう生きるか|Kimitachi wa Dō Ikiru ka|{{lit|How Do You{{efn|Second-person plural, as in "you all"}} Live?}}|lead=yes}} is a 2023 [[Anime|Japanese animated]] [[fantasy film]] written and directed by [[Hayao Miyazaki]]. Produced by [[Studio Ghibli]], the film's Japanese title references Genzaburō Yoshino's 1937 novel ''[[How Do You Live?]]'' but is not an adaptation of it. The film stars the voices of [[Soma Santoki]], [[Masaki Suda]], [[Ko Shibasaki]], [[Aimyon]], [[Yoshino Kimura]], [[Takuya Kimura]], [[Kaoru Kobayashi (actor)|Kaoru Kobayashi]], and [[Shinobu Otake]]. It follows a boy named Mahito Maki who moves to the countryside after his mother's death, discovers an abandoned tower near his new home, and enters a fantastical world with a talking [[grey heron]].

Miyazaki announced his retirement in September 2013 but later reversed this decision after working on the short film ''[[Boro the Caterpillar]]'' (2018). He began [[storyboard]]ing for a new feature-length project in July 2016, and official production began in May 2017. The film's title was announced in October 2017, initially targeting a release around the [[2020 Summer Olympics]]. By May 2020, 36 minutes of the film had been [[Hand-drawn animation|hand-drawn]] by 60 animators, with no set deadline. Production spanned approximately seven years, facing delays due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic in Japan|COVID-19 pandemic]] and Miyazaki's slowed animation pace, before nearing completion in October 2022. Financing for the project involved [[streaming]] deals for previous Ghibli films. According to producer [[Toshio Suzuki]], ''The Boy and the Heron'' is the most expensive film ever produced in Japan. The screenplay draws heavily from Miyazaki's childhood and explores themes of [[coming of age]] and coping with a world marked by conflict and loss. [[Joe Hisaishi]] composed the film's score, while [[Kenshi Yonezu]] wrote and sang the film's theme song "[[Spinning Globe]]".

''The Boy and the Heron'' was released in Japan on July 14, 2023, by [[Toho]], and was screened in both traditional theaters and in other formats such as [[IMAX]]. The release was noted for its intentional absence of any promotion, with Ghibli choosing not to release any trailers, images, synopsis, or casting details of the film in advance of its Japanese premiere except a single poster. The film received critical acclaim and grossed {{USD|294.2 million}} worldwide, making it the sixth [[List of highest-grossing Japanese films|highest-grossing Japanese film of all time]]. Among [[List of accolades received by The Boy and the Heron|its numerous accolades]], the film was named by the [[National Board of Review]] as one of the top 10 films of 2023 and won Best Animated Feature Film at the [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature|Academy Awards]], the [[BAFTA Award for Best Animated Film|BAFTA Awards]], the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Animated Feature Film|Golden Globe Awards]], and the [[Japan Academy Film Prize for Animation of the Year|Japanese Academy Film Prize]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be kept between 400 to 700 words! -->
During the [[Pacific War]], Mahito Maki loses his hospitalized mother, Hisako, in a fire. Mahito's father Shoichi, an [[Arsenal|air munitions factory]] owner, [[Sororate marriage|marries his late wife's sister]], Natsuko, and they evacuate to her rural estate. Mahito, distant to the pregnant Natsuko, encounters a peculiar [[grey heron]] leading him to a sealed tower, the last known location of Natsuko's architect granduncle.

After a school fight, Mahito [[Self-harm|deliberately injures himself]]. The heron, now speaking, entices Mahito with promises of finding his mother. Mahito is nearly kidnapped by a swarm of creatures, but Natsuko saves him with a [[whistling arrow]], inspiring him to craft his own bow and arrow. The arrow is magically imbued with true aim after it is [[fletching|fletched]] with the heron's feather. Mahito's reading of [[How Do You Live?|a book]] left by Hisako is interrupted when an ill Natsuko disappears into the forest. Leading one of the estate's elderly maids, Kiriko, into the tower, Mahito is deceived by a watery imitation of his mother made by the heron, which dissolves at his touch. Affronted, he pierces the heron's beak with his arrow, revealing a flightless creature, the Birdman, living inside it. A wizard appears, ordering the Birdman to guide Mahito and Kiriko as all three sink into the floor.

Mahito descends into an oceanic world. He is rescued from attacking pelicans and a forbidding, [[megalith]]ic [[dolmen]] by a younger Kiriko, an adept fisherwoman who uses fire through a magic wand. They catch and sell a giant fish to bubble-like spirits called Warawara, which fly to the world above to be reborn. A [[pyrokinetic]] young woman, Himi, protects Warawara from predation by the pelicans. A dying pelican explains that their species is desperate to survive after being [[Introduced species|introduced]] to this world with no other food. Kiriko mediates peace between Mahito and the Birdman, and Mahito plugs the Birdman's beak, restoring his flight. The two are separated by anthropomorphic, man-eating [[parakeet]]s. Himi saves Mahito and shows him a counterpart of the tower which contains doors to many worlds. They enter a door leading back to Natsuko's estate and are spotted by Shoichi, but Mahito returns through the door to continue his search for Natsuko.

Infiltrating the parakeets' kingdom, Mahito finds Natsuko in a delivery room. Natsuko rebuffs him, and Mahito calls her his mother. Himi incinerates the paper attacking them, but all three are rendered unconscious by the encounter. In a dream, Mahito meets the wizard, Natsuko's granduncle. The wizard, preoccupied with a stack of stone toy blocks representing their dimension, requests Mahito, possessing the power of his bloodline, to succeed in the custodianship of this world. Mahito notices that the blocks are infused with malice. Waking up, he is freed from captivity by the Birdman. They climb the tower to pursue the Parakeet King, who is delivering Himi to the wizard, hoping to convince him to maintain the world. The wizard has collected replacement blocks free of malice for Mahito and implores him to build a better world with them. Mahito refuses, acknowledging his own malice embodied by his self-inflicted scar, and vows instead to embrace those who love him.

The Parakeet King takes the blocks and tries to build a better world himself, but the stack is too unstable and falls. The world begins to collapse and flood, and Mahito, Himi, and the Birdman escape, reuniting with Natsuko and young Kiriko. Learning that Himi is his birth mother, Mahito warns her of her fate, but she returns to her own time without worry. Mahito returns with Natsuko, amidst an exodus of animals that revert to non-anthropomorphic forms. The Birdman notices Mahito keeping a stone of power, and advises him to forget his experiences. A charm doll carried by Mahito transforms back into the old Kiriko. Two years after the war, Mahito moves back to Tokyo with his father, Natsuko, and his new brother.

==Themes==
''The Boy and the Heron'' has autobiographical features.{{efn|[[GKIDS]] advertised the film as a "semi-autobiographical fantasy about life, death, and creation, in tribute to friendship, from the mind of Hayao Miyazaki."<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Boy and the Heron |url=https://gkids.com/films/the-boy-and-the-heron/ |access-date=2023-09-15 |website=GKIDS Films |language=en-US |archive-date=September 13, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230913050931/https://gkids.com/films/the-boy-and-the-heron/ |url-status=live }}</ref>}} The childhood of the protagonist, Mahito Maki, mirrors Miyazaki's. Miyazaki's father, like Mahito's father, was employed by a company involved in the manufacturing of fighter plane components. Additionally, Miyazaki's family had to evacuate from the city to the countryside during the war.<ref name="THRFirstReact">{{Cite web |last=Brzeski |first=Patrick |date=2023-07-15 |title=First Reactions to Hayao Miyazaki's Final Film, 'The Boy and the Heron,' Begin to Emerge From Japan |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/first-reactions-hayao-miyazaki-studio-ghibli-the-boy-and-the-heron-1235536910/ |access-date=2023-09-11 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US |archive-date=September 7, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230907093741/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/first-reactions-hayao-miyazaki-studio-ghibli-the-boy-and-the-heron-1235536910/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The hospital fire at the beginning of the film evokes personal parallels with Miyazaki's loss of his mother,<ref name="Indiewire-GKIDS" /> who was known for her strong opinions and is believed to have been a source of inspiration for several of the director's female characters. Mahito's emotional connection with his mother parallels Miyazaki's love for his mother.<ref name="JT" /> Taichiro Yoshino, the grandson of Genzaburō Yoshino, recalled that Miyazaki intended to differentiate Mahito from the joyful and optimistic protagonists of his previous films, as he felt that "boys are actually less pure and swirling with all kinds of things."<ref name="THRFirstReact" /><ref name="Asahi" /> ''Fiction Horizon'' found Mahito to embody traits of resilience, vulnerability, and transformative growth.<ref name="FH">{{cite web |author=Milaković, Hrvoje |date=December 25, 2023 |title=The Boy And The Heron: Meet Mahito Maki, The Film's Protagonist |url=https://fictionhorizon.com/the-boy-and-the-heron-meet-mahito-maki/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231225171313/https://fictionhorizon.com/the-boy-and-the-heron-meet-mahito-maki/ |archive-date=December 25, 2023 |accessdate=December 25, 2023 |publisher=Fiction Horizon}}</ref>, and the [[BBC]] defined the film as "a [[coming-of-age tale]] in which a child must overcome his selfishness and learn to live for others."<ref>{{Cite news |date=2023-07-14 |last=Schley |first=Matt |title=How Do You Live: Hayao Miyazaki releases mystery final film |language=en-GB |publisher=[[BBC News]] |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66197993 |access-date=2023-07-14 |archive-date=July 14, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230714062307/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66197993 |url-status=live}}</ref>

For ''[[Nikkei Asia]]''{{'s}} Kaori Shoji, the film's primary driving force is the longing for a mother's presence and a "quest for worthiness", a recurring theme in Miyazaki's works. This emotion fuels the narrative, occasionally diverting into adventures involving fantastical creatures. Mahito's father marries Hisako's sister, evoking [[Freudian]] undertones. Shoji also identified certain "quasi-sexual" elements, such as a scene in which Mahito is covered in frogs.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Hayao Miyazaki's farewell film may be his most personal |url=https://asia.nikkei.com/Life-Arts/Arts/Hayao-Miyazaki-s-farewell-film-may-be-his-most-personal |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230912135203/https://asia.nikkei.com/Life-Arts/Arts/Hayao-Miyazaki-s-farewell-film-may-be-his-most-personal |archive-date=September 12, 2023 |access-date=2023-09-12 |website=[[Nikkei Asia]] |publisher=[[Nikkei, Inc.]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

Suzuki spoke of Miyazaki's desire to create the film based on personal experiences and relationships when he proposed it in 2016. He was initially taken aback, given Miyazaki's avoidance of highly personal themes. The narrative is inspired by Miyazaki's (Mahito) professional relationships with [[Isao Takahata]] (Granduncle) and Suzuki (Heron), and integrates themes of destruction, reconstruction and imagination. Following Takahata's death in 2018, revisions were made to the film, shifting the focus from the Granduncle to the relationship between Mahito and Heron. Suzuki observed that Miyazaki's storytelling choices were deeply influenced by the memories shared between him and Miyazaki.<ref name="IndieWire-Direction">{{Cite web |last=Desowitz |first=Bill |date=2023-11-22 |title={{-'}}The Boy and the Heron' Is So Personal, Hayao Miyazaki Needed a Year to Grieve Before Pivoting in a New Direction |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/animation/boy-and-the-heron-miyazaki-anime-ghibli-1234927630/ |access-date=2023-12-21 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US |archive-date=November 22, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231122162444/https://www.indiewire.com/features/animation/boy-and-the-heron-miyazaki-anime-ghibli-1234927630/ |url-status=live }}</ref> A review on [[RogerEbert.com]] said that the film was "the work of an artist reflecting on a career." It also portrays old age as connected to, and mirrored in, childhood.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tallerico |first=Brian |date=September 9, 2023 |title=TIFF 2023: The Boy and the Heron, We Grown Now, Unicorns {{!}} Festivals & Awards |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/tiff-2023-the-boy-and-the-heron-we-grown-now-unicorns |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230910101829/https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/tiff-2023-the-boy-and-the-heron-we-grown-now-unicorns |archive-date=September 10, 2023 |access-date=2023-09-10 |website=Roger Ebert |language=en}}</ref> ''[[IndieWire]]''{{'}}s Brian Welk argued that the film synthesizes various tonal and stylistic elements from Miyazaki's past work. He drew attention to the film's departure from the traditional children's genre, instead aligning more closely with the "violent" style of ''[[Princess Mononoke]]''{{nbsp}}(1997) than the "cuddly charm" of ''[[My Neighbor Totoro]]''{{nbsp}}(1988).<ref name="Indiewire-GKIDS" />

The film contains the message of creating "a world without conflict with your own hands."<ref name="Asahi">{{Cite web |date=July 14, 2023 |script-title=ja:「君たちはどう生きるか」宮崎駿監督が、新作映画について語っていたこと。そして吉野源三郎のこと｜好書好日 |trans-title=What "How Do You Live?" It was the first 4K release for a Studio Ghibli film.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.ghibli.jp/info/013830/|title=宮﨑駿監督最新作「君たちはどう生きるか」の4K UHD/ブルーレイ/DVDの発売が7月3日に発売されます。|trans-title=The 4K UHD/Blu-ray/DVD of director Hayao Miyazaki's latest film "How Do You Live?" will be released on July 3rd.|language=ja|location=|publisher=[[Studio Ghibli]]|access-date=April 26, 2024|archive-date=May 23, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523212131/https://www.ghibli.jp/info/013830/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.waltdisneystudios.jp/kimitachi|title=君たちはどう生きるか｜スタジオジブリ｜ディズニー公式|trans-title=How Do You Live?｜Studio Ghibli｜Disney Official|language=ja|publisher=[[Walt Disney Japan]]|access-date=April 26, 2024|archive-date=April 26, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240426021939/https://www.waltdisneystudios.jp/kimitachi|url-status=live}}</ref> In North America, it was released by GKIDS and [[Shout! Studios]] in the same formats on July 9, preceded by a digital release on June 25<ref>{{Cite web |last=Massoto |first=Erick |date=2024-04-29 |title={{-'}}The Boy and the Heron' Becomes First-Ever Studio Ghibli Title Released in 4K UHD |url=https://collider.com/the-boy-and-the-heron-4k-uhd-blu-ray-release-date/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240430001308/https://collider.com/the-boy-and-the-heron-4k-uhd-blu-ray-release-date/ |archive-date=2024-04-30 |access-date=2024-04-30 |website=Collider}}</ref> and followed by a streaming release on [[HBO Max]] on September 6.<ref>{{cite magazine | last=Spangler | first=Todd | title=''The Boy and the Heron'' Streaming Release Date Set on Max | magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] | date=2024-07-24 | url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/the-boy-and-the-heron-streaming-date-max-1236082855/ | access-date=2024-09-08 | archive-date=July 27, 2024 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240727225002/https://variety.com/2024/film/news/the-boy-and-the-heron-streaming-date-max-1236082855/ | url-status=live}}</ref> It was released on [[Netflix]] in territories outside the US and Japan on October 7.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Frater |first=Patrick |date=2024-03-21 |title=Oscar Winner 'The Boy and the Heron' Heading to Netflix Outside U.S. and Japan |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/oscar-the-boy-and-the-heron-netflix-1235948075/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240321143154/https://variety.com/2024/film/news/oscar-the-boy-and-the-heron-netflix-1235948075/ |archive-date=2024-03-21 |url-status=live |access-date=2024-04-10 |magazine=Variety}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title={{-'}}The Boy and the Heron' to Stream on Netflix Around the World Starting October 7 |url=https://about.netflix.com/en/news/the-boy-and-the-heron-to-stream-on-netflix-around-the-world |access-date=2024-09-28 |website=About Netflix |language=en}}</ref>

Film journalist Hiroo Ōtaka noted the industry's surprise at the film's rapid box office success despite its unconventional "minimalist promotion" strategy. This success evoked mixed feelings among those within the industry. Distribution representatives told Ōtaka they were concerned that this strategy of not promoting the film threatened their livelihoods and the necessity of traditional advertising methods. In addition to this marketing strategy, Ōtaka identified two important aspects of the film's commercial success. The first is that the deliberate approach of leaving the film by itself capitalized on its existing fan base by generating discussion and interaction on social media. He also mentioned Ghibli's earlier works, regularly featured on Friday Road Show for a decade, indirectly promoted the film.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=大高宏雄 |last2=杉本健太郎 |date=2023-07-21 |title=『君たちはどう生きるか』ヒットに映画関係者も驚く3つの"異変" |trans-title=Three "anomalies" in the success of ''How Do You Live?'' |url=https://www.businessinsider.jp/post-273004 |access-date=2023-09-11 |website=BUSINESS INSIDER JAPAN |language=ja |archive-date=July 28, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230728155637/https://www.businessinsider.jp/post-273004 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Impact==
After the film's release, the novel ''How Do You Live?'' was temporarily in short supply due to the "synergy" with the film. The publisher decided to reprint the book, while the volumes were listed in large quantities on [[Mercari|Mercari marketplace app]]. On July 20, [[Iwanami Shoten]] announced on its official Twitter account that the total circulation of the book had reached 1.8 million copies, making it the number one book published by Iwanami Bunko in its history.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Eisuke |first=Asano |date=2023-07-27 |title=｢君たちはどう生きるか」映画効果で同名小説が再注目 フリマアプリにも大量出品 |url=https://www.sankei.com/article/20230727-ZJPP7YM5HNDEJJ5TGLHBVNKMSM/ |trans-title=The film ''How Do You Live?'' has brought renewed attention to the novel of the same title, which is now being sold in large quantities on flea market apps |access-date=2024-03-12 |website=[[Sankei Shimbun]] |language=ja |archive-date=August 1, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230801025449/https://www.sankei.com/article/20230727-ZJPP7YM5HNDEJJ5TGLHBVNKMSM/ |url-status=live }}</ref> ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]''{{'s}} Gavin J Blair felt that ''The Boy and the Heron'' is a significant contributor to the recent revival of [[Japanese popular culture]] in the West, alongside ''[[Godzilla Minus One]]'' (2023), the first season of ''[[One Piece (2023 TV series)|One Piece]]'' (2023), and the first season of ''[[Shōgun (2024 TV series)|Shōgun]]'' (2024).<ref>{{Cite web |last=Blair |first=Gavin J. |date=February 16, 2024 |title=Berlin: Toei Animation's Yoshi Ikezawa on Ambitious New CGI Feature ''Hypergalactic'' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/hypergalactic-toei-animation-yoshi-ikezawa-berlin-interview-1235823410/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240216210026/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/hypergalactic-toei-animation-yoshi-ikezawa-berlin-interview-1235823410/ |archive-date=February 16, 2024 |access-date=April 3, 2024 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |language=en-US}}</ref>
