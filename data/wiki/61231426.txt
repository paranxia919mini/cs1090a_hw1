'''''Something Different''''' ({{Langx|cs|O něčem jiném|lit=About something different}}) is a 1963 [[Czechoslovakia|Czechoslovak]] film directed by [[Věra Chytilová]]. The film intersperses two separate narratives: one following Věra, a fictional housewife living in Czechoslovakia, and another following Eva, an Olympic gymnast played by real-life Olympic gold medalist [[Eva Bosáková]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=https://www.theartsdesk.com/film/dvd-something-differenta-bagful-fleas|title=DVD: Something Different/A Bagful of Fleas|last=Birchenough|first=Tom|date=5 March 2016|website=[[The Arts Desk]]|access-date=2019-07-07}}</ref>

Chytilová's first feature-length film, it is regarded as one of the breakout films of the [[Czech New Wave]], as well as an early example of [[women's cinema]] in the [[Eastern Bloc]].<ref name=":2" /> While not as well known as some of Chytilová's other films such as [[Daisies (film)|''Daisies'']] and ''[[Fruit of Paradise]]'', it won the main prize at the 1963 [[Mannheim Film Festival]],<ref name=":3" /> and has been praised by both contemporary critics<ref name=":4" /> and 21st century retrospectives,<ref name=":2" /><ref name=":5" /> in addition to receiving a fair amount of attention in academic film literature.<ref name=":6" /><ref name=":1" /><ref name=":7" />

== Background ==
Released in 1963, ''Something Different'' was one of the first films of the [[Czech New Wave]], and Chytilová's first feature-length film. Chytilová was one of the only women filmmakers who participated in the Czech New Wave, and ''Something Different'', like many other of the entries in her filmography, focuses on women's lives and challenges in Czechoslovakia.<ref name=":0" />

== Style and synopsis ==
''Something Different'' merges documentary-style footage of Eva, a Czechoslovak gymnast played by real-life Olympic gold-medalist [[Eva Bosáková]], as she endures incessant training in preparation for a competition. These scenes are juxtaposed against a narrative following the fictional housewife Věra, who is discontented and overwhelmed by housework as she struggles to take care of her misbehaving son Mydla and her inattentive husband Josef, eventually resorting to a similarly unsatisfying affair with Jirka. There is no narrative link between the two women's stories apart from the opening scene in which the documentary images of Eva appear on Vera's television set.<ref>Peter Hames, Visiting Professor in Film Studies, Staffordshire University, DVD Booklet Notes p.6, ''Two Films By Vera Chytilova, Second Run DVD''</ref>

After many scenes of Eva enduring grueling and humiliating training sessions, the film includes scenes of her actually performing her routine at a competition, and a final shot of her working as a gymnastics instructor for a younger woman. Meanwhile, Věra's marriage almost collapses as her husband reveals that he is also having an affair, and tells her that they should divorce, although a final scene shows her together with family.

== Analysis ==
''Something Different'' has been described as a cinematic breakthrough alongside [[Black Peter (film)|''Black Peter'']] and [[The Cry (1964 film)|''The Cry'']], discarding the morality tales of earlier socialist cinema and replacing them with frank depictions of everyday life, in addition to breaking with other traditional conventions of film form.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://newwavefilm.com/interviews/hames-on-chytilova.shtml|title=Peter Hames on Vera Chytilová - Interview with Newwavefilm.com (2015)|website=newwavefilm.com|access-date=2019-07-07}}</ref>

In an overview of Chytilová's early career, [[Jiří Ceslar]], professor of film at the [[Faculty of Arts, Charles University in Prague]], describes the film as a meditation on the meaning of life, expressed through Eva and Věra's lines which are on one hand trivial everyday phrases and on the other hand appear to have metaphorical importance as commentary on life's struggles; these statements also carry a theme set in the film's title, as they are always "about something different".<ref name=":6">{{Cite journal|last=Cieslar|first=Jiří|date=29 April 2002|title="Now I don't know how to keep on going" The early films of Věra Chytilová|url=https://www.kinoeye.org/02/08/cieslar08.php#1|journal=Kinoeye|volume=2}}</ref> While the film invites the viewer to draw connections between Vera and Eva's lives through their narrative juxtaposition, scholars have also noted the stark difference in the nature of Eva and Věra's problems: where Eva is constrained by an extremely rigid regime of exercise, Vera is vexed by a lack of direction.<ref name=":1">McEneaney, Kathleen (2013) [http://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/cine/vol2/iss1/2 ''For Country, For Women: Women Directors in the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, and Romania'']''Cinesthesia'': Vol. 2 : Iss. 1.</ref>

''Something Different'' is also contrasted against Chytilová's following two films, 1965's ''[[Pearls of the Deep]]'' and 1966's [[Daisies (film)|''Daisies'']]. In particular, the three films display a progression in Chytilová's use of structure, with ''Pearls'' ''of the Deep'' as a stepping stone between the tightly structured ''Something Different'' and the anarchic ''Daisies''.<ref name=":1" /> Similarly, ''Something Different'' is an example of Chytilová's use of the style of [[cinema verite]] in her early career, contrasted against more allegorical works such as ''Daisies'' and ''[[Fruit of Paradise]]''.<ref name=":7">{{Cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=A03IAgAAQBAJ&q=something+different&pg=PA211|title=The Politics of Gender Culture under State Socialism: An Expropriated Voice|last1=Havelková|first1=Hana|last2=Oates-Indruchová|first2=Libora|date=2014-02-03|publisher=Routledge|isbn=9781317819080|language=en}}</ref>

== Reception ==
A contemporary review in ''[[Le Monde]]'' praised Chytilová's humor and virtuoso technique.<ref name=":4">{{Cite news|url=https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1966/05/28/quelque-chose-d-autre_2695790_1819218.html|title= QUELQUE CHOSE D'AUTRE |last=de Baroncelli|first=Jean|date=1966-05-28|work=[[Le Monde]]|access-date=2019-07-07|language=fr}}</ref> The film won the main prize at the [[Mannheim Film Festival]] in 1963.<ref name=":3">{{Cite web|url=http://www.aufbruch-der-autorinnen.eu/Filme/oneecem.html|title=O něčem jiném|website=www.aufbruch-der-autorinnen.eu|access-date=2019-07-07}}</ref>

The film has also received renewed attention from Anglophone critics in the 21st century. A review by [[The Arts Desk]] of a DVD release of the film in 2016 praised the film's ability to avoid pretentiousness, thanks to the work of the Chytilová, Čurík, Šlitr, and Hájek.<ref name=":0" /> A review in ''[[The New Yorker]]'' called it "radically and thrillingly different from more or less any film that was being made at the time", praising its camerawork and subject matter.<ref name=":5">{{Cite news|url=https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/what-to-stream-this-weekend-vera-chytilovas-something-different-a-rediscovered-classic-from-the-new-criterion-channel|title=What to Stream This Weekend: "Something Different," a Rediscovered Classic from the New Criterion Channel|last=Brody|first=Richard|date=2019-04-12|access-date=2019-07-07|language=en}}</ref> A review in ''[[Hyperallergic]]'' described the film as "subtle and poignant".<ref name=":2">{{Cite web|url=https://hyperallergic.com/405838/a-czech-filmmaker-who-portrayed-eastern-bloc-life-through-womens-eyes/|title=A Czech Filmmaker Who Portrayed Eastern Bloc Life Through Women's Eyes|last=Bittencourt|first=Ela|date=2017-10-16|website=[[Hyperallergic]]|language=en-US|access-date=2019-07-07}}</ref>
