'''''Howard the Duck''''' (released in the United Kingdom as '''''Howard... A New Breed of Hero'''''<ref name="bbfc.co.uk"/>) is a 1986 American [[Superhero film|superhero]] [[Science fiction comedy|comedy film]] directed by [[Willard Huyck]] and starring [[Lea Thompson]], [[Jeffrey Jones]] and [[Tim Robbins]]. Based on the [[Marvel Comics]] character [[Howard the Duck]], the film was produced by [[Gloria Katz]] and written by Huyck and Katz, with [[George Lucas]] as executive producer. The screenplay was originally intended to be an animated film, but the [[film adaptation]] became live-action because of a contractual obligation. While several TV adaptations of Marvel characters were aired, this was the first theatrical release since the ''[[Captain America (serial)|Captain America]]'' serial of 1944.

Lucas proposed adapting the comic book following the production of ''[[American Graffiti]]'' (1973). After multiple production difficulties and mixed response to test screenings, ''Howard the Duck'' was released in theaters on August 1, 1986. Upon its release, the film was a critical and [[box-office bomb|commercial failure]] and was panned for its humor, performances, inconsistent tone, and appearance of the title character, though the cast performances, effects and soundtrack were mostly praised. In the years since, it has been considered among the [[List of 20th century films considered the worst#Howard the Duck (1986)|worst films ever made]], but has also developed a [[Cult film|cult following]].<ref name="PrimerStories">{{cite web | last = Chipman | first = Bob | title = DUCK FAILS HOLLYWOOD BETS IT ALL ON DUCK BONERS | url = https://www.primerand.co/5/howard | work = Primer&Co. | access-date = May 9, 2021 | archive-date = September 27, 2021 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210927081429/https://www.primerand.co/5/howard | url-status = dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://screencrush.com/howard-the-duck-chad-johnson/|title = The Bizarre Reason 'Howard the Duck' is Suddenly Trending| date=October 13, 2020 }}</ref> It was nominated for seven [[Golden Raspberry Awards]] (winning four), and made about US $16.3 million domestically compared to its US $30–37 million budget.<ref name="mojo" /><ref name="budget" /><ref>{{Cite web |url= http://www.filmsite.org/greatestflops11.html |title= Greatest Box-Office Bombs, Disasters and Film Flops: The Most Notable Examples 1985-1986 |author= Tim Dirks |website= FilmSite.org |access-date= October 11, 2016 }}</ref>

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries should be 400-700 words. -->
On Duckworld, an [[Earth]]-like planet inhabited by [[Anthropomorphism|anthropomorphic]] [[duck]]s, [[Howard the Duck]] is propelled out of his apartment building and into outer space; he lands on Earth, in [[Cleveland]], Ohio. Upon arriving, he encounters a woman being attacked by thugs, whom he defeats using [[Howard the Duck#Powers and abilities|a unique style of martial arts]]. The woman, [[Beverly Switzler]], takes Howard to her apartment and lets him spend the night.

The following day, Beverly takes Howard to Phil Blumburtt, a scientist who Beverly hopes can help Howard return to his world. When Phil is revealed to be only a lab assistant, Howard resigns himself to life on Earth and rejects Beverly's aid. He gets a job as a janitor at a romance spa, but eventually quits and returns to Beverly, who plays in a band called Cherry Bomb. At the club where Cherry Bomb is performing, Howard confronts their manager when he insults the band and plans to coerce Beverly. A fight breaks out, which Howard wins.

Howard rejoins Beverly backstage and accompanies her back to her apartment, where Beverly persuades him to be the band's new manager. The two flirt and get into bed, but they are interrupted by Phil and two of his colleagues, who reveal that a laser spectroscope they were inventing was aimed at Howard's planet and transported him to Earth when it was activated. They theorize that Howard can be sent back to his world through a reversal of this process.

Upon their arrival at the laboratory, the laser spectroscope malfunctions, raising the possibility of something else being transported to Earth. At this point, Dr. Walter Jenning is possessed by a life form from a distant region of space. When they visit a diner, the creature introduces himself as a "Dark Overlord of the Universe" and demonstrates his developing mental powers by destroying the table, utensils, and condiments. A fight ensues when a group of truckers in the diner insults Howard. He is captured and is almost killed by the diner chef, but the Dark Overlord destroys the diner and escapes with Beverly.

Howard locates Phil, and they discover an [[Ultralight aircraft (United States)|ultralight aircraft]], which they use to search for the Dark Overlord and Beverly. At the laboratory, the Dark Overlord plans to transfer another of his kind into Beverly's body with the dimension machine. Howard and Phil arrive and seemingly destroy the Dark Overlord with an experimental neutron disintegrator, but the creature is merely forced out of Jenning's body and attacks them in its true form. Howard fires the neutron disintegrator at the beast, obliterating him. He then destroys the laser spectroscope to seal off Earth from any further invasions, sacrificing his only way back to Duckworld in the process. In the aftermath, Howard takes on the roles of Beverly's manager and tour partner, with Phil joining them as part of the crew.

==Production==
[[File:Time 100 George Lucas.jpg|thumb|right|George Lucas stepped down as the president of [[Lucasfilm]] to focus on producing films, including ''Howard the Duck''.]]
[[George Lucas]] attended film school with [[Willard Huyck]] and [[Gloria Katz]], who later co-wrote ''[[American Graffiti]]'' with Lucas.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Mintzer |first1=John DeFore, Leslie Felperin, Jordan |last2=DeFore |first2=John |last3=Felperin |first3=Leslie |last4=Mintzer |first4=Jordan |date=2022-11-08 |title=All 70 Marvel Movies Ranked Worst to Best |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/all-marvel-movies-ranked-worst-866733/ |access-date=2022-11-26 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref> After the film's production concluded, Lucas told Huyck and Katz about the comic book ''Howard the Duck'', primarily written by [[Steve Gerber]], describing the series as being "very funny" and praising its elements of [[film noir]] and [[absurdism]].<ref name="LookBack-1">{{cite video|people=Huyck, Willard; Katz, Gloria |date=2009 |chapter=A Look Back at Howard the Duck|title=Howard the Duck|medium=DVD (extra) |publisher= Universal Home Video |id=[[UPC-A]] 025195052306}}</ref> In 1984, Lucas relinquished his presidency of [[Lucasfilm]] to focus on producing films.<ref>{{cite book |last = Shone |first = Tom |title = Blockbuster: How Hollywood Learned To Stop Worrying And Love The Summer |year = 2004 |publisher = Simon and Schuster |isbn = 0-7432-3568-1 |page = [https://archive.org/details/blockbusterhowho00shon/page/136 136] |url = https://archive.org/details/blockbusterhowho00shon/page/136 }}</ref> Lucas wanted [[John Landis]] to direct the film, but Landis declined.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/star-wars/10828279/George-Lucas-at-70-the-Star-Wars-creator-on-filmmaking.html | title=George Lucas at 70: The Star Wars creator on filmmaking | date=May 14, 2014 }}</ref> According to the documentary ''A Look Back at Howard the Duck'', Huyck, Katz and Lucas began to seriously consider adapting ''Howard the Duck'' as a film, and met with Gerber to discuss the project.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Steve Gerber's account differs slightly; he recalls that at the time he was approached to discuss the film, Lucas was not yet involved with the project.<ref name="DAK38">{{cite news |last= Zimmerman |first= Dwight Jon |date = September 1986 |title = Steve Gerber (part 2) |work = [[Comics Interview]] |issue = 38 |pages = 6–19 |publisher = [[Fictioneer Books]] }}</ref>

The film was optioned by [[Universal Pictures|Universal Studios]] after a partnership with [[Marvel Comics]]. According to Marvin Antonowsky, "[[Sidney Sheinberg|Sidney [Sheinberg]]] lobbied very hard for ''Howard the Duck''", because the studio had passed on previous projects in which Lucas was involved, which had been very successful.<ref>{{cite book |last = Sharp |first = Kathleen |title = Mr. and Mrs. Hollywood: Edie and Lew Wasserman and Their Entertainment Empire |year = 2004 |publisher = Carroll & Graf Publishers |isbn = 0-7867-1419-0 |page = 451 |chapter = Safeguarding the Legacy: 1981–2002}}</ref> Sheinberg denied any involvement in ''Howard the Duck'', claiming that he never read the screenplay.<ref name = "Dick">{{cite book |last = Dick |first = Bernard F. |title = City of Dreams: The Making and Remaking of Universal Pictures |year = 1997 |publisher = University Press of Kentucky |isbn = 0-8131-2016-0 |page = 178 |chapter = In the Embrace of the Octopus}}</ref> Huyck and Katz strongly felt that the film should be animated. Because Universal needed a film for a summer release and thinking that animating the film would take too long or cost too much, Lucas suggested that the film could be produced in live-action, with special effects created by [[Industrial Light & Magic]].<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

Production designer Peter Jamison and director of photography [[Richard H. Kline]] were hired to give the film a look similar to that of a color comic book.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Throughout the shoot, Huyck shot multiple segments establishing Duckworld, designed by Jamison. In the opening shot, the skyline displayed could easily be [[New York City]], but the two moons visible in the sky (at similar angles from one another as the two suns of [[Tatooine]] in the original ''[[Star Wars (film)|Star Wars]]'' film). [[Howard the Duck]]'s apartment is filled with detailed props, including books and magazines featuring duck-oriented puns.<ref name="LookBack-2">{{cite video |people = Thompson, Lea; Jones, Jeffry; Gale, Ed |date = 2009 |chapter = A Look Back at Howard the Duck |title=Howard the Duck |medium = DVD (extra) |publisher = Universal Home Video |id=[[UPC-A]] 025195052306}}</ref> Because Lucas often worked with [[Dwarfism|dwarf]] actors, he was able to hire several extras to work on these sequences.<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

Though Gerber's schedule generally prevented him from being present during the shooting, he chose to miss the deadline on the first issue of ''[[Spectre (DC Comics character)|The Spectre]]'' so he could watch the final day.<ref name="DAK38"/>

===Development===
Huyck and Katz began to develop ideas. Early on in production, it was decided that the personality of the character would be changed from that of the comics, in which Howard was rude and obnoxious, to make the character nicer.<ref name = "Stempel">{{cite book |last = Tom |first = Stempel |title = Framework: A History of Screenwriting in the American Film |year = 2000 |publisher = Syracuse University Press |isbn = 0-8156-0654-0 |page = 207 |chapter = Alumni}}</ref> Gerber read over the script and offered his comments and suggestions. In addition, Huyck and Katz met with Gerber to discuss a horror sequence with which they were having difficulty.<ref name="DAK38"/>

During the screenwriting process, a stronger emphasis was placed on special effects, rather than [[satire]] or narrative.<ref name = "Stempel" /> The tone of the film is in diametric opposition to the comics, which Gerber described as an [[existential]] joke: "'This is no joke!' There it is. The cosmic giggle. The funniest gag in the universe. Life's most serious moments and most incredibly dumb moments are often distinguishable only by a momentary point of view. Anyone who doesn't believe this probably cannot enjoy reading ''Howard the Duck''."<ref>''Mediascene'' #25.</ref> Regarding the film's approach to the source material, Katz declared, "It's a film about a duck from outer space [...] It's not supposed to be an existential experience [...] We're supposed to have fun with this concept, but for some reason, reviewers weren't able to get over that problem."<ref>{{cite web |url = http://comicsbulletin.com/if-it-aint-funk-he-dont-feel-it-howard-duck-1986/ |title = F.O.O.M. (Flashbacks of Ol' Marvel) #13: "If It Ain't Funk He Don't Feel It: Howard the Duck (1986)" |author = Paul Brian McCoy |publisher = [[Comics Bulletin]] |access-date = June 18, 2010 |archive-date = November 9, 2014 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20141109161616/http://comicsbulletin.com/if-it-aint-funk-he-dont-feel-it-howard-duck-1986/ |url-status = dead }}</ref> Despite this, after shooting had wrapped, Gerber stated that he felt the film was faithful to both the spirit of his comics and the characters of Howard and Beverly.<ref name="DAK38"/>

An early proposed storyline involved the character being transported to Hawaii. Huyck states that this storyline was considered because "we thought it would be sort of fun to shoot there". According to Katz, they did not want to explain how Howard arrived on Earth initially, but later rewrote the screenplay so that the film would begin on Howard's homeworld.<ref>{{cite news |last= Zimmerman |first= Dwight Jon |date = September 1986 |title = Gloria Katz |work = [[Comics Interview]] |issue = 38 |pages = 50–55 |publisher = [[Fictioneer Books]] }}</ref> Huyck and Katz wanted to incorporate both lighter, humorous elements and darker, suspenseful elements. Katz states that some readers were confused by the sexual elements of the screenplay, as they were unsure as to whether the film was intended for adults or children. Huyck and Katz wrote the ending, leaving the story open for a sequel, which was never produced.<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

===Adaptation===
The film was originally intended to be animated based on the character created by Steve Gerber and quoting scripts by [[Bill Mantlo]]. In particular, the "Duckworld" story of ''Howard the Duck'' magazine #6 was to serve as a basis for the script. A contractual obligation required Lucas to provide a distributor with a live-action film, so he decided to make the film using live actors and to use special effects for Howard. Katz said that an animated film "would have taken too much time and too much money."<ref name="Decider">{{cite web|url=https://decider.com/2016/03/11/howard-the-duck-the-oral-history/?_ga=1.192178989.608980329.1302539496|title='Howard The Duck': The Oral History|work=[[New York Post#Website|Decider]]|last=Gaines|first=Caseen|date=Mar 11, 2016|access-date=May 11, 2021}}</ref>

The script significantly altered the personality of the title character, played the story straight instead of as a satire, removed the surrealist elements, and added supernatural elements that could highlight special effects work done by Lucas' Industrial Light & Magic. The filmmakers consulted with [[The Walt Disney Company|Disney]]'s lawyers to make sure Howard did not resemble the Disney character [[Donald Duck]] too closely. Howard's appearance was redesigned several times during the process, including his clothes and the shape of his bill.<ref name="Decider" />

The film's screenplay was adapted into comic book format by the writer [[Danny Fingeroth]] and artist [[Kyle Baker]] for Marvel Comics. The adaptation appeared in both ''[[Marvel Comics Super Special|Marvel Super Special]]'' #41<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.comics.org/issue/42070/|title=GCD :: Issue :: Marvel Super Special #41 |work=comics.org|access-date=December 27, 2016}}</ref> and in a three-issue [[Limited series (comics)|limited series]].<ref>[http://www.comics.org/series/3187/ ''Howard the Duck: The Movie''] at the Grand Comics Database</ref>

===Casting===
After auditioning several actresses, singers, and models for the role of [[Beverly Switzler]], including [[Tori Amos]], [[Lea Thompson]] was cast due to her appearance in ''[[Back to the Future]]''.<ref name = "LookBack-1"/><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1800709/marvel-flop-howard-the-duck-could-have-starred-tori-amos/|title=Marvel's Most Notorious Flop Could Have Starred A Beloved Singer-Songwriter|first=Witney|last=Seibold|date=March 10, 2025|website=Slashfilm.com|access-date=June 13, 2026}}</ref> Thompson purchased clothing from thrift stores because she wanted to appear at the audition as "a cross between [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] and [[Cyndi Lauper]]." During the shoot, Thompson complained that the filmmakers chose to shoot Howard's close-up before hers. Thompson also states that she regrets not wearing a wig, as her hairstyle took two hours a day to prepare.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> [[Jeffrey Jones]] was cast because of his performance in ''[[Amadeus (film)|Amadeus]]''. Although [[Tim Robbins]] had no prior film experience, Huyck and Katz were confident that he was right for the part of Phil Blumburtt.<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

Robbins said in a later interview that he doesn't look back negatively at the film as he "got this big job that was paying a really decent salary and it was for George Lucas [...] so it was a huge deal at the time. And then it wound up going over its shooting schedule, and I ended up getting paid twice for that movie because of all the overtime." Robbins admitted that he thinks more about the money he made than the quality of the film.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.mandatory.com/culture/942359-interview-tim-robbins-perfect-day-howard-the-duck |title=Interview {{!}} Tim Robbins on A Perfect Day and Howard the Duck |date=2016-01-12 |website=Mandatory |access-date=2019-08-07 }}</ref>

To play the physical role of Howard, Huyck and Katz held casting calls with dwarf actors, eventually casting child actor [[Jordan Prentice]] and hiring [[Ed Gale]], who had been rejected because he was too tall for the role, to perform stunts and play the role during evening shoots.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> Prentice found the shooting conditions to be too difficult to handle,<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> and the film's editors were unable to match day and evening sequences because of the difference in the two portrayals.<ref name = "LookBack-2"/> Because Gale also served as an understudy, he took over the role.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /><ref name = "LookBack-2" />

Although many actors and comedians wanted to voice Howard such as [[Jay Leno]], they were all turned down;<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.avclub.com/lea-thompson-1798230109 | title=Lea Thompson | website=[[The A.V. Club]] }}</ref> Huyck and Katz auditioned several actors including [[John Cusack]], [[Robin Williams]] and [[Martin Short]] for Howard's voice. Williams worked for a week before quitting, stating, "I can't do this. It is insane. I can't get the rhythm of this. I am being confined. I am being handcuffed to match the flapping duck's bill."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/howard-the-duck-turns-35-lea-thompson-chip-zien-seth-green-reexamine-notorious-marvel-universal-bomb-1234977028/|title='Howard the Duck' Turns 35: Stars Revisit Notorious Bomb Amid New Fervor for Marvel Character|date=July 26, 2021|first=Ryan|last=Parker|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=July 26, 2021}}</ref> As a result, [[Chip Zien]] was cast because the filmmakers felt his nasal voice suited the part.<ref name="Release-1">{{cite video |people = Huyck, Willard; Katz, Gloria |date = 2009 |chapter = Releasing the Duck |title=Howard the Duck |medium = DVD (extra) |publisher = Universal Home Video |id=[[UPC-A]] 025195052306}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/78156/11-far-out-facts-about-howard-duck |title = 11 Far Out Facts About 'Howard the Duck' |website=Mentalfloss.com|date = April 5, 2016}}</ref> Because Howard's voice was not cast until the film had begun editing, synchronization was extremely difficult.<ref name = "Release-1" />

===Filming===
[[File:4.Howard.jpg|thumb|right|200px|[[Lisa Sturz]] during the filming of the film]]
[[Principal photography]] began on November 11, 1985, and [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] on March 27, 1986.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.mouseplanet.com/10961/The_History_of_Howard_the_Duck_Part_One |title = Mouseplanet - The History of Howard the Duck: Part One by Jim Korkis|website=Mouseplanet.com| date=March 25, 2015 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/67719 | title=AFI&#124;Catalog |website=Catalog.afi.com}}</ref> Jeffrey Jones altered his voice for Dr. Walter Jenning after the character's possession by the Dark Overlord, which he demonstrated when interviewed by [[Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex|Dallas-Fort Worth]] reporter [[Bobbie Wygant]].<ref>Archived at [https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211205/Zgxy2HvNFhI Ghostarchive]{{cbignore}} and the [https://web.archive.org/web/20201115164655/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zgxy2HvNFhI&gl=US&hl=en Wayback Machine]{{cbignore}}: {{cite web| url = https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zgxy2HvNFhI&t=372s| title = Jeffery Jones for "Howard The Duck" 1986 - Bobbie Wygant Archive | website=[[YouTube]]| date = June 3, 2020 }}{{cbignore}}</ref>

The ultralight sequence was difficult, requiring intense coordination and actors Robbins and Gale to actually fly the plane.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> The location scout was stumped for a location for the sequence; after she described what she was looking for, a telephone repairman working in her office in [[San Francisco]] suggested [[Petaluma, California]] for the scene. Because of the limited shooting time, a third unit was hired to speed up the process.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> The climax was shot in a naval installation in San Francisco, where conditions were cold throughout.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> The film cost an estimated US$30–36 million to produce.<ref name="budget" /><ref name = "Matthews">{{cite book |last = Matthews |first = Jack |title = The Battle of Brazil |year = 1998 |publisher = Hal Leonard Corporation |isbn = 1-55783-347-8 |page = 158}}</ref>

===Special effects===
Howard was initially intended to be a fully computerized character, but these attempts proved unsuccessful. The idea of fully puppeteering Howard was explored, but the filmmakers determined they would need an actor in a suit to portray him for much of the film.<ref name="Decider" /> Lucasfilm built [[animatronics|animatronic]] suits, costumes and puppets. Because of the limited preparation time, the varied "ducks" created for the film would explode or lose feathers, and multiple ducks were built with the wrong proportions. On the first day of shooting, the crew realized the poor quality when they found that the inside of the puppet's neck was visible when its mouth opened. Huyck repeatedly reshot scenes involving Howard as the animatronics were improved. Because multiple puppeteers were in charge of controlling different parts of the animatronic body, Huyck was unable to coordinate the shoot properly. The opening sequence, in which Howard's chair is propelled out of his apartment, used wires that were later digitally erased by a computer. This was the first use of this technique, which soon became popular and was used in films such as ''[[Back to the Future Part II]]'' (1989), ''[[Terminator 2: Judgment Day]]'' (1991) and ''[[The Matrix]]'' (1999).<ref name = "Wires">{{cite web |last = Jensen |first = K. Thor |title = George Lucas' Howard the Duck movie made The Matrix possible |date = May 17, 2020 |url = https://www.polygon.com/2020/5/17/21252401/howard-the-duck-the-matrix-special-effects |publisher = [[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] |access-date = May 10, 2021}}</ref> The effect of the feathers on Howard's head becoming erect during the love sequence took months to prepare.<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

The voice of Howard, Chip Zien, was not cast until after the shooting was completed. Because Gale's voice was difficult to hear through the suit, Huyck ordered Gale to perform his scenes without speaking any of the required dialogue, which was later synchronized during the editing process.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /><ref name = "LookBack-2" /> Lead puppeteer [[Tim Rose (actor)|Tim Rose]] was given a microphone attached to a small speaker, which would allow Rose to speak the dialogue to help the actors respond to Howard's dialogue.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> While wearing his suit, Gale could only see through Howard's mouth and had to sense his location without proper eyesight. Gale often had to walk backward before beginning rehearsals.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> In between takes, a hair dryer was stuffed in Howard's bill to keep Gale cool.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Gale taped two of his fingers together to wear the three-fingered hands created for the Howard costume.<ref name="Release-2">{{cite video |people = Thompson, Lea; Jones, Jeffry; Gale, Ed |date = 2009 |chapter = Releasing the Duck |title=Howard the Duck |medium = DVD (extra) |publisher = Universal Home Video |id=[[UPC-A]] 025195052306}}</ref> A total of six actors gave physical performances as Howard.<ref name = "Razzies">{{cite web |last = Wilson |first = John |title = 1986 Archive |url = http://www.razzies.com/asp/content/XcNewsPlus.asp?cmd=view&articleid=25 |publisher = [[Golden Raspberry Award]] |access-date = October 11, 2009 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130602153152/http://www.razzies.com/asp/content/XcNewsPlus.asp?cmd=view&articleid=25 |archive-date = June 2, 2013 |url-status = dead }}</ref>

Gerber was impressed by Howard's appearance, and commented, "It was very bizarre to meet it and [...] realize not just that I created it{{snd}}that would have been bizarre enough [...] you know, it was sort of like meeting a child I didn't know I had".<ref name="DAK38"/>

Makeup artists [[Tom Burman (make-up artist)|Tom Burman]] and [[Bari Dreiband-Burman]] and actor Jones discussed the appearance of the Dark Overlord with Huyck and Katz and developed the character's progressing looks. When Katz's daughter visited during filming, she was terrified by Jones's appearance in makeup. The diner sequence combines practical effects, including [[bullet hit squib|squibs]] and air cannons, with visual effects created by ILM.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Jones provided the altered voice of Jenning during his possession by the Dark Overlord "with a slight bit of enhancement now and then"<ref>Archived at [https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211205/Zgxy2HvNFhI Ghostarchive]{{cbignore}} and the [https://web.archive.org/web/20201115164655/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zgxy2HvNFhI&gl=US&hl=en Wayback Machine]{{cbignore}}: {{cite web| url = https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zgxy2HvNFhI&t=379s| title = Jeffery Jones for "Howard The Duck" 1986 - Bobbie Wygant Archive | website=[[YouTube]]| date = June 3, 2020 }}{{cbignore}}</ref> by sound designer [[Ben Burtt]]. Stop motion effects during the climax were designed by [[Phil Tippett]], who began with a clay model before upgrading to more sophisticated pieces.<ref name = "LookBack-1" />

The film's score was written by [[John Barry (composer)|John Barry]], although some of it was replaced by material composed by [[Sylvester Levay]] (most notably the music for the scene where Howard and Phil fly the ultralight—Barry's original cue is heard on the soundtrack album). [[Thomas Dolby]] wrote the film's songs and chose the members of Cherry Bomb.<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Actress Lea Thompson performed her own singing for the role, although she states that the filmmakers were unsure as to whether they would keep her vocals in the final film. Thompson was required to learn choreography with the band and record the songs so they could be synchronized during filming.<ref name = "LookBack-2" /> The final sequence, in which Cherry Bomb performs the film's title song, was shot in front of a live audience at [[The Warfield]] in San Francisco. The song was co-written by Dolby, [[Allee Willis]], and [[George Clinton (funk musician)|George Clinton]].<ref name = "LookBack-1" /> Gale was choreographed to dance and play guitar as Howard. Dolby built a special guitar for Gale to use for rehearsal and filming.<ref name = "LookBack-2" />

In 2019, [[Intrada Records]] released a 3-disc expansion including the first [[compact disc]] release of the original soundtrack album, as well as John Barry's complete score for the film.

==Reception==
Huyck said that test screenings "went all right" and "people laughed", but Katz claimed she "tore up" negative response cards so that they could say, "Hey, we got a ninety-five percent on the screening!"<ref name="Decider" />

===Critical response===
''Howard the Duck'' received mainly negative reviews from critics. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of {{RT data|score}}, based on {{RT data|count}} reviews, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}, making it the lowest-rated Lucasfilm production. The site's consensus states, "Tonally muddled and joyless, ''Howard the Duck'' squanders its visual effects and offbeat premise on an aimless script, juvenile humor, and a thoroughly unappealing protagonist."<ref name="Tomatoes">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/howard_the_duck|title=''Howard the Duck'' (1986)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] the film has a score of 28 out of 100, based on reviews from 21 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |title=Howard the Duck |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/howard-the-duck/critic-reviews |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=2020-05-04 }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film a grade "B−" on a scale of A to F.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url= https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title= HOWARD THE DUCK (1986) B− |publisher= [[CinemaScore]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 }}</ref>

[[Gene Siskel]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' gave one star out of four, called it a "stupid film" and said, "the story has no center; the duck is not likable, and the [...] special effects [...] are less impressive than a sparkler on a birthday cake."<ref name="GeneSiskel">{{cite news |last= Siskel|first= Gene |date = August 8, 1986 |title = FLICK OF WEEK: 'VAGABOND' ONE OF FINEST FILMS IN YEARS |work = [[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-1986-08-08-8602270452-story.html |access-date=May 7, 2021}}</ref> On their [[At the Movies (1986 TV program)|television show]], both Siskel and his co-host [[Roger Ebert]] complained that the movie was not enough of a comedy and that Howard should have been given either the [[Groucho Marx]]–like personality from his comic books or a fun Donald Duck–like persona. Ebert also criticized the soundtrack for being downbeat and bittersweet.<ref name="SiskelEbert">{{cite AV media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqvWDj2YggY&t=493s |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211219/jqvWDj2YggY |archive-date=2021-12-19|title=Siskel And Ebert-Worst Movies of 1986|date=June 11, 2020|access-date=May 11, 2021|via=YouTube|url-status=live}}{{cbignore}}</ref> ''[[Orange Coast (magazine)|Orange Coast]]'' writer Marc Weinberg and [[Leonard Maltin]] criticized the decision to shoot the film in live action.<ref>{{cite journal |last = Weinberg |first = Marc |date = September 1986 |title = Out-Foxed |journal = [[Orange Coast Magazine]] |volume = 12 |issue = 9 |pages = 143–144 }}</ref><ref name = "Maltin">{{cite book |last = Maltin |first = Leonard |title = Leonard Maltin's 2009 Movie Guide |year = 2008 |publisher = Penguin Group |isbn = 978-0-452-28978-9 |page = [https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780452289789/page/641 641] |chapter = H |url = https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780452289789/page/641 }}</ref> Maltin described the film as "hopeless [...] a gargantuan production which produces a gargantuan headache".<ref name = "Maltin" /> [[People (magazine)|''People'']] magazine seemed to agree: "Lucasfilm promised us ''[[The Man Who Fell to Earth|The Mallard Who Fell to Earth]]''; the result turned out to be more like ''[[Xanadu (film)|Xanaduck]]'' [...] Who'd have imagined that Howard T. Duck, the same web-footed wiseacre who conquered the incredible Space Turnip and the horrible [[Hellcow]], might be done in by something even more ridiculous: Hollywood?"

John Nubbin reviewed ''Howard the Duck'' for ''[[Different Worlds]]'' magazine and stated that "Nor should one try and drag high consequence into a film obviously designed to be nothing more than entertaining. ''Howard The Duck'' is not ''[[Rambo (franchise)|Rambo]]'' in feathers. There is no ideology being pressed to the fore here. The movie was meant to make a few wry observations about human behavior and to give everyone a few laughs and a good time. It did this in spades. Anyone who missed the point, who could look past the good-natured fun being had and see plots to twist our children or turn them into zoophilous little monsters had better slow down and take a vacation. ''Howard the Duck'' was only a comedy - a fast-moving, rollicking barnstorm of a comedy; nothing more, and nothing less. See it when you can."<ref name="dw44">{{cite journal | last = Nubbin |first = John | title = Film Reviews | journal = [[Different Worlds]] | issue = 44 | pages =45, 34 |date=November–December 1986}}</ref>

The appearance of Howard was criticized as being unconvincing due to his poorly functioning mouth and expressionless face. Reviewers also criticized the acting and humor and found the film boring.<ref name = "Stanley" /><ref name = "Hunter" /> In ''[[The Psychotronic Video Guide]]'', Michael Weldon described the reactions to Howard as being inconsistent, and, "It was obviously made in [[Los Angeles|LA]] and suffered from long, boring chase scenes", but praised the stop-motion special effects in the film's final sequences.<ref>{{cite book |last = Weldon |first = Michael |title = The Psychotronic Video Guide |year = 1996 |publisher = 0312131496 |isbn = 0-312-13149-6 |page = 277 |chapter = H}}</ref> ''[[Common Sense Media]]'' criticized the film for the pointless plot lines and the excessive use of sexual innuendo. The group set the appropriate age for the movie at 13+.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/howard-the-duck|title=Howard the Duck|publisher=[[Common Sense Media]]}}</ref>

===Box office===
The film grossed US $16,295,774 in the United States and $21,667,000 Internationally.<ref name="mojo">{{cite web |title= Howard the Duck (1986) |website= [[Box Office Mojo]] |date=1988-07-05 |url= https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt0091225/?ref_=bo_se_r_1 |access-date= 2025-01-24 }}</ref> When the film was screened for Universal, Katz said that the studio's executives left without commenting on the film.<ref name="Release-1"/> Screenings for test audiences were met with mixed responses.<ref name="Release-1"/> Rumors circulated that Universal production heads [[Frank Price]] and Sidney Sheinberg engaged in a fistfight after arguing over who was to blame for green-lighting the film. Both executives denied the rumors.<ref name="Dick"/><ref name="Matthews"/> News reports speculated that one or both would be fired by [[MCA Inc.|MCA]] chairman [[Lew Wasserman]].<ref name="Matthews"/> Price soon left the studio and was succeeded by [[Tom Pollock]]. In an article titled "''Duck'' Cooks Price's Goose", the September 17, 1986 issue of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' attributed his departure to the failure of the movie, although Price had not approved the film's production.<ref name="Dick"/> The film's [[Advertising campaign|marketing campaign]] was also seen as one of the reasons for its box office failure.<ref>{{cite web | title=The Weird Marketing of the Howard the Duck Movie | date=August 2019 | url=https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/the-weird-marketing-of-the-howard-the-duck-movie/ }}</ref>

In July 1986, Huyck and Katz stated that the film's ending left it open for a [[sequel]], which they seemed interested in making.<ref name="Howard the Duck">{{cite video|people=Huyck, Willard; Katz, Gloria|date=2009|chapter=A Look Back at Howard the Duck|title=Howard the Duck|medium=DVD (extra)|website= Universal Home Video |id=[[UPC-A]] 025195052306}}</ref> However, after the film bombed, talks of a sequel ceased.<ref name="Howard the Duck"/> Following the box office failure, Huyck and Katz left for Hawaii and refused to read any reviews.<ref name="Release-1" />

==Home media==
''Howard the Duck'' was first released on [[VHS]] and [[LaserDisc]] in January 1987. It was released on a Special Edition [[DVD]] by [[Universal Pictures Home Entertainment|Universal Studios]] on March 10, 2009.<ref>{{Cite web |title= Howard the Duck [DVD] |website=[[Amazon.com]] |date=March 10, 2009 |url=https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/B001MWUWU8/ |access-date= December 5, 2016 }}</ref> The film was released on [[Blu-ray]] on March 8, 2016.<ref>{{Cite web |url= http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Howard-the-Duck-Blu-ray/123623/ |title= Howard the Duck Blu-ray |website= Blu-ray.com |access-date=December 5, 2016 }}</ref> It was later released on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K Blu-ray]] to commemorate the film's 35th Anniversary in 2021.

A [[novelization]] of ''Howard the Duck'' was written by former ''[[National Lampoon (magazine)|National Lampoon]]'' editor [[Ellis Weiner]].<ref>{{Cite web |title= Weird Things We Learned From The Howard The Duck Novel |website= [[Den of Geek]] |url= https://www.denofgeek.com/comics/weird-things-we-learned-from-the-howard-the-duck-novel/ |access-date=2016-11-03 }}</ref>

==Legacy==
The reaction to the film had a negative effect on the cast, who found themselves unable to work on other projects because of the film.<ref name = "Release-2" /> The bad press right at the opening weekend had Lea Thompson accepting a role in ''[[Some Kind of Wonderful (film)|Some Kind of Wonderful]]'', which she had refused previously, because, as she said, "I had to get on another movie, I wouldn't have done the movie if ''Howard'' wasn't such a bomb."<ref name="thr"/>

According to Ed Gale, he was hired to work on ''[[Spaceballs]]'' because [[Mel Brooks]] had said, "Anybody who's in ''Howard the Duck'' can be in my movie." Gale also said he receives more [[fan mail]] for his Howard the Duck portrayal than for his [[Chucky (Child's Play)|Chucky]] performances, the [[antagonist]] in the ''[[Child's Play (franchise)|Child's Play]]'' horror film series.<ref name = "Release-2" /> After the film's release, Huyck and Katz chose to work on more dramatic projects to separate themselves from ''Howard the Duck''.<ref name = "Release-2" /> Katz said Lucas continued to support the film after its failure because he felt it would later be seen in a better light than it had been at the time of its release.<ref name = "Release-2" /> Huyck said he later encountered fans and supporters of the film who felt that it had been unfairly treated by critics.<ref name = "Release-2" /> Lea Thompson has stated that she had fun making the film and is happy to find fans "celebrating ''Howard the Duck'' in all its great silliness and blemishes."<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.cbr.com/lea-thompson-revels-in-her-incredible-howard-the-duck-comic-book-reunion/ | title=Lea Thompson Revels in Her "Incredible" "Howard the Duck" Comic Book Reunion | date=June 11, 2016 }}</ref> Jeffrey Jones also said he is happy with his role in the film.<ref name = "Release-2"/> In retrospect, Huyck and Katz suggested that the film "could have had a more ''[[Ted (film)|Ted]]''-like tone" and "been edgier and dirtier," but that at the time "Universal wanted a family-friendly movie."<ref name="Decider"/>

In 1997, Steve Gerber expressed his overall dissatisfaction with the film:

<blockquote>What can I say? It sucks. In retrospect, though, after eleven years that have brought us so many worse films, it's not quite as sucky as the reviews might have led you to believe. Still, there are big problems with it, chief among them the duck costume and the duck's bland voice. I liked the performances by Jeffrey Jones, Tim Robbins, and Lea Thompson, though. Lea wasn't playing "my" Beverly, but she did reasonably well with the role as it was written.<ref name="SteveGerberReview">{{cite web |date= December 1997 |last= Mithra |first= Kuljit |title= Interview With Steve Gerber (December 1997)|work= ManWithoutFear.com |url= http://www.manwithoutfear.com/daredevil-interviews/Gerber |access-date =May 8, 2021 }}</ref></blockquote>

In June 2012, the [[YouTube]] series ''[[Marvel Superheroes: What the--?!]]'' featured an episode starring Howard the Duck complaining to Marvel that his movie was not given a special [[Blu-ray Disc|Blu-ray]] re-release to celebrate its 25th anniversary. He eventually gets [[Joe Quesada]] to try to appeal to and bribe George Lucas into supporting the re-release.<ref name = "Marvel Super Heroes: What The--?! - Howard the Duck's Silver Anniversary!">{{cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=71h3Mo3Lbro  |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211219/71h3Mo3Lbro |archive-date=2021-12-19 |url-status=live|title=Marvel Super Heroes: What The--?! - Howard the Duck's Silver Anniversary! |author=Marvel Entertainment |date=June 1, 2012 |publisher= |access-date=December 27, 2016 |via=[[YouTube]] }}{{cbignore}}</ref> In 2014, the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' listed the film as one of the costliest box-office flops of all time.<ref>{{cite web |date= January 15, 2014 |last= Eller |first= Claudia |title= The costliest box office flops of all time |work= [[Los Angeles Times]] |url= https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/envelope/cotown/la-et-box-office-flops-pictures,0,7165703.photogallery#axzz2tJcnZf9r |access-date =March 11, 2018 }}</ref> Filmmaker [[James Gunn]], a fan of the character, stated he was disappointed of the film.<ref>{{cite web | title=Here's How Much 'Guardians of the Galaxy' Director James Gunn Hates 'Howard the Duck' the Movie | date=March 21, 2017 | url=https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/heres-how-much-guardians-of-the-galaxy-director-james-gunn-hates-howard-the-duck-the-movie-184952922.html }}</ref>

Writer [[Chip Zdarsky]], who took on Howard's comics in the 2010s, revealed he was a fan of the movie growing up and had the 2016 run of the title featuring metafictional references to the film.<ref name="thr">{{cite web |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/howard-duck-movie-star-returns-900386/|author=Kit, Borys|title='Howard the Duck' Movie Star Returns for New Comic Version (Exclusive)|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=June 7, 2016 |access-date=December 27, 2016}}</ref> The plot had Lea Thompson hiring Howard and discovering the villain [[Mojo (comics)|Mojo]] had hypnotized her into playing Beverly opposite an alien in a Howard costume.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cbr.com/howard-the-duck-9-shines-a-spotlight-on-lea-thompson-stays-true-to-gerbers-vision/ |title="Howard the Duck" #9 Shines a Spotlight on Lea Thompson, Stays True to Gerber's Vision - Comic Book Review - CBR |work=cbr.com |date=August 2016 |access-date=December 27, 2016}}</ref>

In a June 2018 interview, Lea Thompson said that she was going to pitch [[Marvel Studios]] a new ''Howard the Duck'' movie following [[Howard the Duck (Marvel Cinematic Universe)|the character]]'s [[cameo appearance]]s in ''[[Guardians of the Galaxy (film)|Guardians of the Galaxy]]'' and [[Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2|its 2017 sequel]] voiced by [[Seth Green]], expressing hope at directing it herself.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bigworldtale.com/lifestyle/lea-thompson-looks-back-future-talks-directing-debut-saying-rebooting-past-project-exclusive/|title=Lea Thompson Looks Back & To The Future, Talks Directing Debut, Saying 'Me Too' & Rebooting A Past Project [Exclusive]|website=Big World Tale|last=Tall|first=Kevin|date=June 12, 2018|access-date=May 13, 2021}}</ref> In 2021, following the release of a trailer for the [[Disney+]] animated series ''[[What If...? (TV series)|What If...?]]'', which prominently featured Howard, Thompson once again indicated her interest in directing the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://screenrant.com/howard-duck-reboot-lea-thompson-director-response/|title=Howard The Duck Star Proposes Directing A Reboot For Marvel|website=[[Screen Rant]]|last=Phillips|first=TC|date=July 9, 2021|access-date=July 9, 2021}}</ref> She later revealed that she had pitched the film, following Howard stranded on Earth after the events of ''[[Avengers: Endgame]]'', with Joe Quinones supplying artwork. While she received a positive response, Marvel Studios ultimately passed on it. However, she revealed that they indeed have plans for the character.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://screenrant.com/howard-duck-movie-lea-thompson-reboot-marvel-pitch/|title=Original Howard The Duck Movie Star Lea Thompson Pitched Marvel A Reboot|website=[[Screen Rant]]|last=Deloss|first=John|date=July 26, 2021|access-date=July 26, 2021}}</ref>
