{{nihongo|'''''Linda Linda Linda'''''|リンダ リンダ リンダ|lead=yes}} is a 2005 Japanese [[comedy drama]] film<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/linda-linda-linda-am39787|title=''Linda Linda Linda'' (2005) – Nobuhiro Yamashita |website=[[AllMovie]] |access-date=April 1, 2024}}</ref> directed by [[Nobuhiro Yamashita]]. It stars [[Bae Doona]], [[Aki Maeda]], [[Yu Kashii]], and [[Shiori Sekine]] as teenage girls who form a band to cover songs by the Japanese punk rock band [[the Blue Hearts]]. It was rereleased in [[4K resolution|4K]] restoration in 2025.

==Plot==
With three days to go before the 2004 Shiba High Holly Festival closes out their senior year,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.reelingreviews.com/reviews/linda-linda-linda/|title=''Linda Linda Linda''|last=Clifford|first=Laura|date=January 10, 2020|website=Reeling Reviews|access-date=July 24, 2021}}</ref> a band of schoolgirls has their guitarist sidelined due to a hand injury, followed by the singer that quit after an argument with keyboardist Kei about replacing the guitarist. The remaining members—keyboardist Kei, drummer Kyoko, and bassist Nozomi—decide to still perform in the rock concert of the festival and cover [[The Blue Hearts|Blue Hearts]] songs, including "Linda Linda", with Kei switching to guitar, but they need a new singer. They ask the first girl who walks by, Son, a Korean exchange student who is not fluent in Japanese. The first day ends with all the girls working their hardest to learn their parts. Son practices at a karaoke parlor, and Kyoko talks to her crush, Kazuya.

The next day, the girls begin practicing early at school. When they regroup after school, Kyoko arrives late and they miss their time slot. However, Kei calls her ex-boyfriend Maezano and arranges some practice time in his studio. They leave late at night to return to school and continue practicing through the rest of the night.

By the next morning, Paranmaum is well-rehearsed. As school begins, the girls help at their respective class booths during the festival. Kei practices her guitar parts and talks to her rocker friend Takako. Son is supposed to help with the Japan–Korea culture exchange but daydreams about the band. Kyoko sells crepes and Nozomi falls asleep on her bass guitar in a classroom.

Kei and Kyoko wake up Nozomi and fetch Son. Son rebuffs an awkward confession of love from a schoolmate, Makihara. Over dinner at Nozomi's house, the girls reminisce about memories and persuade Kyoko to talk to her crush Kazuya before the performance the next day. They end the night back at school, practicing until early morning.

On the day of the performance, Paranmaum returns to the studio to practice, but exhausted, they fall asleep. Kei dreams about being celebrated and performing for the [[The Ramones|Ramones]] and [[Pierre Taki]] at the [[Nippon Budokan]]. At school, the performance stage volunteers search for the band but cannot find them; to pass the time, their friends Moe (the original guitarist of the band) and Takako improvise by performing "[[The Water Is Wide (song)|The Water Is Wide]]", "Subarashii Hibi (Wonderful Days)" by [[Unicorn (Japanese band)|Unicorn]], and "Furaibo (Wanderer)" by [[Happy End (band)|Happy End]]. Kei is woken by Kyoko's cell phone when Kazuya calls to ask where she is. The band rushes back to school in a taxi through the pouring rain. Kyoko meets with Kazuya while the band sets up minutes before their performance. When Kyoko finally comes in, Son announces that they are "Paranmaum" and the band performs two of the three songs they had planned, "Linda Linda" and "Owaranai Uta", to an excited crowd.

==Soundtrack==
The soundtrack was released on July 20, 2005. The original instrumental tracks were composed by [[James Iha]] of the [[The Smashing Pumpkins|Smashing Pumpkins]]. The vocal tracks include Paranmaum (the band from the film) singing covers of the [[The Blue Hearts|Blue Hearts]] songs "[[Linda Linda]]", "Boku no Migite (My Right Hand)" and "Owaranai Uta (Never Ending Song)". Kashii and Maeda learned to play their instruments specially for this movie. There are also two songs by [[Base Ball Bear]] (the band Shiori Sekine belongs to in reality); they are "Sayonara Nostalgia" and "April Mirage". These two songs can be heard playing in the "[[crêpe]] shop" scenes featuring Kyoko and Oe Kazuya. Shione Yukawa (who plays Moe Imamura in the movie) and Yuko Yamazaki (from the band me-ism) also perform a few songs on stage at the festival itself. The band, Paranmaum (Korean for "the Blue Hearts"), released a CD single in Japan and South Korea, ''We Are Paranmaum''.{{Citation needed|date=September 2026}}

== Release ==

==Reception==
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|83|7|12|ref=yes|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned ''Linda Linda Linda'' a score of 74 out of 100, based on six critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Linda Linda Linda'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/linda-linda-linda |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=6 September 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> It was [[David Ehrlich]]'s choice for ''[[IndieWire]]'''s 2018 list of the best Japanese films of the 21st century. He wrote that it was less profound than films such as ''[[Spirited Away]]'' (2001), ''[[Millennium Actress]]'' (2001), and ''[[Nobody Knows (2004 film)|Nobody Knows]]'' (2004), but "euphorically fun [...] so rich, so charismatic, and so damn catchy, you'll be itching to show it to all your friends".<ref name="IndieWire2018">{{cite web|last=Ehrlich|first=David|date=2018-03-26|title=The Best Japanese Films of the 21st Century — IndieWire Critics Survey|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2018/03/best-japanese-movies-21st-century-1201943800/|archive-url=|archive-date=|access-date=2021-01-10|website=[[IndieWire]]|author-link=David Ehrlich}}</ref> Reviewing the rerelease in 2025, the ''[[New York Times]]'' critic [[J. Hoberman]] praised ''Linda Linda Linda'' as "sweet but uncloying" and wrote that its "unobtrusive filmmaking is worthy of attention".<ref name="Hoberman 2025">{{cite news |last1=Hoberman |first1=J. |title=‘Linda Linda Linda’: These Girls Just Want to Have Fun |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/03/movies/linda-linda-linda-ifc.html |access-date=18 September 2026 |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=3 September 2025|author-link=J. Hoberman}}</ref>
