'''''The Friends of Eddie Coyle''''' is a 1973 American [[neo-noir]]<ref>[[Alain Silver|Silver, Alain]]; Ward, Elizabeth; eds. (1992). ''Film Noir: An Encyclopedic Reference to the American Style'' (3rd ed.). Woodstock, New York: [[The Overlook Press]]. {{ISBN|0-87951-479-5}}</ref> [[Crime film#Hybrid genres|crime drama film]] directed by [[Peter Yates]], adapted by [[Paul Monash]] (who also produced) from the [[The Friends of Eddie Coyle (novel)|1970 novel]] by [[George V. Higgins]]. It stars [[Robert Mitchum]], [[Peter Boyle]], and [[Richard Jordan]]. The film tells the story of Eddie Coyle (Mitchum), a small-time career hoodlum in the [[Irish mob]] in [[Boston]], [[Massachusetts]]. The title is purely ironic: Eddie has no friends. The plot is inspired by the real-life [[Winter Hill Gang]].<ref name=":0" />

While critical reception was positive, with particular praise for Mitchum's performance, the movie was not popular with filmgoers and failed to rank in the top 30 either in 1973 (when it was released mid-year) or 1974 and failed to recoup its budget in combined box office.

==Plot==
At a diner in [[Quincy, Massachusetts]], low-level [[Arms trafficking|gunrunner]] Eddie Coyle meets with Jackie Brown, a younger gunrunner, in order to obtain guns for a bank robbery crew led by Jimmy Scalise and Artie Van. The crew successfully robs the South Shore National Bank by first kidnapping its manager, Mr. Partridge, and they make a clean getaway. Coyle meets with [[Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives|ATF]] [[Special agent|agent]] Dave Foley to discuss his sentencing for a botched trafficking mission in [[New Hampshire]] set up by Dillon, who owns a local bar that Coyle frequents; Coyle is unaware, however, that Dillon also works as an informant for Foley, earning $20 from him each week. Coyle tells Foley that he could give up Jackie in order to ease the sentencing; Foley sits on this idea.

Jackie meets a hippie couple in [[Cambridge, Massachusetts|Cambridge]] who wish to purchase [[M16 rifle]]s from him in order to rob a bank. He reluctantly agrees to sell them in a discreet location at a specific time. Jackie then meets with Coyle who requires Jackie to acquire guns for the next day. While unsure at first if he can complete the task that quickly, Jackie complies. He heads to [[Rhode Island]] later that night with an associate to get the M16s for the hippies, which he is successful in doing. Meanwhile, the crew robs a second bank in [[Weymouth, Massachusetts|South Weymouth]], but one of the tellers triggers a silent alarm and is shot dead by one of the robbers, requiring a hasty exit. The crew are able to flee without any police following them, but become wanted for murder as a result.

Jackie meets Coyle in the parking lot of a [[Dedham, Massachusetts|Dedham]] grocery store to deliver him the guns. Once the exchange is finished, Coyle calls Foley from a payphone to tip him off about Jackie's exchange with the hippies at the [[Sharon station|Sharon train station]]. There, Foley and a group of agents watch the area from afar with [[sniper rifle]]s. The couple arrives, though Jackie tells them to meet him elsewhere at a later time, believing that he is being watched. Once the couple leaves, Foley and his team move in to make the arrest. Jackie recognizes the agents' cars and attempts to flee, but is boxed in at the exit and arrested, immediately realizing that Coyle had set him up.

Foley later tells Coyle that Jackie's arrest was not enough to clear Coyle's sentence. In preparation for the third robbery, the crew moves in to kidnap the bank's manager, but are ambushed by Foley and other ATF agents and placed under arrest. The next day, Coyle decides to tip Foley off about Scalise and his crew, but is unaware of their arrest. Foley shows him the arrest in the newspaper and departs, leaving Coyle anguished. Soon after, Dillon is told that a mob boss wants him to assassinate Coyle, thinking that Coyle ratted out the robbery crew. Dillon invites Coyle out to a [[Boston Bruins]] game at the [[Boston Garden]] along with a hood whom Dillon claims is his "wife's nephew". At the game, Coyle becomes severely drunk and eventually passes out during the car ride afterward. The hood drives them to a discreet location, where Dillon shoots Coyle in the head. They swap out cars in the parking lot of a bowling alley and leave.

Dillon and Foley meet outside [[Boston City Hall]] the next day, where Foley thanks Dillon for giving him Scalise and his crew. Foley is largely unconcerned that Dillon cannot tell him who murdered Coyle, leaving the impression that he knows Dillon is involved, but likely would not have pursued the killing of Coyle. After they finish conversing, they walk away in separate directions.

==Production==
During the making of the film, Robert Mitchum was interested in meeting the local gangsters as part of his research. Journalist [[George Edward Kimball|George Kimball]], a sports writer on the ''[[Boston Herald]]'' at the time, claimed that Mitchum wanted to meet [[Whitey Bulger]] and was warned against it by Higgins. What is claimed instead is that cast member [[Alex Rocco]], who grew up in Somerville and was a hanger-on of the [[Winter Hill Gang]] during the early 1960s, introduced Mitchum to [[Howie Winter]].<ref name=":0">{{cite news |last=Kimball |first=George |title=Looking Back At An Unlikely Acquaintance With Whitey Bulger |url=http://hereandnow.wbur.org/2011/06/28/bulger-meeting-whitey |publisher=[[WBUR-FM]] |access-date=2 September 2011}}</ref>

[[Howie Carr]] suggested that the character 'Dillon' ([[Peter Boyle]]) is a thinly-veiled reference to Whitey Bulger&nbsp;— both characters operate out of a bar<ref>{{Cite web |last=Quinn |first=Garrett |date=2014-05-15 |title=Infamous Whitey Bulger bar recreated in Cambridge's Inman Square for Johnny Depp movie |url=https://www.masslive.com/news/boston/2014/05/infamous_whitey_bulger_bar_cambridge_inman_square.html |access-date=2025-09-24 |website=masslive |language=en}}</ref> and work as freelance [[Contract killing|contract killers]], and both are police informants; though Bulger's status as an informant was not publicly-known at the time.<ref name=":2">{{cite web |last=Carr |first=Howie |title=George V. Higgins' Eddie Coyle: Even Better than True |url=http://www.criminalelement.com/blogs/2011/05/eddie-coyle-even-better-than-true |accessdate=April 13, 2012 |publisher=CriminalElement.com |archive-date=5 May 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120505101709/http://www.criminalelement.com/blogs/2011/05/eddie-coyle-even-better-than-true |url-status=dead }}</ref> The death of Eddie Coyle mirrors the killing of William "Billy" O'Brien, a Bulger associate and ex-convict who was killed over fears of turning turncoat.<ref name=":2" /> Author George V. Higgins denied Dillon was directly based on Bulger, though admitted he "wrote about Dillon before Whitey became Whitey, if you know what I mean."<ref name=":2" />

=== Filming ===
Filming took place throughout the [[Boston]] area, including [[Government Center, Boston|Government Center]] in Boston, and [[Dedham, Massachusetts|Dedham]], [[Cambridge, Massachusetts|Cambridge]], [[Milton, Massachusetts|Milton]], [[Quincy, Massachusetts|Quincy]], [[Sharon, Massachusetts|Sharon]], [[Somerville, Massachusetts|Somerville]], [[Malden, Massachusetts|Malden]], and [[Weymouth, Massachusetts|Weymouth]], [[Massachusetts]].<ref name="IMDB">{{cite web |title=Filming Locations for The Friends of Eddie Coyle |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0070077/locations |access-date=2007-01-30 |publisher=Internet Movie Database}}</ref> A sequence at [[Boston Garden]] was filmed during an [[National Hockey League|NHL]] ice hockey game between the [[Boston Bruins]] and the [[Chicago Blackhawks]].<ref name=":1" />

On October 17, 1972, [[History_of_Dedham,_Massachusetts,_2000–present#The_Friends_of_Eddie_Coyle_and_the_Citizen's_Bank_robbery|scenes were shot]] at the Dedham Plaza, showing W.T. Grant's, Woolworth's, Barbo's Furniture, Liggett's Drugstore, Capitol Supermarket, Friendly's, and Plaza Liquors.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}} A few weeks later, on December 1, the crew shot the film's opening scene in Dedham Square.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}} South Shore Bank{{efn|As of 2023, it is a Citizen's Bank.}}, located in Dedham Square, was the bank robbed in the film.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}} Local businesses including Geishecker's, P.J.'s Pastry Shop, McLellan's, and Gilbert's Package Store can be seen as the movie's bank manager drives through the Square.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}} Mitchum signed autographs for fans in between takes.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}}

== Reception ==
''The Friends of Eddie Coyle'' was not well received by the filmgoing public. It failed to place in the top 30 in film revenue in 1973<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.the-numbers.com/market/1974/top-grossing-movies|title= North America (US and Canada) Domestic Movie Chart for 1974|work=thenumbers.com|access-date=January 29, 2024}}</ref> (when it was released mid-year)<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Friends-of-Eddie-Coyle-The#tab=summary|title=The Friends of Eddie Coyle (1973)|work=thenumbers.com|access-date=January 29, 2024}}</ref> or 1974,<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.the-numbers.com/market/1974/top-grossing-movies|title=North America (US and Canada) Domestic Movie Chart for 1974|work=thenumbers.com|access-date=January 29, 2024}}</ref> and failed to recoup its estimated $3 million budget in combined box office returns.  It was, however, well-reviewed by critics, and today is among the most highly regarded crime films of the 1970s by some.

Critic of the time, [[Pauline Kael]] explains why the reception might have been critical when she wrote for ''[[The New Yorker]]'' that the film was "shallow and tedious" and that the "police and gangsters had no roots."<ref>{{Cite book |last=Kael |first=Pauline |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_Age_of_Movies_Selected_Writings_of_P/tkShTL84MrcC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=the+age+of+movies+selected+writings+of+pauline+kael&printsec=frontcover |title=The Age of Movies: Selected Writings of Pauline Kael: A Library of America Special Publication |date=2011-10-27 |publisher=Library of America |isbn=978-1-59853-171-8 |pages=381 |language=en}}</ref>

In [[Time (magazine)|''Time'' magazine]], [[Richard Schickel]] wrote: "Now, at last, Mitchum achieves a kind of apotheosis in Peter Yates' strong, realistic and totally absorbing rendition of George V. Higgins' bestselling novel&nbsp;... Self-consciously, and with an old pro's quiet skills, Mitchum explores all of Coyle's contradictory facets. At 56, when many of his contemporaries are hiding out behind the remnants of their youthful images, he has summoned up the skills and the courage to demonstrate a remarkable range of talents."<ref>Schickel, Richard. "Cinema: Friends of Friends." TIME, 2 July 1973.</ref> ''[[The Economist]]'' described it as "a spare, lean thriller."<ref>{{Cite news |date=2 August 2024 |title=We enjoyed reading these books on holiday. You might, too |url=https://www.economist.com/culture/2024/08/02/we-enjoyed-reading-these-books-on-holiday-you-might-too |access-date=2024-08-14 |newspaper=The Economist |issn=0013-0613}}</ref>

Upon the film's release, [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave it four stars, his highest rating, while [[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' also reviewed it favorably, calling it "a good, tough, unsentimental movie".<ref>{{cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |author-link=Vincent Canby |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9F0CE1D6123DE63ABC4F51DFB0668388669EDE |title=The Friends of Eddie Coyle (1973) |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=June 27, 1973 |access-date=June 11, 2010 }}</ref> Both reviewers singled out Mitchum's lead performance as a key ingredient of the film's success. Ebert wrote: "Eddie Coyle is made for him [Mitchum]: a weary middle-aged man, but tough and proud; a man who has been hurt too often in life not to respect pain; a man who will take chances to protect his own territory."<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=June 27, 1973 |title=The Friends of Eddie Coyle |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-friends-of-eddie-coyle-1973 |access-date=14 March 2022 |author-link=Roger Ebert}}</ref>

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], it holds an 98% approval rating, based on 42 reviews with the consensus, "''The Friends of Eddie Coyle'' sees Robert Mitchum in transformative late-career mode in a gritty and credible character study."

==2009 bank robbery==
On June 16, 2009, just a few weeks after the DVD release, the same bank was robbed in a manner reminiscent of how it was done in the film.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}} Delroy George Henry drove up to the bank minutes before it opened.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=82}}{{r|well}} He then forced his way in the bank and tried to get the staff to open the vault.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=81}}{{r|well}} He also ordered staff to sit on the ground while brandishing a gun, just as was done in the film.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=82}}

An employee sent a text message to an employee in another branch who then called the [[Dedham Police Department]].{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=82}}{{r|well}} Lieutenant Frank McMillan, a police officer working a detail 100 yards away, responded quickly and apprehended Henry.{{sfn|Parr|2009|p=82}}{{r|well}}
