'''''El Caleuche''''' or The '''''Caleuche''''' ({{ipa|es|kalˈeutʃe}}),<ref name="contreras1966"/> also known by other names such as '''''Buque de Arte''''' (the Magic Ship) or the '''''Barcoiche''''', is a legendary [[ghost ship]] from [[Chilote mythology]] in southern [[Chile]].

==Nomenclature==
The ''Caleuche'' is also known as the ''Buque de Arte''{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|pp=39–41}} ("the Magic Ship"{{sfnp|Torres Caballero|2009|p=207}}<!--The Enchanted Ship, The Warlocks' Ship{{cn}}-->). Natives also refer to it as the ''Barcoiche'', the ''Gauiteca'', the ''Saiza'',{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=30}}{{sfnp|Torres Caballero|2009|p=207}} '''''El Marino'''''<ref name="cardenas&hall1992"/> or ''Barco Marino''{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=30}} ("Marine Ship"{{sfnp|Torres Caballero|2009|p=207}}) or ''Buque de Fuego''{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=30}} ("Fire Ship"{{sfnp|Torres Caballero|2009|p=207}}).

While {{lang|es|Buque Fantasma}} ("Ghost Ship") has been given as another alias proper name,{{sfnp|García Barría|1997|p=120}}{{sfnp|Torres Caballero|2009|p=207}} earlier commentators use this term for ghost ship lore in general, including the legends from Europe from which the Chilean version may have derived certain base elements.{{sfnp|Saunière|1918|p=41}}{{Refn|"{{lang|es|bajel fantasma}}" is also used as the generic term (for ghost ships of the Strait of Magellan and the seas of Europe) in the gloss for Caleuche by F. J. Cavada.{{sfnp|Cavada|1914|p=303}}}}

===Etymology===
One theory derives "caleuche" from [[Mapuche language|Mapuche]]/Mapudungun/Araucanian {{lang|arn|caleutun}} (var. {{lang|arn|kalewtun}}) "to transform, to change condition" and {{lang|arn|che}} "people".<ref>[[Rodolfo Lenz]] cited by {{harvp|Cavada|1914|pp=303–304}}, {{harvp|Contreras|1966|p=153}}, {{harvp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=9}}, and {{harvp|García Barría|1997|p=120}}</ref> Alternately it may derive from {{lang|arn|calül}} "human body" and ''che'' "people".<ref>Manuel Antonio Román cited by {{harvp|Cavada|1914|pp=303–304}}, {{harvp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=9}} and {{harvp|García Barría|1997|p=120}}</ref>

There is also the hypothesis that the legend originates from an early 17th century Dutch ship ''Kalache'' or ''El Calanche'', lost at sea<ref name="daughters2019"/>{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|pp=7–8, 10, 27, 146, 200}} (Cf. {{section link||Origin explanations}}).

== Legend  ==
The legend of the ''Caleuche'' is related to many aspects of history and the beliefs of the Chiloé archipelago. It is a marvelous ship of music and lights that travels along Chiloé canals.

Allegedly, it is a pirate ship crewed by witches and demons, only witnessed at night, appearing as an intensely illuminated ship,{{Refn|group="lower-alpha"|A barebone version is localized as lore of [[Castro, Chile|Castro]].{{sfnp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=424}}}}{{Refn|name="cavada_d1896"|{{harvp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=425}} citing [[:es:Darío Cavada|Cavada, Darío]] (1896). ''Chiloé''.}}{{sfnp|Saunière|1918|pp=41–42}}{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}<ref name="plath2022"/> and held responsible for kidnappings of young women, night raids and robbery.<ref>{{harvp|Saunière|1918|pp=41–42}}, citing {{ill|Julio Vicuña Cifuentes|es}} from Santiago, and {{ill|Francisco Cavada|es}} from Chiloé.</ref> The ship captures sailors aboard other sailed boats, by attracting them with alluring orchestral music.{{sfnp|García Barría|1997|p=120}} Allegedly ordinary people who board it fall into a kind of stupor, preventing them from divulging any secrets about the ship (lore of [[Puerto Varas]]).{{sfnp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=424}}<ref>{{harvp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=424}} ''apud'' {{harvp|Saunière|1918|p=42}}</ref> Or anyone fallen victim, even in a slight way, turns mad, with his face always turning backwards.{{Refn|name="cavada_d1896"}}
 
The Caleuche can disappear instantly; it can also take on the appearance of other things to escape notice, e.g., turn into a tree trunk (especially cypress{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}) and even come ashore that way,<ref name="garcia_barria1997"/> or remain afloat,<ref name="plath2022"/> or the ship may turn into a rock as well.{{Refn|Cavada (1914),<ref name="cavada1914"/> repeated by Plath.<ref name="plath2022"/>}}<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> Its sailors can transform into seals,<ref name="garcia_barria1997"/> [[sea lion]]s,{{efn|{{langx|es|lobos [marinos]}}.}} dolphins,<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> or sea birds.{{Refn|name="cavada_d1896"}}<ref name="plath2022"/> The ship sails at extraordinary speed.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> In order to observe the ship without being noticed, one must cover one{{'}}s mouth, because they can smell one{{'}}s breath.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> There are also varieties of trees behind which one can hide so as to not be captured by the Caleuche, some being the ''maqui'' berry tree ([[Aristotelia chilensis|Chilean wineberry]]) and the ''tique'' tree ([[Aextoxicon|olivillo]]<ref name="coluccio1953"/>).<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/>

It is said that when certain conditions are met, such as it being a foggy day, the ship becomes discernable to other seafarers (or the luminous ship seems to appear and disappear surrounded by fog{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}): the sounds of chains, parties, music ("flutes, horns, [[fife (instrument)|pipe]]s, and drums"{{efn|{{langx|es|"flautas, cuernos, pitos y tambores"}}}}{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}), and the dominating outline of the ship makes it unmistakable.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> According to some, it is an incorporeal ship, and will pass right through other vessels, but some insist it is real enough.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/>

Many believe that the sailors of this ship have a leg attached to their spine, similar to the {{lang|es|{{linktext|invunche}}}}<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> (''[[imbunche]]'', a deformed humanoid monster). Other informants add that the crew's deformity of one leg bent backward forces them to hop around to maneuver, and, as aforementioned, are purposely kept as forgetful idiots in order to protect the ship's secrets.<ref name="plath2022"/> In contrast, others describe the crew as specially dressed and well-presented ("walking elegantly in long leather boots"{{Refn|"Todos andan muy elegantes, calzando largas botas de charol"<ref name="coluccio1953"/>}}), except when one greets them with a [[handshake]], their hands will feel very cold.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/>

The ''Buque de Arte'' is furthermore a submarine ship, and also navigates up rivers or canals.<ref name="cavada1914"/>{{sfnp|Saunière|1918|p=42}}<ref name="garcia_barria1997"/>{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}} In one version (lore of [[Valdivia]]), it is more particularly a submarine [[steamship]], and the potential recruits are taken to a treasured city at the bottom of the sea, but sworn to secrecy on pain of death.{{sfnp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|pp=424–425}}

When the ship is in need of repair, it prefers to go to a ravine or cliff to work on the damaged hull or machinery during the night.<ref name="cavada1914"/>

=== Merry merchant ship ===
As common as the tale of a haunted ship of dead and enslaved sailors, there also exists a version of this tale where the Magical Ship houses sorcerers of Chiloé, and they party and transport merchandise. {{citation needed span|text=It is also stated that the sorcerers return from a voyage that they make every 3 months in order to strengthen their powers.|date=December 2024}} It is said that only crewmembers can board the ship and use the ''caballo marino'' ("sea-horse", mythological creature that can carry 13 warlocks on its back{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=214}}) as a form of transportation,{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=214}} but the rules of the witches forbid the men from entering the ship by any other means than riding the ''caballo marino''.<ref>{{harvp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=99}} s.v. "El Caballo Marino"</ref>

Some have even claimed to have attended parties held aboard the ship.<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/> But the crew of the ''Caleuche'' would rather cavort with women on land when holding their carousals, so they bargain with merchants to rent out their daughters in exchange for merchandise.{{Refn|group="lower-alpha"|Or they like to dance with the daughters of port captains ({{lang|es|los capitanes del puerto}}) who would be handsomely compensated with money.<ref name="coluccio1953"/>}} Sometimes if a merchant is suspiciously prospering without showing efforts of transacting business, this will invite rumors from the local Chiloense that they are "protegés of ''El Marino''". According to legend, merchants who made a pact with the ship have black hens and tarred boats with ropes of [[Luzuriaga radicans|quilineja]].<ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/>

In a different telling, when the crew of the ghost ship are on their [[shore leave]], they impose themselves on friends or on someone who has committed some petty crime and force them to entertain the sailors at the host's expense.{{efn|Specifically José Huala who used explosives to catch fish was forced to entertain, and the cost ruined his fortune.}}{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}

One collected tale claims a slender ''[[Chalupa (boat)|chalupa]]'' type boat commanded by a certain young man from {{ill|Chonchi (commune)|es|Chonchi (comuna)|lt=Chonchi}} never returned, but the father rather than to mourn only smiled knowingly, indicating he was sure the son was safely aboard the ''Caleuche''. The man thereafter grew increasingly rich, and people heard the sound of chains being lowered at the house over several nights; it was the  ''Caleuche'' furtively unloading its cargo of merchandise to benefit this merchant. The commentary makes connection to the aforementioned popular belief that a man suddenly getting rich will be suspected of carrying on secret commerce with the ghost ship.<ref name="cavada1914"/>{{Refn|The tale is paraphrased or repeated by {{harvp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=426}} and Plath (2022) in Spanish,<ref name="plath2022"/> and by the Insight guide in English, which cites Plath<ref name="Insight2005"/>}}

Rumors like these were rampant following the [[1960 Valdivia earthquake]] because some houses were untouched by the fires that swept through Chiloé afterwards. In that same decade there were stories that the sounds of a ship dropping anchor could be heard around the houses of many prosperous merchants in the Chiloé archipelago. These were supposedly the sounds of the ''Caleuche'' stealthily delivering goods and treasures to those with whom it had a pact. However, most people rejected this supernatural explanation and instead blamed the merchant's prosperity on mortal, rather than supernatural, smugglers.{{citation needed|date=December 2024}}

This ship has three favorite ports of call: Llicaldac and Tren-Tren on the coast of the former {{ill|Castro (department)|es|Departamento de Castro|lt=Department of Castro}}, and Quicaví where the major cave of the [[Warlocks of Chiloé|Warlock king of Chiloé]] ({{lang|es|rey de brujos de Chiloé}}) is located. The warlock king mounts a  ''caballo marino'' (sea-horse) which is swifter than the ship.{{Refn|name="canas_vocab"|Cañas Pinochet (1911)<ref name="canas1911"/> later redacted by {{harvp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=427}}}}<ref name="plath2022"/><ref name="Insight2005"/>

=== Reckoning for sea creatures ===
The Magical Ship is commanded by the sea deity [[Millalobo]] to sail all over the seas to keep track of all marine creatures living and newly born.<ref>{{harvp|Miranda|2019|p=140}} citing {{harvp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=114}}</ref> A legend tells that a man named Pancho Calhuante in the village of Matao took a {{ill|sea lion|es|Lobo marino de un pelo|preserve=1}} (''lobo'') cub from its mother and slaughtered it to extract oil. The crew of the ''Caleuche'' arrived and exacted penalty by killing the man's eldest son.{{sfnp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=114}}

In a different account ''El Caleuche'' itself had taken a sea lioness for a wife, which was killed by fishermen of [[Tenglo Island|Tenglo]], opposite [[Puerto Montt]]. The enraged ''El Caleuche'' sent evils (eruptions of the [[Calbuco (volcano)|Calbuco volcano]]) there, and also abducted the prettiest girls from the port.{{Refn|name="canas_vocab"}}<ref name="plath2022"/>

=== Elysium of the drowned ===
Also, Millalobo's daughter [[Pincoya]] goes around collecting [[Drowning|drowned]] sailors, bringing him ashore if still alive, or else brings the dead to the ghost ship for them to live a new life as crewmembers who will pass eternity in happiness.<ref>{{harvp|Miranda|2019|p=141}} citing {{harvp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=25}} but also {{harvp|Quintana Mansilla|1987|p=113}}</ref> In another telling, the ship itself simply collects the drowned and recruit them as crew.<ref name="plath2022"/>

Some believe that their final destination is the port named [[City of the Caesars]], a marvelous place hidden away somewhere along the Andes where its residents eternally reside.{{sfnp|Cárdenas|1998|p=32}}

==Eyewitness accounts==
An incident being ascribed as an alleged sighting of this ghost ship occurred some time in 1909 or earlier (though the account says there was no vessel). It was witnessed by the various crew members on a [[Chilean Navy|Chilean navy]] boat, as attested by Agustín Prat, 2nd in command of the naval {{ill|escampavía|es|lt=''escampavía''}} ''Huemul'' who wrote an account of it in a letter <!-- to his friend, don Fernando Setit--> that he saw "two large, white lights (each about the size of a lantern).. hovering no more than one metre above surface, with no vessel in sight". The two light or flame bars joined together to form one large flame pillar, and travelled at a speed between 7 and 15 miles per hour.<ref>{{harvp|Cavada|1914|pp= 94–96}}: "A las 3 h. 45 m. A. M. del Domingo 8 del mes en curso,.. acercaban.. dos luces grandes, blancas (como de un farol cada una).. suspendidas en el aire a una altura no menor de un metro, y sin verse embarcación alguna.. no ya una sino dos que se separaban a momentos de un modo brusco para reconstituir después una más grande y sola, ésta avanzando siempre con una velocidad no menor de siete millas en su mínimum y quince en su máximum...  "</ref>{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=56}}<ref>{{harvp|Meurger|Gagnon|1988|p=285}}: "two huge lights, travelling one metre below the surface, with a speed varying between seven and fifteen miles per hour".</ref>

A 1911 testimony of a luminous ship passing by was given by the guard officer and lantern-lighter aboard the ship ''Copiapó'', documented in the [[port authority]] archives of [[Valparaíso]]. The ship "passed near them without stopping, without making noise, without stopping or responding to their signals".{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|p=58}}<ref name="perez2007"/>

==Origin explanations==
Several hypotheses have been proposed to scientifically explain a luminous ship. The light may be some sort of [[phosphorescence]] out at sea; it could be [[bioluminescence]] from the [[glowworm]] ({{lang|es|gusano de luz}}) or a large colony of ''[[Noctiluca scintillans]]''.{{efn|Alias  ''N. milijaris'' given by source.}} Or it could be some electrical phenomenon.<ref>{{harvp|Cavada|1914|p=94}}, later cited by {{harvp|Vicuña Cifuentes|1914|p=426}} and by {{harvp|Saunière|1918|p=42}}</ref>

There are some who classify it as a phenomenon of OSNI (abbreviation of {{langx|es|objeto sumergible no identificado}}, i.e., [[unidentified submerged object]])<ref name="callejo1998"/> or OFNI (abbreviation of {{langx|es|objeto flotante no identificado}}, i.e., "unidentified floating object").<ref name="perez2007"/>

Between the various hypotheses that have been proposed on the origin of the legend, it is suggested that it could be a readaptation of the European legend of the ghost ship known as The ''[[Flying Dutchman]]''.{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|pp=20–21}} It has also been argued that it was based on real events, such as the Dutch ship ''Kalache'' (also given as ''El Calanche''), led by Vincent van Eucht, which disappeared in the southern seas in the early 1600s.<ref name="daughters2019"/><ref name="cardenas-libro1998-caleuche"/>{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|pp=7–8, 10, 27, 146, 200}}<!-- or the mysterious disappearances of the Spanish Strait of Magellan (Estrecho de Magallanes) [machine translated gibberish?]-->{{Refn|group="lower-alpha"|Offered as another possibility is that the legend has origins based on the arrival of Dutch privateer ships led by [[Baltazar de Cordes]], arriving in the archipelago and capturing the island for brief period in the year 1600.{{sfnp|Cárdenas Tabies|1980|pp=7, 26}}}} Another interpretation is that it was originally only an invention to hide contraband operations in the Chiloé Archipelago.{{citation needed|date=January 2025}}

==In media==
{{more citations needed section|date=March 2017}}
The legend of the ''Caleuche'' is referred to in [[Alastair Reynolds]]' 2001 novel ''[[Chasm City]]'', when the Chilean crewmembers of a [[generation ship]] discover a "ghost ship" trailing the flotilla of colony vessels and dub the ship ''Caleuche''.

The ''Caleuche'' is a ghost pirate ship that sails around the globe, captained by the Sirena Chilota mermaid from the original legends in The Vampire Blade Book Series by M.C. Waring.

In film and television, [[Raúl Ruiz (director)|Raúl Ruiz]]'s ''[[Three Crowns of the Sailor]]'' (1983) and ''[[Litoral (film)|Litoral]]'' (2008) and [[Jorge Olguín (director)|Jorge Olguín]]'s ''Caleuche: The Call of the Sea'' (2012) are all loosely inspired by the legend.

==Scientific namesakes==
[[Caleuche Chasma]], the deepest canyon on the moon [[Charon (moon)|Charon]], is named after the ''Caleuche''.<ref name="IAU 2018">{{cite web | title=International Astronomical Union | website=IAU | date=2018-04-11 | url=https://www.iau.org/news/pressreleases/detail/iau1803/ | access-date=2020-09-07 | archive-date=2018-04-12 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180412082822/https://www.iau.org/news/pressreleases/detail/iau1803/ | url-status=dead }}</ref> The exoplanet [[HD 164604 b]] is also formally named Caleuche.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.nameexoworlds.iau.org/final-results|title=Approved names|website=NameExoworlds|language=en|access-date=2020-01-02}}</ref>
