'''''Straight to Hell''''' is a 1987 [[spaghetti Western]] [[parody film]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://collider.com/the-clash-courtney-love-western-straight-to-hell/|work=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|title=The Clash's Frontman & Courtney Love Made a Parody Acid Spaghetti Western|last=Bryant|first=Aidan|date=8 June 2023|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208183159/https://collider.com/the-clash-courtney-love-western-straight-to-hell/|archive-date=8 February 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |quote=One such filmmaker is Alex Cox, director of ''Repo Man'' and ''Sid and Nancy''. In 1987 he made his own Spaghetti Western pastiche ''Straight to Hell'', and, more recently, has written an assessment of the genre in ''10,000 Ways to Die''. To this task, Cox brings a lifelong appreciation of all Westerns, as well as experience behind the camera, both of which give him a unique perspective to the genre. |url=http://www.powells.com/review/2009_09_26 |date=26 September 2009|last=Donaghy |first=Gerry |title=From Liverpool to Cinecitta|work=[[Powell's Books]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110604215543/http://www.powells.com/review/2009_09_26|archive-date=4 June 2011}}</ref> directed by [[Alex Cox]] and starring [[Sy Richardson]], [[Joe Strummer]], [[Dick Rude]], and [[Courtney Love]]. Loosely based on ''[[Django Kill... If You Live, Shoot!]]'' (1967),<ref name="indie" /> the film follows three male bank robbers and their [[gun moll]] who become stranded in the desert, where they stumble upon a surreal Western town inhabited by [[coffee]]-addicted killers. [[Dennis Hopper]], [[Grace Jones]], [[Miguel Sandoval]], [[Elvis Costello]], [[Xander Berkeley]], [[Edward Tudor-Pole]], [[Kathy Burke]], and [[Jim Jarmusch]] are also featured in the film, along with band members of [[the Pogues]], [[Amazulu (band)|Amazulu]], and [[the Circle Jerks]]. The film borrows its title from the Clash's 1982 [[Straight to Hell (The Clash song)|song of the same name]].

Director Cox and star Dick Rude wrote the screenplay for ''Straight to Hell'' over three days, rapidly developing the project after the cancellation of a planned concert tour in Nicaragua. Cox invited several members of the bands involved—including Strummer (of [[The Clash]]), as well as [[The Pogues]]—to appear in the film. Principal photography took place over four weeks in the summer of 1986 in [[Almería]], Spain.

''Straight to Hell'' received few positive reviews upon release, and was not a commercial success, although it later gained a [[cult film]] status. A [[Straight to Hell (soundtrack)|soundtrack]], including music by some of the musician/actors who starred in the film, was also released. On 14 December 2010, an extended cut of the film, titled ''Straight to Hell Returns'', was released on DVD, featuring additional footage and digitally enhanced picture quality.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.alexcox.com/dir_straighttohell.htm|work=Alex Cox Official Website|title=STRAIGHT TO HELL RETURNS|access-date=22 September 2011|archive-date=14 May 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110514235034/http://www.alexcox.com/dir_straighttohell.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> This version of the film, under the collaboration of Alex Cox, was also screened at several cinemas as part of a [[midnight movie]] theatrical run.

==Plot==
Three hitmen, Willy, Norwood, and Simms, are staying in a Los Angeles hotel. After failing a job, they take off in a car with Velma, Norwood's pregnant wife. They flee to Mexico to escape the wrath of their boss, Amos Dade, robbing a bank along the way. While driving through the desert, their car breaks down, having accidentally filled the tank with [[diesel fuel|diesel]]. They bury their suitcase of money and begin to walk.

Night falls, and they come upon a nearly deserted town, where they enter an empty bar and the three men get drunk while Velma pesters them to leave. The group camps out for the night on the edge of town, and the following morning Velma witnesses several trucks of cowboys enter the town, carrying [[espresso machine]]s with them. Much to the dismay of Velma, who insists they keep a low profile and leave, the three men enter the town, which is now full of townspeople, and go back to the bar.

There they are confronted by Bruno and Angel-Eyes, two members of the gang that runs the town, the McMahon clan. A stand-off ensues, but before guns are drawn, another group, led by a man named Rusty Zimmerman, arrives to arrest Bruno and Angel-Eyes for previous (minor) crimes. However the three hitmen kill Zimmerman and his cadre, saving the McMahon brothers. They are ultimately welcomed by the townspeople for this action. The bizarre townspeople include George and Fabienne, a couple who own a general store, Karl, a man running a hot dog stand, and countless cowboys and other unusual characters. The head honcho of the town, Frank McMahon, invites the gang to a party that evening. The following day, Frank's elderly father is pushed off of a building by his relative Sabrina. The entire town has a funeral procession for him, and at the funeral another employee of Mr. Dade, named Whitey, shows up looking for the hitmen and Velma.

The town seizes Whitey for being a "stranger", and accuses him of the murder of the McMahon grandfather. On the [[gallows]], Whitey begins to tell the town about a bounty on the hitmen, but is hanged before he can tell his story. Meanwhile, Simms and Fabienne have an affair, while Willy falls for Louise, one of the few women in the McMahon clan, who spurns his advances and is clearly only interested in learning where they have hidden their money. A man named [[I.G. Farben]], who claims to be a house developer, enters town with his wife Sonia and introduces himself, but quickly disappears, leaving a suitcase of [[machine gun]]s for Norwood, Simms, and Willy. The next morning, Simms sees Mr. Dade's car enter the town, and tries to get a drunken Willy and Norwood to leave with Velma.

George, already angry over Fabienne being unfaithful, kills Angel-Eyes for making sexual advances her, and the three hitmen are blamed. A series of shootouts begin between the townspeople, Mr. Dade's crew, and the hitmen. Frank joins Mr. Dade's team, and everyone begins to turn against each other. Willy seeks Louise, hoping she'll join his side, but instead she shoots him, though he manages to crawl away, badly wounded. As Simms and Willy run into the desert, a shootout ensues with the town priest. They reach the spot where they buried the money, and, after recovering it, Simms shoots the wounded Willy. Simms in turn is shot by Velma. Frank and Velma then take off arm-in-arm with the suitcase of money, while Simms and Willy die.

Meanwhile, in town, chaos has ensued, and the store is set on fire, with Norwood and Fabienne inside. They miraculously survive and shoot  Mr. Dade and his cronies. Eventually virtually everyone is killed, aside from Norwood and several female characters. Frank and Velma leave the town in a truck with the suitcase full of money, but accidentally drive off of a cliff when their brakes go out, killing both in a fiery explosion. Norwood leaves town with the women, and Farben Oil Company trucks enter the town to drill for oil.

''Straight to Hell'' was developed by writer-director [[Alex Cox]] after a preponderance of musicians and bands—among them [[Joe Strummer]], [[The Pogues]]—had gathered to perform a concert tour of [[Nicaragua]].<ref name="latimes">{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2010-nov-15-la-et-alex-cox-20101115-story.html|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=15 November 2010|title=Offbeat Alex Cox film gets second chance|last=Olsen |first=Mark|archive-url=https://archive.ph|archive-date=11 October 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> Political problems arose concerning the support of the left-wing government of Nicaragua, and the tour was cancelled. As an alternative, Cox invited the bands, and several actors he could assemble, to make a movie in Spain.<ref name=latimes/> Cox and co-star [[Dick Rude]] wrote a script over three days in December 1986,{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=75}} loosely basing it on the [[spaghetti Western]] ''[[Django Kill... If You Live, Shoot!]]'' (1967), directed by [[Giulio Questi]].<ref name="indie">{{cite web|url=http://www.indiewire.com/article/alex_cox_straight_to_hell_returns_opens_the_door_to_the_wretched_party/|work=[[IndieWire]]|title=Why Alex Cox Returned to "Straight to Hell"|last=Koh |first=Michelle|date=4 March 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250125051819/https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/why-alex-cox-returned-to-straight-to-hell-243434/|archive-date=25 January 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> Cox described it as an "homage" to Questi's film.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=82}} Cox turned down the chance to direct ''[[Three Amigos]]'' in order to make ''Straight to Hell''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.orartswatch.org/discoveries-repo-man-director-alex-cox-discusses-walker-on-blu-ray-and-much-more/|work=Oregon ArtsWatch|title=‘Disc’overies: ‘Repo Man’ director Alex Cox discusses ‘Walker’ on Blu-ray and much more|last=Mohan|first=Marc|date=24 May 2022|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208200933/https://www.orartswatch.org/discoveries-repo-man-director-alex-cox-discusses-walker-on-blu-ray-and-much-more/|archive-date=8 February 2026}}</ref>

Cox wrote the part of Velma specifically for [[Courtney Love]], who had appeared in a supporting role in his previous film, ''[[Sid and Nancy]]'' (1986).<ref name="hollywood" /><ref>{{cite news|last=Snyder|first=Michael|date=28 June 1987|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/san-francisco-chronicle/190864249/|work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|p=23|title=Actress Love's Story Takes Happy Turn|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Love modeled the character after [[Carroll Baker]]'s performance in the 1956 film ''[[Baby Doll]]''.<ref>{{Cite magazine|magazine=[[Interview (magazine)|Interview]]|date=February 1987|title=Courtney Love|page=25|issn=0149-8932}}</ref>

===Filming=== 
A co-production between the United Kingdom and the United States,<ref name=BFI/> ''Straight to Hell'' was filmed over four weeks on location in [[Almería (province)|Almería]], [[Andalusia]], Spain, beginning on 4 August 1986.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=75}}<ref name=Parscandola>{{cite web|work=Parscandola.com|url=https://parascandola.com/straight-to-hell/|author=Parscandola, Mark|title=Once Upon a Time in Almería: Straight to Hell|date=20 February 2012|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208192456/https://parascandola.com/straight-to-hell/|archive-date=8 February 2026}}</ref> The production budget was approximately $1 million.<ref name=Ebert>{{cite web|date=1 July 1987|last=Ebert|first=Roger|title=Straight to Hell|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/straight-to-hell-1987|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|via=[[RogerEbert.com]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208192939/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/straight-to-hell-1987|archive-date=8 February 2026|author-link=Roger Ebert}}</ref> [[Principal photography]] began at the [[Gran Hotel Almería]], before moving to [[Benahadux]] and the [[White Towns of Andalusia|Pueblos Blancos]].{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=75}}

The Western town featured in the film was located in the [[Tabernas Desert]], and had originally been used for the 1973 film ''[[Chino (1973 film)|Chino]]'' starring [[Charles Bronson]].<ref name=Parscandola/>

Production of the film was troubled, as funds provided by [[Eric Fellner]] of [[List of Island Pictures films|Island Pictures]] ran out approximately midway through filming.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=75}} As a result, the remainder of the film was financed using [[traveller's cheque]]s and credit cards.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=75}}

On an episode of comicbook on IG, both John Cusack and Tim Robbins claimed they had been cast in the film, went to Spain to shoot, but were fired because they refused to shave their heads because of upcoming work.<ref>{{Cite web | title=Instagram | url=https://www.instagram.com/reels/DbZmDaEBuok/ | access-date=2026-09-04 | website=www.instagram.com}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
The soundtrack for the film was composed of all new, original music composed and performed mostly by the musicians who acted in the film, with contributions from [[The Pogues]] and [[Pray for Rain (band)|Pray for Rain]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/san-francisco-chronicle/190861923/|work=[[San Francisco Chronicle]]|date=January 4, 1987|last=Snyder|first=Michael|title=Singer's Sister Helps Answer Their Prayers|via=Newspapers.com|p=43}}</ref> However, the original 1987 soundtrack release contained only some of the music from the film.<ref>Liner notes to the 2004 ''Straight to Hell Returns'' ([[Big Beat Records (American record label)|Big Beat Records]] CDWIKD 239, 2004.</ref> The complete soundtrack was not released until 2004, under the new title ''Straight to Hell Returns''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.discogs.com/Various-Straight-To-Hell-Returns/release/1663279|title=Straight to Hell Returns (2004, Soundtrack, CD)|website=[[Discogs]]|date=13 September 2023 }}</ref>

==Release==
''Straight to Hell'' was theatrically released in the United Kingdom on 12 June 1987.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=78}} In the United States, the film opened in New York City on 26 June 1987,<ref name=Maslin/> followed by a California premiere at the Pickwick Drive-In in [[Burbank, California|Burbank]] on 1 July 1987.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=78}}<ref name=Lozano>{{cite web|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-10-08-me-287-story.html|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|title=Death of a Drive-In: Pickwick Theater Shuts Down, Ending an Era for Burbank Moviegoers and Film Makers|last=Lozano|first=Carlos V.|date=8 October 1989|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200807025303/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-10-08-me-287-story.html|archive-date=7 August 2020}}</ref> Invitees to the Pickwick screening were asked to come dressed in "post-apocalyptic fiesta garb,"<ref name="deans">{{cite news | last = Deans | first = Laurie | title = What on Earth Do You Wear to a 'Post-Apocalyptic Fiesta'? | newspaper = [[The Globe and Mail]] | date = 3 July 1987 }}</ref> and guests in attendance were given complementary [[water gun|water pistol]]s.<ref name="deans"/> Star Hopper was in attendance, along with [[Emilio Estevez]], [[Buck Henry]], [[Shelley Duvall]], and [[Ed Harris]].<ref name=Lozano/>

The film's premiere was a fiasco, and several people at the drive-in left midway into the movie.<ref name="hollywood">{{cite episode|airdate=October 2005|network=E!|series=[[The E! True Hollywood Story]]|title=Courtney Love}}</ref> Courtney Love was reportedly visibly upset at the premiere.<ref name="hollywood" />

In the US ''Straight to Hell'' was rated "[[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system|R]]" for violence and language. The latter reason caught the producers by surprise, as the writers deliberately refrained from including any sort of profanity in the dialogue. Even the word "hell" appears only in the title (at one point a character quite noticeably says "what the ''heck'' is going on here?"), and the insults that fly before a showdown are no worse than "go boil yer head!"

===Home media===
[[Anchor Bay Entertainment]] released ''Straight to Hell'' on [[DVD]] in 2001.<ref>{{cite av media|title=Straight to Hell|oclc= 47023352|publisher=[[Anchor Bay Entertainment]]|medium=[[DVD]]}}</ref> 

====2010 director's cut====
In 2010, Microcinema DVD announced a new director's cut, dubbed ''Straight to Hell Returns''. The new version features a new HD transfer, color correction that changes the look of the film, new effects, and new footage. Blood and additional violence during the shootout scenes was digitally implemented into the film which had not been there prior.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/46591/straight-to-hell-returns/|work=[[DVD Talk]]|title=Straight to Hell returns|last=Hartel |first=Nick|date=14 December 2010|access-date=23 September 2011}}</ref> Cox stated that he was inspired to revisit the film by [[Francis Ford Coppola]]'s ''[[Apocalypse Now Redux]]''.<ref name="indie" /> The DVD was released on 14 December 2010. 

Leading up to the DVD release, ''Straight to Hell Returns'' was screened at several [[arthouse]] theaters across the United States and Canada in October and November 2010.<ref name="latimes" /> In 2018, [[Kino Lorber]] re-released the director's cut on DVD and [[Blu-ray]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/straight-to-hell-lives-again/|work=[[Slant Magazine]]|title=Alex Cox’s ''Straight to Hell'' Lives Again|last=Schwartz|first=Niles|date=23 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220613130341/https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/straight-to-hell-lives-again/|archive-date=13 June 2022|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Straight to Hell'' grossed $210,200 at the [[box office]].<ref name=BOM>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl275940865/|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]|title=Straight to Hell|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208193440/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl275940865/|archive-date=8 February 2026}}</ref>

===Critical response===
The film was not well received by critics, drawing mostly negative reviews.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=81}} In her review for ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Janet Maslin]] wrote, "The result is a mildly engrossing, instantly forgettable [[midnight movie]]."<ref name=Maslin>{{cite news |last=Maslin |first=Janet |date=26 June 1987 |title=Straight to Hell |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/26/movies/film-straight-to-hell.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|author-link=Janet Maslin|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251221072606/https://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/26/movies/film-straight-to-hell.html|archive-date=21 December 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> Hal Hinson, in his review for ''[[The Washington Post]]'', wrote, "The action is so gratuitous, and so indifferently presented, that it's impossible to think that Cox ever truly intended it to be seen by anyone outside of the cast and crew and their immediate families."<ref>{{cite news | last = Hinson | first = Hal | title = "Hell" on Reels | newspaper = [[The Washington Post]]|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1987/07/01/hell-on-reels/162eb705-1d96-43a9-9179-4b7bcad6ae99/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20260208183913/https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1987/07/01/hell-on-reels/162eb705-1d96-43a9-9179-4b7bcad6ae99/|archive-date=8 February 2026 | date = 1 July 1987 }}</ref> 

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film 1.5 stars out of a possible 4. He wrote: "After "Repo Man" and "Sid and Nancy," I believed that [Cox] could scarcely do wrong, and that there was a streak of obsession in his genius that might well carry him into the pantheon. Since then I have seen Cox's "Straight to Hell," and I must report that he is human after all. I still anticipate his next film. I still think he has a special gift. But "Straight to Hell" is an indulgent mess...'<ref name=Ebert/>

===Accolades===
''Straight to Hell'' received the Critics' Prize at the 1987 [[Madrid]] Film Festival, where [[Sergio Leone]] served as a jury member that year.{{sfn|Davies|2000|p=81}}
