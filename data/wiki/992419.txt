'''''Rock 'n' Roll High School''''' is a 1979 American [[musical film|musical]] [[comedy film]] directed by [[Allan Arkush]], produced by [[Michael Finnell]], and starring [[P. J. Soles]], [[Vince Van Patten]], [[Clint Howard]], and [[Dey Young]].<ref name="Icons Of Fright" /> The film features [[punk rock]] group the [[Ramones]].<ref>{{cite book |last1=Armstrong |first1=Stephen B. |title=I Want You Around: The Ramones and the Making of Rock 'n' Roll High School |date=2023 |publisher=Backbeat Books |page=xv}}</ref>

==Plot==
[[Vince Lombardi]] High School keeps losing principals to nervous breakdowns because of the students' love of [[rock and roll]] and their disregard for education. The leader of the students, Riff Randell, is the biggest Ramones fan at the school and also the worst behavioral problem, with a disciplinary record that fills an entire filing cabinet. She waits in line for three days to get tickets to see the band, hoping to meet [[Joey Ramone]], so she can give him a song she wrote for them, "Rock 'n' Roll High School".

When the new tyrannical Principal Togar takes her ticket away, Riff and her best friend Kate Rambeau have to find another way to meet their heroes, winning a radio contest. Riff succeeds in delivering her song to Joey Ramone, but the next day, Principal Togar and a group of parents attempt to burn a pile of the kids' mostly Ramones rock records. In response, the students and music teacher Mr. McGree (who develops a change of heart for Rock and Roll throughout the film), joined by the Ramones (who have made Riff an honorary Ramone), overthrow the teachers and hall monitors Fritz Gretel and Fritz Hansel to take over the high school, with Principal Togar asking the musicians, "Do your parents know you're Ramones?"<ref>{{cite web|title=Rock 'n' Roll High School Quotes|url=http://www.quotes.net/mquote/79823|publisher=Stands4 LLC|access-date=2017-06-27}}</ref> When the police are summoned and demand that the students evacuate the building, they do so, but then the students and the Ramones blow up the school as a final act of youthful rebellion, driving Principal Togar insane.

==Production==
[[Roger Corman]] was looking to make a modern [[teen film]] similar to the ones he made in his early career during the 1960s, but with the focus on current music of the time. The initial title ''Disco High'' was selected for a story idea from Allan Arkush and Joe Dante. A script was developed by [[Richard Whitley]], Russ Dvonch, and Joseph McBride. During this time, the film went through several different title changes including ''Heavy Metal Kids'' and ''Girl's Gym''. Arkush directed the majority of the film, but Dante also helped when Arkush was suffering from exhaustion.<ref name="Sherman" />

Corman originally wanted [[Cheap Trick]] or [[Todd Rundgren]] to play the band, but due to schedule conflicts, he was forced to find an alternative.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Stafford|first1=Jeff|title=Rock'n'Roll High School|url= https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/88502/rock-n-roll-high-school#overview|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20150914052820/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/88502/Rock-N-Roll-High-School/#overview|url-status= dead|archive-date= September 14, 2015|website=Turner Classic Movies|publisher=Turner Classic Movies|access-date=January 17, 2024}}</ref> The Ramones were suggested by Paul Bartel, one of the actors in the film. During their visit to LA, the Ramones stayed at the [[Tropicana Motel]], where they wrote songs for their next album, ''[[End of the Century]]''.<ref name=rammuse>[https://www.ramonesmuseum.com/junkyard/ "take A LOOK INSIDE RAMONES MUSEUM" ramonesmuseum.com]</ref> The genesis for the plot was a favorite story told to the film's original writer by his father, Raymond E. McBride of the ''[[Milwaukee Journal]]'', who staged a walkout from his [[Superior High School (Wisconsin)|Superior (Wis.) Central High school]] in the 1920s.<ref name="Holmstrom" />

The film was shot on the campus of the defunct [[Mount Carmel High School (Los Angeles)|Mount Carmel High School]] in [[South Los Angeles|South Central Los Angeles]], which had been closed in 1976. The nighttime school explosions and fires were so great that many people were scared away and, temporarily, would not return to the on-campus sets. Extras were students from [[Mira Costa High School]] in [[Manhattan Beach, California]]. The opening sequence, the food fight scene, and the dance number “Do You Wanna Dance” featured the drill team and football team wearing their actual uniforms from MCHS.<ref>{{cite news|title=40 Years Ago, the Ramones Roamed L.A. in "Rock 'n' Roll High School"|url=https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/rock-n-roll-high-school-40-the-ramones/|access-date=September 13, 2020|work=lamag.com|date=August 2, 2019}}</ref>

==Release==
The film had a sneak preview in Hollywood. It then had showings in Texas and New Mexico in April, 1979. It then went to San Francisco, then in July to Chicago, and opened in Manhattan on August 3 of that year, then continued on to further markets.<ref>[https://www.facebook.com/steven.grant.750/posts/pfbid02cBZEzesxQBconcrpThTWhc1aLbq1SEQ6qgVYuCLdmFx9VieqokfWYXUZ4Cq5YUDHl Comments by director Allan Arkush on a Facebook post]</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/article/austin-american-statesman/139444305/ Announcement of April 20 premiere in Texas]</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/article/daily-news-rocknroll-high-school-ad/139433151/ Ad announcing start of Manhattan run]</ref>

===Home media===
''Rock 'n' Roll High School'' was originally released on [[VHS]] by Warner Home Video in 1983, and was later re-released on VHS in 1996 by New Horizons Home Video ({{OCLC|36127344}}). A year later, in 1997, it was issued on [[DVD]] by Lumivision.<ref name="Sherman" /> A second DVD release occurred in 1999 from Slingshot.<ref name="Sherman" /> Shortly after Joey Ramone's death in 2001, New Concorde produced a third DVD release.<ref name="Sherman" /> The film was once again issued on DVD in 2005 by Buena Vista Home Entertainment ({{ISBN|978-0-7888-6342-4}} {{OCLC|62756806}}). DVDs in the PAL format were issued by Umbrella Entertainment in 2003 ({{OCLC|223658430}}) and again in 2007 ({{OCLC|368008921}}).

The film was a part of [[Shout! Factory]]'s ''Roger Corman Cult Classics'' series,<ref>[https://www.dvdempire.com/1527161/rock-n-roll-high-school-movie.html DVD Empire]</ref> reissued on DVD in May 2010.<ref name="Creepy" /> Shout! Factory released the film with exclusive content on Blu-ray on May 11, 2010<ref>[https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Rock-n-Roll-High-School-Blu-ray/9217/ Rock 'n' Roll High School Blu-ray Release Date May 11, 2010 (Roger Corman's Cult Classics)]</ref> and again on November 19, 2019, with a new 4K restoration.<ref>[https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Rock-n-Roll-High-School-Blu-ray/250151/ Rock 'n' Roll High School Blu-ray Release Date November 19, 2019 (SteelBook)]</ref>

A soundtrack album on [[Sire Records|Sire]]/[[Warner Records|Warner Bros. Records]] was released around the same time, but it included only a limited number of songs from the film. The two main Ramones songs (the title song and "I Want You Around") were recorded by [[Ed Stasium]] but remixed by [[Phil Spector]] for the soundtrack album. The original Ed Stasium mixes were not issued until the compilation album ''[[Ramones Mania]]'' (1988) and the compilation album ''[[Hey! Ho! Let's Go: The Anthology]]'' (1999), respectively.

==Reception==
''Rock 'n' Roll High School'' received generally positive reviews. {{Rotten Tomatoes prose|83|7|29||access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes" /> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average score]] of 70 out of 100, based on 7 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title= Rock 'n' Roll High School |url= https://www.metacritic.com/movie/rock-n-roll-high-school |website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date= June 4, 2023}}</ref> ''[[The Globe and Mail]]'' wrote that "the plot owes something to ''[[Animal House]]'' (but ''Rock 'n' Roll High School'' is funnier) and something to ''[[Beach Blanket Bingo]]'' and something to many other movies too numerous to mention; it is not, how you say, original."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Scott |first1=Jay |title=These are rock's old rebels |work=The Globe and Mail |date=22 Sep 1979 |page=E5}}</ref> ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' opined: "It has the look of ''[[Grease (film)|Grease]]'' and the soul of ''Animal House''—and it's better than both. The anarchic rock of The Ramones electrifies the film with a driving beat that never lets up. The film, like rock and roll, is just plain fun."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Blowen |first1=Michael |date=August 17, 1980 |title=A Rockin' Trip Back to High School |url=https://bostonglobe.newspapers.com/image/428223837/ |newspaper=[[The Boston Globe]] |publisher=Boston Globe Media Partners, LLC |department=TVW |page=1 
|url-access=subscription |access-date=January 25, 2024}}</ref> Zack Handlen of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' wrote: "Underneath its chipper, anything-for-laugh grin, Rock ‘N’ Roll is as subversive as teen movies get, with an ending that, for all its absurdity, is still surprisingly shocking."<ref>{{cite web |last=Handlen |first=Zack |date=May 5, 2010 |title=Rock 'N' Roll High School |url=https://www.avclub.com/rock-n-roll-high-school-1798164853 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |publisher=Paste Media Group |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241203144623/https://www.avclub.com/rock-n-roll-high-school-1798164853 |archive-date=December 3, 2024 |access-date=December 30, 2025}}</ref>

==Sequel==
In 1991 a sequel called ''[[Rock 'n' Roll High School Forever]]'' was released. The sequel starred [[Corey Feldman]]. [[Mary Woronov]], who played Principal Togar in the original film, appeared in the sequel as Vice Principal Doctor Vadar. The movie was met with poor reviews but over the years it has gained a cult following.

==Proposed remake==
In July 2008, actor/writer [[Alex Winter]] was hired to script a remake of the film for [[Howard Stern]]'s production company.<ref name="Drees" /><ref name="Fleming" />

==In other media==
Corman's short-lived [[comic book]] publishing imprint, [[Roger Corman's Cosmic Comics]], released a two-issue take on the film in 1995, written by [[Bob Fingerman]] with art by [[Shane Oakley]] and [[Jason Lutes]]. Unable to acquire likeness licenses for the [[Ramones]], the comic instead featured the [[Melvins]].<ref>Reed, Patrick A.
