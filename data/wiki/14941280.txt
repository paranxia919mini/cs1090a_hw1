'''''Ratatouille''''' ({{IPAc-en|ˌ|r|æ|t|ə|ˈ|t|uː|i}} {{respell|RAT|ə|TOO|ee}}) is a 2007 American animated [[comedy-drama]] film<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ratatouille (2007) |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/55185-RATATOUILLE?sid=6b43c35d-b005-4eef-a37d-dbf9a4fc90ea&sr=10.701259&cp=1&pos=0 |access-date=July 27, 2022 |publisher=American Film Institute |archive-date=July 27, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220727164423/https://catalog.afi.com/Film/55185-RATATOUILLE?sid=6b43c35d-b005-4eef-a37d-dbf9a4fc90ea&sr=10.701259&cp=1&pos=0 |url-status=live }}</ref> written and directed by [[Brad Bird]], from a story by [[Jan Pinkava]],<ref>{{Cite web |last=Chang |first=Justin |date=June 18, 2007 |title=Film Review: Ratatouille |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/awards/ratatouille-2-1200558501/ |access-date=September 20, 2022 |magazine=Variety |archive-date=September 20, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220920173033/https://variety.com/2007/film/awards/ratatouille-2-1200558501/ |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Jim Capobianco]] and Bird. Produced by [[Pixar Animation Studios]] for [[Walt Disney Pictures]], the film stars the voices of [[Patton Oswalt]], [[Lou Romano]], [[Ian Holm]], [[Janeane Garofalo]], [[Peter O'Toole]], [[Brian Dennehy]], [[Peter Sohn]] and [[Brad Garrett]]. The title refers to the French dish [[ratatouille]], and also references the species of the main character, a rat. Set mostly in Paris, the plot follows a young rat Remy (Oswalt) who dreams of becoming a chef at Auguste Gusteau's (Garrett) restaurant and tries to achieve his goal by forming an unlikely alliance with the restaurant's garbage boy Alfredo Linguini (Romano).

Development for ''Ratatouille'' began in 2000 when Pinkava wrote the original concepts of the film, although he was never formally named the director of the film. In 2005, following Pinkava's departure from Pixar for lacking confidence in the story development, Bird was approached to direct the film and revise the story. Bird and some of the film's crew members also visited Paris for inspiration. To create the food animation used in the film, the crew consulted chefs from both France and the United States. Producer [[Brad Lewis]] interned at [[Thomas Keller]]'s [[The French Laundry]] restaurant, where Keller developed the [[confit byaldi]], a dish used in the film. [[Michael Giacchino]] composed the Paris-inspired music for the film.

''Ratatouille'' premiered on June 22, 2007, at the [[Kodak Theatre]] in Los Angeles, California, with its general release on June 29, in the United States. The film became a critical and commercial success, grossing $623.7 million worldwide. It finished its theatrical run as the [[2007 in film#Highest-grossing films|sixth highest-grossing film of 2007]] and the year's second highest-grossing animated film (behind ''[[Shrek the Third]]''{{--)}}. The film received widespread acclaim for its screenplay, animation, humor, voice acting, and Giacchino's score. It also won the [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature]] and was nominated for several more, including [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]]. ''Ratatouille'' was later voted one of the hundred greatest motion pictures of the 21st century by a [[BBC's 100 Greatest Films of the 21st Century|2016 poll of international critics]] conducted by the [[BBC]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films |title=The 21st Century's 100 greatest films |publisher=BBC |date=August 23, 2016 |access-date=December 16, 2016 |archive-date=November 24, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161124191856/http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. Please discuss any major changes on the talk page. Word Count as of October 28, 2025: 636-->
Remy, a young [[Anthropomorphism|anthropomorphic]] [[rat]] with heightened senses of taste and smell, dreams of becoming a chef like his human idol, the late Auguste Gusteau. Conversely, the rest of his colony, including his older brother Émile and their father, Django, the clan leader, only eat for sustenance and are wary of humans. The rats live in an elderly woman's attic outside [[Paris]], but when the woman discovers them, Remy becomes separated from the others during their hasty evacuation. Encouraged by an imaginary Gusteau, he explores until he finds himself on the roof of Gusteau's namesake restaurant.

Remy sees the restaurant's new garbage boy, Alfredo Linguini, struggling to fix a [[leek soup]] he ruined. Remy sneaks in and improves the soup; Linguini notices and traps Remy while keeping his presence secret from Skinner, Gusteau's former [[sous-chef]] and the restaurant's new owner and head chef. Skinner confronts Linguini about the soup, but it is served by accident and unexpectedly becomes a hit. Colette Tatou, the restaurant's only female chef, persuades Skinner to keep Linguini and support Gusteau's motto, "Anyone can cook." Skinner demands Linguini replicate the soup but spots Remy, ordering Linguini to take him outside and kill him. Alone, Linguini realizes Remy understands him and persuades Remy to assist with cooking.

Remy discovers that he can control Linguini's movements like a [[marionette]] by pulling on his hair while hiding under his [[toque]]. They recreate the soup and continue cooking at the restaurant. Colette begrudgingly trains Linguini but steadily appreciates him heeding her advice. Later, Remy reunites with his clan. After Remy tells Django that he intends to stay at the restaurant, Django shows him a group of exterminated rats to convince him that humans are dangerous, but Remy defies his warnings and leaves.

Meanwhile, Skinner is shocked and enraged to discover through a letter from Linguini's late mother that Gusteau is Linguini's father, making him the rightful owner of the restaurant. After Skinner's lawyer verifies that Linguini is Gusteau's son, Skinner hides the evidence in an envelope; Remy steals the envelope and brings it to Linguini. Skinner is fired and Linguini is promoted to head chef. The restaurant thrives as Remy's recipes become popular, and Linguini develops a romantic relationship with Colette. Food critic Anton Ego, who negatively reviewed the restaurant shortly before Gusteau's death, announces to Linguini that he will review the restaurant again the following day. After Linguini takes credit for Remy's cooking at a [[press conference]], he and Remy have a falling out. As revenge, Remy leads his clan on a raid of the restaurant's pantries. Linguini arrives to apologize, but upon discovering the raid, he furiously expels Remy and his clan from the restaurant.

The next day, Skinner captures Remy, who is quickly freed by Django and Émile. After returning to the restaurant, he and Linguini reconcile, and Linguini reveals Remy and his cooking techniques to his staff, who all immediately quit. Django, impressed by Remy's grit, summons the clan to help him cook while Linguini waits tables. Reminded of Gusteau's motto, Colette returns to help. Skinner and a health inspector attempt to interfere, but the rats tie them up, gag them and lock them in the pantry.

Remy prepares [[confit byaldi]], a variation of [[ratatouille]], which evokes in Ego [[In_Search_of_Lost_Time#Memory|a sudden and involuntary recollection of his childhood]] and of his mother's cooking. Astonished and delighted, Ego asks to meet the chef and -- once all other guests are gone -- is stunned when introduced to Remy. The next day, he writes a glowing review, stating that he has come to understand Gusteau's motto and praising Remy without revealing that he is a rat. After Skinner and the health inspector are released and expose the rat infestation, Gusteau's is shut down, costing Ego his job and reputation. Remy, Linguini, and Colette open a [[bistro]] called La Ratatouille, which a now-happier Ego invests in and frequents. The rat colony settles into the bistro's attic as their new home.

Because ''Ratatouille'' is intended to be a romantic, lush vision of Paris, giving it an identity distinct from the studio's previous films,<ref name="early taste"/> director Brad Bird, producer Brad Lewis and some of the crew spent a week in the city to properly understand its environment, taking a motorcycle tour and eating at five top restaurants.<ref name="parlez">{{cite web|title=Parlez-vous Francais |publisher=Yahoo! |date=May 22, 2007 |url=https://movies.yahoo.com/summer-movies/Ratatouille/1808490293/trailers/8/381 |access-date=May 23, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070526103543/http://movies.yahoo.com/summer-movies/Ratatouille/1808490293/trailers/8/381 |archive-date=May 26, 2007  }}</ref> There are also many water-based sequences in the film, one of which is set in the sewers and is more complex than the [[blue whale]] scene in ''[[Finding Nemo]]''. One scene has Linguini wet after jumping into the [[Seine]] to fetch Remy. A Pixar employee (Shade/Paint department coordinator Kesten Migdal) jumped into Pixar's swimming pool wearing a chef's uniform and apron to see which parts of the suit stuck to his body and which became translucent from water absorption.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Technical Ingredients |publisher=Disney Pictures |url=http://disney.go.com/disneypictures/ratatouille/main.html |access-date=May 23, 2007 |archive-date=May 25, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070525221059/http://disney.go.com/disneypictures/ratatouille/main.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[File:Confit byaldi 1.jpg|thumb|left|The film's take on the traditional ratatouille dish was suggested by gourmet chef Thomas Keller, a variation known as [[confit byaldi]].]]
A challenge for the filmmakers was creating computer-generated images of food that would appear delicious. Gourmet chefs in both the U.S. and France were consulted,<ref name="ignedit"/> and animators attended cooking classes at [[San Francisco Bay Area|San Francisco-area]] culinary schools to understand the workings of a [[commercial kitchen]].<ref name="sfcarticle">{{cite news | access-date=June 29, 2007 | url=http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2007/06/28/MNGGIQNA7M1.DTL | work=San Francisco Chronicle | date=June 28, 2007 | author=Stacy Finz | title=Bay Area flavors food tale: For its new film 'Ratatouille,' Pixar explored our obsession with cuisine | archive-date=June 30, 2007 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070630133627/http://sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2007/06/28/MNGGIQNA7M1.DTL | url-status=dead }}</ref> Sets/Layout department manager Michael Warch, a culinary-academy-trained professional chef before working at Pixar, helped teach and consult animators as they worked. He also prepared dishes used by the Art, Shade/Paint, Effects and Sets Modeling departments.<ref name="cook 101">{{cite web | title=Cooking 101 | publisher=Disney Pictures | url=http://disney.go.com/disneypictures/ratatouille/main.html | access-date=May 23, 2007 | archive-date=May 25, 2007 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070525221059/http://disney.go.com/disneypictures/ratatouille/main.html | url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="nytfood">{{cite news | url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/06/13/dining/13rata.html?ex=1339387200&en=988ed2df8b9bcdb4&ei=5088&partner=rssnyt&emc=rss | author=Kim Severson | date=June 13, 2007 | title=A Rat With a Whisk and a Dream | access-date=June 30, 2007 | work=The New York Times | archive-date=June 14, 2007 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070614124840/http://www.nytimes.com/2007/06/13/dining/13rata.html?ex=1339387200&en=988ed2df8b9bcdb4&ei=5088&partner=rssnyt&emc=rss | url-status=live }}</ref> Renowned chef [[Thomas Keller]] allowed producer Brad Lewis to intern in his [[French Laundry]] kitchen. For the film's [[Climax (narrative)|climax]], Keller suggested a fancy, layered version of the title dish for the rat characters to cook, called "[[confit byaldi]]".<ref name="sfcarticle"/> The same [[Subsurface scattering|sub-surface light scattering]] technique that was used on the skin in ''[[The Incredibles]]'' was used on fruits and vegetables,<ref name="animated edit"/> while new programs gave an organic texture and movement to the food.<ref>{{cite web|title=Cooking Up CG Food |publisher=ComingSoon.net |date=May 24, 2007 |url=https://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=20608 |access-date=May 24, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070526042435/http://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=20608 |archive-date=May 26, 2007 }}</ref> Completing the illusion were music, dialogue, and [[abstract imagery]] representing the characters' mental sensations while appreciating food. ==Soundtrack==
{{Further|Ratatouille (soundtrack)}}

''Ratatouille'' is the second Pixar film to be scored by [[Michael Giacchino]] after ''The Incredibles''. It is also the second Pixar film not to be scored by [[Randy Newman|Randy]] or [[Thomas Newman]]. The scores feature a wide range of instrumentation and are influenced by various music genres. Giacchino wrote two themes for Remy, one about him with the rat colony and the other about his hopes and dreams. He also wrote a buddy theme for both Remy and Linguini that plays when they are together. In addition to the score, Giacchino wrote the main theme song, "Le Festin", about Remy and his dream to be a chef. French artist [[Camille (French singer)|Camille]] (who was 29 at the time of the film's release) was hired to perform "Le Festin" after Giacchino listened to her music and realized she was perfect for the song; as a result, the song is sung in French in almost all versions of the film.<ref name="slashfilm1">{{cite web |last1=Lussier |first1=Germain |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/michael-giacchino-talks-music-pixar-d23-expo/ |title=Michael Giacchino Talks The Music Of Pixar (D23 Expo) |publisher=/Film |date=September 2, 2011 |access-date=February 5, 2014 |archive-date=February 22, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140222062138/http://www.slashfilm.com/michael-giacchino-talks-music-pixar-d23-expo/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The [[soundtrack album]] was released by [[Walt Disney Records]] on June 26, 2007.

==Release==
===Theatrical===

Earlier in the year, it had received an [[Academy Awards|Academy Award]] nomination.<ref name="quint">{{cite news |author=Eric Vespe |title=Quint orders a giant plate of RATATOUILLE and eats it up!!! |publisher=[[Ain't It Cool News]] |date=June 9, 2007 |url=https://www.aintitcool.com/node/32939 |access-date=June 10, 2007 |archive-date=June 12, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070612123731/http://www.aintitcool.com/node/32939 |url-status=live }}</ref> A [[test screening]] of the film was shown at the Harkins Cine Capri Theater in [[Scottsdale, Arizona]] on June 16, 2007, at which a Pixar representative was present to collect viewer feedback.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Huxley |first1=Thomas |url=http://pixarplanet.com/blog/report-from-pixar-screening-of-ratatouille |title=Report from Pixar Screening of Ratatouille |publisher=Pixar Planet |date=June 9, 2007 |access-date=October 5, 2013 |archive-date=January 22, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160122173206/http://pixarplanet.com/blog/report-from-pixar-screening-of-ratatouille/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Disney CEO [[Bob Iger]] announced an upcoming theatrical re-release of the film in 3D at the Disney shareholders meeting in March 2014.<ref name="cartoon">{{cite news |url=http://blog.bcdb.com/incredibles-animated-sequel-6987/ |title=Incredibles Animated Sequel |first=Dave |last=Koch |publisher=[[Big Cartoon DataBase|Big Cartoon News]] |date=March 18, 2014 |access-date=March 18, 2014 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140319172802/http://blog.bcdb.com/incredibles-animated-sequel-6987/ |archive-date=March 19, 2014  }}</ref>

===Marketing===
The [[Trailer (promotion)|trailer]] for ''Ratatouille'' debuted on June 9, 2006, with the release of ''[[Cars (film)|Cars]]'', its immediate predecessor.<ref>{{cite web |last= |date=June 12, 2006 |title=Preview: Ratatouille |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2006/06/12/preview-ratatouille |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260413180705/https://www.ign.com/articles/2006/06/12/preview-ratatouille |archive-date=April 13, 2026 |access-date=April 13, 2026 |website=IGN |df=mdy-all}}</ref> It depicts an original scene where Remy is caught red-handed on the cheese trolley in the restaurant's dining area, sampling the cheese and barely escaping the establishment, intercut with separate scenes of the rat explaining directly to the audience why he is taking such risks. Similar to most of Pixar's teaser trailers, the scene was not present in the final film release.<ref name="yahoo1">{{cite web |url=https://movies.yahoo.com/video/ratatouille-trailer-185316448.html |title='Ratatouille' Theatrical Trailer |publisher=Yahoo! |date=April 15, 2013 |access-date=October 5, 2013 |archive-date=October 12, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012035215/http://movies.yahoo.com/video/ratatouille-trailer-185316448.html |url-status=live }}</ref> A second trailer was released on March 23, 2007.<ref>{{cite news |title=New Ratatouille Trailer Coming Friday |publisher=ComingSoon.net |date=March 19, 2007 |url=https://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=19432 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120724005845/http://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=19432 |url-status=dead |archive-date=July 24, 2012 |access-date=March 19, 2007 }}</ref> The Ratatouille ''Big Cheese Tour'' began on May 11, 2007, with cooking demonstrations and a film preview.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Desowitz|first1=Bill|title=Ratatouille to Kick Off With 'Big Cheese Tour'|url=http://www.awn.com/news/ratatouille-kick-big-cheese-tour|publisher=Animation World Network|access-date=August 7, 2016|date=May 11, 2007|archive-date=October 9, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161009122703/http://www.awn.com/news/ratatouille-kick-big-cheese-tour|url-status=live}}</ref> Voice actor Lou Romano attended the San Francisco leg of the tour for autograph signings.<ref>{{cite news | last1=Ponto |first1=Arya |title=Ratatouille Big Cheese Slide With Lou Romano |publisher=JustPressPlay |date=July 16, 2007 |url=http://www.justpressplay.net/movies/ratatouille/news/ratatouille-big-cheese-slide-with-lou-romano.html |access-date=July 17, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070926235924/http://www.justpressplay.net/movies/ratatouille/news/ratatouille-big-cheese-slide-with-lou-romano.html <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date = September 26, 2007}}</ref>

[[Image:Ratatouille-wine.jpg|right|thumb|The front label of the planned ''Ratatouille'' wine to have been promoted by Disney, Pixar, and Costco, and subsequently recalled for its use of a cartoon character.]]
Disney and Pixar were working to bring a French-produced ''Ratatouille''-branded wine to [[Costco]] stores in August 2007, but abandoned plans because of complaints from the California [[Wine Institute (California)|Wine Institute]], citing standards in labelling that restrict the use of cartoon characters to avoid attracting under-age drinkers.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.latimes.com/la-fi-ratwine28jul28,0,7091322.story?coll=la-home-center | title=Disney backs out of wine promotion |last=Lifster |first=Marc |date=July 28, 2007 |access-date =August 1, 2007 |work=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref> Moreover, both companies faced other challenges trying to lure audiences, as several stores had been overflowing with merchandise themed to other newly released films like ''[[Spider-Man 3]]'', ''[[Shrek the Third]]'' and ''[[Transformers (film)|Transformers]]'', making it harder to persuade parents to spend an additional cost between $7.99 to $19.99 on a plush rat.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-2007-06-30-0706290787-story.html|title=Disney faces a challenging stew in trying to sell 'Ratatouille'|website=[[Chicago Tribune]]|date=June 30, 2007 |access-date=January 23, 2022|archive-date=January 23, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220123165154/https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-2007-06-30-0706290787-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the United Kingdom, in place of releasing a theatrical trailer, a commercial featuring Remy and Emile was released in cinemas before its release to discourage obtaining unlicensed copies of films.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bvimovies.com/spotlight/anti_piracy.html |title=Ratatouille's Ratatouille fights movie piracy |publisher=The Film Factory |access-date=December 23, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071014123003/http://www.bvimovies.com/spotlight/anti_piracy.html |archive-date=October 14, 2007 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Also, in the United Kingdom, the main characters were used for a commercial for the [[Nissan Note]], with Remy and Emile watching an original commercial for it made for the "Surprisingly Spacious" ad campaign and also parodying it, respectively.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.visit4info.com/details.cfm?adid=49783 |title=Nissan Note Exploding Cars It's Possible |publisher=Visit4Info |access-date=April 11, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071011082243/http://www.visit4info.com/details.cfm?adid=49783 |archive-date=October 11, 2007 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Disney/Pixar was concerned that audiences, particularly children, would not be familiar with the word "ratatouille" and its pronunciation. As a result, the title was spelled phonetically (as "rat-a-too-e") within trailers and on posters.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://deepfocusreview.com/Reviews/ratatouille.asp |title=''Ratatouille'' review |last=Eggert |first=Brian |date=June 29, 2007 |access-date=October 15, 2007 |publisher=DeepFocusReview.com |archive-date=October 17, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071017201528/http://deepfocusreview.com/Reviews/ratatouille.asp |url-status=dead }}</ref> For similar reasons, in the American release of the film, on-screen text in French was printed in English, such as the title of Gusteau's cookbook and the sign telling kitchen staff to wash their hands, although in international versions, such as the British English release and the US Spanish-language DVD release, these are rendered in French. In Canada, the film was released theatrically with text in English: on DVD, the majority of the text (including Gusteau's will) was in French.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/features/pixar-hopes-auds-find-ratatouille-tasty-1117967050/ |title=Pixar hopes auds find 'Ratatouille' tasty |last=Graser |first=Marc |date=June 15, 2007 |access-date=October 15, 2007 |work=Variety |archive-date=October 17, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071017205649/http://variety.com/article/VR1117967050.html?categoryid=2520&cs=1 |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Home media===
''Ratatouille'' was released by [[Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment]] on [[Blu-ray]] and [[DVD]] in North America on November 6, 2007.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.highdefdigest.com/news/show/Disney/Pixar/Disc_Announcements/Disney_Serves_Up_Ratatouille_on_Blu-ray_this_November/852 |title=Disney Serves Up 'Ratatouille' on Blu-ray this November |publisher=High-Def Digest |date=August 10, 2007 |access-date=August 20, 2007 |archive-date=December 1, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201081909/https://www.highdefdigest.com/news/show/Disney/Pixar/Disc_Announcements/Disney_Serves_Up_Ratatouille_on_Blu-ray_this_November/852 |url-status=live }}</ref> A new animated short film featuring Remy and Emile entitled ''[[Your Friend the Rat]]'' was included as a special feature, in which the two rats attempt to entreat the viewer, a human, to welcome rats as their friends, demonstrating the benefits and misconceptions of rats towards humanity through several historical examples. The eleven-minute short uses 3-D animation, 2-D animation, live action and even stop-motion animation, a first for Pixar.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lee |first1=Patrick |url=http://www.scifi.com/scifiwire/index.php?category=0&id=45050&type=0 |title=Rat DVD Has First Pixar 2-D Toon |publisher=SCI FI Wire |date=October 30, 2007 |access-date=October 30, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071230070708/http://www.scifi.com/scifiwire/index.php?category=0&id=45050&type=0 |archive-date=December 30, 2007}}</ref>

The disc also includes a CGI short entitled ''[[Lifted (2006 film)|Lifted]]'', which was screened before the film during its theatrical run. It depicts an adolescent [[extraterrestrial life|extraterrestrial]] attempting to kidnap a sleeping human. Throughout the sequence, he is graded by an adult extraterrestrial in a manner reminiscent of a [[driver's license|driver's licensing]] exam road test. ===Accolades===
{{Main|List of accolades received by Ratatouille}}

''Ratatouille'' won the [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature]] at the [[80th Academy Awards]] and was nominated for four others: [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]], [[Academy Award for Best Sound Editing|Best Sound Editing]], [[Academy Award for Best Sound|Best Sound Mixing]], [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]], losing to ''[[Atonement (2007 film)|Atonement]]'', ''[[The Bourne Ultimatum (film)|The Bourne Ultimatum]]'' (for both Best Sound Editing and Best Sound Mixing), and ''[[Juno (film)|Juno]]'', respectively.<ref name="academy">{{cite web|url=http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/oscarlegacy/2000-present/2008/winners.html |title=Winners and Nominees&nbsp;– 80th Academy Awards |date=January 6, 2009 |access-date=January 6, 2009 |publisher=Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012045551/http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/oscarlegacy/2000-present/2008/winners.html |archive-date=October 12, 2013  }}</ref><ref name="oscars2000">{{cite web|url=http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/oscarlegacy/2000-present/2008/winners.html |title=Nominees & Winners of the 2008 (80th) Academy Awards &#124; Academy of Motion Picture Arts & Sciences |publisher=Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences |date=August 24, 2012 |access-date=October 5, 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131012045551/http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/oscarlegacy/2000-present/2008/winners.html |archive-date=October 12, 2013}}</ref> With five Oscar nominations, the film broke the record for an animated feature film, surpassing the four nominations each of ''[[Aladdin (1992 Disney film)|Aladdin]]'', ''[[Monsters, Inc.]]'', ''[[Finding Nemo]]'', and ''[[The Incredibles]]''. ===Theme park attraction===
{{main|Ratatouille: L'Aventure Totalement Toquée de Rémy}}

A Disney theme park attraction based on the film has been constructed in [[Walt Disney Studios Park]], [[Disneyland Paris]]. In the attraction, riders "shrink down to the size of a rat".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dlptoday.com/news/walt-disney-studios-park/toon-studio/ratatouille-dark-ride/ |title=Ratatouille: The Adventure&nbsp;– L'Aventure Totalement Toquée de Remy |work=DLP Today |date=July 30, 2012 |access-date=October 9, 2014 |archive-date=October 23, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141023071902/http://www.dlptoday.com/news/walt-disney-studios-park/toon-studio/ratatouille-dark-ride/ |url-status=live }}</ref> At the 2017 [[D23 (Disney)|D23]] Expo, Disney announced the attraction would be built at the [[France Pavilion at Epcot|France Pavilion]] in [[Epcot|Epcot's]] World Showcase<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2017/07/15/business/media/disney-epcot-theme-parks.html|title=Disney Vows to Give Epcot a Magical, Long-Overdue Makeover|first=Brooks|last=Barnes|date=July 15, 2017|work=The New York Times|access-date=July 16, 2017|archive-date=July 15, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170715181742/https://www.nytimes.com/2017/07/15/business/media/disney-epcot-theme-parks.html|url-status=live}}</ref> which opened on October 1, 2021, during the 50th anniversary of [[Walt Disney World]] and the 39th anniversary of Epcot.

The [[Ministry of Culture (Brazil)|Brazilian Ministry of Culture]] posted Marco Aurélio Canônico's article on its website.<ref name=MinistryCultureBrazil>{{cite web|last1=Canônico |first1=Marco Aurélio| url=http://www.cultura.gov.br/site/?p=107 |title=Vídeo Brinquedo faz sucesso com desenhos como "Os Carrinhos" e "Ratatoing" |language=pt |work=Folha de S.Paulo |publisher=[[Ministry of Culture (Brazil)|Ministry of Culture]] (Brazil) |quote="These descriptions bring to mind ''Cars'' and ''Ratatouille'', the latest two feature films by North American giant Pixar, a part of Disney" |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080429215943/http://www.cultura.gov.br/site/?p=107 |archive-date=April 29, 2008}}</ref> To date no sources have been found to indicate that Pixar took legal action.

=== References in popular culture ===
The film has often been referenced in popular culture since its release, being mentioned or parodied on shows such as ''[[Saturday Night Live]]'', ''[[My Name Is Earl]]'', ''[[The Simpsons]]'', ''[[Breaking Bad]]'', ''[[Key & Peele]]'', ''[[Robot Chicken]]'', ''[[Orange Is the New Black]]'', ''[[Teen Titans Go!]]'', ''[[Difficult People]]'', ''[[The Good Place]]'', [[Once Upon a Time (TV series)|''Once Upon a Time'']], ''[[Brooklyn Nine-Nine]]'', and ''[[Ted Lasso]]'', as well as in the films ''[[The Five-Year Engagement]]'' (2012), [[The Suicide Squad (film)|''The Suicide Squad'']] (2021), ''[[Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Mutant Mayhem]]'' (2023), ''[[Zootopia 2]]'' (2025), and in comedian [[John Mulaney]]'s comedy special ''New in Town''.{{Citation needed|date=June 2025}}

A parody of ''Ratatouille'' is a significant [[Narrative thread|plot thread]] in the 2022 science-fiction film ''[[Everything Everywhere All at Once]]''. In the middle of the film, the main character [[Evelyn Quan Wang]] ([[Michelle Yeoh]]) attempts to explain the [[multiverse]] and the concept of "verse-jumping" (temporarily linking one's consciousness to another version of themselves in a different universe, and accessing all the emotions, memories, and skills in the process)<ref>{{cite web |last1=Dutta |first1=Shubhabrata |date=May 24, 2022 |title=The World And Characters Of 'Everything Everywhere All At Once,' Explained |url=https://dmtalkies.com/characters-of-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-explained/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230320191145/https://dmtalkies.com/characters-of-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-explained/ |archive-date=March 20, 2023 |access-date=20 March 2023 |website=Digital Mafia Talkies}}</ref> to her family using the Pixar film as an analogy, only to misremember it as being about a [[raccoon]] and being titled ''Raccacoonie''. Later, in one of several [[Parallel universes in fiction|parallel universes]], Evelyn is a ''[[teppanyaki]]'' chef who works with another ''teppanyaki'' chef named Chad ([[Harry Shum Jr.]]) who is being puppeteered by the anthropomorphic Raccacoonie (voiced by [[Randy Newman]]) who is hiding under Chad's chef hat, much like Remy and Linguini; during the film's climactic montage, Evelyn exposes Raccacoonie and he is taken away by [[Animal control service|animal control]], before she has a change of heart and helps Chad rescue Raccacoonie. Reportedly inspired by producer Jonathan Wang's father's habit of misremembering the names of popular films,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lee |first=Chris |date=2022-04-13 |title=The Everything Bagel of Influences Behind Everything Everywhere All at Once |url=https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-onces-influences-explained.html |access-date=2022-10-23 |website=Vulture |language=en-us |archive-date=April 16, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220416174415/https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/everything-everywhere-all-at-onces-influences-explained.html |url-status=live }}</ref> the running joke was described by ''[[IGN]]'' as "one of the film's highlights",<ref>{{Cite web |last=Leston |first=Ryan |date=2022-06-29 |title=Everything Everywhere All at Once's Best Prop Was Based Off a Real Raccoon Corpse |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-raccacoonie-real-raccoon-corpse |access-date=2022-10-23 |website=IGN |language=en |archive-date=October 23, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221023001912/https://www.ign.com/articles/everything-everywhere-all-at-once-raccacoonie-real-raccoon-corpse |url-status=live }}</ref> while Alison Herman of ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]'' noted a thematic resonance as both films were about "the virtues of creativity within material constraints".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Herman |first=Alison |date=2022-06-14 |title="Now We're Cooking": How 'Everything Everywhere All at Once' Made Raccacoonie |url=https://www.theringer.com/movies/2022/6/14/23166713/raccacoonie-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-inspiration-pixar |access-date=2022-10-23 |website=The Ringer |language=en |archive-date=October 23, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221023001912/https://www.theringer.com/movies/2022/6/14/23166713/raccacoonie-everything-everywhere-all-at-once-inspiration-pixar |url-status=live }}</ref>
