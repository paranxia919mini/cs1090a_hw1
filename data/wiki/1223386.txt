'''''Nowhere''''' is a 1997 [[Surrealist cinema|surrealist drama]]<ref>https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/unlikely-inspirations-behind-gregg-arakis-nowhere/</ref> film written and directed by [[Gregg Araki]]. Described by Araki as "''[[Beverly Hills, 90210]]'' on [[LSD|acid]]", the film follows a day in the lives of a group of Los Angeles college students and the strange lives that they lead. It stars an [[ensemble cast]] led by [[James Duval]] and [[Rachel True]].

The film is Araki's sixth overall and third entry in his ''Teenage Apocalypse'' film trilogy, preceded by ''[[Totally F***ed Up]]'' (1993) and ''[[The Doom Generation]]'' (1995). Like the other films in the trilogy, it contains scenes of graphic violence and sexuality. The film notably includes several cast members on the verge of stardom, including [[Ryan Phillippe]], [[Mena Suvari]], [[Kathleen Robertson]], and [[Denise Richards]].

Initial reception was mixed, though in subsequent years it has garnered a [[Cult following|cult status]] and its reputation among critics has grown.{{how|date=November 2025}}

==Plot==
In Los Angeles, Dark and Mel are a [[bisexual]] couple in an [[open relationship]]. Mel is dating a girl named Lucifer, whom Dark hates, while Dark is interested in a mysterious boy named Montgomery. Dark, Mel, and Lucifer meet up at a café they frequent, where they encounter other teenagers they know, such as Alyssa, Dingbat, and Egg and Dark's friend Cowboy, and they discuss a party being held that night by a man they know named Jujyfruit. Egg runs into an unnamed TV star from ''[[Baywatch]]''.

Cowboy tells Dark about his boyfriend Bart's [[heroin addiction]]. He offers Bart the chance to fix their relationship if he stops using drugs, but he declines. Alyssa and Dingbat meet with Ducky, Egg's brother and Dingbat's crush, before Alyssa meets up with her boyfriend Elvis. While waiting at a bus stop, Dark witnesses three [[valley girl]]s killed by an alien, which he tries to catch on video before it disappears.

At the ''Baywatch'' star's place, he and Egg watch TV together before he tries to make a move on her. She rejects his advances, and he violently rapes her. Dark and his friends play a drug-induced game of [[kick the can]], during which Montgomery gets abducted by the same alien from earlier, whom Dark encounters in a locker room. Egg and Bart both return home and watch the same televangelist, Moses Helper, on TV, who encourages the two to commit suicide in order to reach heaven.

When he fails to convince Mel to become monogamous at Jujyfruit's party, Dark goes outside and is joined by Dingbat. Suddenly, Ducky, after hearing about his sister's suicide, leaps into a swimming pool, with Dingbat performing [[CPR]] on him. Returning to the party, Dark enters a kitchen where he sees the same alien from earlier. He meets with Handjob and begins to tell him about his day before Alyssa and Elvis arrive. Elvis claims Handjob sold him bad drugs and beats him to death with a can of tomato soup.

Dark returns home and records a diary entry on his video camera, saying how he is "totally doomed". As he attempts to sleep, Montgomery knocks on his window. Dark lets him in as he explains that he was abducted and experimented on by aliens who intend to invade Earth. The two lie down in bed together and Montgomery asks if he can spend the night, with Dark agreeing only if he promises to never leave. The two close their eyes but are disturbed as Montgomery goes into a coughing fit, then explodes into a shower of blood, leaving only a cockroach-like alien who utters, "I'm outta here", before crawling out of the window. A blood-covered Dark sits in stunned silence, before screaming out in horror and anguish.

==Release==
In the summer of 1997, it received a limited North American theatrical release. The theatrical release was handled by [[Fine Line Features]], an indie/arthouse division of [[New Line Cinema]], which itself was owned by [[Time Warner]]. During October 1997, it was also screened at the [[British Film Institute|BFI]] London Film Festival in the United Kingdom. In Japan, it was released theatrically on August 3, 1998.

===Reception===
''Nowhere'' received mixed reviews from critics. On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 50% based on 14 reviews, with an average score of {{RT data|average}}.<ref>{{cite web |title=Nowhere |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/nowhere |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=February 20, 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 44 out of 100, based on 16 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Nowhere'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/ |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date= |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref name="meta">{{Cite web |title=Nowhere Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/nowhere/ |access-date=2025-06-10 |website=www.metacritic.com |language=en}}</ref>

On August 19, 1997, Marc Savlov of ''[[The Austin Chronicle]]'' called it as "beautifully messy" as Araki's previous films ''[[Totally F***ed Up]]'' and ''[[The Doom Generation]]'', but added that "this time Araki employs a far broader and more complex character canvas than previously."<ref name="austin">{{Cite web|url=https://www.austinchronicle.com/events/film/1997-08-15/nowhere/|title=Movie Review: Nowhere|website=www.austinchronicle.com}}</ref> The ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' gave the film a positive review, calling it "high energy" and stating that "Araki is a marvel at controlling shifting tones, and ''Nowhere'', a confident, intricate work, has a great [[pop art]] look, yet its emotions are real."<ref>{{cite news|last=Thomas|first=Kevin|author-link=Kevin Thomas (film critic)|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-05-09-ca-56896-story.html|title=Stylish 'Nowhere' Finds Youthful Despair|date=May 9, 1997|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]}}</ref>

On June 13, 1997, the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' referred to it as Araki's "most expensive and mainstream film",<ref name="meta"/> while in 1997, ''[[The New York Times]]'' described ''Nowhere'' as "California's version of ''[[Kids (film)|Kids]]''", adding that "if it weren't so overpopulated and desperate to shock, ''Nowhere'' might have succeeded as a maliciously cheery satire of Hollywood brats."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/film/nowhere-film-review.html|title=Nowhere|website=archive.nytimes.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1997/05/04/movies/smiling-batman-killer-dinos.html|title=Smiling Batman, Killer Dinos|first=Anita|last=Gates|newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=May 4, 1997 |url-access=limited |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171228063855/https://www.nytimes.com/1997/05/04/movies/smiling-batman-killer-dinos.html |archive-date=December 28, 2017 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Writing for ''[[Empire (film magazine)|Empire]]'', Jake Hamilton gave the film a negative review, stating, "True, there are some dazzling scenes; a brilliant intercutting sex-scene; death by a Campbell's soup tin and a ridiculously absurd finale, but compared to the likes of [[Richard Linklater]]'s endearing ''[[Dazed and Confused (film)|Dazed and Confused]]'', ''Nowhere'' is completely lost up its own arse."<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/nowhere-review/|title=Nowhere|last=Hamilton|first=Jake|magazine=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]| year=2000}}</ref> Canada's ''[[The Globe and Mail|Globe and Mail]]'' wrote on June 14, 1997 that "you can find more entertaining absurdities and thrilling nihilism from watching the average episode of ''[[Melrose Place]]'' or ''[[Beverly Hills, 90210]]''."<ref name="meta"/>

Araki himself described the film as "''Beverly Hills, 90210'' on acid",<ref name="nylon">{{cite magazine |last=Lodi |first=Marie |title=Behind the Fashion and Beauty of Gregg Araki's ''Nowhere'', 25 Years Later |url=https://www.nylon.com/fashion/gregg-araki-nowhere-costume-designer-hair-makeup-artist |access-date=17 April 2023 |magazine=[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]] |date=6 May 2022}}</ref> with several reviewers also using this description for it at the time of release.<ref name="austin"/> In a retrospective article on the film in ''[[Nylon (magazine)|Nylon]]'', Marie Lodi wrote, "''Nowhere''{{'s}} surreal and hyper-saturated visuals were just as ahead of its time as its themes."<ref name="nylon"/>

===Home media===
In the United States, the film was released on [[VHS]]. On December 10, 1997, it was also released on [[LaserDisc]] in the United States by [[Image Entertainment]], who handled many other LaserDisc releases.

Elsewhere, it was made available on [[DVD]] in the United Kingdom, where it was released on a [[DVD region code#2|Region 2]] DVD.<ref>{{cite web |last=Metcalf |first=Paul |title=Film Review: The Doom Generation |url=https://www.pissedoffgeek.com/2012/03/24/film-review-the-doom-generation/ |website=Pissed Off Geek |access-date=20 May 2023 |date=March 24, 2012 |url-status=usurped |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230520153131/https://www.pissedoffgeek.com/2012/03/24/film-review-the-doom-generation/ |archive-date=May 20, 2023}}</ref> It was later re-released in the United Kingdom in 2013 featuring a commentary track with Gregg Araki, James Duval, Rachel True, and Jordan Ladd.<ref>{{cite web |last=Clark |first=Leigh |title=DVD Review: 'Nowhere' |url=https://cine-vue.com/2013/08/dvd-review-nowhere.html |website=CineVue |access-date=20 May 2023 |date=27 August 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Nowhere: 2013 UK DVD Re-Release |url=https://www.tumblr.com/greggarakinews/59968575255/nowhere-2013-uk-dvd-re-release |website=greggarakinews |access-date=20 May 2023 |via=[[Tumblr]]}}</ref> For Australia, it was released on VHS in late 1997,<ref name="05rat"/> and then received a 2005 DVD release by [[20th Century Fox Home Entertainment|20th Century Fox Home Entertainment South Pacific]].<ref>{{Cite web | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050616050746fw_/http://www.foxmovies.com.au/home_ent/june_library_2005.pdf | title=20th Century Fox home entertainment - June 2005 retail | url=http://www.foxmovies.com.au/home_ent/june_library_2005.pdf | archive-date=2005-06-16}}</ref><ref name="05rat">{{Cite web | url=https://www.classification.gov.au/titles/nowhere-0 | title=NOWHERE | website=www.classification.gov.au}}</ref> In Australia, it garnered an R18+ rating (equivalent to an [[NC-17]] rating in the U.S.) due to a combination of sexual references, drug references and medium level violence. However, in the U.S. itself, the film received a less restrictive rating by the [[Motion Picture Association|MPAA]].<ref name="05rat"/>

[[Strand Releasing]] announced plans to release a [[4K resolution|4K]] restoration of ''Nowhere'', including scenes removed from the theatrical version for [[Motion Picture Association|MPAA]] ratings purposes, in late 2023.<ref name=Strand/> On September 16, 2023, the restoration debuted alongside ''The Doom Generation'' and ''Totally F***ed Up'', Araki's other films in his ''Teen Apocalypse'' trilogy, at the [[Academy Museum of Motion Pictures]] in Los Angeles.<ref name=Strand>{{cite magazine |last=Scorziello |first=Sophia |title=Gregg Araki's 1997 Cult Film 'Nowhere' Gets 4K Restoration (EXCLUSIVE) |url=https://variety.com/2023/film/news/gregg-arakis-nowhere-restoration-1235606540/ |access-date=20 May 2023 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=May 8, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Nowhere |url=https://www.academymuseum.org/en/programs/detail/nowhere-0188ff5e-ccfc-c034-d212-2421bc01b3cc |access-date=10 November 2023 |publisher=[[Academy Museum of Motion Pictures]]}}</ref> On September 24, 2024, [[The Criterion Collection]] released this restored version of the film as part of its ''Gregg Araki's Teen Apocalypse Trilogy'' set.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Gregg Araki's Teen Apocalypse Trilogy |url=https://www.criterion.com/boxsets/7581-gregg-araki-s-teen-apocalypse-trilogy |access-date=17 June 2024 |publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240830103258/https://www.criterion.com/boxsets/7581-gregg-araki-s-teen-apocalypse-trilogy |archive-date=August 30, 2024}}</ref>

In 2015, French fashion house [[Kenzo (brand)|Kenzo]] commissioned a short film from Araki that would feature the label's fall/winter campaign.
