'''''Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers'''''{{efn|Onscreen title is ''Sleepaway Camp 2: Unhappy Campers''}} is a 1988 American [[comedy horror|comedy slasher film]] directed by Michael A. Simpson,  written by Fritz Gordon, and starring [[Pamela Springsteen]] and [[Renée Estevez]]. The second installment in the [[Sleepaway Camp (film series)|''Sleepaway Camp'']] film series, it takes place five years after the events of the [[Sleepaway Camp|original]], and features serial killer Angela Baker, working as a counselor, murdering misbehaving teenagers at another summer camp.

''Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers'' features prominent elements of [[black comedy]] and references various [[horror films|horror]] franchises, such as ''[[Friday the 13th (franchise)|Friday the 13th]]'', ''[[A Nightmare on Elm Street (franchise)|A Nightmare on Elm Street]]'', and ''[[The Texas Chainsaw Massacre (franchise)|The Texas Chainsaw Massacre]]''.{{sfn|Willis|1997|p=461}}

It was followed by a direct sequel, ''[[Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland]]'' (1989), one year later.

==Plot==
Five years following the events of the [[Sleepaway Camp|first film]], Phoebe tells a group of boys at a campfire the story of the murders at Camp Arawak. Her head counselor, Angela Johnson, forces her to go back to the cabin. After the pair gets into an argument, Angela bashes a log over Phoebe’s head and then removes her tongue for having a "filthy mouth".

The next day, campers Molly Nagle, Ally, Mare, Demi, Lea and sisters Brooke and Jodi Shote, question Angela on the whereabouts of Phoebe. She tells them she had to send her home. Later that day, Angela discovers Brooke and Jodi smoking [[Cannabis (drug)|marijuana]] and drinking [[alcohol (drug)|alcohol]]. She initially lets them go only to find them fornicating with a boy named Ralph the next day. Angela chases Ralph off and then burns Jodi to death and pours gasoline over Brooke after she wakes up, before burning her to death as well. That night, the boys start a [[panty raid]], which Angela breaks up. During a jock strap raid against the boys Mare flashes her breasts. Angela decides to drive her home and murders her with an electric drill.

The next night, campers Anthony and Judd try to scare Angela, dressed as [[Freddy Krueger]] and [[Jason Voorhees]]. The plan backfires when Angela, dressed as [[Leatherface]] (wearing Mare's skinned face as a mask), slashes Anthony's throat and murders Judd with a chainsaw. The next day, Angela sets a trap for Ally and forces her down an outhouse latrine pit, drowning her in human waste and leeches. That night, Demi reveals to Angela that she phoned the families of the campers "sent home" and discovered the girls aren't at home. Realizing she could be caught, Angela strangles Demi with a guitar string, before stabbing Lea to death when she finds Demi's body.

The next day, head counselors Uncle John and T.C. fire Angela for "sending too many campers home". Molly and Sean Whitmore go into the woods to cheer her up but the pair discover the bodies of the other campers, before Angela ties them up. After learning the whereabouts of Molly and Sean, T.C. goes after them but Angela throws battery acid in his face, killing him instantly. Sean is decapitated after he realizes Angela is the murderer from the Camp Arawak incident five years earlier. Later, Angela leaves the cabin and Molly frees herself. Upon returning, Molly knocks Angela unconscious, steals her knife and escapes. After an extended chase through the woods, Molly falls onto a rock and is presumed dead. After killing the remaining campers, Angela [[Hitchhiking|hitches]] a ride but the driver quickly annoys her and she kills her. Molly regains consciousness and makes it out of the woods but the truck pulls up behind her. Molly is horrified to learn that Angela is the driver and screams.

==Production==
===Development and filming===
Jerry Silva, who had served as a co-producer on ''[[Sleepaway Camp]]'', approached Double Helix Films about producing a follow-up to the film.<ref name="SleepawayII&IIIProd">{{cite magazine|last=Nicoll|first=Gregory|title=Sleepaway Camp 2 & 3|url=https://archive.org/details/cinefantastique_1970-2002/Cinefantastique%20Vol%2020%20No%203%20%28Jan%201990%29/page/n11/mode/1up?view=theater|date=January 1990|magazine=[[Cinefantastique]]|location=|publisher=Fourth Castle Micromedia|access-date=August 1, 2023}}</ref> [[Michael Hitchcock]], then a staffer at Double Helix, was assigned to write both ''Sleepaway Camp II'' and ''[[Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland]]'' and did so under the pseudonym of Fritz Gordon.<ref name= "SleepawayII&IIIProd"/> Michael A. Simpson, a filmmaker based in [[Atlanta, Georgia]], was hired as director in the fall of 1987 and shot both ''Sleepaway Camp II'' and ''III'' [[Back-to-back film production|back-to-back]] in order to keep the production budgets at $1 million or less.<ref name= "SleepawayII&IIIProd"/> Simpson and Hitchcock felt that the "teen slasher film" genre had become "thematically exhausted" at the time. They decided to push the film into a comedic, self-referential direction, focusing on [[dark humor]].<ref name="SCMSimpson">{{cite web|last1=Hayes|first1=Jeff|last2=Klyza|first2=John|title=Twice Red! The Michael A. Simpson Interview|url=https://returntosleepawaycamp.com/interviews/michael-simpson/|date=1999|publisher=SleepawayCampMovies|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="Armstrong">{{cite book|last1=Armstrong|first1=Richard B.|last2=Armstrong|first2=Mary Willems|title=Encyclopedia of Film Themes, Settings and Series|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=LIIwCgAAQBAJ&pg=PA184|date=2001|page=184|publisher=[[McFarland & Company]]|isbn=9781476612300|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="Two Sequels 1">{{cite AV media|last=Simpson|first=Michael A.|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4asalwDqnE&t=401s|title=A Tale of Two Sequels|date=June 9, 2015|publisher=[[Shout! Factory|Scream Factory]]|website=YouTube|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref> Despite being producers, Stan Wakefield and Silva could not understood or contribute to the production process, leaving the line producing to Simpson.<ref name="SCMSimpson"/> A [[YMCA]] youth camp in [[Waco, Georgia]] was used for the fictional Camp Rolling Hills.<ref name="Two Sequels 2">{{cite AV media|last=Simpson|first=Michael A.|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4asalwDqnE&t=759s|title=A Tale of Two Sequels|date=June 9, 2015|publisher=[[Shout! Factory|Scream Factory]]|website=YouTube|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref> Filming began on September 20 and wrapped on October 9, 1987. Angela's dream sequence was shot to pad out the film to the required 80 minutes.<ref name="Two Sequels 3">{{cite AV media|last=Simpson|first=Michael A.|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4asalwDqnE&t=1444s|title=A Tale of Two Sequels|date=June 9, 2015|publisher=[[Shout! Factory|Scream Factory]]|website=YouTube|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref>

===Casting===
Casting was done in [[Atlanta]] (by Shay Griffin), [[New York City]], and [[Los Angeles]].<ref name="SCMSimpsonOld">{{cite web|last=Klyza|first=John|title=Interview With Michael Simpson|url=http://www.sleepawaycampfilms.com/inter_simpson1.htm|date=2000|publisher=SleepawayCampMovies|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="SCMSimpson"/> [[Felissa Rose]], who had played Angela Baker in the original, was approached by the producers to reprise her role, but as she was busy with her enrollment at [[New York University]] at the time, the role was recast with [[Pamela Springsteen]], the younger sister of rock musician [[Bruce Springsteen|Bruce]].{{sfn|Collum|2004|p=142}} [[Tracy Griffith]] originally auditioned for the role.<ref name="SCMSimpson"/> Connie Craig appeared as a model for Angela in promotional material, as Springsteen was unavailable for the photoshoot. Amy Fields was originally cast as Brooke Shote, but was unable to play the role due to child labor laws, and was cast as Jodi Shote instead, with Carol Chambers playing the role of Brooke.<ref name="SCMFields">{{cite web|last=Hayes|first=Jeff|title=Remember the Shit Sisters! An interview with Amy Fields|url=https://returntosleepawaycamp.com/interviews/amy-fields/|publisher=SleepawayCampMovies|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref> Other actors and actresses cast for the film included [[Renée Estevez]], Anthony Higgins, Valerie Hartman, [[Brian Patrick Clarke]], [[Walter Gotell]], [[Susan Marie Snyder]], Terry Hobbs, Kendall Bean, Julie Murphy, Benji Wilhoite,<ref name="Horror Films">{{cite book|last=Muir|first=John Kenneth|title= Horror Films of the 1980s: Volume 2|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=VJ1vcmaOd7wC&pg=PA699|date=2007|page=699|publisher=[[McFarland & Company]]|isbn=9780786455010|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref> Walter Franks III, Justin Nowell, Heather Binion, Jason Ehrlich,<ref name="Horror Films"/> Carol Martin Vines, Tricia Grant, and Jill Jane Clements. Most of the teenager characters were named after the [[Brat Pack]].<ref name="SCMJohnson">{{cite web|last=Hendsbee|first=James|title=Interview With Bill "Splat" Johnson|url=http://www.sleepawaycampfilms.com/inter_johnson.htm|date=2000|publisher=SleepawayCampMovies|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref> Since some of the actors (like Hobbs and Fields) were underage, representatives of the [[Georgia Department of Labor]] were on set to prohibit the actors from being involved in scenes with sex and nudity, present on set during scenes with graphic kills, or using profanity.<ref name="SCMSimpson"/><ref name="SCMJohnson"/><ref name="SCMFields"/>

===Special effects===
The practical and makeup effects were done by Bill "Splat" Johnson, Christina Cobb and Maritza Rodriguez.<ref name="Fangoria">{{cite magazine|last=Nicoll|first=Gregory|title=Assault of the Sleepaway Camp Sequels|url=https://returntosleepawaycamp.com/sleepaway-camp-2/fangoria/|date=May 1988|pages=48–51, 66|magazine=Fangoria|issue=73|publisher=Fangoria Publishing, LLC|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="SCMJohnson"/><ref name="Rue Morgue">{{cite news|last=Gingold|first=Michael|title="SLEEPAWAY CAMP II & III" soundtrack EP coming, plus updated making-of book; exclusive excerpt and photos|url=https://rue-morgue.com/sleepaway-camp-ii-exclusive-excerpt-and-photos/|date=October 10, 2024|publisher=Rue Morgue|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref>

===Music===
The film's score was composed by [[James Oliverio]].<ref name="Horror Films"/> The song "Outta Control" was written and performed by John Altyn.<ref name="SCMAltyn">{{cite web|last=Klyza|first=John|title=Interview With John Altyn|url=http://sleepawaycampfilms.com/inter_altyn.htm|date=2007|publisher=SleepawayCampMovies|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.discogs.com/release/32155074-John-Altyn-Sleepaway-Camp-II-III|title=John Altyn – Sleepaway Camp II & III (Vinyl, 2024)|date=2024|publisher=Discogs|access-date=December 1, 2025}}</ref><ref name="Rue Morgue"/> The instruments used included a custom-made guitar (initially intended to be played by [[Elliot Easton]] of [[The Cars]]), a [[Rockman (amplifier)|Rockman]] digital effects unit, and [[Slingerland]] drums (played by Johnny "O" Bellon). Backup vocals were performed by Fred Schreck and John Baker. Altyn played most of the other instruments on the track himself.<ref name="SCMAltyn"/>
The song "Desperate To Survive" by Obsession featuring vocalist Mike Vescera can be heard closing out the credits similar to the way that Dokken's "Dream Warriors" song was used in "A Nightmare On Elm St. Pt. 3: Dream Warriors.  ''Sleepaway Camp II'' was released theatrically on a [[limited release|limited basis]]. It had its theatrical premiere in Los Angeles on February 28, 1988,<ref name="Cain">{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-atlanta-journal/177413546/|work=[[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution|The Atlanta Journal]]|date=February 26, 1988|p=89|title='Sleepaway Camp 2', thriller filmed in Atlanta, to have premiere Sunday|last=Cain|first=Scott|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> where it was reported to receive "rave" reviews from audiences.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-atlanta-journal/177413642/|work=[[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution|The Atlanta Journal]]|p=9|last=Cain|first=Scott|date=March 4, 1988|via=Newspapers.com|title=Premiere of 'Sleepaway Camp 2' gets raves from delighted director}}</ref> The film opened regionally in Atlanta on October 28, 1988.<ref name="Atlanta">{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-atlanta-journal/177413666/|work=[[The Atlanta Journal-Constitution|The Atlanta Journal]]|date=October 28, 1988|p=5D|title=Sleepaway Camp 2: Unhappy Campers|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

===Home media===
The film was released on [[VHS]] and [[Betamax]] in the United States by [[Nelson Entertainment]] in late 1988.<ref>{{cite news|work=Paragould Daily Press|date=November 27, 1988|p=27|title='Shakedown' is worth missing; likewise for 'Sleepaway Camp II'|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/paragould-daily-press/177413496/|last=Tuohy|first=Theasa|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> The film was released twice on [[DVD]] in the United States by [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]]: The first release was in 2002 as a single DVD edition,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdempire.com/215635/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-movie.html|title=Sleepaway Camp II (DVD)|website=[[DVDEmpire.com]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231003191354/https://www.dvdempire.com/215635/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-movie.html|archive-date=October 3, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> as well as in the ''Sleepaway Camp Survival Kit''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdempire.com/214456/sleepaway-camp-survival-kit-movie.html|title=Sleepaway Camp Survival Kit (DVD)|website=[[DVDEmpire.com]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231216015330/https://www.dvdempire.com/?aspxerrorpath=/214456/sleepaway-camp-survival-kit-movie.html|archive-date=December 16, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Shout! Factory|Scream Factory]], under license from [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM]], released ''Sleepaway Camp II'' and its sequel, ''Sleepaway Camp III: Teenage Wasteland'', for the first time on [[Blu-ray]] disc on June 9, 2015.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://dreadcentral.com/news/91888/scream-factory-unveils-sleepaway-camp-2-sleepaway-camp-3-artwork/|date=March 3, 2015|title=Scream Factory Unveils Sleepaway Camp 2 and Sleepaway Camp 3 Artwork|last=Barton|first=Steve|website=[[Dread Central]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241203092819/https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/91888/scream-factory-unveils-sleepaway-camp-2-sleepaway-camp-3-artwork/|archive-date=December 3, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref> Sandpiper Pictures reissued a Blu-ray edition on December 17, 2024.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Sleepaway-Camp-II-Unhappy-Campers-Blu-ray/372280/|work=Blu-ray.com|title=Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers Blu-ray|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250426080030/https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Sleepaway-Camp-II-Unhappy-Campers-Blu-ray/372280/|archive-date=April 26, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> A [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K UHD Blu-ray]] limited edition release of the film and its sequel are scheduled for release on August 12, 2025, also by Scream Factory.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://shoutfactory.com/products/sleepaway-camp-ii-iii-deluxe-limited-edition|work=[[Shout! Factory]]|title=Sleepaway Camp II & III [Deluxe Limited Edition]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250619223314/https://shoutfactory.com/products/sleepaway-camp-ii-iii-deluxe-limited-edition|archive-date=June 19, 2025}}</ref>

In the United Kingdom, the film was released via Futuristic Entertainment, under the title ''Nightmare Vacation 2''. There was over two minutes of scenes cut from the film in order for it to receive an "18" certificate by the [[British Board of Film Classification|BBFC]]<ref>{{cite web|title=Sleepaway Camp 2|url=https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/sleepaway-camp-2-q29sbgvjdglvbjpwwc0yoty3mdg|website=[[British Board of Film Classification]]|date=October 6, 1988|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231211210809/https://www.bbfc.co.uk/release/sleepaway-camp-2-q29sbgvjdglvbjpwwc0yoty3mdg|archive-date=December 11, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> as the film was deemed too violent for an uncut release. This was due to the strict regime of the time when film distribution had to adhere to rules under the [[Video Recordings Act 1984]]. The scenes cut were the removal of Phoebe (Heather Binion)'s tongue, a shortened version of Ally (Valerie Hartman) being stabbed in the back, the complete scene where Ally is drowned in the [[outhouse]] is removed, and a shortened version of Diane (Carol Martin Vines) being stabbed in the stomach.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sleepaway Camp II (aka Nightmare Vacation II)|url=https://www.movie-censorship.com/report.php?ID=401653|website=Movie-Censorship.com|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240814112248/https://www.movie-censorship.com/report.php?ID=401653|archive-date=August 14, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref> This version was also released on DVD from 23rd Century Home Entertainment.<ref>{{cite web|title=Nightmare Vacation 2 (Region 2) (AKA Sleepaway Camp 2: Unhappy Campers)|url=https://www.amazon.co.uk/Nightmare-Vacation-Region-Sleepaway-Camp/dp/B001GCQ3CU|website=Amazon|access-date=February 1, 2022}}</ref>

''Sleepaway Camp II'' was eventually released completely uncut in the UK for the first time when it was made available as part of the "Sleepaway Camp Trilogy" DVD box set from [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]] on May 31, 2004.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sleepaway Camp Trilogy [1983] [DVD]|url=https://www.amazon.co.uk/Sleepaway-Camp-Trilogy-Pamela-Springsteen/dp/B0001Z5YWI|website=Amazon.co.uk|date=May 31, 2004|access-date=February 1, 2022}}</ref> It was also available in standard case packaging, both of which are now out of print. The film was released on DVD and [[Blu-ray]] by 88 Films as part of their "Slasher Classics Collection" in April 2016.<ref>{{cite web|title=Sleepaway Camp 2 - Unhappy Campers (Blu-ray) - Slasher Classic Collection 17|url=https://88-films.myshopify.com/products/sleepaway-camp-2-unhappy-campers-blu-ray-slasher-classic-collection-17|website=88 Films|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250409221830/https://88-films.myshopify.com/products/sleepaway-camp-2-unhappy-campers-blu-ray-slasher-classic-collection-17|archive-date=April 9, 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Reception==
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|58|4.9|12||access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/sleepaway_camp_ii_unhappy_campers|title=Sleepaway Camp 2: Unhappy Campers|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=March 30, 2025}}</ref>

Tim Salmons of The Digital Bits said that "[Blu-ray release of the film] is half of one of the most enjoyable high-definition horror experiences of the year".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://thedigitalbits.com/item/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-ce-bd|last=Salmons|first=Tim|title=Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers - Collector's Edition|publisher=The Digital Bits|date=June 11, 2015|accessdate=July 12, 2021}}</ref> Brandon Peters of Why So Blu? wrote: "It's unfortunate that the original negative, master what have you has been lost, but I still think the film looks better than it ever has before".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://whysoblu.com/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-collectors-edition-blu-ray-review/|last=Peters|first=Brandon|title=Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers – Collector's Edition (Blu-ray Review)|work=Why So Blu?|date=May 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241203063830/https://whysoblu.com/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-collectors-edition-blu-ray-review/|archive-date=December 3, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref>

According to William Harrison of ''[[DVD Talk]]'', the film is "purposely not scary", adding that "it, instead, ramps up the self-aware humor and ridiculous death scenes".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/67829/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-collectors-edition/|last=Harrison|first=William|website=[[DVD Talk]]|title=Sleepaway Camp II: Unhappy Campers|date=June 9, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241204203306/https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/67829/sleepaway-camp-ii-unhappy-campers-collectors-edition/|archive-date=December 4, 2024|url-status=live}}</ref>
