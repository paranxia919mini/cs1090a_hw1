'''''The Substance''''' is a 2024 <!--- primary genre per [[WP:FILMGENRE]] --->[[body horror]] film written and directed by [[Coralie Fargeat]]. It follows a fading celebrity ([[Demi Moore]]) who is fired by her producer ([[Dennis Quaid]]) due to her age and uses a black market drug that creates a younger woman ([[Margaret Qualley]]) with unexpected side effects.

Fargeat wrote the screenplay to explore [[Societal attitudes towards women|societal pressures surrounding women's bodies and aging]]. [[Principal photography]] took place in France from August to October 2022. The film is noted for its use of [[satire]] and [[grotesque]], [[Hyperrealism (visual arts)|hyperrealistic]] imagery, accomplished through [[prosthetic makeup]] and other [[practical effects]], including bodysuits, [[puppetry]], dummies, and approximately 21,000 liters (5,500 U.S. gallons) of [[theatrical blood]]. Originally set for distribution by [[Universal Pictures]], the rights were later acquired by [[Mubi (streaming service)|Mubi]] after a dispute between Fargeat and Universal over [[final cut privilege]].

''The Substance'' premiered at the [[2024 Cannes Film Festival|77th Cannes Film Festival]] on May 19, 2024, where it won [[Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Screenplay|Best Screenplay]] and was nominated for the [[Palme d'Or]]. The film was theatrically released in the United Kingdom and the United States on September 20, 2024, and in France on November 6, 2024. It received widespread acclaim, with particular praise for Fargeat's writing and directing, the special effects, and Moore's performance. At the box office, the film grossed $77–82 million on a budget of $18 million, becoming Mubi's highest-grossing film. Moore's performance earned her a [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy|Golden Globe Award]], [[Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress|Critics' Choice Award]], and [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role|Screen Actors Guild Award]], as well as nominations for an [[Academy Award for Best Actress|Academy Award]] and [[BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role|BAFTA Award]]. The film also received nominations for Best Picture and Best Director at the Academy Awards, Critics' Choice and Golden Globes.

==Plot==
On her 50th birthday, faded Hollywood film star Elisabeth Sparkle is dismissed from her long-running aerobics TV show by its producer, Harvey, due to her age. Elisabeth crashes her car while distracted by a billboard of herself being taken down. At the hospital, a young male nurse gives her a flash drive advertising "The Substance", a [[black market]] drug that promises a "younger, more beautiful, more perfect" version of oneself.

Elisabeth orders The Substance and injects the single-use serum. She convulses as a younger woman named Sue emerges from a slit in her back. The two bodies must switch consciousness once every week, with the inactive body remaining unconscious and fed intravenously. Daily injections of stabilizer fluid, extracted from the original body, are required to prevent Sue from deteriorating. Sue becomes an overnight sensation after auditioning as Elisabeth's replacement, and Harvey offers her the chance to host the New Year's Eve show. While Sue lives a confident and hedonistic life, Elisabeth becomes a self-hating [[recluse]]. Near the end of one weekly cycle, Sue brings a man home for casual sex; she delays the switch by extracting additional stabilizer fluid, causing Elisabeth's right index finger to rapidly age. Elisabeth contacts the supplier, who warns that delaying the switching schedule will lead to irreversible, rapid aging of the original body.

Elisabeth and Sue gradually become separate personalities and begin to hate each other. Elisabeth resents Sue's frequent disregard of the switching schedule, causing further aging, while Sue is appalled by Elisabeth's descent into unhealthy [[binge-eating]]. Following a particularly destructive episode, Sue stockpiles stabilizer fluid and refuses to switch back. Three months later, on the day before the New Year's Eve show, Sue runs out of stabilizer fluid and contacts the supplier, who informs her she must switch back to replenish the fluid. When they switch, Elisabeth finds herself transformed into a deformed, hag-like [[hunchback]]. To stop Sue aging her further, Elisabeth orders a serum designed to terminate her. However, Elisabeth, still craving Sue's celebrity status, stops before fully injecting the serum and resuscitates Sue, leaving both of them conscious. Realizing Elisabeth's initial intent, Sue beats Elisabeth to death before leaving to host the show.

Without Elisabeth, and due to most of the serum already being injected, Sue's body rapidly deteriorates. She attempts to create a new version of herself using the leftover serum, despite the single-use warning. This results in the creation of a grotesque mutated body, "Monstro Elisasue", with both Elisabeth's and Sue's faces. Wearing a mask cut from a poster of Elisabeth, Monstro Elisasue attempts to host the show, but the audience erupts into chaos. An audience member decapitates her, only for an even more mutated head to grow back, and one of her arms to break and drench the audience and studio in blood.

Fleeing the studio, Monstro Elisasue collapses and explodes into [[viscera]]. Elisabeth's original face detaches from the gore, crawling onto her neglected star on the [[Hollywood Walk of Fame]]. She smiles as she hallucinates being admired before melting into a puddle of blood, which is cleaned up by a sidewalk scrubber the next day.

In lieu of character backstories, Fargeat used colors, actions, and attire to symbolize character traits — Elisabeth Sparkle's yellow jacket represented a "superhero-like" quality before her transformation, and Sue's pink [[leotard]] represented her femininity.<ref name=VogueInterview/> The character Sue, described as blonde in a 2020 draft of the script,<ref name="2020ScriptDraft">{{cite web |last=Fargeat |first=Coralie |date=23 December 2020 |title=THE SUBSTANCE (2020 movie script, 138 pages) |url=https://scriptshadow.net/movie-review-the-substance/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924010649/https://scriptshadow.net/movie-review-the-substance/ |archive-date=24 September 2024 |publisher=ScriptShadow}}</ref> was given her namesake to subconsciously evoke [[Lolita]] and [[Marilyn Monroe]], "baby-doll"-like [[iconography]], and enduring beauty standards.<ref name=VogueInterview/> Fargeat chose the name Elisabeth for its "iconic resonance" with [[Elizabeth Taylor|stars of Old Hollywood]], and Sparkle because of its literal meaning to "shine and be under the light".{{sfn|Collider|2024|loc=6:25}}

Fargeat conceived the pivotal birth scene, in which Sue emerges from Elisabeth's back, while in the shower.<ref name="HammerToNail">{{Cite web |last=Schenker |first=Jack |date=2024-09-21 |title=A Conversation with Coralie Fargeat (THE SUBSTANCE) |url=https://www.hammertonail.com/interviews/coralie-fargeat/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240921182252/https://www.hammertonail.com/interviews/coralie-fargeat/ |archive-date=21 September 2024 |access-date=2024-10-28 |website=Hammer to Nail}}</ref> It was the first that she wrote,<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?t=803&v=BKcMm5RG2M4&feature=youtu.be |title=THE SUBSTANCE {{!}} Q&A with Coralie Fargeat & Guillermo del Toro |date=2024-11-28 |last=MUBI |access-date=2024-12-07 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241226220717/https://www.youtube.com/watch?t=803&v=BKcMm5RG2M4&feature=youtu.be |archive-date=26 December 2024 |via=YouTube}}</ref> and in her view, "the most important scene of the film". She recalls, "I didn't even know who my character would be. It's the first one that really came to my mind, and it holds the core DNA of the movie as a true visceral experience with no words, making you feel what the characters are going to feel".<ref name=Ercolani/> Fargeat later decided that the main character should be an actress to explore societal perceptions of bodies.{{sfn|Rue Morgue TV|2024|loc=6:03}} She chose to have Elisabeth Sparkle host an aerobics class,{{sfn|Rue Morgue TV|2024|loc=6:41}} inspired by [[Jane Fonda]]'s transition from a successful actress to her second career [[Jane Fonda's Workout|starring in exercise tapes]].{{sfn|Rue Morgue TV|2024|loc=7:27}}

Fargeat listened to a variety of music to influence the screenplay. She cited [[Mica Levi]]'s [[Under the Skin (soundtrack)|score]] for ''[[Under the Skin (2013 film)|Under the Skin]]'' (2013), and other [[experimental music]] and composers that had "this kind of heartbeat or pulsation{{nbsp}}... related to the heartbeat of the new human being or the way you can feel with your body".<ref name="RogerEbert">{{Cite web |last=Gates |first=Marya E. |date=2024-10-02 |title=Female Filmmakers in Focus: Coralie Fargeat on "The Substance" |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/female-filmmakers-in-focus-coralie-fargeat-on-the-substance |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241002173816/https://www.rogerebert.com/interviews/female-filmmakers-in-focus-coralie-fargeat-on-the-substance |archive-date=2 October 2024 |access-date=2024-10-28 |website=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> Fargeat also listened to hypersexualized music to influence the in-universe ''Pump It Up'' show.<ref name="RogerEbert"/>

Fargeat knew that casting would be challenging, as the film features minimal dialogue and relies heavily on the characters. Elisabeth Sparkle needed to be cast first, given that she was the center of the film's narrative.<ref name=HammerToNail/> Fargeat wanted to cast an actress who had been at the heart of the [[Star system (filmmaking)|star system]],<ref name="TroisCouleurs">{{cite magazine |last1=Leroy |first1=Joséphine |last2=Reitzer |first2=Juliette |date=19 Nov 2024 |title=The Substance (article in print) |url=https://www.troiscouleurs.fr/le-magazine/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241207022451/https://www.troiscouleurs.fr/le-magazine/ |archive-date=7 December 2024 |access-date=7 Dec 2024 |magazine=[[Trois couleurs (magazine)|Trois couleurs]] |publisher=Trois couleurs (edited by MK2) |location=Paris, France |page=45 |issue=210}}</ref> a real-life icon.<ref name=HammerToNail/>{{sfn|MUBI Podcast|2024|loc=8:41}} Fargeat did not include Demi Moore on the initial list of potential lead actresses, stating 'She will never do it';<ref name=HollywoodReporterInterview/>{{refn|group=note|In another interview, Fargeat similarly claimed that Moore would not be willing to "destroy her image" by accepting the film.{{sfn|Santa Barbara International Film Festival|2024|loc=39:31}}}} several actresses were considered but declined the role before Moore.<ref name="OuestFrance">{{cite news |last=Escolano |first=Véronique |date=5 November 2024 |title=Coralie Fargeat: "Dans The substance, Demi Moore a pris tous les risques" |url=https://www.ouest-france.fr/cinema/the-substance-du-gore-feministe-avec-demi-moore-6e3147ec-96ea-11ef-81c3-38013e2f80e5 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241106031828/https://www.ouest-france.fr/cinema/the-substance-du-gore-feministe-avec-demi-moore-6e3147ec-96ea-11ef-81c3-38013e2f80e5 |archive-date=6 November 2024 |access-date=16 Nov 2024 |website=[[Ouest-France]]}}</ref> Eventually, with nothing to lose, Moore was sent the script.{{sfn|MUBI Podcast|2024|loc=9:32}}

At her agent's insistence, Moore read the script before knowing specific details, later speculating that this was due to the film's sensitive subject of aging.<ref name=NYTQualley/>{{sfn|Awards Chatter|2024|loc=1:00:39}}<ref name="THR-Awards-Chatter">{{cite web |last=Feinberg |first=Scott |date=2024-12-03 |title='Awards Chatter' Pod: Demi Moore on 'The Substance', Aging in Hollywood and Stardom in the '80s and '90s |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/demi-moore-the-substance-aging-hollywood-awards-chatter-1236074855/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241203010104/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/demi-moore-the-substance-aging-hollywood-awards-chatter-1236074855/ |archive-date=3 December 2024 |access-date=2025-02-07 |website=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref> She was impressed by the script and its subject matter,<ref name=NYTQualley>{{cite news |title=Margaret Qualley Is Getting the Hang of Being a Movie Star |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=2024-09-14 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/14/movies/margaret-qualley-substance.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241010032956/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/14/movies/margaret-qualley-substance.html |archive-date=2024-10-10 |access-date=2024-11-17}}</ref><ref name="THR-Awards-Chatter" />{{sfn|Awards Chatter|2024|loc=1:01:24}} though she was unfamiliar with the body horror genre.<ref name=HollywoodReporterInterview/> Moore felt that the film could either "really work and be part of a cultural shift" or "be a fucking disaster".<ref name=HollywoodReporterInterview/> She remained unsure of the audience reaction up until the premiere when she knew the film had worked.{{sfn|Black Film and TV|2024|loc=2:22}}

Surprised by Moore's positive response,<ref name=TroisCouleurs/> Fargeat read [[Inside Out (Moore book)|her autobiography]] and was struck by her resilience.<ref name=RueMorgue/><ref name=TroisCouleurs/> "I read her autobiography, and she had some tough years in her personal life. [...] She made herself on her own [...] in a place that was a totally male-dominated industry, being ahead of her time in many regards, like doing this [[More Demi Moore|naked picture of her pregnant]], taking a lot of risks and having a lot of feminist statements, wanting to be paid as much as her co-stars".<ref name=ThompsonFrenchFilmmaker/> Fargeat had previously worked as a trainee assistant director{{refn|group=note|[[Assistant director#Sub-roles|Second second assistant director]], as credited in the film's credits.}} on the Moore-led film ''[[Passion of Mind]]'' (2000), handling tasks like making copies and bringing her coffee in the mornings.<ref name=ThompsonFrenchFilmmaker/>

Before being offered the role, Moore discussed the film with Fargeat over six meetings.{{sfn|TheMovieReport.com|2024|loc=3:52}}{{sfn|Next Best Picture|2024b|loc=5:11}}{{sfn|Black Film and TV|2024|loc=8:33}} Fargeat explained the film in detail:<ref>{{cite web |date=2024-11-05 |title=Coralie Fargeat : The Substance ou le cri de rage contre le jeunisme hollywoodien |url=https://www.lejdd.fr/culture/coralie-fargeat-substance-ou-le-cri-de-rage-contre-le-jeunisme-hollywoodien-151333 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241105064645/https://www.lejdd.fr/culture/coralie-fargeat-substance-ou-le-cri-de-rage-contre-le-jeunisme-hollywoodien-151333 |archive-date=5 November 2024 |access-date=2024-11-17 |website=lejdd.fr |language=fr}}</ref> the film's extensive prep work, prosthetics, meager resources, shooting location and the level of nudity, which she felt was foundational to the story.<ref name=OuestFrance/>

Moore understood the [[Metafiction|meta]]-nature<ref name=LATimesThereWillBeBlood/> of the role but did not feel that she was the character, as Elisabeth had no family. As she further explained, "she's dedicated her entire life to her career, and when that's taken, what does she have?" However, Moore sympathized with the character's pain.<ref name=NYTMoore>{{cite news |last=Garcia-Navarro |first=Lulu |date=14 Sep 2024 |title=The Interview: Demi Moore Is Done With the Male Gaze |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/14/magazine/demi-moore-interview.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241010013132/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/14/magazine/demi-moore-interview.html |archive-date=10 Oct 2024 |access-date=16 Nov 2024 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> She recognized that the characters were deeply important to Fargeat, and saw them as stand-ins for the director herself: Elisabeth represents Fargeat, while Qualley's Sue is the girl from the '90s that Fargeat always felt pitted against.{{sfn|Black Film and TV|2024|loc=8:15}} Moore would later reflect positively on her role, saying, "What I love is this was a rich, complex, demanding role that gave me an opportunity to really push myself outside of my comfort zone, and in the end to feel like I explored and grew not only as an actor, but as a person".<ref>{{cite web |last=Lattanzio |first=Ryan |date=2024-05-22 |title=The Demi Moore Comeback Is Here with 'The Substance': 'This Was a Part of Waking Myself Up' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/demi-moore-interview-the-substance-cannes-career-comeback-1235007894/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240522165815/https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/demi-moore-interview-the-substance-cannes-career-comeback-1235007894/ |archive-date=22 May 2024 |access-date=2024-11-17 |website=[[IndieWire]]}}</ref>

While talking to Moore, Fargeat thought about potential pairings; later, when she met with [[Margaret Qualley]], she felt they had a common energy. Fargeat liked that Qualley had a background as a dancer.<ref name=HammerToNail/> Moore had prior indirect ties with Qualley and worked with Qualley's mother [[Andie MacDowell]] in ''[[St. Elmo's Fire (film)|St. Elmo's Fire]]'' (1985), while Qualley knew Moore's daughters.{{sfn|Variety Studio|2024|loc=5:27}}

Qualley was in Panama, shooting [[Claire Denis]]'s ''[[Stars at Noon (2022 film)|Stars at Noon]]'' (2022), when she read the script and was drawn to the prospect of playing a character who seemed "really far from [her]" and she had a feeling that it was going to be "special".<ref name=NYTQualley/> During prep, Fargeat emphasized the physicality of the roles (both actors' bodies would be central to their performances). For Qualley, this meant [[Weightlifting|lifting weights]] for months to achieve Sue's [[sex symbol]] figure.<ref name=LATimesThereWillBeBlood/><ref name="RueMorgue">{{cite web |last=Gingold |first=Michael |date=2024-09-18 |title=Exclusive Interview: Coralie Fargeat unleashes "THE SUBSTANCE", the year's best and most extreme horror film |url=https://rue-morgue.com/exclusive-interview-coralie-fargeat-unleashes-the-substance-the-years-best-and-most-extreme-horror-film/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240919051831/https://rue-morgue.com/exclusive-interview-coralie-fargeat-unleashes-the-substance-the-years-best-and-most-extreme-horror-film/ |archive-date=19 September 2024 |access-date=2024-11-17 |website=Rue Morgue}}</ref> Qualley spent a lot of time walking around her apartment practicing her character, "freaking my husband out".{{sfn|Collider|2024|loc=5:17}}

[[Ray Liotta]] was originally cast as Harvey,<ref>{{cite web |last=Hamman |first=Cody |date=16 February 2022 |title=The Substance: Ray Liotta joins Demi Moore, Margaret Qualley in body horror film |url=https://www.joblo.com/the-substance-ray-liotta/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220216163840/https://www.joblo.com/the-substance-ray-liotta/ |archive-date=16 February 2022 |access-date=14 April 2022 |website=[[JoBlo.com]]}}</ref> but died in May 2022. Three months into filming, Liotta was replaced by [[Dennis Quaid]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Shafer |first=Ellise |date=19 May 2024 |title='The Substance' Director Coralie Fargeat on How Her Feminist Body Horror Film With Demi Moore and Margaret Qualley Mirrors #MeToo: 'We Need a Bigger Revolution' |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/festivals/the-substance-coralie-fargeat-feminist-body-horror-demi-moore-margaret-qualley-metoo-1236009167/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240520003709/https://variety.com/2024/film/festivals/the-substance-coralie-fargeat-feminist-body-horror-demi-moore-margaret-qualley-metoo-1236009167/ |archive-date=20 May 2024 |access-date=16 June 2024 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref>

On set, Fargeat read the dialogue for the Substance Voice to provide a [[temp track|temporary track]]. After a lengthy casting process, she chose American actor Yann Bean, who was living in Paris, to voice it. Fargeat wanted a voice with devil-like, tempting, and powerful qualities.<ref>{{cite web |last=Eisenberg |first=Eric |date=2024-10-31 |title='It's The Devil': The Substance Director Discusses The Long Casting Process For That Creepy, Deep Voice In The Body Horror Movie |url=https://www.cinemablend.com/interviews/its-the-devil-the-substance-director-discusses-long-casting-process-creepy-deep-voice-body-horror-movie |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241202123650/https://www.cinemablend.com/interviews/its-the-devil-the-substance-director-discusses-long-casting-process-creepy-deep-voice-body-horror-movie |archive-date=2 December 2024 |access-date=2024-11-17 |website=CinemaBlend}}</ref>

===Filming===
[[File:Camera used on set for The Substance.jpeg|thumb|right|150px|[[Arri Alexa#Alexa LF|Alexa LF]] camera used by cinematographer [[Benjamin Kračun]]]]

{{external media|video1={{YouTube|id=8H64HNvXrqU|title=Official [[behind-the-scenes]] [[featurette]] released by MUBI}}, January 7, 2025 (video length: 28:15)}}

[[Principal photography]] took place entirely in France, with an all-French [[film crew]] except for cinematographer [[Benjamin Kračun]] and composer [[Raffertie]] (both from the United Kingdom).<ref name="CNC">{{Cite web |title=Behind the Scenes of "The Substance" by Coralie Fargeat |url=https://www.cnc.fr/web/en/news/behind-the-scenes-of-the-substance-by-coralie-fargeat_2259840 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240925213116/https://www.cnc.fr/web/en/news/behind-the-scenes-of-the-substance-by-coralie-fargeat_2259840 |archive-date=25 September 2024 |access-date=12 October 2024 |publisher=[[Centre national du cinéma et de l'image animée|CNC]]}}</ref> Studio scenes were filmed at [[Epinay Studios]] in [[Seine-Saint-Denis]], Île-de-France near Paris—the historic studio where [[Jean Cocteau]] shot ''[[Beauty and the Beast (1946 film)|Beauty and the Beast]]'' (1946). Exterior scenes doubling for Los Angeles were filmed on the [[Côte d'Azur]],<ref name=CNC/> including locations in [[Antibes]] such as the Anthéa Theatre, which doubled for a hospital, and the [[Marineland of Antibes|Marineland]] parking lot.<ref name="FranceTVInfoFilmLocations">{{Cite web |date=2024-06-11 |title="The Substance", le film tourné sur la Côte d'Azur, avec Demi Moore sort ce mercredi en France |url=https://france3-regions.francetvinfo.fr/provence-alpes-cote-d-azur/alpes-maritimes/cannes/the-substance-le-film-tourne-sur-la-cote-d-azur-avec-demi-moore-sort-ce-mercredi-en-france-3055714.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241120051714/https://france3-regions.francetvinfo.fr/provence-alpes-cote-d-azur/alpes-maritimes/cannes/the-substance-le-film-tourne-sur-la-cote-d-azur-avec-demi-moore-sort-ce-mercredi-en-france-3055714.html |archive-date=20 November 2024 |access-date=2024-12-07 |website=France 3 Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur |language=fr-FR}}</ref> Palm trees were filmed in [[Cannes]]<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?t=27&v=DBiPdgIZt1g&feature=youtu.be |title=In Conversation with THE SUBSTANCE Filmmaker Coralie Fargeat |date=2024-11-11 |last=American Film Institute |access-date=2024-12-07 |via=YouTube}}</ref> with additional scenes shot in [[Nice]],<ref name="BritishCinematographer" /> Carros and Saint-Laurent-du-Var.<ref name=FranceTVInfoFilmLocations/> France was selected to accommodate the film's extensive shooting schedule due to [[practical effect]]s work, with the country's 40% Tax Rebate for International Productions (TRIP) providing an additional incentive.<ref name=CNC/>

Fargeat selected Kračun as cinematographer after being impressed by his work on ''[[Promising Young Woman]]'' (2020).{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=18:50}} He shot the film primarily with the [[Arri Alexa#Alexa LF|Alexa LF]], for its accurate capture of skin tones,<ref name=TheASC/> and used vintage, spherical [[Canon Inc.|Canon]] K35 lenses to accommodate the large number of [[close-up]]s in the film.{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=48:25}} Other lenses included [[Angénieux]] Optimo zooms, [[Leica Camera|Leitz]] Thalia, macros from [[Cooke Optics]] and Arri and a Leitz M 0.8 for the body-camera work.<ref name=TheASC/> [[Red Digital Cinema#DSMC3 System|Red V-Raptor and Komodo]] were also used: the former for [[visual effects]], with its high resolution [[8K resolution|8K]] sensor, and the latter for the body, requiring a [[helmet camera|helmet rig]].<ref name=TheASC/>

The film was shot in continuity when possible, adopting what the crew called a "lab shoot" approach in its final month. The "lab shoot" was used to capture [[Insert (filmmaking)|insert shots]],{{sfn|French Society of Cinematographers|2024|loc=5:09}} typically assigned to a second unit{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=26:07}} with a reduced crew.<ref name=Ercolani/> The most time-consuming prosthetics shots were filmed during this time period,{{sfn|The Horror Flicks and Guitar Picks Podcast|2024|loc=30:15}} including close-ups of injections and a back splitting open.<ref name="BritishCinematographer">{{Cite web |last=Williams |first=Tom |date=2024-09-20 |title=Benjamin Kračun BSC / The Substance |url=https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/benjamin-kracun-bsc-the-substance/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241201092447/https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/benjamin-kracun-bsc-the-substance/ |archive-date=1 December 2024 |access-date=2024-11-05 |website=British Cinematographer}}</ref> Fargeat [[storyboard]]ed all the prosthetics and birth sections before production began, focusing on the birth scene first during pre-production.{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=39:20}} This helped estimate financial costs for the prosthetic dummies, how many to build, and the extent of their body details.{{sfn|Directors UK Podcast|2024|loc=49:06}} The birth sequence took 15 days to film.{{sfn|Directors UK Podcast|2024|loc=48:20}} Fargeat even went so far as to perform an actual syringe injection of the activator on her own arm, doubling for Demi Moore in the shot.<ref name=BritishCinematographer/> Crew sizes fluctuated significantly throughout production, ranging from just 6 members for the lab shoot,<ref name=BritishCinematographer/> 8 for [[Establishing shot|exterior scenes]], and over 200 for complex practical effects sequences in the studio.<ref name=CNC/>

Kračun initially considered using a safety wire to drop a camera past a series of lights for the infinity tunnel sequence.<ref name=TheASC/> Ultimately, he devised a simpler solution: two horizontal bicycle tires surrounded by [[LED tube]] lights, spinning together at the same speed around a stationary camera to create an infinity tunnel effect.<ref name=TheASC/><ref name=BritishCinematographer/> He decided to film the fire in the shape of a dragon on the studio floor practically, capturing it from above. The footage was then blended into the scene where Sue is seen by the window.<ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/>

For his lunch scene with Demi Moore, Quaid consumed approximately {{convert|2|kg|lb}} of [[Shrimp and prawn as food|shrimp]].{{refn|group=note|Fargeat puts the estimate at 2 kilograms in an interview,{{sfn|Collider|2024|loc=11:08}} while Moore states it was about {{convert|4|lb|kg}}.{{sfn|Late Night with Seth Meyers|2024|loc=5:30}}}}

Margaret Qualley lightheartedly described learning the choreography for ''The Substance'' as a "nightmare" and was overwhelmed by performing with professional dancers who had already memorized the moves which she was new to. Although trained as a ballet dancer,<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=2014-07-27 |title=An Interview with Actress Margaret Qualley of The Leftovers |url=http://www.vogue.com/culture/article/margaret-qualley-of-the-leftovers-hbo/#1 |magazine=[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]] |access-date=2024-11-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140727034013/http://www.vogue.com/culture/article/margaret-qualley-of-the-leftovers-hbo/#1 |archive-date=27 July 2014}}</ref> she explained that "that specific kind of sexuality doesn't lend itself to [me]"<ref name="QualleyCelebrity">{{Cite news |last=Pullman |first=Laura |date=2024-09-14 |title=Margaret Qualley: My mum, Andie MacDowell, has never judged me |url=https://www.thetimes.com/life-style/celebrity/article/margaret-qualley-interview-mum-andie-macdowell-hphvktcrq |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240915032348/https://www.thetimes.com/life-style/celebrity/article/margaret-qualley-interview-mum-andie-macdowell-hphvktcrq |archive-date=15 September 2024 |access-date=2024-11-05 |newspaper=[[The Times]]}}</ref> and that she'd "never [do it] again".{{sfn|Toronto International Film Festival|2024|loc=6:45}} Qualley began the rehearsal with Fargeat present but left the set to go to the bathroom and cry. Fargeat decided to leave the rehearsal as well; Qualley instead chose to learn the moves in a private lesson, allowing her to practice in her hotel room and build confidence as she felt deeply ashamed by the whole series of events.<ref name=QualleyCelebrity/> Nonetheless, on the day of the shoot, her anxiety led her to get "[[Substance intoxication|wasted]] first thing in the morning" by smoking [[Cannabis (drug)|cannabis]] and drinking [[tequila]].<ref name=QualleyCelebrity/> In a live Q&A after the film's release, Qualley expressed happiness in getting to perform the dance, as many previous scenes had been slow-paced and required minimal movement or expression, making the dance sequence a welcome change.{{sfn|Black Film and TV|2024|loc=12:39}}

Moore found Fargeat to be a "very visual director" with a focus on [[Artistic symbol|symbolism]].{{sfn|Black Film and TV|2024|loc=20:10}} While Moore was accustomed to starting scenes with wide shots to establish the scene's space, Fargeat instead began with close-ups. During a one-week break while only Qualley was working, Moore contracted [[shingles]] and lost {{convert|20|lbs}} during the production.<ref name=LATimesThereWillBeBlood/>

However, after Kračun filmed test shots of palm trees early on during filming, Fargeat realized she could use these shots as tableaux, and eliminate extensive exterior shots. Ultimately, the only part of the film actually shot in Los Angeles was the still backdrop (photographed by Rosco Digital) in [[Laurel Canyon, Los Angeles|the Canyons]].{{sfn|French Society of Cinematographers|2024|loc=8:35}}

''The Substance'' required a three-month construction period to build the [[Set (film and TV scenery)|sets]], including Elisabeth's apartment with distinct spaces like the bathroom and secret room as well as the New Year's Eve theater and a TV studio hallway.<ref name=TheASC/> The central feature of the apartment set was the large panoramic window, symbolizing Elisabeth's past and, later, Sue's rebirth and future. Fargeat envisioned the apartment with a "timeless, old-fashioned but also futuristic quality", allowing it to transcend specific eras and enrich the story with symbolism.<ref name="VogueInterview">{{Cite web |last=Suh |first=Elissa |date=2024-09-18 |title='The Movie Is Fundamentally About the Violence of Control': Writer-Director Coralie Fargeat Talks 'The Substance' |url=https://www.vogue.com/article/coralie-fargeat-the-substance-interview |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240918144729/https://www.vogue.com/article/coralie-fargeat-the-substance-interview |archive-date=18 September 2024 |access-date=2024-11-05 |website=[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]]}}</ref> Initially, Fargeat and Kračun considered [[StageCraft|LED-screen technology]] from Darkmatters{{sfn|Camera & Light Magazine|2024|loc=5:34}} for the window's scenic Los Angeles cityscape, but Kračun determined it was costly and technically challenging, involving nine technicians to operate. He additionally felt that LEDs could not achieve Fargeat's vision of [[Hard and soft light|hard sunlight]] for Los Angeles.{{sfn|Camera & Light Magazine|2024|loc=8:05}} Instead, they opted for a 115&nbsp;ft x 42&nbsp;ft Rosco SoftDrop [[Theater drapes and stage curtains#Backdrops|backdrop]],<ref name="IndieWireCinematicMetamorphosis">{{Cite web |last=O'Falt |first=Chris |date=2024-12-20 |title='The Substance': The Cinematic Metamorphosis of Elisabeth Sparkle |url=https://www.indiewire.com/deep-dive/the-substance-behind-the-scenes-production-1235076205/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241220165702/https://www.indiewire.com/deep-dive/the-substance-behind-the-scenes-production-1235076205/ |archive-date=20 December 2024 |access-date=2025-01-31 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US}}</ref> evoking a romantic, [[Hitchcockian]] quality;{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=33:12}} Kračun described the overall look of the film as "pink noir".{{sfn|French Society of Cinematographers|2024|loc=4:20}} Fargeat expressed great satisfaction seeing the practical set for the first time as she had anticipated shooting it on [[Chroma key|greenscreen]].<ref name="VolcanoOfBlood">{{Cite magazine |last=Canfield |first=David |date=2024-10-24 |title=How Do You Make 'The Substance'? Start With "a Volcano of Blood" |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/the-substance-shot-list-coralie-fargeat-awards-insider |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241031172545/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/the-substance-shot-list-coralie-fargeat-awards-insider |archive-date=31 October 2024 |access-date=2024-11-05 |magazine=Vanity Fair}}</ref>

Fargeat wanted the bathroom set to function as a metaphorical "cocoon" and envisioned it as a mental space that felt abstract, stylized, and empty. She pushed back against the [[production designer]] who wanted a more realistic look and who asked: "Are you sure you don't want any furniture in the bathroom at all?"<ref name=VogueInterview/> Kračun wanted [[Sconce (light fixture)|sconces]] to help modify the lighting but Fargeat ultimately rejected this idea, and the lighting was kept harsh.{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=35:39}}

During the birth scene's [[point-of-view shot]], two identical bathroom sets were built to simulate a mirror: Qualley acted in one, while Kračun (wearing a head-mounted camera) and a body double mimicked her movements in the other.<ref name="TheASC"/><ref name="BritishCinematographer"/> The hand movements on the mirror were later reshot in post-production in front of a green screen due to difficulties syncing the movements.{{sfn|Directors UK Podcast|2024|loc=45:13}} For some shots, an actual mirror was placed between the two bathrooms to capture Qualley's reflection.{{sfn|Directors UK Podcast|2024|loc=45:25}} The scene with Moore lying on the floor and striking her head, while the camera [[Camera dolly|booms up]], was shot fully in camera and achieved by constructing a shower three times taller than standard height.{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=42:54}}

After the production finished shooting on the apartment set, it was destroyed to build the theater in the same space, being "basically [...] built on the ashes of the apartment" according to Fargeat.<ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/> Initially, there were plans to shoot in a real theater that was going to be refurbished completely,{{sfn|The Cinematography Podcast|2024|loc=44:48}} but the crew discovered that the venue, while initially welcoming at the idea of shooting a small splattering of blood, became apprehensive upon realizing the extent of the blood effects. As one producer remarked, "Okay, I don't want to finish in jail. We can't shoot in a real theater, because there is no way we can protect it in a way that it's not going to be destroyed".<ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/>

===Costumes===
[[File:La Reine Margot 1994 film poster.png|thumb|upright|The poster for ''[[La Reine Margot (1994 film)|La Reine Margot]]'' (1994) was cited among the influences for the costume design.<ref name="BackRow"/>]]

[[Costume designer]] Emmanuelle Youchnovski was hired on the film after reading the script and creating a [[Pinterest]] board to show Fargeat.<ref name="BackRow">{{Cite web |last=Odell |first=Amy |date=2025-01-22 |title=How 'The Substance' Dressed Its Monster |url=https://amyodell.substack.com/p/how-the-substance-dressed-its-monster |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250122190414/https://amyodell.substack.com/p/how-the-substance-dressed-its-monster |archive-date=22 January 2025 |access-date=2025-02-04 |website=Back Row |via=Substack}}</ref> She drew inspiration from fashion photos and the poster for the film ''[[La Reine Margot (1994 film)|Queen Margot]]'', in particular for the Monstro finale.<ref name="BackRow"/>

Youchnovski developed contrasting looks for Elisabeth and Sue.<ref name="BackRow"/> Elisabeth's design emphasized masculinity, using suits with minimal skin exposure, shiny fabrics, and primary colors like blue, red, and black.<ref name="BackRow"/> For Elisabeth's yellow coat, Youchnovski purchased 20 options; unhappy with their fit and color, finding them too mustard or too green, she sketched a design and she and her team made the coat using fabric sourced from London in a month.<ref name="BackRow"/> The yellow coat matched sunshine and egg hues found in the film, and enveloped Elisabeth "like armor" with its wide shoulders and large pockets.<ref name="BackRow"/> During preproduction, Moore initially questioned the exaggerated choice, noting that no one in Los Angeles would wear a heavy coat in summer,{{sfn|Santa Barbara International Film Festival|2024|loc=13:10}} but Fargeat said that it was essential to her character.{{sfn|Santa Barbara International Film Festival|2024|loc=13:10}} After production ended, Moore took the yellow jacket as a keepsake.<ref name="VanityFairKeepingCoat">{{Cite magazine |last=Canfield |first=David |date=2024-11-12 |title=Demi Moore on 'The Substance' Mania, "Scary" Oscar Buzz, and Keeping That Yellow Coat |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/demi-moore-the-substance-little-gold-men-awards-insider |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241112162524/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/demi-moore-the-substance-little-gold-men-awards-insider |archive-date=12 November 2024 |access-date=2025-02-04 |magazine=Vanity Fair |language=en-US}}</ref>

Sue's wardrobe emphasized femininity, exposing her body, focusing on clichés associated with the [[male gaze]], including pink metallic colors, black [[Christian Louboutin|Louboutin]] boots and a tennis skirt.<ref name="BackRow"/> Her pink leotard was inspired by [[Beyoncé]] in the music video for "[[Blow (Beyoncé song)|Blow]]" and an outfit worn by [[Dua Lipa]] during her concerts.<ref name="BackRow"/><ref name="IndieWireNightmares">{{Cite web |last=Bergeson |first=Samantha |date=2025-01-17 |title=Margaret Qualley Still Has 'Nightmares' About Filming Gory 'The Substance' Scene: It Will 'Stay with Me Forever' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/margaret-qualley-the-substance-interview-1235086132/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250117212110/https://www.indiewire.com/features/interviews/margaret-qualley-the-substance-interview-1235086132/ |archive-date=17 January 2025 |access-date=2025-02-04 |website=IndieWire |language=en-US}}</ref> Sue's clothing becomes darker as the film progresses{{sfn|Santa Barbara International Film Festival|2024|loc=11:55}} and was designed thematically to represent danger<ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/> and snake-like qualities.<ref name="BackRow"/> Her custom-made [[bomber jacket]] features the letter "S" on the back for "Sue" and for "Snake"<ref name="BackRow"/> and her [[catsuit]] as a second skin.{{sfn|Santa Barbara International Film Festival|2024|loc=12:30}} Youchnovski had to redo the snakeskin pattern on the catsuit as the initial attempt wasn't shiny enough.<ref name="BackRow"/> Sue's dragon robe, representing rebirth and domination,<ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/> required the most work, with 10,000 [[sequin]]s attached by hand over 2 months.<ref name="BackRow"/>

For Monstro's dress, Youchnovski first designed the version for Monstro, then adapted it for Qualley, reversing her usual process since she didn't have access to Qualley in the prosthetics.<ref name="BackRow"/> She surveyed the prosthetic in London and fitted the costume to a mockup of Monstro, struggling with the design of the top due to Monstro's number of breasts.<ref name="BackRow"/>

Youchnovski dressed Harvey in colorful [[Etro]] and [[Dolce & Gabbana]] suits.<ref name="BackRow"/> She explained: "I wanted his style to be very strange. Like what rich people wear."<ref name="BackRow"/>

Youchnovski emphasized the motif of the [[spinal column]] in the costumes.<ref name="BackRow"/><ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/> Elisabeth appears at the beginning of the film, before taking the substance, with an open-back dress.<ref name="BackRow"/> After taking the substance, she appears in a red [[Balmain (fashion house)|Balmain]] dress with a zipper, while Sue mirrors this design choice by donning a catsuit, also featuring a zipper.<ref name="BackRow"/><ref name=VolcanoOfBlood/> Moore and Qualley had approximately three fittings each, with Youchnovski focusing on ensuring that the leotard was comfortable and the character's silhouette was clearly defined.<ref name="BackRow"/> She would then sketch the look, adjusting color or other details as needed.<ref name="BackRow"/>

===Post-production===
{{external media|video1={{YouTube|id=kaDVhwacasI|title=Official VFX breakdown}}, April 4, 2025 (video length: 4:01)}}

Fargeat described post-production as the most challenging period of the production,{{sfn|Academia de Cine|2024|loc=58:25}} taking a year and a half.{{sfn|Soundtracking with Edith Bowman|2024|loc=33:43}}<ref name=LePointCoulisses/> With over 300 hours of [[dailies]],{{sfn|Blue Collar Post Collective|2024|loc=27:19}} one of the main challenges was finding the right pacing for scenes with little dialogue.{{sfn|Academia de Cine|2024|loc=58:31, 59:41, and 1:00:29}} Scenes were frequently changed based on the interaction of the music, visual effects and sound design,{{sfn|Academia de Cine|2024|loc=58:52}} with post-production finishing just three days before the premiere.<ref name=LePointCoulisses/> Visual effects by NOID Studio took one year to complete.<ref name="DefinitionMagazine">{{Cite web |title=The Substance |url=https://definitionmagazine.com/features/the-substance/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241206025503/https://definitionmagazine.com/features/the-substance/ |archive-date=6 December 2024 |access-date=2024-12-07 |website=Definition}}</ref>

The film was [[Film editing|edited]] by Jérôme Eltabet,{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=0:12}} Fargeat, and Valentin Feron. Eltabet had previously collaborated with Fargeat on ''Revenge'' and taught her editing while working her children's show ''Les Fées Cloches''{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=4:42}}{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=0:24}}{{sfn|Blue Collar Post Collective|2024|loc=30:42}} He began editing midway through the shoot, approximately three months in{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=18:19}} and independently created the film's initial 3.5 hour [[rough cut]],{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=18:19}} which did not include many of the inserts or closeups.{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=4:16}} After filming wrapped, Eltabet and Fargeat collaborated over six months{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=4:18}} bringing the runtime to 4.5 hours.{{sfn|Blue Collar Post Collective|2024|loc=27:40}} Eltabet and Fargeat collaborated by editing separate sequences in different rooms using [[Adobe Premiere Pro]],{{sfn|Blue Collar Post Collective|2024|loc=30:12}} then swapping the sequences between each other for further refinement.{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=2:32}} Due to the film's numerous iterations with some sequences having between 150 and 200 different versions,{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=36:10}}{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=3:10}} Feron was later brought in to give a new, outside perspective to the film.{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=36:22}} Several scenes with Elisabeth's agent were shot but ultimately not included as they were not deemed compelling according to Eltabet.{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=4:56}} He found the theater climax the most difficult to cut, as he had to edit around prosthetics that did not always look convincing.{{sfn|Kingdom Of Dreams Podcast|2025|loc=13:44}}{{sfn|Commission supérieure technique|2024|loc=3:18}}

For the sound design, Fargeat placed sound effects and notes in the editing software timeline for sound designers and editors Valérie Deloof and Victor Fleurant.<ref name="ASoundEffect">{{Cite web |last=Walden |first=Jennifer |date=2024-12-18 |title=Behind the Disturbing Sound of 'The Substance' – with Valérie Deloof, Victor Fleurant and Gregory Vincent |url=https://www.asoundeffect.com/the-substance-film-sound/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241219190107/https://www.asoundeffect.com/the-substance-film-sound/ |archive-date=19 December 2024 |access-date=2024-12-20 |website=A Sound Effect |language=en-US}}</ref> The team was tasked with finding the right textures, effects, and tone for each scene, using exaggerated or realistic sounds to explore deeper meanings.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> For example, a chainsaw was used for the [[electric mixer]] in Elisabeth Sparkle's cooking scene, and pistol handling sounds were used for the triggering of the capsule during injection shots.<ref name=ASoundEffect/>

For the [[First-person view (film)|first person view]] shots in the birth sequence, Fargeat wanted Sue's movements to sound as if immersed in [[amniotic fluid]]. To achieve this, the sound team placed microphone capsules in the actors' ears to capture breathing through cranial resonance.{{sfn|Next Best Picture|2024a|loc=17:20}}<ref name=ASoundEffect/> Simultaneously, a [[boom mic]] and a high-frequency mic captured audio to allow for further edits or to switch to an external viewpoint.<ref name=ASoundEffect/>

For scenes with Harvey, the team wanted to emphasize his larger-than-life character.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> Before entering a scene like the bathroom or restaurant, sounds were mixed realistically.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> Once on screen, they became heightened; for example, for the restaurant, sounds were layered emphasizing his eating and the juiciness of the shrimp.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> Ambient sounds and music in the restaurant were muted at this point.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> When Harvey enters his office, the sound team emphasized the "blinged-out heaviness" of his boots.<ref name=ASoundEffect/>

The team created soundscapes to enhance the film's visuals and themes.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> When Elisabeth leaves to retrieve the substance, the sound design shifts from a noisy street to deserted suburbs with dogs, police sirens, and crows used to foreshadow danger.<ref name=ASoundEffect/> Inside the storage facility, sounds were shaped musically with metallic materials and sustained notes to give a feeling of discomfort, and then drawn back to only a low and clinical neon light.<ref name=ASoundEffect/>

For the low-angle shot of "Gollum" banging on the bathroom door, visual effects were used to combine Moore's prosthetics with the prosthetics on the thinner body double.{{sfn|Next Best Picture|2024a|loc=20:07}}

When projected for Kračun, the film appeared too sharp, so it was downscaled to [[2K resolution|2K]] and then [[Image scaling|upscaled]] to [[4K resolution|4K]] to retain the softness he found on the set. When Sue is on the TV show, the footage was kept at the original pre-processed 4K for a sharper look.<ref name=TheASC/>

Persin read the script, made a bid, and, while working on another project, sculpted a small [[plasticine]] [[maquette]]{{sfn|The Horror Flicks and Guitar Picks Podcast|2024|loc=23:30}} of his design for the creature at the climax, "Monstro Elisasue"{{refn|group=note|The monster is instead referred to as MonstroElisaSue (no spaces) in the 2020 draft<ref name=2020ScriptDraft/> and shooting script<ref name=2024Script/> and as MONSTROELISASUE in the film credits.}} over a few nights.<ref name=AwardsWatch/> In the script, Monstro Elisasue was loosely outlined, only described as having Elisabeth's face located on its back.<ref name=Chaos/> Persin wanted to give Monstro some "humility" and "gracefulness"<ref name=MarieClaire/> and for her to resemble Sue.<ref name="IndieWire">{{Cite web |last=Desowitz |first=Bill |date=1 October 2024 |title=Inside Elisabeth's Transformation Into 'Gollum' and 'Monstro' in 'The Substance' |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/craft/the-substance-prosthetic-designer-gollum-monstro-interview-1235051839/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241001160648/https://www.indiewire.com/features/craft/the-substance-prosthetic-designer-gollum-monstro-interview-1235051839/ |archive-date=1 October 2024 |access-date=12 October 2024 |website=IndieWire}}</ref> Fargeat was impressed with his choices, such as the tilted head, backward-facing arm, and the inclusion of breasts, and offered him the position.<ref name=AwardsWatch/> Fargeat and Persin later spent a month refining Monstro's design.<ref name=AwardsWatch/><ref name=TDBMonster/> Persin would later remark in an interview: "Coralie wanted her as if the body was put in a shaker."<ref name=IndieWire/>

Persin and Fargeat conducted a technical read-through over several days, outlining the requirements of each sequence. Persin visualized Fargeat's ideas for each transformation through [[Photoshop]], [[Digital sculpting|digital sculpture]], and regular [[sculpture]].<ref name=MarieClaire/> He created designs based on digital scans of the actors, which Fargeat could select, combine, or modify prior to the creation of actual prosthetics for screen tests.<ref name=MarieClaire/>

Elisabeth's transformation process was organized into a series of five stages, following the birth of Sue: a withered finger ("The Finger"),<ref name=MarieClaire/> a more aged look called "Requiem" (inspired by ''[[Requiem for a Dream]]''), a hunched-backed design referred to as "[[Gollum]]", "Monstro Elisasue", and "The Blob".<ref name="TDBMonster">{{Cite news |last=Cadenas |first=Kerensa |date=23 September 2024 |title=How Demi Moore Became a Grotesque, Hideous Monster |url=https://www.thedailybeast.com/obsessed/the-substance-how-demi-moore-became-a-grotesque-hideous-monster |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240923115216/https://www.thedailybeast.com/obsessed/the-substance-how-demi-moore-became-a-grotesque-hideous-monster |archive-date=23 September 2024 |access-date=12 October 2024 |website=[[The Daily Beast]]}}</ref><ref name=IndieWire/> Fargeat deliberately sought to avoid making the effects look realistic, aiming instead to create a deformed representation of the aging process, shaped by the characters' fear and anger.<ref name="FilmmakerMagazine">{{Cite magazine |last=Loayza |first=Beatrice |date=18 September 2024 |title=The Monster You Become: Coralie Fargeat on The Substance |url=https://filmmakermagazine.com/127299-interview-coralie-fargeat-substance/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240919125448/https://filmmakermagazine.com/127299-interview-coralie-fargeat-substance/ |archive-date=19 September 2024 |access-date=12 October 2024 |magazine=[[Filmmaker (magazine)|Filmmaker]]}}</ref>

Similarly, Persin felt that pacing the changes more gradually would enhance their impact, explaining, "It'll be wild and insane eventually."<ref name=MarieClaire/>

Fargeat described the dialogue-free scene where Moore removes her makeup in front of a mirror as "the most challenging" of the film.<ref name="MakeupChallenging">{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?t=230&v=5l9EZDdKB-E |title=Demi Moore et sa relation à son corps – Interview The Substance avec Demi Moore et Coralie Fargeat |date=2024-11-10 |author=AlloCiné – Interviews |access-date=2024-11-30 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241207080816/https://www.youtube.com/watch?t=230&v=5l9EZDdKB-E |archive-date=7 December 2024 |via=YouTube}}</ref> Fargeat was stressed on the day of the shoot because she knew she was creating the "emotional heart of the movie" where performance, rhythm and lighting had to be perfect.<ref name="MakeupChallenging" /> Fargeat felt if Moore and the crew could pull it off, it would be incredibly powerful.<ref name="MakeupChallenging" /> After the 11th take, Guillon intervened to stop filming for the day. "I took the remover pad and I squashed everything, and I said, 'I removed everything, that's over. You have already 11. You cannot have more because tomorrow she will have a red face.' ===Requiem===
Persin praised Moore's professionalism during the five- to six-hour prosthetic application for the "Requiem" stage, likening it to "going to the dentist for six hours."<ref name=MarieClaire/> The crew were especially impressed when Moore decided to postpone filming one of her scenes until the next day after noticing that her prosthetics had deteriorated over several hours of earlier shots, as she felt it would be extremely disrespectful to their hard work.<ref name=MarieClaire/>

Due to Moore being born with [[Amblyopia|Amblyopia]] in one eye<ref>{{Cite news |title=World's Most Beautiful: Of Course It's Demi! ===Gollum===
Moore's transformation into "Gollum" was more extensive, with layers of silicone prosthetics glued directly onto her body. Red and blue wool was embedded in the silicone to mimic the appearance of veins beneath the skin.<ref name=AwardsWatch/>{{sfn|Scenecraft|2024|loc=31:30}} Persin used silicone because it looked more natural and did not easily wrinkle during the filming of action scenes.{{sfn|Scenecraft|2024|loc=31:17}} When Elisabeth runs down the hallway, Moore was switched out for a thinner body double to match the idea of Gollum having a "spider-like" physicality.<ref name=AwardsWatch/>{{sfn|Scenecraft|2024|loc=30:23}}

A sophisticated fake head, capable of realistic bleeding, was created for the mirror-smashing scene.<ref name=HollywoodReporterTransformation/> Qualley smashed the fake head against the mirror and Moore mimed the action later with her real head; the visual effects team seamlessly blended the two shots to depict the head-on-mirror impact and Moore's reaction.<ref name=HollywoodReporterTransformation/>

|url=https://3dvf.com/en/redaction/from-mission-impossible-to-dune-2-and-the-substance-meet-3d-scanning-specialists-clear-angle-studios/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241211121326/https://3dvf.com/en/redaction/from-mission-impossible-to-dune-2-and-the-substance-meet-3d-scanning-specialists-clear-angle-studios/ |archive-date=11 December 2024 |access-date=2024-12-20 |website=3DVF |language=en-US}}</ref>

Persin drew inspiration from [[Niki de Saint Phalle]], a French sculptor known for her vibrant and curvaceous figures (especially her depictions of female dancers);<ref name=MarieClaire/> artist [[Fernando Botero]], whose sculptures often feature women with exaggerated proportions;<ref name=Vulture/> and [[David Cronenberg]]'s 1986 film ''[[The Fly (1986 film)|The Fly]]''.<ref name=IndieWire/>

During [[pre-production]], Persin expressed concern about the plan to have Qualley wear the full suit, noting that only Qualley's eye would be visible.<ref name=TDBMonster/> Fargeat ultimately had Qualley do close-up shots for Monstro, recognizing the importance of her performance. Persin later acknowledged that this decision was essential to making Monstro's scenes visibly impactful in the final film.<ref name=MarieClaire/>

Qualley would later describe wearing the prosthetics as "torture", adding, "I had this awesome team of prosthetic artists that put it on me and took it off of me and got me through the day, making me laugh a couple of times while I was just on the brink of panic."<ref name="USATodayStars">{{Cite web |last=Truitt |first=Brian |title='The Substance' stars discuss that 'beautiful' bloody finale (spoilers!) |url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2024/09/21/demi-moore-the-substance-movie-spoilers/75240970007/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240921130110/https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2024/09/21/demi-moore-the-substance-movie-spoilers/75240970007/ |archive-date=21 September 2024 |access-date=12 October 2024 |website=USA TODAY}}</ref> "My problem was I had to cry while I had the monster costume on. At a certain point, you're just swimming—there's like a layer of tears and snot inside your prosthetics, and they're just trying to reglue it down."{{sfn|Collider|2024|loc=19:51}} The prosthetics ===The Blob===
Ending the film with "The Blob" featuring Moore's face, Fargeat aimed for a clear visual that would allow for full facial expressions. Persin and his team constructed and manually maneuvered a puppet blob for the scene, which was overlaid on Moore's face with [[visual effects]].<ref name=TDBMonster/> It was referred to as "Gremlin"<ref name=MarieClaire/> in the shooting schedule, a nod to Stripe melting at the end of the 1984 film ''[[Gremlins]]''.{{sfn|The Horror Flicks and Guitar Picks Podcast|2024|loc=26:26}}

==Themes and influences==

Wendy Ide of ''The Guardian'' noted ''The Substance'' for its [[Feminism|feminist]] perspective of older women, contrasting it with other female-led horror films like ''[[Carrie (1976 film)|Carrie]]'' and ''[[Rosemary's Baby (film)|Rosemary's Baby]]'' which centre on themes of menstruation and childbirth. She wrote that ''The Substance'', in contrast, "not only offers a female perspective on women's bodies, but also argues that things only start to get properly messy once fertility is a dim memory."<ref name="GuardianReviewIde">{{cite news |last=Ide |first=Wendy |date=22 September 2024 |title='The Substance review' – Demi Moore is fearless in visceral feminist body horror |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2024/sep/22/the-substance-review-demi-moore-is-fearless-in-visceral-female-body-horror-coralie-fargeat |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250125180805/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2024/sep/22/the-substance-review-demi-moore-is-fearless-in-visceral-female-body-horror-coralie-fargeat |archive-date=25 January 2025 |access-date=22 September 2024 |newspaper=[[The Guardian]] |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> ''[[The New York Times]]'' critic Alissa Wilkinson described the film as an exploration of the [[male gaze]], noting the satirically exaggerated camera angles and shots, depicting the female characters in a way "that feels reminiscent mostly of [[Pornographic film|porn]]".<ref name="NYTReviewWilkinson">{{cite news |last=Wilkinson |first=Alissa |date=19 September 2024 |title='The Substance' Review: An Indecent Disclosure |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/19/movies/the-substance-review.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250126110254/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/19/movies/the-substance-review.html |archive-date=26 January 2025 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]]}}</ref> In exaggerating these portrayals of female beauty standards to the point of absurdity, the film becomes humorous: "the worse things become for Elisabeth, the harder it is not to giggle with glee."<ref name="NYTReviewWilkinson"/> She also notes that the film can be seen as exploring [[mind–body dualism]].<ref name="NYTReviewWilkinson"/>

Alison Willmore of ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'' said that the film's strongest theme is about the dangers of addiction, comparing it to ''Requiem for a Dream''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Willmore
