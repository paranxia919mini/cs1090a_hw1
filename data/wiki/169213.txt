==1826–1899==
{{See also|19th century in film}}{{See also|1870s in film}}{{See also|1880s in film}}{{See also|1890s in film}}
* '''[[1826]]''' – ''[[View from the Window at Le Gras]]''; [[Nicéphore Niépce]] takes the oldest known extant photograph.
* '''[[1833]]''' – Since 1833 and onward, "animated films"—or, rather, animated effects—began to be created with the use of [[choreutoscope]]s, [[phenakistiscope|phénakisticopes]]'', ''[[zoetrope]]s, and [[praxinoscope]]s.
* '''[[1865]]''' – ''Revolving'', self-portrait by [[French people|French]] photographer [[Nadar]]. Around 1865, Nadar produced ''Revolving''—a series of self-portraits consisting of 12 [[frame (film)|frame]]s showing different angles of him sitting still in a chair. Except for a smile in one frame, not even a fold in his jacket or a single hair seems to change between the different angles. The portrait could be regarded as a predecessor to the [[chronophotography]], with which Marey and Muybridge started to experiment more than 10 years later. As the sequence revolves around space, rather than time, it is even more related to the [[bullet time|bullet-time effect]] popularized by ''[[The Matrix]]'' about 135 years later. There is no clue if more than one camera was used in the shoot, but it is certainly well-executed.
* '''[[1874]]''' – ''[[Passage de Vénus]]'', first precedent of a film. On December 9, 1874, French astronomer [[Pierre Janssen]] and [[Brazilians|Brazilian]] engineer [[Francisco Antônio de Almeida]] created ''Passage de Vénus'' using Janssen's "[[photographic revolver]]" to photograph the [[1874 transit of Venus|transit of the planet Venus across the Sun]]. The photographs were purportedly taken in [[Japan]]. It is the oldest film on [[IMDb]].

Before [[British people|British]] photographer [[Eadweard Muybridge]]'s 1878 work, photo sequences were not recorded in real-time because light-sensitive emulsions needed a long exposure time. The sequences were basically made as time-lapse recordings. It is possible that people at the time actually viewed such photographs come to life with a phénakisticope or zoetrope. (This certainly happened with Muybridge's work.)

* '''[[1878]]''' –
** ''[[The Horse in Motion]]''; Muybridge took a series of "automatic electro-photographs" depicting the movement of a horse. He shot the photographs in June 1878. An additional card reprinted the single image of the horse "Occident" trotting at high speed, which had previously been published by Muybridge in 1877. The most famous of these electro-photographs is "Sallie Gardner," taken on June 19, 1878. Railroad tycoon [[Leland Stanford]] hired Muybridge to settle the questions of whether a galloping horse ever has all four of its feet off the ground. Muybridge's photos showed the horse with all four feet off the ground. He went on a lecture tour showing his photographs on a moving-image device he called the [[zoopraxiscope]].
** ''Le singe musicien'', first animated movie using the [[praxinoscope]].
*'''[[1885 in film|1885]]''' – American inventors [[George Eastman]] and [[Hannibal Goodwin]] each invented a sensitized [[celluloid]] [[film base|base]] roll [[photographic film]] to replace the glass plates then in use. ''L'homme Machine'' was directed by French scientist [[Étienne-Jules Marey]]; it is the oldest known black and white [[animation|animated]] film.
*'''[[1886 in film|1886]]''' – [[Louis Le Prince]] was granted an American dual-patent for [[Louis Le Prince#LePrince Cine Camera-Projector types|a 16-lens device]], which combines a [[Movie camera|motion picture camera]] with a [[Movie projector|projector]].
*'''[[1887 in film|1887]]''' – ''[[Man Walking Around a Corner]]'', directed by French inventor [[Louis Le Prince]]; it is the oldest known film. Although, according to David Wilkinson's 2015 documentary ''[[The First Film]]'', it is not film, but a series of photographs, 16 in all, each taken from one of the lenses from Le Prince's camera. Pictures from the film were sent in a letter dated August 18, 1887, to Le Prince's wife. Le Prince later developed the one-lens camera, and, on October 14, 1888, he finally made the world's first moving image, ''[[Roundhay Garden Scene]].''
*'''[[1888 in film|1888]]''' – ''[[Roundhay Garden Scene]]'', the earliest surviving film by French inventor [[Louis Le Prince]]; is shot in [[Leeds]]'', ''[[West Yorkshire]], in England with a groundbreaking 20 [[film frame|frames]] per second. Other short films made at the same time were ''Accordion Player'' and ''Traffic Crossing Leeds Bridge''.
*'''[[1889 in film|1889]]''' – [[Eastman Kodak]] is the first company to begin commercial production of film on a flexible transparent [[film base|base]]'', ''[[celluloid]].
*'''[[1891 in film|1891]]''' – ''[[Dickson Greeting]]'', ''[[Men Boxing]]'', ''[[Newark Athlete]]''
*'''[[1892 in film|1892]]''' – ''[[Le Clown et ses chiens]]'', ''[[Pauvre Pierrot]]'', ''[[Un bon bock]]''; first projected [[animation|animated]] films released by [[Émile Reynaud]]
*'''[[1893 in film|1893]]''' – ''[[Blacksmith Scene|Blacksmiths]]'', the first film shown publicly on the [[Kinetoscope]], a system given to Edison; [[Thomas Edison]] created "America's First Film Studio", [[Edison's Black Maria|Black Maria]]
*'''[[1894 in film|1894]]''' – 
**''[[Carmencita (film)|Carmencita]]'', directed by [[William Kennedy Dickson|William K.L. Dickson]], who was the [[Scottish people|Scottish]] [[inventor]] credited with the invention of the [[film|motion picture]] [[movie camera|camera]] under the employ of Edison. According to film historian [[Charles Musser]], the film featured the first woman to appear in front of an Edison motion picture camera, who may have also been the first woman to appear in a motion picture in the United States.<ref name="congress">{{cite web |url=http://memory.loc.gov/cgi-bin/query/r?ammem/papr:@filreq(@field(NUMBER+@band(edmp+4019))+@field(COLLID+edison)) |title=Library of Congress American Memory |access-date=2007-03-10 }}</ref><ref>{{cite book|last=Musser|first=Charles|author-link=Charles Musser|title=Edison Motion Pictures, 1890-1900: An Annotated Filmography|date=1997|publisher=Smithsonian Institution Press|location=Washington, D.C.|isbn=1-56098-567-4|pages=34–35}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last=Ramsaye |first=Terry |author-link=Terry Ramsaye |title=The Romantic History of the Motion Picture |journal=Photoplay |volume=22 |issue=6 |pages=32–35, 95 |publisher=Photoplay Publishing Company |location=New York City |date=May 1922 |url=https://archive.org/details/phojun22chic |access-date=2015-02-26}}</ref>
**''[[The Dickson Experimental Sound Film]]'', made by Dickson in late 1894 or early 1895, is the first known film with live-recorded sound and appears to be the first motion picture made for the [[Kinetoscope#Kinetophone|Kinetophone]], the proto-[[sound film|sound-film]] system developed by Dickson and Edison.
*'''[[1895 in film|1895]]''' – In Paris on December 28, 1895, [[Auguste and Louis Lumière|the Lumière brothers]] screen 10 films at the Salon Indien du Grand Café in [[Paris]] making the first commercial public screening ever made, marked traditionally as '''the birth date of the film'''. [[Gaumont]], the oldest ever [[film studio]], was founded by inventor [[Léon Gaumont]].
*'''[[1896 in film|1896]]''' – ''[[L'Arrivée d'un train en gare de La Ciotat]]'', one of the six additional short films released by the Lumière brothers; [[Pathé-Frères]] is founded; ''[[Le Manoir du diable|Le Manoir du diable (The Haunted Castle)]]'' is a French short silent film directed by [[Georges Méliès]], which depicts a pantomimed sketch in the style of a theatrical comic fantasy.
*'''[[1897 in film|1897]]''' – [[Vitagraph]] is founded in New York City
*'''[[1898 in film|1898]]''' –''[[The Astronomer's Dream]]'', ''[[The Cavalier's Dream]]'', ''[[Photographing a Ghost]]'', ''[[Santa Claus (1898 film)|Santa Claus]]''
*'''[[1898 in film|1898]] ''' –'' Shinin No Sosei'' and'' Bake Jizo'' were made by Ejiro Hatta, some of the first films in Japan that were ghost stories.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://viterbi-web.usc.edu/~jdsolomo/itp104/assignment_06/home.html | title=A History of Horror }}</ref> Salvador Toscano created the film "Don Juan Tenorio", which is considered one of the first films in [[Mexico]] and perhaps the first fictional film in Mexico, as [[South America]], as a continent, focuses on making documentaries in its early film history.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1340117/ | title=Salvador Toscano &#124; Director, Cinematographer, Producer | website=[[IMDb]] }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.victorian-cinema.net/toscano.php | title=Who's Who of Victorian Cinema }}</ref>
*'''[[1898 in film|1898]] ''' – [[Hiralal Sen]] was inspired by and filmed the play ''the Flower of Persia''<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0784006/#:~:text=Hiralal%20Sen(1868%2D1917)&text=He%20was%20the%20son%20of,the%20Star%20Theatre%20in%20Calcutta | title=Hiralal Sen &#124; Director | website=[[IMDb]] }}</ref> 
*'''[[1899 in film|1899]]''' – ''[[The Dreyfus Affair (film series)|The Dreyfus Affair]]'' and ''[[Cendrillon (1899 film)|Cendrillon]]'' (first screen adaptation of the traditional fairy tale ''[[Cinderella]]'') was released by [[Georges Méliès]]; earliest known use of a color motion picture film footage by [[Edward Raymond Turner]]<ref>{{cite news|title=World's first colour film footage viewed for first time|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-19557914|publisher=BBC News England|access-date=14 September 2012|date=12 September 2012}}</ref>
