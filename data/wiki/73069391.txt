'''''Immaculate''''' is a 2024 American [[horror film]]  directed by [[Michael Mohan]] and written by Andrew Lobel. It stars [[Sydney Sweeney]] (who also produced), [[Álvaro Morte]], [[Benedetta Porcaroli]], Dora Romano, [[Giorgio Colangeli]], and [[Simona Tabasco]]. The plot focuses on a young [[Novitiate|novice]] nun, who is invited to reside at a picturesque Italian [[convent]], but slowly realizes the terrifying secrets it harbors.

''Immaculate'' premiered at [[South by Southwest]] on March 12, 2024, and was released theatrically in the United States by [[Neon (company)|Neon]] on March 22, 2024. The film received mostly positive reviews and grossed $35&nbsp;million worldwide.

==Plot==
[[Religious sister|Sister]] Mary sneaks into a [[Mother superior|superior]]'s bedroom at night and steals keys to escape through the locked gates of a [[Catholic Church|Catholic]] convent. Four hooded figures capture her and break her leg, then knock her unconscious. Mary later wakes up in a coffin and is [[Premature burial|buried alive]].

After surviving a drowning in a frozen lake and being declared dead for seven minutes, young [[Novitiate|novice]] Sister Cecilia, who turned to [[Christianity]] at an early age, becomes convinced God had saved her for a purpose. She receives an invitation from Father Sal Tedeschi to join a [[convent]] in Italy that tends to dying nuns. Cecilia becomes a [[Nun#Christianity|nun]].

Cecilia befriends Sister Gwen and is struck by certain oddities, such as an elder nun having cross-shaped scars on the soles of her feet. The convent's chapel houses a relic, a [[Holy Nail]] that was used to [[Crucifixion of Jesus|crucify Jesus]]. After having nightmares of hooded figures, Cecilia is shocked to learn that she is [[Miraculous births|pregnant, even though she is a virgin]]. The convent's inhabitants begin to treat her as the next [[Virgin Mary]], with many proclaiming the child is a "blessing".

Another nun, Sister Isabelle, tries to drown Cecilia out of jealousy and later dies by suicide. Cecilia throws up one of her own teeth and her health worsens. Her request to be taken to a proper hospital is denied by Father Tedeschi. Gwen publicly chastises the convent superiors for this abnormality but is then taken away by Father Tedeschi and Deacon Enzo. At night, Cecilia sneaks around the convent and finds her personal file, and is shocked to find information about her childhood accident and a [[karyotype]]. Hearing a woman screaming, she investigates and is horrified to witness a hooded figure cutting out Gwen's tongue. An elder nun appears and silences Cecilia. She begs to escape, but the elder chastises her, saying that she will never leave the convent.

Cecilia fakes a miscarriage to be taken to the hospital, but the deception is exposed and she is tied up. Tedeschi explains that he was a geneticist before becoming a priest and [[Genetic engineering|used DNA sample]]s taken from the Holy Nail to impregnate nuns in the hopes of ushering in a new messiah. Before Cecilia, Tedeschi's numerous attempts failed, often resulting in [[Monstrous birth|malformed fetuses]]. Tedeschi [[Human branding|brands]] a cross into Cecilia's feet, as seen in other nuns.

Cecilia makes another attempt to escape. She beats Mother Superior to death using a crucifix, and shortly afterward, her [[Amniorrhexis|water breaks]]. She manages to strangle the cardinal with her rosary before dousing Tedeschi's lab in [[ethanol]]. Tedeschi arrives and tries to stop her, but Cecilia narrowly escapes by igniting the ethanol with a lighter.

Tedeschi breaks out of the laboratory after extinguishing the flames and continues his pursuit of Cecilia, forcing her to flee into the [[catacomb]]s under the convent. Cecilia finds Gwen's mutilated corpse along with a hole in the catacomb's walls. Tedeschi finds her just as she is about to escape and a struggle ensues. Tedeschi tries to [[Caesarean section|cut]] Cecilia's stomach open, but she stabs him in the throat with the Holy Nail relic, which she had previously stolen from the chapel.
After exiting the catacombs, Cecilia agonizingly gives birth and bites through the umbilical cord herself. She is horrified by the sight of the baby, which is heard making strange, animalistic noises. Cecilia stumbles away to grab and lift a nearby rock, which she uses to crush it to death.{{efn-ua|Given that the neonate does not sound like a baby, but rather gurgles and growls, it has been interpreted as being the [[Antichrist]].<ref name="Garrett2024">{{cite web |last1=Garrett |first1=Mars |title='Immaculate' - Demonic Forces Attack a Pregnant Nun in Bloody First Trailer |url=https://www.belloflostsouls.net/2024/02/immaculate-demonic-forces-are-at-work-in-unsettling-first-trailerimmaculate-demonic-forces-attack-a-pregnant-nun-in-unsettling-first-trailer.html |publisher=Bell of Lost Souls |access-date=7 April 2024 |language=en |date=1 February 2024}}</ref><ref name="Wallace2024">{{cite magazine |last=Wallace |first=Lindsay Lee |title='Immaculate' Ending Explained |url=https://time.com/6959849/immaculate-ending-explained/ |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |access-date=7 April 2024 |date=22 March 2024 |quote=But from the sinister gurgling, growling, wheezing sounds the creature makes upon its emergence, it's clear all is not well.}}</ref>}}

=== Critical response ===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|72|6.3|211|''Immaculate'' in conception if not always in execution, this religiously themed horror outing is saved by a divine performance from Sydney Sweeney.|access-date=August 5, 2024|ref=y}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 57 out of 100, based on 39 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Immaculate'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/immaculate |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=March 31, 2024 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "C" on an A+ to F scale, while those polled by [[PostTrak]] gave the film a 52% overall positive score, with 30% saying they would definitely recommend it.<ref name=opening/>

Tim Robey of ''[[The Daily Telegraph|The Telegraph]]'' awarded the film three stars out of five, saying that "Sydney Sweeney's pregnant-nun horror takes time to deliver… Only at the very end does ''Immaculate'' deliver, so to speak, on some of (its) hinted-at promise."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/2024/03/21/immaculate-review-sydney-sweeneys-pregnant-nun-horror-takes/|title=Immaculate: Sydney Sweeney's pregnant-nun horror takes time to deliver|website=The Telegraph|date=21 March 2024|access-date=22 March 2024|archive-date=March 29, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240329080117/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/2024/03/21/immaculate-review-sydney-sweeneys-pregnant-nun-horror-takes/|url-status=live}}</ref> Manohla Dargis of ''[[The New York Times]]'' called the film a Critic's Pick, writing that the film "is a scare-fest with a plucky heroine, an irreverent hot-button twist and just enough narrative ambiguity to give viewers something to argue about."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Dargis |first=Manohla |date=March 21, 2024 |title='Immaculate' Review: Sydney Sweeney Is Wide-Eyed but Sly |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/21/movies/immaculate-review-sydney-sweeney.html |url-status=live |access-date=March 25, 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]] |archive-date=March 28, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240328094919/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/03/21/movies/immaculate-review-sydney-sweeney.html }}</ref>

=== Comparisons to ''The First Omen'' ===
Due to sharing similar premises (and a common Italian setting) and released at about the same time, ''Immaculate'' and ''[[The First Omen]]'' have been dubbed [[Twin film|"twin" films]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Meenan |first=Devin |date=April 7, 2024 |title=Everything Immaculate And The First Omen Have In Common (Besides Evil Pregnancies) |url=https://www.slashfilm.com/1557055/imacculate-first-omen-evil-pregnancy-plot/ |website=[[/Film]]}}</ref> Some reviewers have suggested both films explore the issue of female [[Bodily integrity|bodily autonomy]], depicting the "systemic control of women's bodies reduced to vessels".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sarrubba |first=Stefania |date=April 7, 2024 |title=Why The First Omen's full-frontal childbirth scene is so important |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a60410360/first-omen-vagina-childbirth-scene/ |website=[[Digital Spy]]}}</ref> [[Bilge Ebiri]] of ''[[Vulture (magazine)|Vulture]]'' wrote "why should anyone be surprised that suddenly, in the wake of the Supreme Court's overturning of ''[[Roe v. Wade]]'', as state after state attempts to enact religious laws depriving women of bodily agency, America is getting horror movies about people forced into monstrous births by religious institutions worried about their growing irrelevance".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ebiri |first=Bilge |author-link=Bilge Ebiri |date=April 5, 2024 |title=Behold, an Actually Good Omen Movie |url=https://www.vulture.com/article/review-the-first-omen-is-actually-a-good-omen-movie.html |website=[[Vulture (magazine)|Vulture]]}}</ref>

== Themes ==
The film addresses themes of genetic modification and debates surrounding childbirth, abortion, and [[infanticide]]. Speaking with ''[[Fangoria]]'', director Mohan recited a story wherein his family stopped attending church after hearing a pro-life sermon; however he held that the movie was not intended to have a social message.<ref name=":0" /> The article reads

<blockquote>And though the film – which is, at the end of the day, about a forced birth by a religious institution laying claim to a woman's body – is already getting its share of blowback from certain conservative corners of the internet, Mohan is unbothered. "I don't think of it as a problem. I think it's a necessary conversation, and if a film can inspire that conversation, then we've done our job. But I also want to be really clear: I want that conversation to be started by the audience. I don't want them to look at us and go 'Look at those [[social justice warrior]]s!{{' "}} Mohan seems more invested in having ''Immaculate'' carry out horror's other important mission: scarring people for life.<ref name=":0" /></blockquote>
