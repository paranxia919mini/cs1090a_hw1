'''''Beasts of the Southern Wild''''' is a 2012 American [[magical realism]] film directed, co-written, and co-scored by [[Benh Zeitlin]]. It was adapted by Zeitlin and [[Lucy Alibar]] from Alibar's [[one-act play]] ''Juicy and Delicious''. The film stars [[Quvenzhané Wallis]] and [[Dwight Henry (actor)|Dwight Henry]]. After playing at [[film festival]]s, its [[limited theatrical release]] began in New York and Los Angeles on June 27, 2012, before expanding to additional markets.

''Beasts of the Southern Wild'' was met with commercial success and acclaim from critics, with praise going to the cinematography and Wallis's performance. The film was nominated for four [[Academy Awards]] in [[85th Academy Awards|2013]]: [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]], [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]], and [[Academy Award for Best Actress|Best Actress]] (Wallis). At age 9, Wallis became the [[List of oldest and youngest Academy Award winners and nominees#Best Actress in a Leading Role|youngest]] Best Actress nominee in history.<ref name="TheTimes">{{cite news |url=https://www.thetimes.com/article/youngest-v-oldest-actress-vie-for-oscar-as-lincoln-leads-the-pack-sw9wnnmfn5n |title=Youngest v oldest actress vie for Oscar as Lincoln leads the pack |access-date=January 10, 2013 |work=[[The Times]]|last=Blakely|first=Rhys|date=January 10, 2013}}</ref><ref name="TheIndependent">{{cite news |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/quvenzhan-wallis-v-emmanuelle-riva--best-actress-oscar-contested-by-oldest-and-youngest-ever-nominees-8446248.html |title=Quvenzhané Wallis v Emmanuelle Riva: Best actress Oscar contested by oldest and youngest ever nominees |access-date=January 10, 2013 |work=The Independent |publisher=Independent Print, Ltd.|location=London, England |first=Tim |last=Walker |date=January 10, 2013}}</ref>

==Plot==
Six-year-old Hushpuppy and her ailing, hot-tempered father Wink live in a small community on an island in the [[Louisiana]] [[bayou]] called the "Bathtub". Although it lies beyond the [[levee]] system that helps protect the land to the north from rising sea levels, Wink thinks it is the most beautiful place in the world and looks down on the way people live on the other side of the levee.

In a rustic community schoolhouse, Miss Bathsheba teaches the children of the Bathtub about prehistoric creatures she calls [[aurochs]] (portrayed as giant horned boar-like creatures in the film, unlike the true aurochs, a species of wild cattle) that terrorized [[cavemen]] and ate their children. She says the cavemen did not bemoan their fate, however, and the students should remember this lesson and learn how to survive, since the fabric of the universe will soon come "unraveled", causing the ice caps to melt and the Bathtub to end up underwater.

At home, Hushpuppy finds Wink has gone missing, so she fends for herself. When he returns, he is wearing a hospital gown and bracelet. They argue, and he tells Hushpuppy to leave him alone. She returns to her house, which is a separate building from the one in which Wink resides, and finds her food burning on the stove. She turns up the heat, which sets her house on fire and draws Wink's attention. A chase ensues between the two, and she ends up getting slapped by Wink. When she retaliates by punching him in the chest, he collapses. At the same moment, there is a rumble of thunder. Hushpuppy thinks she has thrown off the balance of the universe by striking her father and runs to get help. Miss Bathsheba gives her some [[herbal medicine]], but when Hushpuppy gets back home Wink is nowhere to be found.

Meanwhile, Hushpuppy imagines that the [[ice calving]] releases some aurochs that have been frozen in polar ice into the ocean. Throughout the rest of the film, they are seen to reach land, break out of the ice that encases them, and make their way toward the Bathtub.

As the weather worsens and Hushpuppy watches many residents of the Bathtub fleeing the impending flood, she sees Wink staggering along the side of the road. He finds some friends and encourages them to ride out the storm before taking Hushpuppy home to do the same. The Bathtub floods overnight, and the next day Wink and Hushpuppy tour the devastation and reconnect with the handful of their neighbors who have also stayed behind.

The remaining Bathtub residents build floating homes and make plans to rebuild their community. Some of them think the flooding is only temporary, but Miss Bathsheba thinks it is permanent and says the amount of time before they will have to move is limited. As time passes, it seems likely that she is right, so Wink hatches a plan to drain the water away by blowing a hole in the [[levee]] with an [[alligator gar]] carcass stuffed with explosives. The water recedes, but then authorities arrive to enforce a mandatory evacuation of the Bathtub. They remove the remaining residents to an emergency shelter and Wink undergoes an operation for his ailment against his wishes. It has come too late to restore his health, however, and he tries to send Hushpuppy to be raised by someone else, but she refuses to go. At the first opportunity, the evacuees escape back to their homes.

While Wink lies dying, Hushpuppy and a few of her friends attempt to swim to a flashing light across the water that she feels might lead her to her absent mother. They are picked up by a boat that takes them to a floating bar known as the [[Elysium|Elysian Fields]]. Hushpuppy thinks that the cook is her mother, though the woman doesn't recognize her. The cook says Hushpuppy can stay with her if she wants, but Hushpuppy says she needs to go home.

Hushpuppy gets back to the Bathtub just as the aurochs are also arriving. Her friends run away, but she calmly stands her ground and confronts the aurochs. She convinces them to leave and goes to be with Wink. They say their last goodbyes, and she and the remaining residents of the Bathtub give him the funeral he asked for.

==Setting and location==
The film's fictional setting, "Isle de Charles Doucet", known to its residents as the Bathtub, was inspired by several isolated and independent fishing communities threatened by erosion, hurricanes, and rising sea levels in Louisiana's [[Terrebonne Parish, Louisiana|Terrebonne Parish]], most notably the rapidly eroding [[Isle de Jean Charles, Louisiana|Isle de Jean Charles]]. It was filmed in [[Montegut, Louisiana|Montegut]], a town in Terrebonne Parish.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/06/10/movies/the-making-of-beasts-of-the-southern-wild.html|first=Rachel|last=Arons|title=A Mythical Bayou's All-Too-Real Peril: The Making of 'Beasts of the Southern Wild|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=June 8, 2012}}</ref>

==Production==
The film was shot on 16mm film, and director [[Benh Zeitlin]] created the production with a small professional crew and dozens of local residents in and around [[Montegut, Louisiana]]. The filmmakers call themselves "Court 13", which is the first credit at the end of the film.<ref>{{cite web|last=Lapin|first=Andrew|title=The Real Bathtub: Back To The Bayou With 'Beasts Of The Southern Wild'|url=http://www.indiewire.com/article/the-real-bathtub-back-to-the-bayou-with-beasts-of-the-southern-wild|work=IndieWire|date=June 27, 2012}}</ref>

At her audition, [[Quvenzhané Wallis]] (who was five years old,<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Wells|first=Veronica|title=Black Girl Going Places: Quvenzhané Wallis|url=http://madamenoire.com/199587/black-girl-going-places-quvenzhane-wallis/|magazine=Madame Noire|date=July 24, 2012|access-date=August 4, 2012|archive-date=July 7, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150707090306/http://madamenoire.com/199587/black-girl-going-places-quvenzhane-wallis/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Rivas|first=Jorge|title=8-year-old tells off Leno|url=https://www.salon.com/2012/07/05/8_year_old_tells_off_leno/|publisher=Colorlines/Salon Media Group, Inc.|date=July 5, 2012 |access-date=April 25, 2026}}</ref> though the casting call had been for girls between six and nine) impressed the filmmakers with her reading ability, tremendous scream, and ability to burp on command, all of which are used in the film.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |title=Quvenzhané. A small force of nature. |url=http://blogs.suntimes.com/ebert/2012/06/_quvenzhane_a_small_force_of_n.html |newspaper=Chicago Sun-Times |date=June 22, 2012 |access-date=August 3, 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120816010029/http://blogs.suntimes.com/ebert/2012/06/_quvenzhane_a_small_force_of_n.html |archive-date=August 16, 2012  }}</ref> After refusing Zeitlin's direction to throw a water bottle at another actor because she believed that it wasn't right to do so, the filmmakers were further convinced and the director said, "I realised this is Hushpuppy, that's what this whole movie is about: this moral girl who believes in right and wrong so strongly."<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/article/2012/11/29/beasts-of-the-southern-wild-quvenzhane-wallis/|title='Beasts of the Southern Wild': Casting Quvenzhané Wallis|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|first=Anthony|last=Breznican|date=November 29, 2012|language=en|access-date=August 6, 2019}}</ref>

[[Dwight Henry (actor)|Dwight Henry]], who plays Wink, was not looking for an acting job and had no acting experience. As he explained in an interview with the ''[[San Diego Reader]]'':

{{blockquote|Before I was cast in the part I owned a bakery called Henry's Bakery and Deli right across the street from the casting agency where Court 13 had their studio. They used to come over and have lunch or breakfast in the morning. After a few months we kinda developed a relationship. They used to put these fliers in the bakery with a phone number to call if you were interested in appearing in one of their movies.<ref name="sdreader">{{cite news|last=Marks|first=Scott|title=Interview: Quvenzhané Wallis and Dwight Henry, Stars of Beasts of the Southern Wild|url=http://www.sandiegoreader.com/weblogs/big-screen/2012/jul/13/interview-quvenzhane-wallis-and-dwight-henry-stars/|work=[[San Diego Reader]]|date=July 13, 2012|access-date=3 August 2012}}</ref>}}

He read for the part during a slow hour and was chosen, but at the time he was in the middle of moving to a larger building (which would become the Buttermilk Drop Bakery and Café, in the [[Tremé]] neighborhood of New Orleans<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.buttermilkdrop.com/|title=The Buttermilk Drop Bakery and Cafe|access-date=August 13, 2012|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120828071450/http://www.buttermilkdrop.com/|archive-date=August 28, 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Persall|first=Steve|title=New Orleans baker Dwight Henry sizzles in 'Beasts of Southern Wild' role|url=https://www.tampabay.com/incoming/new-orleans-baker-dwight-henry-sizzles-in-beasts-of-southern-wild-role/1243273/|newspaper=[[Tampa Bay Times]]|date=August 1, 2012 |access-date=August 4, 2012}}</ref>) and the filmmakers had trouble finding him to let him know. When they did, he explained that he could not leave his new business, but they were determined to have him and he eventually agreed to take the role. Henry later concluded, "I was in [[Hurricane Katrina]] in neck-high water. I have an inside understanding for what this movie is about. I brought a passion to the part that an outside actor who had never seen a storm or been in a flood or faced losing everything couldn't have. … I was two years old when [[Hurricane Betsy]] hit [[New Orleans]] and my parents had to put me on the roof of the house. An outsider couldn't have brought the passion to the role that I did."<ref name="sdreader" />

==Reception==
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 87% based on 208 reviews, with an average rating of 8.30/10. The website's critical consensus reads: "''Beasts of the Southern Wild'' is a fantastical, emotionally powerful journey and a strong case of filmmaking that values imagination over money."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/beasts_of_the_southern_wild/|title=Beasts of the Southern Wild (2012)|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=January 21, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 86 out of 100 based on 45 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/beasts-of-the-southern-wild|title=Beasts of the Southern Wild Reviews|work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=October 11, 2020}}</ref> 

The film was designated a 2012 "Critics' Pick" by the reviewers of ''[[The New York Times]]''. Author and critic [[A. O. Scott]], writing for ''Times'', calls ''Beasts'' a

{{blockquote|blast of sheer, improbable joy, a boisterous, thrilling action movie with a protagonist who can hold her own... Hushpuppy, the 6-year-old heroine of 'Beasts of the Southern Wild,' has a smile to charm fish out of the water and a scowl so fierce it can stop monsters in their tracks. The movie, a passionate and unruly explosion of [[Americana (culture)|Americana]], directed by Benh Zeitlin, winks at skepticism, laughs at sober analysis and stares down criticism.<ref name="A.O. Scott">{{cite news|first=A.O.|last=Scott|author-link=A.O. Scott|url=https://movies.nytimes.com/2012/06/27/movies/beasts-of-the-southern-wild-directed-by-benh-zeitlin.html|title=She's the Man of This Swamp, Review: 'Beasts of the Southern Wild' Directed by Benh Zeitlin|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=27 June 2012|access-date=25 July 2012}}</ref>}}

Scott subsequently named ''Beasts of the Southern Wild'' the third-best film of 2012.<ref>{{cite news|first=A. O.|author-link=A.O. Scott|last=Scott|title=25 Favorites From A Year When 10 Aren't Enough|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/12/16/movies/a-o-scotts-25-best-films-of-2012.html|work=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=December 14, 2012|date=December 14, 2012}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' called the film a "remarkable creation... Sometimes miraculous films come into being, made by people you've never heard of, starring unknown faces, blindsiding you with creative genius. "Beasts of the Southern Wild" is one of the year's best films."<ref name="Ebert">{{cite web|first=Roger|last=Ebert|author-link=Roger Ebert|url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20120704/REVIEWS/120709994 |title=Beasts of the Southern Wild |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |date=July 4, 2012 |access-date=December 3, 2012|via=rogerebert.com}}</ref>

Michael Phillips of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' said that ''Beasts'' was "the most divisive film of 2012," opining that "The filmmaker comes from a perspective of great empathy and considerable skill. But he's a pile driver as a dramatist. The film's screw-tightening methods are so overbearing, the story, the characters, the little girl's plight have to struggle to breathe or develop anything like an inner life."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/2012/07/05/manipulative-music-plot-devices-keep-beasts-of-the-southern-wild-from-greatness/|title=Manipulative music, plot devices keep 'Beasts of the Southern Wild' from greatness ★★|first=Michael|last=Phillips|newspaper=[[Chicago Tribune]]|date=July 5, 2012|access-date=April 25, 2026}}</ref> Author and activist [[bell hooks]] wrote a negative review of the film, saying "the vibrancy in this film is generated by a crude pornography of violence" and calling Hushpuppy "a miniature version of the 'strong black female matriarch,' racist and sexist representations have depicted from [[slavery]] on into the present day."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://newblackman.blogspot.com/2012/09/bell-hooks-no-love-in-wild.html|first=bell|last=hooks|author-link=bell hooks|title=No Love in the Wild|work=NewBlackMan (in Exile): The Digital Home of Mark Anthony Neal|date=September 5, 2012|access-date=November 28, 2015}}</ref>

The performance of newcomer [[Quvenzhané Wallis]] was met with critical acclaim. Amy Biancolli of the ''[[San Francisco Chronicle]]'' says,

{{blockquote|Regarding Wallis' performance as Hushpuppy: it isn't one. It's a fact. Onscreen she simply is, a being as elemental, incontestable and strong as the advancing aurochs. She was 6 when the film was shot, yet the ferociousness of her presence – the anger and wisdom inside her – suggest someone older or ageless.<ref>{{cite news|title='Beasts of the Southern Wild' review: Wet and wild|url=http://www.sfgate.com/movies/article/Beasts-of-the-Southern-Wild-review-Wet-and-wild-3686131.php|access-date=January 29, 2013|newspaper=[[The San Francisco Chronicle]]|first=Amy|last=Biancolli|date=July 5, 2012}}</ref>}}

[[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' describes Wallis as "flat-out amazement." He adds that "there's no way you won't be captivated by Wallis, chosen ahead of 3,500 candidates to play the tiny folk hero who narrates the story. Her performance in this deceptively small film is a towering achievement."<ref>{{cite magazine|first=Peter|last=Travers|author-link=Peter Travers|title=Beasts of the Southern Wild|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/beasts-of-southern-wild-20120628#ixzz2JPJNY1yg|date=June 28, 2012|access-date=January 29, 2013|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]}}</ref> Ebert wrote in his review that Hushpuppy is "played by a force of nature named Quvenzhané Wallis... She is so uniquely and particularly herself that I wonder if the movie would have been possible without her."<ref name="Ebert"/> Scott said: "Played by Quvenzhané Wallis, an untrained sprite who holds the camera's attention with a charismatic poise that might make grown-up movie stars weep in envy, Hushpuppy is an American original, a rambunctious blend of individualism and fellow feeling."<ref name="A.O. Scott"/> On January 10, 2013, Wallis was nominated for the [[Academy Award for Best Actress]]. At 9 years old, she is the youngest ever nominee in that category.<ref>{{cite news|title=Quvenzhané Wallis & Oscars: 9-Year-Old Is Youngest Best Actress Nominee Ever|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2013/01/10/quvenzhane-wallis-oscars-best-actress_n_2446855.html|access-date=January 29, 2013|website=[[Huffington Post]]|first=Madeline|last=Boardman|date=January 10, 2013}}</ref>

In an interview with ''[[People (magazine)|People]]'' magazine, President [[Barack Obama]] described the film as "spectacular."<ref>{{cite news|title=2012 – The White House Interview|url=https://www.facebook.com/photo.php?fbid=323993407700436&set=a.177117985721313.28244.154369681329477&type=1&theater|access-date=December 31, 2012|newspaper=People Magazine}}</ref> The film's acclaim resulted in its Centerpiece screening at the 2012 [[Traverse City Film Festival]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mandell |first=Zack |date=2012-07-01 |title=The Best of Sundance: Beasts of the Southern Wild, An Education, Winter's Bone and More |url=https://www.reellifewithjane.com/2012/06/the-best-of-sundance-beasts-of-the-southern-wild-an-education-winters-bone/ |access-date=2026-04-25 |website=Reel Life With Jane |language=en-US}}</ref> ''[[Sight & Sound]]'' film magazine listed the film at #5 on its list of best films of 2012.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/paul-thomas-andersons-the-master-tops-sight-sounds-best-of-2012-20121201|title=Paul Thomas Anderson's 'The Master' Tops Sight & Sound's Best Of 2012|date=December 1, 2012|publisher=indiewire.com|access-date=December 1, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121203205058/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/paul-thomas-andersons-the-master-tops-sight-sounds-best-of-2012-20121201|archive-date=December 3, 2012|url-status=dead|last=Jagernauth|first=Kevin}}</ref>
