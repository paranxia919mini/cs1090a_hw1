'''''Les Amants du Pont-Neuf''''' ({{IPA|fr|lez‿amɑ̃ dy pɔ̃ nœf}}) is a 1991 French [[Romance film|romantic]] [[Drama film|drama]] film directed by [[Leos Carax]], starring [[Juliette Binoche]] and [[Denis Lavant]]. The film follows a love story between two young vagrants: Alex, a would-be circus performer addicted to alcohol and sedatives, and Michèle, a painter with a disease that is slowly turning her blind. The streets, skies and waterways of [[Paris]] are used as a backdrop for the story in a series of set-pieces set during the French Bicentennial celebrations in 1989.

The film became notorious for its troubled and lengthy production and for the amount of money it was reported to have cost. It has been referred to several times as the most expensive French film ever made at the time of its release, although this has been contested.

The title refers to the [[Pont Neuf]] bridge in Paris. For various reasons, the film-makers ultimately built a scale replica of the bridge, which greatly increased the budget. Though it was released under its original title in other English-speaking territories, the North American title of the film is '''''The Lovers on the Bridge''''',<ref>[http://www.dvdbeaver.com/film/DVDCompare2/loversonthebridge.htm ''The Lovers on the Bridge''], DVDBeaver (illustrates the front covers of the North American Region 1 DVD and the British Region 2 DVD)</ref><ref name="markey" /> and, in a mistranslation of the original title, the Australian title is '''''Lovers on the Ninth Bridge''''' (instead of "Lovers on the New Bridge").<ref>{{cite web |url=https://acmi.net.au/works/86861--lovers-on-the-ninth-bridge-les-amants-du-pont-neuf/ |title=Lovers on the ninth bridge = Les Amants du Pont-Neuf |author=Australian Centre for the Moving Image |access-date=2 September 2023 |publisher=[[Australian Centre for the Moving Image]]}}</ref>{{efn|Several critics comment on the bridge's name: "Despite its name, it is the oldest surviving bridge in Paris... it was the first bridge in the city to break with the medieval tradition of constructing a row of houses along each side";{{sfn|Hayes|1999|p=200}} "Despite its name, it is the oldest bridge in Paris, although when it was built (1578–1606) it would have symbolised progress and modernity".{{sfn|Everett|2006|p=210}}}}

== Plot ==
Alex is a street performer addicted to [[alcohol (drug)|alcohol]] and [[sedative]]s; he scrapes together money by [[Fire breathing (circus act)|fire breathing]] and performing acrobatics, sometimes simultaneously. Michèle is a painter driven to a life on the streets because of a failed [[Intimate relationship|relationship]] and a disease which is slowly destroying her sight. Accompanied in her new environment only by a cat, she has grown up in a military family but is passionate about art and longs to see [[Rembrandt]]'s self-portrait in the [[Louvre]] before her vision disappears entirely.

The pair's paths first cross when Alex has passed out, drunk, on the [[Boulevard de Sébastopol]] and Michèle makes a sketch of him. Later she is dazzled by one of his street exhibitions and the two become an item. The film portrays their harsh existence living on the bridge with Hans, an older vagrant. Hans is initially hostile to Michèle, but he has secret reasons for this. When he warms to her, he uses his former life to help her realise her dream. The capital's festivities for the 1989 bicentennial of the [[French Revolution]] happen in the background to the story: while the skies of Paris are lit up with an extravagant fireworks display, Alex steals a speedboat and takes Michèle water-skiing on the [[Seine]].

Alex's love for Michèle proves to have a dark side, however. As her vision deteriorates, she becomes increasingly dependent on him. When a possible treatment for her condition becomes available, Michèle's family use street posters and radio appeals to trace her. Fearing that she will leave him if she receives the treatment, Alex tries to keep Michèle from becoming aware of her family's attempts to find her by burning the posters; but he also sets fire to the van of the bill-sticker putting them up, and the man burns to death. When Alex goes to jail for this and Michèle has surgery, the pair have to separate; then, after his release, they meet up on the bridge for one last time. Alex's love for her is unchanged but Michèle has doubts and, enraged, Alex hurls both of them into the Seine. Underwater, they gaze at each other with new eyes in a moment of connection. A westbound barge picks them up, taking the pair onwards to the [[Atlantic]] and a new life.

==Production==
[[File:Pont Neuf Paris by Francis Hannaway.jpg|thumb|left|The film is set on the Pont Neuf in Paris.]]

===Pre-production===
When he started planning it in 1987, Leos Carax wanted to make a simple film, originally intending to shoot in black and white and via [[Super 8 film|Super 8]].<ref name="heymann1">{{cite news |last1=Heymann |first1=Danièle |author-link=Danièle Heymann |title=Les amants du pont d'or: Un tournage interrompu deux fois, un décor qui n'en finit pas d'être construit, un feuilleton juridico-financier, "Les Amants du Pont Neuf" de Léos Carax est un film à hauts risques mais un film à sauver |language=French |work=[[Le Monde]] |pages=13–14 |date=17 March 1990}}</ref><ref name="pascal" /><ref name="sterritt">{{Cite news |last=Sterritt |first=David |author-link=David Sterritt |date=2017 |title=The Lovers on the Bridge |work=[[Cineaste (magazine)|Cineaste]] |url=https://www.cineaste.com/summer2017/the-lovers-on-the-bridge |access-date=13 August 2023}}</ref> His first feature ''[[Boy Meets Girl (1984 film)|Boy Meets Girl]]'' had been a small affair (costing 3 million francs), whereas ''[[Mauvais Sang]]'' had been considerably larger and more costly (at 17 million),<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="mallat" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}} albeit more successful at the box office.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=6700|title = Boy Meets Girl (1984)|publisher=Jpbox-office.com|accessdate=30 December 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=6226|title=Mauvais sang (1986)|publisher=Jpbox-office.com|accessdate=30 December 2023}}</ref>

From the beginning, shooting a movie on a public bridge in the centre of Paris was complicated.{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}} The production team wanted to block off the bridge for three months, but the application was rejected.<ref name="sterritt" /> Instead, a model was created by the set designer Michel Vandestien.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="canby" /> Initially, the intention was to film the daylight scenes on the actual bridge, and the night-time scenes on the simplified model.<ref name="labarde">{{cite news |last1=Labarde |first1=Christophe |title=Un Pont-Neuf en Camargue: Grâce à un sauvetage financier de dernière minute, Léos Carax pourra achever son film. Avec le plus grand décor jamais réalisé en France |language=French |work=[[Le Figaro]] |issue=14000 |page=31 |date=31 August 1989}}</ref><ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="thompson">{{cite magazine |last=Thompson |first=David |date=September 1992 |title=Once Upon a Time in Paris: David Thompson considers Leos Carax's extravagant 'Les amants du Pont Neuf' |magazine=[[Sight and Sound]] |pages=6–11 }}</ref>{{sfn|Everett|2006|p=209}} At this point, the budget was estimated at 32 million francs.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="heymann">{{cite news |last1=Heymann |first1=Danièle |author-link=Danièle Heymann |title=Les amants du Pont Neuf sauvés: Après un feuilleton juridico-financier, Christian Fechner a décidé de reprendre le film de Léos Carax |language=French |work=[[Le Monde]] |page=12 |date=27 June 1990}}</ref><ref name="toubiana" /><ref name="thompson" />{{sfn|Beugnet|2000|p=157}}{{sfn|Daly|Dowd|2003|p=107}}{{sfn|Everett|2006|p=209}}{{efn|[[Jean-Michel Frodon]] calls this initial budget expensive but not excessive, saying for context that, although the average cost of a French film at this time was 13.5 million francs, 37 other films from 1988 were in the 20-50 million bracket.{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}}}} After scouting in [[Orléans]] and the Paris area for locations to build the set, the film-makers settled on the town of [[Lansargues]], in the [[Departments of France|department]] of [[Hérault]] in southern France.<ref name="mallat" /> Construction began in August 1988.<ref name="royer">{{cite book |last=Royer |first=Isabelle |date=1999 |editor1-last=Ingham |editor1-first=Marc |editor2-last=Koenig |editor2-first=Gérard |title=Perspectives en Management Stratégique |language=French |publisher=Éditions Management et Sociétés |location=[[Caen]] |pages=89–110 |chapter=L’escalade de l’engagement: décideurs et responsabilité. Étude du cas 'Les Amants du Pont Neuf' |chapter-url=https://www.strategie-aims.com/conferences/16-viieme-conference-de-l-aims/communications/1005-escalade-de-l-engagement-decideurs-et-responsabilite-etude-du-cas-les-amants-du-pont-neuf}}</ref>

===First shoot===
The mayor and police of Paris had authorised filming on the actual Pont Neuf for a period of three to four weeks ending 15 August 1988 (renovation work was to begin on 16 August, so the permit could not be extended).<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="mallat" /><ref name="gumbel" /><ref name="rambali" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}}{{sfn|Beugnet|2000|p=157}}{{sfn|Daly|Dowd|2003|p=107}}<ref name="sterritt" /> During rehearsals, lead actor [[Denis Lavant]] injured his hand so badly that filming could not be completed in the given time.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="mallat" /><ref name="gumbel" /><ref name="johnston" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}}{{sfn|Beugnet|2000|p=157}}<ref name="midilibre" /> After consultation with the insurers, the solution decided on was to extend the Lansargues model for daytime use.<ref name="labarde" /><ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="mallat" />

Although the insurance company made a payment to cover the accident, this extra budget seemed too low given the extent of work required at the Lansargues site. It soon became obvious that more money would be required.<ref name="labarde" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=789}} In December 1988, production shut down:<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="lavant">{{cite magazine |last=Ostria |first=Vincent |date=October 1991 |title=Entretien avec Denis Lavant |language=French |magazine=[[Cahiers du Cinéma]] |issue=448 |pages=18–20 }}</ref> at this point 14 minutes of footage had been shot.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="heymann" /><ref name="toubiana" /><ref name="mallat" /> 

===Second shoot===
In this interim period, Carax circulated [[videocassettes]] of the completed footage to create interest, and received praise from film-makers such as [[Steven Spielberg]] and [[Patrice Chéreau]], who encouraged him to continue.<ref name="mallat" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=790}}{{sfn|Everett|2006|p=209}} In June 1989, Dominique Vignet, together with the Swiss millionaire Francis von Buren, agreed to invest 30 million francs based on the rushes filmed the previous year.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="mallat" /> In July, shooting began again.<ref name="heymann1" />

''[[Le Figaro]]'' called the expanded Pont-Neuf the biggest film set ever built in France.<ref name="labarde" /> It had a full-scale model of the bridge at its centre and used [[forced perspective]] to recreate the streets and buildings on either bank.<ref name="klawans2" /><ref name="klawans" /> Details included the bridge's equestrian statue of [[Henri IV]], a facade of the nearby [[La Samaritaine]] department store with working lights, the entrance to [[Pont Neuf station|the local Métro station]], and a replica of the adjacent [[Square du Vert-Galant|Vert-Galant park]] with actual trees.<ref name="labarde" /><ref name="mallat" /><ref name="canby" /><ref name="ebert" />

It became apparent that the new injection of cash was not enough for the total cost of construction and to finish the film;{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=790}} sources give the amount of money already spent by this point as 80-90 million francs,<ref name="labarde" /><ref name="heymann1" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=790}} and ''[[Le Monde]]'' calculated that a further 80 would be needed to complete the project.<ref name="heymann1" /> Von Buren withdrew his funding late in 1989 with an estimated loss of 18 million francs.<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="thompson" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=790}} Yet again, production stopped. For nearly a year, the site in Lansargues was almost deserted except for a guard;<ref name="rambali" /> additionally, a number of storms followed over that winter causing massive water damage to the uncompleted set.<ref name="heymann" /><ref name="mallat" /><ref name="rambali" />

===Third shoot===
At [[1990 Cannes Film Festival|the Cannes festival in 1990]], [[Christian Fechner]] undertook to provide funding in order to finish the film. Unable to find financing partners, he provided his own money, purchasing both rights and debts of the picture.<ref name="heymann" /><ref name="toubiana" /><ref name="mallat">{{cite magazine |last=Mallat |first=Robert |date=1 April 1991 |title=''Les amants du Pont-Neuf'': L'odyssée d'un film |language=French |magazine=[[Le Point]] |issue=967 |pages=58–64 }}</ref> (In the completed film, "Christian Fechner" is the name of the riverboat that rescues the two protagonists.)<ref name="gumbel">{{Cite news |last=Gumbel |first=Andrew |date=15 November 1991 |title=So Beautiful and Costly |work=[[Tampa Bay Times]] |url=https://www.tampabay.com/archive/1991/11/15/so-beautiful-and-costly/ |access-date=6 August 2023}}</ref> It was announced that shooting would recommence on 15 August 1990.<ref name="heymann" /> Recreating the city's fireworks display for [[Bastille Day]] in 1989 was among the factors driving up the cost.<ref name="gumbel" /><ref name="klawans2" /><ref name="klawans" /> Filming on the set was completed in December 1990<ref name="royer" /> and production finally ceased in March 1991.<ref name="mallat" />{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=792}} To Fechner and Carax's regret, in January 1991 the farmer who owned the site had the entire bridge set destroyed;<ref name="mallat" /><ref name="johnston" /> it had attracted visitors as well as money to the area, and the mayor of Lansargues had hoped it might be preserved.<ref name="labarde" />

Estimates of the final budget vary:<ref name="thompson" />{{sfn|Everett|2006|p=209}} different sources have given it as 100,<ref>{{cite magazine |date=16 October 1991 |title=Le robot et les clochards |language=French |magazine=[[Le Figaro]] |issue=14664 |page=1 }}</ref> 130,{{sfn|Moullet|1999|p=45}}<ref name="midilibre">{{Cite news |last=Coste |first=Vincent |date=28 July 2016 |title=Un lieu un tournage (3/4): La Seine a coulé à Lansargues |language=French |work=[[Midi Libre]] |url=https://www.midilibre.fr/2016/07/28/la-seine-a-coule-a-lansargues-un-quart-de-siecle-plus-tard-que-reste-t-il-de-ces-amants,1372791.php |access-date=30 December 2023}}</ref> 160<ref name="heymann1" /><ref name="parismatch">{{cite magazine |last=Frain |first=Irene |date=17 October 1991 |title=Juliette Binoche: "J'ai l'impression d'etre dans un monde ou le desir ne suffit pas" |language=French |magazine=[[Paris Match]] |pages=40–43 }}</ref><ref name="gumbel" /><ref name="klawans" /> and 200 million francs.<ref name="bloom" /> Fechner was adamant that it was 100 (which still made it the most expensive film made in France that year);<ref name="rambali" /><ref name="thompson" /> Carax, promoting the film in Britain in 1992, contested the figures that the media had reported, while saying that he did not know the film's ultimate cost himself.<ref name="johnston" />

==Release==
The film premiered at the [[1991 Cannes Film Festival]]<ref name="sterritt" /> before arriving in French theatres on 16 October 1991.<ref name="binoche">{{cite magazine |last=Toubiana |first=Serge |date=May 1991 |title=Entretien avec Juliette Binoche: La croix et la foi |language=French |magazine=[[Cahiers du Cinéma]] |issue=443 |pages=36–40 }}</ref><ref name="esposito" /> The film had 867,197 admissions in France, where it was the 34th highest earning film of 1991.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=5061&affich=france |title=Les Amants du Pont-Neuf (1991) |publisher=JPBox-Office |accessdate=2013-02-11}}</ref> 260,000 of the tickets sold were in Paris alone.{{sfn|Frodon|1995|p=792}}{{sfn|Moullet|1999|pp=45-46}}

In 2025, the film received a 4K restoration, supervised by [[StudioCanal]]. [[Janus Films]] released the restored film in American cinemas.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Lovers on the Bridge |url=https://www.janusfilms.com/films/2272 |access-date=December 10, 2025 |website=Janus Films}}</ref>

==Reception==

===Domestic===
The film divided critical opinion in France.<ref name="gumbel" />{{sfn|Beugnet|2000|p=157}} In the popular press, while ''[[L'Express]]'' responded dismissively ("One hundred and sixty-million francs, and for what? One bridge, three tramps and a cat"), ''[[Le Monde]]'' was enthusiastic ("a film whose form, more than its content, oozes an emotion as pure and immediate as the unforgettable pre-war melodramas"),<ref name="gumbel" /> as was ''[[Le Point]]'' ("magical, mad, uncompromising, like its creator... [an] artistic miracle").<ref name="pascal">{{cite magazine |last=Pascal |first=Michel |date=12 October 1991 |title=''Les amants du Pont-Neuf'': Pont d'or pour enfant prodige |language=French |magazine=[[Le Point]] |issue=995 |pages=67–68 }}</ref> ''Le Figaro'' praised Binoche and Lavant's performances but not the film itself.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Baignères |first=Claude |date=16 October 1991 |title=Zola congélé |language=French |magazine=[[Le Figaro]] |issue=14664 |page=22 }}</ref> Among film journals, [[Marc Esposito]] in ''[[Studio magazine|Studio]]'' made wounding comments about the film and Carax personally,<ref name="esposito" /> as did ''La Revue du Cinéma'',<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Zimmer |first=Jacques |date=October 1991 |title=''Les amants du Pont-Neuf'': Histoire de l'œil |language=French |magazine=La Revue du Cinéma |issue=475 |pages=21–22 }} Zimmer calls Carax neurotic and narcissistic (''névrotique'' and ''narcissique'') and describes the affectations (''afféteries'') that characterise his style. Esposito tempered his criticisms of Carax's perceived failures as an individual with recognition of his talent, and compared ''Les Amants'' unfavourably to ''Mauvais Sang'': Zimmer did not do this.</ref> but the reaction among what [[Stuart Klawans]] calls "auteurist critics" was warmer:<ref name="klawans2" /> ''[[Positif (magazine)|Positif]]'' was favourable,<ref name="bourguignon" /> and ''[[Cahiers du Cinéma]]'' not only ran a rave review<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Taboulay |first=Camille |date=October 1991 |title=Explosante-fixe |language=French |magazine=[[Cahiers du Cinéma]] |issue=448 |pages=16–17}}</ref> but devoted a special issue to the movie.<ref name="rambali">{{cite magazine |last=Rambali |first=Paul |author-link=Paul Rambali |date=30 January 1992 |title=When French film built a bridge too far: Les Amants du Pont Neuf began as a simple love story. But it turned into France's answer to Heaven's Gate |magazine=[[The Guardian]] |page=28}}</ref><ref name="baumgarten" />{{sfn|Beugnet|2000|pp=157, 159}}{{sfn|Daly|Dowd|2003|p=112}} A factor in the critical hostility was the amount of money reported to have been spent:<ref name="johnston">{{Cite news |last=Johnston |first=Sheila |date=3 September 1992 |title=Underneath the arches: Director Leos Carax recreated the centre of Paris in the middle of a field in the South of France for Les Amants du Pont Neuf. ===International===
In order to recoup its costs, ''Les Amants'' needed to do well in overseas markets.<ref name="klawans2" /> It opened in [[United Kingdom|Britain]] in September 1992, and received positive reviews in ''[[The Guardian]]'',<ref>{{Cite news |last=Malcolm |first=Derek |author-link=Derek Malcolm |date=10 September 1992 |title=Camping it up with Christopher |work=[[The Guardian]] |page=28}}</ref> ''[[The Independent]]''<ref name="johnston2">{{Cite news |last=Johnston |first=Sheila |date=10 September 1992 |title=Throwing sand in your eyes |work=[[The Independent]] |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/film-throwing-sand-in-your-eyes-1550670.html |access-date=28 August 2023}}</ref> and ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'',<ref name="bloom" /> though more enthusiastic were ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' ("exciting and innovative... one of the most visually exhilarating and surprising films of recent years"),<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Vincendeau |first=Ginette |author-link=Ginette Vincendeau |date=September 1992 |title=Les amants du Pont-Neuf |magazine=[[Sight and Sound]] |pages=46–47 }}</ref> ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' ("this strange, beautiful film... shows Carax as one of the most poetic, individual talents in European cinema"),<ref name="telegraph">{{Cite news |date=10 September 1992 |title=The strange confusions of love |work=[[The Daily Telegraph]] |issue=42679 |page=14}}</ref> and ''[[The Sunday Telegraph]]'' ("a glorious monument to the follies of megalomaniac film-making").<ref name="sundaytelegraph" /> ''[[The Times]]'' felt that, for all its visual charm and unpredictability, the film flagged in its second half, but concluded, "when mediocrity runs riot, cinema desperately needs its movie movies, and ''Les Amants du Pont Neuf'', flaws and all, is the real McCoy".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Brown |first=Geoff |date=10 September 1992 |title=The fastest but surely not the best |work=[[The Times]] |issue=64434 |page=33}}</ref>

When it played at the 1992 [[New York Film Festival]], however, influential critic [[Vincent Canby]] reviewed the film for ''[[The New York Times]]''.{{efn|[[Derek Malcolm]] called Canby "one of the most powerful film critics in America. A bad review from him... was the death knell for any independent, or foreign, film in the United States".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Malcolm |first=Derek |author-link=Derek Malcolm |date=17 October 2000 |title=Vincent Canby: Influential US critic whose wry judgment could make or break movies |work=[[The Guardian]] |url=https://www.theguardian.com/news/2000/oct/17/guardianobituaries.filmnews |access-date=29 October 2023}}</ref>}} While praising the technical achievement of Vandestien's set design, and the power of the fireworks and speedboat sequences, he was cool about the film overall:

{{blockquote|one of the most extravagant and delirious follies perpetrated on French soil since [[Marie-Antoinette]] played the milkmaid at the [[Petit Trianon]]. Never has so much money been spent so heedlessly at the whim of so few... [a] huge plywood cream puff of a movie... more exhausting than entertaining.<ref name="canby">{{Cite news |last=Canby |first=Vincent |author-link=Vincent Canby |date=6 October 1992 |title=Lovers on the Streets of Paris, Literally |work=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/10/06/movies/review-film-lovers-on-the-streets-of-paris-literally.html |access-date=6 August 2023}}</ref>}}

===1999 American release===
When the film finally went on general release in America, after [[Martin Scorsese]] had championed it, Klawans in ''[[The Nation]]'' saluted it as "one of the most splendidly reckless films ever made".<ref name="klawans" /><ref name="sterritt" /> ''[[CNN.com]]'' struck similarly hyperbolic notes: "the sort of multi-leveled exhilaration that can be achieved on film when talented artists bother to give it a go... more flashes of legitimate brilliance than you'll find in a dozen helpings of ''[[Armageddon (1998 film)|Armageddon]]''".<ref name="cnn" /> [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] in ''[[The Los Angeles Times]]'' called the film "a go-for-broke dazzler"; he rated Binoche's performance higher than her [[Academy Awards|Oscar]]-winning work in ''The English Patient'', as did Marjorie Baumgarten in ''[[The Austin Chronicle]]''.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Thomas |first=Kevin |author-link=Kevin Thomas (film critic) |date=2 July 1999 |title=A Daring 'Bridge' Over Pair of Troubled Souls in 1989 Paris |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1999-jul-02-ca-52149-story.html |access-date=29 October 2023}}</ref><ref name="baumgarten" /> In contrast, Charles Taylor in ''[[Salon.com|Salon]]'' called Binoche "mannered, utterly inauthentic" and Lavant "singularly unappealing", summing up Carax's work by asking "do we have to pretend that his films are about anything more than his second-hand image-mongering?"<ref>{{Cite news |last=Taylor |first=Charles |date=29 June 1999 |title=Lovers on the Bridge |work=[[Salon.com|Salon]] |url=https://www.salon.com/1999/06/29/bridge_2/ |access-date=19 December 2023}}</ref> [[Richard Corliss]] in ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' praised Binoche, and the film's visual style, but said that "The plot groans with lower-depths anomie";<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Corliss |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Corliss |date=5 July 1999 |title=Short Takes: The Lovers on the Bridge Directed by Leos Carax |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |issue=154 |pages=84 }}</ref> [[Lisa Schwarzbaum]] in ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' called the film an "unwieldy mixture of gorgeousness and incoherence".<ref name="ew" /> [[Roger Ebert]] was likewise more balanced: "a film both glorious and goofy, inspiring affection and exasperation in nearly equal measure... It is not the masterpiece its defenders claim, nor is it the completely self-indulgent folly described by its critics. It has grand gestures and touching moments of truth, perched precariously on a foundation of horsefeathers."<ref name="ebert">{{cite web |first=Roger |last=Ebert |authorlink=Roger Ebert |title=The Lovers on the Bridge |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-lovers-on-the-bridge-1999 |website=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]| via=[[RogerEbert.com]] |date=November 3, 1999 |access-date=13 August 2023}}</ref>
