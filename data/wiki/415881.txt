'''''Beau Travail''''' ({{IPA|fr|bo tʁa.vaj|pron}}, French for "good job" or "nice work") is a 1999 French [[Art film|arthouse]] [[psychological drama]] film directed by [[Claire Denis]], who co-wrote the screenplay alongside [[Jean-Pol Fargeau]] as an adaptation of the novella ''[[Billy Budd]]'' by [[Herman Melville]]. Starring [[Denis Lavant]], [[Michel Subor]] and [[Grégoire Colin]], it follows the story of a [[section (military unit)|section]] of [[French Foreign Legion]] soldiers training in the desert of [[Djibouti]].

The film received widespread critical acclaim with special attention towards its direction, acting, cinematography, soundtrack and handling of its themes. It is considered one of the best [[Cinema of France|French films]] and best movies directed by a female director. It is currently ranked the 7th best movie of all time by ''[[Sight and Sound]]''.

==Plot==
[[Chief Warrant Officer|Adjudant-Chef]] Galoup of the [[French Foreign Legion]] reflects on his life from his home in [[Marseille]]. He recalls his time in [[Djibouti]], where he led a [[section (military unit)|section]] of men under the command of Commandant Bruno Forestier. Galoup admired and envied many of Forestier's qualities, including his clear affection from the men, and retains a wristband with Forestier's name. Galoup has a Djiboutian girlfriend, and they often go out dancing.

A new recruit named Gilles Sentain joins Galoup's section. Galoup harbors an immediate and seemingly irrational hostility towards Sentain, and vows to destroy him. When Sentain hands a canteen of water to another soldier who is being punished by being forced to dig a large hole in the heat of the day, Galoup chastises Sentain and knocks the canteen from his hand. Sentain punches Galoup, who retaliates by taking Sentain into the desert and ordering him to walk back to the base alone. Galoup had tampered with Sentain's compass, causing him to become lost and collapse from dehydration in the arid [[salt pan (geology)|salt flats]].

Sentain is found and rescued by locals, but never returns to the base and is presumed to have [[Desertion|deserted]]. His sabotaged compass is later found by the legionnaires. On the assumption that Galoup had attempted to kill Sentain, Galoup is sent back to France by Forestier for a [[court-martial]], ending his career in the Foreign Legion. He makes his bed in the immaculate military manner, then lies on top clutching a pistol, and reads aloud the phrase tattooed on his chest: "{{lang|fr|Sers la bonne cause et meurs}}" ("Serve the good cause and die"). In the final scene, Galoup energetically dances to "[[The Rhythm of the Night]]" in the Djiboutian nightclub he once patronized.

==Production==
The film was shot in Djibouti.<ref>{{Cite web |title=« Ce poids d'ici-bas » - Vacarme |url=https://vacarme.org/article84.html |access-date=3 August 2026 |website=vacarme.org}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=13 May 2000 |title=Film. Claire Denis travaille le beau pour mieux saisir la soldatesque - Le Temps |url=https://www.letemps.ch/culture/film-claire-denis-travaille-beau-mieux-saisir-soldatesque |access-date=3 August 2026 |language=fr |issn=1423-3967}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=13 June 2023 |title="Desire is violence": Claire Denis on Beau Travail |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/claire-denis-beau-travail-michel-subor-denis-lavant-gregoire-colin-french-foreign-legion-djibouti |access-date=3 August 2026 |website=BFI |language=en}}</ref> The exercise scenes of the soldiers in the desert were designed by choreographer Bernardo Montet, whose job, he recalled, was to "release the poetic powers" of their bodies.<ref name="Times Dance">{{cite news|title='Beau Travail' Finds the Rhythm of Life (and Dances Away the Pain)|last= Kourlas|first= Gia|work=[[The New York Times]] |date=28 September 2020|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2020/09/28/arts/dance/Beau-Travail-Denis-Lavant.html}}</ref> Claire Denis said, "One of the cast had actually been in the Legion, so we took all their real exercises and did them together every day, to concentrate the actors as a group. We never said we were going to choreograph the film. But afterwards, when we started shooting, using [[Benjamin Britten|Britten]]'s music, those exercises became like a dance."<ref name="Telegraph">{{cite news|title=Interview: Claire Denis and ''Beau Travail'' |work=[[The Daily Telegraph]] |date=16 August 2003|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/film/3600479/Film-makers-on-film-Claire-Denis.html}}</ref> Parts of the film's soundtrack are taken from Benjamin Britten's 1951 [[Billy Budd (opera)|opera]] based on Melville's novella.

The final dance scene in which Galoup dances in the club was not originally shot to end the film. "In the script, it was because he was leaving for good," Denis recalled. "He wanted to go to the bar for the last time. He starts dancing like the last dance of his life." This sequence was only placed at the end of the film at the editing stage.<ref name="Times Dance"/>

==Release==
The film was screened on 4 September 1999 in the Cinema del Presente section at the [[56th Venice International Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://ricerca.repubblica.it/repubblica/archivio/repubblica/1999/09/04/il-programma.html|title=Il programma|newspaper=[[la Repubblica]]|language=it|date=4 September 1999|access-date=4 January 2024}}</ref> It was theatrically released in France on 3 May 2000 by Pyramide Distribution.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://distrib.pyramidefilms.com/pyramidefilms-distribution-catalogue-b/beau-travail.html|title=Beau Travail|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204005123/http://distrib.pyramidefilms.com/pyramidefilms-distribution-catalogue-b/beau-travail.html|archive-date=4 December 2020|url-status=dead|access-date=4 January 2024|language=fr}}</ref>

==Reception==
The film was highly acclaimed in the United States, topping the ''[[Village Voice]]'''s Film Critics' Poll in 2000, with Claire Denis also placing at #2 for best director.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2009-12-22/film/where-are-they-now-winners-of-past-film-polls/ |title=Where Are They Now: Winners of Past Film Polls |date=22 December 2009 |work=[[The Village Voice]] |access-date=30 January 2011 |archive-date=14 April 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100414090258/http://www.villagevoice.com/2009-12-22/film/where-are-they-now-winners-of-past-film-polls/ |url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' rated it a "masterpiece", giving it the paper's highest rating of four stars.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://jonathanrosenbaum.net/2024/01/unsatisfied-men/ |title=Unsatisfied Men |last=Rosenbaum |first=Jonathan |work=[[Chicago Reader]] |date=26 May 2000 |via=www.jonathanrosenbaum.net |access-date=16 October 2020 |archive-date=25 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201025215105/http://www.jonathanrosenbaum.net/2018/04/unsatisfied-men/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Charles Taylor of ''[[Salon.com]]'' wrote that "''Beau Travail'' is the most extreme example of [Denis'] talent, baffling and exhilarating. I don't know when I've seen a movie that is in so many ways foreign to what draws me to movies and still felt under a spell."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.salon.com/entertainment/movies/review/2000/03/31/beau_travail |title=''Beau Travail'' |last=Taylor |first=Charles |date=31 March 2000 |website=[[Salon.com]]}}</ref> [[J. Hoberman]] of ''The Village Voice'' wrote that the film is "so tactile in its cinematography, inventive in its camera placement, and sensuous in its editing that the purposefully oblique and languid narrative is all but eclipsed."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.villagevoice.com/work-in-progress/ |work=The Village Voice |last=Hoberman |first=J. |author-link=J. Hoberman |title=Work in Progress |date=28 March 2000 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010709065942/http://www.villagevoice.com/issues/0013/hoberman.shtml |archive-date=9 July 2001 |url-status=dead}}</ref> 

The film's reputation has only grown with time. More recently, film scholar Erika Balsom has written that the ending sequence "is perhaps the best ending of any film, ever," explaining that "Galoup finds a rhythm of life that follows none of the patterns of colonial, patriarchal power the film so skilfully traces and complicates... he inhabits a utopia of movement without rules".<ref>{{Cite news|last=Balsom |first=Erika |date=8 January 2019 |title=The Closing Sequence of Claire Denis's 'Beau Travail' |language=en |work=Frieze |issue=200 |url=https://www.frieze.com/article/closing-sequence-claire-deniss-beau-travail |access-date=13 December 2022 |issn=0962-0672|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191015124556/https://www.frieze.com/article/closing-sequence-claire-deniss-beau-travail|archive-date=15 October 2019|url-status=live}}</ref> Critic Simran Hans wrote for ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' that "Denis's great gift is her ability to evoke emotion with gesture and juxtaposition. In the Djibouti desert, water shimmers and ripples, naked shoulders perspire and black mosquito nets recall sheer lingerie."<ref>{{cite web |last= Hans|first= Simran|date= 2022|title= Beau travail (1998)|url= https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/bc9dc6fa-0761-553d-8324-f1fbd4bd2005/beau-travail|publisher= Sight and Sound|access-date=6 April 2026}}</ref> In ''[[The New Yorker]]'', [[Alex Ross]] has called it a "modern classic," adding that "it feels, in a way, older than it is, a gorgeous relic of a period when gay desire could achieve only incomplete expression or had to be concealed altogether. Denis's movie seems like the completion of an arc—the rise and decline of the tragedy of the closet as an artistic genre."<ref>{{cite web |last= Ross|first= Alex|title= Pushed to the Edge by "Beau Travail"|date= 20 October 2020|url= https://www.newyorker.com/culture/cultural-comment/pushed-to-the-edge-by-beau-travail|publisher= The New Yorker|access-date=6 April 2026}}</ref>

The review aggregator website, [[Metacritic]], gave the film a score of 91 out of 100 based on 20 reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/beau-travail |title=''Beau Travail'' |website=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref> On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], another aggregator, it has an 87% approval rating based on 47 reviews, with an average rating of 7.9/10. The site's consensus reads: "''Beau Travail'' finds director Claire Denis drawing on classic literature to construct a modern tragedy fueled by timeless desires".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/beau_travail|title=Beau Travail (2000)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=14 September 2022}}</ref>

In 2017, ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' named the film as the 12th best film in their list The 100 Greatest Movies of the Nineties.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=The 100 Greatest Movies of the Nineties - 'Beau Travail' |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/the-100-greatest-movies-of-the-nineties-195513/beau-travail-1999-197843/ |access-date=21 December 2023 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] |date=12 July 2017}}</ref> In the 2022 ''[[Sight and Sound]]'' critic's poll, ''Beau Travail'' was ranked the 7th best movie of all time.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=Revealed: the results of the 2022 Sight and Sound Greatest Films of All Time poll |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/news/revealed-results-2022-sight-sound-greatest-films-all-time-poll |website=bfi.org.uk |access-date=21 December 2023 |date=1 December 2022}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' magazine ranked ''Beau Travail'' 69th  for its list The 100 Greatest Movies of All Time.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=The 100 Greatest Movies of All Time - Beau Travail (1999) |url=https://variety.com/lists/best-movies-of-all-time/beau-travail-1999/ |access-date=21 December 2023 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=21 December 2022}}</ref> In 2025, it was among of the films voted for the "Readers' Choice" edition of ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}} list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century," finishing at number 284.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |date=2 July 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref>
