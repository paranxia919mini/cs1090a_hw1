{{nihongo|'''''House'''''|ハウス|''Hausu''|lead=yes}} is a 1977 Japanese [[comedy horror]] film directed and produced by [[Nobuhiko Obayashi]]. It is about a schoolgirl traveling with her six friends to her ailing aunt's country home, where they come face to face with [[supernatural]] events as the girls are, one by one, devoured by the home. It stars mostly [[Amateur theatre|amateur actors]], with only [[Kimiko Ikegami]] and [[Yōko Minamida]] having any notable previous acting experience. The musical score was performed by the rock band [[Godiego]].

[[Toho|Toho Studios]] approached Obayashi with the suggestion to make a film like ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]''. Influenced by ideas from his daughter Chigumi, he developed ideas for a script by Chiho Katsura. After the project was [[Green-light|green-lit]], it was put on hold for two years as no one at Toho wanted to direct it. However, Obayashi kept promoting the film until the studio allowed him to direct it himself. ''House'' was filmed on one of Toho's largest sets, where Obayashi shot the film without a [[storyboard]] over a period of about two months.

The film, which received generally negative reviews, was a box office hit in Japan. After being widely released in North America in 2009 and 2010, it was met with more favorable response and has since gained a [[cult following]].

== Plot ==
In [[Tokyo]],{{efn|The location is identified on screen by the name of the train station where the characters depart.}} a teenage girl known as Gorgeous, so called for her beauty, has plans for a summer vacation with her widowed father, a wealthy film composer who has been away in [[Italy]] on business. When he returns home, he surprises Gorgeous by announcing he has married a woman named Ryoko Ema. Distraught, Gorgeous goes to her bedroom and writes a letter to her aunt, asking if she can visit her for the summer instead. Her aunt replies and allows her to come. Gorgeous invites her six friends along: Prof, who is highly academic and very good at problem-solving; Melody, who has an affinity for music; Kung Fu, who is athletic and especially skilled at kung fu; Mac, who has a big appetite; Sweet, who is bubbly and gentle; and Fantasy, who is a constant [[Daydreaming|daydreamer]].

On arriving at the aunt's house in the countryside, the girls are greeted by her and present a [[watermelon]] as a gift. After a tour of the home, they leave the watermelon in a well to keep it cold, as the house's refrigerator is broken. Mac later goes to retrieve it and does not return. When Fantasy goes to the well, she finds Mac's disembodied head, which flies into the air and bites Fantasy's buttocks before she escapes. The incident is initially disregarded by the others, but over time, they begin to encounter supernatural traps throughout the house.

The aunt disappears after entering the broken refrigerator. The girls are attacked or possessed by various items that have seemingly become alive: Gorgeous becomes possessed after using her aunt's mirror; Kung Fu is attacked by flaming logs, losing her skirt; Sweet disappears after being attacked by mattresses. The girls try to escape, but after Gorgeous manages to leave through a door, the others find themselves locked in. Searching for the aunt, they instead discover Mac's severed hand in a jar. Melody plays the piano to lift their spirits, and they hear Gorgeous singing upstairs. As Prof and Kung Fu investigate, the piano comes to life, bites off Melody's fingers and ultimately eats her.

Upstairs, Kung Fu and Prof find Gorgeous wearing a bridal gown, who shows them her aunt's diary. Kung Fu follows Gorgeous, only to find Sweet's body trapped in a [[grandfather clock]], which starts bleeding. The remaining girls barricade the upper floor while Prof, Fantasy, and Kung Fu read the diary.

They are interrupted by a giant disembodied head of Gorgeous, who reveals that her aunt died waiting for her fiancé to return from [[World War II]], and now eats unmarried girls who arrive at her home. The three girls are attacked by household items. Prof shouts to Kung Fu to attack the aunt's cat, Blanche. As Kung Fu lunges into a [[flying kick]], she is eaten by a light fixture. Her legs escape and damage the cat's portrait, killing Blanche. The portrait gushes blood, flooding the room. Prof is pulled underwater by a jar with teeth and dissolves. Fantasy sees Gorgeous in a bridal gown and paddles toward her. In the reflection, Gorgeous appears as her aunt and cradles Fantasy.

In the morning, Ryoko arrives at the house and finds Gorgeous in a [[kimono]]. Gorgeous tells her the others will wake soon and that they will be hungry. She then shakes hands with Ryoko, who burns away to nothing.

==Production==

===Development===
Following the success of the American film ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]'', a proposition came from the [[Toho]] film studio for [[Nobuhiko Obayashi]] to develop a similar script.<ref name="cc-cah">{{cite video
 | people      = Obayashi, Nobuhiko  (Director)
 | date        = 2009
 | title       = Constructing a House
 | url         = https://criterion.com/films/27523-house
 | format      = DVD
 | location    = United States
 | language    = ja
 | publisher   = The Criterion Collection
 | access-date  = October 29, 2010
}}</ref><ref name="moc-beginning">{{cite video
 | people      = Obayashi, Nobuhiko (Director)
 | date        = 2002
 | title       = Interviews: Beginning
 | url         = http://eurekavideo.co.uk/moc/catalogue/house-hausu/
 | format      = DVD
 | location    = United Kingdom
 | language    = ja
 | publisher   = Eureka Entertainment
 | access-date  = October 30, 2010
}}</ref> To find inspiration for the story, Obayashi discussed ideas with his pre-teen daughter Chigumi Obayashi. Nobuhiko sought her ideas, believing that adults "only think about things they understand ... everything stays on that boring human level" while "children can come up with things that can't be explained".<ref name="cc-cah" /> Several of Chigumi's ideas were included in ''House'' such as a reflection in a mirror attacking the viewer, a watermelon being pulled out of a well appearing like a human head, and a house that eats girls.<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="cc-cah-katsura">{{cite video
 | people      = Katsura, Chiho  (Screenwriter)
 | date        = 2009
 | title       = Constructing a House
 | url         = https://criterion.com/films/27523-house
 | format      = DVD
 | location    = United States
 | language    = ja
 | publisher   = The Criterion Collection
 | access-date  = October 29, 2010
}}</ref> Other themes Chigumi suggested drew upon her own childhood fears. These fears included a pile of [[Futon|futons]] falling on her that felt like a monster attacking her, a large loud clock at her grandparents' home, and getting her fingers caught in between her piano keys.<ref name="cc-cah-chigumi">{{cite video
 | people      = Obayashi, Chigumi (Story scenarist)
 | date        = 2009
 | title       = Constructing a House
 | url         = https://criterion.com/films/27523-house
 | format      = DVD
 | location    = United States
 | language    = ja
 | publisher   = The Criterion Collection
 | access-date  = October 29, 2010
}}</ref> Nobuhiko shared these story ideas with screenwriter Chiho Katsura. These ideas reminded Katsura of a short story by [[Walter de la Mare]] about an old woman who is visited by her granddaughters and then puts them in a trunk.<ref name="cc-cah-katsura" />

Obayashi incorporated themes of the [[atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki]] into the script. Obayashi was born in [[Hiroshima]] and lost all his childhood friends from these bombings. Obayashi applied these themes with the plot element of a woman's ghost waiting for her lover's return from World War II. The woman's bitterness about the war turns her into an [[Demon|evil spirit]] that devours the girls who were unaffected by the bombings.<ref name="cc-cah" /> Obayashi and Katsura had worked previously on a script titled ''[[Hanagatami]]'' before being assigned to ''House'', which made the screenwriting process easy for both of them. Obayashi titled the script ''House'' as he felt that a foreign title for a Japanese film would be "taboo".<ref name="cc-cah" />

===Pre-production===
The script for ''House'' was green-lit shortly after being presented to Toho. No directors at Toho were interested in directing the film as they felt it would end their career.<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="cc-cah-katsura" /> Obayashi proposed that he would direct it but was turned down as he was not a staff member at Toho. ''House'' did not start filming until two years after the script's completion.<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="cc-cah-katsura" /> Toho allowed Obayashi to announce that the film had been green-lit and began promoting the film by passing out business cards that advertised the film. In the 1960s, Obayashi created a [[short film]] titled ''[[Emotion (film)|Emotion]]'' that was popular at Japanese universities and event halls. Fans of his [[television advertisement|television commercial]] and film work helped him promote ''House'' before it was even in production.<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="cc-cah-katsura" /> Products based on ''House'' that were released included [[manga]], a novelization of the script and a [[radio drama]].<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="cc-cah-katsura" /> The soundtrack for the film was created and released before the film was made.<ref name="cc-cah" />

===Casting===
The majority of the cast of ''House'' were not established actors, with many having primarily only worked with Obayashi on his commercials and independent films.<ref name="moc-castprod" /> During the two-year waiting period to start filming ''House'', Obayashi created several commercials and began casting the seven girls from models who were in his commercials.<ref name="cc-cah" />

The most experienced members of the main cast were [[Kimiko Ikegami]] and [[Yōko Minamida]].<ref name="moc-castprod" /> Obayashi was friends with Minamida who he filmed in commercials for [[Calpis]].<ref name="moc-castprod">{{cite video
 | people      = Obayashi, Nobuhiko (Director)
 | date        = 2002
 | title       = Interviews: Casting & Production
 | url         = http://eurekavideo.co.uk/moc/catalogue/house-hausu/
 | format      = DVD
 | location    = United Kingdom
 | language    = ja
 | publisher   = Eureka Entertainment
 | access-date  = October 29, 2010
}}</ref> Minamida was mostly working television and theater at the time and worried that taking the role of the older woman would have a negative effect on the roles she would be subsequently offered, but still agreed to play the part.<ref name="cc-cah" />

Singer [[Kiyohiko Ozaki]], who plays Mr. Togo in the film, was cast because he was friends with Obayashi through their shared hobby of [[Equestrianism|horseback riding]].<ref name="cc-cah" />

Other roles were filled by members of the crew and their families; for example, Nobuhiko Obayashi's daughter Chigumi plays the little girl at the shoemaker's shop, and the film's production designer plays the shoemaker.<ref name="cc-cah-chigumi" />

===Filming===
[[File:House (1977) special effects cat.png|thumb|left|The special effects in ''House'' were purposely made to look unrealistic.|alt=A cartoon-like image of a grinning cat leaps out of a picture of Blanche in the aunt's house]]

Obayashi recalled that his producer told him that Toho was tired of losing money on comprehensible films and were ready to let Obayashi direct the ''House'' script, which they felt was incomprehensible.<ref>{{cite web |author=Roquet |first=Paul |date=10 November 2009 |title=Nobuhiko Obayashi, Vagabond of Time |url=http://www.midnighteye.com/features/nobuhiko-obayashi-vagabond-of-time/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160420205444/http://www.midnighteye.com/features/nobuhiko-obayashi-vagabond-of-time/ |archive-date=20 April 2016 |access-date=30 October 2010 |work=[[Midnight Eye]]}}</ref> Toho officially green-lit the film's production after the success of the radio drama based on ''House''.<ref name="rue-morgue">{{cite journal  |date=November 2010 |title=Maniac Mansion |journal=[[Rue Morgue (magazine)|Rue Morgue]] |publisher=Rodrigo Gudino |issue=106 |pages=47 |last=Hartzheim|first=Bryan}}</ref> Obayashi received special permission to direct the film despite not being a member of the Toho staff.<ref name="rue-morgue" />

''House'' was filmed on one of Toho's largest sets, where Obayashi shot the film without a storyboard for about two months.<ref name="cc-cah" /><ref name="rue-morgue" /> Obayashi described the attitude on the set as very upbeat as he often skipped, sang and played quiz games with the younger actresses on the set. Despite having fun on the set, members of the Toho crew felt the film was nonsense.<ref name="cc-cah" /> Obayashi found the acting of the seven girls to be poor while trying to direct them verbally. He began playing the film's soundtrack on set, which changed the way the girls were acting in the film as they got into the spirit of the music.<ref name="cc-cah" /> Actress Kimiko Ikegami was uncomfortable about a nude scene in the film. To make her more comfortable, Yoko Minamida, who had never done a nude scene before, also took off her clothes. After Obayashi saw Minamida nude, he included a topless scene for her in the film which was not in the original script.<ref name="cc-cah" />

Obayashi already had experience with special effects from his work on television commercials.<ref name="cc-cah-katsura" /> Obayashi and the cameraman oversaw the special effects for the film. Obayashi desired the special effects to look unrealistic as if a child created them. For the scene in which Ai Matsubara's character vanishes under the blood, Obayashi had her suspended nude, pouring buckets of blue paint on her to create a blue-screen [[chroma key]] effect where the blue-colored parts of her body would deteriorate on camera.<ref name="cc-cah" /> The outcome of a lot of these effects would be unknown until the film was completed. Obayashi stated that sometimes the effects did not turn out how he originally envisioned them.<ref name="cc-cah" />

=== Music ===
The soundtrack for the film was created and released before the film's production. Asei Kobayashi, who worked with Obayashi on his television commercials, contributed the piano pieces for the film's soundtrack.<ref name="cc-cah" /> Kobayashi felt that younger people should contribute to the film's soundtrack and suggested [[Mickie Yoshino]] and his band [[Godiego]] should contribute songs based on Yoshino's piano pieces.<ref name="cc-cah" /> Singer [[Ken Narita (singer)|Ken Narita]] played [[blues harp]] on the track "Hungry House Blues", and also contributed vocals to "House Love Theme".<ref name="Godiego.co.jp">{{cite web |title=OST 「ハウス」 |trans-title=OST "House" |url=http://godiego.co.jp/godiego/works/04-01d-01g-01album-03-house.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210824135940/http://godiego.co.jp/godiego/works/04-01d-01g-01album-03-house.html |archive-date=24 August 2021 |access-date=24 August 2021 |website=Godiego.co.jp |language=ja}}</ref> All tracks were arranged by Mickie Yoshino, and produced by Yoshino with Asei Kobayashi.<ref name="Godiego.co.jp" />

''House'' was released by the [[Masters of Cinema]] label in the United Kingdom on DVD. Bonus features on the disc included interviews with the cast and crew and the theatrical trailer.<ref>{{cite web |title=''House'' (Hausu) |url=http://eurekavideo.co.uk/moc/catalogue/house-hausu/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120829082759/http://eurekavideo.co.uk/moc/catalogue/house-hausu/ |archive-date=29 August 2012 |access-date=31 October 2010 |work=[[Masters of Cinema]]}}</ref> ''House'' was released by the Criterion Collection on DVD and [[Blu-ray]] on 26 October 2010.<ref>{{cite web |title=''House'' Criterion Collection Blu-ray |url=https://allmovie.com/movie/house-criterion-collection-blu-ray-e244222 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121024052051/https://allmovie.com/movie/house-v146852/releases |archive-date=24 October 2012 |access-date=7 February 2017 |work=[[AllMovie]]}}</ref> Bonus features on the disc include a making-of [[featurette]] that features interviews with the crew; director Obayashi's short film ''Emotion'', which was first released in 1966; an appreciation video featuring American filmmaker [[Ti West]]; and a promotional [[Trailer (promotion)|trailer]] for ''House''.<ref>{{cite web |title=''House'' |url=https://criterion.com/films/27523-house |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170127185900/https://www.criterion.com/films/27523-house |archive-date=27 January 2017 |access-date=31 October 2010 |work=[[The Criterion Collection]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=David Harley |date=15 October 2010 |title=Blu-ray Review: The 1977 Classic ''Hausu''! |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/22042/blu-ray-review-the-1977-classic-hausu/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170211080727/https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/22042/blu-ray-review-the-1977-classic-hausu/ |archive-date=11 February 2017 |access-date=9 February 2017 |website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]}}</ref>

==Legacy==
In the years following its release, ''House'' has gradually accumulated a [[cult following]] and is now considered a cult classic.<ref>{{cite encyclopedia |year=2016 |title=''House'' (1977) |encyclopedia=The Encyclopedia of Japanese Horror Films |publisher=[[Rowman & Littlefield]] |location=Lanham, Maryland |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=tfasDAAAQBAJ&pg=PA138 |last=McRoy |first=Jay |date= |editor-last=Murguía |editor-first=Salvador Jimenez |page=138 |isbn=978-1-4422-6166-2}}</ref><ref name="FarOut" /><ref name="ScreenRantTop16">{{cite web |last1=Casalena |first1=Em |date=8 September 2016 |title=The 16 Best Japanese Horror Movies of All Time |url=https://screenrant.com/best-japanese-horror-movies/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160909050837/https://screenrant.com/best-japanese-horror-movies/ |archive-date=9 September 2016 |access-date=8 April 2021 |website=[[Screen Rant]]}}</ref> Contemporary review aggregation website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] offers a {{RT data|score}} approval rating from {{RT data|count}} critics—an average rating of {{RT data|average}}, which provides the consensus, "''House'' is a gleefully demented collage of [[Grand Guignol|grand guignol]] guffaws and bizarre sequences."<ref name="RottenTomatoes">{{cite web |author1= |date= |title=''House'' (1977) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hausu |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220812165705/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hausu |archive-date=12 August 2022 |access-date=27 September 2022 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]]}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 75 out of 100, based on 12 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''House'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/house-1977 |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=16 May 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> According to film critic and scholar Jasper Sharp, the film successfully managed to "recapture a younger audience demographic believed lost to television and Hollywood".{{sfn|Sharp|2011|p=260}}

''House'' has been included in multiple lists by various media outlets. In 2009, the Japanese film magazine ''[[Kinema Junpo]]'' placed ''House'' at number 160 on their list of top 200 Japanese films.<ref>{{cite web |title= |script-title=ja:「オールタイム・ベスト 映画遺産200」全ランキング公開 |trans-title="All-Time Best 200 Film Heritage" full rankings revealed |url=http://www.kinejun.jp/special/90alltimebest/index.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110127004124/http://www.kinejun.jp/special/90alltimebest/index.html |archive-date=27 January 2011 |access-date=1 November 2010 |work=[[Kinema Junpo]] |language=ja}}</ref> It was placed at number 117 by Rotten Tomatoes based on its average review score, in their list of ''200 Greatest Horror Movies of All Time.''<ref name="RottenTomatoesLegacy">{{cite web |author1= |date= |title=200 Best Horror Movies of All Time |url=https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/best-horror-movies-of-all-time/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210401155217/https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/guide/best-horror-movies-of-all-time/ |archive-date=1 April 2021 |access-date=8 April 2021 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]]}}</ref> ''[[Screen Rant]]'' ranked the film at number 9 in their list of ''The 16 Best Japanese Horror Movies of All Time''.<ref name="ScreenRantTop16" /> ''[[Bloody Disgusting]]'' included the film in ''The 20 All-Time Best Haunted House Horror Movies'', stating that the film "takes the haunted house concept to wacky extremes".<ref name="BloodyDisgustingTop20">{{cite web |last1=Navarro |first1=Meagan |date=6 August 2019 |title=The 20 All-Time Best Haunted House Horror Movies - Bloody Disgusting |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3571728/20-time-best-haunted-house-horror-movies/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190806225441/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3571728/20-time-best-haunted-house-horror-movies/ |archive-date=6 August 2019 |access-date=8 April 2021 |website=[[Bloody Disgusting]]}}</ref>

Sarah Cleary for [[BFI]] commented that the film was "a fevered flight of [[Dark fantasy|horror-fantasy]] like no other."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Cleary |first=Sarah |date=22 July 2020 |title=In Praise of ''Hausu'', the World's Most Demented Haunted House Film |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/praise-hausu-worlds-most-demented-haunted-house-film |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240119223903/https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/praise-hausu-worlds-most-demented-haunted-house-film |archive-date=19 January 2024 |access-date=3 July 2024 |website=[[British Film Institute]] |language=en-UK}}</ref> Similarly, ''Far Out'' described it as "a psychedelic trip like no other, featuring a flurry of animation, surreal violence and enigmatic Japanese energy [...] Obayashi suffuses his world with a mix of vivid hand-drawn animation and surreal cinematic choices to take the viewer on a dance of phantasmagorical absurdity."<ref name="FarOut">{{Cite web |last=Russell |first=Calum |date=26 October 2022 |title=The Insane Japanese Horror ''Hausu'' Is Perfect for Halloween |url=https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/why-insane-japanese-horror-movie-hausu-perfect-pick-halloween/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221026173925/https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/why-insane-japanese-horror-movie-hausu-perfect-pick-halloween/ |archive-date=26 October 2022 |access-date=3 July 2024 |website=Far Out Magazine |language=en-UK}}</ref>
