'''Redd Kross''' is an American [[Rock music|rock]] band from [[Hawthorne, California]], who had their roots in 1978 in a [[punk rock]] band called '''the Tourists''', which was started by brothers Jeff and [[Steven Shane McDonald|Steve McDonald]] while Steve was still in [[middle school]].<ref name="Larkin80">{{cite book|title=[[Encyclopedia of Popular Music|The Virgin Encyclopedia of Eighties Music]]|editor=Colin Larkin|editor-link=Colin Larkin (writer)|publisher=[[Virgin Books]]|date=2003|edition=Third|isbn=1-85227-969-9|pages=404/5}}</ref> With the addition of friends [[Greg Hetson]] on guitar and John Stielow on drums, the band's first gig was opening for [[Black Flag (band)|Black Flag]]. The band changed their name to '''Red Cross''' in 1979, and in 1982 changed the spelling due to threatened legal action by the charitable organization [[International Red Cross]]. The band has since released eight albums and three EPs. Their style was originally [[hardcore punk]], but they have since branched out to touch on various rock and roll styles.

==Career==
===Red Cross===
In April 1979, the band had their first practice in the living room of original drummer, John Stielow's parent's home. They eventually changed the band name to Red Cross, which was allegedly inspired by the masturbation scene in the film ''[[The Exorcist]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.dannolte.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=381|title=The Last • View topic - A few interesting dates that nobody asked for|date=March 4, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304064400/http://www.dannolte.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=381|access-date=July 21, 2021|archive-date=March 4, 2016}}</ref> They soon began working on their 1980 debut [[Red Cross (EP)|self-titled EP]].   Eventually, Hetson left to join the [[Circle Jerks]] (and later [[Bad Religion]]) and Reyes left for [[Black Flag (band)|Black Flag]].<ref name="Larkin80"/> They appeared on the Posh Boy compilation ''The Siren'', and then to complete the lineup on their first full-length album, ''[[Born Innocent (Redd Kross album)|Born Innocent]]'', they assembled a [[revolving door]] of musicians including original drummer John Stielow.<ref name="Larkin80"/> Full of the brothers' [[popular culture|pop culture]] obsessions, ''Born Innocent'' featured odes to [[Linda Blair]] (who starred in a [[Born Innocent (film)|television movie of the same name]]), a cover of "Look on Up at the Bottom" by the Carrie Nations from ''[[Beyond the Valley of the Dolls]]'', and [[Charles Manson]] (whose song "[[Cease to Exist (Charles Manson song)|Cease to Exist]]" they covered). The album also contains nods to [[Jim Bakker|Jim]] and [[Tammy Faye Messner|Tammy Faye Bakker]], [[Tatum O'Neal]], and [[Lita Ford]]. Not long after the release of the album, the group was threatened with a [[lawsuit]] from the [[International Red Cross]] and changed the spelling of their name to Redd Kross.<ref name="Larkin80"/>

===Redd Kross===
In 1984, Redd Kross returned with drummer Dave Peterson to record ''[[Teen Babes from Monsanto]]'', an album featuring covers. Later that year, lead guitarist [[Robert Hecker]] joined the band, as they embarked on tour in support of ''Teen Babes from Monsanto''. In that same year, they were featured on [[Desperate Teenage Lovedolls (soundtrack)|the soundtrack]] of ''[[Desperate Teenage Lovedolls]]'' with their cover of the [[The Brady Bunch|Brady Bunch Kids]]' "It's a Sunshine Day".

In 1987, Redd Kross released ''[[Neurotica (album)|Neurotica]]''.

===1990s===
[[File:Jeff McDonald of Redd Kross at Burger Boogaloo 2017, by Aaron Rubin.jpg|thumb|Jeff McDonald at Burger Boogaloo, Mosswood Park, Oakland, California, 2017]]

On February 20, 1990, Redd Kross appeared on Episode 2 of the cult [[Public-access television]] show "Decoupage"  with Summer Caprice.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.decoupagetv.com/yesclips0201.html |title=Yesterday Clips Gallery |website=Decoupagetv.com |access-date=2012-04-03}}</ref>

In 1990, Redd Kross signed with [[Atlantic Records]], releasing ''[[Third Eye (Redd Kross album)|Third Eye]]'',<ref name="Larkin80"/> and appeared with [[David Cassidy]] in the film ''[[Spirit of '76 (1990 film)|Spirit of '76]]'', and issued several singles, including "Annie's Gone", which had some mild success on college radio.

The album ''[[Phaseshifter]]'' was released in 1993,<ref name="Larkin80"/> with new band members Eddie Kurdziel, Gere Fennelly, and Brian Reitzell. The videos for "Jimmy's Fantasy" and "Lady In The Front Row" were both shown on MTV's [[120 Minutes]]. They toured on ''Phaseshifter'' for over a year, headlining their own shows as well as tours supporting [[The Lemonheads]] and [[The Spin Doctors]] in late 1993 and [[Stone Temple Pilots]] in 1994.

In 1997, Redd Kross released ''[[Show World]]'',<ref name="Larkin80"/> produced by [[Chris Shaw (recording engineer)|Chris Shaw]] (who also produced albums for [[Weezer]] and [[Soul Asylum]]) and toured supporting [[The Presidents of the United States of America (band)|The Presidents of the United States of America]].  The band took an indefinite hiatus after the ''[[Show World]]'' tour, and their future was uncertain after the death of guitarist Eddie Kurdziel on June 6, 1999.

=== 2010s ===
[[File:Steve McDonald of Redd Kross at Burger Boogaloo 2017, by Aaron Rubin.jpg|thumb|Steve McDonald of Redd Kross at Burger Boogaloo, Mosswood Park, Oakland, California, 2017]]

In 2019, Emmy Award–winning television comedy writer Andrew Reich, best known as an Executive Producer on ''[[Friends]]'', was directing a documentary on the band called ''Born Innocent: The Redd Kross Story''.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Aswad |first=Jem |date=2019-09-10 |title=Legendary L.A. Band Redd Kross Drop Trailer for 'Born Innocent' Documentary |url=https://variety.com/2019/film/news/redd-kross-trailer-born-innocent-documentary-1203328857/ |access-date=2022-03-27 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref>

[[Dale Crover]] joined Redd Kross on drums fulltime.

=== 2020s ===
On June 26, 2020, the band reissued their debut EP on Merge Records.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.punknews.org/article/72427/redd-kross-to-re-issue-debut-ep|title=Redd Kross to re-issue debut EP|website=Punknews.org|date=April 16, 2020 |access-date=September 20, 2020}}</ref>

Steven McDonald and Redd Kross were included in a feature article for the 25th anniversary of [[TapeOp]] magazine.<ref>{{cite web |title=Steven McDonald & Redd Kross: What's a Band to Do? |url=https://tapeop.com/interviews/142/steven-mcdonald-amp-redd-kross/ |website=tapeop.com |access-date=21 April 2021}}</ref>

The documentary ''Born Innocent: The Redd Kross Story'' received its Los Angeles premiere at the Don't Knock the Rock Festival on May 23, 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Aswad |first=Jem |date=2024-05-21 |title=The Story of Redd Kross, the Most Underrated Band of Their Generation, Is Told in Fascinating Detail in the New Documentary 'Born Innocent' |url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/redd-kross-born-innocent-documentary-film-review-1236010942/ |access-date=2024-06-24 |website=Variety.com |language=en-US}}</ref> <ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.reddkrossfilm.com/|title=BORN INNOCENT: THE REDD KROSS STORY|website=Reddkrossfilm.com|access-date=June 27, 2026}}</ref>

On June 28, 2024, Redd Kross released its eighth studio album, a self-titled double album, featuring singles "Candy Coloured Catastrophe" and "Too Good to Be True."

[[File:Redd Kross at Electric Ballroom.jpg|thumb|Redd Kross at Electric Ballroom, London (August 12, 2025)]]

The brothers released a memoir, ''Now You’re One of Us: The Incredible Story of Redd Kross'', in October 2024, co-written with Dan Epstein and published by [[Omnibus Press]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-12-03 |title=45 years ago, an 11-year-old and his brother started a band. Redd Kross won't quit |url=https://www.ocregister.com/2024/12/03/45-years-ago-an-11-year-old-and-his-brother-started-a-band-redd-kross-wont-quit/ |access-date=2024-12-07 |website=Orange County Register |language=en-US}}</ref>

==Side projects==
The McDonald brothers collaborated with Astrid McDonald (Jeff's daughter with [[Charlotte Caffey]], guitarist for [[The Go-Go's]]) and [[Anna Waronker]] (Steve's wife, frontwoman of [[That Dog]]) on a side project. Performing as Ze Malibu Kids, they released the album ''Sound It Out'' in 2002.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.allmusic.com/album/sound-it-out-mw0000368783 |title=Sound It Out - Ze Malibu Kids : Songs, Reviews, Credits, Awards |publisher=AllMusic |access-date=2013-06-04}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.artistdirect.com/artist/ze-malibu-kids/1686719 |title=Ze Malibu Kids @ARTISTdirect |publisher=Artistdirect.com |access-date=2013-06-04}}</ref>

Both McDonald brothers appeared with [[David Cassidy]] in the comedy film ''[[The Spirit of '76 (1990 film)|The Spirit of '76]]''.
<ref>{{cite web | title=The Spirit of '76 (1990) | website=IMDb | date=22 October 2023 | url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0100670/fullcredits?ref_=tt_ov_st_sm | access-date=22 October 2023}}</ref>

==Soundtracks==
Redd Kross songs appear on the soundtracks to ''[[Desperate Teenage Lovedolls]]'', ''[[Good Burger]]'', ''[[PCU (movie)|PCU]]'', ''[[Bordello of Blood]]'', ''[[An American Werewolf in Paris]]'', and ''[[Varsity Blues (film)|Varsity Blues]]''.  Their song "1976" plays over the end credits of the 1990 movie ''Spirit of '76''. They contributed one song "It's a Scream" for the 2000 film ''[[Shriek If You Know What I Did Last Friday The Thirteenth]]'' but the soundtrack was never released and the song itself wasn't heard until it appeared on ''Hot Issue'' in 2016. The song "Uglier" was licensed and used in the film ''[[Hits (film)|Hits]]'' by [[David Cross]].{{cn|date=October 2023}}
