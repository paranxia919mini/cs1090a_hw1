'''''Interview with the Vampire''''' is a 1994 American [[horror film]] directed by [[Neil Jordan]] and written by [[Anne Rice]], based on her [[Interview with the Vampire|1976 novel of the same name]], and starring [[Tom Cruise]] and [[Brad Pitt]]. It focuses on [[Lestat de Lioncourt|Lestat]] (Cruise) and [[Louis de Pointe du Lac|Louis]] (Pitt), beginning with Louis's transformation into a vampire by Lestat in 1791. The film chronicles their time together, and their turning of young [[Claudia (The Vampire Chronicles)|Claudia]] ([[Kirsten Dunst]]) into a vampire. The narrative is framed by a modern-day interview, in which Louis tells his story to a San Francisco reporter ([[Christian Slater]]). The supporting cast features [[Antonio Banderas]] and [[Stephen Rea]].

The film was released by [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] on November 11, 1994 to generally positive reviews and was a commercial success, grossing $223.7 million against a $60 million budget. It received two [[67th Academy Awards|Academy Award]] nominations for [[Academy Award for Best Production Design|Best Art Direction]] and [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]].<ref name="BackstageOscars">{{cite news |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/detroit-free-press-backstage-improv-adds/123755063/ |title=Backstage improv adds life to Oscars |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230429205610/https://www.newspapers.com/article/detroit-free-press-backstage-improv-adds/123755063/ |date=March 29, 1995 |access-date=April 29, 2023 |archive-date=April 29, 2023 |page=60 |newspaper=[[Detroit Free Press]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref><ref name="Oscars1995">{{cite web|url=https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1995 |title=The 67th Academy Awards (1995) Nominees and Winners |access-date=August 5, 2011|work=oscars.org}}</ref> Kirsten Dunst was additionally nominated for a [[52nd Golden Globe Awards|Golden Globe]] for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actress – Motion Picture|Best Supporting Actress]] for her role in the film. A [[stand-alone sequel]], ''[[Queen of the Damned]]'', was released in 2002, with [[Stuart Townsend]] and [[Matthew Newton]] replacing Cruise and Banderas, respectively.

==Plot==
In 1991 [[San Francisco]], reporter [[Daniel Molloy]] interviews [[Louis de Pointe du Lac]], who claims to be a [[vampire]]. He begins with his human life as a wealthy [[plantation]] owner in 1791 [[Louisiana (New Spain)|Louisiana]]. Despondent following the death of his pregnant wife and their future baby, he drunkenly wanders the waterfront of [[New Orleans]] one night and is attacked by vampire [[Lestat de Lioncourt]]. Lestat senses Louis's dissatisfaction with life and offers to turn him into a vampire.

Louis accepts but quickly comes to regret it. While Lestat revels in the hunt and killing of humans, Louis resists his instinct to kill, to Lestat's annoyance, instead drinking animal blood to sustain himself.

Eventually, amid an outbreak of [[Plague (disease)|plague]] in New Orleans, Louis feeds on a little girl whose mother died in the plague. To entice Louis to stay with him, Lestat turns the dying girl, [[Claudia (The Vampire Chronicles)|Claudia]], into a vampire. Together, they raise her as a daughter. Louis loves Claudia, while Lestat spoils and treats her more as a pupil, training her to become a merciless killer.

After thirty years, Claudia matures psychologically but remains a little girl in appearance. When she realizes that she will never grow older, she is furious with Lestat and tells Louis that they should leave him. She tricks Lestat into drinking the "dead blood" of twin boys whom she killed by overdose with [[laudanum]]. When he is weakened, she slits his throat. Though Louis is shocked and upset, he helps Claudia dump Lestat's body in a swamp.

They spend weeks planning a voyage to Europe to search for other vampires, but Lestat returns on the night of their departure, having survived on the blood of swamp creatures. Lestat attacks them, but Louis sets him on fire, causing the fire to spread throughout the French Quarter. In the furor, they escape to their ship and depart.

After traveling around Europe and the Mediterranean but finding no other vampires, Louis and Claudia settle in [[Paris]] in September 1870, where Louis encounters vampires [[Santiago (The Vampire Chronicles)|Santiago]] and [[Armand (The Vampire Chronicles)|Armand]] by chance. Armand invites Louis and Claudia to his [[coven]], the Théâtre des Vampires, where vampires stage theatrical horror shows for humans, in the manner of The [[Grand Guignol]]. On their way out of the theater, Santiago reads Louis's mind and suspects that Louis and Claudia murdered Lestat.

Claudia demands that Louis turn a human woman, Madeleine, into a vampire to be her new protector and companion, and he reluctantly complies. Shortly thereafter, the Parisian vampires abduct the three of them and punish them for Lestat's murder, imprisoning Louis in an iron coffin for slow starvation and trapping Claudia and Madeleine in a chamber where sunlight burns them to ash. Armand does nothing to prevent this, but the next day he frees Louis.

Seeking revenge, Louis returns to the theater at dawn and sets it on fire, killing all the vampires. Armand arrives in time to help Louis escape the sunrise and  offers him a place by his side. Louis rejects him and leaves, unable to accept Armand's uncompromising way of life and his unpredictable nature as well as knowing that Armand allowed Claudia's murder.

As decades pass, Louis never recovers from the loss of Claudia and dejectedly explores the world alone. He returns to New Orleans in 1988 and encounters a decayed, weakened Lestat, living as a recluse in an abandoned mansion and surviving on rat blood as Louis once had. Lestat expresses regret for turning Claudia into a vampire and asks Louis to rejoin him, but Louis declines and leaves.

Louis concludes his interview with Molloy, who beseeches him to make him his new vampire companion. Louis is outraged that Molloy has not understood the tale of suffering he has relayed and attacks Molloy to scare him into abandoning the idea. Molloy runs to his car and takes off while playing the cassette tapes of Louis's interview. On the [[Golden Gate Bridge]], Lestat appears and attacks Molloy, taking control of the car. Revived by Molloy's blood, Lestat offers him the choice that Lestat "never had"—whether to become a vampire—and, laughing, continues driving.

==Production==
===Development===
The rights to Anne Rice's novel were initially purchased by [[Paramount Pictures]] in April 1976, shortly before the book was published. The script lingered in [[development hell]] for years, with the rights being sold to [[Lorimar Television|Lorimar]] before ending up with [[Warner Bros. Pictures]].<ref name="Anthony Hogg">{{cite news| url=https://vamped.org/2014/11/11/20-things-you-probably-didnt-know-about-the-interview-with-the-vampire-movie-pt-1/| title=20 Things You Probably Didn't Know About the 'Interview with the Vampire' Movie, Part 1| first=Anthony |last=Hogg |website=vamped.org| date=2014-11-11| access-date=2018-06-25}}</ref> Director Neil Jordan was approached by Warner Bros. to direct after the huge success of his movie ''[[The Crying Game]]'' (1992). Jordan was intrigued by the script, calling it "really interesting and slightly theatrical", but was especially interested after reading Rice's novel.<ref name="Belfast Telegraph">{{cite news| url=https://www.belfasttelegraph.co.uk/life/interview-with-a-vampire-director-neil-jordan-i-had-a-great-time-making-this-movie-but-theres-a-dark-catholic-guilt-underneath-30733778.html| title=Interview with a Vampire director Neil Jordan: I had a great time making this movie, but there's a dark Catholic guilt underneath |work=Belfast Telegraph| date=2014-11-11| access-date=2018-06-25}}</ref> He agreed to direct on the condition that he be allowed to write his own script, though he did not gain a writing credit. The themes of [[Catholic guilt]] which pervade the novel attracted Jordan, who called the story "the most wonderful parable about wallowing in guilt that I'd ever come across. But these things are unconscious, I don't have an agenda."<ref name="Belfast Telegraph"/>

With [[David Geffen]] producing, the movie was given a $70 million budget, unprecedented for a film in the vampire genre. Jordan stated that:

{{blockquote|It's not very often you can make a complicated, dark, dangerous movie and get a big budget for it. Vampire movies were traditionally made at the lower end of the scale, on a shoestring, on rudimentary sets. David Geffen is very powerful and he poured money into ''Interview''. I wanted to make it on an epic scale of something like ''[[Gone with the Wind (film)|Gone with the Wind]]''.<ref name="Belfast Telegraph"/>}}

Jordan also cited [[Francis Ford Coppola]]'s ''[[Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992 film)|Dracula]]'' as an influence:

{{blockquote|Up to that point, Francis Ford Coppola with ''Bram Stoker's Dracula'', he introduced opulence and theatricality. Normally, before that one, I always thought of vampire movies as cheap, cobbled together, brilliant use of minimal resources. Francis made it this epic, didn't he? So when I was given the opportunity to make ''Interview with the Vampire'', I thought, "Oh, it would be really great to expand on that epic sense of darkness and to give these characters huge, kind of romantic destinies and longings and feelings."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/1266199-interview-neil-jordan-on-marlowe-interview-with-the-vampire | title=Interview: Neil Jordan on Marlowe & Interview with the Vampire |website=ComingSoon.net | date=February 14, 2023 }}</ref>}}

===Casting===
Rice adapted her 1976 novel ''[[Interview with the Vampire]]'' into a screenplay with French actor [[Alain Delon]] in mind for the role of [[Louis de Pointe du Lac|Louis]].<ref name="Ramsland2010">{{cite book|first=Katherine |last=Ramsland|title=Anne Rice Reader|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=9GiIB-SHonYC&pg=PT170|date=December 22, 2010|publisher=Random House Publishing Group|isbn=978-0-307-77563-4|pages=170–}}</ref> Later on, when ''Interview'' entered the casting stage, British [[actor]] [[Julian Sands]] was championed by Rice and fans of the novel to play [[Lestat de Lioncourt|Lestat]],<ref name="Anthony Hogg"/> but because Sands was not a well-known name at the time (being only famed for his performance in ''[[A Room with a View (1986 film)|A Room with a View]]''), he was rejected and the role was given to [[Tom Cruise]]. Because of his star power, Cruise received a record $10 million salary and a percentage of the profits.<ref>{{cite news| title=Cinema Year By Year: 1894-2002 |editor-first1=Robyn |editor-last1=Karney |editor-first2=Joel W. |editor-last2=Finler |publisher=Dorling Kindersley| year=2002| page=853 |isbn=978-0789490681}}</ref> Before Cruise was cast, Rice and the studio wanted [[Daniel Day-Lewis]] to portray Lestat after [[Brad Pitt]] was cast as Louis, but Day-Lewis turned it down as he didn't want to portray a vampire.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/2024/06/09/neil-jordan-brad-pitt-tom-cruise-interview-with-the-vampire/ | title='Tom Cruise had made a contract with the hidden forces, whatever they turned out to be' | work=The Telegraph | date=June 9, 2024 | last1=Jordan | first1=Neil }}</ref><ref name="variety.com">{{cite web | url=https://variety.com/2024/film/features/interview-with-the-vampire-tom-cruise-brad-pitt-neil-jordan-1236205381/ | title='Interview with the Vampire' at 30: Director Neil Jordan on the Tom Cruise Casting Controversy, Brad Pitt's Misery and More | date=November 11, 2024 }}</ref>

The casting was initially criticized by Rice, who said that Cruise was "no more my vampire Lestat than [[Edward G. Robinson]] is [[Rhett Butler]]",<ref name="Ramsland2010"/> and the casting was "so bizarre; it's almost impossible to imagine how it's going to work". She recommended a number of other actors including [[John Malkovich]], [[Peter Weller]], [[Jeremy Irons]], [[Rutger Hauer]], and [[Alexander Godunov]]. She suggested that Pitt and Cruise switch roles, stating that "I tried for a long time to tell them that they should just reverse these roles—have Brad Pitt play Lestat and have Tom Cruise play Louis. Of course, they don't listen to me."<ref>{{cite news| url=http://movieline.com/1994/01/01/interview-with-the-author-of-interview-with-the-vampire/| title=Anne Rice: Interview With the Author of Interview with the Vampire |first=Martha |last=Frankel |work=Movieline| date=1994-01-01| access-date=2018-06-25}}</ref><ref name="variety.com"/>

Eventually, Rice became satisfied with Cruise's performance after seeing the completed film, saying that "from the moment he appeared, Tom was Lestat for me" and "that Tom ''did'' make Lestat work was something I could not see in a crystal ball." She called Cruise to compliment him and admit that she was wrong.<ref name="Judy Brennan">{{cite news| url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-09-21-ca-41119-story.html| title=Rice's About-Face: Cruise is Lestat: After Screening 'Interview with the Vampire', Author Lauds His Work|first=Judy |last=Brennan| work=Los Angeles Times| date=1994-09-21| access-date=2018-06-25}}</ref>

Due to Rice's perception of Hollywood's [[homophobia]], at one point she rewrote the part of Louis, changing his sex to female, in order to specifically heterosexualize the character's relationship with Lestat.<ref name="Vampire">{{cite news |last=Benshoff |first=Harry M. |title=Monsters in the closet: homosexuality and the horror film |publisher=Manchester University Press |year=1997 |isbn=0-7190-4473-1}}</ref> At the time, Rice felt it was the only way to get the film made, and singer-actress [[Cher]] was considered for the part.<ref name="Vampire"/> A song titled "Lovers Forever", which Cher wrote along with [[Shirley Eikhard]] for the film's soundtrack, got rejected as Pitt was ultimately cast for the role, though a dance-pop version of the song was released on Cher's 2013 album, ''[[Closer to the Truth (Cher album)|Closer to the Truth]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/1567352/cher-on-closer-to-the-truth-i-took-some-chances-on-this-album |title=Cher On 'Closer to the Truth': I Took Some Chances on This Album' |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] |date=June 19, 2013 |first=Phil |last=Gallo}}</ref>

Originally, [[River Phoenix]] was cast for the role of Daniel Molloy (as Anne Rice liked the idea), but he died four weeks before he was due to begin filming. When [[Christian Slater]] was cast in his place as Molloy, he donated his entire salary to Phoenix's favorite charitable organizations.<ref name="Petrucelli2009">{{cite book|first=Alan W. |last=Petrucelli|title=Morbid Curiosity: The Disturbing Demises of the Famous and Infamous|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=r7csXXH7S9UC&pg=PT41|date=September 29, 2009|publisher=Penguin Publishing Group|isbn=978-1-101-14049-9|pages=41–}}</ref> The film has a dedication to Phoenix after the end credits.<ref name="McCarthy" />

Ten-year-old actress Kirsten Dunst was spotted by talent scouts and was the first girl tested for the role of Claudia.<ref name="Ramsland2010" /> Said producer [[Stephen Woolley]]: "We started looking at 6-year-olds, which is about Claudia's age in the book, but the role is too demanding for a 6-year-old. We needed a child with a mind capable of grasping the fine points of the difficult monologues Claudia has, and Kirsten was the first actress we saw. She gave a wonderful reading but we thought it was too good to be true, so we saw thousands of other girls. In the end we came back to Kirsten--she's quite extraordinary in the part."<ref name="McKenna">{{cite news |last1=McKenna |first1=Kristine |title=Interview With the Vampire's Vamp |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-11-08-ca-60162-story.html |access-date=10 November 2023 |work=Los Angeles Times |date=1994-11-08 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221127201427/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-11-08-ca-60162-story.html |archive-date=2022-11-27}}</ref> [[Julia Stiles]] also auditioned for Claudia but Neil Jordan considered her "too old".<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/2016/07/18/julia-stiles-i-get-chills-when-i-think-about-what-i-did/ | title=Julia Stiles: 'I get chills when I think about what I did' | newspaper=The Telegraph | date=July 18, 2016 | last1=Wood | first1=Gaby }}</ref>

Brad Pitt admitted in a 2011 interview with ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' that he was "miserable" while making the film and even tried to buy himself out of his contract at one point.<ref name="Jeff Giles"/> Pitt called the production "six months in the f—ing dark" because of the almost-exclusive night shoots,<ref name="ASC">{{cite news |last1=Williams |first1=David E. |title=Wrap Shot: Interview with the Vampire |url=https://theasc.com/articles/wrap-shot-interview-with-the-vampire |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=[[American Cinematographer]] |date=2016-11-10 |quote=Over the course of a 100-day shoot that involved just two half-days of daytime photography}}</ref> filmed mostly in London in the depths of winter, which sent him into a depression.<ref name="Jeff Giles"/> The script, which he received only two weeks prior to filming, was also a source of disappointment. He unfavorably contrasted the character of Louis which he had admired in the book to that presented in the script:

{{blockquote|In the book you have this guy asking, "Who am I?" Which was probably applicable to me at that time: "Am I good? Am I of the angels? Am I bad? Am I of the devil?" In the book it is a guy going on this search of discovery. And in the meantime, he has this Lestat character that he's entranced by and abhors. ... In the movie, they took the sensational aspects of Lestat and made that the pulse of the film, and those things are very enjoyable and very good, but for me, there was just nothing to do—you just sit and watch.<ref name="Jeff Giles"/>}}

===Visual effects===
Visual effects were overseen by [[Stan Winston]] and his team. At the same time, the new visual effects company [[Digital Domain]] was responsible for creating the visual effects under Visual Effects Supervisor [[Robert Legato]].<ref name="Interview with the Vampire">{{cite web |title=Interview with the Vampire |url=https://digitaldomain.com/work/interview-with-the-vampire/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151122030045/http://www.digitaldomain.com/work/interview-with-the-vampire/ |archive-date=2015-11-22 |access-date=2017-12-12 |website=digitaldomain.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite AV media| title=Commentary with Director Neil Jordan| medium=DVD| publisher=Warner Bros. Home Video| year=2008}}</ref> Director Neil Jordan was initially hesitant to use Stan Winston Studios, because they had gained a reputation for specializing in large-scale [[animatronics]] and [[computer-generated imagery|CGI]] with [[Jurassic Park (film)|''Jurassic Park'']] and ''[[Terminator 2: Judgment Day]]''; ''Interview with the Vampire'' was going to require mostly special makeup effects.<ref name="Anthony Hogg"/> Winston designed the characters' vampire appearances and makeup effects, including a technique for stenciling translucent blue veins on the actors' faces.<ref name="Warner Home Video">{{cite AV media| title=Commentary with Director Neil Jordan| publisher=Warner Home Video| medium=DVD| year=2008}}</ref> This required the actors to hang upside down for 30 minutes, so that the blood would rush to their heads and cause their veins to protrude, enabling the makeup artists to trace realistic patterns.<ref name="Anthony Hogg"/>

Digital effects were used mainly to add small details or to enhance certain physical effects, like the burning of the New Orleans set or the burning of Louis's plantation, whereby CGI flames were imposed on a miniature of the house.<ref name="Interview with the Vampire"/> The most difficult digital effects to illustrate were Louis and Claudia's transformations into vampires, which were technologically very advanced for the time.<ref name="Warner Home Video"/> The scene where Claudia cuts Lestat's throat was achieved by transferring from Tom Cruise bleeding from a prosthetic wound to an animatronic model designed to "wither" as it bled out, enhanced with CGI blood.  Winston also sculpted the rough model for the charred remains of Claudia and Madeleine, using archival photographs of victims from [[atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki|Hiroshima]] for inspiration.<ref name="Warner Brothers Home Video">{{cite AV media| title=In the Shadow of the Vampire - The Making of Interview with the Vampire| medium=DVD| publisher=Warner Brothers Home Video | year=1994}}</ref>

===Pre-screening===
A rough-cut of ''Interview with the Vampire'' was shown to [[test audience]]s, who, according to producer David Geffen, felt "there was a little too much blood and violence." The screenings were held over the objection of Neil Jordan, who was planning on further paring down the length of the film before previewing it, but Geffen wanted to show the longer version to "get a feel for what the audience wanted." Eventually about 20 minutes' worth of footage was either cut or rearranged before the theatrical version was ready.<ref name="Judy Brennan"/>

==Release==
===Box office===
''Interview with the Vampire'' was a box office success. The film opened on November 11, 1994 ([[Veterans Day]]) and opening weekend grosses amounted to $36.4 million, surpassing ''[[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York]]'' to achieve a November record, and placing it in the [[List of 1994 box office number-one films in the United States|number one]] position at the US box office above ''[[The Santa Clause]]'', which opened with $19.3 million.<ref>{{cite news|title= Love at First Bite: 'Vampire' Tears Into Box Office : Movies: Warners film looks to be the fourth largest debut ever. 'Santa Clause' sleighs into the No. 2 spot with a solid take.|newspaper= [[Los Angeles Times]]|date=November 14, 1994|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-11-14-ca-62651-story.html|access-date=December 22, 2010|first=Richard|last=Natale}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=12|title=Top opening weekends of November|date=November 15, 1994}}</ref> Some in the industry disputed the figure and the range of estimates by others were from $34 to $37 million.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=3|title=Playing the numbers|date=November 15, 1994|last=Klady|first=Leonard}}</ref> At that time, ''Interview with the Vampire'' had the |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220831145829/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/108642886/vampire-makes-a-weekend-killing-at/ |date=November 17, 1994 |access-date=August 31, 2022 |archive-date=August 31, 2022 |page=40 |work=Los Angeles Daily News |publisher=[[Star Tribune]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Screen ''Interview with the Vampire'' held the record for having the highest opening weekend for a [[Brad Pitt]] film until it was taken by ''[[Ocean's Eleven]]'' in 2001.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Karger|first=Dave|title=''Ocean's Eleven'' topples ''Harry Potter''|url=https://ew.com/article/2001/12/11/oceans-eleven-topples-harry-potter/|magazine=Entertainment Weekly|access-date=October 22, 2022|date=December 11, 2001}}</ref> In subsequent weeks, it struggled against ''[[Star Trek Generations]]'' and ''The Santa Clause''. Its total gross in the United States and Canada was $105 million, while the worldwide gross was $224 million, against an estimated budget of $60 million.<ref name="mojo" />

===Critical reception===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 66% based on reviews from 64 critics. The site's critics consensus reads: "Despite lacking some of the book's subtler shadings, and suffering from some clumsy casting, ''Interview with the Vampire'' benefits from Neil Jordan's atmospheric direction and a surfeit of gothic thrills."<ref name=RT>{{cite web|url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/interview_with_the_vampire/ |title=Interview with the Vampire (1994) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=October 29, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film holds a weighted average score of 62 out of 100 based on 20 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=Interview with the Vampire: The Vampire Chronicles Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/interview-with-the-vampire-the-vampire-chronicles |access-date=January 16, 2017 |website=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-12-20|title=Cinemascore :: Movie Title Search|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|access-date=2020-07-28|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|archive-date=December 20, 2018}}</ref>

Critics praised the film's production design, cinematography, and special effects, as well as the performances of Cruise and Dunst. Of Cruise, [[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote the actor "is flabbergastingly right for this role. The vampire Lestat, the most commanding and teasingly malicious of Ms. Rice's creations, brings out in Mr. Cruise a fiery, mature sexual magnetism he has not previously displayed on screen. Except for a few angry outbursts here, there are no signs of the actor's usual boyishness. Instead, adopting a worldly manner and an exquisite otherworldly look, he transforms himself into a darkly captivating roue who's seen it all".<ref name=Maslin>{{cite news |last=Maslin |first=Janet |author-link=Janet Maslin |date=November 11, 1994|title=Film Review: ''Interview with the Vampire''; Rapture and Terror, Bound by Blood|work=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1994/11/11/movies/film-review-interview-with-the-vampire-rapture-and-terror-bound-by-blood.html|url-status=live|access-date=April 17, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101114032146/http://www.nytimes.com/1994/11/11/movies/film-review-interview-with-the-vampire-rapture-and-terror-bound-by-blood.html|archive-date=14 November 2010}}</ref> ''[[The Washington Post]]''{{'}}s Rita Kempley said "Cruise brings a wicked wit to the ghoulish role" of Lestat.<ref name="Kempley">{{Cite news |last=Kempley |first=Rita |date=11 November 1994 |title=Interview With the Vampire |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/interviewwiththevampirerkempley_a0a49a.htm |url-status=live |access-date=1 January 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19970505223058/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/interviewwiththevampirerkempley_a0a49a.htm |archive-date=5 May 1997}}</ref>

The ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]''{{'}} [[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three out of four stars, writing "the movie never makes vampirism look like anything but an endless sadness. That is its greatest strength. Vampires throughout movie history have often chortled as if they'd gotten away with something. But the first great vampire movie, ''[[Nosferatu]]'' (1922), knew better, and so does this one."<ref name=Ebert/> He opined production designer Dante Ferretti "combines the elegance of [[The Age of Innocence (1993 film)|''The Age of Innocence'']] and the fantastic images of ''[[The Adventures of Baron Munchausen]]'' into a vampire world of eerie beauty", and called the Paris catacomb sets "one of the great sets of movie history".<ref name=Ebert/> He also praised the casting of Cruise and added, "Dunst, perhaps with the help of Stan Winston's subtle makeup, is somehow able to convey the notion of great age inside apparent youth."<ref name=Ebert>{{Cite web|last=Ebert|first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=11 November 1994|title=Interview with the Vampire (1994)|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/interview-with-the-vampire-1994|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130602222733/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/interview-with-the-vampire-1994|archive-date=2 June 2013|access-date=2021-01-01|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|language=en|via=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]}}</ref>

[[Desson Thomson]], also of ''The Washington Post'', was not as enthusiastic about Cruise's performance.<ref name="Thomson">{{cite news |last=Thomson |first=Desson |author-link=Desson Thomson |date=November 14, 1994 |title=Interview with the Vampire |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/interviewwiththevampirerhowe_a01dbb.htm |url-status=live |access-date=December 22, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121111182418/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/interviewwiththevampirerhowe_a01dbb.htm |archive-date=11 November 2012}}</ref> He added, "The humor, more subtly embedded in the book, has been brought to the surface as if this were a weekly [[sitcom]] called 'Pardon Me but Your Teeth Are in My Neck.'"<ref name="Thomson" /> More critical reviews noted the compressed nature of the film adaptation "left out information crucial to understanding the characters' behavior".<ref name="Kempley" /> In ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', [[Todd McCarthy]] wrote while the film "has its share of riveting moments" and bears a "wonderfully evocative mood", what is "missing is a strong sense of emotional exchange and development among the main characters. The intense bonds of love, resentment and hatred that arc through the centuries among Lestat, Louis, the vampire he creates, and their 'daughter,' Claudia, are only lightly felt".<ref name="McCarthy">{{cite news |last1=McCarthy |first1=Todd |author-link=Todd McCarthy |title=Interview with the Vampire |url=https://variety.com/1994/film/reviews/interview-with-the-vampire-1200439504/ |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=Variety |date=November 6, 1994}}</ref>

Of Pitt's Louis, McCarthy said "there is no depth to his melancholy, no pungency to his sense of loss. He also doesn't seem to connect in a meaningful way with any of the other actors except, perhaps, to Slater's interviewer. This is unfortunate because his profound feelings for Claudia, Lestat and Armand are meant to be among the primary driving forces of the story."<ref name="McCarthy" /> Critics also pointed out the film loses narrative steam in its third act,<ref name="Kempley" /><ref>{{cite news |last=Corliss |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Corliss |date=November 21, 1994 |title=CINEMA: Toothless: Interview with the Vampire falls flat, despite Tom Cruise |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |url=https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,981862,00.html |url-status=live |access-date=December 22, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110122194417/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,981862,00.html |archive-date=January 22, 2011}}</ref><ref name="Thomson" /> though Maslin commented that the film's final scene provides some rejuvenating thrills.<ref name="Maslin" /> Ebert conceded his only criticism of the film was its thin plot, but concluded that the movie is overall "a skillful exercise in macabre imagination".<ref name="Ebert" />

[[Oprah Winfrey]] walked out of an advance screening of the movie only 10 minutes in, because of the gore and dark themes. She considered canceling an interview with Tom Cruise promoting the film, stating, "I believe there are forces of light and darkness in the world, and I don't want to be a contributor to the force of darkness".<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/1994/10/20/cruises-vampire-turns-off-oprah-she-walks-out/ | title=Cruise's Vampire Turns Off Oprah - She Walks Out |newspaper=[[Orlando Sentinel]] | date=1994-10-20 | access-date=2018-06-25 | archive-date=June 25, 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180625103906/http://articles.orlandosentinel.com/1994-10-20/news/9410200300_1_winfrey-oprah-vampire | url-status=live }}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{main|Interview with the Vampire (soundtrack){{!}}''Interview with the Vampire'' (soundtrack)}}
The film's musical score was written by [[Elliot Goldenthal]]. It received an [[67th Academy Awards|Oscar]] nomination for [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]], but lost to ''[[The Lion King]]''.<ref name="BackstageOscars" /> The score opens with the Catholic hymn ''[[Libera Me]]'' slightly rewritten to reflect Louis's character. The opening line "Libera me, Domine, de morte æterna" ("Save me, Lord, from eternal death") was changed to "Libera me, Domine, de vita æterna" ("Save me, Lord, from eternal life").

The end credits cover for "[[Sympathy for the Devil]]" was performed by [[Guns N' Roses]]. This was the band's last major release before the departure of [[Slash (musician)|Slash]] and [[Duff McKagan]].

==Post-film period==
{{main|Queen of the Damned{{!}}''Queen of the Damned''}}
After the commercial and critical success of ''Interview with the Vampire'', Neil Jordan began development on an adaptation of the novel's sequel, ''[[The Vampire Lestat]]'', which ultimately did not materialize.<ref name="Lestat">{{cite news |title=Neil Jordan Bit at Opportunity to Direct 'Vampire' |url=https://www.orlandosentinel.com/1994/11/11/neil-jordan-bit-at-opportunity-to-direct-vampire/ |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=Orlando Sentinel |date=1994-11-11}}</ref> Almost a decade after this film, [[Queen of the Damned|an adaptation]] for the third book in the series, ''[[The Queen of the Damned]]'', was produced and distributed once again by Warner Bros. Cruise and Pitt did not reprise their roles as Lestat and Louis. Many characters and important plotlines were written out of the film, which actually combined elements of ''[[The Vampire Lestat]]'' with ''The Queen of the Damned''. The film was negatively received by critics, and Rice dismissed it completely as she felt the filmmakers had "mutilated" her work. During preproduction, Rice had pleaded with the studio not to produce a film of the book just yet since she believed her readers wanted a film based on ''The Vampire Lestat''.<ref name="Stax">{{cite news |title=The Stax Report: Script Review of Queen of the Damned |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2000/07/17/the-stax-report-script-review-of-queen-of-the-damned |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=IGN |date=July 17, 2000}}</ref> Rice offered to write the screenplay herself but was turned down by the studio.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Haflidason |first1=Almar |title=Film of the Damned - the Tragedy Behind "Queen of the Damned |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2002/04/10/making_queen_of_the_damned_article.shtml |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=[[BBC]] |date=2002-04-10}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Rice |first1=Anne |title=Questions: Movies and Other Ventures |url=https://www.annerice.com/ques_mov.htm |website=annerice.com |access-date=11 November 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19971016124523/http://www.annerice.com/ques_mov.htm#movies |archive-date=1997-10-16 |date=June 22, 1997 |quote="The rights to LESTAT will come back to me in about three years, and it doesn't look like Warner Bros. is going to make the movie... |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="TheAge">{{cite news |title=Death and the Damned |url=https://www.theage.com.au/entertainment/movies/death-and-the-damned-20020329-gdu34f.html |access-date=11 November 2023 |work=[[The Age]] |date=March 29, 2002}}</ref>

In February 2012, a film adaptation of ''[[The Tale of the Body Thief]]'', the fourth book in the series, entered development with [[Brian Grazer]] and [[Ron Howard]]'s film production company, [[Imagine Entertainment]]. It was reported that screenwriter Lee Patterson was going to pen the screenplay. However, Rice's son, [[Christopher Rice|Christopher]], apparently had drafted a screenplay based on the novel that was met with praise from those involved in the developmental stage. Rice later confirmed that creative differences that were beyond those involved resulted in the dismissal of the project in April 2013.<ref>{{cite news| url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/anne-rice-tale-of-body-thief-imagine-kurtzman-orci-287742 | work=The Hollywood Reporter | first=Borys | last=Kit |date=2012-02-07 | title=Anne Rice's 'Tale of the Body Thief' In Development With Imagine, Kurtzman/Orci}}</ref>

In August 2014, [[Universal Pictures]] acquired the rights to the entire ''Vampire Chronicles'' series. Alex Kurtzman and Roberto Orci were named as producers, and the deal included the aforementioned screenplay for ''The Tale of the Body Thief'' written by Christopher Rice.<ref name="Variety 2014-08">{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2014/film/news/anne-rices-vampire-chronicles-takes-flight-at-universal-1201278069/ |title=Anne Rice's ''Vampire Chronicles'' Takes Flight at Universal |first=Dave |last=McNary |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=August 7, 2014 |access-date=August 10, 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/aug/08/universal-anne-rice-vampire-chronicles-book-series|title=Universal buys rights to Anne Rice's Vampire Chronicles book series|first=Iman |last=Amrani |work=The Guardian|date=August 8, 2014}}</ref>

A new film adaptation of the book was written by [[Josh Boone (director)|Josh Boone]] and was announced in May 2016, with Boone suggesting actor [[Jared Leto]] play the role of Lestat.<ref name="newscomau">{{cite news|title=Interview with the Vampire remake: Jared Leto is screenwriters pick to play Lestat|url=https://www.news.com.au/entertainment/movies/upcoming-movies/interview-with-the-vampire-remake--jared-leto-is-screenwriters-pick-to-play-lestat/news-story/06b71210d467f4b4709536a32cf49cfe|access-date=October 10, 2016|work=news.com.au|date=May 7, 2016}}</ref> In November 2016, all plans for a theatrical reboot were scrapped, with Rice announcing she had regained the rights to her novels and intended to create a television series starting with ''The Vampire Lestat''.

==Television series==
{{Main|Interview with the Vampire (TV series){{!}}''Interview with the Vampire'' (TV series)}}
In June 2021, [[AMC (TV channel)|AMC]] announced a television adaptation of ''Interview with the Vampire'', giving a series order consisting of seven episodes. The series was created by [[Rolin Jones]], who executive produced alongside [[Mark Johnson (producer)|Mark Johnson]], [[Alan Taylor (director)|Alan Taylor]], Anne Rice, and Christopher Rice.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2021/06/interview-with-the-vampire-series-greenlit-amc-rolin-jones-mark-johnson-1234780879/|title='Interview With the Vampire' Series Greenlighted At AMC; Rolin Jones Set As Showrunner, Mark Johnson To Oversee Franchise|work=Deadline Hollywood|first=Peter|last=White|date=June 24, 2021|access-date=June 24, 2021}}</ref> The novel is adapted through the first two seasons, with elements from ''The Vampire Lestat'' being used during season two.
