'''''The Untouchables''''' is a 1987 American [[crime film]] directed by [[Brian De Palma]], produced by [[Art Linson]] and written by [[David Mamet]]. It stars [[Kevin Costner]], [[Charles Martin Smith]], [[Andy García]], [[Robert De Niro]] and [[Sean Connery]]. Set in 1930 [[Chicago]], the film follows [[Eliot Ness]] (Costner) as he forms the [[Untouchables (law enforcement)|Untouchables]] team to bring [[Al Capone]] (De Niro) to justice during [[Prohibition in the United States|Prohibition]]. It is the fourth film on which De Niro and De Palma collaborated, after ''[[Greetings (1968 film)|Greetings]]'' (De Niro's first lead in a feature role), ''[[The Wedding Party (1969 film)|The Wedding Party]]'' and ''[[Hi, Mom!]]''.

The screenplay is loosely based on Ness's and [[Oscar Fraley]]'s 1957 book ''[[The Untouchables (book)|The Untouchables]]'' and the real-life events on which it is based, although most of its plot is fictionalized.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=The Untouchables |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/57873 |access-date=2022-07-07 |website=AFI Catalog}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=30 years later, The Untouchables is still, well, untouchable |url=https://www.tampabay.com/30-years-later-the-untouchables-is-still-well-untouchable/2326201/ |access-date=2022-07-07 |website=Tampa Bay Times |language=en}}</ref> The [[Academy Award]]-nominated, [[Grammy Award]]-winning score is composed by [[Ennio Morricone]] and features period music by [[Duke Ellington]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://ricerca.repubblica.it/repubblica/archivio/repubblica/1987/09/06/piace-al-capone-superstar.html|title=Piace Al Capone superstar|author=Tullio Kezich|newspaper=[[la Repubblica]]|date=September 6, 1987|page=23|language=it|access-date=May 28, 2017|archive-date=December 1, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201033849/http://ricerca.repubblica.it/repubblica/archivio/repubblica/1987/09/06/piace-al-capone-superstar.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Untouchables'' premiered on June 2, 1987, in New York City, and went into general release by [[Paramount Pictures]] on June 3, 1987, in the United States. The film grossed $187 million worldwide and received generally positive reviews from critics. It was nominated for four [[Academy Awards]]; Connery won the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor]],<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/legacy/ceremony/60th-winners.html |title=The 60th Academy Awards (1988) Nominees and Winners |access-date=2011-07-31 |publisher=oscars.org |archive-date=2014-10-01 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141001073353/https://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/legacy/ceremony/60th-winners.html |url-status=live }}</ref> as well as the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words.
 -->
In 1930, during [[Prohibition in the United States|Prohibition]], the notorious gangland boss [[Al Capone]] supplies illegal liquor and controls most of [[Chicago]]. [[Bureau of Prohibition]] agent [[Eliot Ness]] has been tasked with halting Capone's activities by hitting a warehouse with crates shipped from [[Canada]], but his first attempt at a liquor raid fails due to corrupt policemen alerting Capone.

He soon encounters veteran [[Irish American|Irish-American]] officer James Malone, who opposes the rampant corruption and offers to help Ness, suggesting that they find a man from the police academy who is not yet under Capone's influence and still believes in the idealistic aspects of law enforcement. They recruit Italian-American trainee George Stone (born Giuseppe Petri) for his superior marksmanship and integrity. Joined by accountant Oscar Wallace, they successfully raid a Capone liquor warehouse and start to gain positive publicity, with the press dubbing them The [[Untouchables (law enforcement)|Untouchables]]. During an organizational dinner team meeting, Capone kills the warehouse manager with a [[baseball bat]] to warn his other subordinates.

Discovering that Capone has not filed an [[income tax]] return for four years, Wallace suggests trying to build a [[tax evasion]] case against him, as Capone's network keeps him well insulated from his other crimes. Crooked [[alderman]] John O'Shea offers Ness a bribe to drop his investigation, but Ness refuses. After Capone's enforcer [[Frank Nitti]] threatens Ness' family, Ness immediately moves his pregnant wife Catherine and their daughter to a safe house. In a subsequent raid on the [[Canada–United States border|Canadian border]] with the assistance of some [[Royal Canadian Mounted Police|Mounties]], Ness and his team intercept an incoming liquor shipment, killing several gangsters and capturing a Capone [[bookkeeper]] named George, who ends up agreeing to help them testify against his employer. Back in Chicago, Nitti, dressed as a policeman, murders Wallace and George in the elevator of the police station and leaves a taunting message for Ness. Thereupon Ness confronts Capone at the [[Lexington Hotel (Chicago)|Lexington Hotel]] after the murders, but Malone intervenes, urging Ness to focus on persuading the [[district attorney]] not to dismiss the charges against Capone.

Realizing that police chief Mike Dorsett betrayed Wallace and George earlier on, Malone forces Dorsett to reveal where Capone's accountant Walter Payne is hiding, then tells Ness to meet him to plan their next move. After Malone returns home, Capone's henchmen attack him. He chases one away with a shotgun, but Nitti ambushes him with a [[Thompson submachine gun]] before retreating. Ness and Stone arrive to find Malone mortally wounded, but before dying he manages to tell them which train Payne will take to leave town. 

As Ness and Stone await Payne's arrival at [[Chicago Union Station]], Ness notices a young mother with two suitcases and her baby in a carriage laboriously climbing the lobby steps. Ness ultimately decides to assist her, but the gangsters guarding Payne appear just as Ness and the woman reach the top of the stairs, and a bloody shootout occurs. Although outnumbered, Ness and Stone manage to capture Payne alive and kill all the gangsters, keeping both the mother and child unharmed.

When Payne testifies at Capone's trial, Ness observes that Capone appears strangely calm. He then sees Nitti is wearing a gun in the courtroom and has a [[bailiff]] remove him. Outside the courtroom, Ness searches Nitti and finds a note from Chicago Mayor [[William Hale Thompson]] that effectively permits him to carry the weapon. Ness is frustrated until he discovers that Nitti also has a [[matchbook]] with Malone's address written inside his coat, which causes Ness to realize that Nitti is Malone's killer. In a panic, Nitti brandishes his gun and shoots the bailiff before fleeing to the courthouse roof. Ness eventually apprehends him and prepares to take him in custody, but then Nitti insults the memory of Malone and gloats that he will escape conviction for the murder; this enrages Ness and instead he tosses Nitti off the roof to his death, avenging both Wallace and Malone.

Back in the courtroom, Stone gives Ness a list – taken from Nitti's coat – which shows that the jurors in the trial are all on Capone's payroll, thereby explaining Capone's calm and overconfident demeanor earlier on. Ness secretly persuades the judge to switch Capone's jury with one hearing an unrelated divorce case, and the judge relents after Ness tricks him into believing that his name is on Capone's payroll as well. This prompts Capone's lawyer to enter a guilty plea against Capone's wishes; as Capone furiously objects in a violent outburst, Ness victoriously taunts him by repeating Malone's motto: "Never stop fighting until the fight is done. Here endeth the lesson". Capone is ultimately convicted of tax evasion and sentenced to 11 years in prison. 

On the day of Capone's sentencing, Ness closes up his office and meets up with Stone to part ways with him; he gives Malone's [[Jude Thaddeus the Apostle|St. Jude]] medallion and callbox key to Stone as a farewell present. As Ness leaves the police station, a reporter asks him what he will do after the probable repeal of Prohibition; Ness replies "I think I'll have a drink" before walking away.

== Background ==

===Development===
[[Ned Tanen]] spent years trying to obtain the rights to [[Eliot Ness]]' life story while working as an executive at [[Universal Pictures]] in the 1970s and the 1980s. After becoming head of motion picture productions at [[Paramount Pictures]], which owned the film and television rights to Ness' memoir ''[[The Untouchables (book)|The Untouchables]]'', Tanen immediately hired [[Art Linson]] to begin producing a film adaptation. Linson was not interested in adapting the [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] [[The Untouchables (1959 TV series)|television series based on Ness's book]], and sought to create a more "serious, authentic" depiction of Ness' career in [[Chicago]]. Linson hired playwright [[David Mamet]] to compose an original script for the film. Most of Mamet's screenplay was used, but director [[Brian De Palma]] slightly rewrote some scenes during production in order to incorporate new locations. For instance, the scene paying homage to the [[Potemkin Stairs]] from ''[[Battleship Potemkin]]'' (1925) was moved from a hospital to [[Chicago Union Station]].<ref name=":0" /> A month after the film was released, De Palma downplayed his own role on the script:

<blockquote>Being a writer myself, I don't like to take credit for things I didn't do. I didn't develop this script. David [Mamet] used some of my ideas and he didn't use some of them. I looked upon it more clinically, as a piece of material that has to be shaped, with certain scenes here or there. But as for the moral dimension, that's more or less the conception of the script, and I just implemented it with my skills – which are well developed. It's good to walk in somebody else's shoes for a while. You get out of your own obsessions; you are in the service of somebody else's vision, and that's a great discipline for a director.<ref>{{cite news |last=Bennetts |first=Leslie |date=July 6, 1987 |title=''The Untouchables'': De Palma's Departure |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1987/07/06/movies/the-untouchables-de-palma-s-departure.html?pagewanted=all |url-status=live |access-date=2010-06-04 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130512093940/http://www.nytimes.com/1987/07/06/movies/the-untouchables-de-palma-s-departure.html?pagewanted=all |archive-date=2013-05-12}}</ref></blockquote>The character of the [[Internal Revenue Service|IRS]] agent Oscar Wallace was partially based on [[Frank J. Wilson]], the IRS criminal investigator who spent years keeping tabs on Capone's financial dealings before laying charges.<ref name="richcrouseblog">{{cite news |last=Crouse |first=Richard |author-link=Richard Crouse |title=Metro in Focus: The Accountant & Crooks with Pocket Protectors! |url=http://www.richardcrouse.ca/metro-in-focus-the-accountant-crooks-with-pocket-protectors/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170907033655/http://www.richardcrouse.ca/metro-in-focus-the-accountant-crooks-with-pocket-protectors/ |archive-date=2017-09-07 |access-date=2017-09-06 |work=[[I Watch Bad Movies So You Don't Have To]]}}</ref> Unlike Wallace, Wilson was not killed during the investigation, and was later involved in the [[Lindbergh kidnapping]] case.

=== Casting ===
Linson and De Palma wanted to have a more tender portrayal of Ness than [[Robert Stack]]'s "tough" portrayal from the 1950s television series, seeking to portray him as a "vulnerable family man". De Palma initially wanted [[Don Johnson]] to portray Eliot Ness. [[Mickey Rourke]], [[Jeff Bridges]], [[William Hurt]], [[Harrison Ford]] and [[Michael Douglas]] also turned down the role.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Brian De Palma originally wanted Don Johnson to star in 'The Untouchables' |url=https://ew.com/article/2016/06/14/brian-de-palma-untouchables-don-johnson-exclusive/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180826183050/https://ew.com/article/2016/06/14/brian-de-palma-untouchables-don-johnson-exclusive/ |archive-date=2018-08-26 |access-date=2018-08-26 |website=EW.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |title=Mickey Rourke: a life in film |url=http://www.timeout.com/london/film/mickey-rourke-a-life-in-film-1 |url-status=live |magazine=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151022162508/http://www.timeout.com/london/film/mickey-rourke-a-life-in-film-1 |archive-date=22 October 2015 |access-date=10 September 2015}}</ref><ref name=":1" /> A 1985 issue of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' announced the casting of [[Jack Nicholson]] as Ness, but he was ultimately replaced by [[Kevin Costner]].<ref name=":0" /> In preparing for his role as Ness, Kevin Costner met with former [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] agent and [[Untouchables (law enforcement)|Untouchable]] Al "Wallpaper" Wolff at his home in [[Lincolnwood, Illinois|Lincolnwood]] for historical context and to learn about Ness' mannerisms.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Last Untouchable |url=https://people.com/archive/the-last-untouchable-vol-28-no-2/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190604195214/https://people.com/archive/the-last-untouchable-vol-28-no-2/ |archive-date=2019-06-04 |access-date=2019-06-04 |website=PEOPLE.com}}</ref>

[[Robert De Niro]] was De Palma's first choice to play [[Al Capone]], but it was uncertain if he could appear in the film because of his appearance in the [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]] play ''Cuba and his Teddy Bear''. He also wanted to gain about {{convert|30|lb}} to play Capone; according to De Palma, De Niro was "very concerned about the shape of his face for the part."<ref name="chitrib86">{{cite news |last=Siskel |first=Gene |author-link=Gene Siskel |date=September 21, 1986 |title=De Niro, De Palma, Mamet Organize Crime with a Difference |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://pqasb.pqarchiver.com/chicagotribune/access/24941825.html?dids=24941825:24941825&FMT=ABS&FMTS=ABS:FT |url-status=dead |access-date=2010-06-04 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110820165638/http://pqasb.pqarchiver.com/chicagotribune/access/24941825.html?dids=24941825:24941825&FMT=ABS&FMTS=ABS:FT |archive-date=2011-08-20}}</ref> De Palma met with [[Bob Hoskins]] to discuss the role in case De Niro could not appear. When De Niro took the part, De Palma mailed Hoskins a check for £20,000 with a "Thank You" note, which prompted Hoskins to call up De Palma and ask him if there were any more films he didn't want him to be in.<ref>{{cite news |date=March 19, 2009 |title=Bob Hoskins paid not to play Capone |publisher=Metro Newspapers |url=https://metro.co.uk/2009/03/19/bob-hoskins-paid-not-to-play-capone-553319/ |url-status=live |access-date=2019-10-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191024162327/https://metro.co.uk/2009/03/19/bob-hoskins-paid-not-to-play-capone-553319/ |archive-date=2019-10-24}}</ref><ref>{{Cite tweet|number=971841161827667969|user=MrKenShabby|title=LOVE this Bob Hoskins story about how he didn't get the part of Al Capone in The Untouchables}}</ref> [[Gene Hackman]] and [[Marlon Brando]] were also considered as options in case both De Niro and Hoskins proved unable to perform the role.<ref name=":1" /> De Niro's research for the role of Capone included reading about him and watching historical footage.<ref name="entweekly">{{cite magazine| title= The stars of The Untouchables look back, 30 years later| date= June 5, 2017| url= http://ew.com/movies/2017/06/05/the-untouchables-kevin-costner-robert-deniro-sean-connery-look-back/| last= Sollosi| first= Mary| author-link= Mary Sollosi| magazine= [[Entertainment Weekly]]| access-date= 2017-09-06| archive-date= 2017-09-07| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20170907075319/http://ew.com/movies/2017/06/05/the-untouchables-kevin-costner-robert-deniro-sean-connery-look-back/| url-status= live}}</ref> He had one extra scene written for his character, and contacted Capone's original tailors to have identical suits and silk underwear made for him. He was paid $2 million for the role.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" />

[[Patricia Clarkson]] was cast to play Ness' wife, Catherine, in her debut film role.<ref name=":0" /> A young [[John Barrowman]] appears uncredited as a street person.

===Filming===
Principal photography began on August 18, 1986, in Chicago, Illinois, where Ness' story begins with him recruiting his Untouchables team with the intent of taking down Capone.<ref name=":0" />

Filming locations included the [[Rookery Building]] (Ness' police headquarters),<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |title=Filming Locations for The Untouchables (1987), in Chicago and Montana. |url=http://www.movie-locations.com/movies/u/Untouchables.php |access-date=2024-03-18 |website=The Worldwide Guide to Movie Locations}}</ref> [[LaSalle Street]],<ref name=":2" /> the [[DuSable Bridge]], [[Chicago Cultural Center]] (the opera house, the courthouse lobby, and the rooftop chase),<ref name=":2" /> the [[The Blackstone Hotel|Blackstone Hotel]] (the mob banquet),<ref name=":2" /> [[Our Lady of Sorrows Basilica]] (the church),<ref name=":2" /> and [[Chicago Union Station]] (the railway shootout). The [[Lexington Hotel (Chicago)|Lexington Hotel]], Capone's residence, had been closed since 1980, so the location was portrayed through three locations in the film: the exterior and lower lobby was filmed at [[Roosevelt University]], while Capone's suite was the upper foyer of the [[Chicago Theatre]].<ref name=":2" /> A [[West Side, Chicago|West Side]] warehouse served as a [[Sound stage|soundstage]].<ref name=":2" />

In August 1986, Paramount Pictures contacted Garry Wunderwald of the Montana Film Commissioner's Office to find a 1930s-period bridge to portray a [[Canada–United States border|border crossing between the United States and Canada]]. Wunderwald suggested the [[Hardy Bridge]], which crosses the [[Missouri River]] near the small town of [[Cascade, Montana|Cascade]], southwest of [[Great Falls, Montana|Great Falls]].

From {{nowrap|October 6–20,}} the bridge was closed to traffic to film the shootout sequence. 25 local residents were cast to ride horseback as [[Royal Canadian Mounted Police]] during the scene. The crew then built cabins and summer homes along the river, and 600 trees were brought in from [[Lincoln, Montana|Lincoln]] and [[Kalispell, Montana|Kalispell]] areas, and planted in a day and a half. Several 1920s and 1930s-era vehicles were rented from ranchers from [[Conrad, Montana|Conrad]] and Great Falls. Actual filming took approximately 10 days, but the production staff reserved the bridge for enough time to allow for production delays. Hundreds were allowed to watch filming from a nearby field.<ref>{{Cite news |last=D'Ambrosio |first=Brian |title=The Untouchables' Montana touch: Hollywood shoot-out scene comes to Montana |language=en |work=Montana Magazines |url=http://montanamagazines.com/montana-magazine/the-untouchables-montana-touch-hollywood-shoot-out-scene-comes-to/article_8aa88877-876b-5a96-8231-4b9b758347fc.html |url-status=live |access-date=2017-03-09 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201102183048/http://montanamagazines.com/montana-magazine/the-untouchables-montana-touch-hollywood-shoot-out-scene-comes-to/article_8aa88877-876b-5a96-8231-4b9b758347fc.html |archive-date=2020-11-02}}</ref>

The railway station shoot-out is a homage to the Odessa Steps montage in [[Sergei Eisenstein]]'s famous 1925 silent movie ''Battleship Potemkin'', and it was parodied in the 1994 movie ''[[Naked Gun 33 1/3: The Final Insult|Naked Gun {{frac|33|1|3}}: The Final Insult]]'' as a [[dream sequence]].<ref>{{cite web |date=16 November 2011 |title=Iconic movie scene: The Untouchables' Union Station shoot-out |url=http://www.denofgeek.us/movies/18343/iconic-movie-scene-the-untouchables%E2%80%99-union-station-shoot-out |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190419031700/https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/18343/iconic-movie-scene-the-untouchables%E2%80%99-union-station-shoot-out |archive-date=2019-04-19 |access-date=2020-10-15 |publisher=Den of Geek}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Xan Brooks |date=1 February 2008 |title=Films influenced by Battleship Potemkin |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2008/feb/01/features.features |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160420055015/http://www.theguardian.com/film/2008/feb/01/features.features |archive-date=20 April 2016 |access-date=10 October 2016 |work=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref>

==Historical accuracy==
While the film is based on historic events, most of the film is fictionalized or inaccurate.<ref name="guardianaccuracy">{{Cite web|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/mar/18/reel-history-the-untouchables|title=Historian Alex von Tunzelmann: The Untouchables is punch-drunk with inaccuracies|date=March 19, 2009|website=the Guardian|access-date=March 3, 2021|archive-date=January 11, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210111235021/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/mar/18/reel-history-the-untouchables|url-status=live}}</ref> The raid at the Canada–United States border never happened,<ref name="guardianaccuracy"/> and neither did the courthouse or railway station shootouts.<ref name="fsraccuracy"/> Ness did not kill [[Frank Nitti]], who died in 1943, 12 years after the Capone trial, by suicide the day before Nitti himself was scheduled to be in court.<ref name="fsraccuracy">{{Cite web|url=https://filmschoolrejects.com/retouched-inaccuracy-improves-de-palmas-untouchables/|title=Retouched: How Inaccuracy Improves De Palma's 'Untouchables'|date=May 27, 2017|website=Film School Rejects|access-date=March 3, 2021|archive-date=September 8, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180908033348/https://filmschoolrejects.com/retouched-inaccuracy-improves-de-palmas-untouchables/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Koziol |first1=Ronald |last2=Baumann |first2=Edward |date=June 29, 1987 |title=How Frank Nitti Met His Fate |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/1987/06/29/how-frank-nitti-met-his-fate/ |access-date=March 19, 2020 |archive-date=October 16, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121016133456/http://articles.chicagotribune.com/1987-06-29/features/8702170754_1_frank-nitti-crime-syndicate-pulled/2 |url-status=live }}</ref> In reality, Ness' unit had very little to do with Capone's final tax evasion conviction, which was orchestrated by U.S. Attorney [[George E. Q. Johnson]] and IRS Agent Frank J. Wilson, who inspired the character of Oscar Wallace ([[Charles Martin Smith]]) but was never part of the Untouchables.<ref name="fsraccuracy" /><ref name="guardianaccuracy"/><ref name="independentaccuracy">{{Cite web|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/books/features/unbelievables-truth-lies-and-myth-eliot-ness-legendary-battles-al-capone-9142496.html|title=The Unbelievables: truth, lies and the myth of Eliot Ness|date=February 21, 2014|website=The Independent|access-date=March 3, 2021|archive-date=November 11, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201111204338/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/books/features/unbelievables-truth-lies-and-myth-eliot-ness-legendary-battles-al-capone-9142496.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=How Al Capone was caught out by a forensic accountant |url=https://intheblack.cpaaustralia.com.au/accounting/how-al-capone-caught-out-forensic-accountant |website=In the Black |access-date=15 March 2024}}</ref>

The scene where Capone beats a lieutenant (John Bracci) to death with a baseball bat is based on an urban legend, albeit one that historians doubt actually happened: after discovering that [[John Scalise]], Albert Anselmi and [[Joseph Giunta (mobster)|Joseph Guinta]] were planning to betray him, Capone reportedly invited the trio to a dinner party and beat them to death.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Monroe |first1=James |title=Why the Legend of Al Capone Still Fascinates |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2016/12/02/books/review/al-capone-life-legacy-legend-deirdre-bair.html |agency=The New York Times |date=December 2, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite book| title=Some Historical Stories of Chicago| publisher=Southern Illinois University Press| author=Albert A. Hoffman Jr. | date=29 Oct 2010| pages=191| isbn=9781453539705| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=awdx10jLhAcC&pg=PA191}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Pasley |first1=Fred |title=Al Capone: The Biography of a Self-Made Man |date=1930 |page=331}}</ref>

In the film, Ness' wife is called Catherine ([[Patricia Clarkson]]) and they have a young daughter (Kaitlin Montgomery).<ref name=":0" /> In real life, Ness's wife at the time was named Edna and the couple had no children, though Ness would later adopt a son, Robert.<ref name=":0" /><ref name="Vigil, Vicki Blum 2007">{{cite book |author=Vigil, Vicki Blum |title=Cemeteries of Northeast Ohio: Stones, Symbols & Stories |date=2007 |publisher=Gray & Company, Publishers |isbn=978-1-59851-025-6 |location=Cleveland, OH}}</ref>

==Reception==
''The Untouchables'' opened on June 3, 1987, in 1,012 theatres in the United States and Canada where it grossed $10,023,094 on its opening weekend and ranked the sixth-highest opening weekend of 1987. It went on to gross $76.2&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada.<ref name="boxoffice">{{cite news | title = ''The Untouchables'' | work = [[Box Office Mojo]] | url = http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=untouchables.htm | access-date = 2008-07-17 | archive-date = 2007-10-01 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20071001030405/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=untouchables.htm | url-status = live }}</ref> According to producer Art Linson, the polls conducted for the film showed that approximately 50% of the audience were women. "Ordinarily, a violent film attracts predominantly men, but this is also touching, about redemption and relationships and because of that the audience tends to forgive the excesses when it comes to violence".<ref name="darnton">{{cite news | last = Darnton | first = Nina | title = At the Movies | work = [[The New York Times]] | date = June 12, 1987 | url = https://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/12/movies/at-the-movies.html?scp=27&sq= | access-date = 2010-03-31 | archive-date = 2013-05-12 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20130512113719/http://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/12/movies/at-the-movies.html?scp=27&sq= | url-status = live }}</ref> Internationally, the film grossed $110.5 million,<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Par Posts Record Foreign Biz For Year; Four Big Hits The Key|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=November 2, 1988|page=3}}</ref> for a worldwide total of $186.7 million.

===Critical response===
''The Untouchables'' received positive reviews from film critics. On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 83% based on reviews from 75 critics, with an average rating of 7.60/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "Slick on the surface but loaded with artful touches, Brian DePalma's classical gangster thriller is a sharp look at period Chicago crime, featuring excellent performances from a top-notch cast."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/untouchables/ |title=The Untouchables (1987) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=October 17, 2023 |archive-date=February 24, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080224073010/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/untouchables/ |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 79 out of 100, based on 16 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-untouchables |title=The Untouchables Reviews |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=March 20, 2020 |archive-date=July 21, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200721150720/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-untouchables |url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences polled by CinemaScore gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-12-20 |title=Cinemascore :: Movie Title Search|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|access-date=2020-07-27 |website=[[Cinemascore]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|archive-date=2018-12-20}}</ref>

[[Vincent Canby]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' gave the film a positive review, calling it "a smashing work" and saying it was "vulgar, violent, funny and sometimes breathtakingly beautiful".<ref name="Canby">{{cite web | title = De Niro in ''The Untouchables'' | work = [[The New York Times]] | date = June 3, 1987 | url = https://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/03/movies/film-deniro-in-the-untouchables.html | access-date = 2008-07-17 | archive-date = 2015-05-24 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20150524203549/http://www.nytimes.com/1987/06/03/movies/film-deniro-in-the-untouchables.html | url-status = live }}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' praised the film for its action sequences and locations, but disapproved of David Mamet's script and Brian De Palma's direction. Ebert singled out the film's depiction of Al Capone as arrogant and childish, to the point of misbehaving in public and in court, as the biggest disappointment of the film, while giving praise to Sean Connery's work.<ref name="Ebert">{{cite web | last = Ebert | first = Roger | author-link = Roger Ebert | title = The Untouchables | work = RogerEbert.com | publisher = Ebert Digital LLC | date = June 3, 1987 | url = https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-untouchables-1987 | access-date = March 20, 2018 | archive-date = February 4, 2017 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170204034448/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-untouchables-1987 | url-status = live }}</ref> [[Hal Hinson]], in his review for ''[[The Washington Post]]'', also criticized De Palma's direction, saying "somehow we're put off here by the spectacular stuff he throws up onto the screen. De Palma's storytelling instincts have given way completely to his interest in film as a visual medium. His only real concern is his own style."<ref name="Hinson">{{cite news | last = Hinson | first = Hal | title = ''The Untouchables'' | newspaper = [[Washington Post]] | date = June 3, 1987 | url = https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/theuntouchablesrhinson_a0c952.htm | access-date = 2008-07-17 | archive-date = 2012-11-05 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121105140137/http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/theuntouchablesrhinson_a0c952.htm | url-status = live }}</ref>

''[[The New Yorker]]''{{'}}s [[Pauline Kael]] wrote that it was "not a great movie; it's too banal, too morally comfortable. The great gangster pictures don't make good and evil mutually exclusive, the way they are here [...] But it's a great audience movie—a wonderful potboiler."<ref>{{cite magazine | url= http://www.thestacksreader.com/the-untouchables/ | title= The Untouchables| magazine=The New Yorker | first=Pauline | last=Kael | date=June 29, 1987 | access-date=5 December 2021}}</ref> [[Richard Schickel]] of ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' wrote, "Mamet's elegantly efficient script does not waste a word, and De Palma does not waste a shot. The result is a densely layered work moving with confident, compulsive energy".<ref name="Schickel">{{cite magazine | last = Schickel | first = Richard | author-link=Richard Schickel | title = In The American Grain | magazine = [[Time (magazine)|Time]] | date = June 8, 1987 | url = http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,964593,00.html | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20080307074723/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,964593,00.html | url-status = dead | archive-date = March 7, 2008 | access-date = 2010-03-31 }}</ref> ''Time'' ranked it as one of the best films of 1987.<ref name="TimeBest">{{cite magazine | title = Best of '87: Cinema | magazine = [[Time (magazine)|Time]] | date = January 4, 1988 | url = http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,966369,00.html | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20071012153630/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,966369,00.html | url-status = dead | archive-date = October 12, 2007 | access-date = 2010-03-31 }}</ref> Adrian Turner of ''[[Radio Times]]'' awarded it a full five stars, writing that "David Mamet's dialogue crackles, [[Ennio Morricone]]'s music soars and the production design sparkles. Yet for many the main attraction of this modern classic is [[Sean Connery]]'s [[Academy Awards|Oscar]]-winning turn as the veteran Irish cop who shows Ness the ropes."<ref>{{cite web | url= https://www.radiotimes.com/movie-guide/b-c47mmr/the-untouchables/ | title= The Untouchables| work=[[Radio Times]] | first=Adrian | last=Turner| access-date=5 December 2021}}</ref>

Despite receiving the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor]] for his performance, Connery was voted first place in a 2003 ''[[Empire (film magazine)|Empire]]'' poll for worst film accent because his Scottish accent was still very noticeable.<ref name="BBC">{{cite news | title = Connery 'has worst film accent' | work = [[BBC]] | date = June 30, 2003 | url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/entertainment/3032052.stm | access-date = 2008-07-17 | archive-date = 2007-08-24 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070824041828/http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/entertainment/film/3032052.stm | url-status = live }}</ref>

==Video game==
A [[side-scrolling video game]], ''[[The Untouchables (video game)|The Untouchables]]'', was released by [[Ocean Software]] in 1989 on multiple platforms. The game plays out some of the more significant parts of the film. Set in Chicago, the primary goal of the game is to take down Al Capone's henchmen and eventually detain Capone.

==Cancelled prequel==
It was reported on July 2, 2004, that [[Antoine Fuqua]] would direct an ''Untouchables'' spinoff titled ''The Untouchables: Mother's Day'',<ref>{{cite news|last=B.|first=Brian|url=https://movieweb.com/antoine-fuqua-seeking-the-untouchables-mothers-day/|title=Antoine Fuqua seeking The Untouchables: Mother's Day|work=MovieWeb|date=July 2, 2004|access-date=April 1, 2023}}</ref> which was later changed to ''Capone Rising''.<ref>{{cite news|author1=Fleming, Michael|author2=McNary, Dave|url=https://variety.com/2004/film/markets-festivals/par-guns-it-on-capone-1117909534/|title=Par guns it on 'Capone'|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=August 24, 2004|access-date=April 1, 2023}}</ref> The film's script, which was written by [[Brian Koppelman]] and [[David Levien]], focused on the rise of Chicago mob kingpin Capone in the years before the encounter with Ness and his lawmen. In June 2005, original director Brian De Palma replaced Fuqua on the film.<ref>{{cite news|last=Fleming|first=Michael|url=https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/capone-reloads-1117925206/|title=Capone reloads|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=June 27, 2005|access-date=March 28, 2023}}</ref> According to him, the project went into development several times with [[Nicolas Cage]], [[Gerard Butler]] and [[Benicio del Toro]] all attached to play Capone at different stages.<ref>{{cite news|last=Brown|first=Phil|url=https://collider.com/brian-de-palma-passion-untouchables-prequel-capone-rising/|title=Brian De Palma Talks PASSION, THE UNTOUCHABLES Prequel CAPONE RISING, and His Upcoming Jason Statham Movie at TIFF 2012|work=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|date=September 13, 2012|access-date=March 28, 2023}}</ref>

In June 2006, original ''Untouchables'' composer [[Ennio Morricone]] told ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' that he had already turned down the offer to return to score the prequel because he didn't want to lose his inspiration by spending too much time in Hollywood.<ref>{{Cite web|author=Vivarelli, Nick|date=June 4, 2006|url=http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117944539.html?categoryid=16&cs=1|title=Morricone kicks off tour with La Scala orchestra|publisher=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070622110313/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117944539.html?categoryid=16&cs=1|archive-date=June 22, 2007|url-status=dead}}</ref> While promoting ''[[The Black Dahlia (film)|The Black Dahlia]]'', on September 8, De Palma revealed in an interview for [[CHUD.com]] that his longtime writer-collaborator [[David Rabe]] was brought aboard the project to make revisions to the Koppelman/Levien draft. He added,
{{Blockquote|"There's an assassination in ''[[Tosca]]'' that I've always wanted to do that I wrote for another script, and I was able to get it into this script. It's an incredible set piece where they kill [James] Colosimo while he's watching ''Tosca'', which is something I've wanted to do for years. So I've got a whole bunch of good ideas. And then we have this whole relationship between the young Sean Connery character and Al Capone with a girl in between them; I came up with this idea of a saloon singer. It's good."<ref>{{Cite web |author=Smith, Jeremy |date=September 8, 2006 |url=http://www.chud.com/index.php?type=interviews&id=7568 |title=EXCLUSIVE INTERVIEW: BRIAN DE PALMA (THE BLACK DAHLIA) |website=[[CHUD.com]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080113182101/http://www.chud.com/index.php?type=interviews&id=7568 |archive-date=January 13, 2008 |url-status=dead}}</ref>}} On September 26, he confirmed during a separate interview for the fansite ''De Palma a la Mod'' that they were planning to shoot the prequel "late next summer" and that another project of his, ''Toyer'', was postponed to accommodate that schedule.<ref>{{Cite web |author=Beran, Geoff |date=September 26, 2006 |url=https://www.angelfire.com/de/palma/interviewdahlia.html |title=''Interview with Brian De Palma'' |website=De Palma a la Mod |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240801214815/https://www.angelfire.com/de/palma/interviewdahlia.html |archive-date=August 1, 2024 |url-status=dead}}</ref>
