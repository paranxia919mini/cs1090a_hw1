'''''Rabid''''' is a 1977 [[body horror]] film written and directed by [[David Cronenberg]], and starring [[Marilyn Chambers]], [[Frank Moore (Canadian actor)|Frank Moore]], [[Joe Silver]], and [[Howard Ryshpan]]. The film follows Rose Miller, a woman who, after being injured in a motorcycle accident and undergoing a surgical operation, develops an orifice under one of her arms that hides a phallic/clitoral stinger she uses to feed on human blood, spreading a disease from the [[Quebec]] countryside into urban [[Montreal]].

A co-production between Canada and the United States,<ref name=afi/> ''Rabid'' was filmed on location in Montreal in the late fall of 1976. Released in Canada in the spring of 1977, the film was a major box-office success, particularly in the United States; the film grossed over $7 million internationally.<ref name=Adilman/>

''Rabid'' has developed a [[cult film|cult following]] in the years since its release,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://player.bfi.org.uk/subscription/film/watch-rabid-1976-online|work=[[British Film Institute|BFI Player]]|title=Rabid|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220090230/https://player.bfi.org.uk/subscription/film/watch-rabid-1976-online|archive-date=December 20, 2025}}</ref> and been noted by film critics and scholars for its political themes of societal dissolution in the wake of [[martial law]], echoing the [[October Crisis]] of 1970 in Montreal.{{sfn|Leeder|Loiselle|2024|p=817}} A [[Rabid (2019 film)|remake of the same name]], directed by [[Jen and Sylvia Soska]], was released in 2019.

==Plot==
Rose Miller and her boyfriend, Hart Reed, are involved in a motorcycle accident in the [[Quebec]] countryside after encountering a van in the middle of the road. Hart suffers a broken hand, a separated shoulder and a concussion, while Rose is severely injured and burned. They are taken to the Keloid Clinic for Plastic Surgery, where Dr. Dan Keloid performs an experimental procedure on Rose, using [[morphogenetic]]ally neutral grafts to replace her damaged skin and organs. Hart is released after a month, but Rose remains in a coma.

Rose suddenly awakens one night screaming and pierces fellow patient Lloyd Walsh's skin. The doctors are puzzled as Lloyd has no memory of the incident and his blood fails to clot. Unknown to them, Rose's procedure has caused her to mutate, allowing her to survive only on human blood. A red stinger-like organ emerges under her armpit, which she uses to feed on her victims. One night, Rose leaves the clinic and attempts to feed on a cow but becomes sick. A farmer attacks her, but she retaliates by feeding on him and then calls Hart to pick her up. The next day, the farmer transforms into a zombie-like creature and attacks a waitress at a roadside diner. Meanwhile, Lloyd leaves the clinic but, during a taxi ride, begins foaming at the mouth and attacks the driver, resulting in a fatal crash.

At the clinic, Dr. Keloid becomes infected by Rose's stinger, causing chaos as he attacks a nurse during an operation. Rose escapes and hitchhikes to Montreal, where she infects a truck driver. Hart, along with Dr. Keloid's business partner Murray Cypher, teams up with police chief Claude LePointe and public health officials to discuss the escalating epidemic. Hart witnesses an infected police officer before the officer is shot. He calls Rose's friend, Mindy, and asks her to keep Rose at her apartment if she shows up. Rose arrives in Montreal and stays with Mindy. While Mindy watches a news broadcast about a rabies-like epidemic spreading in the city, Rose goes to an adult movie theater and infects a patron. Later, Mindy encounters an infected woman attacking passengers on the subway. Meanwhile, LePointe and his team are attacked by infected workmen. With the outbreak worsening and rabies treatments failing, Dr. Royce Gentry advises a shoot-to-kill policy to prevent further infections. Martial law is declared and the Canadian Army sets up roadblocks, while [[NBC suit|NBC-suited]] soldiers help dispose of bodies. Rose attempts to seduce a male victim among a crowd of [[Christmas shopping season|Christmas shopper]]s in a mall, but is interrupted when an infected man attacks, spurring a violent shootout by armed forces.

Murray and Hart arrive at Murray's home, where Murray finds his baby dead and is attacked by his infected wife. Hart drives into the abandoned city to search for Rose. An infected person jumps onto his car but is shot by soldiers, who disinfect Hart's vehicle before allowing him to continue. Mindy watches a news report indicating the infection may have originated at the Keloid Clinic. Rose returns to Mindy's apartment and feeds on her. Hart finds Rose in the act and pleads with her to seek treatment, but she denies responsibility for the epidemic. Rose infects a man in the apartment lobby and locks herself in a room with him, planning to test whether he becomes infected. As Hart desperately tries to convince her to leave, the man turns rabid and attacks Rose. The next morning, Rose's body is found in an alley by NBC-suited soldiers, who dispose of her corpse in a garbage truck.

==Themes==
''Rabid'' has been noted by several journalists and critics for its political themes, particularly the breakdown of societal institutions, paralleling the [[October Crisis]] of 1970 in which Prime Minister [[Pierre Trudeau]] controversially activated the ''[[War Measures Act]]'' in several Canadian cities, including Montreal.{{sfn|Leeder|Loiselle|2024|p=817}}<ref>{{cite web|url=https://thecinematheque.ca/films/2022/rabid?srsltid=AfmBOormHuedO3es6fFGuh4RklPhpTDoY-N27HN2lifXkDbTHpeT9D_q|work=[[The Cinematheque]]|title=Rabid|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220084640/https://thecinematheque.ca/films/2022/rabid|archive-date=December 20, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|work=[[Mubi (streaming service)|Mubi]]|title=“Don't let anyone bite you”: Close-Up on David Cronenberg's "Rabid"|last=Cole|first=Meredyth|date=October 6, 2017|url-status=live|url=https://mubi.com/en/notebook/posts/don-t-let-anyone-bite-you-close-up-on-david-cronenberg-s-rabid|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220084940/https://mubi.com/en/notebook/posts/don-t-let-anyone-bite-you-close-up-on-david-cronenberg-s-rabid|archive-date=December 20, 2025}}</ref> Journalist Linda Gross of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' noted in her 1977 review of the film that ''Rabid'' contains "ominous political overtones, effectively showing how [[martial law]] and police tyranny can become another kind of [[epidemic]]."<ref name=Gross/> ''The Canadian Film Encyclopedia'' echoes this sentiment, describing the film as a "subversive commentary on the October Crisis of 1970; the film was shot in Montreal and features a world so crippled by fear that martial law has been imposed."<ref>{{cite web|work=The Canadian Film Encyclopedia|publisher=[[Toronto International Film Festival]]|title=Rabid|url=https://cfe.tiff.net/canadianfilmencyclopedia/content/films/rabid#:~:text=Rabid%20also%20serves%20as%20Cronenberg's,martial%20law%20has%20been%20imposed.|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220083747/https://cfe.tiff.net/canadianfilmencyclopedia/content/films/rabid|archive-date=December 20, 2025}}</ref>

==Production==
===Development===
[[Lionsgate Films|Cinépix]] requested another film from [[David Cronenberg]] following the success of ''[[Shivers (1975 film)|Shivers]]''.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=53}} Criticism from [[Robert Fulford (journalist)|Robert Fulford]] about the [[Telefilm Canada|Canadian Film Development Corporation]]'s funding of ''Shivers'' made it more difficult for Cronenberg to gain funding for his films.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=34-35}} The CFDC funded the film through [[cross-collateralization]] with ''Convoy'', which was never completed, to avoid a direct connection.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=40}}{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=54}}

Initially titled ''Mosquitoes'' Cronenberg had difficulty writing the script due to the broader nature of the film's city setting compared to ''Shivers'' which took place in one apartment complex. At one point he told [[John Dunning (film producer)|John Dunning]] that "John, I just woke up this morning and realized this is nuts. Do you know what this movie's about? This woman grows a cock thing in her armpit and sucks people's blood through it. It's ridiculous! I can't do this. It's not going to work."{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=53}}

===Casting===
Cronenberg wanted [[Sissy Spacek]] to play the lead character after seeing her performance in ''[[Badlands (film)|Badlands]]''.<ref name=CBC>{{cite av media|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUHyTtZfjbo|title=David Cronenberg on casting porn star Marilyn Chambers: CBC Archives|publisher=[[Canadian Broadcasting Company]]|date=1979|via=[[YouTube]]|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201210215655/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XUHyTtZfjbo|archive-date=December 10, 2020}}</ref> However, Dunning rejected her citing her freckles and her Texan accent.{{sfn|Morris|1994|p=80}} [[Marilyn Chambers]] was suggested by executive producer [[Ivan Reitman]].{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=54}} Reitman heard that Chambers was looking for a mainstream role and had existing name recognition.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|pages=54-56}} Cronenberg agreed to cast Chambers after being impressed by her audition,<ref name=CBC/> even though he had not seen her best-known movie, ''[[Behind the Green Door]]''. Cronenberg would later say that he had been impressed by Chambers and her work ethic during the filming of ''Rabid''.<ref>{{cite video| people = Cronenberg, David| title = Rabid| medium = DVD| publisher = Somerville House |date = 2004 }}</ref> Spacek would star in the hit film ''[[Carrie (1976 film)|Carrie]]''; Cronenberg included a poster for that movie in the background of a scene in ''Rabid''.<ref name="afi" />

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] of ''Rabid'' took place in Montreal<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-montreal-star/187142076/|work=[[Montreal Star]]|date=December 11, 1976|last=Malina|first=Martin|title=Film: Alive and well in Montreal|p=D-15|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> from November 1 to December 5, 1976, on a budget of approximately $530,000 ({{Inflation|CA|530,000|1976|fmt=eq}})–$550,000 ({{Inflation|CA|550,000|1976|fmt=eq}}),<ref name=Adilman/><ref name=Adilman2>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-toronto-star-william-fruets-unmade/140772484/|work=[[Toronto Star]]|last=Adilman|first=Sid|title=Sophia Loren in Montreal|date=October 13, 1976|p=F1|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> with $200,000 coming from the CFDC.{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=235}} According to Cronenberg, at the time, the only CFDC films that had turned a profit were the two that he had made for the corporation, ''Shivers'' and ''[[Crimes of the Future (1970 film)|Crimes of the Future]]''.<ref name=afi/> Cronenberg's editing decisions to tighten and reduce the film's runtime produced criticism such as his removal of a scene, which he regretted cutting, explaining the development of the armpit orifice.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=57}}

Some filming occurred at [[Neighbourhood shopping centres in Montreal#Cavendish Mall|Cavendish Mall]], as well as [[Hôpital Notre-Dame|Notre-Dame Hospital]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cultmtl.com/2023/02/montreal-played-itself-in-david-cronenbergs-classic-horror-movie-rabid/|work=[[Cult MTL]]|title=Montreal played itself in David Cronenberg's classic horror movie Rabid|date=February 18, 2023|last=Rose|first=Alex|url-status=live|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220085307/https://cultmtl.com/2023/02/montreal-played-itself-in-david-cronenbergs-classic-horror-movie-rabid/|archive-date=December 20, 2025}}</ref>

==Release==
The film was distributed by [[Lionsgate Films|Cinépix]] in Canada and released on April 8, 1977, in Montreal.{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=236}} It was released theatrically in the United States by [[New World Pictures]] on July 6, 1977.<ref name=afi/> ''Rabid'' and ''[[Shivers (1975 film)|Shivers]]'' were re-released as a [[double feature]] in Paris in December 1994.{{sfn|Mathijs|2008|p=45}}

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Rabid'' grossed [[Canadian dollar|CA]]$100,000 in the first ten days after opening in Montreal.<ref>{{cite news|last=Kohl|first=Helen|date=October 13, 1979|title=It's a Horrible Way to Make a Living|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/84034937/national-post/|work=[[Financial Post]]|page=172|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> The film was one of the highest-grossing Canadian films of all time, earning approximately $7 million at the box office, including over $900,000 in its native Canada.<ref name=Adilman/><ref name=CAEnc>{{cite web|work=[[The Canadian Encyclopedia]]|title=David Cronenberg|url=https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/david-cronenberg|date=May 29, 2012|author1=Handling, Piers|author2=Mullen, Patrick|author3=Wise, Wyndham|url-status=live|archive-date=December 20, 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220080402/https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/david-cronenberg}}</ref>

===Critical response===
''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called ''Rabid'' "an extremely violent, sometimes nauseating, picture".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/1976/film/reviews/rabid-1200423939/|title=Rabid|date=31 December 1976|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=24 August 2021}}</ref> Les Wedman of the ''[[Vancouver Sun]]''  described the movie's story as "dreadful" and criticized the film for "relying heavily on shocking special effects" as opposed to suspense. He opined that, with the exception of Joe Silver, "there isn't a decent performance in the movie."<ref>{{cite news|last=Wedman|first=Les|date=June 6, 1977|title=Yup, it's Rabid|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/84034790/the-vancouver-sun/|work=[[Vancouver Sun]]|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> Martin Malina, who reviewed the film alongside similar films ''[[Demon Seed]]'' and ''[[Audrey Rose (film)|Audrey Rose]]'' in the same column of the ''[[Montreal Star]]'', said that it "has some genuinely frightening moments but they are few, and the story progresses in fits and starts. The dialogue is witless, the exposition awkward, and the film spends far too much time travelling in cars between one gory scene and another.".<ref>{{cite news| last= Malina| first = Martin| date=April 12, 1977|title= Devil busy in local cinemas| url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-montreal-star/134908469/| work= [[Montreal Star]]| via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> [[Clyde Gilmour]] of ''[[The Toronto Star]]'' noted of the film's box office success, but stated that he was "not sure what this proves, except possibly that Canadians, when they really try, can make nauseating movies as efficiently as Americans."<ref>{{cite news| last= Gilmour| first= Clyde| date= May 4, 1977| title= Colors pretty to look at but Ragtime Summer drags| url= https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-toronto-star/134912818/| work= [[Toronto Star]]| via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

Conversely, ''[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]'' called the film "far better staged" than ''Shivers'', and concluded, "None of the other recent apocalypse movies has shown so much political or cinematic sophistication."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.timeout.com/movies/rabid|title=Rabid 1976, directed by David Cronenberg|author=DP|website=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|date=September 10, 2012 |access-date=August 24, 2021}}</ref> Linda Gross of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' described the film as a "scary and crude variation of the traditional [[vampire film|vampire story]]" and praised Cronenberg's direction and the film's "grueling tension," though she found the film's storytelling inconsistent; she did, however, commend the performances, particularly Chambers', describing it as conveying "just the proper combination of vulnerability and deadliness."<ref name=Gross>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times/187141622/|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=July 7, 1977|last=Gross|first=Linda|via=Newspapers.com|title=A Variation of the Vampire Bit|p=16}}</ref>

Bruce McCabe of ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' found the film obscene, deeming it "a depraved film that no adult with any kind of sensitivity should be caught dead at."<ref>{{cite news|last=McCabe|first=Bruce|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-boston-globe/59164551/|work=[[The Boston Globe]]|date=July 26, 1977|title='Rabid' as insensitive as its rating|via=Newspapers.com|p=1}}</ref>

===Modern assessment===
{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|78||27|consensus=Its gruesome scares may nauseate more than thrill, but ''Rabid'' bites into its story with distinct sophistication and thought-provoking themes.|access-date=December 20, 2025|ref=y}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 56 out of 100, based on 9 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Rabid Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/rabid-1977/ |access-date=2026-02-10 |website=Metacritic |language=en}}</ref>

Critic [[Glenn Erickson]], reviewing the film in 2016, praised it, writing: "''Rabid'' remains a highly entertaining horror show that hasn’t lost an iota of shock value. It certainly lined up the fans—and investors—to allow the director to continue his personal exploration of queasy, outrageous biological delights."<ref name=Erickson>{{cite web|work=[[Trailers from Hell]]|last=Erickson|first=Glenn|author-link=Glenn Erickson|title=Rabid|date=November 19, 2016|url=https://trailersfromhell.com/rabid-2/|url-status=live|archive-date=December 20, 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251220092950/https://trailersfromhell.com/rabid-2/}}</ref>
