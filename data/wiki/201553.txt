'''''Romy and Michele's High School Reunion''''' is a 1997 American [[comedy film]] directed by [[David Mirkin]] and written by [[Robin Schiff]], based on characters from her play ''Ladies Room''. The film stars [[Mira Sorvino]] and [[Lisa Kudrow]] as two girls who have not achieved much success in life, and decide to invent fake careers to impress former classmates at their ten-year high-school reunion.

Upon its theatrical release on April 25, 1997, ''Romy and Michele'' received mixed to favorable reviews from critics, and grossed $29.2 million on a $20 million budget. Over the years, it has since come to be considered a [[Cult following|cult classic]].<ref name=BMedia>{{Cite web |last=Vorona Cote |first=Rachel |date=27 September 2017 |title="Romy and Michele" Is Still a Powerful Ode to Female Friendships |url=https://www.bitchmedia.org/article/turning-20/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion |access-date=1 April 2022 |website=[[Bitch Media]] |language=en |archive-date=April 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220401022240/https://www.bitchmedia.org/article/turning-20/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref name="Levine_id">{{Cite web |last=Levine |first=Nick |date=25 April 2017 |title=5 reasons why romy and michele's high school reunion is a low key feminist classic |url=https://i-d.co/article/5-reasons-why-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-is-a-low-key-feminist-classic/ |access-date=1 April 2022 |website=[[i-D]] |language=en |archive-date=April 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220401022241/https://i-d.vice.com/en_uk/article/ywvve7/5-reasons-why-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-is-a-low-key-feminist-classic |url-status=live }}</ref>

It was followed by a made-for-TV prequel, ''[[Romy and Michele: In the Beginning]]'', in 2005. A direct sequel titled, ''[[Romy and Michele 2]]'' starring Sorvino and Kudrow is scheduled for release on [[Hulu]] in 2027.

==Plot==
As high school students in [[Tucson, Arizona]] in 1987, Romy White and Michele Weinberger are continually bullied by the "A-Group," a small group of popular, yet mean, girls, led by cheerleader Christie Masters, who humiliates them repeatedly. Romy also has a crush on Christie's boyfriend, athlete Billy Christianson. Their classmate Heather Mooney is in love with a geek named Sandy Frink, but Sandy has a crush on Michele. Finally, at the prom, Romy asks Billy to dance with her. He agrees, but he and Christie trick the girls into thinking he has dumped Christie to be with Romy. Romy waits all night to dance with him, but he has already left with Christie. Michele dances with Romy instead.

Ten years later, Romy and Michele live together in an apartment in [[Los Angeles]], [[California]]. Romy works as a cashier in the service department at a [[Jaguar Cars|Jaguar]] dealership and Michele is unemployed. They are single, unambitious, and enjoy a casual lifestyle consisting mostly of dancing at nightclubs, eating junk food, and making fun of movies together. At work one day, Romy encounters Heather, who is now a successful businesswoman. Heather informs her about their upcoming ten-year high school reunion.

Romy realizes that their lack of achievements will not make a good impression at the reunion. Failing at their last-ditch attempts to improve themselves, they decide to fake success by showing up in an expensive car and business suits. Romy borrows a Jaguar from a co-worker, and Michele makes their outfits. En route to the reunion, they decide to claim that they invented the [[Post-it note]], believing that no one will know better. A confrontation over the details of their lie escalates into a fight about their friendship, and they decide to part ways once they reach the reunion.

When they arrive at the reunion, Romy leaves Michele asleep in the car, and Michele dreams that she and Romy each separately claim to reunion attendees to have invented Post-its without the help of the other. She begins a romance with Sandy, who is now wealthy and attractive, and Romy gets together with Billy. The two refuse to speak to each other for decades until Romy is on her deathbed; Michele calls her to make amends, but they rehash old confrontations and the dream ends with Romy's death.

Michele wakes up and enters the real reunion. The A-group are all pregnant, and Christie is married to Billy and has two children already. Romy starts to tell the Post-it story, but Heather arrives and unintentionally reveals the lie by telling everyone the [[Arthur Fry|real inventor]]'s name. Christie and her friends taunt Romy, who runs out of the room. Michele comforts her and the pair reconcile, deciding to be themselves instead of trying to impress others. They change into brightly colored homemade outfits and return to the reunion.

Christie makes fun of their clothes, but Lisa Luder, a former A-group girl who is now a fashion editor for ''[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]],'' approves of the outfits; Lisa coolly dismisses Christie's objections and the rest of the A-group girls abandon her.

Sandy, now wealthy and successful, arrives via helicopter. He confesses that he still loves Michele and asks her to dance with him. Michele agrees, as long as Romy can dance with them. Their dance receives huge applause, and Sandy escorts them to his helicopter. On their way out, they encounter Billy, who is unhappy in his marriage to Christie and boorishly hits on Romy. As turnabout for the trick he and Christie played on her at their prom, she convinces him to return to his hotel room to wait for her, but instead leaves the reunion with Michele and Sandy in the latter's helicopter.

Six months later, back in Los Angeles, Romy and Michele have opened a successful fashion boutique with their homemade designs using money borrowed from Sandy.

== Production ==

=== Development ===
The Romy and Michele characters first appeared in the 1988 stage play ''Ladies Room'', which was written by [[Robin Schiff]]. Schiff was in the comedy troupe [[The Groundlings]] with [[Lisa Kudrow]], who starred in the play as Michele opposite Christie Mellor as Romy.<ref name="VF" /> Schiff said her inspirations for the characters of Romy and Michele "were loosely based, just visually, on these girls I used to see going into a club on [[Sunset Boulevard|Sunset Blvd.]] You'd see these two friends, and they looked like they got dressed together and were wearing different versions of the same thing."<ref name="VF">{{Cite web |last=Pirnia |first=Garin |date=April 24, 2017 |title=The Woman Who Created Romy and Michele Never Thought They'd Be So Popular |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2017/04/romy-and-michele-high-school-reunion-anniversary-screenwriter-interview |access-date=April 9, 2022 |website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |language=en-US |archive-date=January 21, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220121093024/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2017/04/romy-and-michele-high-school-reunion-anniversary-screenwriter-interview |url-status=live }}</ref>

''Ladies Room'' was then adapted into a [[Television pilot|pilot]] for a sitcom called ''Just Temporary,'' with Kudrow and Mellor reprising their roles, but the pilot was not picked up to series.<ref name="VF" /><ref>{{Cite episode |people=Friedman, Kim (producer) |title=Pilot |series=Just Temporary |date=1989-09-01 |type=Comedy |publisher=Spelling Television }}</ref> Around this time, film executives at the Disney subsidiary [[Touchstone Pictures|Touchstone]] came across Schiff's play while "looking for a 'female version of ''[[Wayne's World (film)|Wayne's World]]''{{-'}}{{-"}}.<ref name="VF" /> Schiff was initially reluctant to adapt the play into a film, thinking some scenes would not transfer well to a movie. After Schiff pondered what it might be like if Romy and Michele were invited to their high school reunion ("…And it wasn't until they fill out the questionnaire when they realize their lives hadn't amounted to anything. That seemed funny to me"), Schiff began work on the script, which she would spend the next five years developing.<ref name="VF" /> Schiff based the characters' friendship partly on her relationship with her best friend. "One day we were stuck on a plane on a tarmac, and started reading the ''Sky Mall'' catalog and laughing our asses off. That was the kind of friend you want to hang out with—that even stuck on a plane on the tarmac you can still have fun."<ref name="VF" />

=== Casting ===
Kudrow's rising stardom from the show ''[[Friends]]'' played a part in the film successfully getting through the development stage.<ref name="VF" /> Australian actress [[Toni Collette]] was a strong contender for the role of Romy and met with director [[David Mirkin]].<ref name="Vogue">{{Cite web |last=Bell |first=Keaton |date=April 27, 2022 |title=The Blonde Leading the Blonde: An Oral History of Romy and Michele's High School Reunion |url=https://www.vogue.com/article/an-oral-history-of-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion |access-date=2022-05-10 |website=[[Vogue (magazine)|Vogue]] |language=en-US |archive-date=May 3, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220503195349/https://www.vogue.com/article/an-oral-history-of-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion |url-status=live }}</ref> The studio offered the role to [[Mira Sorvino]], who at that point was about to win a [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress Oscar]] for the film ''[[Mighty Aphrodite]]''.<ref name="Vogue" /> Said Mirkin, "It was definitely a long shot, but I heard she wanted to meet so we had lunch, and I instantly knew that she could do it. Romy and Michele were conceived as one tall and one short, but I loved the idea of Lisa and Mira playing this idiot blonde power couple."<ref name="Vogue" />

=== Box office ===
The film opened at number two in the North American box office, making $7.4 million in its opening weekend, finishing behind ''[[Volcano (1997 film)|Volcano]]''. It grossed a total of $29 million in North America.<ref name=mojo>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2372699649/weekend/ |title=Romy and Michele's High School Reunion |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220409232746/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/release/rl2372699649/weekend/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Critical response ===
{{RT prose|76||66|''Romy and Michele's High School Reunion'' has an admittedly slight premise, but it's elevated by ample heart, an infectiously playful spirit, and the buoyant chemistry of Lisa Kudrow and Mira Sorvino.|ref=yes|access-date=May 16, 2026}} On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 60 out of 100, based on 18 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.metacritic.com/movie/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion?ftag=MCD-06-10aaa1c |title= Romy and Michele's High School Reunion Reviews |website= [[Metacritic]] |access-date= February 19, 2021 |archive-date= June 21, 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20200621142834/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion?ftag=MCD-06-10aaa1c |url-status= live }}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "C" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemascore.com/|title=Find CinemaScore|format=Type "Romy" in the search box|website=[[CinemaScore]]|access-date=February 19, 2021|archive-date=August 9, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190809062201/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] gave the film three out of four stars, declaring it was "one of the brightest and goofiest comedies in a while, a film that has a share of truth, but isn't afraid to cut loose with the weirdest choreography I have seen outside a 1960s revival."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=April 25, 1997 |title=Romy And Michele's High School Reunion movie review (1997) |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-1997 |access-date=April 9, 2022 |website=[[Rogerebert.com]] |language=en |archive-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220409013014/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-1997 |url-status=live }}</ref>

[[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote the "candy-colored ''Romy and Michele's High School Reunion'' [is] cheerful, giddy fun" and praised the two female leads, saying "Ms. Kudrow and Ms. Sorvino make a fine team, elevating bubble-headedness to new levels of comic ingenuity."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Maslin |first=Janet |author-link=Janet Maslin |date=25 April 1997 |title=Being Smart Isn't, Like, Everything |language=en-US |work=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1997/04/25/movies/being-smart-isn-t-like-everything.html |access-date=1 April 2022 |issn=0362-4331 |archive-date=April 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220401042852/https://www.nytimes.com/1997/04/25/movies/being-smart-isn-t-like-everything.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Jack Mathews of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' said "beneath the endless silliness of the movie beats a real heart, and its theme of loyal friendship keeps propping it up every time the thin walls of the story seem about to collapse."<ref name="LATimes">{{Cite web |last=Mathews |first=Jack |date=April 25, 1997 |title='Romy and Michelle' Goes to Two Delightful Reunions |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-04-25-ca-52108-story.html |access-date=April 9, 2022 |website=[[Los Angeles Times]] |language=en-US |archive-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220409013014/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-04-25-ca-52108-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Mathews also praised "the dead-pan performances of Sorvino and Kudrow...Romy and Michele are cartoon characters, but the actresses make them both real and enormously sympathetic."<ref name="LATimes" />

==Legacy==
Though a modest success at the box office, the film steadily gained a cult following through home video and repeat cable TV airings since release.<ref name="Levine_id" /><ref name="HuffPost" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Nolfi |first=Joey |date=February 27, 2022 |title=Lisa Kudrow and Mira Sorvino bring Post-its for 'Romy and Michele' reunion at SAG Awards |url=https://ew.com/awards/sag-awards/romy-michele-high-school-reunion-lisa-kudrow-mira-sorvino-sag-awards-2022/ |access-date=2022-04-09 |website=[[EW.com]] |language=en |archive-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220409235610/https://ew.com/awards/sag-awards/romy-michele-high-school-reunion-lisa-kudrow-mira-sorvino-sag-awards-2022/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Dirt" />

In 2005, ''[[Romy and Michele: In the Beginning]]'', a [[prequel]] [[television film]] written and directed by Schiff, premiered on [[Freeform (TV channel)|ABC Family]].<ref name="VF" /> [[Katherine Heigl]] plays Romy and [[Alexandra Breckenridge]] stars as Michele.

In 2022, Kudrow and Sorvino appeared as their characters to present the award for the 2022 [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Best Ensemble - Comedy Series]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Radloff |first=Jessica |date=2022-02-28 |title=Mira Sorvino and Lisa Kudrow Brought Back Romy and Michele at the SAG Awards |url=https://www.glamour.com/story/lisa-kudrow-and-mira-sorvino-brought-back-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-at-the-2022-sag-awards |access-date=2024-08-13 |website=Glamour |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Musical ===
A musical adaptation premiered at the [[5th Avenue Theatre]] in [[Seattle|Seattle, Washington]] in June 2017.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hetrick |first=Adam |date=June 20, 2017 |title=What Do Critics Think of Romy and Michele's High School Reunion The Musical? |url=https://www.playbill.com/article/what-do-critics-think-of-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-the-musical |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170623032338/https://www.playbill.com/article/what-do-critics-think-of-romy-and-micheles-high-school-reunion-the-musical |archive-date=June 23, 2017 |website=[[Playbill]]}}</ref> The musical, directed by [[Kristin Hanggi]] (Broadway's ''[[Rock of Ages (musical)|Rock of Ages]]'') stars Cortney Wolfson and Stephanie Renee as Romy and Michele, respectively. ''[[Orange Is the New Black]]'' and ''[[Weeds (TV series)|Weeds]]'' composers [[Gwendolyn Sanford]] and Brandon Jay wrote the music and lyrics to original songs, including "Business Woman Special", "10 Years", "I Invented Post-Its" and "Changing Lives One Outfit at a Time".<ref name="VF" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=March 10, 2020 |title=Romy and Michele Musical Eyes New York City Bow After Developmental Workshop |url=https://playbill.com/article/romy-and-michele-musical-eyes-new-york-city-bow-after-developmental-workshop |access-date=2022-04-09 |website=Playbill |language=en |archive-date=April 9, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220409235610/https://playbill.com/article/romy-and-michele-musical-eyes-new-york-city-bow-after-developmental-workshop |url-status=live }}</ref>

In July 2025, it was announced that a new production of the stage musical would premiere [[off-Broadway]] on October 14, with a book by Schiff, Sanford, Jay, and direction by Hanggi.<ref>{{Cite web |date=July 2, 2025 |title="Romy & Michele" Stage Musical Will Bow Off-Broadway This Fall |url=https://playbill.com/article/romy-michele-stage-musical-will-bow-off-broadway-this-fall |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250813223613/https://playbill.com/article/romy-michele-stage-musical-will-bow-off-broadway-this-fall |archive-date=2025-08-13 |access-date=2025-09-10 |website=Playbill |language=en-US}}</ref> [[Laura Bell Bundy]] was cast as Romy White and [[Kara Lindsay]] was cast as Michele Weinberger.<ref>{{Cite web |date=September 4, 2025 |title=Laura Bell Bundy and Kara Lindsay Will Be "Romy & Michele" Off-Broadway |url=https://playbill.com/article/laura-bell-bundy-and-kara-lindsay-will-be-romy-michele-off-broadway |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250904153801/https://playbill.com/article/laura-bell-bundy-and-kara-lindsay-will-be-romy-michele-off-broadway |archive-date=2025-09-04 |access-date=2025-09-10 |website=Playbill |language=en-US}}</ref>

Two soundtrack albums featuring music from ''Romy and Michele's High School Reunion'' were released in 1997 by the Disney-owned [[Hollywood Records]]. The first album, titled "Original Soundtrack", was made available ten days before the official North American film release, while the second album, "More Music From the Motion Picture", was released four months later. Due to copyright issues, several songs which featured in the film did not appear on either soundtrack album; songs omitted include the film's opening song "[[Just a Girl]]" by [[No Doubt]], "[[Y.M.C.A. (song)|Y.M.C.A.]]" by [[Village People]], "[[Addicted to Love (song)|Addicted to Love]]" by [[Robert Palmer]], "[[Time After Time (Cyndi Lauper song)|Time After Time]]" by [[Cyndi Lauper]], "[[Ain't No Love (Ain't No Use)]]" by [[Sub Sub]], featuring [[Melanie Williams]], "[[Footloose (song)|Footloose]]" by [[Kenny Loggins]], "[[Hello Trouble (song)|Hello Trouble]]" by [[The Desert Rose Band]], "[[Don't Get Me Wrong]]" by [[The Pretenders]] and "Have a Good Time" by Talawah Crew. Both albums were reissued as a 2-CD set as part of the Double Features series.
