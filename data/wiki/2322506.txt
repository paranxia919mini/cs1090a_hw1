'''''Fantastic Planet''''' ({{langx|fr|La Planète Sauvage}}; {{langx|cs|Divoká planeta}}, {{lit|The Wild Planet}}) is a 1973 [[Surrealist cinema|surrealist]] [[Adult animation|adult animated]] [[science fiction film|science fiction]] [[art film]] directed by [[René Laloux]]. Its [[Allegory|allegorical]] story, about humans living on a strange planet dominated by giant [[humanoid]] aliens who consider them animals, is based on the 1957 novel ''[[Oms en série]]'' by the French writer [[Stefan Wul]]. The film was written by Laloux and [[Roland Topor]]; the latter also made the [[production design]]. It was a co-production between companies from France and Czechoslovakia and was animated at Jiří Trnka Studio in [[Prague]].<ref name="bcdb">{{Cite news|url=http://www.bcdb.com/cartoon/20518-La_Planete_Sauvage.html|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130117201914/http://www.bcdb.com/cartoon/20518-La_Planete_Sauvage.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=17 January 2013|title=La Planète Sauvage (Fantastic Planet) @ BCDB|publisher=BCDB|date=2012-11-16}}</ref>

A working title while in development was ''Sur la planète Ygam'' (''On the Planet Ygam''),<ref>{{cite book|last=Stephenson|first=Ralph|title=Animation in the Cinema|editor=Peter Cowie|publisher=A. Zwemmer|location=London|date=1967|series=International Film Guide|pages=173|chapter=15. Filmographies}}</ref> which is where most of the story takes place; the actual title (''The Fantastic/Savage Planet'') is the name of Ygam's [[Natural satellite|moon]]. Production began in 1963. ''Fantastic Planet'' was awarded the [[Grand Prix (Cannes Film Festival)|Grand Prix special jury prize]] at the [[1973 Cannes Film Festival]],<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/2288/year/1973.html |title=Festival de Cannes: Fantastic Planet |access-date=2009-04-19 |work=festival-cannes.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110822192952/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/2288/year/1973.html |archive-date=22 August 2011 |df=dmy }}</ref> and in 2016, it was ranked the 36th greatest animated movie ever by ''[[Rolling Stone]]''.<ref name=rollingstone>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/pictures/40-greatest-animated-movies-ever-20160628/36-fantastic-planet-1973-20160627|title=40 Greatest Animated Movies Ever - 36. ==Plot summary==
In the distant future, the giant blue [[humanoid]] Draags<ref>{{cite book |last1=Wul |first1=Stephan |title=Fantastic Planet Paperback |date=31 October 2010 |publisher=Creation Oneiros|isbn=978-1902197319 }}</ref>{{efn|In French and Czech spelling: Draag.}} have brought human beings (who are called Oms){{efn|This is a play on the French word for "man", ''homme''.}} from [[Earth]] to the planet Ygam, where the Draags maintain a technologically and spiritually advanced society. The Draags consider Oms animals, and while they keep some as [[Pet|pets]], others live in the wilderness and are periodically slaughtered by the Draags to [[Population control|control their population]]. Draags have much longer lifespans than Oms, but reproduce much less.

When an Om woman is killed by three Draag children, her orphaned infant is found by Master Sinh, a key Draag leader, and his daughter Tiwa, who keeps the boy as a pet and names him Terr. Tiwa loves Terr and is careful not to hurt him, but, in accordance with her parents' instructions, gives him a [[Collar (animal)|collar]] with which she can pull him in any direction. She brings Terr to sessions in which she receives her education using a headset that transmits knowledge into her mind; a defect in Terr's collar allows him to receive the knowledge too. Around the time that Tiwa grows into her teens and first performs Draag [[meditation]], which allows the species to travel with their minds, she loses some interest in Terr, who has become a young man and acquired much Draag knowledge. He escapes into the wilderness, stealing Tiwa's headset.

There he runs into a wild female Om, who cuts off his collar and introduces him to her [[tribe]], which lives in an abandoned Draag park full of strange creatures and landscapes. Terr shows them how to use the headset to acquire Draag knowledge and literacy, winning the right to do so in a [[duel]]. The literacy they gain allows them to read a Draag announcement that the park will be purged of Oms, and, when the purge comes, some are slaughtered by Draag technology while others escape, joining forces with another tribe. They are attacked by two Draag passers-by and manage to kill one of them before escaping to an abandoned Draag [[rocket]] depot, much to the outrage of Draag leaders.

They live there for years, joined by many other Oms. Due to the knowledge acquired from Terr's headset, they manage to replicate Draag technology, including two rockets; they hope to leave Ygam for its [[Natural satellite|moon]], the Fantastic Planet, and live there safe from Draags. When a planet-wide Om purge begins, a group led by Terr uses the rockets to flee to the Fantastic Planet, where they discover large [[Statue|statues]] that Draags travel to during meditation and use to meet beings from other galaxies in a strange mating ritual that maintains their species. The Oms destroy some of the statues, threatening the Draags' existence; the genocide of Oms is halted on Ygam, and, facing a crisis, the Draags negotiate for peace. After reaching peace, the Oms leave the Fantastic Planet to the Draags for their meditations and build an [[Satellite|artificial satellite]] named 'Terr' as their new home. This leads to an era of peaceful coexistence between the two species, who now benefit from each other's way of thinking.

The film's score was composed by [[Alain Goraguer]]. In a review for [[AllMusic]], François Couture noted:<ref name=allmusic>{{cite web |author=François Couture |title=La Planete Sauvage - Alain Goraguer |url=http://www.allmusic.com/album/la-planete-sauvage-mw0000107021 |publisher=[[AllMusic]] |access-date=12 July 2017 |archive-date=6 April 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170406032335/http://www.allmusic.com/album/la-planete-sauvage-mw0000107021 |url-status=live }}</ref>

{{blockquote|The main theme is very reminiscent of [[Pink Floyd]]'s ''[[Atom Heart Mother Suite]]'' (same half-time tempo, [[mellotron]], [[harpsichord]], and [[Wah-wah (music)|wah-wah]] [[guitar]]), and the other two are a ballad and a circus-like [[waltz]]. The music is very '70s-clichéd and will appeal to fans of French and Italian '70s soundtrack stylings. Although repetitive, the album itself creates an interesting [[marijuana]]-induced sci-fi floating mood, blending [[psychedelic music|psychedelia]], [[jazz]], and [[funk]]… [It] has been sampled by a few [[hip hop music|hip-hop]] artists.}}

The soundtrack was originally pressed on vinyl during the mid-70s in France. These original pressings command a high price on the secondary market given the soundtrack's limited release. In 2000, [[DC Recordings]] released the soundtrack on [[CD]], and the soundtrack was later reissued on [[LP Record|LP]].

Two of the tracks (''Terr et Tiwa Dorment'' and ''Méditation des Enfants'') also appear in enhanced editions of [[Grand Theft Auto V]] on the FlyLo FM radio station. However, no credits are given referring to them.

==Interpretations==
The film's narrative has been considered to be an [[allegory]] about  [[animal rights]] and [[human rights]] as well as [[racism]].<ref name=axmaker>{{cite web|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/74548/fantastic-planet#articles-reviews|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170308065818/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/74548/Fantastic-Planet/#articles-reviews|url-status=dead|archive-date=8 March 2017|title=Fantastic Planet (1973) - Articles|author=San Axmaker|website=Turner Classic Movies|publisher=[[Turner Classic Movies]]|access-date=12 July 2017}}</ref><ref name=avclub>{{cite web|url=https://www.avclub.com/fantastic-planet-looks-as-strange-today-as-it-must-have-1798188151|title=Fantastic Planet looks as strange today as it must have 40 years ago|author=Mike D'Angelo|author-link=Mike D'Angelo|website=[[The A.V. Club]]|date=18 June 2016|access-date=2 Dec 2025|archive-date=19 December 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241219021429/https://www.avclub.com/fantastic-planet-looks-as-strange-today-as-it-must-have-1798188151|url-status=live}}</ref> Sean Axmaker of [[Turner Classic Movies]] referred to the film as "nothing if not allegorical", writing that "it's not a stretch to see the fight against oppression reflected in the [[Civil Rights Movement|civil rights struggle in the United States]], the [[Algerian War|French in Algeria]], [[apartheid]] in South Africa, and (when injustice takes a turn to wholesale annihilation of the 'inferior' race) [[the Holocaust]] itself".<ref name=axmaker />

Liz Ohanesian of ''[[LA Weekly]]'' speculated on the film being a commentary on [[animal rights]], using the Draag's treatment of the Oms as evidence and writing that the film places "humans in roles of pets and pests".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.laweekly.com/arts/is-fantastic-planet-just-a-cool-movie-or-is-it-also-a-commentary-on-animal-rights-2373489|title=Is Fantastic Planet Just a Cool Movie or Is It Also a Commentary on Animal Rights?|author=Liz Ohanesian|work=[[LA Weekly]]|date=7 January 2011|access-date=12 July 2017|archive-date=14 September 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170914173623/http://www.laweekly.com/arts/is-fantastic-planet-just-a-cool-movie-or-is-it-also-a-commentary-on-animal-rights-2373489|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Mike D'Angelo]] of ''[[The A.V. Club]]'' wrote that "The Traag-Om dynamic is broad enough to be multipurpose, reflecting both racism and animal rights via 'How would ''you'' like it?' role reversal".<ref name=avclub />

==Reception and legacy==
===Box office===
The film was reported to have a total of 809,945 admissions in France.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=8569|title=La Planète sauvage|website=jp-boxoffice.com|access-date=12 July 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170713020218/http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=8569|archive-date=13 July 2017}}</ref>

===Critical response===
''Fantastic Planet'' has received generally positive reviews. On the [[Review aggregator|review aggregation]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 91% based on 35 reviews with an [[Weighted arithmetic mean|average rating]] of 7.4/10. The site's critical consensus reads "''Fantastic Planet'' is an animated epic that is by turns surreal and lovely, fantastic and graceful".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/fantastic_planet/|title=Fantastic Planet (1973)|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=6 October 2023|archive-date=2 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171202193755/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/fantastic_planet/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 73 out of 100, based on 13 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Fantastic Planet'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/fantastic-planet |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=6 October 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

[[Howard Thompson (film critic)|Howard Thompson]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote that the film offers "original, thoughtful, often strong (but tasteful) animation".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9B01E6DF1039E73ABC4152DFB4678388669EDE|title=Movie Review - - Fantastic Planet' Is Animated Feature|author=Howard Thompson|author-link=Howard Thompson (film critic)|date=19 December 1973|work=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=12 July 2017|archive-date=1 February 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180201043939/http://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9B01E6DF1039E73ABC4152DFB4678388669EDE|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' called it "disquieting, eerie and vastly imaginative."<ref>Thomas, Kevin (6 February 1974). "Life on a 'Fantastic Planet'". ''[[Los Angeles Times]]''. Part IV, p. 11.</ref> [[Gene Siskel]] of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'', on the other hand, gave the film one-and-a-half stars out of four and called it "an animated piece of science-fiction pretending to be a Meaningful Statement ... According to publicists for the film, the visuals and story begin to make sense if your mind is chemically altered. I doubt it."<ref>Siskel, Gene (5 March 1974). "What's this? Jellyfish with jaws?" ''[[Chicago Tribune]]''. Section 2, p. 5.</ref>

On 23 October 2007, Facets Video and Accent Cinema released a newly restored version on DVD, including many bonus features never available before.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/Fantastic-Planet-Lubomir-Rejthar/dp/B000TZN7KQ/ref=tmm_dvd_title_2?_encoding=UTF8&qid=&sr=|title=Fantastic Planet (35th Anniversary)|website=[[Amazon.com]]|access-date=12 July 2017|archive-date=23 January 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250123211238/https://www.amazon.com/Fantastic-Planet-Lubomir-Rejthar/dp/B000TZN7KQ/ref=tmm_dvd_title_2?_encoding=UTF8&qid=&sr=|url-status=live}}</ref> In June 2016, [[the Criterion Collection]] released the film on Blu-ray and DVD.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.criterion.com/films/28636-fantastic-planet|title=Fantastic Planet (1973)|publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]]|access-date=12 July 2017|archive-date=30 October 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171030193230/https://www.criterion.com/films/28636-fantastic-planet|url-status=live}}</ref>
