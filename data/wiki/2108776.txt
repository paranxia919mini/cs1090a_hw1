'''''FernGully: The Last Rainforest''''' is a 1992 animated musical [[fantasy film]] directed by [[Bill Kroyer]] from a screenplay by Jim Cox, based on the ''FernGully'' stories by Diana Young.<ref name="BFI">{{cite web |url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b7a8906ef |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180309083349/http://www.bfi.org.uk/films-tv-people/4ce2b7a8906ef |archive-date=March 9, 2018 |title=Ferngully The Last Rainforest |publisher=British Film Institute }}</ref> It stars the voices of [[Samantha Mathis]], [[Jonathan Ward (actor)|Jonathan Ward]], [[Tim Curry]], [[Robin Williams]], [[Christian Slater]], and [[Grace Zabriskie]].

The film is set in an [[Australia]]n [[rainforest]] inhabited by [[Fairy|fairies]], including Crysta, who accidentally shrinks a young [[Lumberjack|logger]] named Zak to the size of a fairy. Together, they rally the fairies and the animals of the rainforest to protect their home from the loggers and Hexxus, a malevolent pollution entity. Wayne Young, the film's producer, said that the film was "blatantly environmental", although he made an effort to avoid "preaching".

''FernGully'' was released in North America on April 10, 1992, to mainly positive reviews, and was generally considered a moderate financial success at both the box office and in home video sales. In 1998, it was followed by a [[direct-to-video]] sequel, ''[[FernGully 2: The Magical Rescue]]'', although none of the original voice cast reprised their roles.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be 400 to 700 words. -->
Crysta is a curious young fairy who lives in FernGully, a secluded rainforest east of [[Mount Warning]]. The fairies of FernGully once lived in harmony with humans, who are believed to be extinct after having been driven away by Hexxus, the dreaded spirit of destruction. Crysta is the apprentice of Magi Lune, the fairy tribe's leader, who ended Hexxus's reign by sealing him inside [[Adansonia gregorii|a boab tree]]. One day, Crysta explores a new part of the forest and meets Batty Koda, a cowardly but comical [[Megabat|bat]] who was experimented on by humans, giving him a manic and deluded personality, and an electric device sticking out of his head. Crysta accompanies Batty to investigate the humans' potential return. She meets Zak, a young teenage logger whom she inadvertently shrinks when she tries saving him from being crushed by a falling tree, although she does not know how to restore him to normal size. After she rescues him from being eaten by Lou, a hungry [[goanna]], Zak gains Crysta's trust and agrees to go with her to FernGully so Magi can unshrink him. Along the way, they bond by talking about their lives, but Zak hides the true reason for the humans' return.

Meanwhile, the tree containing Hexxus, which Zak accidentally marked while using his spray can against a fly, is cut down and processed by his supervisors, Tony and Ralph, using a mobile [[sawmill]].  Freed from his imprisonment, Hexxus quickly regains his powers by consuming the machine's polluting elements, and tricks Tony and Ralph into driving to FernGully. In FernGully, Crysta and Zak fall in love, but they soon discover the forest's destruction by Hexxus and the machine, exposing Zak's lie about the humans' intentions.  Feeling betrayed and hurt by his dishonesty, Crysta runs off as Zak comes clean.  Magi sacrifices herself to give the fairies a chance to fight back, telling Crysta to remember what she has learned.

The next morning, Hexxus and the machine arrive in FernGully.  Knowing the fairies' fight is hopeless, Zak convinces Batty to help him stop the machine before it destroys them.  Hexxus seemingly kills Batty and scares Tony and Ralph away, but Zak, with Pips's help, manages to access the machine's cab and disable it, cutting Hexxus off from the emissions he drew upon.  With Hexxus still manifesting via the oil in the machine, Crysta takes a seed and allows him to devour her, causing a tree to start growing from inside of him.  Pips and the other fairies use the powers Magi bestowed them with to re-imprison Hexxus, along with the machine, within the new tree at FernGully's border.  With FernGully saved, Crysta reunites with Zak and succeeds Magi as head of the fairy clan. She gives Zak a seed, asking him to remember his adventure and its lessons, before solemnly restoring him to his human size.  Zak finds and revives Batty, reunites with Tony and Ralph, and plants the seed as Crysta looks on.  With Hexxus defeated, the humans leave the forest and Crysta helps the seed sprout new growth for FernGully before playfully chasing Pips, with Batty following.

==Themes==
In the book ''Disney, Pixar, and the Hidden Messages of Children's Films'', M. Keith Booker states that ''FernGully'' "focuses on the theme of the destruction of the Earth's rainforests. In this case the rainforest is located near [[Mount Warning]], on the eastern coast of Australia, but the theme is global, and the specific location is not particularly emphasized." Despite the environmental theme, Booker wrote that the film was "somewhat vague in its explanation of the dire consequences of rainforest destruction, and it addresses the economic impetus behind this destruction hardly at all"; the fact that the rainforest was saved at the end of the film "diminishes the urgency of its environmentalist message", and that the character of Hexxus "displaces the real blame for environmental destruction from its real perpetrators onto nonexistent supernatural perpetrators, further diluting the political message". The character of Batty was said to introduce "the secondary theme of animal experimentation, though with a light touch that presents this potentially horrifying motif as essentially humorous".<ref>{{cite book |last=Booker |first=M. Keith |date=November 25, 2009 |title=Disney, Pixar, and the Hidden Messages of Children's Films |publisher=[[Greenwood Publishing Group|Praeger]] |pages=122–124 |isbn=978-0-31337-672-6}}</ref>

In the book, ''Eco-Impacts and the Greening of Postmodernity'', Tom Jagtenberg and [[David McKie]] comment that radical views of [[ecology]] flourished in the film, perhaps because it was "aimed at a younger generation... and belong[s] to relatively discredited genres". As Zak is shrunk to fairy size and integrated into the fairy world, more similarities rather than differences are implied with the nonhuman characters. Crysta is said to defeat the evil Hexxus "in the manner of classic western genre heroes", although with the key difference that her weapon is a seed rather than a revolver, allowing the produce of nature to share the heroic role with her.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Jagtenberg |first1=Tom |last2=McKie |first2=David |date=November 7, 1996 |title=Eco-Impacts and the Greening of Postmodernity |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=VaCnBAAAQBAJ&q=%22aimed+at+a+younger+generation%22+%22to+relatively+discredited+genres%22.&pg=PA177 |publisher=Sage Publications |pages=177–178 |isbn=978-0803974074}}</ref>

==Production==
Producer Wayne Young said that his passion for the environment was his motivation for making the film, saying that the film was "blatantly environmental, although we have gone to a lot of trouble to avoid preaching. We also want it to be viewed as entertainment." The inspiration for ''FernGully'' came from stories written by his former wife, Diana Young.<ref name="vanityfair">{{cite web |last=Tattoli |first=Chantel |date=April 25, 2017 |title=FernGully at 25: How an Upstart Disney Rival Created a Millennial Silent Spring |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2017/04/ferngully-making-of |work=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |access-date=October 10, 2020 |archive-date=October 11, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201011041845/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2017/04/ferngully-making-of |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Portman 1992">{{cite news |last=Portman |first=Jamie |date=April 14, 1992 |title=Ferngully an Enchantment |newspaper=[[Calgary Herald]] |agency=Southam News |page=C7 |issn=1197-2823 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/120204715/ferngully-an-enchantment-jamie-portman/ |archive-date=March 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230305034444/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/120204715/ferngully-an-enchantment-jamie-portman/ |url-status=live |access-date=January 13, 2025 }}</ref> Diana first wrote the story of ''FernGully'' fifteen years before the film's release. Wayne said that the couple planned a film adaptation for five years, then spent "seven years of dreaming and hustling, followed by another three years of production". Wayne stated that their dream was not possible until the success of [[Walt Disney Animation Studios|Walt Disney Feature Animation]]'s 1989 film, ''[[The Little Mermaid (1989 film)|The Little Mermaid]]'', which helped bring popularity back to animation.<ref name="brownstein">{{cite news |last=Brownstein |first=Bill |date=April 16, 1992 |title=It's hip, it's animated, and it's eco-friendly; Cartoon adventure FernGully began with an idea 15 years ago |newspaper=[[The Gazette (Montreal)|The Gazette]] |page=F1 |issn=0384-1294}}</ref> Hand-drawn scenes in the film were complemented by [[computer animation]], which was used to create elements, such as [[flocks of birds]] that would have taken much longer to traditionally animate. Kroyer stated that 40,000 frames of computer-generated graphics were used in the film, and that the use of such animation halved the production time.<ref>{{cite book |last=Rickitt |first=Richard |title=Special Effects: The History and Technique |publisher=Billboard Books |date=2000 |isbn=0-8230-7733-0 |page=147 }}</ref> Most of the film's $24 million budget was spent on the animation and the soundtrack.<ref name="brownstein" />

The film marked [[Robin Williams]]'s first animation role, with the character Batty Koda being created specifically for him. Williams provided fourteen hours of improvised lines for the part, which had been originally conceived as an eight-minute role. Director [[Bill Kroyer]] was so impressed with the voice work that he ended up tripling the screen time given to the character. Williams would provide the voice of the [[Genie (Disney)|Genie]] in Disney's ''[[Aladdin (1992 Disney film)|Aladdin]]'' later in the year, receiving critical acclaim.<ref>{{cite news |last=Rusoff |first=Jane Wollman |date=June 9, 1992 |title=Animation gives stars a whole other way to express themselves |newspaper=[[Milwaukee Journal Sentinel]] |page=D1 |issn=1082-8850}}</ref> Williams had already agreed to voice Batty Koda before being approached to do ''Aladdin''. [[Jeffrey Katzenberg]], then-chairman of [[Walt Disney Studios (division)|Walt Disney Studios]], tried to force Williams to withdraw from ''FernGully'', on the grounds that he did not want him voicing two animated characters around the same time, but Williams refused. According to Wayne Young, Disney repeatedly interfered with the production of ''FernGully'', twice taking over spaces that the producers had rented, by offering to pay more. When the producers eventually set up a studio in a former brewery in the [[San Fernando Valley]], Disney attempted to purchase it. Katzenberg declined to comment on the issue when approached by ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]'' in 2017.<ref name="vanityfair" /> The voice cast of ''FernGully'' agreed with the film's message, and worked for scale wages.<ref name="brownstein" /><ref>{{cite news |last=Wuntch |first=Phillip |date=December 18, 1992 |title=Williams serious about Toys: It's a whimsical response to military mind |newspaper=[[The Province]] |page=C10 |issn=0839-3311}}</ref><ref name="green">{{cite news |last=Green |first=Tom |date=December 18, 1992 |title=Wild Child: Playful role fits the boyish soul |newspaper=[[USA Today]] |page=01D |issn=0734-7456}}</ref> The film marked the first time that both members of [[Cheech & Chong]] had worked together in six years, with the two voicing beetle brothers, Stump and Root. [[Cheech Marin]] said that "it was just like old times, but we only worked for two or three hours, had a pizza and split".<ref>{{cite news |last=Rusoff |first=Jane Wollman |date=May 13, 1992 |title=Speaking Up: Stars lend their voices to animated characters |newspaper=[[St. Louis Post-Dispatch]] |pages=1W, [https://www.newspapers.com/clip/21507616/starslendtheirvoicestoanimatedchar/ 4W] |issn=1930-9600 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/21507611/speakingupstarslendtheirvoicestoa/ |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |access-date=June 12, 2022 |archive-date=June 12, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220612023622/https://www.newspapers.com/clip/21507611/speakingupstarslendtheirvoicestoa/ |url-status=live }} {{Open access}}</ref>

==Release==
''FernGully'' was released in the United States on April 10, 1992, and in Australia on September 17. The film was shown at the [[United Nations General Assembly]] on [[Earth Day]], April 22.<ref>{{cite news |last=Kahlenberg |first=Richard |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-04-09-vl-272-story.html |date=April 9, 1992 |title=An Aussie Vision: The creators of 'FernGully-The Last Rainforest' find a taste of home in Ojai. Their film opens tomorrow |newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]] |page=14 |issn=0458-3035 |url-access=limited |access-date=June 12, 2022 |archive-date=June 12, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220612023622/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-04-09-vl-272-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Box office===
''FernGully'' grossed US$32.7 million worldwide, including $24.7 million from the United States,<ref name="boxoffice" /> and $3.4 million in Australia.<ref name="newcastle">{{cite news |date=May 29, 1998 |title=Own Your Own |newspaper=[[The Newcastle Herald]] |page=12}}</ref> The box-office performance was described as a moderate success,<ref name="horn" /><ref name="scally" /> although it grossed below expectations, possibly because of its ecological message.<ref name="green" /> Joseph Gelmis from ''[[Newsday]]'', however, described ''FernGully''{{'}}s box-office performance as "dismal", although he noted that it was the most successful recent non-Disney animated film.<ref>{{cite news |last=Gelmis |first=Joseph |date=December 13, 1992 |title=That Disney Touch |newspaper=[[Newsday]] |page=6}}</ref> Co-executive producers, Jaime Willett and Josh Baran, who worked on the film's marketing, both spoke of the difficulties of getting attention to an animated film that was not produced by Disney, with Willett stating that box-office revenue would have at least doubled by simply having the headline, "Walt Disney presents", on the film.<ref name="horn" /> ''[[USA Today]]'' noted that the combined box-office gross of ''FernGully'' and the five other non-Disney animated films released in 1992 did not equal a third of the gross for Disney's 1991 film, ''[[Beauty and the Beast (1991 film)|Beauty and the Beast]]''.<ref>{{cite news|last=Wloszczyna|first=Susan|date=November 4, 1992|title=A wish upon 'Aladdin': Disney rubs magic lamp of animation|newspaper=[[USA Today]]|page=01D|issn=0734-7456}}</ref>

===Legacy===
Wayne Young stated that portions of the film's gross would be donated to [[Greenpeace]], the [[Rainforest Foundation Fund]] and the [[Sierra Club]], as well as a special fund benefiting environmental projects administered worldwide by the [[Smithsonian Institution]],<ref name="brownstein" /> although he did not disclose exact figures.<ref name="Portman 1992" /> The film also inspired a 1992 [[video game]] by [[Capstone Software]] and [[IntraCorp]], called ''The FernGully [[Computerized Coloring Books|Computerized Coloring Book]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|title=FernGully - Taking a Peek|url=https://archive.org/stream/Computer_Gaming_World_Issue_95#page/n9|magazine=[[Computer Gaming World]]|issue=95|publisher=[[Ziff Davis]]|date=June 1992 |pages=8, 10}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=DeCoster|last2=Crook|first1=Jeane|first2=David|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1992-04-18-ca-518-story.html|title=Coloring Book Caper|date=April 18, 1992|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|archive-url=https://archive.today/20180827014217/http://articles.latimes.com/1992-04-18/entertainment/ca-518_1_color-book-lines|archive-date=August 27, 2018|access-date=August 27, 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> In 1998, the film was followed by a [[direct-to-video]] sequel, ''[[FernGully 2: The Magical Rescue]]''. [[FAI Films]], which produced only ''FernGully'' and its sequel, was acquired by [[HIH Insurance]] in 1998. HIH closed in 2001. In June 2012, administrators for HIH placed advertisements trying to sell the rights to both films.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.smartcompany.com.au/finance/economy/cartoon-crisis-as-australian-animated-film-companies-are-liquidated/|title=Cartoon crisis as Australian animated film companies are liquidated|last=Schmidl|first=Engel|date=June 4, 2012|work=SmartCompany|archive-url=https://archive.today/20180827010727/https://www.smartcompany.com.au/finance/economy/cartoon-crisis-as-australian-animated-film-companies-are-liquidated/|archive-date=August 27, 2018|url-status=live|access-date=August 27, 2018}}</ref> In November 2021, [[Shout! Factory]] made a deal with Machine Media Advisors, acquiring worldwide distribution rights to the film.<ref name="Milligan">{{cite web|url=https://www.animationmagazine.net/home-entertainment/exclusive-shout-studios-secures-worldwide-rights-to-ferngully-the-last-rainforest/|title=Exclusive: Shout! ==Home media==
Four months after the theatrical release, [[20th Century Studios Home Entertainment|20th Century Fox Home Entertainment]], under its previous name, "Fox Video", released ''FernGully'' on [[VHS]] and [[LaserDisc]] on August 26, 1992. Sales were strong,<ref name=horn>{{cite news|last=Horn|first=John|date=December 3, 1993|title=Animated features not always a draw|newspaper=[[The Globe and Mail]]|page=D3|issn=0319-0714}}</ref> with approximately five million units sold by 1998,<ref name="scally">{{cite news|last=Scally|first=Robert|date=March 23, 1998|title=Studios forgo kiddie matinees to build direct-to-video branding|newspaper=Discount Store News|pages=53–55}}</ref> including 125,000 in Australia.<ref name="newcastle" /> Fox re-released the film on [[DVD-Video|DVD]] in 2002. Christopher Simons from [[DVD Talk]] gave the 2002 DVD three-and-a-half stars out of five for both audio and video, although only one star for special features, noting that the only extras included were trailers for other films.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/3337/ferngully/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304073817/http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/3337/ferngully/|archive-date=March 4, 2016|url-status=live|title=FernGully|first=Christopher|last=Simons|date=February 3, 2002|work=[[DVD Talk]]|access-date=June 3, 2015}}</ref> A "Family Fun Edition" DVD was released in 2005. Special features included commentary with director [[Bill Kroyer]], art director [[Ralph Eggleston]], and coordinating art director Susan Kroyer, several featurettes, including the original featurette from 1992, the music video for "If I'm Gonna Eat Somebody (It Might As Well Be You)" by [[Tone Loc]], as well as trailers and TV spots. Scott Weinberg from ''DVD Talk'' gave this version four stars out of five for both audio and video, and four stars for special features.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/18312/ferngully-the-last-rainforest-family-fun-edition/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304074939/http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/18312/ferngully-the-last-rainforest-family-fun-edition/|archive-date=March 4, 2016|url-status=live|title=FernGully: The Last Rainforest - Family Fun Edition|last=Weinberg|first=Scott|date=October 20, 2005|work=[[DVD Talk]]|access-date=June 3, 2015}}</ref> For its 20th anniversary, ''FernGully'' was released on [[Blu-ray]] on March 6, 2012, containing the same special features as the "Family Fun Edition". [[Aaron Peck (writer)|Aaron Peck]] from ''High Def Digest'' gave it 3 stars out of five for video quality, four stars for audio and three-and-a-half stars for extras.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bluray.highdefdigest.com/6293/ferngully.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161109040213/http://bluray.highdefdigest.com/6293/ferngully.html|archive-date=November 9, 2016|url-status=live|title=FernGully: The Last Rainforest|last1=Peck|first1=Aaron|date=March 8, 2012|work=High Def Digest|access-date=June 3, 2015}}</ref> Brian Orndorf from Blu-ray.com gave the release three stars out of five for video quality, three-and-a-half stars for audio and four stars for special features.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/FernGully-The-Last-Rainforest-Blu-ray/35209/#Review|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160728082915/http://www.blu-ray.com/movies/FernGully-The-Last-Rainforest-Blu-ray/35209/|archive-date=July 28, 2016|url-status=live|title=FernGully: The Last Rainforest Blu-ray Review|last=Orndorf|first=Brian|date=October 4, 2012|publisher=Blu-ray.com|access-date=June 3, 2015}}</ref> In 2022, for its 30th anniversary, ''FernGully'' received a new Blu-ray release by [[Shout! Studios]].<ref name="Milligan" />

==Live-action film==
In April 2026, a live-action remake was announced to be directed and written by [[Marielle Heller]] was announced by [[Amazon MGM Studios]]. The film will be produced by Stacey Sher, Susan Ursitti‑Sheinberg, Jon Sheinberg, Leah Holzer, Marielle Heller, and Matt Feige. Moonli Singha will serve as executive producer.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2026/04/ferngully-live-action-film-amazon-mgm-marielle-heller-directs-1236864500/|title='FernGully' Returning As Live Action Film; Amazon MGM Studios Sets Marielle Heller To Direct|last=Fleming Jr.|first=Mike|date=April 17, 2026|publisher=Deadline Hollywood|access-date=April 17, 2026}}</ref>
