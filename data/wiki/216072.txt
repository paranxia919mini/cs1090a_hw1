'''''Home Alone''''' is a 1990 American [[Christmas comedy film]] directed by [[Chris Columbus (filmmaker)|Chris Columbus]] and written and produced by [[John Hughes (filmmaker)|John Hughes]]. The first film in the [[Home Alone (franchise)|''Home Alone'' franchise]], the film stars [[Macaulay Culkin]] as Kevin McCallister, an eight-year-old boy who defends his [[Chicago metropolitan area|suburban Chicago]] home from a [[home invasion]] by a pair of robbers after his family accidentally leaves him behind on their [[Christmas vacation]] to [[Paris]]. The cast also features [[Joe Pesci]], [[Daniel Stern (actor)|Daniel Stern]], [[John Heard (actor)|John Heard]], and [[Catherine O'Hara]].

Hughes conceived ''Home Alone'' while on vacation, with [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] being originally intended to finance and distribute the film. Warner Bros. shut down the production after it exceeded its assigned budget, but it quickly resumed under [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]] following meetings with Hughes; Columbus and Culkin were hired soon afterwards. Filming took place between February and May 1990 on location across [[Illinois]].

''Home Alone'' had a [[sneak preview]] across 1,000 theaters on November 10, 1990, and was theatrically released in the United States on November 16. It received mixed-to-positive reviews and grossed $476.7 million worldwide, becoming the [[second-highest-grossing film of 1990]]. It made Culkin a child star and was the highest-grossing live-action comedy for two decades. It was nominated for two [[Academy Awards]] and two [[Golden Globe Awards]]. ''Home Alone'' has since been considered one of the [[best Christmas films]]. In 2023, ''Home Alone'' was selected for preservation in the [[National Film Registry]] by the [[Library of Congress]] as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". Five sequels have been released: ''[[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York]]'' (1992), ''[[Home Alone 3]]'' (1997), ''[[Home Alone 4: Taking Back the House]]'' (2002), ''[[Home Alone: The Holiday Heist]]'' (2012), and ''[[Home Sweet Home Alone]]'' (2021).

==Plot==
<!-- NOTICE: Plot summaries should be 400-700 words. See WP:FILMPLOT. Do not add unnecessary detail to this section. -->
The McCallister family is preparing to spend Christmas in Paris, gathering at Peter and Kate's home in a [[Chicago]] suburb the night before their departure. Their youngest son, Kevin, is the subject of ridicule by his older siblings because of his immaturity. During a scuffle with his oldest brother Buzz, Kevin accidentally ruins the family dinner; while cleaning up the mess, Peter inadvertently throws away Kevin's flight ticket. As punishment, Kate sends Kevin to the [[attic]], where he angrily berates his mother and wishes his family would disappear. During the night, heavy winds cause a [[power outage]] that resets the [[Digital clock|alarm clocks]] and leads the family to oversleep. The next morning, in the chaotic rush to leave for the airport, Kevin is accidentally left behind.

Kevin wakes to find the house empty and, thinking that his wish has come true, initially enjoys his newfound freedom. He ransacks Buzz's room, buys and eats whatever he wants, and watches violent movies. However, Kevin soon becomes frightened by his next door neighbor, Old Man Marley, who is rumored by Buzz to be a [[serial killer]] who murdered his own family in the 1950's, as well as the "Wet Bandits", Harry and Marv, a pair of burglars who have been breaking into other vacant houses in the neighborhood and have targeted the McCallisters' house. Kevin tricks them into thinking that his family is still home, forcing them to put their plans on hold.

Kate realizes mid-flight that Kevin was left behind. Upon arrival in Paris, the family discovers that all flights for the next two days are fully booked. While the rest of the family stay in Peter's brother's apartment, Kate manages to get a flight to the United States, but it is a flight to [[Dallas|Dallas, Texas]].

On Christmas Eve, Kate ends up in [[Scranton, Pennsylvania]], where all flights to Chicago are fully booked. She ends up riding with a polka band who are driving to [[Milwaukee]] and whose leader, Gus Polinski, offered to let her ride with them to Chicago after overhearing her desperation to get home. Meanwhile, Harry and Marv finally realize that Kevin is home alone, and Kevin overhears them discussing plans to break into his house. Kevin starts to miss his family and asks the local [[Santa Claus]] impersonator if he could bring them back for Christmas. He goes to a church and watches a [[choir]] perform, then meets Old Man Marley, who dispels the rumors about him. He points out his granddaughter in the choir, whom he is unable to spend Christmas with, as he and his son are estranged; Kevin suggests that he should reconcile with his son.

Kevin returns home and rigs the house with [[booby trap]]s to take on the burglars. Harry and Marv break in, spring the traps, and suffer various injuries. While the duo pursues Kevin around the house, he calls the police and flees, then lures Harry and Marv into a neighboring home which they previously broke into. They catch him and prepare to get their revenge, but Marley, who is next door checking on his son's house, intervenes and knocks them unconscious with his snow shovel. The police arrive and arrest Harry and Marv, having identified all the houses that they broke into due to Marv's destructive characteristic of flooding them.

On Christmas morning, Kevin is disappointed to find that his family is still gone. He then hears Kate enter the house and call for him; they reconcile and are soon joined by Peter, Buzz, Jeff, Megan, and Linnie, who managed to return by catching a flight to Dallas and then a connecting flight to Chicago. Kevin keeps silent about his encounter with Harry and Marv, although Peter finds Harry's knocked-out gold tooth. Kevin then observes Marley reuniting with his son, daughter-in-law, and granddaughter. Marley notices Kevin, and the pair wave to each other knowingly. Buzz sees what Kevin did to his room and screams in anger as Kevin runs off.

== Production ==
===Development===
Writer and producer [[John Hughes (filmmaker)|John Hughes]] conceived ''Home Alone'' while preparing to go on vacation. He said: "I was going away on vacation, and making a list of everything I didn't want to forget. I thought, 'Well, I'd better not forget my kids.' Then I thought, 'What if I left my 10-year-old son at home? What would he do?{{Single+double}}<ref name="conception">{{cite magazine |url=https://time.com/4105161/home-alone-1990/ |title=How John Hughes Came Up With Home Alone |date=November 16, 2015 |magazine=Time|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190519015208/http://time.com/4105161/home-alone-1990/|archive-date=May 19, 2019|access-date=April 10, 2020}}</ref> Hughes wrote eight pages of notes that developed into the screenplay.<ref name=conception/> Imagining that children are naturally most scared of robbers, Hughes also worked that aspect into the plot of the film.<ref name=conception/>

''Home Alone'' was initially set to be financed and distributed by [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] Hughes promised that he could make the movie for less than $10 million, considerably less than most feature film production budgets of that era. Concerned that the film might exceed that amount, Hughes met secretly with [[20th Century Fox]] before production to see if they would fund the project if Warner Bros. proved inflexible. According to executive producer Scott Rosenfelt, a copy of the script was "clandestinely" delivered to Fox, bypassing the legal restrictions that would have otherwise prevented Fox from seeing it until the project was in [[Turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]].<ref name="The Movies that Made Us">{{cite news |last=Bucklow |first=Andrew |url=https://www.news.com.au/entertainment/movies/home-alone-secrets-revealed-in-netflix-show-the-movies-that-made-us/news-story/7dd4896680c9c157e18b7f4e264c418f |title=''Home Alone'' secrets revealed in Netflix show 'The Movies That Made Us' |date=December 4, 2019 |newspaper=[[news.com.au]]|access-date=December 4, 2019|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200212233609/https://www.news.com.au/entertainment/movies/home-alone-secrets-revealed-in-netflix-show-the-movies-that-made-us/news-story/7dd4896680c9c157e18b7f4e264c418f|archive-date=February 12, 2020}}</ref> Early in production, the budget grew to $14.7 million. Warner Bros. demanded that it be cut by $1.2 million; the producers responded with a memo arguing that the budget could not be cut any further. Unconvinced, Warner Bros. shut down production the next day, but it quickly resumed when Fox took up Hughes on his offer. The final budget grew to $18 million.<ref name="The Movies that Made Us" />

Hughes had asked [[Patrick Read Johnson]] to direct, but he was committed to directing ''[[Spaced Invaders]]'' (1990).<ref>{{Cite book |title=John Hughes: A Life in Film |last=Honeycutt |first=Kirk |publisher=Race Point Publishing |year=2015 |isbn=978-1631060229}}</ref> He turned to [[Chris Columbus (filmmaker)|Chris Columbus]], who had left ''[[National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation]]'' (1989) before shooting started because of a personality clash with starring actor [[Chevy Chase]], who Columbus said treated him "like dirt".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.chicagomag.com/Chicago-Magazine/December-2015/Home-Alone/ |title=Holy Cow, Home Alone Is 25! |date=November 10, 2015 |publisher=Chicago Magazine|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200406032958/https://www.chicagomag.com/Chicago-Magazine/December-2015/Home-Alone/|archive-date=April 6, 2020|access-date=April 10, 2020}}</ref> Hughes gave him the scripts for both ''Home Alone'' and ''[[Reach the Rock]]'' (1998); Columbus chose to direct ''Home Alone'', as he found it funnier and liked the Christmas theme.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.vulture.com/2015/11/columbus-directed-home-alone-because-chevy-chase.html |title=Chris Columbus Directed Home Alone Instead of Christmas Vacation Because He Met Chevy Chase |last1=Madison III |first1=Ira |date=November 10, 2015 |website=Vulture|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191219083024/https://www.vulture.com/2015/11/columbus-directed-home-alone-because-chevy-chase.html|archive-date=December 19, 2019|access-date=June 10, 2016}}</ref><ref name="EW2016" /> Columbus did an uncredited rewrite of the script; among his contributions was the character of Old Man Marley, which he created to give the story a more serious layer, as well as a more emotional, happier ending.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Honeycutt |first1=Kirk |title=John Hughes: A Life in Film |date=March 25, 2015 |publisher=RacePoint Publishing |location=Paragraph 8 |isbn=978-1631060229 |page=160}}</ref>

===Casting===
[[File:Macaulay Culkin 1991 B.jpg|thumb|150px|[[Macaulay Culkin]] (pictured in 1991) was the child star of the film.]]
Hughes suggested to Columbus that they cast [[Macaulay Culkin]] as Kevin, because of his experience while shooting ''[[Uncle Buck]]'' (1989). Columbus met with 200 other actors for the part, as he felt it was his "directorial responsibility".<ref name="EW2016">{{cite magazine |last1=Wilkinson |first1=Amy |date=November 6, 2015 |title=Home Alone turns 25: A deep dive with director Chris Columbus |url=https://ew.com/article/2015/11/06/home-alone-turns-25-chris-columbus/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191229151131/https://ew.com/article/2015/11/06/home-alone-turns-25-chris-columbus/|archive-date=December 29, 2019|access-date=June 10, 2016 |magazine=Entertainment Weekly}}</ref><ref name=":1" /> [[John Mulaney]] was asked to audition for the role of Kevin after being scouted in a children's [[sketch comedy]] group, but his parents refused the opportunity.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Swartz |first=Tracy |title=Chicago native John Mulaney reveals he was asked to audition for 'Home Alone' |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/entertainment/ct-home-alone-john-mulaney-20161220-story.html|access-date=August 9, 2021 |newspaper=Chicago Tribune|date=December 20, 2016 |archive-date=August 9, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210809201154/https://www.chicagotribune.com/entertainment/ct-home-alone-john-mulaney-20161220-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Columbus finally met with Culkin and agreed he was the right choice.<ref name=EW2016/>

Before [[Catherine O'Hara]] and [[John Heard (actor)|John Heard]] would be cast as the parents, considerations for the roles included [[Susan Sarandon]], [[Kirstie Alley]], [[Blythe Danner]], [[Joanna Cassidy]] and [[Candice Bergen]] for Kate, and [[Sam Waterston]], [[David Dukes]] and [[Danny Aiello]] for Peter. [[James Stewart]] and [[Vincent Gardenia]] had been considered for the role of Marley before [[Roberts Blossom]] was chosen.<ref name=CastingAlternatives>{{cite web |last1=Oganesyan |first1=Natalie |title=Original 'Home Alone' Casting Notes Reveal Shortlist Of Potential Stars, Including Jimmy Stewart, Susan Sarandon, Tim Curry & More |url=https://deadline.com/2025/12/home-alone-casting-notes-shortlist-susan-sarandon-tim-curry-1236648163/ |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |access-date=December 15, 2025 |date=December 14, 2025}}</ref>

[[Robert De Niro]] was considered for the role of Harry, but after [[Jon Lovitz]] turned it down, [[Joe Pesci]] was cast.<ref name=":2" /><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.businessinsider.com/home-alone-30-anniversary-chris-columbus-trump-cameo-2020-11|title='Home Alone' at 30: Director Chris Columbus talks Trump 'bullying his way' into a cameo and more never-before-told stories|first=Jason|last=Guerrasio|website=Business Insider}}</ref> He informed the producers he was "offer only", so the producers did not audition him. [[Tim Curry]], [[Joe Pantoliano]] and [[Zack Norman]] were also considered before Pesci ultimately accepted the role.<ref name=CastingAlternatives/> The role of Uncle Frank was written for [[Kelsey Grammer]], but was given to [[Gerry Bamman]] when Grammer was unavailable.<ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.glamour.com/story/15-facts-about-home-alone |title=15 Fascinating Facts You Never Knew About Home Alone |last=Eidell |first=Lynsey |date=November 16, 2015 |magazine=Glamour |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200221155545/https://www.glamour.com/story/15-facts-about-home-alone|archive-date=February 21, 2020|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.looper.com/33705/cast-home-alone-today/ |title=Where the cast of Home Alone is today |last=Moore |first=Trent |date=July 22, 2017 |website=Looper.com |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200221155549/https://www.looper.com/33705/cast-home-alone-today/|archive-date=February 21, 2020|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref>

[[Daniel Stern (actor)|Daniel Stern]] was cast as Marv, but before shooting started, he was told that the production schedule had been extended from six weeks to eight. He dropped out after as he would not be paid more for the extended schedule. [[Daniel Roebuck]] was hired to replace him, but after two days of rehearsal, Columbus felt he was lacking chemistry with Pesci and brought back Stern.<ref name="The Movies that Made Us" /> Roebuck later said that, although he was upset to be fired from the production, he now believed the experience was "a little blip of unimportance".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://mediamikes.com/2010/06/interview-with-daniel-roebuck/ |title=Interview with Daniel Roebuck |last=Gencarelli |first=Mike |date=June 9, 2010 |website=MediaMikes|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191204183429/https://mediamikes.com/2010/06/interview-with-daniel-roebuck/|archive-date=December 4, 2019|access-date=April 10, 2020}}</ref> [[Chris Farley]] auditioned for the role of the Santa Claus impersonator, but he failed to impress Columbus.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/home-alone-interview-macaulay-culkin-b1721771.html |title=An oral history of Home Alone |website=[[Independent.co.uk]] |date=November 20, 2020|access-date=August 20, 2021|archive-date=August 20, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210820210216/https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/home-alone-interview-macaulay-culkin-b1721771.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=December 23, 2024 |title=A future 'SNL' star was almost cast in 'Home Alone,' but he blew his audition |url=https://fox8.com/news/a-future-snl-star-was-almost-cast-in-home-alone-but-he-blew-his-audition/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250711220823/https://fox8.com/news/a-future-snl-star-was-almost-cast-in-home-alone-but-he-blew-his-audition/ |archive-date=July 11, 2025 |access-date=August 3, 2025 |work=Fox 8 Cleveland WJW |language=en-US}}</ref>

[[John Candy]] was available for only one day to film his scenes, which took 23 hours to shoot. He was paid only $414, since he did the film as a favor to Hughes. In return, he was the only actor Hughes allowed to go off-script; according to Columbus, all his dialogue was improvised.<ref name="The Movies that Made Us" />

===Filming===

[[Principal photography]] took place from February 14, 1990, to May 8, 1990, over a course of 83 days on an $18.3 million budget.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |title=Home Alone |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Film/58544-HOME-ALONE?cxt=filmography |access-date=April 20, 2023 |publisher=American Film Institute |archive-date=April 25, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230425030032/https://catalog.afi.com/Film/58544-HOME-ALONE?cxt=filmography |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>Weekly Variety Magazine; February 14, 1990; Page 28</ref><ref>Daily Variety Magazine; May 8, 1990; Page 18</ref> The house exterior scenes were [[location shooting|filmed on location]] at a three-story single-family house located at 671 Lincoln Avenue<ref name= "sold2012">{{cite web |title=''Home Alone'' House Sells For $1.585 Million |url=http://www.chicagonow.com/getting-real/2012/03/home-alone-house-sells-for-1-585-million/ |date=March 9, 2012 |first=Gary |last=Lucido |work=Chicago Tribune | access-date=March 11, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120310170239/http://www.chicagonow.com/getting-real/2012/03/home-alone-house-sells-for-1-585-million/|archive-date=March 10, 2012}}</ref> in the [[North Shore (Chicago)|North Shore]] village of [[Winnetka, Illinois]], where many of Hughes's previous films had also been shot,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://movie-locations.com/movies/h/Home-Alone.php |title=Home Alone filming locations |publisher=movie-locations.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190522193126/http://movie-locations.com/movies/h/Home-Alone.php|archive-date=May 22, 2019|access-date=June 13, 2008}}</ref> but at different houses. The only interiors of the house used for filming that made it to the finished film were the main staircase, attic and most of the first floor landing,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.movielocationsguide.com/Home_Alone_2:_Lost_in_New_York/filming_locations |title=Home Alone 2: Lost in New York Filming Locations|access-date=June 15, 2008 |publisher=movielocationsguide.com|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141117153723/http://www.movielocationsguide.com/Home_Alone_2:_Lost_in_New_York/filming_locations|archive-date=November 17, 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref> while all the other interiors of the house were duplicated on a [[sound stage]] to allow more room for equipment and crew. These sets were built in the then-disused gym of [[New Trier High School]]'s west campus, previously used by Hughes for ''Uncle Buck'' and ''[[Ferris Bueller's Day Off]]'', where the production company had already set up its offices.<ref name="The Movies that Made Us" /> 671 Lincoln Avenue later became a tourist attraction.<ref>{{cite news |title=The 'Home Alone' house still draws tourists to Winnetka, Illinois - The Washington Post |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/travel/2021/12/03/home-alone-house-chicago/ |url-status=live |access-date=December 17, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211203132222/https://www.washingtonpost.com/travel/2021/12/03/home-alone-house-chicago/ |archive-date=December 3, 2021}}</ref> The tree house in the backyard was built specifically for the film and dismantled after filming ended.<ref name=":2">{{cite magazine |last=Wood |first=Jennifer |date=November 16, 2015 |title=25 Things You Might Not Know About Home Alone |url=https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/60262/25-things-you-might-not-know-about-home-alone |magazine=Mental Floss|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200406160806/https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/60262/25-things-you-might-not-know-about-home-alone|url-status=dead|archive-date=April 6, 2020|access-date=April 8, 2016}}</ref>

Kevin runs away from his third encounter with Marley in Hubbard Woods Park in Winnetka.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/suburbs/winnetka/ct-wtk-home-alone-gazebo-demolition-tl-0910-20150908-story.html |title=Winnetka's 'Home Alone' gazebo faces demolition crew |publisher=The Chicago Tribune |first=Bridget |last=O'Shea |date=September 8, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200327024837/https://www.chicagotribune.com/suburbs/winnetka/ct-wtk-home-alone-gazebo-demolition-tl-0910-20150908-story.html|archive-date=March 27, 2020|access-date=December 6, 2020}}</ref> In addition, the scene where Kevin wades in his neighbor's flooded basement was shot at the empty swimming pool of the aforementioned campus of New Trier High School, with the [[American Airlines]] [[DC-10]] first class cabin interiors done on the basketball courts. John Candy filmed his cameo appearance for the film over the course of one day at [[Meigs Field]].<ref name=":4" />

{{anchor|Angels with Filthy Souls}}For the [[film within a film]], ''Angels with Filthy Souls'' (the title of which parodies the 1938 crime film ''[[Angels with Dirty Faces]]''), shooting took one day, on the final "test day" prior to the official start of principal photography. To create the illusion that the film was an authentic 1940s [[gangster film]], the scene was filmed on black-and-white negative film, and Johnny's office featured props from that era. Like much of the film, most of the sequence was shot with low, wide angles that, according to journalist Darryn King, "capture the action as if a child were perceiving it".<ref name="Vanity Fair">{{cite magazine |last1=King |first1=Darryn |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/home-alone-gangster-movie |title="Merry Christmas, Ya Filthy Animal": Inside the Making of Home Alone's Fake Gangster Movie |date=December 22, 2015 |magazine=Vanity Fair|access-date=November 20, 2016|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200530074909/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/home-alone-gangster-movie|archive-date=May 30, 2020}}</ref> Originally, [[Ralph Foody]] was to play Snakes and Michael Guido was to play Johnny, but since Foody had recently undergone knee replacement surgery, he was unable to do the death scene where Snakes drops to his knees, leading to the roles being switched.<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=December 21, 2015 |title="Merry Christmas, Ya Filthy Animal": Inside the Making of Home Alone's Fake Gangster Movie |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/home-alone-gangster-movie |access-date=June 7, 2023 |magazine=Vanity Fair |archive-date=May 30, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200530074909/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/home-alone-gangster-movie |url-status=live }}</ref>

Cinematographer [[Julio Macat]] recalled that Pesci was more difficult to work with than Culkin. The older actor believed some of the dialogue was not of a quality commensurate with his acting ability. He also resented the early unit calls, since they prevented him from starting his day with nine holes of golf as he preferred to do.<ref name="The Movies that Made Us" /> After he took the [[assistant director]] by the collar one day to complain about this, daily call times were moved back from 7 to 9 a.m. to accommodate his rounds.<ref>{{cite news |last=Reilly |first=Tim |url=https://www.golf.com/news/2019/12/02/joe-pesci-demanded-call-time-change-to-play-golf-while-filming-home-alone/ |title=Joe Pesci demanded call time change to play golf while filming Home Alone |date=December 2, 2019 |newspaper=[[Golf (magazine)|Golf]]|access-date=December 4, 2019|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191206125647/https://www.golf.com/news/2019/12/02/joe-pesci-demanded-call-time-change-to-play-golf-while-filming-home-alone/|archive-date=December 6, 2019}}</ref> However, strict child labor laws limiting Culkin to a 10 p.m. finish meant the crew had limited time to shoot night scenes.<ref name=EW2016/>

During the scene where Kate forces her son, Kevin, to sleep in the attic due to him misbehaving, O'Hara struggled to say the line "Then say it again, it'll maybe happen" as she found it horrific to say to a child.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.irishstar.com/culture/entertainment/home-alone-worst-line-movie-34374368|title=Home Alone star says 'horrific' line she had to say in movie almost 'killed' her|first1=Rudi|last1=Kinsella|first2=Zara|last2=Woodcock|first3=Alex|last3=West|date=December 25, 2024|website=Irish Star}}</ref>

Pesci said in a 2022 interview with ''[[People (magazine)|People]]'' of working with Culkin, "I intentionally limited my interactions with him to preserve the dynamic" and made sure not "to come across on the screen that we were in any way friendly" in order to "maintain the integrity of the adversarial relationship".<ref name=McArdle>{{cite magazine |url=https://people.com/movies/joe-pesci-reflects-on-making-home-alone-2-with-macaulay-culkin-as-movie-turns-30/ |title=Joe Pesci Reflects on Making 'Home Alone 2' as Movie Turns 30: 'I Did Sustain Serious Burns' |last=McArdle |first=Tommy |magazine=[[People Magazine]] |date=November 29, 2022 |access-date=December 7, 2022 |archive-date=December 6, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221206075926/https://people.com/movies/joe-pesci-reflects-on-making-home-alone-2-with-macaulay-culkin-as-movie-turns-30/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

On the set, Pesci and Stern both had difficulty refraining from cursing, which became annoying to Pesci, since Culkin was on set as well. The curse words that made it into the film were "shit", accidentally said by Daniel Stern when his shoe fell through the [[pet door]], and "hell", which was said by both Pesci and Stern after their characters encounter one another after going through Kevin's booby traps and by Johnny to the character of Snakes in ''Angels with Filthy Souls''.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Snopek |first=Amy |url=https://www.buzzfeed.com/amysno/17-things-you-didnt-know-about-home-alone-gk57 |title=17 Things You Didn't Know About "Home Alone" |date=November 15, 2013 |publisher=BuzzFeed|access-date=April 5, 2018|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200406160808/https://www.buzzfeed.com/amysno/17-things-you-didnt-know-about-home-alone-gk57|archive-date=April 6, 2020}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |url=https://www.buzzfeed.com/terripous/youre-what-the-french-like-to-call-les-incompetent |title=24 Things You Probably Didn't Know About "Home Alone" |last=Pous |first=Terri |date=December 4, 2016 |website=BuzzFeed |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190614162658/https://www.buzzfeed.com/terripous/youre-what-the-french-like-to-call-les-incompetent|archive-date=June 14, 2019|access-date=April 5, 2018}}</ref> Pesci's use of "cartoon cursing", or menacing gibberish, garnered comparisons to ''[[Looney Tunes]]'' character [[Yosemite Sam]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cbc.ca/archives/where-joe-pesci-saw-a-connection-between-looney-tunes-and-home-alone-1.4940775 |title=Where Joe Pesci saw a connection between Looney Tunes and Home Alone |publisher=cbc.ca |date=December 11, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181211185216/https://www.cbc.ca/archives/where-joe-pesci-saw-a-connection-between-looney-tunes-and-home-alone-1.4940775|archive-date=December 11, 2018}}</ref>

The film's stunts also created tension for the crew during shooting. Columbus said, "Every time the stunt guys did one of those stunts it wasn't funny. [[Senta Moses]], who played Tracy, recalled in 2020 that one of the most difficult scenes to shoot was the family's run through O'Hare International Airport to catch their flight. ==Music==
{{main|Home Alone: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack{{!}}''Home Alone: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack''}}
Columbus initially hoped to have [[Bruce Broughton]] score the film, and early posters listed him as the composer. However, Broughton was busy with ''[[The Rescuers Down Under]]'' (1990), and he had to cancel at the last minute.<ref name=EW2016/> Columbus then jokingly suggested getting [[John Williams]] for the film as a replacement but soon considered the idea and was later able to get in touch with [[Steven Spielberg]], who helped him contact Williams to produce the final score.<ref name=EW2016/> Traditional Christmas songs, such as "[[O Holy Night]]" and "[[Carol of the Bells]]", are featured prominently in the film, as well as the film's theme song "Somewhere in My Memory". ==Release==
===Theatrical===
''Home Alone'' had a sneak preview screening for about 1,000 theaters on November 10, 1990. The advanced screening proved successful, with 5% of the preview audience returning for its official release the following week. Originally scheduled to be released on Thanksgiving weekend, its opening date was brought forward to avoid competition with ''[[Three Men and a Little Lady]]''.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1990/12/10/movies/how-a-9-year-old-boy-rode-a-dark-horse-to-success.html|title=How a 9-Year-Old Boy Rode A Dark Horse to Success|work=[[The New York Times]]|page=13|first=Larry|last=Rohter| date=December 10, 1990}}</ref> It was given a wide release on November 16, 1990.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Home-Alone#tab=summary |title=Home Alone (1990) |publisher=The Numbers|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200405092005/https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Home-Alone#tab=summary|archive-date=April 5, 2020|access-date=April 11, 2020}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Home Alone'' grossed $285.8 million in the United States and Canada and $190.9 million in other countries for a worldwide total of $476.7 million, against a production budget of $18 million.<ref name=mojo /> In its opening weekend, ''Home Alone'' grossed $17 million from 1,202 theaters, averaging $14,211 per site and just 6% of the final total and added screens over the next six weeks, with a peak screen count of 2,174 during its eighth weekend at the start of January 1991.<ref name="Forbes" />

''Home Alone'' was the number-one film at the box office for 12 consecutive weeks, from its release weekend of November 16–18, 1990, through the weekend of February 1–3, 1991.<ref>{{cite news |last=Broeske |first=Pat H. |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-14-ca-310-story.html |title=Home Alone in 9th Week as No. 1 Film : Movies: 'Godfather Part III' takes dramatic slide from second to sixth place in its third week out. ===Critical response===
Upon release, ''Home Alone'' received mixed reviews from critics.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Langston |first=Keith |date=September 4, 2023 |title=Every Home Alone movie, ranked |url=https://ew.com/movies/home-alone-movies-ranked/ |access-date=April 4, 2025 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |quote=Upon its release, the movie was initially met with mixed reviews}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Bland |first=Simon |date=November 20, 2020 |title='Macaulay Culkin would try to make me laugh during takes': An oral history of Home Alone |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/features/home-alone-interview-macaulay-culkin-b1721771.html |access-date=April 4, 2025 |work=[[The Independent]]}}</ref><ref name=":5" /> On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Home Alone'' holds an approval rating of 66% based on 116 reviews, with an average rating of 5.9/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Home Alone''{{'}}s uneven but frequently funny premise stretched unreasonably thin is buoyed by Macaulay Culkin's cute performance and strong supporting stars."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/home_alone |title=''Home Alone'' (1990) |publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=December 14, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220417213130/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/home_alone|archive-date=April 17, 2022|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]] score of 63 out of 100 based on nine critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/home-alone |title=''Home Alone'' (1990) |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191205113241/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/home-alone|archive-date=December 5, 2019|access-date=May 4, 2020}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |title=Cinemascore |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= December 20, 2018 |access-date= July 24, 2019}}</ref>

''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' magazine praised the film for its cast.<ref>{{cite news |author=<!--Not stated--> |url=http://variety.com/1989/film/reviews/home-alone-1200428389/ |title=Variety Reviews – Home Alone |date=November 16, 1990 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] | access-date=August 7, 2020 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131112143006/http://variety.com/1989/film/reviews/home-alone-1200428389/|archive-date=November 12, 2013}}</ref> Jeanne Cooper of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' praised the film for its comedic approach.<ref>{{cite news |last=Cooper |first=Jeanne |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/homealonepgcooper_a09ecc.htm |title=Home Alone |date=November 16, 1990 |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|access-date=October 7, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200406160806/https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/homealonepgcooper_a09ecc.htm|archive-date=April 6, 2020}}</ref> Hal Hinson, also of ''The Washington Post'', praised Columbus's direction and Culkin's performance.<ref>{{cite news |last=Hinson |first=Hal |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/homealonepghinson_a0a9b9.htm |title=Home Alone |date=November 16, 1990 |newspaper=The Washington Post|access-date=October 7, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200225112000/https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/homealonepghinson_a0a9b9.htm|archive-date=February 25, 2020}}</ref> Although [[Caryn James]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' complained that the film's first half is "flat and unsurprising as its cute little premise suggests", she praised the second half for its slapstick humor. She also praised the dialogue between Kevin and Marley, as well as the film's final scenes.<ref>{{cite news |last=James |first=Caryn |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1990/11/16/movies/review-film-holiday-black-comedy-for-modern-children.html |title=Movie Review – Home Alone |date=November 16, 1990 |work=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=October 7, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190417184530/https://www.nytimes.com/1990/11/16/movies/review-film-holiday-black-comedy-for-modern-children.html|archive-date=April 17, 2019}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' gave the film a {{frac|2|1|2}} out of a 4-star rating. He compared the elaborate booby-traps in the film to [[Rube Goldberg machine]]s, writing "they're the kinds of traps that any 8-year-old could devise, if he had a budget of tens of thousands of dollars and the assistance of a crew of movie special effects people" and criticized the plot as "so implausible that it makes it hard for [him] to really care about the plight of the kid [Kevin]". However, he praised Culkin's performance.<ref>{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/home-alone-1990 |title=Home Alone |date=November 16, 1990 |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|access-date=October 7, 2011|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180918123627/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/home-alone-1990|archive-date=September 18, 2018|author-link=Roger Ebert}}</ref>

Owen Gleiberman of ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' magazine gave the film a "D" grade, criticizing the film for its "sadistic festival of adult-bashing". Gleiberman said that John Hughes "is pulling our strings as though he'd never learn to do anything else".<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Gleiberman |first=Owen |url=https://ew.com/article/2007/07/25/home-alone-2/ |title=Home Alone |date=July 25, 2007 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=October 7, 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190904145938/https://ew.com/article/2007/07/25/home-alone-2/|archive-date=September 4, 2019}}</ref> [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film three out of five and praised Culkin's "vivid screen presence, almost incandescent with confidence". However, he criticized his acting, calling it "a bit broad and mannered".<ref name=":3">{{Cite news |last=Bradshaw |first=Peter |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2018/nov/30/home-alone-review-yuletide-rerelease |title=Home Alone review – 1990 Christmas cracker resurfaces |date=November 30, 2018 |newspaper=The Guardian|access-date=February 21, 2020|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200407214024/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2018/nov/30/home-alone-review-yuletide-rerelease|archive-date=April 7, 2020 |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> Ali Barclay of the [[BBC]] wrote, "Culkin walks a fine line between annoyance and endearment throughout the film." He also called ''Home Alone'' "a film which manages to capture some of the best qualities of Christmas".<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2000/12/04/home_alone_1990_review.shtml |title=BBC – Films – review – Home Alone |last=Barclay |first=Ali |date=December 4, 2000 |publisher=BBC|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190919113621/http://www.bbc.co.uk/films/2000/12/04/home_alone_1990_review.shtml|archive-date=September 19, 2019|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref>

Naomi Barnwell of ''Roobla'' said that "''Home Alone'' has all the ingredients that make for a great kids' film".<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://roobla.com/movies/1419/home-alone-1990/ |title=Review: Home Alone (1990) |last=Barnwell |first=Naomi |date=December 13, 2010 |website=Roobla |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200221203921/https://roobla.com/movies/1419/home-alone-1990/|archive-date=February 21, 2020|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref> Adrian Turner of Radio Times called the movie "a celebration of enterprise that captured the heart and wickedness of every child on the planet."<ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.radiotimes.com/film/cnmkh/home-alone/ |title=Home Alone|last=Turner|first=Adrian|magazine=Radio Times|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161228121514/https://www.radiotimes.com/film/cnmkh/home-alone/|archive-date=December 28, 2016|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref> According to ''[[TV Guide]]'', "[''Home Alone'']'s slapstick falls flat and only the pain remains."<ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.tvguide.com/movies/home-alone/review/128202/ |title=Home Alone|magazine=TV Guide|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200221203931/https://www.tvguide.com/movies/home-alone/review/128202/|archive-date=February 21, 2020|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref> Peter Rainer of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' criticized the gags, writing they leave "a sour aftertaste". He added "this film plays better as a trailer than as a full-length film" when a trailer lets viewers appreciate those "without having to fight off a lot of unsettling associations".<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-11-16-ca-4515-story.html |title=MOVIE REVIEW : Kid Plays It for Laughs in 'Home Alone' |last=Rainer |first=Peter |date=November 16, 1990 |newspaper=Los Angeles Times |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200222133123/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-11-16-ca-4515-story.html|archive-date=February 22, 2020|access-date=February 22, 2020}}</ref>

===Accolades===
At the [[12th Youth in Film Awards]], Macaulay Culkin won Best Young Actor Starring in a Motion Picture.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.youngartistawards.org/pastnoms12.htm |title=Twelfth Annual Youth in Film Awards |website=YoungArtistAwards.org|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110707191726/http://www.youngartistawards.org/pastnoms12.htm|archive-date=July 7, 2011|access-date=February 24, 2020}}</ref> The film was nominated for two [[Academy Award]]s, one for [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]], which was written by [[John Williams]], and the other for [[Academy Award for Best Original Song|Best Original Song]] for "Somewhere in My Memory", music by Williams and lyrics by [[Leslie Bricusse]], but lost to ''[[Dances with Wolves]]'' and ''[[Dick Tracy (1990 film)|Dick Tracy]]'' respectively.<ref>{{cite news |last=Horn |first=John |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/democrat-and-chronicle-stars-wheel-and-d/125041543/ |title=Oscar show lets viewers see movie stars as real people |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230520232718/https://www.newspapers.com/article/democrat-and-chronicle-stars-wheel-and-d/125041543/ |date=March 26, 1991 |access-date=May 20, 2023 |archive-date=May 20, 2023 |page=24 |work=The Associated Press |publisher=[[Democrat and Chronicle]] |via=[[Newspapers.com]] |url-status=live}} {{Open access}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://awardsdatabase.oscars.org/ampas_awards/DisplayMain.jsp?curTime=1354610533731 |title=Home Alone search |work=The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences |access-date=December 4, 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130415161322/http://awardsdatabase.oscars.org/ampas_awards/DisplayMain.jsp?curTime=1354610533731 |archive-date=April 15, 2013}}</ref>

===Accusations of plagiarism===
The 1989 French horror [[thriller film]] ''[[3615 code Père Noël]]'', which is about a young boy who is home alone with his elderly grandfather and has to fend off a home invader dressed up as [[Santa Claus]], has been noted for its plot similarities to ''Home Alone''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.20minutes.fr/culture/1756103-20151224-video-pere-noel-mode-trash |title=Le père Noël en mode trash |last=Demoulin |first=Anne |date=December 24, 2015 |website=20minutes.fr |publisher=[[20 minutes (France)|20 minutes]] |language=fr|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190418032406/https://www.20minutes.fr/culture/1756103-20151224-video-pere-noel-mode-trash|archive-date=April 18, 2019|access-date=December 24, 2016}}</ref> ''3615 code Père Noël'' director René Manzor threatened the producers of ''Home Alone'' with legal action on the grounds of [[plagiarism]], alleging that ''Home Alone'' was a remake of his film.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.vanityfair.fr/culture/ecrans/articles/ces-choses-que-vous-ignorez-encore-sur-maman-jai-rate-lavion-/48735 |title=Cinéma : Tout ce que vous ignorez encore sur " Maman, j'ai raté l'avion " |trans-title=Cinema: Everything you still don't know about "Home Alone" |last=Vanwetswinkel |first=Margaux |date=December 15, 2016 |magazine=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |language=fr|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200412171138/https://www.vanityfair.fr/culture/ecrans/articles/ces-choses-que-vous-ignorez-encore-sur-maman-jai-rate-lavion-/48735|archive-date=April 12, 2020|access-date=December 24, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/03/15/movies/film-hollywood-law-whose-idea-is-it-anyway.html?pagewanted=2 |title=Hollywood Law: Whose Idea Is It, Anyway? |last=Horowitz |first=Joy |date=March 15, 1992 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200124074001/https://www.nytimes.com/1992/03/15/movies/film-hollywood-law-whose-idea-is-it-anyway.html?pagewanted=2|archive-date=January 24, 2020|access-date=December 23, 2016}}</ref> ''3615 code Père Noël'' was not released in the United States during its original theatrical run in January 1990 and did not become widely available there until 2018.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.americangenrefilm.com/theatrical-film-catalog/dial-code-santa-claus-aka-deadly-games/ |title=American Genre Film Archive DIAL CODE SANTA CLAUS |website=www.americangenrefilm.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200131194718/https://www.americangenrefilm.com/theatrical-film-catalog/dial-code-santa-claus-aka-deadly-games/|archive-date=January 31, 2020|access-date=December 14, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=December 19, 2018 |first=Buzz |last=Adams |title='Home Alone' was a Rip-off of a Much More Insane French Movie |url=https://klaq.com/home-alone-was-a-rip-off-of-a-much-more-insane-french-movie/|access-date=September 5, 2021 |publisher=95.5 KLAQ |archive-date=September 5, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210905023244/https://klaq.com/home-alone-was-a-rip-off-of-a-much-more-insane-french-movie/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Use in media==
The music video for [[Snoop Dogg]]'s 1994 song "[[Gin and Juice]]" opens with a gag where, after a teenaged Snoop's parents have left him to watch the house in their absence, he places his hands to his face and yells in the manner of Kevin McCallister in the first film, while a title comes on screen reading "Home Boy Alone".<ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.dazeddigital.com/artsandculture/article/18468/1/gin-and-juice-turns-20-gettin-loose-for-a-good-reason |title=Gin and Juice turns 20: Gettin' loose for a good reason |last=Dazed |date=January 16, 2014 |magazine=Dazed |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200202101636/https://www.dazeddigital.com/artsandculture/article/18468/1/gin-and-juice-turns-20-gettin-loose-for-a-good-reason|archive-date=February 2, 2020|access-date=February 2, 2020}}</ref>

In December 2015, Culkin reprised his role as an adult Kevin McCallister in the inaugural episode of the [[Jack Dishel]] web series ''DRYVRS'' in which a visibly disturbed McCallister recounts his experiences from the events of the first film and subsequently uses his signature tactics against a gunman.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-news/see-macaulay-culkin-revisit-traumatized-home-alone-character-55069/ |title=See Macaulay Culkin Revisit Traumatized 'Home Alone' Character |author=Ehrlich |first=David |date=December 17, 2015 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190531074926/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-news/see-macaulay-culkin-revisit-traumatized-home-alone-character-55069/|archive-date=May 31, 2019|access-date=December 18, 2015}}</ref> In response, Daniel Stern posted a short video reprising his role as Marv, released in conjunction with Stern's [[Reddit AMA]], pleading for support from Harry against McCallister's traps.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://people.com/celebrity/the-wet-bandits-are-back-daniel-stern-releases-a-video-response-to-macaulay-culkins-home-alone-parody/ |title=The Wet Bandits Are Back! Daniel Stern Releases a Video Response to Macaulay Culkin's Home Alone Parody |author=Pearl |first=Diana |date=December 26, 2015 |work=People Magazine|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190827164938/https://people.com/celebrity/the-wet-bandits-are-back-daniel-stern-releases-a-video-response-to-macaulay-culkins-home-alone-parody/|archive-date=August 27, 2019|access-date=December 27, 2015}}</ref>

The 2016 Christmas-set horror film ''[[Better Watch Out]]'' includes a scene where a character who is obsessed with the ''Home Alone'' films demonstrates how, in real life, it would be deadly for someone to be hit in the face with a paint can swung from a distance.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3597558/revisiting-better-watch-memorable-home-alone-inspired-kill/ |title=Revisiting 'Better Watch Out' and Its Memorable 'Home Alone' Inspired Kill! |last=Navarro |first=Meagan |date=December 18, 2019 |website=Bloody Disgusting! |url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200407230205/https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3597558/revisiting-better-watch-memorable-home-alone-inspired-kill/|archive-date=April 7, 2020|access-date=February 21, 2020}}</ref>

The [[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia season 13|season 13]] episode of ''[[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia]]'' "Charlie's Home Alone" is intended to be a direct parody of the first ''Home Alone'' film. In the episode, [[Charlie Kelly (character)|Charlie Kelly]] is accidentally forgotten while the rest of "the gang" attend [[Super Bowl LII]]. Charlie mistakenly believes he must protect the bar by setting up traps, only for himself to accidentally activate said traps, nearly preventing from performing his Super Bowl "rituals".<ref>{{Cite web |date=February 6, 2023 |title=What 'It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia' gets right about Eagles fans |url=https://www.phillyvoice.com/always-sunny-in-philadelphia-super-bowl-episode-eagles-fans-2018/ |access-date=March 3, 2023 |website=PhillyVoice |archive-date=March 3, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230303070353/https://www.phillyvoice.com/always-sunny-in-philadelphia-super-bowl-episode-eagles-fans-2018/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

On December 15, 2018, Culkin made a guest appearance as himself in an episode of ''[[Angry Video Game Nerd]]'' to review multiple video game adaptations of the first two ''Home Alone'' films, as well as a gameplay session of [[The Pagemaster (video game)|''The Pagemaster'']] with [[James Rolfe]] and Mike Matei in the days following that episode's release.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.polygon.com/2018/12/16/18143355/angry-video-game-nerd-macaulay-culkin-home-alone-video-games |title=Macaulay Culkin teams with Angry Video Game Nerd to dump on Home Alone |last1=Good |first1=Owen S. |date=December 16, 2018 |website=Polygon|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190804153639/https://www.polygon.com/2018/12/16/18143355/angry-video-game-nerd-macaulay-culkin-home-alone-video-games|archive-date=August 4, 2019|access-date=December 21, 2018}}</ref>

On December 19, 2018, Culkin reprised his role as McCallister in a 60-second advertisement for [[Google Assistant]] titled ''Home Alone Again,'' which parodies the original film. The commercial contains [[Shot-for-shot|shot-for-shot remakes]] of several scenes from the film in which McCallister uses several of the product's functions. The concluding scene involves a command sequence intended to make the house look active, parodying the original "[[Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree]]" scene.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Parker |first1=Ryan |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/macaulay-culkin-creates-home-alone-scenes-google-assistant-ad-1170770 |title=Macaulay Culkin Re-Creates 'Home Alone' Scenes for Google Assistant Ad |date=December 19, 2018 |work=The Hollywood Reporter|access-date=December 21, 2018|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190327091419/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/macaulay-culkin-creates-home-alone-scenes-google-assistant-ad-1170770|archive-date=March 27, 2019}}</ref>

''Home Alone'' is prominently referred to in the 2022 Christmas action comedy film ''[[Violent Night]]'', in which the character Trudy Lightston, a fan of the film, imitates Kevin's booby traps against burglars who take her family hostage.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Gleiberman|first=Owen|date=November 29, 2022|access-date=December 1, 2022|title='Violent Night' Review: David Harbour Stars in Your Basic, Everyday Heist Meets Bloody Action Santa Meets 'Home Alone' Christmas Thriller|url=https://variety.com/2022/film/reviews/violent-night-review-david-harbour-1235444598/|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|archive-date=February 4, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230204065714/https://variety.com/2022/film/reviews/violent-night-review-david-harbour-1235444598/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Sequels and franchise===
{{main|Home Alone (franchise){{!}}''Home Alone'' (franchise)}}

The film was followed by a commercially successful sequel in 1992, ''[[Home Alone 2: Lost in New York]]'', which brought back most of the first film's cast. Culkin was paid $4.5 million to appear in the sequel, compared to $110,000 for the original.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/59269 |title=AFI{{!}}Catalog – Home Alone 2: Lost in New York|website=catalog.afi.com|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200211152658/https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/59269|archive-date=February 11, 2020|access-date=April 13, 2020}}</ref> The film within a film, ''Angels with Filthy Souls'', had a sequel in ''Home Alone 2'', ''Angels with Even Filthier Souls''. Both ''Angels'' meta-films featured character actor [[Ralph Foody]] as stereotypical 1930s mobster Johnny.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://thefw.com/things-you-didnt-know-about-home-alone/ |title=10 Things You Didn't Know About 'Home Alone' |publisher=The FW|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200205025718/https://thefw.com/things-you-didnt-know-about-home-alone/|archive-date=February 5, 2020|access-date=April 9, 2016}}</ref> A third film, ''[[Home Alone 3]]'', was released in 1997; it has entirely different actors and characters as well as a different storyline, with Hughes writing the screenplay.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/home-alone-3-1997 |title=Home Alone 3 movie review & film summary (1997)|last=Ebert|first=Roger|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200404100501/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/home-alone-3-1997|archive-date=April 4, 2020|access-date=February 2, 2020}}</ref>
