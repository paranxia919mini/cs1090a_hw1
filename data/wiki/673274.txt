'''''The Devil's Advocate''''' (marketed as '''''Devil's Advocate''''') is a 1997 American [[supernatural horror film]] directed by [[Taylor Hackford]], written by [[Jonathan Lemkin]] and [[Tony Gilroy]], and starring [[Keanu Reeves]], [[Al Pacino]] and [[Charlize Theron]]. Based on [[Andrew Neiderman]]'s 1990 novel, it is about a gifted young [[Florida]] lawyer invited to work for a major [[New York City]] law firm. As his wife becomes haunted by frightening visions, the lawyer slowly realizes the firm's owner, John Milton, is the [[Devil in Christianity|Devil]].

The name John Milton is one of several allusions to ''[[Paradise Lost]]'', as well as to [[Dante Alighieri]]'s ''[[Inferno (Dante)|Inferno]]'' and the legend of [[Faust]]. An adaptation of Neiderman's novel went into a [[development hell]] during the 1990s, with Hackford gaining control of the production. Filming took place around New York City and Florida.

''The Devil's Advocate'' received mixed reviews, with critics crediting it for entertainment value and Pacino's performance. It grossed $153 million at the box office and won the [[Saturn Award for Best Horror Film]]. It also became the subject of the copyright lawsuit ''[[#Lawsuit|Hart v. Warner Bros., Inc.]]'' for its visual art.

==Plot==
Kevin Lomax is a [[Criminal defense lawyer|defense attorney]] based in [[Gainesville, Florida]] who has never lost a case. While defending schoolteacher Lloyd Gettys against a charge of [[child molestation]], he realizes his client is guilty. He proceeds with his defense, destroying the victim's credibility through a harsh [[cross-examination]] and securing an acquittal for Gettys.

A New York City law firm asks Kevin to assist them with a [[jury selection]]. The head of the firm, John Milton, offers Kevin a job, and he and his wife Mary Ann move to Manhattan. Kevin is soon working long hours and neglecting Mary Ann.

Kevin is assigned to represent billionaire Alex Cullen, who is accused of murdering his wife, his stepson, and a maid. As a result, he sees even less of Mary Ann and fantasizes about his co-worker Christabella. Meanwhile, Mary Ann has demonic visions and has a nightmare about a baby playing with her removed [[ovaries]]. A doctor declares her to be [[Infertility|infertile]]. Milton suggests Kevin step down from the trial to care for Mary Ann's deteriorating mental health, but Kevin thinks he will resent her for costing him the case.

Eddie Barzoon, the firm's [[managing partner]], discovers Kevin's name on the firm's charter and believes that Kevin covets his job. Eddie threatens to inform the [[United States Attorney]]'s office of the firm's dubious activities, and Kevin tells Milton. Although Milton seems to dismiss Kevin's concerns, Barzoon is beaten to death in [[Central Park]].

Preparing Melissa Black, Alex Cullen's secretary, to testify, Kevin realizes she is lying to give Cullen a false [[alibi]]. He tells Milton he thinks Cullen is guilty, but Milton offers to support him anyway. Kevin proceeds, winning an acquittal with Black's [[perjury|perjured]] testimony. Mary Ann claims Milton raped her that day and shows Kevin her body covered with cuts. Knowing that Milton was in court with him, Kevin believes her to be mentally ill and harming herself.  He admits her into a psychiatric institution.

[[United States Department of Justice]] agent Mitch Weaver, who is investigating the clandestine activities of Milton's firm, warns Kevin that Milton is corrupt and reveals that Gettys, acquitted in Florida thanks to Kevin, has been arrested for killing a young girl. Moments later, Weaver is struck by a car and killed.

Kevin, his [[Christian fundamentalism|Christian fundamentalist]] mother Alice, and his case manager Pam, visit Mary Ann at the institution. Envisioning Pam as a demon, Mary Ann kills herself. Alice tearfully reveals that Milton is Kevin's father. Kevin confronts Milton, who admits to raping Mary Ann. He shoots Milton, but the bullets do not harm him. Milton reveals that he is [[Satan]].

Kevin blames Milton for everything, but Milton points out he merely "set the stage". It was Kevin who chose to neglect his wife and defend people he knew were guilty. Kevin realizes he always wanted to win, regardless of the cost. Milton reveals that Christabella is Kevin's half-sister and he wants the two of them to conceive the [[Antichrist]]. Kevin appears to acquiesce but abruptly shoots himself in the head. Milton's demonic rage burns Christabella alive, and he turns into a winged angel that resembles Kevin.

Kevin finds himself back at the Gainesville courthouse during the recess of the Gettys trial. He returns to the courtroom and sees that Mary Ann is alive and well. When the trial resumes, he announces that he cannot represent his client, despite the risk of [[disbarment]]. His reporter friend Larry offers to do a high-profile interview with him, promising to make him famous. Encouraged by Mary Ann, Kevin agrees.

After they leave, Larry transforms into Milton, who [[fourth wall|breaks the fourth wall]] and declares, "Vanity — definitely my favorite sin."

The Devil character's name is a direct homage to [[John Milton]], who wrote ''[[Paradise Lost]]'',{{sfn|Fry|2008|pp=116–117}} quoted by Lomax with the line, "''Better to reign in Hell, than serve in Heav'n''".{{sfn|Brown|2006|p=93}} Despite this, the thrust of Milton's epic was to rebuke the devil.{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=114}} As a rebel against God, complaining of being perpetually "underestimated", the Milton character, like ''Paradise Lost''{{'}}s Satan, is "''Heav'n running from Heav'n''" with a "''sense of injur'd merit''".{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|p=12}}

Professor Eric C. Brown judges the climax, in which Milton attempts to persuade Lomax to have sex with his half-sister to conceive the [[Antichrist]], to be the most "Miltonic", as the sculptures become animated in carnal activities evoking ''Paradise Lost''{{'}}s "Downfall of the Rebel Angels".{{sfn|Brown|2006|p=93}} The tirade Milton gives in this sequence is also reminiscent of Satan's lines in ''Paradise Lost'', Books I and II.{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=116}} In U.S. literary education, Milton's temptation of Lomax in the climax, in which he rationalizes rebellion against God for a "look-but-don't-touch" model, has been compared to Satan urging [[Eve]] to eat [[forbidden fruit]] in ''Paradise Lost'', Book IX, lines 720–730:{{sfn|Webb|2007|p=126}}

{{Blockquote|If they all things, who enclos'd Knowledge of good and evil in this tree, That whoso eats thereof forthwith attains Wisdom without their leave? and wherein lies Th' offence, that Man should thus attain to know?}}

In his [[DVD]] commentary, Taylor Hackford did not name ''Paradise Lost'' as an inspiration, instead citing the legend of [[Faust]].{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=115}} An underlying concept of the story is a "[[Faustian bargain]]", offered to a character with [[free will]].{{sfn|Brown|2006|pp=91–92}} Philosopher [[Peter van Inwagen]] writes that Milton referring to free will as a "bitch" when Lomax contemplates selling his soul moves away from a legalistic definition of "free will" as "uncoerced" into the philosophical realm of its definition.{{sfn|van Inwagen|2017|p=151}}

As with ''[[Goethe's Faust]]'', the Devil that is commonly depicted in cinema is associated with lust and temptation.{{sfn|Wyman|2009|p=303}} Milton shows Lomax many seductive women to induce his "fall". Sex or rape is usually also the means by which Satan creates the Antichrist, as in [[Roman Polanski]]'s 1968 film ''[[Rosemary's Baby (film)|Rosemary's Baby]]''. In ''The Devil's Advocate'', someone other than Satan will have sex to conceive the Antichrist, although Milton nevertheless brutally rapes Mary Ann.{{sfn|Wyman|2009|p=303}} [[Incest in film and television|Incest]] becomes a way of creating the Antichrist, for the offspring of Satan's son and daughter will inherit much of Satan's genetic makeup.{{sfn|Fry|2008|pp=116–117}}

[[Dante Alighieri]]'s ''[[Inferno (Dante)|Inferno]]'' raised "visual potential" that informed the film.{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|p=x}} Dantean scholar Amilcare A. Iannucci argues the plot follows the model of the ''[[Divine Comedy]]'' in beginning with ''selva oscura'', with Lomax losing his conscience defending a guilty man, and entering and exploring deeper circles of [[Hell]].{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|p=13}}

Iannucci compares the office building structure to the circles, listing fireplaces where flames are always present; demonic visual phenomena; and water outside of Milton's office, analogized to [[Dante's Satan]]'s icy home, albeit situated at the top of Hell, as opposed to the bottom.{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|pp=13–14}} Free will is also a major theme in the ''Divine Comedy'', with the film's musings on the concept being similar to Dante's ''[[Purgatorio]]'', 16.82–83 ("if the present world has gone astray, in you is the cause, in you it's to be sought").{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|pp=14–15}}

Other religious references are present. In describing New York City as [[Babylon]], Alice Lomax invokes [[Revelation 18]]:{{sfn|Brown|2006|p=92}}

{{Blockquote|Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and has become a dwelling place of demons, a prison for every foul spirit, and a cage for every unclean and hated bird!}}

Milton tempting Lomax is also possibly inspired by the Biblical [[Temptation of Christ]].{{sfn|Fry|2008|pp=116–117}} Aside from Milton, other character names have been commented on: Author Kelly J. Wyman matches Mary Ann, the virginal figure who falls victim to Milton, to the [[Mary, mother of Jesus|Virgin Mary]], and adds the literal translation of Christabella is "Beautiful Christ",{{sfn|Wyman|2009|p=307}} and that the title refers to the Catholic Church's [[Devil's advocate]]s and lawyers as advocates;{{sfn|Wyman|2009|pp=307–308}} Eric C. Brown finds Barzoon's name and character to be reminiscent of the demon prince [[Beelzebub]].{{sfn|Brown|2006|p=93}} Scholars [[Miguel A. De La Torre]] and Albert Hernández observe the vision of Satan as CEO, wearing expensive clothing and engaging in business, had appeared in popular culture before, including the 1942 novel ''[[The Screwtape Letters]]''.{{sfn|De La Torre|Hernández|2011|p=14}}

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Milton diktiert seinen Töchtern das »Paradise Lost« (Eugène Ferdinand Victor Delacroix).jpg|200px|thumb|[[Eugène Delacroix]]'s depiction of Milton dictating ''Paradise Lost''; Milton's line "Better to reign in Hell, than serve in Heav'n" is used in the screenplay.]]
[[Andrew Neiderman]] wrote ''The Devil's Advocate'' as a novel, and it was published in 1990 by [[Simon and Schuster]].{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=123}} Believing that his story could be adapted into a film, Neiderman approached [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] and claimed to have led his successful sale with the synopsis, "It's about a law firm in New York that represents only guilty people, and never loses".<ref name="Desert">{{cite web |url=http://www.desertsun.com/story/life/entertainment/books/2014/05/23/andrew-neiderman-vc-andrews-devils-advocate-author/9507065/ |title=Author Andrew Neiderman enjoys literary, TV success |author=TDS |work=[[The Desert Sun]] |date=May 23, 2014 |access-date=August 19, 2017 |archive-date=July 4, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210704015316/https://www.desertsun.com/story/life/entertainment/books/2014/05/23/andrew-neiderman-vc-andrews-devils-advocate-author/9507065/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Various screenplay adaptations of ''The Devil's Advocate'' were pitched to U.S. cinema studios, with [[Joel Schumacher]] planned to direct it, with [[Brad Pitt]] as the young lawyer.<ref name="Mathews">{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-oct-15-ca-42772-story.html |title=Jumping into the Fire: In 'Advocate,' Al Pacino takes a walk on the dark side. Luckily, he's no stranger to these mean streets |last=Mathews |first=Jack |work=[[The Los Angeles Times]] |date=October 15, 1997 |access-date=August 17, 2017 |archive-date=September 18, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150918190935/http://articles.latimes.com/1997/oct/15/entertainment/ca-42772 |url-status=live }}</ref> Schumacher planned a sequence in which Pitt would descend into the [[New York City Subway|New York subway system]] that would be modeled on the [[circles of hell]] in Dante's ''Divine Comedy''.<ref name="Brennan"/> With no actor to play Satan, this project collapsed.<ref name="Mathews"/>

The [[O.J. Simpson murder trial]] and its controversial outcome gave new impetus to relaunching the project, with a $60&nbsp;million budget.<ref name="Mathews"/> Warner Bros. hired Taylor Hackford to direct the new attempt.<ref name="Brennan">{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-11-27-ca-3267-story.html |title=On Pacino Film, They're Having Devil of a Time |last=Brennan |first=Judy |work=[[The Los Angeles Times]] |date=November 27, 1996 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=May 9, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140509204721/http://articles.latimes.com/1996-11-27/entertainment/ca-3267_1_pacino-film |url-status=live }}</ref> The director embraced the legal drama aspect, theorizing, "The courtroom has become the gladiator arena of the late twentieth century. Following the progress of a sensational trial is a spectator sport."{{sfn|Levi|2005|p=128}}

[[Tony Gilroy]] led much of the rewrite with supervision by Hackford, who envisioned it as "a modern-day [[morality play]]" and "[[Faust]]ian tale".<ref name="Hamill">{{cite web |url=http://www.nydailynews.com/eye-evil-devil-advocate-taylor-hackford-takes-satan-court-article-1.755745 |title=Eye On Evil In 'Devil's Advocate,' Taylor Hackford Takes Satan To Court |last=Hamill |first=Denis |work=[[New York Daily News]] |date=February 16, 1997 |access-date=August 24, 2017 |archive-date=August 24, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170824235852/http://www.nydailynews.com/eye-evil-devil-advocate-taylor-hackford-takes-satan-court-article-1.755745 |url-status=live }}</ref> As the screenplay developed, free-will became a theme in which Milton does not actually cause events. Hackford wanted suggestions that Milton does not kill Barzoon as he defied his muggers, or United States Attorney Weaver, who arrogantly did not watch for vehicles before stepping onto the road.<ref name="Hackford"/>

The screenwriters added the plot element that Lomax was Milton's son, and that Milton could produce the Antichrist, neither of which are in the novel.{{sfn|Schoell|2016|p=113}} Hackford cited the films ''Rosemary's Baby'' and ''[[The Omen]]'' as influences, and both had explored the Antichrist mythology.{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=115}}{{sfn|Schoell|2016|p=113}} Another change from the novel was converting the book's [[lesbian]] client to the [[Pedophilia|pedophile]] Lloyd Gettys, avoiding undertones of [[homophobia]].{{sfn|Schoell|2016|p=112|ps=: "In the novel the pedophile client was a woman, a lesbian, and it was strongly implied that she was guilty, although many of the characters seemed to think it was bad enough that she was a lesbian."}} In an early version of the screenplay, the "better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven" quotation is given to Milton rather than Lomax.{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=114}}

===Casting===
Al Pacino was previously offered the role of the Devil in an attempt to adapt Neiderman's novel, but before final rewrites, he rejected it on three occasions,<ref name="Hamill"/> because of the clichéd nature of the character.<ref name="Mathews"/> Pacino suggested [[Robert Redford]] and [[Sean Connery]] for the role.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://screencrush.com/the-devils-advocate-then-and-now/|title=See the Cast of 'The Devil's Advocate' then and Now|date=February 22, 2014 |website=Screen Crush|last=Hayes|first=Britt}}</ref> [[Keanu Reeves]] chose to star in ''The Devil's Advocate'' instead of ''[[Speed 2: Cruise Control|Speed 2]]'', despite a promised $11&nbsp;million for the sequel to his 1994 hit ''[[Speed (1994 film)|Speed]].''<ref name="Hamill"/> According to Reeves's staff, the actor was averse to performing in two consecutive [[action film]]s after ''[[Chain Reaction (1996 film)|Chain Reaction]]'' (1996).<ref>{{cite magazine|title='Speed' bump |last=Nashawaty |first=Chris |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |issue=331 |page=7 |date=June 14, 1996 }}</ref> On ''The Devil's Advocate'', Reeves agreed to a pay cut worth millions of dollars so that the producers could meet Pacino's salary demands.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/story?id=102572&page=1 |title=Keanu Gives Up 'Matrix' Money |work=[[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]] |date=September 10, 1999 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=July 1, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120701224329/http://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/story?id=102572&page=1 |url-status=live }}</ref> To prepare for the role, Pacino watched the 1941 film ''[[The Devil and Daniel Webster (film)|The Devil and Daniel Webster]]'' and observed tips from [[Walter Huston]] as [[Mr. Scratch]]. He also read Dante's ''Inferno'' and ''Paradise Lost''.<ref name="Mathews"/>

[[Connie Nielsen]], a Danish actress, was selected by Hackford for Christabella, who speaks several languages, with Hackford asserting that Nielsen already spoke multiple languages.<ref name="Hackford"/> [[Craig T. Nelson]] was cast against type in a villainous role.<ref name="Hackford"/>

[[Principal photography]] began in New York in 1996, but struggled by November. Delays were caused by the dismissals of the original cinematographer and assistant directors, while an anonymous source claimed Pacino found Hackford to be conceited and loud. An executive alleged Pacino was typically late to work, although producer [[Arnold Kopelson]] said this was not the case.<ref name="Brennan"/> Hackford said Pacino was professional, although his status meant that he did not need to be.{{sfn|Grobel|2008|p=xxxii}}

[[Production designer]] [[Bruno Rubeo]] was tasked to create Milton's apartment, aiming for a "very loose and very sexy" appearance, "so you can't really tell where it goes".<ref name="OnLocation">{{cite AV media |last=Rubeo |first=Bruno |date=1998 |chapter=On Location |title=The Devil's Advocate |medium=DVD |publisher=Warner Home Video }}</ref> Hackford said on this set that he encouraged Reeves and Pacino to "feel the room" and develop some [[improvisation]].<ref name="Guerrasio">{{cite web |url=http://www.businessinsider.com/taylor-hackford-directing-robert-de-niro-and-al-pacino-2017-2/#pacino-gave-him-the-greatest-improv-performance-hes-ever-seen-2 |title=This Oscar-winning director reveals the secrets of working with De Niro and Pacino |last=Guerrasio |first=Jason |work=[[Business Insider]] |date=February 1, 2017 |access-date=August 24, 2017 |archive-date=August 25, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170825061531/http://www.businessinsider.com/taylor-hackford-directing-robert-de-niro-and-al-pacino-2017-2/#pacino-gave-him-the-greatest-improv-performance-hes-ever-seen-2 |url-status=live }}</ref> Pacino came up with the idea of dancing to "[[It Happened in Monterey]]" by [[Frank Sinatra]], and Hackford immediately adopted the idea.{{sfn|Grobel|2008|p=xxxii}}

When Lomax leaves to meet Milton, he walks on [[57th Street (Manhattan)|57th Street]] in New York, abnormally devoid of people or vehicles. It was shot on the actual 57th Street, with the filmmakers having it emptied at 7:30&nbsp;a.m. on a Sunday.<ref name="Hackford"/> The offices were shot at the Continental Club in Manhattan, and at [[Continental Center (New York City)|180 Maiden Lane]], although the water outside of Milton's office was added later by computer effects.<ref name="OnLocation"/> In constructing the firm sets, Hackford and Rubeo consulted architects from Japan and Italy to craft an "ultra-modern" look, to display Milton's taste.<ref name="Hackford"/> [[Donald Trump]]'s penthouse in [[Trump Tower]], [[Fifth Avenue]], was lent to the production for Alexander Cullen's residence.<ref name="OnLocation"/>{{sfn|Ocker|2012|p=200}}

A number of churches and courts hosted production. The interior of New York City's [[Church of the Heavenly Rest]] was used for the scene in which Theron's character says Milton raped her.<ref name="Hamill"/>{{sfn|Ocker|2012|p=200}} The outside of [[Central Presbyterian Church (New York City)|Central Presbyterian Church]] was photographed for Barzoon's funeral, while Pacino was inside the [[Church of the Most Holy Redeemer (Manhattan)|Manhattan Church of the Most Holy Redeemer]] for the holy water sequence.{{sfn|Ocker|2012|p=200}} For court scenes, [[New Jersey]]'s [[Bergen County Court House]] was employed for production,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.bcsd.us/court-house/history |title=History of the Bergen County Courthouse |publisher=Bergen County Sheriff's Office |date=2017 |access-date=August 25, 2017 |archive-date=August 25, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170825180826/https://www.bcsd.us/court-house/history |url-status=live }}</ref> as were historic courthouses in New York.{{sfn|Ocker|2012|p=199}}

After the completion of the New York shoot in March 1997, production moved to Florida in July 1997.<ref name="TimesUnion">{{cite web |url=http://jacksonville.com/tu-online/stories/070697/1b3keanu.html#.WZ9udiiGPIU |title='Devil's Advocate' brings Keanu Reeves to Florida |work=[[The Florida Times-Union]] |location=[[Jacksonville, Florida]] |date=July 6, 1997 |access-date=August 24, 2017 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170825062930/http://jacksonville.com/tu-online/stories/070697/1b3keanu.html#.WZ9udiiGPIU |archive-date=August 25, 2017 |df=mdy-all }}</ref> In [[Jacksonville, Florida|Jacksonville]], Florida, the interior of Mrs. Howard's, an interior design business located in [[Riverside and Avondale]], was used for New York scenes. Its co-owner, Jim Howard, remodeled the store and appeared as an [[extra (acting)|extra]].<ref name="TimesUnion"/> The Gainesville church scenes were shot at a Gainesville church after Hackford persuaded the pastor and his members to participate, telling them that his story was about combating Satan.<ref name="Hackford">{{cite AV media |last=Hackford |first=Taylor |date=1998 |title=The Devil's Advocate audio commentary |medium=DVD |publisher=Warner Home Video }}</ref>

Theron had to briefly leave the United States while filming because she was working illegally.<ref name="contactmusic/1116156">{{cite news |title=Theron Thrown Out Of America While Filming Devil's Advocate |url=https://www.contactmusic.com/charlize-theron/news/theron-thrown-out-of-america-while-filming-devils-advocate_1116156 |access-date=12 October 2023 |work=Contactmusic.com |agency=WENN |date=16 September 2009}}</ref>

===Post-production===
At the end of the film, John Milton morphs into [[Lucifer]] as a [[fallen angel]]. The crew created the effect by combining [[life mask]]s depicting Reeves, Pacino in 1997 and Pacino as he appeared in the 1972 film ''[[The Godfather]]''.<ref>{{cite AV media |last=Hackford |first=Taylor |date=1998 |chapter=Special Effects |title=The Devil's Advocate |medium=DVD |publisher=Warner Home Video }}</ref> ''The Godfather'' makeup artist [[Dick Smith (make-up artist)|Dick Smith]] supplied to ''The Devil's Advocate'' artist [[Rick Baker]], Smith's former protégé, the life mask he made in the 1970s.{{sfn|Robb|2003|p=144}} Additionally, Baker created images for demonic faces seen on real actresses and actors, with hands also appearing to move underneath [[Tamara Tunie]]'s skin, a digital creation with the contributions of [[Richard Greenberg]] and Stephanie Powell.<ref name="Hackford"/>

Shots of [[Ballet dancer|ballerinas]] moving in water were used as a basis for Milton's animated sculpture.<ref name="Hackford"/> Special-effects producer Edward L. Williams said that he filmed the people for the statue effect, and that they were naked and placed in a tank next to a [[Chroma key|blue screen]].<ref name="Wilkinson">{{cite magazine|title=His effects are truly special |last=Wilkinson |first=Deborah |magazine=[[Black Enterprise]] |date=March 2000 |page=66}}</ref> It took three months to film the people and add the computer effects, at a cost of $2&nbsp;million, which was 40% of the overall budget for special effects.<ref name="Wilkinson"/>

[[James Newton Howard]], a past collaborator with Hackford, was tasked to write the [[film score|score]].{{sfn|Heine|2016|p=7}} Hackford dubbed Pacino's performance of "It Happened in Monterey" with Sinatra's voice.<ref name="Guerrasio"/> "[[Paint It Black]]" by [[The Rolling Stones]] is also used for the film's conclusion.{{sfn|Kubernik|2006|p=46}}

==Release==
During early stages of photography, Warner Bros. aspired to a release in August 1997.<ref name="Brennan"/> The film eventually had its release on October 17, 1997,<ref name="Mathews"/> on the same day as another horror film, ''[[I Know What You Did Last Summer]]''.<ref name="Mendelson">{{cite web |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/scottmendelson/2015/10/13/halloween-saw-ii-and-the-10-biggest-horror-hits-of-october/#4297b3de477e |title='Halloween,' 'Saw II' And The 10 Biggest Horror Hits of October |last=Mendelson |first=Scott |work=[[Forbes]] |date=October 13, 2015 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=August 19, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170819105824/https://www.forbes.com/sites/scottmendelson/2015/10/13/halloween-saw-ii-and-the-10-biggest-horror-hits-of-october/#4297b3de477e |url-status=live }}</ref> To promote the release, Warner Bros.'s website included the warning on Hell's Gate from Dante's ''Inferno'' Canto III ("''Abandon every hope, ye who enter here''"), with credits presented as circles of Hell.{{sfn|Iannucci|2004|p=12}} The television advertising and [[film poster|poster]] were upfront regarding Milton being Satan, although it is not explicitly revealed in the film until its later acts.<ref name="Ebert"/>

Approximately 475,000 copies of the [[VHS]] and [[DVD]] were produced by February 1998, but their release into the home-video market was originaly delayed pending the ''[[#Hart v. Warner Bros., Inc.|Hart v. Warner Bros., Inc.]]'' lawsuit,<ref name="Stern"/> however a deal was reached and it was released on its scheduled release date on February 17, 1998.<ref>{{cite news |title=Advocates reach accord to release 'Devil's' video|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-star-ledger-the-devils-advocate-vid/202421303/|newspaper=The Star-Ledger|page=19| date=February 14, 1998}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Quick hits|url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/lansing-state-journal-quick-hits/202420720/|newspaper=Lansing State Journal|page=29| date=February 17, 1998}}</ref> It proved successful in the home-video market, becoming the 5th most-rented film in the United States for 1998.<ref>{{cite web |date = January 9, 1999 |title = Top Video Rentals |url = https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/90s/1999/BB-1999-01-09.pdf |page = 54 |magazine = [[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]] }}</ref> Afterward, the film went into regular airings on [[TNT (American TV network)|TNT]] and [[TBS (American TV channel)|TBS]].{{sfn|Netzley|2006|p=113}} In 2012, a [[Blu-ray]] edition was released in [[DVD region code|Region A]] as an "Unrated Director's Cut", in which the climax's art that was previously subject to the lawsuit is digitally redone.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/56289/devils-advocate-the-unrated-directors-cut-the/ |title=The Devil's Advocate (Blu-ray) |last=Spurlin |first=Thomas |work=[[DVD Talk]] |date=September 22, 2012 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=August 19, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170819105230/http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/56289/devils-advocate-the-unrated-directors-cut-the/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
On its opening weekend in October 1997, ''The Devil's Advocate'' earned $12.2&nbsp;million, finishing second in the U.S. box office to ''[[I Know What You Did Last Summer]]'', which made $16.1&nbsp;million.<ref name="IKnow">{{cite web |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1997-oct-20-ca-44693-story.html |title='I Know What You Did' Scares Up Big Box Office |last=Associated Press |author-link=Associated Press |work=[[The Los Angeles Times]] |date=October 20, 1997 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=May 27, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160527162805/http://articles.latimes.com/1997/oct/20/entertainment/ca-44693 |url-status=live }}</ref> ''Devil's Advocate'' was largely competing against thriller films aimed at youth in the [[Halloween]] season.<ref name="IKnow"/> By December 6, 1997, it grossed $56.1&nbsp;million.<ref name="Masters">{{cite news |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/local/1997/12/06/sculptor-cathedral-sue-over-movies-art/bb3e41ea-4c37-412c-9105-87ca83a6f083/ |title=Sculptor, Cathedral Sue Over Movie's Art |last=Masters |first=Brooke A. |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |date=December 6, 1997 |access-date=August 19, 2017 |archive-date=August 20, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170820045108/https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/local/1997/12/06/sculptor-cathedral-sue-over-movies-art/bb3e41ea-4c37-412c-9105-87ca83a6f083/ |url-status=live }}</ref> It ended its run on February 12, 1998, with a gross of $61&nbsp;million in North America and $92&nbsp;million elsewhere.<ref name="mojo">{{cite web |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=devilsadvocate.htm |title=The Devil's Advocate (1997) |work=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=August 10, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170810055252/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=devilsadvocate.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Legacy==
===Lawsuit<span class="anchor" id="Hart v. Warner Bros., Inc."></span>===
The film was the subject of legal action in ''Hart v. Warner Bros., Inc.'' in 1997. The claim was that the sculpture featuring human forms in John Milton's apartment closely resembled the ''[[Frederick Hart (sculptor)#Washington National Cathedral|Ex nihilo]]'' sculpture by [[Frederick Hart (sculptor)|Frederick Hart]] on the facade of the [[Washington National Cathedral|Episcopal National Cathedral]] in Washington, D.C., and that a scene involving the sculpture infringed Hart's rights under [[copyright law in the United States]].<ref>{{cite web |url= https://www.nytimes.com/1997/12/05/us/sculpture-in-a-movie-leads-to-suit.html?mcubz=3 |title=Sculpture in a Movie Leads to Suit |last=Niebuhr |first=Gustav |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=December 5, 1997 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date= August 19, 2017 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20170819011134/http://www.nytimes.com/1997/12/05/us/sculpture-in-a-movie-leads-to-suit.html?mcubz=3 |url-status=live }}</ref> Hart and the National Cathedral jointly initiated the action, with an argument similar to architect [[Lebbeus Woods]]'s successful lawsuit over imagery in the film ''[[12 Monkeys]]''.<ref name="Masters"/> Defenses available to Warner Bros. were that the effect was designed without knowledge of ''Ex nihilo'', or [[fair use]].<ref name= "Masters" />

After a federal judge ruled the film's video release would be delayed until the case went to trial unless a settlement was reached, Warner Bros. agreed to edit the scene for future releases and to attach stickers to unedited videotapes to indicate there was no relation between the art in the film and Hart's work.<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.rcfp.org/newsitems/index.php?i=1757 |title=Film studio settles claim over copyrighted sculpture |publisher=[[Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press]] |date=February 23, 1998 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=July 27, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110727202936/http://www.rcfp.org/newsitems/index.php?i=1757 |url-status= live }}</ref> The settlement in February 1998 meant 475,000 copies of the VHS and DVD could go into rental stores and businesses.<ref name="Stern">{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/1998/film/news/settlement-reached-in-devil-s-advocate-case-1117467814/ |title=Settlement reached in 'Devil's Advocate' case |last=Stern |first=Christopher |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=February 16, 1998 |access-date=August 18, 2017 |archive-date=August 19, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170819103343/http://variety.com/1998/film/news/settlement-reached-in-devil-s-advocate-case-1117467814/ |url-status=live }}</ref>
