'''''Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga''''' is a 2024 [[Epic film|epic]] [[Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction|post-apocalyptic]] [[action film]] directed and produced by [[George Miller (filmmaker)|George Miller]], who wrote the screenplay with [[Nico Lathouris]]. It is the fifth installment in the ''[[Mad Max]]'' franchise, and the first not focused on [[Max Rockatansky]], instead a [[Spinoff (media)|spinoff]] [[prequel]] to ''[[Mad Max: Fury Road]]'' (2015) and an [[origin story]] for [[Imperator Furiosa|Furiosa]]. Starring [[Anya Taylor-Joy]] and [[Alyla Browne]] as said character and set years before ''Fury Road'', the film follows her life for over a decade, from her kidnapping by the forces of warlord Dementus ([[Chris Hemsworth]]) to her ascension to the rank of [[Imperator]]. [[Tom Burke (actor)|Tom Burke]] also stars as Praetorian Jack, a military commander who befriends Furiosa. Several ''Fury Road'' cast members return in supporting roles, including [[John Howard (Australian actor)|John Howard]], [[Nathan Jones (wrestler)|Nathan Jones]], and [[Angus Sampson]] reprising their characters. The film begins in what can be seen as a green paradise of a [[solarpunk]] future and quickly moves to the more traditional [[dieselpunk]], which the ''Mad Max ''franchise is known for.<ref>{{cite web |last=Mould |first=Oli |date=3 June 2024 |title='Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga' – blink and you'll miss a solarpunk alternative to series' usual dystopia |url=https://theconversation.com/furiosa-a-mad-max-saga-blink-and-youll-miss-a-solarpunk-alternative-to-series-usual-dystopia-230460 |access-date=28 December 2024 |website=The Conversation |language=en-US}}</ref>

Miller initially intended to shoot ''Furiosa'' [[Back-to-back film production|back-to-back]] with ''Fury Road'', but the former spent several years in [[development hell]] amidst [[salary]] disputes with [[Warner Bros. Pictures]], ''Fury Road''{{'}}s distributor. Several crew members from ''Fury Road'' returned for ''Furiosa'', including Lathouris, producer [[Doug Mitchell (film producer)|Doug Mitchell]], composer [[Tom Holkenborg]], costume designer [[Jenny Beavan]], and editor [[Margaret Sixel]] (Miller's wife). Filming took place in Australia from June to October 2022.

''Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga'' premiered at the [[2024 Cannes Film Festival|77th Cannes Film Festival]] on 15 May 2024. It was released theatrically in Australia on 23 May 2024 and in the United States the following day. The film received highly positive reviews from critics and multiple award nominations. ==Plot==
<!--- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 and 700 words! Word Count: 685 --->
===Chapter 1: The Pole of Inaccessibility===
Decades after the [[Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction|apocalypse]], Australia is a radioactive wasteland and the Green Place of Many Mothers is one of the last remaining areas with fresh water and plant cultivation. Raiders discover the Green Place while two children, [[Imperator Furiosa|Furiosa]] and Valkyrie, are picking peaches. Furiosa tries to sabotage their motorcycles, but the raiders capture her as a prize for Dementus, leader of the Biker Horde.

Furiosa's mother Mary Jabassa pursues the raiders to the Horde's camp to rescue Furiosa, but Dementus tracks them down. Mary stays behind to buy Furiosa time to escape and gives her a peach pit to remember her by, but Furiosa refuses to leave Mary behind. Dementus forces Furiosa to watch her mother's [[crucifixion]] and torture at the hands of Dementus lieutenant The Octoboss. Haunted by his own family's death, Dementus adopts Furiosa as his daughter. She tattoos a [[star chart]] to the Green Place on her left arm to find her way home.

===Chapter 2: Lessons from the Wasteland===
Sometime after, Dementus besieges the Citadel, another settlement with fresh water and plants. However, the Horde is repelled by the War Boys, the fanatical [[army]] of Citadel warlord [[Immortan Joe]]. Dementus uses a [[Trojan Horse]] strategy to capture Gastown, an oil refinery that supplies the Citadel with gasoline, from Joe's brother. At peace negotiations, during which Joe's brother is accidentally killed, Joe recognizes Dementus's authority over Gastown and increases its supplies of food and water in exchange for the Horde's Organic Mechanic and Furiosa. Afterwards, Joe imprisons Furiosa with his stable of "wives" inside a vault. After Joe's son Rictus shows an attraction towards her, Furiosa devises a plan to escape. One night, Rictus breaks Furiosa out of Joe's vault to rape her, but she slips from his grasp using a wig made from her own hair and disappears. Disguised as a [[Muteness|mute]] War Boy, Furiosa works her way up the ranks of Joe's men for over a decade.

===Chapter 3: The Stowaway===
Furiosa helps build the War Rig, a heavily armed supply tanker that can withstand raider attacks in the lawless Wasteland. Furiosa plans to escape by hiding on the Rig when Joe sends his top driver, Praetorian Jack, on a supply run. Disillusioned by Dementus's callousness, The Octoboss goes rogue and launches an [[air assault]] on the Rig. His Mortifiers slaughter the Rig's entire crew and destroy Furiosa's hidden motorcycle, but Furiosa and Jack team up to defeat them. Furiosa tries to carjack the Rig and drive home, but Jack easily thwarts her. However, he recognizes her potential and offers to train her to escape if she helps him rebuild his crew.

===Chapter 4: Homeward===
Furiosa becomes Jack's second-in-command and is promoted to Praetorian. She and Jack develop a bond and resolve to escape together. They see an opportunity when Joe decides to attack Gastown, which Dementus has mismanaged to near-ruin. Joe orders Furiosa and Jack to collect weapons and ammunition from the Bullet Farm, an allied mining facility. However, Dementus, having already taken possession of the farm, ambushes them when they arrive. Furiosa and Jack barely escape, and Furiosa's left arm is injured. Dementus chases them down and has Jack dragged to death. Furiosa escapes her chains by severing her own injured arm, sacrificing her star map.

Instead, Dementus is lured into a trap at the Citadel, and the War Boys crush the Horde. Having lost her path home, Furiosa shaves her head and builds a mechanical prosthetic in place of her severed arm.

===Chapter 5: Beyond Vengeance===
Furiosa pursues the fleeing Dementus. After an extended chase, Furiosa subdues Dementus in the desert. She imprisons Dementus in the Citadel and uses his still-living body as [[fertilizer]] to grow a peach tree from her mother's pit. Joe promotes Furiosa to "Imperator" and gives her command of a new War Rig.

She meets Joe's five breeder wives in the vault where Joe once held her prisoner and shows them a peach from the tree. Miller stated that ''Furiosa'' was an easier shoot than ''Fury Road'', alluding to the latter's [[Mad Max: Fury Road#Filming|troubled production]], and complimented Warner Bros.' new leadership for implementing an "approach to filmmaking [that was] much more collaborative than it was adversarial".<ref>{{cite web |last=Williams |first=Andrew |date=24 May 2024 |title=George Miller on the making of 'Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga' |url=https://www.shortlist.com/news/george-miller-on-the-making-of-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga-404539 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240526141430/https://www.shortlist.com/news/george-miller-on-the-making-of-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga-404539 |archive-date=26 May 2024 |access-date=28 May 2024 |website=Shortlist |language=en-US}}</ref> Burke said that Miller wanted a different kind of filming style from ''Fury Road'', which used short takes and long cuts; he noted that Miller specifically wanted to shoot the scene where Dementus taunts Jack and Furiosa in one take.<ref name="Burke"/>

In lighting most of ''Furiosa''{{'}}s exterior shots, Duggan and [[Gaffer (occupation)|gaffer]] Shaun Conway recognized that "nearly all of our story would be told during daytime, so everything relied on sunlight", Duggan said. "And because we were in mid-[[El Niño–Southern Oscillation|El Niño]], the weather during the shoot was unpredictable, with pouring rains and winds up to 50 miles per hour. So, we added a lot of artificial light to create sunlight, which helped us create a harsh look that was quite different than what you'd expect."<ref name="ASC"/> To augment their locations' natural light, the filmmakers relied on a uniform approach, employing an array of six 18K [[Hydrargyrum medium-arc iodide lamp|HMI]] [[Parabolic aluminized reflector|PARs]] at varying degrees of spot. "The six PARs were mounted to three heavy-duty telescopic handlers that were easily moveable and could withstand the conditions, and the lamps were protected from the rain", Duggan noted. "We found that the 18K array could cover almost half a football field and accommodated the size of most of our exterior-location sets. We could put the light wherever we wanted to; whenever the sun was coming in and out, we'd follow and match that direction."<ref name="ASC"/> He continued: "George told us we weren't going to wait for anything to light our sets, so we had to be prepared with one solution. And that meant we didn't have to stop shooting — we could just keep on going. Of course, we always oriented the sets, or our camera, to make use of sidelight or backlight from the actual sun, but the brute power of those PARs gave us all the light we needed."<ref name="ASC"/>

Taylor-Joy praised Miller's commitment to safety on set,<ref>{{cite web |last=Handler |first=Rachel |date=16 May 2024 |title=Anya Taylor-Joy Says It 'Doesn't Make Any Sense How Safe Furiosa Was' |url=https://www.vulture.com/article/anya-taylor-joy-on-how-safe-the-stunts-of-furiosa-were.html |access-date=31 May 2024 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]] |url-access=registration |archive-date=16 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240516163354/https://www.vulture.com/article/anya-taylor-joy-on-how-safe-the-stunts-of-furiosa-were.html |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> but said that working on the film was, nonetheless, a challenging experience. With just 30-odd spoken lines of dialogue, she would go "months" on the film's set without speaking a single word on camera: "I've never been more alone than making that movie ... I don't want to go too deep into it, but everything that I thought was going to be easy was hard." When asked what proved more difficult than she expected on the ''Furiosa'' set, the actress said: "Next question, sorry. Talk to me in 20 years."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/12/movies/anya-taylor-joy-furiosa.html |title=Anya Taylor-Joy Still Can't Make Sense of What She Went Through |last=Buchanan |first=Kyle |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=12 May 2024 |access-date=13 May 2024 |url-access=subscription |archive-date=13 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240513213952/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/12/movies/anya-taylor-joy-furiosa.html |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> In a later interview, responding to a question regarding how she was "able to portray the nuance and complexity of Furiosa without much dialogue", Taylor-Joy explained: "[The character] was just immediately there. I think I fought more for this character than I had fought for any other character. George had such a specific vision for what he wanted her to be and I just felt like it was my responsibility to fight for any moment where you could see a little bit of her rage come out."<ref name="VogueHK">{{cite web |url=https://www.voguehk.com/en/article/celebrity/anya-taylor-joy-vogue-hong-kong-2024/ |title=Anya Taylor-Joy on Dance, Discipline & the Power of Conviction |last=Fischer |first=Carina |website=[[Vogue (magazine)#Vogue Hong Kong|Vogue Hong Kong]] |date=1 September 2024 |access-date=3 September 2024 |language=en-US |archive-date=7 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007001406/https://www.voguehk.com/en/article/celebrity/anya-taylor-joy-vogue-hong-kong-2024/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Sharing her most memorable note given by Miller throughout the filming process, she said: "[George] wanted [Furiosa] to be incredibly stoic. And I felt like my contribution was that I've always felt like you need to see the humanity behind that, if you want people to fully invest in a character. George encourages you to be in almost like a university-type setting where every choice you have to justify – and you don't justify it once. You justify it thousands of times if it's going to make it in the movie. It was really great training for me not only as an actor but also as somebody that hopes to direct one day. Your conviction has to be unwavering if you want something to make it into one of his movies."<ref name="VogueHK"/>

Hemsworth arrived on set nursing a back injury, but said that he was excited to work on ''Furiosa'' because playing Dementus allowed him to get "out of that typecast space of the muscly action guy and ... play a character with complications and darkness". He explained that "suffering without a purpose is awful", but "suffering with purpose can be rejuvenating and replenishing".<ref>{{cite web |last=Valby |first=Karen |date=30 April 2024 |title=Chris Hemsworth on Fear, Love, 'Furiosa'—And Naming a Son After a Brad Pitt Character |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/chris-hemsworth-cover-story |access-date=28 May 2024 |website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |archive-date=29 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240529163707/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/chris-hemsworth-cover-story |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> Burke said that Miller was willing to collaborate with his actors to structure Furiosa and Jack's relationship, explaining that while Miller wanted a romance, Burke felt the characters should "push romance to the side until they believe they are riding off to a safer place".<ref name="Zuckerman">{{cite web |last=Zuckerman |first=Esther |date=28 May 2024 |title=Tom Burke is Glad There Wasn't More Romance in 'Furiosa' |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/furiosa-tom-burke-praetorian-jack |access-date=5 June 2024 |website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |url-access=limited |archive-date=28 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240528202219/https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/story/furiosa-tom-burke-praetorian-jack |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref>

While promoting the film, the actors disclosed several ideas that Miller considered or even shot but ultimately cut. ===Impact on the Australian film industry===
According to the [[Australian Broadcasting Corporation]] (ABC), ''Furiosa'' was the most expensive film in Australian history, with a budget of AU$333.2 million.<ref name="ABC News"/> Over 3,000 people worked on the film, including some ex-convicts who were hired as supporting artists.<ref name="ABC News"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Dalton |first=Ben |date=4 December 2023 |title=Chris Hemsworth says George Miller hired 'ex-criminals' as extras on 'Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga' |url=https://www.screendaily.com/news/chris-hemsworth-says-george-miller-hired-ex-criminals-as-extras-on-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga/5188478.article |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231208160209/https://www.screendaily.com/news/chris-hemsworth-says-george-miller-hired-ex-criminals-as-extras-on-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga/5188478.article |archive-date=8 December 2023 |access-date=10 March 2024 |website=[[Screen International#Screen Daily|Screen Daily]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> In addition, to take advantage of Australian tax credits for VFX work, DNEG opened a Sydney branch to spearhead [[special effect]]s work on ''Furiosa''; it estimated that the office, once fully staffed, would employ up to 500 VFX artists.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Keast |first=Jackie |date=16 November 2022 |title=DNEG to set up shop in Sydney, with first project 'Furiosa' |url=https://if.com.au/dneg-sets-up-shop-in-sydney-with-first-project-furiosa/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221125021838/https://if.com.au/dneg-sets-up-shop-in-sydney-with-first-project-furiosa/ |archive-date=25 November 2022 |access-date=3 June 2024 |magazine=[[IF Magazine]] |language=en-AU}}</ref>

The film was awarded extensive government subsidies, including a [[Film and television financing in Australia|filming incentive]] from the [[Screen NSW|New South Wales government]] and various "offsets from the federal government".<ref name="ABC News"/> Miller said that government support "made [shooting in Australia] possible".<ref name="Incentives">{{cite web |last=Frater |first=Patrick |date=19 April 2021 |title=George Miller's 'Furiosa' Receives Incentives for Australia Shoot |url=https://variety.com/2021/film/asia/george-miller-furiosa-receives-incentives-for-australia-shoot-1234954435/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231031142904/https://variety.com/2021/film/asia/george-miller-furiosa-receives-incentives-for-australia-shoot-1234954435/ |archive-date=31 October 2023 |access-date=19 April 2021 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |language=en-US}}</ref> [[Queensland University of Technology]] professor [[Amanda D. Lotz|Amanda Lotz]] estimated that Screen NSW contributed AU$50 million in direct subsidies to ''Furiosa''{{'}}s budget.<ref name="Lotz">{{cite web |last=Lotz |first=Amanda |author-link=Amanda D. Lotz |date=16 June 2024 |title=Australia's taxpayers have reasons to be mad about 'Furiosa' |url=https://asia.nikkei.com/Opinion/Australia-s-taxpayers-have-reasons-to-be-mad-about-Furiosa |access-date=18 June 2024 |website=[[The Nikkei|Nikkei Asia]] |language=en-JA |archive-date=7 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241007001406/https://asia.nikkei.com/Opinion/Australia-s-taxpayers-have-reasons-to-be-mad-about-Furiosa |url-status=live}}</ref> Additionally, the federal government offers all qualifying films a tax rebate equivalent to 40% of the amount of money the production spends in Australia.<ref>{{cite web |title=Screen Australia |url=https://www.ausfilm.com.au/why-film-in-australia/australian-screen-businesses/federal-state-local-government-agencies/screen-australia/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240515031447/https://www.ausfilm.com.au/why-film-in-australia/work-with-the-best/federal-state-local-government-agencies/screen-australia/ |archive-date=15 May 2024 |access-date=2 June 2024 |publisher=[[Ausfilm]] |language=en-AU}}</ref> Lotz estimated this federal rebate at AU$133 million (40% of AU$333.2 million),<ref name="Lotz"/> and NSW premier [[Gladys Berejiklian]] said that she hoped ''Furiosa'' would contribute AU$350 million to the Australian economy.<ref name="Incentives"/> Although a May 2024 ABC report estimated the size of the NSW filming incentive as AU$175 million (over half the film's reported budget), the ABC subsequently amended its report to remove that estimate.<ref name="ABC News"/> Other reports suggested that the AU$175 million figure applies to the total size of the NSW subsidy fund (spread out over five years) and not to ''Furiosa'' specifically.<ref name="Incentives"/>

''Furiosa''{{'}}s VFX artists allowed production to keep shooting in Australia even though the weather in New South Wales was not ideal for a desert-based film.<ref name="Failes"/> By contrast, Miller had to move the ''Fury Road'' shoot from Australia to Namibia because rain caused wildflowers to grow in the Australian desert, which would not have happened in a post-apocalyptic wasteland.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 September 2015 |title=DP John Seale Takes Codex into the Namibian Desert for 'Mad Max: Fury Road' |url=https://www.btlnews.com/crafts/post-production/dp-john-seale-takes-codex-into-the-namibian-desert-for-mad-max-fury-road/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405151152/https://www.btlnews.com/crafts/post-production/dp-john-seale-takes-codex-into-the-namibian-desert-for-mad-max-fury-road/ |archive-date=5 April 2024 |access-date=8 June 2024 |website=Below the Line |language=en-US}}</ref> According to Framestore, during the opening chase sequence where Mary tracks Furiosa's kidnappers through a series of sand dunes, "we ended up pretty much having to replace all of the ground".<ref name="Failes"/> Colourist Eric Whipp, who also worked on ''Fury Road'', said that because the desert scenes needed to look like Namibia and "there was a huge mix of sunny and cloudy days" during the shoot, "a lot of the backgrounds in this film are full CG".<ref>{{cite web |date=11 June 2024 |title=Creating George Miller's graphic prequel 'Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga' |url=https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/creating-george-millers-graphic-prequel-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga |access-date=11 June 2024 |publisher=[[TVB-Europe (broadcaster)|TVB-Europe]] |archive-date=11 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240611185127/https://www.tvbeurope.com/production-post/creating-george-millers-graphic-prequel-furiosa-a-mad-max-saga |url-status=live |language=en-GB}}</ref>

''Fury Road'' composer [[Tom Holkenborg]] (also known as Junkie XL) returned to [[Film score|score]] ''Furiosa'', as his third collaboration with Miller after ''Fury Road'' and ''Three Thousand Years of Longing''.<ref name="Junkie XL">{{cite web |last=Williams |first=Joe |title=Junkie XL on returning to the 'Mad Max' saga for 'Furiosa' |url=https://composer.spitfireaudio.com/en/articles/junkie-xl-on-returning-to-the-mad-max-saga-for-furiosa |access-date=31 May 2024 |website=Composer Magazine |archive-date=26 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240526020139/https://composer.spitfireaudio.com/en/articles/junkie-xl-on-returning-to-the-mad-max-saga-for-furiosa |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> Holkenborg moved to Sydney to pen the score and also helped prepare the final mix.<ref name="Junkie XL"/><ref name="Score">{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2024/05/furiosa-junkie-xl-tom-holkenborg-interview-cannes-1235941833/ |title='Furiosa' Composer Tom Holkenborg on His Approach to 'Mad Max Saga' Score: 'Restraint Was the Way Forward' |last=D'Alessandro |first=Anthony |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=27 May 2024 |access-date=28 May 2024 |archive-date=27 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240527191545/https://deadline.com/2024/05/furiosa-junkie-xl-tom-holkenborg-interview-cannes-1235941833/ |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> He explained that because ''Furiosa'' was a character-driven film, the film's score had to be character-driven as well: "Musically, everything was being told from a [[First-person narrative|first-person perspective]], which is being her [Furiosa], how she, watches the world around her, The Wasteland, its cruelties."<ref name="Score"/>

Holkenborg added that Miller wanted a "way, ''way'' more subtle score" for ''Furiosa'' than ''Fury Road'',<ref name="Junkie XL"/> the latter of which was "just massive action over 48 hours" and an "over-the-top rock opera".<ref name="Curzon">{{cite web |last=Warmann |first=Amon |date=24 May 2024 |title='Furiosa's' Composer Tom Holkenborg on Returning to the 'Mad Max' Wasteland |url=https://www.curzon.com/journal/furiosa-composer-interview/ |access-date=31 May 2024 |publisher=[[Curzon Cinemas|Curzon]] |archive-date=26 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240526154606/https://www.curzon.com/journal/furiosa-composer-interview/ |url-status=live |language=en-GB}}</ref> In particular, Miller vetoed reusing the song "Brothers in Arms" from ''Fury Road'' (which plays when Max and Furiosa help each other escape the Rock Rider canyon) during the Bullet Farm action sequence because he wanted to focus the audience's attention on the fact that Furiosa was willing to sacrifice her best chance at finding the Green Place to save Jack's life, and directed Holkenborg to use ''Fury Road''{{'}}s musical motifs for the Green Place instead.<ref name="Curzon"/> Miller explained that the Bullet Farm sequence needed to be "kind of a love story in the middle of an action scene". He wanted to show that "through their actions, [Furiosa and Jack] actually are prepared to give of themselves entirely to the other".<ref>{{cite news |last=Murphy |first=Mekado |date=31 May 2024 |title=Watch an Ambush at the Bullet Farm in 'Furiosa' |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/31/movies/furiosa-clip.html |access-date=1 June 2024 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |issn=0362-4331 |url-access=subscription |archive-date=31 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240531174308/https://www.nytimes.com/2024/05/31/movies/furiosa-clip.html |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref>

Furthermore, Holkenborg "used AI to make [[Audio deepfake|deep fake voices]] from another voice", explaining: "What if the source sound was a [[Drum beat|drum rhythm]], and what if the destination sound was an [[electric guitar]]? But the software doesn't know what to make of it. So it gave us a happy accident that we used throughout the score".<ref>{{cite web |last=Tangcay |first=Jazz |date=31 May 2024 |title='Furiosa' Composer Tom Holkenborg Reveals How He Used AI in the Score to Create 'Deep Fake Voices' |url=https://variety.com/2024/artisans/news/furiosa-composer-score-ai-deep-fake-voices-1236021377/ |access-date=1 June 2024 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |archive-date=1 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240601094150/https://variety.com/2024/artisans/news/furiosa-composer-score-ai-deep-fake-voices-1236021377/ |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> Warner Bros. in-house record label [[WaterTower Music]] released the official soundtrack album on 17 May 2024.<ref>{{cite web |date=3 May 2024 |title='Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga' Soundtrack Album Details |url=https://filmmusicreporter.com/2024/05/03/furiosa-a-mad-max-saga-soundtrack-album-details/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240506073147/https://filmmusicreporter.com/2024/05/03/furiosa-a-mad-max-saga-soundtrack-album-details/ |archive-date=6 May 2024 |access-date=28 May 2024 |website=Film Music Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref>

''Furiosa''{{'}}s domestic grosses disproportionately came from [[Movie theater#Premium large format|premium large format]] screenings (PLFs) like [[IMAX]] and [[Dolby Cinema]], which command higher ticket prices than screenings in regular theatres, and (according to Warner Bros. executives) may appeal more to diehard fans.<ref name="McClintock">{{cite web |last=McClintock |first=Pamela |date=2 April 2024 |title='Godzilla' and 'Dune' Power IMAX-Led Premium Screen Boom While Theaters Struggle |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/godzilla-dune-imax-premium-screen-boom-1235863930/ |access-date=3 June 2024 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |archive-date=16 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240516164109/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/godzilla-dune-imax-premium-screen-boom-1235863930/ |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> In its opening weekend, 54% of ''Furiosa''{{'}}s domestic gross came from PLFs, compared to 48% for ''Oppenheimer'', 44% for ''[[Dune: Part Two]]'', 42% for ''[[Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning Part One]]'', and 37% for ''[[Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny]]''.<ref name="Opening"/><ref name="McClintock"/><ref>{{cite web |date=18 March 2024 |title=Marketing Spice: the evolution of 'Dune: Part Two's' audience |url=https://vistagroup.co.nz/blog/marketing-spice-the-evolution-of-dune-part-twos-audience |access-date=3 June 2024 |publisher=[[Vista Group]] |archive-date=7 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240507110743/https://vistagroup.co.nz/blog/marketing-spice-the-evolution-of-dune-part-twos-audience |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> Conversely, ''[[Screen Rant]]''{{'}}s Kate Bove suggested that Hollywood studios' eagerness to push content onto their own streaming services had encouraged everyday filmgoers to put off watching ''Furiosa'' in theatres on the assumption that Warner Bros. would "quickly transition" ''Furiosa'' to Max. She added that high budgets for tentpole features were "rais[ing] the bar for box office success".<ref>{{cite web |last=Bove |first=Kate |date=28 May 2024 |title=6 Reasons So Many Movies Are Struggling at the Box Office in 2024 |url=https://screenrant.com/movies-box-office-2024-struggling-reasons/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240529170055/https://screenrant.com/movies-box-office-2024-struggling-reasons/ |archive-date=29 May 2024 |access-date=30 May 2024 |website=[[Screen Rant]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

Club]] |archive-date=16 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240516031909/https://www.avclub.com/3000-years-of-longing-george-miller-interview-mad-max-1849431458 |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> At that time (May 2024), ''The Hollywood Reporter'' reported that ''The Wasteland'' was not yet in development.<ref name="Underperformance"/><ref name="THR"/> Miller had previously clarified that ''The Wasteland''{{'}}s source material (lore and other background material written in preparation for ''Fury Road'') had not yet been adapted into a screenplay.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Huff |first=Lauren |date=1 May 2024 |title=George Miller provides update on his plans for the future of the 'Mad Max' franchise |url=https://ew.com/george-miller-update-future-mad-max-franchise-furiosa-8640844 |access-date=31 May 2024 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |archive-date=17 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240517014128/https://ew.com/george-miller-update-future-mad-max-franchise-furiosa-8640844 |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> He also said that he was "waiting to see the reception on ''Furiosa''" before taking more concrete steps to develop ''The Wasteland'' into a feature film.<ref>{{cite web |last=Miller |first=Martin |date=28 May 2024 |title='The Wasteland': George Miller's Next 'Mad Max' Film is a 'Saga' Similar to 'Furiosa' |url=https://theplaylist.net/the-wasteland-george-millers-next-mad-max-film-is-a-saga-similar-to-furiosa-20240528/ |access-date=31 May 2024 |website=The Playlist |archive-date=28 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240528161157/https://theplaylist.net/the-wasteland-george-millers-next-mad-max-film-is-a-saga-similar-to-furiosa-20240528/ |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref>

Several weeks after the release of ''Furiosa'', Tom Hardy (who was promoting ''[[The Bikeriders]]'' at the time) said "I don't think it's happening" in an interview, either talking about his involvement in the film or the film itself.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://ew.com/tom-hardy-addresses-mad-max-movie-the-wasteland-8665527 |title=Tom Hardy doesn't think he'll be in the rumored 'Mad Max' movie 'The Wasteland' |last=Wang |first=Jessica |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=18 June 2024 |access-date=22 July 2024 |archive-date=18 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240618212303/https://ew.com/tom-hardy-addresses-mad-max-movie-the-wasteland-8665527 |url-status=live |language=en-US}}</ref> In October 2024, while promoting ''[[Venom: The Last Dance]]'', Hardy was further asked about whether he'll reprise his role for an additional ''Mad Max'' film, responding: "No, I haven't been told anything about it yet, but obviously I'd love to do that ... George already has a script called ''The Wasteland'', which is like quite specific, so I'm aware of that. It depends on whether they're making it."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://screenrant.com/mad-max-the-wastelands-tom-hardy-return-update/ |title='Mad Max': Tom Hardy Gives Far More Hopeful Response to 'The Wasteland's' Return |last=Northrup |first=Ryan |website=[[Screen Rant]] |date=24 October 2024 |access-date=25 November 2024 |language=en-US}}</ref>
