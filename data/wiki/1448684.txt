'''''Fever Pitch''''' (released as '''''The Perfect Catch''''' outside North America) is a 2005 American [[Romantic comedy|romantic]] [[comedy-drama]] film directed by the [[Farrelly brothers]]. It stars [[Drew Barrymore]] and [[Jimmy Fallon]] and is a remake of the British [[Fever Pitch (1997 film)|1997 film of the same title]]. [[Nick Hornby]], who had written the [[Fever Pitch|original 1992 book]] and the 1997 screenplay adaptation, acted as an executive producer for the American remake.

While both the book and the original 1997 film are about [[association football]], the 2005 adaptation, aimed specifically at the American market, is about [[baseball]]. Both ''Fever Pitch'' films feature real-life dramatic sporting victories, the original focusing on [[Arsenal F.C.|Arsenal]]'s last-minute [[Liverpool 0–2 Arsenal (26 May 1989)|League title win]] in the final game of the [[1988–89 Football League|1988–1989 season]], and the remake on the [[Boston Red Sox]]'s [[2004 World Series|World Series Championship]] in 2004, unanticipated while the film was in production.

The film premiered in [[Boston]] on April 6, 2005 and was released by [[20th Century Fox]] on April 8, 2005, in the United States, with ''[[American Dad! season 1#Extras|Inside the CIA]]'', a promotional short film for ''[[American Dad!]]'', premiering with it. It received mixed reviews from critics and grossed $50.6 million against a $30 million budget.

==Plot==

In 1980, 7-year-old Ben Wrightman had just moved to [[Boston]] with his mother after his parents' divorce. His uncle Carl took him to a [[Boston Red Sox]] game at [[Fenway Park]] to cheer him up. From that day on, he was a [[Red Sox Nation|die-hard Red Sox fan]] for life.

23 years later, Ben is still in Boston, working as a school teacher, and has inherited his uncle's season tickets since his death from [[cancer]]. Almost all of his possessions bear the Red Sox logo (except for his toilet paper, which is of the [[New York Yankees]]). On a school trip, Ben meets Lindsey Meeks, a successful, dedicated corporate executive, and they begin dating.

Lindsey, who knows little about baseball or the Red Sox, learns about the [[Curse of the Bambino]] from Ben's [[Fenway Park|stadium]] friends (including Al Waterman, a sponge salesman who also narrates the story). They continue attending the games together, but tension arises when Lindsey is up for a promotion and begins working on her laptop during the game. Lindsey is knocked unconscious by a [[line drive]] foul and recovers but stops going to the games, suggesting Ben will have more fun if he goes with his friends.

Things get worse when Lindsey invites Ben to accompany her to [[Paris]], and he declines because the Red Sox are in the heat of the pennant race. Before leaving, she tells Ben she might be pregnant. She expresses concern that he is more committed to the Red Sox than her, and days later, she calls him and confirms she isn't pregnant.

To prove he is not obsessed, Ben misses a game [[Yankees–Red Sox rivalry|against the Yankees]] to escort Lindsey to her friend's birthday party. Ben and Lindsey enjoy the party, and after making love, he tells her it was one of the best nights of his life. Moments later, he gets an ecstatic call from his friend Troy, who tells him the Red Sox overcame a seven-run deficit in the bottom of the ninth to pull off one of the greatest comebacks in team history. Ben becomes irate that he missed such a historic Red Sox moment, blaming Lindsey for making him miss the game. She is heartbroken, and they break up.

Ben soon misses Lindsey and visits her in an attempt to reconcile. Lindsey thinks he's only there because the Sox are [[List of teams that have overcome 3–0 series deficits|about to be eliminated]] and rejects Ben. He then plans to sell his season tickets to prove she means more to him than the Red Sox. Lindsey finds out during the celebration for her much-anticipated promotion and rushes to stop him. She buys a scalped outfield ticket and, during the 9th inning of the [[2004 American League Championship Series#Game 4|Red Sox–Yankees playoff game]], when the Red Sox are just 3 outs away from being swept. Ben is in the stands, about to finalize the ticket sale.

Meanwhile, Lindsey drops over the outfield wall and runs across the field and around players to avoid security. She reaches Ben, tears up the contract, and tells him that if he loves her enough to sell his seats, then she loves him enough not to let him do it. They kiss in front of the entire crowd before Lindsey is arrested.

Al narrates the epilogue: the Red Sox won that game and then beat the Yankees three more times to win the [[American League]] [[Pennant (sports)|pennant]], later sweeping the [[National League (baseball)|National League]] champion [[St. Louis Cardinals]] for their first [[World Series]] title in 86 years. Lindsay and Ben travel to [[Busch Stadium (II)|Busch Stadium]] in St. Louis for the decisive [[2004 World Series|Game 4]]. Eventually, they marry, and Lindsey conceives a "[[player to be named later]]". Al explains that the baby will be named [[Ted Williams]] Wrightman if it's a boy, "[[Carl Yastrzemski|Carla Yastrzemski]]" Wrightman if it's a girl, adding, "Let's all hope for a boy."

A [[post-credit scene]] shows a group of children (presumably, the children of Ben and Lindsey and their friends) chanting "LET'S GO, RED SOX!".

==Production==
The original plot had assumed the [[Boston Red Sox|Red Sox]] would lose in the playoffs. However, the Red Sox stunned the baseball world when they won four straight games to win the [[2004 American League Championship Series|2004 ALCS]] against the rival [[New York Yankees|Yankees]] (becoming the first [[Major League Baseball|MLB]] team to [[List of teams that have overcome 3–0 series deficits|overcome a 3–0 series deficit]]) and subsequent [[2004 World Series|World Series]] against the [[St. Louis Cardinals]] to break the "[[Curse of the Bambino]]". Thus, the ending had to be rewritten. On the day of Game 4, with the Red Sox on the verge of a sweep, the Farrellys decided to bring Barrymore, Fallon, and a film crew to St. Louis hours before the first pitch — and Barrymore and Fallon attended the game at [[Busch Stadium II|Busch Stadium]] in character. When the Red Sox made the final out to secure a 4–0 win over the Cardinals that broke the Curse, Fox cameras on the live broadcast caught Barrymore and Fallon, as Lindsey and Ben, running onto the field and kissing to celebrate.<ref>{{cite magazine |title = Sox Change |url = https://ew.com/article/2004/11/12/fever-pitch-ending-changed-after-red-sox-win/ |magazine = [[Entertainment Weekly]] |first = Whitney |last = Pastorek |date = November 12, 2004 |access-date = October 9, 2011 |archive-date = July 3, 2011 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20110703060835/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,767456,00.html |url-status = live }}</ref> The film, with its updated ending, was also screened at Fenway Park the following August on a screen in center field.<ref>Perry Eaton (July 31, 2015). [https://www.boston.com/culture/entertainment/2015/07/31/you-can-watch-fever-pitch-at-fenway-in-august/ "You can watch ''Fever Pitch'' at Fenway in August"]. ''[[Boston Globe]]''. Retrieved April 13, 2022.</ref>

Originally, [[Shawn Levy]], who was a huge fan of [[Nick Hornby]]'s works for years, was attached to direct, with [[Gwyneth Paltrow]] playing Lindsey.<ref>{{cite news|work=The Telegraph|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/worldnews/northamerica/usa/1405285/Can-Hornby-remake-bring-fever-pitch-to-baseball.html|title=Can Hornby remake bring fever pitch to baseball?|first=Sam |last=Leith |date=24 August 2002|access-date=April 13, 2022}}</ref> However, Paltrow found the script mediocre and turned down the role.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.notstarring.com/actors/paltrow-gwyneth|work=NotStarring.com|title=Gwyneth Paltrow|access-date=April 13, 2022}}</ref> [[Brian Robbins]] replaced Levy, but he quit the project as well.<ref>{{cite news|work=Variety|url=https://variety.com/2003/film/news/robbins-catches-pitch-from-fox-2000-1117886165/|title=Robbins catches 'Pitch' from Fox 2000 |date=May 13, 2003 |access-date=April 13, 2022|first=Claude |last=Brodesser}}</ref> After [[Drew Barrymore]] replaced Paltrow and [[Jimmy Fallon]] joined the cast, [[Jay Russell]],<ref>{{cite news|first=Benjamin |last=Potter|url=http://www.memphis.edu/magazinearchive/v20i3/feat3.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100531200523/http://www.memphis.edu/magazinearchive/v20i3/feat3.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=2010-05-31|date=Summer 2002|work=Memphis Magazine |title=The Right Direction|access-date=April 13, 2022}}</ref> [[P. ==Reception==

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating 66% based on 195 reviews, with an average rating of 6.3/10. The site's critical consensus reads: "While not a home run, ''Fever Pitch'' has enough charm and on-screen chemistry between the two leads to make it a solid hit."<ref>{{cite web |title = Fever Pitch (2005) |url = http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/fever_pitch/ |work = [[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher = [[Flixster]] |access-date = February 1, 2021 }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[Weighted mean|weighted average score]] of 56 out of 100, based on 37 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |title = Fever Pitch reviews |url = http://www.metacritic.com/movie/fever-pitch |work = [[Metacritic]] |publisher = [[CBS Interactive]] |access-date = August 28, 2010 }}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web|url=https://m.cinemascore.com |title=CinemaScore |work=cinemascore.com |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date= 2018-12-20 }}</ref>

The film, and especially its script, received some praise from critics [[Roger Ebert]]<ref>{{cite news | first=Roger | last=Ebert | title=''Fever Pitch'' by Roger Ebert | date=April 8, 2005 | publisher=Chicago Sun-Times | url =https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/fever-pitch-2005 | work =RogerEbert.com | access-date = April 14, 2008 }}</ref> and [[James Berardinelli]].<ref>{{cite news | first=James | last=Berardinelli | title=''Fever Pitch'' - A Film Review by James Berardinelli | year=2005 | url =http://preview.reelviews.net/movies/f/fever_pitch2005.html | work =ReelViews.com | access-date = April 14, 2008 }}</ref>

=== Box office ===
The film opened at No. 3 and grossed $12.4 million in its opening weekend. The final North American gross of the film was $42.1 million, and the worldwide gross was $50.5 million.<ref name="mojo">{{cite web |title = Fever Pitch (2005) |url = http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=feverpitch.htm |work = [[Box Office Mojo]] |publisher = [[IMDb]] |access-date = August 28, 2010 }}</ref>

== Home media ==
''Fever Pitch'' was released on [[DVD]] and [[VHS]] on September 13, 2005.
