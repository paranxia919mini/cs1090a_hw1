'''''Il grido''''' (English: "The Cry", initially released as '''''The Outcry''''' in the US<ref name="Pitts2">{{cite book |last=Pitts |first=Michael R. |title=Astor Pictures: A Filmography and History of the Reissue King, 1933-1965 |publisher=McFarland |year=2019 |isbn=9781476676494 |pages=85–86}}</ref>)  is a 1957 [[Road film|road]] [[drama film]] directed by [[Michelangelo Antonioni]] and starring [[Steve Cochran]], [[Alida Valli]], [[Dorian Gray (actress)|Dorian Gray]], [[Gabriella Pallotta]] and [[Betsy Blair]].<ref name="Antonioni3">{{cite book |title=Michaelangelo Antonioni |publisher=Carl Hanser Verlag |year=1984 |editor1-last=Jansen |editor1-first=Peter W. |location=Munich and Vienna |editor2-last=Schütte |editor2-first=Wolfram}}</ref><ref name="Chatman3">{{cite book |last=Chatman |first=Seymour |title=Antonioni, or, The Surface of the World |publisher=University of California Press |year=1985 |isbn=978-0-520-05341-0 |location=Berkeley}}</ref>

An Italian and American co-production,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Il grido (1957) |url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/5065caa9-10fb-5109-a2d0-ab31424babbd/il-grido |access-date=2024-12-30 |website=BFI |language=en}}</ref> the film premiered at the 1957 [[Locarno Festival|Locarno Film Festival]], where it won the [[Golden Leopard]].<ref name="Locarno2">{{Cite web |title=Winners of the Golden Leopard |url=http://www.pardo.ch/jahia/Jahia/home/Festival/History/Winners-of-the-Golden-Leopard/lang/en |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090719031720/http://www.pardo.ch/jahia/Jahia/home/Festival/History/Winners-of-the-Golden-Leopard/lang/en |archive-date=2009-07-19 |access-date=2012-08-12 |work=Locarno}}</ref> In 2008, the film was included on the [[Ministry of Cultural Heritage and Activities and Tourism|Italian Ministry of Cultural Heritage]]’s [[100 film italiani da salvare|100 Italian films to be saved]], a list of 100 films that "have changed the collective memory of the country between 1942 and 1978."<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ecco i cento film italiani da salvare Corriere della Sera |url=https://www.corriere.it/spettacoli/08_febbraio_28/elenco_cento_film_d83cacd8-e5ce-11dc-ab61-0003ba99c667.shtml |access-date=2021-03-11 |website=www.corriere.it}}</ref>

==Plot==
Aldo, a mechanic working in a sugar refinery in the [[Po Valley]] town of Goriano, has been in a seven-year relationship with Irma, with whom he shares a daughter, Rosina. When Irma learns that her estranged husband, who left for Australia years earlier, has died, Aldo sees an opportunity to marry her and legitimize their daughter. However, Irma confesses that she has fallen in love with another man. Devastated, Aldo attempts to win her back, but after a public altercation where he slaps her, Irma ends the relationship definitively.

In response to his heartbreak, Aldo leaves Goriano with Rosina, embarking on a journey through the Po Valley. Their first stop is at the home of Elvia, Aldo's former girlfriend, who works as a seamstress. Aldo tries to rekindle their past relationship, but his lingering sorrow over Irma hinders any genuine connection. Irma visits Elvia to deliver Aldo's belongings and informs her of their separation. Elvia confronts Aldo about returning only after his breakup and encourages him to move on. Realizing he cannot stay, Aldo departs with Rosina the next morning.

As they continue traveling, Aldo struggles to find steady work that accommodates Rosina's schooling. Their relationship becomes strained, culminating in an incident where Aldo slaps Rosina after she nearly gets hit by a car. They hitch a ride atop a petrol truck but are forced to disembark before a police checkpoint. Seeking shelter, they arrive at a filling station run by Virginia, a young widow living with her elderly father. She offers them a place to stay and gives Aldo a job. Over time, Aldo and Virginia become romantically involved.

Rosina spends her days with Virginia's father, who is bitter about selling his farmland and exhibits erratic behavior. Frustrated, Virginia decides to place him in a retirement home. Tensions escalate when Rosina witnesses Aldo and Virginia's intimacy, prompting Virginia to suggest that they can no longer care for the child. Aldo reluctantly sends Rosina back to Irma, chasing after the departing bus to declare his love for his daughter.

After parting ways with Virginia, Aldo finds employment with a dredge crew and contemplates moving to Venezuela, even beginning to learn Spanish. However, he soon loses interest. He befriends Andreina, a local prostitute, helping her secure medical attention. Mistakenly believing the police are after him, Aldo flees, leaving his coat behind. Andreina locates him, and they share stories of their troubled pasts. Heavy rains and scarce resources strain their situation, leading Andreina to seek food by offering herself to a restaurant owner. A confrontation ensues, and an exasperated Aldo abandons her.

Returning to Goriano, Aldo encounters Virginia at the filling station. She returns his belongings and mentions a misplaced postcard from Irma, inciting Aldo's frustration. In Goriano, he discovers the town in turmoil over the construction of an airfield that threatens to displace residents. Observing from a distance, Aldo sees Rosina entering Irma's home and notices Irma caring for a new child. Irma spots him and follows as he makes his way to the deserted sugar refinery where he once worked.

Aldo climbs the refinery tower, appearing disoriented and weary. From below, Irma calls out to him. He turns to see her but then falls to his death. Irma rushes to his side, grieving over his lifeless body as townspeople pass by en route to a protest rally.

== Themes ==
In retrospect, critics such as Leslie Camhi (''[[The Village Voice]]''), [[Philip French]] (''[[The Guardian]]'')<ref>{{cite news |last=French |first=Philip |date=31 May 2009 |title=Philip French's classic DVD: Il grido |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/may/31/philip-french-classic-film-grido |access-date=31 January 2023 |work=The Guardian}}</ref> and Keith Phipps (''[[The A.V. Club]]'') saw ''Il grido'' as a transitional work between Antonioni's [[Italian neorealism|neorealist]] roots and his later films.<ref name="vv2">{{cite news |last=Camhi |first=Leslie |date=30 July 2002 |title=Distribute the Wealth |url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2002-07-30/film/distribute-the-wealth/1/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090212124656/http://www.villagevoice.com/2002-07-30/film/distribute-the-wealth/1 |url-status=dead |archive-date=February 12, 2009 |access-date=May 28, 2012 |newspaper=The Village Voice}}</ref><ref name="avclub2">{{cite news |last=Phipps |first=Keith |date=March 29, 2002 |title=Il grido |url=https://www.avclub.com/il-grido-1798195340 |access-date=31 May 2012 |newspaper=A.V. Club}}</ref> In her 1984 analysis of Italian cinema, [[Mira Liehm]] writes that while ''Il grido'' contains neo-realist elements, "particularly the interdependence between the landscapes and the characters and the emphasis on objects", protagonist Aldo "foreshadows Sandro in ''L'avventura'' and Giovanni in ''La notte'' in his refusal to acknowledge the fading of love".<ref name="Liehm2">{{cite book |last=Liehm |first=Mira |title=Passion and Defiance: Italian Film from 1942 to the Present |publisher=University of California Press |year=1984 |isbn=9780520057449 |pages=156–59}}</ref>

Reviewers disagree about whether Aldo's death at the end is intentional or not. While French critic Gérard Gozlan (''[[Positif (magazine)|Positif]]'') saw it as a suicide,<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Gozlan |first=Gérard |date=July–August 1960 |title=Le Cri ou la faillite de nos sentiments |magazine=Positif |number=35}}</ref> Seymour Chatman argues that Aldo is overcome with vertigo as he stands atop the tower, causing him to fall to his death. Chatman found support in the original screenplay, which mentions that Aldo attempts to resist a sudden onset of vertigo as he looks down on the ground.<ref name="Chatman3"/> [[Peter Brunette]] regards the ending as being ambiguous: Aldo's death can be viewed either as caused by a fall or a deliberate jump.<ref name="brunette2">{{cite book |last=Brunette |first=Peter |title=The Films of Michelangelo Antonioni |publisher=Cambridge University Press |year=1998 |isbn=978-0-521-38992-1 |location=New York |page=21}}</ref>

Liehm, Brunette and Geoffrey Nowell-Smith also point out that as an exception in Antonioni's films, the protagonist of ''Il grido'' is a member of the working class instead of the bourgeoisie,<ref name="Liehm2" /><ref name="brunette2" /><ref name="nowell2">{{cite book |last=Nowell-Smith |first=Geoffrey |title=L'avventura |publisher=Bloomsbury |year=2019 |isbn=9780851705347 |page=19}}</ref> an observation confirmed by the director in an interview in which he stated that only ''Il grido'' and his early documentary short ''Gente del Po'' (1947) were about working class concerns.<ref name="Interviews2"/>

==Production==
Antonioni had written the script for ''Il grido'' in the late 1940s while working on another production in the Po area.<ref name="Antonioni3"/> The film was realised as an Italian–American co-production and shot in Winter 1956/1957 on location in the lower Po Valley, including [[Occhiobello]], Pontelogoscuro, [[Ferrara]], Stienta and Ca'Venier.<ref name="Antonioni3" /> Antonioni later spoke of problems he had with some of the American actors: with Betsy Blair, because she wanted the meaning behind her complete dialogue explained in detail, and with Steve Cochran for regularly refusing to follow the director's instructions.<ref>{{cite book |title=Il grido (DVD release booklet) |publisher=Eureka Entertainment |year=2009 |chapter=Michelangelo Antonioni on ''Il grido''}}</ref>

== Release ==
''Il grido'' premiered at the Locarno Film Festival on 14 July 1957<ref name="Antonioni3" /> and in [[Rome]] on 29 November 1957.<ref name="Chatman3" /> Of the films Antonioni had made up to this point, ''Il grido'' proved to be the least successful at the box office, earning a mere 25 million [[Italian lira|lire]] during its initial release.<ref name="brunette2" />

In English-speaking countries, the film saw a delayed release, in late 1961 in the UK and in October 1962 in the US.<ref name="Pitts3">{{cite book |last=Pitts |first=Michael R. |title=Astor Pictures: A Filmography and History of the Reissue King, 1933-1965 |publisher=McFarland |year=2019 |isbn=9781476676494 |pages=85–86}}</ref> In the US, it was also shown in a dubbed and shortened version, released by [[Astor Pictures]].<ref name="Pitts3" />

In 2024, ''Il grido'' was restored by [[The Film Foundation]], [[Cineteca di Bologna]], and Compass Film. The restoration includes several scenes that had previously been censored. It received a theatrical release at [[Film Forum]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Newman |first1=Nick |title=Michelangelo Antonioni's Early Triumph Il Grido Receives New Restoration and Trailer |url=https://thefilmstage.com/michelangelo-antonionis-early-triumph-il-grido-receives-new-restoration-and-trailer/ |website=The Film Stage |access-date=22 May 2025 |date=21 October 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Hoberman |first1=J. |author1-link=J. Hoberman |title='Il Grido': Love and Loss in Italy's Po River Valley |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/07/movies/il-grido-film-forum-antonioni.html |access-date=22 May 2025 |volume=[[The New York Times]] |date=7 November 2024}}</ref>

==Reception==
While Antonioni's previous film ''[[Le Amiche]]'' had been artistically acknowledged by Italian critics, the reactions towards ''Il grido'' were, depending on the source, unanimously negative<ref name="Antonioni3" /> or sympathetic only in a handful of cases,<ref name="nowell2" /> calling its director "cold" and "inhuman".<ref name="Interviews2" /> However, [[Seymour Chatman]] in ''Michaelangelo Antonioni: The Investigation'' cites ''Il grido'' as his "first genuine critical success",<ref>{{cite book |last=Chatman |first=Seymour |title=Michaelangelo Antonioni: The Investigation |publisher=Taschen |year=2004 |isbn=9783822830895 |page=45}}</ref> and [[Alexandre Astruc]] in his review talked about the "warmth" and "anguish" it inspires in the viewer and called Antonioni "one of the greatest living directors."<ref>In ''L'Express'', Dec. 11, 1958: "Mais de quel nom appeler alors, et comment expliquer cette émotion qui sans cesse vous étreint, cette chaleur secrète, cette angoisse qui peu à peu s’installe? Je tiens Antonioni pour un des plus grands metteurs en scène vivants." Quoted in Jean Douchet, ''Nouvelle vague'' (Hazan, 1998;  ISBN 2850256196), p. 108</ref> In a later interview, Antonioni explained that he had been in a state of depression at the time, and that the film reflected this in a more pessimistic, desperate tone.<ref name="Interviews2" /> As a result of the film's disappointing domestic reception, Antonioni was forced to cancel planned film projects and turn to theatre work, before returning to the cinema with his 1960 ''[[L'Avventura]]''.<ref name="Antonioni3" />

On the occasion of the film's 1962 [[New York City|New York]] release (where it was screened in Italian with English subtitles), ''[[The New York Times]]'' critic [[A. H. Weiler]] saw an "interesting, sometimes arresting slice of life at the lower depths", but the outlook of its director as "dismal and depressed".<ref name="nytimes2">{{cite news |last=Weiler |first=A.H. |date=23 October 1962 |title=Antonioni's 'Il grido' Arrives: '57 Film a Forerunner of 'L' Avventura' |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9902EEDE123CE63ABC4B51DFB6678389679EDE |access-date=31 January 2023 |work=The New York Times}}</ref> [[F. Maurice Speed]], writing for the British ''[[Film Review (magazine)|Film Review]]'', was more enthusiastic, calling it "brilliant" and "sad, fascinating and finely directed".<ref name="Pitts3" />
