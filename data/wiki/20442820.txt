'''''Like You Know It All''''' ({{Korean/auto|hangul=잘 알지도 못하면서|rr=yes|lit=You Think You Know It All But You Don't}}) is a 2009 South Korean [[comedy-drama]] film written and directed by [[Hong Sang-soo]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Shin|first=Hae-in|title=Introspective satire draws laughter in 'Like You Know It All'|url=http://english.yonhapnews.co.kr/culturesports/2009/04/28/4/0703000000AEN20090428000800315F.HTML|work=[[Yonhap]]|date=28 April 2009|accessdate=2012-11-18|archive-date=2016-03-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304204637/http://english.yonhapnews.co.kr/culturesports/2009/04/28/4/0703000000AEN20090428000800315F.HTML|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Plot ==
[[art film|Arthouse]] filmmaker Goo can't seem to direct a hit, but at least the critics love him. He goes to [[Jecheon]], [[North Chungcheong Province]] to judge the local film festival, but the common practice for jurors is to schmooze by day, drink at night, and sleep through movies. He bumps into an old friend Boo Sang-yong in town and drinks till he passes out, but not before soundly offending his friend's wife.

After Jecheon, Goo heads to [[Jeju Island]] to give a college lecture. == Production ==
''Like You Know It All'' is Hong's second film to be shot on [[High-definition video|HD video]] following his previous feature ''[[Night and Day (2008 film)|Night and Day]]'', released in 2008.<ref name="D'Sa" /> The film was produced independently with a short shooting period and low budget of [[United States dollar|$]]100,000, with the cast — some of whom have appeared in Hong's earlier films — working without any fees upfront.<ref name="D'Sa" /><ref name=Moon /> Shooting commenced in [[Jecheon]], August 2008, where the real-life [[Jecheon International Music & Film Festival]] is held annually, before moving to [[Jeju Island]], where filming continued into September.<ref name="D'Sa" />

A member of the main production staff said, "the film steps away from the sharp beauty of cinematic form, trying to make some changes while being more loose in form but more dynamic
in atmosphere. It is more hilarious but also bitter at the same time."<ref name=Moon />

''[[YesAsia]]'' said, "Hong's characteristic episodic narrative and elliptical reflection provide the brooding framework for a bitingly funny send-up of filmmakers, festivals, and the people and places in between."
