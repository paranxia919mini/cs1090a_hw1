'''''City of God''''' ({{langx|pt|'''Cidade de Deus'''}}) is a 2002 Brazilian [[Epic film|epic]] [[crime film]] directed by [[Fernando Meirelles]] and co-directed by [[Kátia Lund]]. The screenplay, written by [[Bráulio Mantovani]], is adapted from the [[City of God (Lins novel)|1997 novel of the same name]] by [[Paulo Lins]], though the plot is also loosely based on real events. The film portrays the rise of [[organized crime]] in the [[Cidade de Deus, Rio de Janeiro|Cidade de Deus]] suburb of [[Rio de Janeiro]] between the late 1960s and early 1980s, culminating in a war between drug dealer Li'l Zé and vigilante-turned-criminal Knockout Ned. Its tagline is, "If you run, the beast catches you; if you stay, the beast eats you."

The film features a cast including [[Alexandre Rodrigues (actor)|Alexandre Rodrigues]], [[Leandro Firmino]], [[Jonathan Haagensen|Jonathan]] and [[Phellipe Haagensen]], [[Douglas Silva]], Daniel Zettel, [[Seu Jorge]], and the film debut of [[Alice Braga]]. Most of the actors were actual residents of [[favela]]s such as [[Vidigal (favela)|Vidigal]] and Cidade de Deus itself.

''City of God'' received widespread critical acclaim and earned four nominations at the [[76th Academy Awards]]: [[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] ([[César Charlone (cinematographer)|César Charlone]]), [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] (Meirelles), [[Academy Award for Best Film Editing|Best Film Editing]] (Daniel Rezende), and [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]] (Mantovani). At the previous [[75th Academy Awards]], it was [[List of Brazilian submissions for the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Brazil's submission]] for [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film|Best Foreign Language Film]] but was not selected as a finalist. In 2015, the Brazilian Film Critics Association (Abraccine) ranked ''City of God'' 8th on its list of the 100 greatest Brazilian films.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://abraccine.org/2015/11/27/abraccine-organiza-ranking-dos-100-melhores-filmes-brasileiros/|title=Abraccine organiza ranking dos 100 melhores filmes brasileiros|work=Abraccine - Associação Brasileira de Críticos de Cinema|date=27 November 2015|access-date=10 August 2024|language=Portuguese}}</ref>

Following the success of ''City of God'', Meirelles and Lund created the ''[[City of Men]]'' TV series and its [[City of Men (film)|2007 film adaptation]]. Both projects share some actors (notably leads Silva and [[Darlan Cunha]]) and the same setting as ''City of God''.

==Plot==
An armed gang chases after an escaped chicken in a ''[[favela]]'' called the ''City of God''. The chicken stops between the gang and a young man nicknamed Rocket.

In the 1960s, three impoverished, amateur thieves known as the "Tender Trio"—Shaggy, Clipper, and Rocket's older brother, Goose—rob business owners and share the money with the community who, in turn, hide them from the police.

Li'l Dice, a young boy, convinces them to hold up a motel and rob its occupants. The gang resolves not to kill anyone and tells Li'l Dice to be a lookout. Instead, Li'l Dice guns down the motel occupants after falsely warning the trio that the police are coming. The massacre attracts so much police attention that the trio is forced to split up: Clipper joins the Church, Shaggy is shot by the police while trying to escape the ''favela'', and Goose is shot by Li'l Dice after taking his money while Li'l Dice's friend Benny, Shaggy's brother, watches.

In the 1970s, Rocket has joined a group of young [[hippie]]s. He enjoys [[photography]] and likes one girl, Angélica, but his attempt to get close to her is ruined by a gang of petty criminal kids known as "The Runts". Li'l Dice, who now calls himself "Li'l Zé", has established a drug empire with Benny by eliminating all of the competition, except for Carrot, who is a good friend of Benny's. Rocket witnesses Li'l Zé take over 'the apartment', a known drug distribution center, and forces Carrot's underboss Blacky, to work for him instead.

Because of this monopoly, a relative peace comes over the City of God under the reign of Li'l Zé, who manages to avoid police attention by executing petty criminals, including a member of The Runts. Benny decides to branch out of the drug dealer crowd and befriends Tiago, Angélica's ex-boyfriend, who introduces him to his friend group. Benny and Angélica begin dating. Together, they decide to leave the city and the drug trade.

During Benny's farewell party, Zé and Benny get into an argument about Benny leaving; the argument is interrupted by Blacky accidentally killing Benny while trying to shoot Li'l Zé. Benny's death leaves Li'l Zé unchecked. Carrot kills Blacky for endangering his life. Li'l Zé and a group of his soldiers start to make their way to Carrot's hideout to kill him.

On the way, Zé follows a girl who dismissed his advances at Benny's party. He beats up her boyfriend, a peaceful ex-soldier named Knockout Ned, and rapes her. After Ned's brother stabs Li'l Zé, his gang retaliates by shooting into his house, killing his brother and uncle in the process. A gang war breaks out between Carrot and Li'l Zé. A vengeful Ned sides with Carrot, initially trying to stay true to his ideals of only harming criminals, but he quickly loses his morals after having to shoot a policeman during a bank robbery. Tiago also is drawn into the conflict to support his drug addiction, siding with Li'l Zé.

The war is still ongoing a year later, in 1981, the origin forgotten. Both sides enlist more "soldiers" and Li'l Zé gives the Runts weapons. One day, Li'l Zé has Rocket take photos of him and his gang. A reporter publishes the photos, a significant scoop since no outsiders can safely enter the City of God anymore. Rocket believes Li'l Zé will kill him for publishing the photo of him and his gang. The reporter takes Rocket in for the night, and he loses his virginity to her. Unbeknownst to him, Li'l Zé, jealous of Ned's media fame, is pleased with the photos and with his own increased notoriety.

Rocket returns to the city for more photographs. Rocket finds himself caught between Zé's gang and the arriving police, who quickly withdraw when they realize they are outnumbered and outgunned. Rocket is surprised that Zé asks him to take pictures, but as he prepares to take the photo, Carrot's gang arrives. In the ensuing gunfight, Ned kills Tiago but is then killed by a boy who has infiltrated Carrot's gang to avenge his father, the policeman whom Ned shot during the bank robbery. The police capture Li'l Zé and Carrot and plan to show Carrot off to the media. Since Li'l Zé has been bribing the police, they take all of Li'l Zé's money and let him go, but Rocket secretly photographs the scene. The Runts meet with Zé, seeming to want to talk, but tackle him to the floor, mock him, and shoot him dozens of times in the head, back and legs in retaliation for Li'l Zé murdering their friend a year prior to set an example for the Runts previous petty crimes spree. Rocket approaches Zé's mangled body, and takes a final photograph.

Rocket contemplates whether to publish the cops' photo, expose corruption, and become famous, or the picture of Li'l Zé's dead body, which will get him an internship at the newspaper. He decides on the latter, and the Runts walk around the City of God, making a hit list of the dealers they plan to kill to take over the drug business.

"Dado" is a common nickname for Eduardo and means "[[dice]]", and "inho" is a diminutive suffix in Portuguese. As an adult, he is given the name Zé Pequeno in a [[Candomblé]] ceremony, which may be unrelated to his actual name and more to his short stature. Zé is a nickname for José, while pequeno means "little". [[Douglas Silva]] portrays Li'l Dice as a child.
* [[Phellipe Haagensen]] as Benny – Zé's longtime partner in crime, he is a friendly City of God drug dealer who fancies himself a sort of Robin Hood and eventually wants to lead an honest life. [[Michel Gomes]] portrays Benny as a child.

* [[Seu Jorge]] as Knockout Ned {{small|{{bracket|[[:pt:Mané Galinha|pt]]}}}} – A handsome, charismatic gentleman. As a veteran, he is an excellent shot and can shoot better than most "soldiers". His name was changed from "Mané Galinha" for the English subtitles because in English, "chicken", which "Galinha" translates to, is a term for a coward (in Brazil it denotes womanizing tendencies and popularity among women). "Mané" is a nickname for Manuel, but also denotes an individual not intellectually gifted.
* [[Jonathan Haagensen]] as Shaggy – Older brother of Benny and the leader of the Tender Trio ("Trio Ternura"), a group of thieves who share their profits with the population of the City of God.

* Renato de Souza as Goose – One of the Tender Trio, and Rocket's brother. He sleeps with a bartender's wife and gets kicked out by his father when the police arrive, he is killed by Li'l Dice later that day.

* Edson Oliveira as Stringy – Childhood friend of Rocket. Emerson Gomes portrays Stringy as a child.

* [[Darlan Cunha]] as Steak n' Fries – A young boy who joins Zé's gang.

* [[Charles Paraventi]] as Charles / Uncle Sam – A weapons dealer who's supplied by the police. After returning from a deal empty-handed, the police kill him.
* [[Graziella Moretto]] as Marina Cintra – A journalist for ''[[Jornal do Brasil]]'', who hires Rocket as a photographer.
* Luiz Carlos Ribeiro Seixas as Touro – An honest police officer.

==Production==
''City of God'' was filmed on [[16 mm film|16mm]] film stock.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Prince |first=Ron |title=Shooting On Film {{!}} The Changing Face Of Cinematography: Special Report |url=https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/shooting-on-film/ |access-date=2022-03-17 |website=British Cinematographer |language=en-GB}}</ref>

On the bonus DVD, it is revealed that the only professional actor in the film was [[Matheus Nachtergaele]], who played the supporting role of Carrot.<ref name="dvdcom">''City of God DVD extras''</ref> Most of the remaining cast were from real-life favelas, and in some cases, even the real-life City of God favela itself. According to Meirelles, amateur actors were used for two reasons: the lack of available professional black actors, and the desire for authenticity. Meirelles explained: "Today I can open a casting call and have 500 black actors, but just ten years ago this possibility did not exist. In Brazil, there were three or four young black actors and at the same time I felt that actors from the middle class could not make the film. I needed authenticity."<ref name="Bessa/iG">{{cite web|last=Bessa|first=Priscila|date=4 June 2012|title=Dez anos depois, diretor de Cidade de Deus diz ter prejuízo de R$4 milhões|url=http://ultimosegundo.ig.com.br/cultura/cinema/2012-06-04/dez-anos-depois-diretor-de-cidade-de-deus-diz-ter-prejuizo-de-r.html|url-status=live|access-date=9 December 2012|work=Último Segundo|publisher=[[Internet Group]]|archive-date=7 June 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120607210447/http://ultimosegundo.ig.com.br/cultura/cinema/2012-06-04/dez-anos-depois-diretor-de-cidade-de-deus-diz-ter-prejuizo-de-r.html}}</ref>

Beginning around 2000, about a hundred children and young people were hand-picked and placed into an "actors' workshop" for several months. In contrast to more traditional methods (e.g. studying theatre and rehearsing), it focused on simulating authentic street war scenes, such as a hold-up, scuffle, and shoot-out. Much came from [[improvisation]], as it was thought better to create an authentic, gritty atmosphere. This way, the inexperienced cast soon learned to move and act naturally. After filming, the crew could not leave the cast to return to their old lives in the favelas. Assistance groups were set up to help those involved in the production to build more promising futures.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last=Vieira|first=Else Ribeiro Pires|title=City of God in Several Voices: Brazilian Social Cinema as Action|date=2005|publisher=Critical, Cultural and Communication Press|others=Else Ribeiro Pires Vieira|isbn=1-905510-00-4|location=Nottingham, England|oclc=62368025}}</ref>{{rp|135–139}}

Meirelles went into the film with the intention of staying true to the "casual nature" of the violence in [[City of God (Lins novel)|the novel by Lins]]. Critic Jean Oppenheimer wrote on the production of the film saying that: "A second guiding principle was to avoid glamorising the violence" and that "many of the killings are either shown indistinctly or kept out of frame."<ref name=":0" />{{rp|26–32}}

Because the real Cidade de Deus favela was in the middle of a conflict, a large majority of the film was shot in Cidade Alta, a different favela within Rio. During the production, slumlords did not allow for the production company to have their own security, so local security guards were hired for the safety of the set.<ref>{{Cite news|last1=Watts|first1=Jonathan|last2=Vieira|first2=Karina|last3=Kaiser|first3=Anna|date=9 June 2014|title=How we made City of God|work=[[The Guardian]]|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/jun/09/how-we-made-city-of-god|access-date=8 April 2021|archive-date=9 January 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190109010350/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2014/jun/09/how-we-made-city-of-god|url-status=live}}</ref>

Lund and Meirelles filmed the [[short film]] ''Golden Gate'' as a test run<ref name=dvdcom/> while casting for ''City of God'' was in the initial stages.<ref>{{cite web|last=Kuykendall|first=Niija|date=2 May 2003|title=We in Cinema: City of God Benefit|url=http://www.blackfilm.com/20030502/features/cityofgodbenefit.shtml|url-status=live|access-date=2019-12-31|website=BlackFilm.com|archive-date=31 December 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191231171751/http://www.blackfilm.com/20030502/features/cityofgodbenefit.shtml}}</ref>

==Reception==

===Box office===
The film was screened out of competition at the [[2002 Cannes Film Festival]].<ref name="festival-cannes.com">{{cite web|title=Cidade de Deus|url=http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/3162008/year/2002.html|url-status=dead|access-date=1 November 2009|work=[[Festival de Cannes]]|archive-date=10 October 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121010001505/http://www.festival-cannes.com/en/archives/ficheFilm/id/3162008/year/2002.html}}</ref> The film could not be part of the official selection because [[Walter Salles]], one of the executive producers, was in the festival jury.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/interviews/city-god-phenomenon-new-interview-with-fernando-meirelles|title=The City of God phenomenon: a new interview with Fernando Meirelles|website=[[British Film Institute]]|date=23 February 2024|access-date=17 October 2024}}</ref> In Brazil, ''City of God'' garnered the largest audience for a domestic film in 2002, with over 3.1&nbsp;million tickets sold, and a gross of [[Brazilian real|R$]]18.6&nbsp;million ($10.3&nbsp;million).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.filmeb.com.br/informe/269/n269.pdf|title=Informe 269|publisher=Filme B|language=pt|access-date=21 April 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110706152444/http://www.filmeb.com.br/informe/269/n269.pdf|archive-date=6 July 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> The film grossed over $7.5 million in the U.S. and over US$30.5 million worldwide.<ref>[http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=cityofgod.htm ''City of God''] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171018152832/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=cityofgod.htm |date=18 October 2017 }} at [[Box Office Mojo]].</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''City of God'' has an approval rating of 91% based on reviews from 162 critics, with an average rating of 8.6/10. The website's consensus reads, "''City of God'' offers a shocking and disturbing—but always compelling—look at life in the slums of Rio de Janeiro."<ref>{{Cite web|title=City of God (2002)|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/city_of_god|url-status=live|access-date=9 February 2023|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|archive-date=9 March 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210309040348/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/city_of_god}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film holds a score of 79 out of 100 based on 33 critic reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|title=City of God|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/city-of-god|url-status=live|access-date=8 April 2021|website=[[Metacritic]]|archive-date=8 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210408105018/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/city-of-god}}</ref>

Colin Kennedy from ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' awarded the film a full 5 out of 5 stars, comparing it favorably to ''[[Goodfellas]]'' and writing in his review, "At once a laboratory for cinema technique and a victory for raw heart, this is a snot-nosed, blood-stained masterpiece. If you see even one or two better movies this year, you will be very lucky indeed."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Kennedy|first=Colin|date=1 January 2000|title=City Of God Review|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/city-god-review/|url-status=live|access-date=2021-04-08|website=[[Empire (film magazine)|Empire]]|publisher=[[Bauer Media Group]]|archive-date=8 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210408134552/https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/city-god-review/}}</ref> In 2008, the magazine chose ''City of God'' as the 177th-best film of all time.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Green|first=Willow|date=3 October 2008|title=The 500 Greatest Movies Of All Time|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/features/500-greatest-movies/|url-status=live|access-date=8 April 2021|website=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]|publisher=[[Bauer Media Group]]|archive-date=10 March 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120310180609/http://www.empireonline.com/500/91.asp}}</ref>

[[Film critic]] [[Roger Ebert]] awarded the film 4 stars out of 4, writing in his review, "''City of God'' churns with furious energy as it plunges into the story of the slum gangs of Rio de Janeiro. Breathtaking and terrifying, urgently involved with its characters, it announces a new director of great gifts and passions: Fernando Meirelles. Remember the name."<ref>{{cite news|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|date=24 January 2003|title=City of God (2002)|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/20030124/REVIEWS/301240301/1023|url-status=dead|access-date=28 April 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121008033157/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F20030124%2FREVIEWS%2F301240301%2F1023|archive-date=8 October 2012}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|date=24 January 2003|title=City of God|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/city-of-god-2003|url-status=live|access-date=8 April 2021|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|archive-date=12 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210112174107/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/city-of-god-2003}}</ref>

Filmmaker [[Robert Altman]] stated, "I don't know how Fernando Meirelles made ''City of God''. It's so courageous, so truthful. I think it's the best picture I've ever seen".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.gamesradar.com/the-total-film-interview-robert-altman/|title=The Total Film Interview - Robert Altman|date=1 June 2004}}</ref> Meirelles himself cited Altman's work as an influence on his own career.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://nitrateonline.com/2004/fcityofgod.html|title = City of God - Interview with Fernando Meirelles|date=2004|website=Nitrate Online Feature|last=Gorringe|first=Carrie}}</ref>

The film was not without criticism. Peter Rainer of ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' magazine stated that while the film was "powerful", it was also "rather numbing".<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Rainer|first=Peter|date=8 January 2003|title=Gangs of Rio|url=https://nymag.com/nymetro/movies/reviews/n_8237/|url-status=live|access-date=2021-04-08|magazine=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|archive-date=22 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200922051414/https://nymag.com/nymetro/movies/reviews/n_8237/}}</ref> [[John Powers (film critic)|John Powers]] of ''[[LA Weekly]]'' wrote that although "[the film] whirs with energy for nearly its full 130-minute running time, it is oddly lacking in emotional heft for a work that aspires to be so epic—it is essentially a tarted up exploitation picture whose business is to make ghastly things fun".<ref>{{Cite news|last=Powers|first=John|date=16 January 2003|title=Straight Outta Ipanema|work=[[LA Weekly]]|url=https://www.laweekly.com/straight-outta-ipanema/|access-date=8 April 2021|archive-date=8 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210408124831/https://www.laweekly.com/straight-outta-ipanema/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Ivana Bentes, a Brazilian film critic, criticised the film for its depiction of the favela and her view that it glorified issues of poverty and violence as means of "domestication of the most radical themes of [[Culture of Brazil|culture]] and [[Cinema of Brazil|Brazilian cinema]]&nbsp;... as products for export."<ref>{{Cite book|last=Bentes|first=Ivana|title=The New Brazilian Cinema|date=2003|publisher=[[I.B. Tauris]]|isbn=1-4175-1979-7|editor-last=Nagib|editor-first=Lúcia|location=London|pages=121–136|oclc=55808559}}</ref> Bentes targets the film specifically in saying that: "''City of God'' promotes tourism in hell".<ref name="Freire">{{cite journal|last=Freire-Medeiros|first=Bianca|date=9 May 2011|title='I went to the City of God': Gringos, guns and the touristic favela|url=http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/13569325.2011.562631|journal=Journal of Latin American Cultural Studies|language=en|volume=20|issue=1|page=25|doi=10.1080/13569325.2011.562631|issn=1356-9325|s2cid=194097832|url-access=subscription}}</ref>

Brazilian rapper [[MV Bill]], a resident of [[Cidade de Deus, Rio de Janeiro|Cidade de Deus]], said the film had "brought no good to the favela, no social, moral, or human benefit."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Setti|first=Ana|date=9 February 2003|title=REALIDADE E FICÇÃO Cidade de Deus sofre com o estigma da violência|url=https://www.folhadelondrina.com.br/folha-2/realidade-e-ficcao-cidade-de-deus-sofre-com-o-estigma-da-violencia-434340.html|url-access=subscription|url-status=live|access-date=2020-02-23|website=Folha de Londrina|language=pt|archive-date=23 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200223073508/https://www.folhadelondrina.com.br/folha-2/realidade-e-ficcao-cidade-de-deus-sofre-com-o-estigma-da-violencia-434340.html}}</ref> He said, "The world will know that they exploited the image of the children who live here in Cidade de Deus. What is obvious is that they are going to carry a bigger stigma throughout their lives; it has only become greater because of the film."<ref name=":0" />{{rp|123}}

==Legacy==
In an interview with ''[[Slant Magazine]]'', Meirelles states he had met with Brazil's former and current president [[Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva]] who told him about the impact the film has had on both policies and public security within the country. The film has also sparked major increase in film productions, with over 45 being done during 2002. Films such as [[The Motorcycle Diaries (film)|''The Motorcycle Diaries'']] and ''The Intruder<!--By Berto Brechi, about whom I can find nothing-->'' are some of the films which have used Brazil for film production.<ref name="Gonzalez">{{cite web|last=Gonzalez|first=Ed|date=27 August 2003|title=Interview: Fernando Meirelles Talks City of God|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/city-of-gods-an-interview-with-fernando-meirelles/|url-status=live|access-date=15 February 2019|website=[[Slant Magazine]]|archive-date=23 June 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200623203331/https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/city-of-gods-an-interview-with-fernando-meirelles/}}</ref>

The 2013 documentary ''[[City of God – 10 Years Later]]'' reunites the cast and crew of ''City of God'' and takes a look at how their lives have changed after the original film's release. In a ''[[BBC]]'' article written at the time of the documentary's release, Firmino mentions that the cast had mixed careers after the film's release. Firmino says that Jefechander Suplino, who played Clipper, could not be found by the documentary producers. His mother, however, believes him to still be alive, but is unaware of his whereabouts. [[Seu Jorge]], who played Knockout Ned, had a better career after the film and became a major musician, performing at the [[2012 Summer Olympics closing ceremony|London 2012 Olympic Games closing ceremony]].<ref name="Bowater">{{cite news|last=Bowater|first=Donna|date=6 August 2013|title=City of God, 10 years on|work=[[BBC News]]|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-23333368|url-status=live|access-date=15 February 2019|archive-date=13 January 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190113184205/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-23333368}}</ref>

''[[City of God: The Fight Rages On]]'', a miniseries set two decades after the events of the film, premiered in August 2024.<ref>{{cite press release|url=https://press.wbd.com/us/media-release/new-hbo-original-series-city-god-fight-rages-premieres-august-max|title=New HBO Original Series CITY OF GOD: THE FIGHT RAGES ON Premieres This August On Max|location=São Paulo|publisher=[[Warner Bros. Discovery]]|date=28 May 2024}}</ref>

==Home media==
===North America===
The film's international distributor [[Miramax]] released the film on DVD in the United States on June 8, 2004.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.avforums.com/reviews/city-of-god-dvd-review.2258/|title=City Of God DVD Review|date=June 1, 2004|website=AVForums|last=Hinton|first=Phil}}</ref>

In 2010, Miramax was sold by [[The Walt Disney Company|Disney]] (their owners since 1993), with the studio being taken over by private equity firm [[Filmyard Holdings]] that same year.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/business/2010/jul/30/film-industry-privateequity|title=Disney sells Miramax to investment group for $660m|first=David|last=Teather|work=The Guardian |date=July 30, 2010}}</ref> Filmyard sublicensed the home video rights for several Miramax titles to [[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]], who released ''City of God'' on [[Blu-ray]] on December 13, 2011.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/52606/city-of-god/|title=DVD Talk|website=dvdtalk.com}}</ref>

Filmyard sold Miramax to Qatari company [[beIN Media Group]] during March 2016.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2016/mar/02/bein-media-group-miramax-film-studio-acquisition|title=Iconic film studio Miramax sells to Doha-based beIN Media Group|first=Nigel M.|last=Smith|work=The Guardian |date=March 2, 2016}}</ref> In April 2020, [[ViacomCBS]] (now known as [[Paramount Skydance]]) acquired the rights to Miramax's library, after buying a 49% stake in the studio from beIN.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/viacomcbs-closes-acquisition-miramax-stake-375-million-deal-1286478/|title=ViacomCBS Closes Acquisition of 49 Percent Miramax Stake in $375 Million Deal|first=Georg|last=Szalai|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=April 3, 2020}}</ref> The film was made available on their streaming service [[Paramount+]],<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.paramountplus.com/movies/video/3uvC1zV2_j4xJ__Oa2guWHABX_GmTxoF/|title=City of God - Watch Full Movie on Paramount Plus|date=June 7, 2002|website=paramountplus.com}}</ref> and in 2021, [[Paramount Home Entertainment]] reissued ''City of God'' on Blu-ray, along with many other Miramax titles.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=28707|title=Paramount to Reissue Select Miramax Titles on Blu-ray (UPDATED)|website=blu-ray.com}}</ref> Paramount Home Entertainment also issued the film on a double-feature Blu-ray with ''[[City of Men (film)|City of Men]]'', another Miramax title they had acquired in 2020.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com.au/City-God-2-Movie-Collection-Blu-ray/dp/B093RPHWP4/ |title=City of God / City of Men 2-Movie Collection [Blu-ray]}}</ref>
