'''''Footloose''''' is a 1984 American [[musical film|musical]]<ref>{{Cite web|title=Footloose (1984) |url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/footloose-v62614 |website=[[AllMovie]] |access-date=January 11, 2023}}</ref> [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] directed by [[Herbert Ross]] and written by [[Dean Pitchford]]. It tells the story of Ren McCormack ([[Kevin Bacon]]), a teenager from Chicago who moves to a small town, where he attempts to overturn a ban on dancing enforced by the efforts of a local minister ([[John Lithgow]]).

The film was released on February 17, 1984, by [[Paramount Pictures]], and received mixed reviews from critics, but was a box office success, grossing $80 million in North America, becoming the [[1984 in film|seventh highest-grossing film of 1984]]. The songs "[[Footloose (song)|Footloose]]" by [[Kenny Loggins]] and "[[Let's Hear It for the Boy]]" by [[Deniece Williams]] were nominated for the [[Academy Award for Best Original Song]].

==Plot==
<!-- Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]] plot summaries are to be 400-700 words. -->
[[Chicago]] natives Ren McCormack and his mother Ethel move to the small town of Bomont, [[Utah]], to live with Ren's aunt and uncle. At church he meets the strict Reverend Shaw Moore, his wife Vi, and their carefree and rebellious daredevil daughter Ariel.

At school Ren befriends Willard Hewitt. He learns that the town council has banned dancing and rock music within the [[City limits|town boundary]], though Reverend Moore's influence stretches beyond Bomont. He even gets fined by the police for having his music up too loud. Ren falls for Ariel, angering her boyfriend Chuck Cranston, who challenges Ren to a game of [[chicken (game)|chicken]] involving tractors; Ren wins when his shoelace gets caught on the tractor's pedal, preventing him from jumping away.

Seeing Ren as a bad influence, Reverend Moore forbids Ariel from seeing Ren. Across state lines, Ren takes Willard, Ariel, and her friend Rusty to a [[honky-tonk]]. Willard is embarrassed he cannot dance and upon seeing Rusty dancing with a stranger, he winds up getting in a fight; the group then leaves the bar.

On the drive home, Ariel describes how, five years earlier, her older brother died in a car accident after a night of alcohol and dancing. Their father then persuaded the [[town council]] to enact strict [[Temperance movement|anti-liquor]], anti-drug, and anti-dance laws. Ren decides to challenge the dancing and rock music ban so the high school can hold a [[Prom|senior prom]].

Ren teaches Willard to dance so Willard can impress Rusty. Chuck confronts Ariel about her feelings for Ren and they get into a physical altercation before breaking up. Ren helps Ariel conceal the physical assault, and Ariel gifts Ren a [[music box]]. They kiss, cementing their relationship.

Someone throws a brick through Ren's window with the words, "Burn in Hell". When his uncle criticizes Ren's outspoken behavior, Ethel tells Ren that though his actions cost her her job, he should stand up for what he believes is right.

With Ariel's help, Ren goes before the town council to advocate revoking the anti-dancing law. He reads [[Bible]] verses and cites the scriptural significance of dancing to rejoice, exercise, and celebrate. Though Reverend Moore is moved, the council ultimately votes against Ren. Vi supports Ren and tells Shaw that he cannot be everyone's father and that he is hardly one to Ariel.

Despite discussing with Ren his own family losses and Ariel telling her father she is not a virgin, Reverend Moore does not change his stance. He finds members of his congregation [[book burning|burning library books]] that they claim endanger the town's youth. Moore chastises the book burners and sends them home.

That Sunday, Reverend Moore asks his congregation to pray for the students putting on the prom in a [[Gristmill|grain mill]] just over the county line beyond Bomont's jurisdiction, thereby signifying his consent for the dance, to the students’ delight. 

On prom night, Moore and Vi listen outside the mill. The high school students, sitting on benches as the music plays, finally begin dancing when Ren and Ariel arrive and take the dance floor. Chuck and his friends arrive to ruin the prom and attack Ren. They begin assaulting Willard, who only starts fighting after his date Rusty’s consent. Ren joins the fight to help Willard and knocks Chuck unconscious. Ren, Ariel, Willard and Rusty rejoin the prom and dance the night away.

==Production==
[[Dean Pitchford]] came up with the idea for ''Footloose'' in 1979 and teamed up with [[Daniel Melnick]]'s IndieProd who set the production up at [[20th Century Fox]] in 1981.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Daily Variety]]|page=1|title=Cimino and Melnick Working Together on 'Footloose'|last=Ginsberg|first=Steven}}</ref><ref name=AFI>{{AFI film|57086}}</ref> Pitchford wrote the screenplay (his first) and most of the lyrics. However, Fox put it into [[turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]].<ref name=AFI/> In 1982, [[Paramount Pictures]] made a [[pay-or-play]] deal for the film.<ref name=AFI/> When negotiations with [[Herbert Ross]] initially stalled, [[Ron Howard]] was approached to direct the film but he turned it down to direct ''[[Splash (film)|Splash]]'' instead.<ref>{{Cite web | url=https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/76738/15-surprising-facts-about-splash | title=15 Surprising Facts About Splash | date=March 16, 2016|first=Roger |last=Cormier  |website=[[Mental Floss]] |access-date=September 10, 2025}}</ref> [[Michael Cimino]] was hired by Paramount to direct the film, his first film since ''[[Heaven's Gate (film)|Heaven's Gate]]''.<ref name=AFI/>

After a month of working on the film, the studio fired Cimino, who was making extravagant demands for the production, including demanding an additional $250,000 for his work, and ended up hiring Ross.<ref name=AFI/><ref name=bom>{{cite web |first=Scott |last=Holleran |date=October 12, 2004 |title=Shall We Footloose? |url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/features/?id=1510&p=.htm |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |access-date=September 10, 2025 |archive-date=April 15, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090415182633/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/features/?id=1510&p=.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref>

===Casting===
[[Tom Cruise]] and [[Rob Lowe]] were both slated to play the lead. The producers were impressed with Cruise because of the famous underwear dance sequence in ''[[Risky Business]]'', but he was unavailable for the part because he was filming ''[[All the Right Moves (film)|All the Right Moves]]''. Lowe auditioned three times and had the dancing ability and the "neutral teen" look that the director wanted, but injury prevented him from taking the part.<ref name=Lowe>{{cite web|last=Wenn|title=Rob Lowe: 'I refused to sing Footloose karaoke duet with Loggins|url=http://www.hollywood.com/news/brief/48288286/rob-lowe-i-refused-to-sing-footloose-karaoke-duet-with-loggins|work=Hollywood.com|access-date=March 11, 2014|date=January 16, 2013|quote=Years ago I auditioned for Footloose and I blew out my ACL (anterior cruciate ligament), so I have post-traumatic stress with anything having to do with Footloose.|archive-date=March 11, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140311075821/http://www.hollywood.com/news/brief/48288286/rob-lowe-i-refused-to-sing-footloose-karaoke-duet-with-loggins|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | title=20 Actors Who Turned Down Big Roles | url=https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/20-actors-who-turned-down-big-roles|first=Benjamin |last=Hurston |date=July 16, 2013|access-date=September 10, 2025|work=[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]]}}</ref> [[Christopher Atkins]] claims that he was cast as Ren, but lost the role.<ref>{{Cite web | url=http://www.upi.com/Entertainment_News/2009/01/26/Atkins-I-almost-starred-in-Footloose/62571233008228/ | title=Atkins: I almost starred in 'Footloose'|work=United Press International |access-date=September 10, 2025|date=January 26, 2009}}</ref> Bacon had been offered the main role for the [[Stephen King]] film ''[[Christine (1983 film)|Christine]]'' at the same time that he was asked to do the screen test for ''Footloose''. He chose to take the gamble on the screen test. After watching his earlier film ''[[Diner (1982 film)|Diner]]'', the director persuaded the producers to go with Bacon.

The film also stars [[Lori Singer]] as Reverend Moore's independent daughter Ariel, a role for which [[Madonna (entertainer)|Madonna]] and [[Haviland Morris]]<ref>{{cite magazine| url = https://ew.com/article/2011/09/13/footloose-2/| title = Footloose | magazine = [[Entertainment Weekly]]|first=Chris |last=Nashawaty |date=September 13, 2011|access-date=September 10, 2025}}</ref> also auditioned. [[Valerie Bertinelli]] and [[Jennifer Jason Leigh]] were also considered.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.etonline.com/valerie-bertinelli-reveals-why-she-was-passed-over-footloose-role-and-its-pretty-good-reason-89507 | title=Valerie Bertinelli Reveals Why She Was Passed over for 'Footloose' Role -- and It's a Pretty Good Reason | date=October 19, 2017|first=Jackie|last=Willis |access-date=September 10, 2025|work=[[Entertainment Tonight]]}}</ref> [[Dianne Wiest]] appears as Vi, the Reverend's devoted yet conflicted wife.

[[Tracy Nelson (actress)|Tracy Nelson]] was considered for the role of Rusty.<ref name=bom/>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] began on May 9, 1983, and took place at various locations in [[Utah County, Utah]].<ref name="AFI"/>  The high school and tractor scenes were filmed in and around [[Payson, Utah|Payson]] and [[Nebo School District#Payson High School|Payson High School]]. The church scenes were filmed at the First Presbyterian Church in [[American Fork, Utah|American Fork]],<ref>{{cite web | url=https://jacobbarlow.com/2019/05/25/bomont-first-christian-church-footloose/ | title=Bomont First Christian Church – Footloose | date=May 25, 2019 }}</ref> while the steel mill was the [[Geneva Steel]] facility in [[Vineyard, Utah|Vineyard]]<ref>{{cite web | url=https://jacobbarlow.com/2019/06/30/steel-mill-footloose-filming-location/ | title=Steel Mill – Footloose Filming Location | date=June 30, 2019 }}</ref> and The [[Lehi Roller Mills]] were the location where Bacon's character worked (Bacon briefly worked at the roller mill as research for his performance).<ref>{{Cite web|last=Hyde|first=Jesse|date=October 23, 2004|title=Flour mill grows up after 'Footloose'|url=https://www.deseret.com/2004/9/23/19851729/flour-mill-grows-up-after-footloose|website=Deseret News}}</ref> The drive-in scenes were filmed in [[Provo, Utah|Provo]] at what was then a branch of the Hi-Spot burger chain.<ref name="jacobbarlow.com">{{cite web | url=https://jacobbarlow.com/2019/06/20/the-hi-spot-footloose-filming-location/ | title=The Hi Spot – Footloose Filming Location | date=June 20, 2019 }}</ref> The restaurant chain closed in the late 1980s, and there is now an auto parts store at that location. The bar scene was filmed at The Silver Spur bar in downtown Provo. The bar and other surrounding buildings were demolished to make way for the Utah Valley Convention Center.<ref name="jacobbarlow.com"/>

For his dance scene in the warehouse, Bacon said he had four [[stunt double]]s: "I had a stunt double, a dance double [Peter Tramm]<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.people.com/people/archive/article/0,,20087493,00.html | title=Hoofers Hidden in the Shadows Dream of the Limelight | date=2 April 1984 | work=[[People (magazine)|People]] | publisher=Time Inc. | access-date=15 December 2011 | archive-date=10 March 2011 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110310161855/http://www.people.com/people/archive/article/0,,20087493,00.html | url-status=live }}</ref> and two gymnastics doubles."<ref name="Jones-10-14-2011">{{cite web | last=Jones | first=Oliver | title=Kevin Bacon 'Furious' over Having a Dance Double in Footloose | url=http://www.people.com/people/article/0,,20536704,00.html | work=[[People (magazine)|People]] | date=October 14, 2011 | publisher=Time Inc. | access-date=October 18, 2011 | archive-date=October 16, 2011 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111016190450/http://www.people.com/people/article/0,,20536704,00.html | url-status=dead }}</ref>

=== Film inspiration ===
''Footloose'' is loosely based on the town of [[Elmore City, Oklahoma]]. The town had banned dancing since its founding in 1898 in an attempt to decrease the amount of heavy drinking. One advocate of the dancing ban was the Reverend from the nearby town of [[Hennepin, Oklahoma|Hennepin]], F. R. Johnson. He said, "No good has ever come from a dance. If you have a dance somebody will crash it and they'll be looking for only two things—women and booze. When boys and girls hold each other, they get sexually aroused. You can believe what you want, but one thing leads to another." Because of the ban on dancing, the town never held a prom. In February 1980, the junior class of Elmore City's high school made national news when they requested permission to hold a junior prom and it was granted. The request to overturn the ban to hold the prom was met with a 2–2 decision from the school board when school board president Raymond Lee broke the tie with the words, "Let 'em dance."<ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.405magazine.com/June-2015/Dance-Fever-The-Town-That-Inspired-and-Got-Footloose/ |title=Dance Fever: The Town That Inspired (and Got) Footloose Oklahoma City |website=405 Magazine | date=June 2015 |orig-year=May 26, 2015 (Web publication)  |access-date=May 14, 2019|archive-date=May 2, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190502191807/https://www.405magazine.com//June-2015/Dance-Fever-The-Town-That-Inspired-and-Got-Footloose|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{Main|Footloose (1984 soundtrack){{!}}''Footloose'' (1984 soundtrack)}}
The soundtrack was released in [[Compact Cassette|cassette]], [[8-track tape]], [[Gramophone record|vinyl]], [[Reel-to-reel audio tape recording|reel-to-reel]] and CD format. The 1984 open reel release was among the last commercial releases on the format. The soundtrack was also re-released on CD for the 15th anniversary of the film in 1999. The re-release included four new songs: "[[Metal Health (song)|Bang Your Head (Metal Health)]]" by [[Quiet Riot]], "[[Hurts So Good]]" by [[John Mellencamp]], "[[Waiting for a Girl Like You]]" by [[Foreigner (band)|Foreigner]], and the extended 12" remix of "Dancing in the Sheets".

The album includes "[[Footloose (song)|Footloose]]" and "[[I'm Free (Heaven Helps the Man)]]", both by [[Kenny Loggins]], "[[Holding Out for a Hero]]" by [[Bonnie Tyler]] (co-written and produced by [[Jim Steinman]]), "Girl Gets Around" by [[Sammy Hagar]], "[[Never (Moving Pictures song)|Never]]" by Australian rock band [[Moving Pictures (band)|Moving Pictures]], "[[Let's Hear It for the Boy]]" by [[Deniece Williams]], "[[Somebody's Eyes]]" by [[Karla Bonoff]], "[[Dancing In The Sheets]]" by [[Shalamar]], and the romantic theme "[[Almost Paradise (song)|Almost Paradise]]" by Mike Reno from [[Loverboy]] and [[Ann Wilson]] of [[Heart (band)|Heart]] (co-written by [[Eric Carmen]]). The soundtrack went on to sell over 10 million copies in the USA. All songs in the initial release were co-written by Pitchford based on various songwriting styles: for "Holding Out for a Hero", he listened to various songs written by Steinman such as his work with [[Meat Loaf]] and then wrote the first two lines ("Where have all the good men gone/And where are all the gods?/Where's the streetwise Hercules/To fight the rising odds?") in this manner to spark Steinman's creativity.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Wiser |first1=Carl |title=Dean Pitchford : Songwriter Interviews |url=https://www.songfacts.com/blog/interviews/dean-pitchford |website=Songfacts |access-date=July 4, 2022 |date=March 13, 2012}}</ref>

"Footloose" and "Let's Hear It for the Boy" both topped the [[Billboard Hot 100|''Billboard'' Hot 100]] and received [[57th Academy Awards|1984 Academy Award]] nominations for [[Academy Award for Best Original Song#1980s|Best Music (Original Song)]].{{citation needed|date=February 2024}} "Footloose" also received a 1985 [[Golden Globe Award]] nomination for Best Original Song – Motion Picture.{{citation needed|date=February 2024}}

Composer [[Miles Goodman]] adapted and orchestrated the film's score.<ref>{{cite news|title=Miles Goodman, 47, Composer for Films|date=August 20, 1996|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1996/08/20/arts/miles-goodman-47-composer-for-films.html|access-date=March 25, 2015|archive-date=February 21, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150221075229/http://www.nytimes.com/1996/08/20/arts/miles-goodman-47-composer-for-films.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Oliver|first=Myrna|title=Miles Goodman; Record Producer, Film Composer|date=August 20, 1996|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-08-20-me-35989-story.html|access-date=March 25, 2015|archive-date=February 21, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150221092601/http://articles.latimes.com/1996-08-20/local/me-35989_1_goodman-s-co-producer-reclusive-composer-miles-goodman|url-status=live}}</ref>

The music from the soundtrack was released prior to the film's premiere. The filmmakers felt that songs produced a stronger emotional response from people already familiar with them, which heightened the experience of watching the movie. The music video for "Footloose" had scenes from the movie, rather than footage of Loggins.<ref>The DVD commentary</ref>

According to Bacon, whenever he attends at weddings, the DJs would play "Footloose" and people would start clapping for him like "a trained monkey". He would request DJs to not play the song so he would not look like one.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2025/film/news/kevin-bacon-footloose-song-dance-1236331560/|title=Kevin Bacon Asks DJs Not to Play 'Footloose' Song at Weddings: People 'Form a Circle' Around Me 'Like I'm a Trained Monkey'|first=Emily|last=Longeretta|website=Variety.com|date=March 7, 2025|access-date=May 13, 2026}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
The film received mixed reviews from critics. ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' critic [[Roger Ebert]] called it "a seriously confused movie that tries to do three things, and does all of them badly. It wants to tell the story of a conflict in a town, it wants to introduce some flashy teenage characters and part of the time it wants to be a music video."<ref>{{cite news | date = January 1, 1984 | author = Roger Ebert | author-link = Roger Ebert | title = Footloose | url = https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/footloose-1984 | work = [[Chicago Sun-Times]] | access-date = September 7, 2019 | archive-date = May 3, 2021 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210503010700/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/footloose-1984 | url-status = live }}</ref> Dave Denby in ''[[New York (magazine)|New York]]'' rechristened the film "Schlockdance", writing: "''Footloose'' may be a hit, but it's trash – high powered fodder for the teen market... The only person to come out of the film better off is the smooth-cheeked, pug-nosed Bacon, who gives a cocky but likable Mr. Cool performance."<ref>{{cite news | last=Denby | first=David | title=Schlockdance | work=[[New York (magazine)|New York]] | date=February 27, 1984 | volume=17 | issue=9 | pages=60 | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=heUCAAAAMBAJ&q=kevin+bacon+footloose+dance&pg=PA60 | access-date=October 18, 2011 | archive-date=May 3, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210503010732/https://books.google.com/books?id=heUCAAAAMBAJ&q=kevin+bacon+footloose+dance&pg=PA60 | url-status=live }}</ref>

Jane Lamacraft reassessed the film for ''[[Sight and Sound]]''{{'s}} "Forgotten pleasures of the multiplex" feature in 2010, writing "Nearly three decades on, Bacon's vest-clad set-piece dance in a flour mill looks cheesily 1980s, but the rest of Ross's drama wears its age well, real song-and-dance joy for the pre-''[[Glee (TV series)|Glee]]'' generation."<ref>{{cite web | last=Lamacraft | first=Jane | title=Forgotten treasures of the multiplex| url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/forgotten-treasures-multiplex-overlooked-mainstream-movies | work=[[Sight & Sound]] | publisher=British Film Institute|date=August 28, 2020 | access-date=September 10, 2025}}</ref>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|55|5.8|47|consensus=There's not much dancing, but what's there is great. The rest of the time, ''Footloose'' is a nice hunk of trashy teenage cheese.}}<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=1007657-footloose |type=movie |title=Footloose |access-date=February 14, 2024}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 42 out of 100 based on 12 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.metacritic.com/movie/footloose-1984 |title= Footloose (1984) Reviews |website= [[Metacritic]] |publisher= [[CBS Interactive]] |access-date= April 29, 2018 |archive-date= October 13, 2018 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181013192453/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/footloose-1984 |url-status= live }}</ref>

==Musical adaptation==
{{Main|Footloose (musical)}}
In 1998, a [[Footloose (musical)|musical version]] of ''Footloose'' premiered.<ref>{{cite book|last=Willis|first=John|title=Theatre World 1998-1999|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=X1K_2uvK--IC&pg=PA20|date=June 1, 2002|publisher=Hal Leonard Corporation|isbn=978-1-55783-432-4|page=20|access-date=October 28, 2016|archive-date=March 21, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170321025314/https://books.google.com/books?id=X1K_2uvK--IC&pg=PA20|url-status=live}}</ref> Featuring many of the songs from the film, the show has been presented on London's [[West End theatre|West End]], on [[Broadway theater|Broadway]], and elsewhere. The musical is generally faithful to the film version, with some slight differences in the story and characters.

==Remake==
{{main|Footloose (2011 film)}}
Paramount announced plans to fast-track a [[remake]] of ''Footloose''. The remake was written and directed by [[Craig Brewer]]. Filming started in September 2010. It was budgeted at $25 million.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.commercialappeal.com/news/2010/apr/24/footloose-runs-off-with-well-heeled-suitor/|title='Footloose' runs off with well-heeled suitor: Georgia|author=John Beifuss|work=MCA|access-date=April 25, 2010|archive-date=April 26, 2010|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100426234518/http://www.commercialappeal.com/news/2010/apr/24/footloose-runs-off-with-well-heeled-suitor/|url-status=live}}</ref> It was released October 14, 2011.
