'''''Lust, Caution''''' ({{lang-zh|t=色，戒|p=Sè, Jiè|j=Sik<sup>1</sup>Gaai<sup>3</sup>}}) is a 2007 [[erotic film|erotic]] [[Spy film|spy]] [[mystery film|film]]<ref>{{Citation|title=Lust, Caution (2007) - Ang Lee {{!}} Synopsis, Characteristics, Moods, Themes and Related {{!}} AllMovie|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/lust-caution-v351200|language=en-us|access-date=2020-04-20}}</ref> directed by [[Ang Lee]], based on [[Lust, Caution (novella)|the 1979 novella]] by [[Eileen Chang]]. The story is set during [[World War II]], when a group of Chinese students plot to assassinate a high-ranking official in the [[Wang Jingwei regime|puppet government]] of Japanese-occupied Shanghai by luring him into a [[Honey trapping|honey trap]]. 

The film won Lee a second [[Golden Lion]] at the [[Venice Film Festival]] after ''[[Brokeback Mountain]]''.<ref>[http://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/history/awards1.html?back=true The awards of the Venice Film Festival on the Festival's site]</ref> It propelled [[Tang Wei]] to stardom but also drew controversy for its explicit sex scenes, which resulted in a two-year ban imposed on her by the Chinese government.<ref>[https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E5%BA%8A%E6%88%B2%E9%83%BD%E4%BE%86%E7%9C%9F%E7%9A%84-%E8%89%B2%E6%88%92-%E5%A5%AA21%E4%B8%96%E7%B4%8020%E5%A4%A7%E5%9C%8B%E7%89%87%E5%86%A0%E8%BB%8D-%E6%9D%8E%E5%AE%89-%E7%94%A8%E6%83%85%E6%9C%80%E6%B7%B1-042240867.html The bed scenes are real! "Lust and Caution" wins the championship of the 21st century's top 20 films! Ang Lee "uses the deepest love" and dares not remember (in Chinese)] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220807221844/https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E5%BA%8A%E6%88%B2%E9%83%BD%E4%BE%86%E7%9C%9F%E7%9A%84-%E8%89%B2%E6%88%92-%E5%A5%AA21%E4%B8%96%E7%B4%8020%E5%A4%A7%E5%9C%8B%E7%89%87%E5%86%A0%E8%BB%8D-%E6%9D%8E%E5%AE%89-%E7%94%A8%E6%83%85%E6%9C%80%E6%B7%B1-042240867.html |date=7 August 2022 }} ''www.tw.news.yahoo.com''</ref> The film grossed $67 million worldwide over a $16 million budget, making it the highest grossing [[NC-17]] film of all time.<ref>{{Cite web|title=All Time Worldwide Box Office for NC-17 Movies|url=https://m.the-numbers.com/box-office-records/worldwide/all-movies/mpaa-ratings/nc-17-(us)|access-date=2021-12-04|website=The Numbers}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Adkins|first=Frankie|date=2021-05-02|title=The 30 highest grossing NC-17 rated movies of all time|url=https://www.newsweek.com/30-highest-grossing-nc-17-rated-movies-all-time-1587560|access-date=2021-12-04|website=Newsweek|language=en}}</ref>

==Plot==
===Hong Kong, 1938===
During the [[Second Sino-Japanese War]], a shy and inexperienced university student, Wong Chia Chi, travels from Shanghai to Hong Kong to begin studying at [[Lingnan University (Hong Kong)|Lingnan University]]. Male student Kuang Yumin invites her to join his patriotic drama club and she soon becomes a lead actress, inspiring both her audience and her colleagues. Inspired by the club's patriotic plays, Kuang persuades the group to make a more concrete contribution to the war against Japan. He devises a plan to assassinate Mr. Yee, a [[special agent]] of the [[Wang Jingwei regime|puppet government]] of [[Wang Jingwei]] set up by the Japanese occupation in China. The beautiful Wong is chosen to take on the undercover role of "Mrs. Mak", the elegant wife of a trading company owner. She inserts herself into the social circle of Mrs. Yee.

Wong is still a virgin, and she reluctantly consents to having sex with another student involved in the plot in order to practice her role as a married woman. Attracted to Wong, Yee nearly falls for the trap but backs out at the last minute. Soon after, Mr. and Mrs. Yee move back to Shanghai, leaving the students with no further chance to complete their mission. While they are preparing to disband, a former subordinate of Yee turns up unannounced and tells them that he is aware of their plans. After a violent struggle, the students kill the subordinate and then go into hiding.

===Shanghai, 1942===
Three years later, in Japanese-occupied Shanghai governed by the [[Wang Jingwei regime]], Wong again encounters Kuang, who is now an undercover agent of the [[Kuomintang]] (KMT) secret service, the [[Bureau of Investigation and Statistics|''Juntong'']], which is seeking to overthrow the Japanese occupation forces and the [[Wang Jingwei regime|puppet government]]. Kuang enlists her into a renewed assassination plan to kill Yee, who has become head of Wang Jingwei's secret police department, and is responsible for torturing and executing resistance members. Wong's advances to Yee are reciprocated. During their first sexual encounter, Yee violently rapes Wong.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/reviews/lust-caution-18-768434.html|title=Lust, Caution (18)|date=2008-01-06|website=The Independent|language=en|access-date=2020-04-20|first=Jonathan|last=Romney}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last=Gallagher|first=Mark|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OSBdDwAAQBAJ&q=lust+caution+assault|title=Tony Leung Chiu-Wai|date=2018-01-19|publisher=Bloomsbury Publishing|isbn=978-1-84457-783-5|language=en}}</ref> Over the next few weeks, their sexual relationship becomes more consensual and affectionate.<ref>{{Cite journal|url=https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v30/n02/michael-wood/at-the-movies|title=Michael Wood · At the Movies: 'Lust, Caution' · LRB 24 January 2008|last=Wood|first=Michael|date=2008-01-24|journal=London Review of Books|volume=30|issue=2|language=en|access-date=2020-04-20}}</ref>

When Wong reports to her KMT superior officer, she exhorts him to carry out the assassination soon, so that she will not have to continue her sexual liaison with Yee, but she is told that the assassination needs to be delayed for strategic reasons. Wong describes the emotional conflict she finds herself embroiled in, sentimentally bound to a man whom she is plotting to help assassinate. When Yee sends Wong to a jewelry store with a sealed envelope, she discovers that he has arranged for a large and extremely rare six-carat pink diamond for her, to be mounted in a ring. This provides the Chinese resistance with a chance to get at Yee when he is not accompanied by his bodyguards.

Soon after, Wong invites Yee to accompany her to collect the diamond ring. While entering the jewelry store, she notices that all those involved in the assassination plot are undercover outside. When she puts on the ring, she is overcome by emotion and quietly urges Yee to leave. Understanding her meaning, he immediately flees the shop and escapes the assassination attempt. By the end of the day, most members of the resistance group have been captured. Yee's deputy reports to him that they had been surveilling the cell for some time. An infuriated Yee demands to know why he was not informed, to which the deputy reluctantly cites Yee's relationship with Wong, before giving him the diamond ring they had recovered. Yee signs the resistance members' death warrants. Wong, Kuang and others are taken to a quarry to be executed. When Yee returns home that night, he sits on Wong's empty bed in the family guest room while his wife comes to ask him what is going on, since his secretary and two men took Wong's belongings and some papers from his office. Yee tells her that if anyone asks, she should say that Mrs. Mak has returned to Hong Kong, and then tells her to continue playing mahjong downstairs. He sits alone in the dark for a while before leaving the room.

==Production==
The actors who played university classmates spent six months of preproduction in Hong Kong to get into character and understand the period before filming. During this period the group of actors, including Tang Wei and [[Wang Leehom]], became very close friends.

==Themes==
===Background===
The original novella by Eileen Chang was sometimes credited to be inspired by the historical event of [[Zheng Pingru]]'s failed attempt to assassinate the Japanese collaborator [[Ding Mocun]].<ref name="Peng2014">{{cite book|first1=Hsiao-yen|last1=Peng|last2=Dilley|first2=Whitney Crothers|title=From Eileen Chang to Ang Lee: Lust/Caution|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=UJziAgAAQBAJ&pg=PT58|date=2014|publisher=Taylor & Francis|isbn=978-1-317-91102-9|page=58}}</ref> 

===Meaning of the title===
The title of the work, ''Lust, Caution'', has a double meaning in Chinese. The character for "lust" (色, ''sè'') can be read as "colour", while "caution" (戒, ''jiè'') can be read as "ring", therefore the title can also read as "colored ring", an object that plays a pivotal role in the story.  The two alternate readings of the title are interwoven into a cautionary tale of lust and love through the symbolic use of the ring.<ref name=":4">{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=dZziAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA38 |title=From Eileen Chang to Ang Lee: Lust/Caution |date=January 10, 2014 |publisher=Routledge |isbn=9781317911036 |editor=Peng Hsiao-yen |pages=38–39 |editor2=Whitney Crothers Dilley}}</ref>

===Sexuality and power===
In the movie, Wong's virginity is used as a symbol of both her status as an innocent woman and a barrier to the role she must play in order to prove her patriotism. Wong's virginity is ultimately given as a sacrifice but consequently, her sexuality that has been awakened is used as a weapon against Mr. Yee in order to ensnare him into a relationship.<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal |last=Wang |first=Xiaoping |date=2010-04-28 |title=Making a Historical Fable: the narrative strategy of Lust, Caution and its social repercussions |url=http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/10670561003666178 |journal=Journal of Contemporary China |volume=19 |issue=65 |pages=573–590 |doi=10.1080/10670561003666178 |issn=1067-0564 |s2cid=143582106|url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Hao |date=2021 |title=Lust, Caution, and Enlightenment: A Reexamination of Su Wukong's Sexuality in ''Xiyou ji'' |url=http://dx.doi.org/10.7817/jameroriesoci.141.4.0911 |journal=Journal of the American Oriental Society |volume=141 |issue=4 |pages=911 |doi=10.7817/jameroriesoci.141.4.0911 |issn=0003-0279 |s2cid=245561403|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

The portrayal of female sexuality and desire in the film emphasizes the shame and awkwardness of Wong's sexuality versus the role she must play as "Mrs. Mak", which serves the nation rather than her needs as a woman.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Leihua Weng |date=2017 |title=Time, History, and Nation in Ang Lee's ''Lust, Caution'' |url=http://dx.doi.org/10.5250/symploke.25.1-2.0331 |journal=Symplokē |volume=25 |issue=1–2 |pages=331 |doi=10.5250/symploke.25.1-2.0331 |issn=1069-0697 |s2cid=148839936|url-access=subscription }}</ref> Sex and sexuality are used as tangible tools in proving patriotism in this film, and in each instance of Wong's bodily sacrifice, she is representing the recognizable symbol of violation experienced by China as a nation while under Japanese occupation.<ref name=":1">{{Cite journal |last=Thompson |first=Zoë Brigley |date=2016 |title=Beyond Symbolic Rape: The Insidious Trauma of Conquest in Marguerite Duras's The Lover and Eileen Chang's "Lust, Caution" |url=https://muse.jhu.edu/article/646904 |journal=Feminist Formations |language=en |volume=28 |issue=3 |pages=1–26 |doi=10.1353/ff.2016.0041 |issn=2151-7371 |s2cid=151579978|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

Through each of the sex scenes, a tangible but subtle difference can be seen in Wong Chia Chi's character as she becomes more comfortable with her sexual desires; a gradual acceptance of pleasure along with a growing role of dominance in hers and Mr. Yee's relationship as compared to the submissive and easily manipulated role she fills in the group of comrades that she is plotting against the Japanese and their collaborators with.<ref name=":1" />

Eileen Chang's original work from which Ang is drawing from does not contain the sex scenes of the film, yet with their addition a change can be seen in the levels of participation and assertiveness from Wong Chia Chi and her own agency in them: the first sex scene focuses on the forced and unpleasant intercourse between the couple; stronger levels of consent and enjoyment from Wong is found in the second sex scene; finally, Wong Chia Chi has recognized her full agency in the third sex scene and is acting with assertion by taking control of her own desires and pleasure with Mr. Yee.<ref name=":1" />

==Release==
''Lust, Caution'' premiered at the [[Venice Film Festival]], where it won the [[Golden Lion]], the second such award for Ang Lee. It was released in U.S. theaters on September 28, 2007, where it was rated [[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system#NC-17 replaces X|NC-17]] by the [[Motion Picture Association of America]] due to some explicit sex scenes. Lee stated that he would make no changes to attempt to get an [[Motion Picture Association of America film rating system|R rating]].<ref name="rating">{{cite news|title=Focus won't sweat NC-17 for 'Lust' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film/news/e3i5259606488c5ad20cc20a5480de02cdc |last=Goldstein |first=Gregg |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |date=August 24, 2007 |access-date=September 9, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930221004/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/film/news/e3i5259606488c5ad20cc20a5480de02cdc |archive-date=September 30, 2007 }}</ref> After the movie's premiere, director Ang Lee was displeased that Chinese news media (including those from Taiwan) had greatly emphasized the sex scenes in the movie.<ref>{{cite web |url= http://showbiz.chinatimes.com/Chinatimes/ExteriorContent/Showbiz/Showbiz-Page/0,4434,content+110511+112007090300232,00.html |script-title= zh:媒體猛炒性愛 李安痛心 |access-date= August 16, 2008 |language= zh |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20081209025408/http://showbiz.chinatimes.com/Chinatimes/ExteriorContent/Showbiz/Showbiz-Page/0,4434,content+110511+112007090300232,00.html |archive-date= December 9, 2008 |url-status= dead |df= mdy-all }}</ref> The version released in the People's Republic of China was cut by about seven minutes (by the director himself) to make it suitable for younger audiences, since China has no rating system.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://articles.cnn.com/2007-10-08/world/talkasia.anglee_1_tang-wei-chinese-films-american-film/2? |work=CNN |title=Ang Lee celebrates golden success of "Lust, Caution" |date=December 21, 2007 }}{{dead link|date=August 2024|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref> Scenes of violence were also removed from the mainland version, and the total minutes of the cuts was around 30.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/no-sex-scenes-in-china-s-version-of-lust-caution-1.653704 |title=No sex scenes in China's version of Lust, Caution |publisher=[[CBC News]] |date=2007-09-12 |access-date=2022-01-24 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070916142519/http://www.cbc.ca/arts/film/story/2007/09/12/lust-caution.html |archive-date=16 September 2007 |url-status=live}}</ref> The version released in Malaysia was approved by the Film Censorship Board of Malaysia without alterations and was rated [[Motion picture rating system#Malaysia|18SX]]—those under 18 are barred from the cinema. The uncut version was released on DVD in 2008, as well as R-rated version since certain rental outlets and stores like [[Blockbuster LLC|Blockbuster video]] did not carry NC-17 titles.

==Controversies==
===Censorship===

In a number of countries, notably China and India, the sex scenes had to be cut before the film could be released. In Singapore, the film's producers initially decided to release a cut version with an NC-16 rating, but a public outcry stating that the producers of the film were underestimating censorship standards in the country (the film was released uncut in Hong Kong and Taiwan) prompted them to eventually release the uncut version with an R-21 rating. The film is rated R18 and was released uncut in New Zealand.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Lust, Caution |url=https://www.nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/lust-caution/OKGLOJXE7ND5MQIJPAGGGDX4YM/?c_id=1501119|first=Francesca |last=Rudkin|date=23 January 2008|access-date=30 October 2025|work=[[The New Zealand Herald]]}}</ref>

The following scenes were cut from the mainland China version: 
* Wong Chia Chi walking past dead [[refugee]]s in the street
* [[Stabbing]] scene cut to only one knife stab
* Two of the sex scenes featuring Wong Chia Chi and a male student, and three featuring her and Mr. Yee
* A nude shot of Wong Chia Chi at a window
* Wong Chia Chi in bed after being raped by Mr. Yee
* Dialogue modified in diamond ring scene so that Wong Chia Chi did not betray the resistance by warning Mr. Yee.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.metro.co.uk/fame/article.html?in_article_id=78856&in_page_id=7 |title=Lee admits 'political edit' of film|work=Metro|url-status=dead|date=8 December 2007|archive-date=2 October 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121002190618/http://www.metro.co.uk/showbiz/78856-lee-admits-political-edit-of-film}}</ref>
In a further example of censorship affecting the mainland China release of ''Lust, Caution'', the line in which Wong whispers "Go, go quickly" to the Japanese collaborator that she has fallen in love with in order to save him from capture and death; in the edited version, Ang Lee changes this to "Let's go" in order to redeem the lead protagonist's sabotage of the assassination attempt of Mr. Yee<ref name=":22">{{Cite book |last=Hsiao-hung |first=Chang |title=Transnational Affect: Cold Anger, Hot Tears, and Lust/Caution |publisher=[[Routledge]] |year=2014 |isbn=978-1315849829 |location=New York |pages=182–185}}</ref> by implicating them both in the escape rather than Wong sacrificing herself and her classmates alone. This form of censorship was done in order to avoid criticism for glorifying a traitor such as Mr. Yee during a time of Japanese occupation in World War II.<ref name=":22" /><ref name=":3">{{Cite book |last=Peng |first=Hsiao-Yen |title=From Eileen Chang to Ang Lee: Lust, Caution |publisher=[[Routledge]] |year=2014 |isbn=9781315849829 |location=New York |pages=154–181 |language=English |chapter=9}}</ref> Wong's betrayal of her classmates and China as a nation in order to save a traitor was received by some mainland Chinese audiences with distaste, with some media websites referring to the film as an insult to China.<ref name=":3" />

The film's end credits are followed by a [[Child Protection and Obscenity Enforcement Act|18 U.S.C § 2257 notice]].<ref>The notice reads: "18 U.S.C § 2257 records custodian – Joyce Hsieh, custodian of records, Mr. Yee Productions LLC, C/O Schreck Rose Dapello Adams & Hurwitz LLP, 1790 Broadway, New York, NY 10019. manufacturer above."</ref>

===Blacklisting===
Tang Wei was ostracized from the mainland Chinese movie industry and did not work for three years because the State Administration of Radio Film and Television (SARFT) disapproved of her sexual acts in the film.<ref>{{cite web |date=9 March 2008 |title="Lust, Caution" actress banned in China |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-ca-mn-tang-wei-china-20170825-story.html |website=[[Los Angeles Times]] |agency=Reuters}}</ref> All print ads and video content using Tang's image were removed and her endorsements were discontinued.<ref>{{cite news |date=7 August 2017 |title=Tang Wei's spectacular career comeback after being banned in China |newspaper=LA Times |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/film-lust-dc-idUSN0935238620080309}}</ref> She was set to star in [[Tian Zhuangzhuang]]'s big-budget period film ''[[The Warrior and the Wolf]]'' (2009), but was replaced by [[Maggie Q]].<ref>[https://variety.com/2008/film/news/maggie-q-to-star-in-wolf-1117992952/ "Maggie Q to star in 'Wolf'"] ''Variety''. 25 September 2008.</ref> Director Ang Lee released a statement saying that he is "very regretful" over Tang's blacklisting and that he "will do everything I can to support her in this difficult time".<ref>{{Cite web |date=March 11, 2008 |title=Director Lee defends actor banned from Chinese media |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2008/mar/11/news |website=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref> Co-star Tony Leung stated that "our work is only to express our roles and I don't think that an actor should be blacklisted because of this" and that "the whole crew should have a responsibility. We are a team and not an individual, and I'm a part of this team".<ref>{{Cite web |date=March 18, 2008 |title=Tony Leung defends blacklisted Chinese "Lust" actress |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/uk-hongkong-filmawards-lust-idUKL1725862420080317 |website=[[Reuters]]}}</ref> In February 2009, during her absence from the movie industry, Tang was reported to have briefly attended drama classes at the [[University of Reading]] in the United Kingdom.<ref name="return">{{cite web |date=15 April 2010 |title=Tang Wei returns, with caution |url=http://en.people.cn/90001/90782/90875/6951726.html |work=People's Daily}}</ref>

===Country of production===
The film was co-produced by American companies [[Focus Features]] and [[River Road Entertainment]], Chinese companies [[Shanghai Film Group Corporation]] and Haishang Films, and the Taiwanese Hai Sheng Film Production Company. The film was directed by Ang Lee, a Taiwanese citizen, and the actors/actresses are from mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and the United States. It was shot in Shanghai, the neighboring province of [[Zhejiang]], Hong Kong (at the [[University of Hong Kong]]), and some locations in [[Penang]] and [[Ipoh]] in Malaysia used as 1930s/1940s Hong Kong.

Initially, the film's country was identified as "China-USA" by the organizers of the [[Venice Film Festival]]. However, the Venice Film Festival changed the film to "USA-China-Taiwan, China" on its official schedule shortly thereafter.<ref name="title2">{{cite web |title=64th Venice Film Festival – In Competition |url=http://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/festival/program/en/14372.1.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060821012540/http://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/festival/program/en/14372.1.html |archive-date=August 21, 2006 |access-date=September 9, 2007 |df=mdy-all}}</ref> When the film premiered at the event, Taiwan's [[Mainland Affairs Council]] protested the Venice event's use of "Taiwan, China" to identify films from the island and blamed the People's Republic of China for the move.<ref name="title3">{{cite news |title=Taiwan protests Chinese credit for Ang Lee's movie at Venice festival |url=http://www.cbc.ca/cp/entertainment/070828/e082804A.html |url-status=dead |access-date=September 9, 2007 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130115112757/http://www.cbc.ca/cp/entertainment/070828/e082804A.html |archive-date=January 15, 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=August 28, 2007 |title=Venice Film Fest faces faux pas over Taiwan |work=CBC News |url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/venice-film-fest-faces-faux-pas-over-taiwan-1.648701 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081209054408/http://www.cbc.ca/news/story/2007/08/28/venice-taiwan.html |url-status=live |archive-date=December 9, 2008 |access-date=February 9, 2008}}</ref>

After the film's premiere, Taiwan submitted the film as its [[Academy Award for Best Foreign Film|Best Foreign Film Oscar]] entry. However, the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences asked Taiwan to withdraw the film because key members of the film crew were not locals. Academy spokeswoman Teni Melidonian said in an e-mail organizers refused to accept the film because "an insufficient number of Taiwanese participated in the production of the film," violating a rule that requires foreign countries to certify their locals "exercised artistic control" over their submission.

===Defamation===
On September 13, 2007, an elderly lady, Zheng Tianru, staged a press conference in Los Angeles, claiming that the movie was about real-life events that happened in World War II, and wrongfully portrayed her older sister, [[Zheng Pingru]], as a promiscuous secret agent who seduced and eventually fell in love with the assassination target [[Ding Mocun]], alleging the characters were renamed to Wong Chia Chi and Mr. Yee in the film.<ref>{{cite web |script-title=zh:色‧戒」影射鄭蘋如？鄭家人不滿 |url=http://news.pchome.com.tw/entertainment/tvbs/20070913/index-20070913101808391700.html |access-date=August 16, 2008 |language=zh}}</ref> Taiwan's investigation bureau confirmed that Zheng Pingru failed to kill Ding Mocun because her gun jammed, rather than developing a romantic relationship with the assassin's target.<ref name="people2008">{{cite web |title=湯唯情欲戲被指褻瀆烈士 <色戒>遭原型家人聲討 |url=http://ent.people.com.cn/BIG5/8222/86596/100104/100111/6262679.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080612053510/http://ent.people.com.cn/BIG5/8222/86596/100104/100111/6262679.html |archive-date=June 12, 2008 |access-date=August 16, 2008 |language=zh}}</ref> Ang Lee maintains that Eileen Chang wrote the original short story as fiction.<ref name="people2008" />

==Reception==
===Critical reception===
On the review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 73% based on 149 reviews, with an average rating of 7/10. The site's consensus reads: "Ang Lee's ''Lust, Caution'' is a tense, sensual and beautifully-shot espionage film".<ref>{{cite web |title=Lust, Caution |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/lust_caution/ |access-date=November 15, 2023 |publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film had a weighted average score of 61 out of 100, based on 35 reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |title=Lust, Caution |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/lust-caution/ |access-date=November 16, 2023 |publisher=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref>

The Chinese press gave the film generally positive reviews. In analyzing how successful Lee's film was as an adaptation of Eileen Chang's short story, literary critic [[Leo Ou-fan Lee]] wrote in [[Muse (Hong Kong magazine)|''Muse'']] Magazine that he 'found [his] loyalties divided between Eileen Chang and Ang Lee. But after three viewings of the film, I have finally opted for Lee because deep down I believe in film magic which can sometimes displace textual fidelity.'<ref>{{cite journal |last=Lee |first=Leo Ou-fan |date=November 2007 |title=''Lust, Caution'': Vision and revision |url=https://archive.org/details/lustcaution00eile |journal=Muse Magazine |issue=10 |pages=96 |isbn=9780307387448 |url-access=registration}}</ref> In an earlier issue of ''Muse'' however, film critic Perry Lam had criticized Lee's direction: 'in his eagerness to make the movie appealing to a mass audience, Lee seems guilty of sentimentalism.'<ref>{{cite journal |last=Lam |first=Perry |date=October 2007 |title=Great expectations |journal=Muse Magazine |issue=9 |pages=103}}</ref> Sentimental or not, there is certainly a palpable trace of Lee's sympathy for Chang's personal love life, "It was hard for me to live in Eileen Chang's world...There are days I hated her for it. It's so sad, so tragic. But you realize there's a shortage of love in her life: romantic love, family love." He added, "This is the story of what killed love for her."<ref>{{cite news |last=Lim |first=Dennis |date=August 26, 2007 |title=Love as an Illusion: Beautiful to See, Impossible to Hold |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2007/08/26/movies/26lim.html}}</ref>

Audiences across Taiwan received the premiere of ''Lust, Caution'' with excitement stemming from pride over the fact that Ang Lee hails from Taiwan and the fact that the film received many international awards.<ref name=":2">{{Cite book |last=Chang |first=Hsiao-hung |title=Transnational Affect: Cold Anger, Hot Tears, and Lust/Caution. |publisher=Routledge |year=2014 |isbn=978-0-415-73120-1 |location=New York |pages=182–196 |language=english}}</ref> The mainstream media in Taiwan built up an enormous amount of anticipation and fever for the world premiere of ''Lust, Caution'' with a continuous gossip channel focused on the explicit and controversial portrayal of sexuality<ref name=":2" /> which seemed to indicate to the positive, or at least curious reception of the film.

Jack Mathews of the [[Daily News (New York)|New York ''Daily News'']] named it the 5th best film of 2007.<ref name="mc07top10">{{cite web |title=Metacritic: 2007 Film Critic Top Ten Lists |url=https://www.metacritic.com/film/awards/2007/toptens.shtml |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080102102034/http://www.metacritic.com/film/awards/2007/toptens.shtml <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date=January 2, 2008 |access-date=January 8, 2008 |publisher=[[Metacritic]]}}</ref> [[Kenneth Turan]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' named it the 6th best film of 2007.<ref name="mc07top10" />

British actor [[Hugh Grant]] is on record as being a fan of the film.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y3lUJt4KmJw |title=Conversations with Hugh Grant |date=2016-08-16 |author=SAG-AFTRA Foundation |publisher=YouTube |access-date=2018-06-27 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211222/y3lUJt4KmJw |archive-date=2021-12-22 |url-status=live}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

===Anachronisms===
It has been noted by critics (including [[Bryan Appleyard]]<ref>{{cite web |last=Appleyard |first=Bryan |author-link=Bryan Appleyard |date=January 21, 2008 |title=A Protocol Problem and the Lust Caution Taxi |url=http://www.bryanappleyard.com/blog/2008/01/protocol-problem-and-lust-caution-taxi.php |url-status=usurped |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081209080650/http://www.bryanappleyard.com/blog/2008/01/protocol-problem-and-lust-caution-taxi.php |archive-date=December 9, 2008 |access-date=August 16, 2008 |work=Thought Experiments: The Blog |publisher=brianappleyard.com}}</ref>) that the Hong Kong sequences in the film set in the late 1930s<ref>See {{cite video |url=http://www.focusfeatures.com/clips/lust_caution/epk1-320x240.wmv |title=Lust, Caution (clip) |medium=Motion picture |publisher=[[Focus Features]] |access-date=August 16, 2008 |format=WMV}}{{dead link|date=May 2017|bot=InternetArchiveBot|fix-attempted=yes}} (5.2 MB)</ref> include "London taxis" of two types ([[Austin FX3|FX3]], [[Austin FX4|FX4]]) that were only manufactured onwards from 1948 and 1958 respectively.<ref>{{cite web |year=2006 |title=The FX series |url=http://www.lti.co.uk/previous-models/fx |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080624025708/http://www.lti.co.uk/previous-models/fx/ |archive-date=June 24, 2008 |access-date=August 16, 2008 |publisher=[[London Taxis International|LTI Vehicles]]}}</ref> The film features [[Modern kana usage|Japanese orthography that wasn't used before 1946]] in a classroom scene. Tony Leung's wrist watch is not period-accurate for the 1930s. The size is much larger than a men's rectangular-shaped watch from this period.

===Box office===
''Lust, Caution'' was produced on a budget of approximately $15&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM1">{{cite web |title=Lust, Caution |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=lustcaution.htm |access-date=December 5, 2010 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

In Hong Kong, where the film was screened uncut, ''Lust, Caution'' grossed US$6,249,342 (approximately $48&nbsp;million HKD) despite being saddled with a restrictive [[Hong Kong motion picture rating system|Category III]] rating. It was the territory's highest-grossing Chinese language film of the year, and third highest overall (behind ''[[Spider-Man 3]]'' and ''[[Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix (film)|Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix]]'').<ref>{{cite web |title=Hong Kong Yearly Box Office (2007) |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/intl/hongkong/yearly/?yr=2007&p=.htm |access-date=August 16, 2008 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

The film was also a box office success in mainland China despite the cuts made to allow screening. There, the film grossed US$17,109,185, making it the Mainland's fifth highest-grossing film of 2007 and third highest-grossing domestic production.<ref>{{cite web |title=China Yearly Box Office (2007) |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/intl/china/yearly/?yr=2007&p=.htm |access-date=August 16, 2008 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

In North America, the film received NC-17 rating, which severely limited the number of cinemas willing to screen it. In its opening weekend in one U.S. theater, the film grossed $63,918.<ref name="BOM1" /> Expanding to seventeen venues the next week, its per-screen average was $21,341, before cooling down to $4,639 at 125 screens.<ref name="BOM2">{{cite web |title=Lust, Caution – Weekend Box Office |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=lustcaution.htm |access-date=August 16, 2008 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> Never playing at more than 143 cinemas for the duration of its entire U.S. run, the film ultimately grossed $4,604,982.<ref name="BOM2" /> As of August 15, 2008, the film was the fifth highest-grossing NC-17 production in North America.<ref>{{cite web |title=Domestic Grosses by MPAA Rating – NC-17 |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/alltime/domestic/mpaa.htm?page=NC-17&p=.htm |access-date=August 16, 2008 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> [[Focus Features]] was very satisfied with the United States release of this film.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Sperling |first=Nicole |date=March 19, 2008 |title=Ang Lee and James Schamus Get Frank |url=https://ew.com/article/2008/03/19/ang-lee-and-james-schamus-discuss-nc-17/ |magazine=Entertainment Weekly}}</ref>

Worldwide, ''Lust, Caution'' grossed $67,091,915.<ref name="BOM1" />

===DVD sales===
The film has generated more than $24&nbsp;million from its DVD sales and rentals in the United States,<ref>{{cite web |title=Lust, Caution (2007) - DVD / Home Video Rentals - Box Office Mojo |url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=homevideo&id=lustcaution.htm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170228015430/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=homevideo&id=lustcaution.htm |archive-date=28 February 2017 |access-date=17 January 2022 |website=www.boxofficemojo.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Hendrix |first=Grady |date=April 23, 2008 |title=Dirty DVD sales |url=http://www.varietyasiaonline.com/component/option,com_myblog/show,5955 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080606070619/http://www.varietyasiaonline.com/component/option%2Ccom_myblog/show%2C5955 |archive-date=June 6, 2008 |access-date=August 16, 2008 |work=Kaiju Shakedown blog |publisher=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety Asia]]}}</ref> an impressive result for a film that only grossed $4.6&nbsp;million in limited theatrical release in the United States.<ref name="BOM1" />
