'''''Minari''''' ({{Korean|hangul=미나리|lit=[[Oenanthe javanica|water celery]]}}; {{IPA|ko|minaɾi|}}) is a 2020 American [[Drama (film and television)|drama film]] written and directed by [[Lee Isaac Chung]]. It stars [[Steven Yeun]], [[Han Ye-ri]], [[Alan Kim]], Noel Kate Cho, [[Youn Yuh-jung]], and [[Will Patton]]. A semi-autobiographical take on Chung's upbringing, its plot follows a family of [[Korean Americans|South Korean immigrants]] who move to rural [[Arkansas]] during the 1980s.<ref>{{Cite web|date=January 2, 2021|title='Minari' Summary & Analysis – Embraces Acceptance And Change {{!}} DMT|url=https://dmtalkies.com/minari-2020-film-analysis/|access-date=April 5, 2021|website=Digital Mafia Talkies}}</ref>

''Minari'' had its world premiere at the [[2020 Sundance Film Festival]] on January 26, 2020, winning both the [[List of Sundance Film Festival award winners#2020|U.S. Dramatic Grand Jury Prize]] and the U.S. Dramatic Audience Award.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2020/09/minari-trailer-a24-oscars-1234589534/|title='Minari' Trailer: Lee Isaac Chung's Sundance Winner Is A24's Big Oscar Hopeful|last=Sharf|first=Zack|date=September 30, 2020|website=[[IndieWire]]|access-date=October 1, 2020|archive-date=October 22, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201022044252/https://www.indiewire.com/2020/09/minari-trailer-a24-oscars-1234589534/|url-status=live}}</ref> It began a one-week virtual release on December 11, 2020, and was released theatrically and via [[virtual cinema]] on February 12, 2021, by [[A24]].

The film received critical acclaim, with praise for Chung's direction and screenplay, Yeun's and Youn's performances, and the score. Both the [[National Board of Review]] and the [[American Film Institute]] named ''Minari'' one of the best films of 2020, and it has since been cited as [[List of films voted the best|one of the best films of the 21st century]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=February 6, 2022 |title=The 100 best films of the 21st century (so far) |url=https://www.timeout.com/film/the-100-best-movies-of-the-21st-century-so-far |access-date=October 18, 2023 |website=Time Out Worldwide}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Gaughan |first=Liam |date=August 24, 2023 |title="Don't be so eager to be offended" The 20 Best Drama Movies of the 2020s So Far, Ranked |url=https://collider.com/best-drama-movies-2020s/ |access-date=October 18, 2023 |website=Collider}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.indiewire.com/gallery/best-american-indie-movies-21st-century/minari-10/ | title=The Best American Independent Films of the 21st Century | date=January 3, 2024}}</ref> At the [[93rd Academy Awards]], it earned six nominations, including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], and won [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] for Youn, who became the first Korean actor to win an Oscar. ''Minari'' also won the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Foreign Language Film]], was nominated for the [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture]], and earned six nominations at the [[74th British Academy Film Awards]], including [[BAFTA Award for Best Film Not in the English Language|Best Film Not in the English Language]].

==Plot==
In 1983, the [[Korean Americans|South Korean immigrant]] Yi family moves from [[California]] to a plot of land in [[Arkansas]], where the father, Jacob, believes the soil is fertile and hopes to grow [[Korean cuisine|Korean produce]] to sell to vendors in [[Dallas]]. One of his first decisions is to decline the services of a [[Dowsing|water diviner]]; he digs a well in a spot he chooses himself. He enlists the help of Paul, an eccentric [[Korean War]] veteran who is fervently religious and occasionally [[Glossolalia|speaks in tongues]]. Jacob is optimistic, but his wife, Monica, is disappointed with the [[mobile home]] and the distance from urban amenities, especially a hospital for their son David, who has a heart condition. They monitor David's [[heart murmur]]s and frequently tell him not to run. The couple work [[Chick sexing|sexing chicks]] at a nearby [[hatchery]] and argue frequently while David and his sister, Anne, eavesdrop.

To watch the children during the day, Monica's mother, Soon-ja, travels from South Korea to live with them. Forced to share a room with her, David avoids her because she does not fit his idea of a grandmother. Still, Soon-ja attempts to bond with the children and adjust to life in the United States. The well Jacob dug runs dry. He is forced to pay for county water, and they eventually lose water in the home. He runs into additional difficulties when the vendor in Dallas cancels an order for their produce at the last minute. He perseveres despite Monica's desire to return to California, straining their marriage.

Soon-ja takes David to plant [[Oenanthe javanica|minari]] seeds by the creek, which she has brought with her from South Korea. She tells him how resilient and useful the plant is and predicts plentiful growth. David finally begins to warm to her after she teaches him [[Hanafuda|hwatu]], bandages his wounds, and soothes his fears over his heart condition. Soon-ja also encourages him to do more physical activity, saying he is stronger than his parents think. Soon-ja then suffers a [[stroke]] overnight. She survives but is left with impaired movement and speech. Monica considers moving back to California with Soon-ja, with David going with them if his health does not improve.

The family leaves Soon-ja at home while they head to [[Oklahoma City]] for David's heart appointment and to meet a vendor for Jacob's produce. They learn that David's condition has dramatically improved, and he will not need surgery, and Jacob makes a deal to sell vegetables to a Korean grocer. But Jacob also indirectly admits to Monica that his crops' success is more important to him than their family's stability and, after an emotional argument over whether they can support each other only during good times, the two agree to separate.

During their absence, Soon-ja accidentally sets fire to the barn containing the produce. On arriving home, Jacob rushes in to save the crops, followed by Monica. The fire grows out of control, and they save each other, leaving the barn to burn. Distraught and overwhelmed with guilt, Soon-ja wanders off while Anne and David call for her to return. David runs to her and blocks her path, before she takes his hand, and her grandchildren lead her back home. That night, the exhausted family sleeps together on the floor, as Soon-ja sits and watches over them.

Some time later, the water diviner guides Jacob and Monica to a spot to dig a new well, signifying their intention to stay. Jacob and David head to the creek to harvest the successfully grown minari, with Jacob noting that Soon-ja picked a good spot to plant it.

==Production==
===Development===
Chung had initially hoped to make an adaptation of ''[[My Ántonia]]'' by [[Willa Cather]] but found out that Cather did not want film adaptations of her works to be made. He was inspired to make a film about his childhood instead.<ref>{{cite web|last=Chung|first=Lee Isaac|author-link=Lee Isaac Chung|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/awards/story/2021-02-22/minari-writer-director-lee-isaac-chung|title=Some unusual guidance is behind writing 'Minari.' Director Lee Isaac Chung explains|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=February 22, 2021|access-date=March 12, 2021|archive-date=March 13, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210313094018/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/awards/story/2021-02-22/minari-writer-director-lee-isaac-chung|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.npr.org/2021/03/03/973262034/lee-isaac-chung-jotted-down-some-family-memories-they-became-minari|title=Lee Isaac Chung Jotted Down Some Family Memories – They Became 'Minari'|publisher=[[National Public Radio]]|date=March 3, 2021|accessdate=March 24, 2021}}</ref>

Chung wrote the screenplay for ''Minari'' in 2018 shortly before taking on an instructor position at the University of Utah Asia Campus in [[Songdo]], South Korea.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Kay|first=Jeremy|date=February 10, 2021|title=Why Lee Isaac Chung almost quit filmmaking before directing 'Minari'|url=https://www.screendaily.com/features/why-lee-isaac-chung-almost-quit-filmmaking-before-directing-minari/5156907.article|access-date=February 22, 2021|website=ScreenDaily}}</ref> He cited Cather and [[Fyodor Dostoevsky]] as inspirations during the writing process, recalling the former's saying "that her life really began when she stopped admiring and started remembering".<ref name=":0" /> In an interview with ''The Los Angeles Times'', Chung spoke about the challenges of drawing on his family's experiences: "It was very difficult in the sense that I know that my parents are private people. And I didn't even tell them that I was making this film until I was in the editing room with it after I had shot it, because I was just so scared about what they would say."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Olsen|first=Mark|date=February 17, 2021|title=Lee Isaac Chung turns inward for inspiration in 'Minari'|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/newsletter/2021-02-17/lee-isaac-chung-minari-envelope-newsletter-the-envelope-podcast-edition|url-status=live|access-date=February 22, 2021|website=Los Angeles Times|archive-date=February 22, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210222113612/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/newsletter/2021-02-17/lee-isaac-chung-minari-envelope-newsletter-the-envelope-podcast-edition}}</ref>

Chung wrote the script in English. Some dialogue was translated into colloquial Korean<ref name="JungEAlexYoungYuhjung">{{cite web|last=Jung|first=E. Alex|url=https://www.vulture.com/article/youn-yuh-jung-minari-profile.html|title=Youn Yuh-jung Comes to America|work=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]|date=March 2021 |accessdate=March 24, 2021}}</ref> by Hong Yeo-ul, in consultation with Chung and the actors;<ref>{{cite web |last=Kim |first=Ji-eun |date=March 4, 2021 |title='미나리' 빛난 이유 있었네...미술감독등 제작진도 한국인 |trans-title=There's a reason why 'Minari' shined... the production team, including the art director, were also Korean |url=https://newsis.com/view/?id=NISX20210304_0001358707&cID=10601&pID=10600 |accessdate=March 26, 2021 |work=[[Newsis]] |language=ko}}</ref> Han and Youn assisted in the translation efforts.<ref name="HanSheon" />

In early 2019, [[Christina Oh]] and [[Plan B Entertainment]] signed on as producers of the film. Oh later brought on [[A24]] to distribute it.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" />

===Filming===
Principal photography began in July 2019 in [[Tulsa, Oklahoma]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://collider.com/steven-yeun-minari-a24-movie/|title=Steven Yeun to Star in Korean Immigrant Drama 'Minari' for A24|website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]]|first=Jeff|last=Sneider|date=July 11, 2019|access-date=July 13, 2019|archive-date=July 12, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190712130338/http://collider.com/steven-yeun-minari-a24-movie/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url= https://www.afi.com/news/production-designer-yong-ok-lee-on-her-sundance-films-minari-and-the-farewell/ |title= Production Designer Yong Ok Lee on her Sundance films Minari and The Farewell |date= February 4, 2020 |work= AFI |access-date= December 10, 2020 |archive-date= February 22, 2021 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210222032605/https://www.afi.com/news/production-designer-yong-ok-lee-on-her-sundance-films-minari-and-the-farewell/ |url-status= live}}</ref> Filming lasted 25 days. In order to make a deadline for [[Sundance Film Festival|Sundance]], [[Film editing|editor]] Harry Yoon edited the film as production took place.<ref name="THR-filming">{{cite web |last= Galuppo |first= Mia |date= January 21, 2021 |url= https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/features/making-of-minari-how-lee-isaac-chung-created-a-unique-american-story-rarely-seen-onscreen |title= Making of 'Minari': How Lee Isaac Chung Created a Unique American Story Rarely Seen Onscreen |website= [[The Hollywood Reporter]] |access-date= February 21, 2021 |archive-date= February 24, 2021 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210224091438/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/features/making-of-minari-how-lee-isaac-chung-created-a-unique-american-story-rarely-seen-onscreen |url-status= live}}</ref>

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], {{RT data|score}} of {{RT data|count}} reviews were positive, with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The site's critics consensus reads: "Led by arresting performances from Steven Yeun and Yeri Han, ''Minari'' offers an intimate and heart-wrenching portrait of family and assimilation in 1980s America."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/minari|title=''Minari (2020)''|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210116150756/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/minari|archive-date=January 16, 2021|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a weighted average score 89 out of 100, based on 49 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://metacritic.com/movie/minari |title=''Minari''|website=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=March 26, 2021 |archive-date=February 15, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210215094022/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/minari |url-status=live}}</ref>

[[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' gave the film a positive review, writing: "''Minari'' is modest, specific and thrifty, like the lives it surveys. There's nothing small about it, though, because it operates at the true scale of life."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Scott |first=A. O. |author-link=A. O. Scott |date=February 11, 2021|title='Minari' Review: Sinking Korean Roots in the Arkansas Soil|work=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/02/11/movies/minari-review.html|access-date=March 9, 2021|issn=0362-4331|archive-date=March 9, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210309132745/https://www.nytimes.com/2021/02/11/movies/minari-review.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Nicholas Barber at the [[BBC]] rated the film 5/5, writing, "Sensitively written and acted, beautifully shot, and with a charming, sparingly used score, ''Minari'' is so engaging that it's easy to forget how radical it is."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Barber|first=Nicholas|title=Five stars for 'uncontrived, authentic' Minari|url=https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20210211-five-stars-for-uncontrived-authentic-minari|access-date=March 9, 2021|website=[[BBC]]|date=February 15, 2021 |archive-date=February 15, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210215055055/https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20210211-five-stars-for-uncontrived-authentic-minari|url-status=live}}</ref> Reviewing the film for the [[Associated Press]], Lindsey Bahr gave the film 4/4 stars, reporting, "One of the great triumphs of ''Minari'' is its presentation of authentic childhood. These kids are not saints or stand-ins or mouthpieces. They are their own persons."<ref>{{Cite web|date=February 10, 2021|title=Review: An essential portrait of a family in 'Minari'|url=https://apnews.com/article/minari-movie-review-5e8f9a21a337fa22add285b11a501932|access-date=March 9, 2021|website=[[Associated Press]]|archive-date=February 21, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210221003724/https://apnews.com/article/minari-movie-review-5e8f9a21a337fa22add285b11a501932|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Robbie Collin]] of ''[[The Telegraph (London)|The Telegraph]]'' rated the film 4/5, writing, "Lee Isaac Chung's tender story is a finely observed portrait of family relations and rural American values". Benjamin Lee at ''[[The Guardian]]'' also rated the film 4/5, writing, "The autobiographical story of a Korean American family trying to sustain a farm in rural Arkansas has deservedly become the festival's most universally loved film".<ref>{{Cite web|date=January 29, 2020|title=Minari review – moving and modest coming-of-age Sundance hit|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/jan/29/minari-sundance-korean-american-film|access-date=March 9, 2021|website=[[The Guardian]]|archive-date=March 8, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210308193514/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2020/jan/29/minari-sundance-korean-american-film|url-status=live}}</ref>

Filmmaker [[Bong Joon-ho]] praised the film, saying, "I think it takes a lot of courage to shoot a film about yourself or your family, since it's autobiographical. But what I appreciated more about this film is that it doesn't wallow in nostalgia. It's a story about [Chung], but there's a sense of distance too. So rather than it being about the history and story of a Korean immigrant family, the film can appeal to families all over the world or anyone who still carries the memories of their parents."<ref>{{cite web|title=Directors on Directors: Filmmakers Analyze 2020 Favorites |url=https://variety.com/lists/directors-on-directors-2020/minari-3/ |website=variety.com |date=February 4, 2021 |access-date=March 6, 2025}}</ref>

''Minari'' appeared on 68 critics' year-end top-ten lists, including first place on five year-end lists and second place on nine.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/feature/film-critics-pick-10-best-movies-of-2020|title=Best of 2020: Film Critic Top Ten Lists|last=Dietz|first=Jason|date=February 12, 2021|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=March 14, 2021|archive-date=December 15, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201215081138/https://www.metacritic.com/feature/film-critics-pick-10-best-movies-of-2020|url-status=live}}</ref> In 2023, it ranked ninth on ''[[Collider (website)|Collider]]''{{'}}s list of "The 20 Best Drama Movies of the 2020s So Far".<ref>{{cite web | url=https://collider.com/best-drama-movies-2020s/ | title="Don't be so eager to be offended". The 20 Best Drama Movies of the 2020s So Far, Ranked | website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]] | date=August 24, 2023}}</ref> ''[[Marie Claire]]'' also included it on its list of "The 100 Best Movies of All Time."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.marieclaire.com/culture/g2509/movies-to-watch-before-30/ | title=We're Obsessed with Movies—These Are the Films You Have to Watch at Least Once in Your Life | date=September 14, 2022}}</ref>

The May 2021 issue of ''[[New York Magazine]]'' lists ''Minari'' as among "The Best Movies That Lost Best Picture at the Oscars."<ref>{{cite news|title=The Best Movies That Lost Best Picture at the Oscars|url=https://www.vulture.com/article/best-oscar-best-picture-losers.html|magazine=[[New York Magazine]]|access-date=March 30, 2025}}</ref>

In 2024, the website [[Looper (website)|Looper]] ranked it number 34 on its list of "50 Best PG-13 Movies of All Time", writing, "Everyone has got a story to tell and the quietly powerful filmmaking of ''Minari'' conveys this truth magnificently. That's not the only place Chung's directorial vision impresses, though, as subtly detailed instances of filming things through the points-of-view of other characters — as well as the specific details of each central family member — reflect what a masterfully conceived project this is."<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.looper.com/806086/best-pg-13-movies-of-all-time-ranked/ | title=51 Best PG-13 Movies of All Time Ranked | website=Looper | date=October 14, 2024}}</ref> In June 2025, ''[[IndieWire]]'' ranked the film at number 86 on its list of "100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)".<ref name="Best">{{cite web |date=June 16, 2025 |title=The 100 Best Movies of the 2020s (So Far)|url=https://www.indiewire.com/lists/best-movies-2020s/ |access-date=June 23, 2025}}</ref>

===Accolades===
{{main|List of accolades received by Minari}}

''Minari'' premiered at the 2020 [[Sundance Film Festival]], where it received the [[List of Sundance Film Festival award winners#2020|U.S. Dramatic Grand Jury Prize]] and the U.S. Dramatic Audience Award. It was named one of the ten best films of 2020 by the [[American Film Institute]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://apnews.com/article/frances-mcdormand-regina-king-david-fincher-chadwick-boseman-spike-lee-c59f5b21bf925425a76da79d6da473be|title='Soul,' 'Ma Rainey's' among AFI's top 10 films of the year|work=Associated Press|date=January 25, 2021|access-date=January 25, 2021|archive-date=January 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210126213030/https://apnews.com/article/frances-mcdormand-regina-king-david-fincher-chadwick-boseman-spike-lee-c59f5b21bf925425a76da79d6da473be|url-status=live}}</ref> and the [[National Board of Review]], and received six nominations at the [[93rd Academy Awards]]: [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]], [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]], [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]], [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]] (Yeun), and [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] (Youn).<ref>{{cite news |url= https://deadline.com/2021/01/national-board-of-review-2020-winners-list-da-five-bloods-1234680677/ |title= Spike Lee's 'Da 5 Bloods' Named Best Film of 2020 by National Board of Review |website= Deadline Hollywood |date= January 26, 2021 |access-date= January 26, 2021 |archive-date= January 26, 2021 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20210126214212/https://deadline.com/2021/01/national-board-of-review-2020-winners-list-da-five-bloods-1234680677/ |url-status= live}}</ref> It also received three [[Screen Actors Guild Award]] nominations, ten [[Critics' Choice Movie Awards]] nominations, and six [[Independent Spirit Award]] nominations.
