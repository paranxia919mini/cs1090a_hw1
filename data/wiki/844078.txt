'''''Smokey and the Bandit''''' is a 1977 American [[action comedy]] [[Road movie|road film]] starring [[Burt Reynolds]], [[Sally Field]], [[Jackie Gleason]], [[Jerry Reed]], [[Pat McCormick (actor)|Pat McCormick]], [[Paul Williams (songwriter)|Paul Williams]], and [[Mike Henry (American football)|Mike Henry]]. The film marks the directorial debut of stuntman [[Hal Needham]].

The film follows Bo "Bandit" Darville (Reynolds) and Cledus "Snowman" Snow (Reed), two truck-driving [[Rum-running|bootleggers]] attempting to illegally transport 400 cases of [[Coors Brewing Company|Coors beer]] from [[Texarkana, Texas|Texarkana]] to [[Atlanta, Georgia|Atlanta]]. During their run, they are pursued by Sheriff Buford T. Justice (Gleason), of [[Montague County, Texas]], after his son's runaway bride Carrie (Field) joins The Bandit and Snowman in their rum-run.

''Smokey and the Bandit'' was a box office success following initial screenings at [[drive-in theaters]] across the [[Southern United States]] that prompted Universal to reopen the film nationwide,<ref name="belth">{{cite web |last1=Belth |first1=Alex |title=Remembering Hal Needham And The Birth Of Redneck Cinema |url=https://deadspin.com/remembering-hal-needham-and-the-birth-of-redneck-cinema-1498633096/ |website=[[Deadspin]] |access-date=March 10, 2026 |date=January 10, 2014}}</ref> grossing $127 million (${{formatprice|{{inflation|US-GDP|127000000|1977|r=-3}}}} in {{Inflation-year|US-GDP}}) against a $4.3 million (${{formatprice|{{inflation|US-GDP|4300000|1977|r=-3}}}} in {{Inflation-year|US-GDP}}) budget,{{inflation-fn|US-GDP}} becoming the [[1977 in film|second-highest-grossing domestic film of 1977]] in the United States, behind [[Star Wars (film)|''Star Wars'']].<ref>{{Cite web |title=1977 Worldwide Box Office |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/year/world/1977/ |access-date=2023-09-18 |website=Box Office Mojo}}</ref>

The film became the first installment in the [[Smokey and the Bandit (franchise)|''Smokey and the Bandit'' franchise]] and the first film of the ''Smokey and the Bandit'' trilogy.

==Plot==
Wealthy Texan capitalist Big Enos Burdette and his son, Little Enos, frequently bet notable truckers in [[Atlanta, Georgia|Atlanta]] to [[Rum-running|bootleg]] 400 cases of [[Coors Brewing Company#Products|Coors beer]] from [[Texarkana, Texas]], to Atlanta in 28 hours, but all have failed with many being arrested.  They find local legend Bo "Bandit" Darville at a truck [[rodeo]] at [[Lakewood Fairgrounds]] and offer him $80,000 (equivalent to $440,860 in 2026) to take the bet. The Bandit accepts and recruits his friend Cledus "Snowman" Snow and Fred, Cledus' [[Basset Hound]], to drive the truck, while Bandit drives a black 1977 [[Pontiac Firebird|Pontiac Trans Am]] (Trigger) as a "blocker" to divert attention away from the truck and its illegal cargo.

The pair arrives in Texarkana an hour early and load the truck, but as they begin their return journey, Bandit is intercepted by Carrie, a [[wikt:runaway bride|runaway bride]] who suddenly jumps into his car. Unbeknownst to him, this makes Bandit an indirect target of Sheriff Buford T. Justice, a seasoned Texas law enforcement officer whose dimwitted son, Junior, was supposed to marry Carrie. The Justice father and son engage in a high-speed [[hot pursuit]], doggedly chasing Bandit all the way to Georgia to retrieve Carrie and arrest the Bandit. Successive comical mishaps cause Justice's cruiser to experience increasing damage along the way.

Bandit attracts more police attention across [[Dixie]] as Cledus barrels toward Atlanta with the contraband beer. They are helped en route by many colorful characters via [[Citizens band radio|CB radio]]. Neither Buford nor any other lawmen are aware of Cledus's illegal manifest, while Bandit is likewise unaware that Buford is chasing him because of Carrie.

Just after re-entering Georgia, Cledus is rescued by Bandit after being pulled over by a [[Georgia State Patrol]] motorcycle trooper, as state and local police intensely pursue Bandit with roadblocks and a helicopter to track his movement. With {{cvt|4|mi|km}} remaining, Bandit, discouraged by the unexpected mounting attention, is ready to relent, but Cledus, who initially thought they would fail, takes the lead and smashes through the roadblock at the fairgrounds' main entrance. They reach their destination with ten minutes to spare. However, instead of taking the winnings, Carrie and Bandit accept a [[Double or nothing|double-or-nothing]] offer from Little Enos: a challenge to run up to [[Boston]] and bring back a shipment of [[clam chowder]] in 18 hours. They quickly escape in Big Enos's 1974 [[Cadillac Eldorado]] convertible as police flood the racetrack.

Buford arrives at the fairgrounds, his cruiser now a barely functioning wreck. When Bandit passes him, he uses his CB to contact Buford, and the pair briefly exchange some mutual respect before Buford demands to understand Bandit's whereabouts. Bandit initially directs him to the Burdettes, but he then respectfully gives his real location right behind Buford, who continues his chase, leaving Junior behind, and with more parts falling off his cruiser as he limps off after Bandit.

==Production==
===Development===
[[Hal Needham]] originally planned the film as a low-budget [[B movie]] with a production cost of $1 million,<ref name="CNN" /> with [[Jerry Reed]] as Bo "The Bandit" Darville. Needham had great difficulty getting any studios or producers to take his project seriously, being better known in the film industry as a [[Stunt performer|stuntman]]. He obtained the attention of studios and aimed the film at a more mainstream release after his friend [[Burt Reynolds]] read the script and agreed to portray the Bandit, with Reed now portraying the Bandit's friend Cledus "The Snowman" Snow. Reed would eventually play the Bandit in ''[[Smokey and the Bandit Part 3]]'', in which Reynolds only appeared in a cameo near the end. At the time, Reynolds was the top box office star in the world.

In the original script, Carrie was called Kate while Big Enos and Little Enos Burdette were called Kyle and Dickey. The Bandit's car was a second-generation [[Trans-Am Series|Trans Am]] and the prize for completing the run was a new truck rather than $80,000.<ref name="mentalfloss.com">{{cite web |url=http://mentalfloss.com/article/80484/13-fast-facts-about-smokey-and-bandit |title=13 Fast Facts About ''Smokey and the Bandit'' |first=Roger |last=Cormier |date=May 27, 2017 |website=mentalfloss.com |access-date=December 27, 2018 |archive-date=November 19, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181119053950/http://mentalfloss.com/article/80484/13-fast-facts-about-smokey-and-bandit |url-status=live}}</ref> Reynolds revealed in his autobiography that Needham had written the first draft script on legal pads. Upon showing it to his friend, Reynolds told Needham that it was the worst script he had ever read, but that he would still make the movie. Most of the dialogue was improvised on set.<ref name="mentalfloss.com" />

[[Universal Pictures|Universal Studios]] bankrolled ''Smokey and the Bandit'' for $5.3 million, figuring it was a good risk.<ref name="CNN" /> Just two days before production was to begin, Universal sent a "hatchet man" to [[Atlanta]] to inform Needham that the budget was being trimmed by $1 million. With Reynolds' salary at $1 million, Needham was left with only $3.3 million to make the film. Needham and assistant director David Hamburger spent 30 hours revising the shooting schedule.<ref name="CNN" />

Buford T. Justice was the name of a real [[Florida Highway Patrol]]man known to Reynolds's father Burt Sr., who was once police chief of [[Riviera Beach, Florida]]. His father was also the inspiration for the word "sumbitch" used in the film, a variation of the phrase "son-of-a-bitch" that, according to Reynolds, he uttered quite often. [[Jackie Gleason]] was given free rein to ad-lib dialogue and make suggestions. It was his idea to have Junior ([[Mike Henry (American football)|Mike Henry]]) alongside him throughout the film. In particular, the scene where Sheriff Justice unknowingly encounters the Bandit in a roadside diner (a "choke and puke" in CB lingo) was not in the original story but was rather Gleason's idea.

The film's theme song, "[[East Bound and Down]]", was written virtually overnight by Reed. He gave Needham a preview of the song and, getting no reaction, offered to rewrite it. In response, Needham told Reed that he liked the song so much that if Reed changed even a word or a note, Needham would "choke him".<ref name="SatB-Movieline">{{cite web |last1=Abrams |first1=Simon |title=Smokey and the Bandit at 35: A Case for the Genius of Hal Needham |url=http://movieline.com/2012/06/07/smokey-and-the-bandit-at-35-a-case-for-the-genius-of-hal-needham/ |website=Movieline |date=June 7, 2012 |publisher=pmc.com |access-date=April 27, 2021}}</ref> It became one of Reed's biggest hits and his signature song.<ref>{{cite web |last=Moore |first=Bobby |title=Top 10 Jerry Reed Songs |url=https://theboot.com/jerry-reed-songs/ |website=The Boot |publisher=Townsquare Media, Inc |access-date=April 27, 2021}}</ref>

The film features the custom clothing and costuming of Niver Western Wear of [[Fort Worth, Texas]].<ref>Niver Western Wear Corporate Records</ref> Niver provided much of the western attire worn in the film, as well as the custom-made sheriff's uniforms Gleason wore throughout the film.

While made to take advantage of the ongoing 1970s [[Citizens band radio#1970s popularity|CB radio fad]], the film added to the craze.<ref>{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=jUR-BAAAQBAJ&q=cb+radio+craze+smokey+and+the+bandit.&pg=PT405 |title=Media Now: Understanding Media, Culture, and Technology |page=377 |first1=Joseph |last1=Straubhaar |first2=Robert |last2=LaRose |first3=Lucinda |last3=Davenport |year=2016 |edition=9th |publisher=Cengage Learning |location=Boston |isbn=978-1-305-08035-5 |access-date=February 24, 2016 |archive-date=February 6, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210206085525/https://books.google.com/books?id=jUR-BAAAQBAJ&q=cb+radio+craze+smokey+and+the+bandit.&pg=PT405 |url-status=live}}</ref> Though the film ''[[Moonrunners]]'' (1975) is the precursor to the television series ''[[The Dukes of Hazzard]]'' (1979–1985), from the same creator and with many identical settings and concepts, the popularity of ''Smokey and the Bandit'' and similar films helped get the ''Dukes'' series on the air. Three actors from the main cast of ''The Dukes of Hazzard'' appear in small uncredited roles in ''Smokey and the Bandit'': [[Ben Jones (American actor and politician)|Ben Jones]], [[John Schneider (screen actor)|John Schneider]], and [[Sonny Shroyer]] (who played a police officer in both). In return, Reynolds portrayed [[Boss Hogg]] (originally portrayed by [[Sorrell Booke]]) in the film adaptation ''[[The Dukes of Hazzard (film)|The Dukes of Hazzard]]'' (2005). Reynolds is referenced by name in several early episodes of the series.

===Casting===
Before Gleason was cast in the film, [[Richard Boone]] was originally considered for the role of Buford T. Justice.<ref name="mentalfloss.com" /> [[Sally Field]] only accepted the part after her agent advised her that she needed a big movie role on her résumé. Universal executives initially resisted casting Field, claiming she was not attractive enough, but Reynolds insisted on her involvement. Field enjoyed making the film, but remembers that virtually the entire project was improvised.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.southbankcentre.co.uk/blog/8-sally-field's-most-memorable-characters |title=Southbank Centre}}</ref>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] of the film began on August 30, 1976.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.atlantatimemachine.com/smokey/poster.htm |title=Smokey & the Bandit poster |access-date=July 26, 2020 |archive-date=July 26, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200726233500/http://www.atlantatimemachine.com/smokey/poster.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> The movie was primarily filmed in Georgia, in the cities of [[McDonough, Georgia|McDonough]], [[Jonesboro, Georgia|Jonesboro]] and [[Lithonia, Georgia|Lithonia]]. The scenes set in [[Texarkana, Texas|Texarkana]] were filmed in Jonesboro and the surrounding area and many of the chase scenes were filmed in the surrounding areas on [[Georgia State Route 54|Highway 54]] between [[Fayetteville, Georgia|Fayetteville]] and Jonesboro for a majority of the driving scenes, Mundy's Mill Road, Main Street in Jonesboro, [[Georgia State Route 400]], [[Interstate 85 in Georgia|I-85]] (Pleasant Hill exit) and in McDonough. However, the scene where they drive through the [[Royal Dutch Shell|Shell]] gas station was filmed in [[Ojai, California]], on the corner of Ojai Avenue and El Paseo Road. Much of the surrounding scene comes from that immediate vicinity. The scene featuring the racetrack was filmed at [[Lakewood Speedway]] at the old [[Lakewood Fairgrounds]] on Atlanta's south side. The [[roller coaster]] in the movie was the Greyhound. It had not been used for some time and was repainted for the film. It was destroyed in ''[[Smokey and the Bandit II]]'' and in a flashback scene in ''Part 3''.<ref>{{cite web |title=Greyhound – Lakewood Park (Atlanta, Georgia, USA) |url=http://rcdb.com/1965.htm |publisher=Roller Coaster DataBase |access-date=March 21, 2013 |archive-date=March 17, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140317110927/http://rcdb.com/1965.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> The area around [[Helen, Georgia]], was used for some locations. The scene where Sheriff Justice's car has the door knocked off by a passing semi-truck was shot on [[Georgia State Route 75]], {{convert|3|miles|km}} north of Helen. The tow truck driver was a local garage owner, Berlin Wike. Reynolds and Field began dating during the filming.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://worldgarages.com/news/Burt-Reynolds-old-Pontiac-Trans-Am-replica-sold-for-317500 |title=Burt Reynolds' old Pontiac Trans Am replica sold for $317,500 |access-date=June 21, 2019 |archive-date=June 21, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190621202344/https://worldgarages.com/news/Burt-Reynolds-old-Pontiac-Trans-Am-replica-sold-for-317500 |url-status=live}}</ref>

According to an interview with Susie McIver Ewing on ''The RetroZest Podcast'', she was picked to play the role of Hot Pants Hilliard after the dailies footage (shot in the Atlanta area) of the original actress playing the role was inadvertently destroyed on its way from Atlanta to [[Los Angeles]]. The new Hot Pants scene had to quickly be refilmed as a part of a [[second unit]] in the [[San Fernando Valley]] and was shot at a drive-in restaurant near [[Hansen Dam]] named Baby Beef Burgers, which has since been demolished. This same restaurant was used for a scene in the 1983 Stephen King Movie ''[[Christine (1983 film)|Christine]]'', which was ultimately cut in the final film (it can be viewed as a deleted scene on the DVD release of the film).

===Vehicles===
[[File:1978 Trans-Am bandit.jpg|thumb|A 1978 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am in a similar specification to the car used in the movie]]
Hal Needham saw an advertisement for the soon-to-be-released 1977 [[Pontiac Firebird|Pontiac Trans Am]] and knew right away that it would be the Bandit's car, or, as Needham referred to it, a character in the movie. He contacted Pontiac and an agreement was made that four 1977 Trans Ams and two [[Pontiac LeMans]] four-door sedans would be provided for the movie.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.pontiacsonline.com/JIM%20WANGERS.htm |title=An Interview With Jim Wangers: Pontiac and the 1970's |first=John |last=Chung |website=PontiacsOnline.com |access-date=December 27, 2018 |archive-date=August 9, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160809150816/http://www.pontiacsonline.com/JIM%20WANGERS.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> The Trans Ams were actually 1976-model cars with 1977 front ends (from 1970 to 1976, both the Firebird/Trans Am and [[Chevrolet Camaro]] have two round headlights and in 1977, the Firebird/Trans Am was changed to four rectangular headlights, and the Camaro remained unchanged). The decals were changed to 1977-style units, as evidenced by the engine size callouts on the hood scoop being in liters rather than cubic inches, as had been the case in 1976. The hood scoop on these cars says "6.6 LITRE", which, in 1977, would have denoted an [[Oldsmobile]] 403-equipped car or a non-W-72, 180&nbsp;hp version of the 400 Pontiac engine.

The cars were 1976 models, the engines fitted to them were 455ci power plants, the last year these engines were offered for sale before withdrawal.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.iconicpremier.world/smokey-and-the-bandit-trans-am-collectors/ |title=Trans Am – ICONIC PREMIER |access-date=October 19, 2020 |archive-date=February 6, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210206091340/http://www.iconicpremier.world/smokey-and-the-bandit-trans-am-collectors/ |url-status=live}}</ref> All four of the cars were badly damaged during production,<ref>{{cite web |url=http://fortune.com/2011/02/09/how-smokey-and-the-bandit-was-born/ |title=How ''Smokey and the Bandit'' was born |first1=Scott |last1=Olster |first2=Hal |last2=Needham |work=Fortune |date=February 9, 2011 |access-date=December 27, 2018 |archive-date=October 27, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181027121433/http://fortune.com/2011/02/09/how-smokey-and-the-bandit-was-born/ |url-status=live}}</ref> one of which was all but destroyed during the jump over the dismantled Mulberry bridge. Needham served as the driver for the stunt (in place of Reynolds), while Lada St. Edmund was in the same car (in place of Field). By the film's ending, the final surviving Trans Am and LeMans were both barely running and the other cars had become parts donors to keep them running. This gives rise to various continuity errors with Justice's police cruiser, which during some chase sequences is shown with a black rear fender, which then reverts to the car's bronze color again in later scenes.  The car also sustains visible damage in various collisions which then disappears in later scenes.

The Burdettes' car is a 1974 [[Cadillac Eldorado]] convertible painted in a "Candy Red" color scheme and is seen briefly at the beginning of the movie and in the final scene as the Bandit, the Snowman, Fred the dog, and Frog use it to make their escape. Three [[Kenworth W900]] A short-frame semi trucks, driven by Reed, were each equipped with 38-inch sleepers. Two units were 1974 models, as evidenced by standard silver Kenworth emblems on the truck grille, and one unit was a 1973 model, as evidenced by the gold-painted Kenworth emblem on the truck's grille, signifying Kenworth's 50 years in business. The paint code for each truck was coffee brown with gold trims and the {{convert|48|ft|m|adj=on}} mural trailer used was manufactured by Hobbs Trailers in Texas with a non-operational Thermo King Refrigeration unit. This is obvious, because there is no fuel tank on the underside of the trailer to power the refrigeration unit, and the unit is never heard running.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.hankstruckpictures.com/forum01/index.php?topic%3D1459.0 |title=BIZARRE QUESTION |access-date=September 14, 2007 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071016064743/http://hankstruckpictures.com/forum01/index.php?topic=1459.0 |archive-date=October 16, 2007}}</ref>

===Legal status of Coors beer===
In 1977, [[Coors Brewing Company|Coors]] was unavailable for sale east of [[Texas]]. Its lack of additives and preservatives meant that Coors could spoil in one week without refrigeration, explaining the film's 28-hour deadline.<ref>{{cite web |last=Koerth-Baker |first=Maggie |title=How the Bandit, Coors and a bunch of Makers changed the course of booze history |url=http://boingboing.net/2010/11/30/how-the-bandit-coors.html |date=November 30, 2010 |website=Boing Boing |access-date=December 27, 2018 |archive-date=May 17, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130517180921/http://boingboing.net/2010/11/30/how-the-bandit-coors.html |url-status=live}}</ref> A 1974 ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine article explains that Coors was so coveted for its lack of stabilizers and preservatives, and Coors Banquet Beer had a brief renaissance. Future President [[Gerald Ford]], after a trip to [[Colorado]], hid it in his luggage to take it back to [[Washington, D.C.]] President [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] had a steady supply airlifted by the [[United States Air Force|Air Force]] to Washington. [[Carl Yastrzemski]] of the [[Boston Red Sox]] would bring home several cases, after playing on the West Coast, by stashing them in the equipment trunks on the team's plane. Frederick Amon smuggled it once a week from Colorado to [[North Carolina]] and sold it for four times the retail price.<ref>{{cite magazine |title=BREWING: The Beer That Won the West |magazine=Time |date=February 11, 1974 |url=http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,908509,00.html |url-access=subscription |access-date=November 21, 2012 |archive-date=November 22, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121122220018/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,908509,00.html |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Coors, now owned jointly by the [[Pete Coors|Coors]] and [[Molson family|Molson families]] and based in [[Montréal]], [[Quebec]], is still brewed just outside [[Denver]], Colorado.  It is now sold in all states, as Molson ships it in refrigerated train cars and bottled locally and sold in different parts of the country, including the eastern United States.

The theme music "East Bound and Down" was sung and co-written by Reed (credited under his birth name, Jerry Hubbard) and [[Dick Feller]]. It became Reed's signature song and is on multiple albums, including ''Country Legends'' and his live album ''Jerry Reed "Live!" (Still)''. In 1991, it was arranged for orchestra by Crafton Beck and recorded by [[Erich Kunzel]] and the [[Cincinnati Pops Orchestra]] for their album ''Down on the Farm''. Several other groups, such as the U.S. [[Rock music|rock]] band [[Tonic (band)|Tonic]] and U.K.-based [[Country music|country]] cover band We Be Ploughin' have also covered it. Reed also penned and performed the song for the opening credits, titled "The Legend", which tells of some of the Bandit's escapades before the events of the film, and the ballad "The Bandit", which is in several versions in the movie and on the soundtrack. [[Bill Justis]] is the first name on the credits for the soundtrack, as he composed and arranged original music throughout the film. Musicians such as [[Beegie Adair]] and George Tidwell<ref>{{cite web |first=George |last=Tidwell |url=https://www.allmusic.com/artist/george-tidwell-mn0000768487/credits |title=Allmusic |publisher=Allmusic |access-date=July 9, 2019 |archive-date=July 7, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180707200749/https://www.allmusic.com/artist/george-tidwell-mn0000768487/credits |url-status=live}}</ref> played on the soundtrack. Five-string banjo player Bobby Thompson is prominent toward the end of "East Bound and Down". ==Reception==
===Box office===
''Smokey and the Bandit'' was a [[sleeper hit]].<ref>{{cite news |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |title=July Key-City B.O. A Record Buster |last=Murphy |first=AD |date=August 17, 1977 |page=1}}</ref> It premiered at [[Radio City Music Hall]] in [[New York City]], where it performed poorly.<ref>{{cite news |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |title='Star Wars'; What Else Was News in 1977 |last=Frederick |first=Robert B. |date=January 4, 1978 |page=21}}</ref> It then opened in just the South of the United States over the Memorial Day weekend and grossed $2,689,851 in 386 theaters. By the end of June, it had played in major Southern markets, including [[Charlotte, North Carolina|Charlotte]], Atlanta, [[Jacksonville, Florida|Jacksonville]], [[New Orleans]], [[Memphis, Tennessee|Memphis]], [[Dallas]], and [[Oklahoma City]], grossing $11.9 million. It opened in other Northern states at the end of July.<ref>{{cite magazine |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |title='Smokey' Doing Fine: $11.9 Mil in Four Weeks |date=July 6, 1977 |page=3}}</ref> With an original budget of $5.3 million (cut to $4.3 million two days before initial production),<ref name="CNN" /> the film eventually grossed $126,737,428 in North America,<ref name="BOM">{{cite web |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=smokeyandthebandit.htm |website=[[Box Office Mojo]] |title=Smokey and the Bandit, Box Office Information |access-date=January 29, 2012 |archive-date=January 29, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120129044959/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=smokeyandthebandit.htm |url-status=live}}</ref> making it the [[1977 in film|second-highest-grossing movie of 1977]] (only ''[[Star Wars (film)|Star Wars]]'' earned more, with $221.3 million). The worldwide gross is estimated at over $300 million.<ref name="CNN" /> Reynolds said in 2015 that he most enjoyed this film, and had the most fun making it, of his career.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://cwatlanta.cbslocal.com/2015/07/21/burt-reynolds-at-the-2015-macon-film-festival/ |title=Burt Reynolds at the 2015 Macon Film Festival |date=July 21, 2015 |website=Atlanta's CW69 |access-date=March 27, 2017 |archive-date=May 28, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160528154222/http://cwatlanta.cbslocal.com/2015/07/21/burt-reynolds-at-the-2015-macon-film-festival/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On the review aggregation website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 71% based on 38 reviews. The site's critics consensus states: "Not much in the head but plenty beneath the hood, ''Smokey and the Bandit'' is infectious fun with plenty of car wrecks to keep your eyes glued".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/smokey_and_the_bandit/ |title=Smokey and the Bandit (1977) |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=April 14, 2026 |archive-date=May 23, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190523213454/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/smokey_and_the_bandit/ |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 50 out of 100, based on six critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Smokey and the Bandit'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/smokey-and-the-bandit |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=June 22, 2025 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

Film critic [[Leonard Maltin]] gave the film a good rating (3 stars out of a possible 4) and characterized it as "about as subtle as ''[[The Three Stooges]]'', but a classic compared to the sequels and countless rip-offs which followed".<ref>{{cite book |title=Leonard Maltin's Movie & Video Guide: 2004 Edition |editor1-last=Maltin |editor1-first=Leonard |year=2003 |publisher=Penguin}}</ref> In his review in the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'', [[Gene Siskel]] gave the film two stars and complained that the film failed to tell the audience when the clock started on the beer run, thus reducing suspense. He claimed that the Bandit is never made aware of Frog (Field)'s leaving Junior at the altar, which is why the Bandit continually asks why a Texas sheriff is chasing him.<ref>{{cite news |last=Siskel |first=Gene |date=August 4, 1977 |url=https://chicagotribune.newspapers.com/clip/52871701/gene-siskel-movie-reviewsthe-bad/ |title=The game is good news in 'Bears' |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200606031712/https://chicagotribune.newspapers.com/clip/52871701/gene-siskel-movie-reviewsthe-bad/ |archive-date=2020-06-06 |newspaper=[[Chicago Tribune]] |page=6}}</ref> The film's editors, [[Walter Hannemann]] and [[Angelo Ross]], were nominated for the [[Academy Award for Best Film Editing]] at the [[50th Academy Awards]].

[[Alfred Hitchcock]] claimed that the film was one of his favorites.<ref>{{cite web |date=July 15, 2012 |author=Stephen Saito |website=MoveableFest.com |url=http://moveablefest.com/seven-quirkiest-favorite-films-of-seriously-famous-directors/ |title=Seven Quirky Lowbrow Favorite Films of Highbrow Directors |access-date=September 6, 2018 |archive-date=September 7, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180907032544/http://moveablefest.com/seven-quirkiest-favorite-films-of-seriously-famous-directors/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Upon meeting Reynolds, [[Billy Bob Thornton]] told him that Americans from the region where the film is set consider the film to be less cinema and more documentary.<ref>{{cite magazine |date=October 25, 2013 |first=Stephen |last=Rebello |url=http://playboysfw.kinja.com/remembering-hal-needham-and-the-birth-of-redneck-cinema-1452470692 |title=Remembering Hal Needham and the Birth of Redneck Cinema |magazine=[[Playboy]] |access-date=May 22, 2017 |archive-date=August 18, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180818115208/https://playboysfw.kinja.com/remembering-hal-needham-and-the-birth-of-redneck-cinema-1452470692 |url-status=live}}</ref> ''Smokey and the Bandit'' was released in the United Kingdom on August 28 and was a success there, garnering positive reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.radiotimes.com/film/jdspv/smokey-and-the-bandit |title=Smokey and the Bandit – Film from RadioTimes |website=RadioTimes |access-date=December 27, 2016 |archive-date=December 27, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161227130846/http://www.radiotimes.com/film/jdspv/smokey-and-the-bandit |url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2025, ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' listed ''Smokey and the Bandit'' as having the best stunts of 1977.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Doherty |first=Thomas |date=June 7, 2025 |title=The Best Stunts of All Time, Over Nearly 100 Years of the Oscars |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-stunts-all-time-movies-oscars-stunt-design-100-years/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250720043450/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-stunts-all-time-movies-oscars-stunt-design-100-years/ |archive-date=July 20, 2025}}</ref>

==Cultural influence==
===Pontiac Trans Am===
After the debut of the film, the Pontiac Trans Am became wildly popular, with sales almost doubling within two years of the film's release. It outsold its [[Chevrolet Camaro]] counterpart for the first time.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0076729/trivia |title=Smokey and the Bandit (1977) |work=IMDb |access-date=February 24, 2016 |archive-date=March 19, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160319232108/http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0076729/trivia |url-status=live}}</ref> Reynolds was given the 1977 vehicle used during promotion of the film as a gift, though the car itself never actually appeared in the film. Because of the popularity of the film and the sales success of the Trans Am, the president of [[Pontiac (automobile)|Pontiac]] Alex Mair promised to supply Reynolds with a Trans Am each year. Due to his financial difficulties in 2014, Reynolds put his vast collection of artwork and memorabilia up for auction, including the Trans Am. High estimates for the car were up to $80,000, but the actual sale price was $450,000. Also up for auction was a [[go-kart]] replica of the car, which sold for nearly $14,000.<ref>{{cite magazine |last1=Nunez |first1=Alex |title=Burt Reynolds' Bandit Trans Am sells for $450,000 |url=http://www.roadandtrack.com/car-culture/entertainment/news/a24515/burt-reynolds-bandit-trans-am-sells-for-450000/ |magazine=[[Road & Track]] |access-date=August 10, 2015 |date=December 15, 2014 |archive-date=August 16, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150816033951/http://www.roadandtrack.com/car-culture/entertainment/news/a24515/burt-reynolds-bandit-trans-am-sells-for-450000/ |url-status=live}}</ref> In 2015, a Florida-based automobile customization company built 77 Trans Ams modeled after the car that Reynolds drove in the film. These new models were built off the same Camaro platform, came with the Pontiac arrowhead, flaming bird, and Bandit logos, and the instrument panels, center consoles, and hood scoops emulating their 1977 counterparts, and were signed by Reynolds. Some differences included the use of a supercharged 454-CID (7.4-liter) Chevrolet-sourced engine that put out 840 HP, and four round headlights, which appeared on the 1967–69 Firebirds/Trans Ams only; the actual 1977–81 models had rectangular headlights.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Hlavaty |first1=Craig |title=See a "New" Vintage Bandit Trans Am Approved by Burt Reynolds Himself |url=https://www.chron.com/cars/news/article/See-a-new-vintage-Bandit-Trans-Am-approved-by-7217360.php |newspaper=[[Houston Chronicle]] |access-date=June 10, 2016 |date=March 30, 2016 |archive-date=May 29, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160529190654/http://www.chron.com/cars/news/article/See-a-new-vintage-Bandit-Trans-Am-approved-by-7217360.php |url-status=live}}</ref>

===''The Dukes of Hazzard''===
A TV series, titled ''[[The Dukes of Hazzard]]'', was produced by [[Warner Bros. Television]] and created by [[Gy Waldron]]. It was aired on [[CBS]] from January 26, 1979 to February 8, 1985, which was inspired by the movie's success. Elements from the movie influenced the show, such as Sally Field's character in the movie, whose costuming was notably similar to Daisy Duke and featured small shorts.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Chandrasekhar |first=Jay |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JqpEDAAAQBAJ&dq=dukes+of+hazzard+moonrunners&pg=PA210 |title=Mustache Shenanigans: Making Super Troopers and Other Adventures in Comedy |date=2017-03-28 |publisher=Penguin |isbn=978-1-101-98525-0 |pages=210 |language=en}}</ref>

===Diablo sandwich===
The "diablo sandwich" ordered by Sheriff Justice in the [[Arkansas]] [[barbecue restaurant]] scene has entered popular culture as a minor reference to the film. Though no authoritative source identifies the composition of the sandwich, there are several possibilities. A segment of the [[CMT (U.S. TV channel)|CMT]] program ''Reel Eats'' used a [[sloppy joe]]-style recipe consisting of seasoned [[ground beef]], [[Corn kernel|corn]], and [[sour cream]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://oaklandcountymoms.com/diablo-sandwich-recipe-cmts-reel-eats-33963/ |title=Diablo Sandwich Recipe from CMT's Reel Eats |first=Lisa |last=LaGrou |date=February 9, 2014 |publisher=Oakland County Moms |access-date=March 1, 2016 |archive-date=February 22, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160222051857/http://oaklandcountymoms.com/diablo-sandwich-recipe-cmts-reel-eats-33963/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Other sources in East Texas (whence Sheriff Justice hails) are familiar with the popular regional delicacy known as the Diablo Sandwich. It consists of any of the various [[Louisiana]]-style hot sauces on [[Texas toast]]-style bread alongside the fourth most famous product of [[Pittsburg, Texas]] (behind [[Pilgrim's Pride]], Cavender's, and [[Carroll Shelby]])—Pittsburg Hot Links.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.roadfood.com/Forums/Diablo-Sandwich-quotDiablo-amp-Docquot-From-Smokey-amp-the-Bandit-Identified-m778897.aspx |title=Diablo Sandwich / "Diablo & Doc" From Smokey & the Bandit, Identified! |publisher=[[Roadfood.com]] |access-date=March 1, 2016 |archive-date=March 3, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303194001/http://www.roadfood.com/Forums/Diablo-Sandwich-quotDiablo-amp-Docquot-From-Smokey-amp-the-Bandit-Identified-m778897.aspx |url-status=live}}</ref>

===The Bandit Run===
The first run in 2007 to celebrate the 30th anniversary of the movie, [[The Bandit Run]] was the brainchild of Dave Hall, owner of Restore A Muscle Car. A group of Trans Am owners and fans of the movie take part in an annual road trip from Texarkana to Jonesboro, recreating the route taken by the characters in the film. The Bandit Run quickly became a fixture, celebrating the 40th anniversary of the movie with a special 2017 screening of the film attended by Reynolds and a recreation of the jump undertaken by the Bandit and Frog across a river.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://thebanditrun.com/ |title=The Bandit Run – Brought to you by Restore A Muscle Car |website=The Bandit Run |access-date=June 25, 2017 |archive-date=June 24, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170624024611/http://thebanditrun.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

The commercials were produced after Stewart mentioned that the movie was one of his favorites.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.nascar.com/en_us/news-media/articles/2014/3/6/tony-stewart-bandit-commercial-mobil-1-ad.html |title=Stewart plays off 'Smokey and the Bandit in Mobil 1 ad |access-date=April 6, 2017 |archive-date=April 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170407054208/http://www.nascar.com/en_us/news-media/articles/2014/3/6/tony-stewart-bandit-commercial-mobil-1-ad.html |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Television censorship and alternative versions==
{{more citations needed section|date=September 2018}}
When ''Smokey and the Bandit'' first aired on American network television in early 1979, [[Censorship|censors]] were faced with the challenge of toning down the raw language of the original film. For this purpose, they overdubbed dialogue deemed offensive, which was common practice. The most noted change made for network broadcast was the replacing of Buford's often-spoken phrase "sumbitch" (a contraction of "son of a bitch"; usually in reference to the Bandit) with the phrase "scum bum". This phrase achieved a level of popularity with children and the 2007 [[Hot Wheels]] release of the 1970 Firebird Trans Am has "scum bum" emblazoned on its ducktail.

Except for Gleason, the original actors mostly redubbed their lines for the television version. Actor [[Henry Corden]], who voiced [[Fred Flintstone]] after original performer [[Alan Reed]] died the same year the film was released, was used to replace a considerable amount of Sheriff Justice's dialogue. In the U.K., the heavily dubbed version was shown for several years, particularly by the [[BBC]]. However, in more recent years, the original version has been shown (on [[ITV Network|ITV]], a commercial channel), usually with the stronger language edited out, often quite awkwardly. The theatrical release has a few lines deleted, including an edit in which Sheriff Justice tells a state trooper to "[[fuck]] off" after the sheriff called his vehicle "a piece of crap". His expletive is obscured when a passing [[Semi-trailer truck|big rig]] sounds its horn. At the time, using "fuck" would immediately require an [[Motion picture rating system|R rating]] and this self-censorship allowed the film to avoid this rating and reach a much larger audience, however the scene and the obscured expletive were played for comedy value and written as such, with the passing truck being the gag of the scene more than a way to avoid the censors.

In 2006, a DVD re-release was issued of ''Smokey and the Bandit'' featuring a digitally remastered audio track with 5.1 Dolby-compatible surround sound. Many of the film's original sounds were replaced. For instance, the diesel engine start and run-up sequence in the opening sequence of the film were completely dubbed over with a new sound. A few other examples of sound effect replacement occur when the Bandit takes off after getting the reluctant Cledus involved in the bet, the Bandit comes to a screeching halt on a roadway moments before picking up Carrie, and when Buford's car top comes off. Some of the original sound effects (such as Cledus's dog Fred barking) and music (such as the final chase to the Southern Classic) were removed and not replaced. Earlier DVD releases and the 40th Anniversary Blu-ray of the film have the original soundtrack intact.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.thedigitalbits.com/item/smokey-and-the-bandit-40th-brd |title=Smokey and the Bandit: 40th Anniversary Edition |last=Salmons |first=Tim |date=May 29, 2017 |work=The Digital Bits |access-date=November 12, 2017 |language=en-GB |archive-date=November 11, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171111132707/http://www.thedigitalbits.com/item/smokey-and-the-bandit-40th-brd |url-status=live}}</ref> Major portions of the audio background have been modified with different engine sounds or tire squeals from the original film. The updated version of the film features sound inaccurate for what would be produced by the Trans Am or the numerous other Pontiac vehicles in the film. The original film had correct sounds that were usually recorded live as the action took place.

==Sequels==
The film was followed by two theatrical [[Smokey and the Bandit (franchise)|sequels]]: ''[[Smokey and the Bandit II]]'' (1980) and ''[[Smokey and the Bandit Part 3]]'' (1983). The second film was a modest box office success, earning $66.1 million against a $17 million budget.

The third film – which had no involvement from either Hal Needham or Sally Field and contained only a short cameo appearance by Burt Reynolds – revolved entirely around Jackie Gleason's character, was panned by critics, and was a [[box office bomb]], earning only $7 million against a $9 million budget.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=smokeyandthebandit2.htm |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]] |title=Smokey and the Bandit II, Box Office Information |access-date=August 25, 2012 |archive-date=July 21, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120721175259/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=smokeyandthebandit2.htm |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |title=The Unstoppables |publisher=New York, New York: Sussex Publishers, LLC |author=Spy |author-link=Spy (magazine) |journal=Spy: The New York Monthly |date=Nov 1988 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=KJly6nVC7qkC&q=An+Officer+and+a+Gentleman+1982+budget&pg=PA92 |issn=0890-1759 |page=92 |access-date=October 20, 2020 |archive-date=February 6, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210206085526/https://books.google.com/books?id=KJly6nVC7qkC&q=An+Officer+and+a+Gentleman+1982+budget&pg=PA92 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>[https://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1983/0SNB3.php Box Office Information for ''Smokey and the Bandit Part 3''.] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130921130005/http://www.the-numbers.com/movies/1983/0SNB3.php |date=September 21, 2013 }} The Numbers.
