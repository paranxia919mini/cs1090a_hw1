'''''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night''''' ({{langx|fa|دختری در شب تنها به خانه می‌رود}} {{transliteration|fa|''Dokhtari dar šab tanhâ be xâne miravad''}}) is a 2014 [[Persian language|Persian]]-language [[horror Western]] film<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2015/jul/24/a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-dvd-blu-ray|title=A Girl Walks Home Alone At Night: 'the first Iranian vampire western'|last=Lyne|first=Charlie|date=2015-07-24|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=December 10, 2016}}</ref> written and directed by [[Ana Lily Amirpour]] and produced in the United States. Promoted as "The first [[Cinema of Iran|Iranian]] [[Vampire film|vampire]] Western", it stars [[Sheila Vand]], Arash Marandi, [[Mozhan Marnò]], [[Marshall Manesh]], and [[Dominic Rains]]. It was financed in part by a [[crowdfunding]] campaign on [[Indiegogo]].

''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night'' takes place in "the Iranian [[Ghost town|ghost-town]] Bad City" and depicts the doings of "a lonesome vampire".<ref name="1stwave">{{cite web |last=Barton |first=Steve |date=December 5, 2013 |title=First Wave of Sundance 2014 Films Announced; See the Horror Highlights Here |website=[[Dread Central]] |url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/50233/first-wave-of-sundance-2014-films-announced-see-the-horror-highlights-here/}}</ref> It was filmed in [[Taft, California]], in [[black-and-white]]. It was chosen to show in the "Next" program of the [[2014 Sundance Film Festival]].<ref name= "lineup">{{cite news|last=Barnes|first=Brooks|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/12/05/movies/a-sundance-lineup-laced-with-race-horror-and-family-turmoil.html|title=A Sundance Lineup Laced With Race, Horror and Family Turmoil|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=December 4, 2013|url-access=limited|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150910000522/http://www.nytimes.com/2013/12/05/movies/a-sundance-lineup-laced-with-race-horror-and-family-turmoil.html|archive-date=September 10, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
A hard-working young Iranian man named Arash lives with and takes care of his [[Opioid use disorder|heroin-addicted]] father, Hossein. They are harassed by a ruthless, [[Drug dealer|drug-dealing]] [[Procuring (prostitution)|pimp]] named Saeed, who seizes the young man's prized car in exchange for money the father owes him. In a crime of opportunity, Arash steals a pair of diamond earrings from the wealthy young woman he works for, Shaydah.

Saeed comes across a strange young woman in a [[chador]] at night. She persuades Saeed to take her back to his apartment. While there, she grows long [[fang]]s and first bites off his finger, then goes for his neck, killing him. As she leaves, she passes by Arash, who has come to offer the earrings for his car. He finds Saeed dead, and takes back his car keys along with a suitcase of drugs and cash. Arash decides to sell the drugs, allowing him to quit his job working for Shaydah. Later, he goes to a costume party at a nightclub dressed up as [[Count Dracula|Dracula]], where he is persuaded by Shaydah into taking one of the [[MDMA|ecstasy]] pills he is selling. Under the influence, he becomes disoriented, and ends up lost at night on the street.

The woman with the chador spends her time listening to music alone in her apartment, [[skateboarding]], or bedeviling pedestrians at night, until she comes across the lost Arash. He shows vulnerability and compassion, and she takes him to her home, where they listen to music, and she resists his exposed neck. They meet the next night, and she says that he does not know the terrible things she has done. He is unfazed, gives her the earrings and, at her request, pierces her ears with a [[safety pin]], but she eventually leaves.

Atti, a prostitute who worked for Saeed, is followed at night by the woman, and they retreat to the prostitute's apartment. The woman gives Atti the payment Saeed owed her. They have a conversation during which the woman realizes that Atti no longer remembers what it is to desire. She leaves.

Suffering from [[heroin withdrawal]], Hossein has an episode where he believes that Arash's cat is his dead wife. Infuriated by his father, Arash gives him drugs and money and throws him out, telling him to take the cat with him. Hossein goes to Atti and forces her to take heroin with him. They are found by the woman, who kills Hossein. After Atti helps her dispose of Hossein's body, she tells the woman to take the cat and leave.

The following morning, Arash discovers Hossein's body. Distressed, he runs to the woman's apartment and begs her to run away with him. As she is gathering up her things, the cat appears and Arash realizes that she had something to do with his father's death. Arash and the woman drive off together, but he pulls off to the side of the road, unsure about what to do. He eventually gets back in the car and the two continue their trip onwards.

== Themes ==
Director Ana Lily Amirpour, regarding the mythology of the vampire, has stated that: "A vampire is so many things: serial killer, a romantic, a historian, a drug addict – they're sort of all these things in one."<ref name=":3">{{Cite magazine|last=Watercutter|first=Angela|date=2014-02-05|title=Meet the Woman Who Directed the World's Only Iranian Vampire Western|magazine=[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]]|url=https://www.wired.com/2014/02/girl-walks-home-alone-at-night/|access-date=2020-04-30}}</ref> The film examines these facets of a vampire as described by Amirpour in a variety of ways.

The film's protagonist, The Girl, can be seen as an [[antihero]] [[vigilantism|vigilante]] with a taste for bad men. As a vampire, she is able to roam the streets at night without being concerned for her safety, subverting the implications of the film's title.<ref>{{Cite magazine|last=Romney|first=Jonathan|title=Film of the Week: A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night|url=https://www.filmcomment.com/blog/film-of-the-week-a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night/|date=2014-11-19|magazine=[[Film Comment]]|access-date=2020-04-30}}</ref> She is the perpetrator and possesses agency and power atypical of a young woman, especially in a crime-ridden urban setting such as Bad City. In this sense, the film has feminist leanings.

Rockabilly, a gender-bending, minor background character who exists on the fringe of Bad City throughout the duration of the movie, acts as a silent observer to the events around them. Director Amirpour states, "If there's one political thing [in the film], it's not the chador, it's Rockabilly, because it's not okay to be [[LGBT history in Iran|gay in Iran]]."<ref name=":3"/>

==Production==
An early short film with the same title from Amirpour screened at festivals and won Best Short Film at the [[Noor Iranian Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://noorfilmfestival.com/index.php/2012-festival-awards|title=2012 NIFF Award Winners|publisher=[[Noor Iranian Film Festival]]|access-date=October 16, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304091455/http://noorfilmfestival.com/index.php/2012-festival-awards|archive-date=March 4, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

An [[Indiegogo]] campaign was launched in July 2012 to fund the feature-length version of the film. On August 27, 2012, the campaign's goal of $55,000 was surpassed.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiegogo.com/projects/a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-feature-film#/updates|title=August 27, 2012 Indiegogo Update|access-date=July 1, 2017|via=[[Indiegogo]]}}</ref> The project ended up with a total sum of $56,903 raised by 290 backers.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiegogo.com/projects/a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-feature-film#/|title=A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night Indiegogo Page|access-date=July 1, 2017|via=[[Indiegogo]]}}</ref>

''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night'' was shot over the course of twenty-four days, in the town of [[Taft, California|Taft]] in [[Kern County, California|Kern County]] in [[Southern California]]. This allowed Amirpour to not be constrained by the restrictions of filming in Iran, which include having to obtain a filming permit and facing content censorship, such as the prohibition of almost all depictions of physical romantic love.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://dailybruin.com/2015/01/08/cross-cut-iranian-filmmakers-seek-artistic-freedom|title=Cross Cut: Iranian filmmakers seek artistic freedom|last=Li|first=Eileen|date=January 8, 2015|newspaper=[[Daily Bruin]]|access-date=September 9, 2021}}</ref>

The film premiered at the [[2014 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 20, 2014.<ref name=":2">{{Cite magazine|title=Know Thyself: Ana Lily Amirpour on the Question "Why" and A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night|url=https://www.moviemaker.com/know-thyself-ana-lily-amirpour-a-girl-walks-home-alone-night/|last=Amirpour|first=Ana Lily|date=2014-11-18|magazine=[[MovieMaker]]|access-date=2020-04-30}}</ref>

In an interview with ''[[MovieMaker]]'', Amirpour spoke regarding her relationship with filmmaking:

<blockquote>People ask me, "Why did you make ''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night''?" My answer is that I was lonely—that's why. But taking that a step further, the truth is that I make films to make friends and find real intimacy; a connection with others based on something that's meaningful to me. The people who make these films with you, your cast and crew—it's like they're on a vision quest with you. That is an incomparable experience. And then when the film is done and out there, the people who are attracted to your film—the audiences, festivals that embrace it, other filmmakers, artists, the critics who like what you do—those are my friends. And I don't expect to be friends with everyone.</blockquote>

Amirpour is a lifelong skater, and was a stand-in for the skateboarding sequences present in the film.<ref name="Chador">{{Cite news|last=Ito|first=Robert|date=2014-11-12|title=The Shadow in the Chador|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2014/11/16/movies/ana-lily-amirpours-world-a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night.html|access-date=2020-05-01|url-access=limited|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151107101257/https://www.nytimes.com/2014/11/16/movies/ana-lily-amirpours-world-a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night.html|archive-date=November 7, 2015|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Style==
The film is directed and imagined by Amirpour with cinematography by Lyle Vincent.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Schager |first=Nick |date=January 9, 2015 |title=A striking vampire film deserves to be among the Best Cinematography nominees |url=https://www.avclub.com/a-striking-vampire-film-deserves-to-be-among-the-best-c-1798275387 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |access-date=January 12, 2023}}</ref> Its style is clearly inspired by [[Spaghetti Western]]s like those of [[Sergio Leone]], featuring a mysterious, lone, [[antihero]] with a vigilante streak. However, the genre is reimagined with a female lead, and is a hybrid spaghetti Western-vampire film.<ref>{{Cite journal|last1=Abdi|first1=Shadee|last2=Calafell|first2=Bernadette Marie|date=March 16, 2017|title=Queer utopias and a (Feminist) Iranian vampire: a critical analysis of resistive monstrosity in ''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night''|journal=[[Critical Studies in Media Communication]]|volume=34|issue=4|pages=358–370|doi=10.1080/15295036.2017.1302092|s2cid=151915636|issn=1529-5036}}</ref> As a vampire film it serves as an homage to its legacy of predecessors especially the 1922 [[German Expressionism|German Expressionist]] film ''[[Nosferatu]]''. Echoes of the film are seen in the choice of shooting in black-and-white, the use of shadows, and the minimal dialogue.<ref name=":0" /> The film's dialogue is entirely in [[Persian language|Persian]], and the film blends elements of Iranian culture with the spaghetti Western-vampire imagery described above.<ref>{{Cite news|last=O'Sullivan|first=Michael|date=2015-01-08|title='A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night' movie review|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/goingoutguide/movies/a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-movie-review/2015/01/07/2c254caa-95c8-11e4-aabd-d0b93ff613d5_story.html|access-date=2020-05-01}}</ref>

The film was shot digitally with [[anamorphic lens]]es, which Amirpour and Vincent selected in an effort to emphasize the bleak, otherworldly atmosphere of the film.<ref name="Variety"/>

Amirpour has stated that graphic novels are a major source of inspiration for her. The visual language of the film is not unlike that of a comic book, with its "high-contrast monochrome aesthetic". Additionally, Bad City, the fictional location in which the film is set, may perhaps be a nod to Frank Miller's ''[[Sin City]]''.<ref name="Variety"/>

Amirpour has said that she had her actors watch ''Nosferatu'' and many spaghetti Westerns in preparation for their roles.<ref name="Chador"/>

She has also said that "every piece of the story, every character, every costume, every bit of music" is something that she "love[s] to the point of obsession."<ref name=":2" />

==Reception==
The film received positive reviews from critics. On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 96% based on 134 reviews, with an average rating of 8/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "''A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night'' blends conventional elements into something brilliantly original – and serves as a striking calling card for writer-director Ana Lily Amirpour."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/a_girl_walks_home_alone_at_night|title=A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=June 11, 2024}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 81 out of 100, based on 28 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{Cite web |title=A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night/ |access-date=2026-04-18 |website=Metacritic |language=en}}</ref>

Guy Lodge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' said in his review that "Ana Lily Amirpour's auspicious debut feature is a sly, slinky vampire romance set in an imaginary Iranian underworld".<ref name="Variety" /> Andrew O'Hehir of ''[[Salon (website)|Salon]]'' called the film "the year's biggest discovery" and praised its [[Feminism|feminist]] themes.<ref>{{cite web|last=O'Hehir|first=Andrew|title="A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night": The black-and-white, feminist Iranian vampire western you've been waiting for|work=[[Salon (website)|Salon]]|url=http://www.salon.com/2014/11/21/a_girl_walks_home_alone_at_night_the_black_and_white_feminist_iranian_vampire_western_youve_been_waiting_for/|date=November 20, 2014|access-date=September 7, 2015}}</ref> Boyd van Hoeij, in his review for ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'', praised the film, saying "this moody and gorgeous film is finally more about atmosphere and emotions than narrative {{ndash}} and none the worse for it".<ref>{{cite magazine|last=van Hoeij|first=Boyd|title=A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night: Sundance Review|magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/a-girl-walks-home-alone-672551/|date=January 20, 2014|access-date=January 12, 2023}}</ref> Drew Taylor of [[IndieWire]] graded the film A− and said that it gives "the impression that you're witnessing something iconic and important unfold before you".<ref>{{cite web|last=Taylor|first=Drew|title=New Directors/New Films Review: 'A Girl Walks Home Alone At Night' Is A New Vampire Classic|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/new-directors-new-films-review-a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-is-a-new-vampire-classic-20140320|website=[[IndieWire]]|date=March 20, 2014 |access-date=May 7, 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141129121135/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/new-directors-new-films-review-a-girl-walks-home-alone-at-night-is-a-new-vampire-classic-20140320|archive-date=November 29, 2014}}</ref>

==Adaptations==
The film was adapted into a six-part [[graphic novel]] series published by Radco in 2014.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Hanley|first=Ken W.|url=http://www.fangoria.com/new/qa-radcos-jon-conrad-on-environmental-horror-graphic-novel-bad-summer/|title=Q&A: Radco's Jon Conrad on Environmental Horror Graphic Novel "BAD SUMMER"|magazine=[[Fangoria]]|date=March 23, 2015|access-date=March 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150501115528/http://www.fangoria.com/new/qa-radcos-jon-conrad-on-environmental-horror-graphic-novel-bad-summer/|archive-date=May 1, 2015}}</ref> The series explores The Girl's backstory with art from Michael DeWeese.<ref>{{Cite magazine|title=First look: Vampire film 'Girl Walks Home Alone at Night,' as a comic|url=https://ew.com/article/2014/10/13/a-girl-walks-home-alone-night/|date=2014-10-13|last=Collis|first=Clark|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|access-date=2020-05-01|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150215065405/https://ew.com/article/2014/10/13/a-girl-walks-home-alone-night/|archive-date=February 15, 2015}}</ref>
