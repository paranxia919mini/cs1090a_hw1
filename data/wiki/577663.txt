'''''Singles''''' is a 1992 American [[romantic comedy]] film written, co-produced, and directed by [[Cameron Crowe]], and starring [[Bridget Fonda]], [[Campbell Scott]], [[Kyra Sedgwick]], and [[Matt Dillon]]. It features appearances from several musicians prominent in the early 1990s [[grunge]] movement in [[Seattle]].

The film was distributed by [[Warner Bros. Pictures|Warner Bros.]] and released theatrically on September 18, 1992, to positive reviews from critics and moderate box office success, grossing over $18 million.

"It's the story of disconnected single people making their way, forming their own unspoken family," Crowe wrote.<ref name="make"/>

== Plot ==
{{Expand section|date=August 2026}}
''Singles'' centers on the precarious romantic lives of a group of twentysomethings in Seattle, Washington, at the start of the 1990s [[grunge]] phenomenon. Most of the characters dwell in an apartment block, a sign in front of which advertises "Singles" (studio apartments) for rent. Divided into chapters, the film focuses on the course of two couples' rocky romances, as well as the love lives of their friends and associates.

The film revolves around Janet Livermore, a coffee-bar waitress fawning over Cliff Poncier, an aspiring yet slightly aloof grunge rock musician of the fictional grunge/rock band Citizen Dick; Linda Powell and Steve Dunne, a couple wavering on whether to commit to each other; Debbie Hunt, trying to find Mr. Right.

== Production ==
===Development===
Cameron Crowe said the film was "a love letter to Seattle, a romance set in that city. It had a really simple theme—single people coming together to create their own family."<ref name="Emery p96">Emery p96</ref>

Crowe rewrote the script after the death of singer [[Andrew_Wood_(singer)|Andy Wood]]. He wanted to capture "the story of how people instinctively need to be together. Is anybody truly single?"<ref name="make"/>

Crowe said the critical success of ''[[Say Anything...]]'' along with "the success of Nora Ephron's movies, gave Warner Bros. the feeling that people might embrace a love story within a romantic comedy."<ref name="Emery p96"/>

===Casting===
Crowe wanted the film to be an ensemble piece about six characters with "the feeling that you can easily follow any one of these actors and their character off into their own little movie."<ref>Emery p 96</ref>

Crowe wrote Bridget Fonda's part specifically for her, having been impressed by her performance in ''[[Scandal (1989 film)|Scandal]]''. He later wrote, "For months, in person or on the telephone, we've discussed every aspect of ''Singles''. Like a dry young Barbara Stanwyck, she nailed down every small detail of Janet's life."<ref name="make"/>

[[Johnny Depp]] was offered the role of Steve Dunne but declined.<ref>{{cite web|last=Chacksfield|first=Marc|title=Cameron Crowe On Cusack, Cruise and Zoos |url=https://www.shortlist.com/news/cameron-crowe|work=[[ShortList]]|date=March 12, 2012|access-date=September 3, 2026}}</ref>

Campbell Scott was cast on the same day he was cast as Julia Roberts' co star in ''[[Dying Young]]'', which would be filmed first. Scott played a [[leukemia]] victim in that film and Crowe's journals from making ''Singles'' are full of references to concern from the studio over his hair.<ref name="make"/>

According to Art Linson, $50,000 was budgeted for the role of Cliff. Dillon wanted to play it but his fee was $1 million and the studio would not pay it. After negotiations, it was agreed he would play the part for $400,000.<ref>{{cite book|first=Art|last=Linson|title=A Pound Of Flesh Perilous Tales Of How To Produce Movies|pages= 145–148|year=1993|publisher=Grove Press|isbn=9780802115430}}</ref>

===Shooting===
Filming began on March 11, 1991. Principal photography wrapped on May 24, 1991.<ref name="make">{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/making-the-scene-cameron-crowe-on-making-singles-19921001 |title=Making the Scene: A Filmmaker's Diary by Cameron Crowe |last=Crowe |first=Cameron |date=October 1, 1992 |magazine=Rolling Stone |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170819035548/https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/making-the-scene-cameron-crowe-on-making-singles-19921001 |archive-date=August 19, 2017 |url-status=live |access-date=March 3, 2018}}</ref>

The film was shot at a number of locations around Seattle and includes scenes at [[Gas Works Park]], [[Capitol Hill, Seattle|Capitol Hill]], [[Jimi Hendrix]]'s original grave at [[Greenwood Memorial Park (Renton, Washington)|Greenwood Memorial Park]] in [[Renton, Washington|Renton]], the [[Pike Place Market]] and the [[Virginia Inn]].<ref>{{cite news|newspaper=Seattle Post-Intelligencer|title='Singles': Where did it happen in Seattle?|author=Daniel DeMay|date=September 18, 2019|url=https://www.seattlepi.com/seattlenews/article/Singles-25-years-on-Where-did-it-happen-in-12202415.php}}</ref> The central coffee shop featured in the film is the now-closed [[OK Hotel]]. The apartment building is located on the northwest corner of the intersection of E. Thomas St & 19th Ave E. (1820 E. Thomas St.). Additional concert footage was shot in the now-defunct RKCNDY bar. [[Alice in Chains]]' concert was filmed at the Desoto nightclub.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/59392|title=Singles (1992)|date=October 1, 1992 |website=American Film Institute |access-date=March 3, 2018}}</ref> Also, [[Soundgarden]] makes an appearance in the film.

[[File:SinglesMovieApartment1.jpg|thumb|220px|The apartment building that was used as the main set for ''Singles''.]] Most of Dillon's wardrobe in the film actually belonged to [[Pearl Jam]] bassist [[Jeff Ament]].<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Hajari |first=Nisid |date=March 5, 1993 |title=Northwestern Exposure |url=https://ew.com/article/1993/03/05/singles-secrets/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150715211820/https://ew.com/article/1993/03/05/singles-secrets/ |archive-date=July 15, 2015 |access-date=September 3, 2026 |magazine=Entertainment Weekly}}</ref> During the making of the film, Ament produced a list of song titles for the fictional band, Citizen Dick. [[Chris Cornell]] took it as a challenge to write songs for the film using those titles, and "[[Spoonman]]" was one of them. An early [[Acoustic music|acoustic]] version of the song was created and can be heard in the background during a scene of the film. Citizen Dick's song "Touch Me, I'm Dick" is a parody of the song "[[Touch Me I'm Sick|Touch Me, I'm Sick]]" by the Seattle band [[Mudhoney]]. On the inside cover photo of the [[Singles: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack|soundtrack]], there is a Citizen Dick CD with the track listing on the CD itself. One of the songs is called "Louder Than Larry (Steiner)", a wordplay on the Soundgarden album, ''[[Louder Than Love]]''.

On 8 April Crowe wrote in his journal, "My working relationship with Campbell is deteriorating daily. The air is thick with the unspoken. I know it's not easy for him. Steve Dunne is the hardest part in the movie. All around him are characters with odd and interesting quirks. He is the Curse of the Normal Guy."<ref name="make"/> This resulted in a big argument on set two days later.<ref name="make"/>

Eric Stoltz, who had been in other Crowe films such as ''[[Fast Times at Ridgemont High]]'' and ''Say Anything...'', had a cameo as a mime. He and Fonda were dating at the time.<ref name="make"/>

On April 30, Crowe wrote:
<blockquote>The Car Crash scene goes well. Today I overhear two grips having a conversation about me on the radio-mike headset. I hear that I look especially happy today, that I must have gotten laid last night and that after all the talky scenes it was great to get out there today and "T-bone that fuckin' car."<ref name="make"/></blockquote>

==Post-production==
The film previewed disappointingly. Crowe persuaded Warner Bros to finance a reshoot of the scene where Steve and Linda broke up. This included a scene where Steve spoke on the phone to Linda. The film previewed better.<ref name="make"/>

In January 1992 Crowe wrote:
<blockquote>WB has offered new title suggestions: ''In the Midnight Hour'', ''Love in Seattle''
, ''Leave Me a Message'' and a grim selection of others. It's all done politely, of course, but the pressure is unmistakable. Now, with the success of Nirvana, they've come up with yet another title: ''Come as You Are''. I am powerless to stop them.<ref name="make"/></blockquote>
The film was meant to be released in April 1992 but this date was pulled back. The soundtrack was released and this helped the film secure a release date.<ref name="make"/>

== Reception ==
''Singles'' holds a 79% critical approval rating on the review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]] based on 53 reviews with an [[Weighted arithmetic mean|average rating]] of 6.9/10. The site's critical consensus reads "Smart, funny, and engagingly scruffy, ''Singles'' is a clear-eyed look at modern romance that doubles as a credible grunge-era time capsule".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1040379-singles/ |title=Singles Movie Reviews, Pictures |access-date=2023-10-16|publisher=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 71 out of 100, based on 24 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Singles'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/singles |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=3 September 2026 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of ''The Chicago Sun-Times'' was complimentary, giving ''Singles'' three out of four stars and declaring it "not a great cutting-edge movie, and parts of it may be too whimsical and disorganized for audiences raised on cause-and-effect plots{{spaces}}[... but] I found myself smiling a lot during the movie, sometimes with amusement, sometimes with recognition. It's easy to like these characters, and care about them."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/singles-1992|title = Singles movie review & film summary (1992)|date=September 18, 1992|website=Chicago Sun-Times|last=Ebert|first=Roger|via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> Tim Appelo wrote in ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'', "With{{spaces}}[...] an ambling, naturalistic style, Crowe captures the eccentric appeal of a town where espresso carts sprout on every corner and kids in ratty flannel shirts can cut records that make them millionaires."<ref>Appelo, Tim. [https://ew.com/article/2005/09/30/set-singles/ "Seattle Night Fever"]. ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]''. September 18, 1992, p. 46.</ref> Meanwhile, Seattle's ''[[The Stranger (newspaper)|The Stranger]]'' was less kind to Crowe's use of the local background, reviewing "he's relying on the general hipness of our little burg and on the star power of a few local musicians/bit actors to make a bundle of dough, and he hasn't bothered to back them up with anything worth remembering. ''Pleasant'' is about the only word I can think of to describe the thing."<ref name="the stranger">{{cite news|last=Cook|first=Matt|title=Down in Front: Before and After Matrimony|newspaper=The Stranger|date=23 September 1992}}</ref>

Crowe later said "To me the problem with Singles is the structure. I loved every ''Singles'' vignette, but I don't think they hang together as they should have... you've got to make the vignettes hang together to tell a bigger story, and then you're not going to have a stop-and-start feeling. ''Singles'', to me, has a little bit of that stop-and-start feel. Each individual part stands alone, but together they stand on rickety legs."<ref>Emery p 97</ref>

[[Warner Bros. Television]] immediately tried to turn ''Singles'' into a [[television series]]. Crowe claimed that ''Singles'' inspired the television series ''[[Friends]]''.<ref>{{Cite news |last=DeRogatis |first=Jim |date=September 3, 2000 |title=As Crowe flies |url=https://www.jimdero.com/Bangs/on_the_set.htm |access-date=September 3, 2026 |work=Chicago Sun-Times |via=Jimdero.com}}</ref><ref name="RollingStone">{{Cite magazine |last=Sottile |first=Alexis |date=2017-09-18 |title='Singles' at 25: Cameron Crowe on Making the Definitive Grunge Movie |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/singles-at-25-cameron-crowe-on-making-the-definitive-grunge-movie-118103/ |magazine=Rolling Stone |language=en-US |access-date=2025-06-30 |archive-date=May 1, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230501120632/https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-features/singles-at-25-cameron-crowe-on-making-the-definitive-grunge-movie-118103/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

On July 5, 2015, [[Derek Erdman]] held a public screening of the film in the courtyard of Capitol Hill's Coryell Court Apartments—the building in which some of the main characters live.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.seattleweekly.com/music/959377-129/1000-people-to-watch-singles-on|title=Seattle News and Events - 1000 People to Watch 'Singles' on a Single|work=Seattle Weekly|access-date=6 July 2015|date=June 30, 2015|last=Henry|first=Dusty|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150703074559/http://www.seattleweekly.com/music/959377-129/1000-people-to-watch-singles-on|archive-date=July 3, 2015}}</ref> The event was attended by over 1,000 people. Despite initial concerns by the landlord, the event progressed smoothly. The crowd was respectful and cleaned up after themselves. Reports of [[Bridget Fonda]] being in attendance were false, but her aunt{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}[[Jane Fonda]]{{px2}}{{mdash}}{{hsp}}was indeed there.<ref name=":0" />{{Not verified in body|date=September 2026}}

== Soundtrack ==
{{Main|Singles: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack{{!}}''Singles: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack''}}
The ''Singles'' [[Singles: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack|soundtrack]] was released on June 30, 1992, through [[Epic Records]] and became a best seller three months before the release of the film. The soundtrack included music from key bands from the grunge scene of the time, such as [[Alice in Chains]], [[Pearl Jam]], [[Screaming Trees]], and [[Soundgarden]]. Pearl Jam performed two previously unreleased songs on the soundtrack: "[[Breath (Pearl Jam song)|Breath]]" and "[[State of Love and Trust]]". The Soundgarden song "Birth Ritual" and Chris Cornell's solo song "Seasons" appear on the soundtrack. [[Paul Westerberg]] of [[The Replacements (band)|The Replacements]] contributed two songs to the soundtrack and provided the score for the film. [[The Smashing Pumpkins]] also contributed to the soundtrack with the song "[[Drown (The Smashing Pumpkins song)|Drown]]".
