'''''The Brood''''' is a 1979 Canadian [[psychological horror|psychological]] [[body horror]] film written and directed by [[David Cronenberg]], and starring [[Oliver Reed]], [[Samantha Eggar]], and [[Art Hindle]]. Its plot follows a man and his mentally ill ex-wife, who has been sequestered by a psychiatrist known for his controversial therapy techniques. A series of brutal unsolved murders serves as the backdrop for the central narrative.

Written by Cronenberg after his own acrimonious divorce, he intended the screenplay as a meditation on a fractured relationship between a husband and wife who share a child, and cast Eggar and Hindle as loose facsimiles of himself and his ex-wife. He would later state that, despite its incorporation of [[science fiction]] elements, he considered it his sole feature that most embodied a "classic horror film". Principal photography of ''The Brood'' took place in late 1978 in Toronto on a budget of $1.5 million. The film's score was composed by [[Howard Shore]].

Released in the spring of 1979 by [[New World Pictures]], ''The Brood'' proved profitable for the studio, grossing over $5 million. Though it initially received mixed reviews from critics, it would establish itself as a [[cult following|cult film]] in the following decades. It has attracted scholarly interest from academics in the areas of [[film theory]] for its themes regarding [[mental illness]] and parenthood. In 2006, the [[Chicago Film Critics Association]] named it the 88th-scariest film of all time. In 2013, it was selected for restoration by [[the Criterion Collection]], which subsequently released it on [[Blu-ray]].

==Plot==
Dr. Hal Raglan, a [[psychotherapy|psychotherapist]], encourages patients with mental disturbances to let go of their suppressed emotions through physiological changes to their bodies in a technique he calls "psychoplasmics". One of his patients is Nola Carveth, a severely disturbed woman who is legally embattled with her husband Frank for [[child custody|custody]] of their five-year-old daughter Candice. When Frank discovers bruises and scratches on Candice following a visit with Nola, he informs Raglan of his intent to stop visitation rights. Wanting to protect his patient, Raglan begins to intensify the sessions with Nola to resolve the issue quickly. During the therapy sessions, he discovers that Nola was abused by her alcoholic mother, Juliana, while neglected by her father Barton, who refused to protect Nola out of shame and denial.

Frank, intending to invalidate Raglan's methods, questions Jan Hartog, a former patient who is dying of psychoplasmic-induced [[lymphoma]]. He leaves Candice with Juliana and the two spend the evening viewing old photographs. Juliana tells Candice that Nola was frequently hospitalized as a child and often exhibited strange unexplained [[Wheal response|wheals]] on her skin that doctors were unable to diagnose. While in the kitchen, Juliana is attacked and bludgeoned to death by a small, [[dwarfism|dwarf]]-like child. Candice is traumatized, but physically unharmed.

Juliana's ex-husband Barton returns for the funeral and attempts to contact Nola, but Raglan turns him away. Frank invites Candice's teacher, Ruth Mayer, home for dinner to discuss his daughter's performance in school. Barton interrupts with a drunken phone call from Juliana's home, demanding that Frank and he go to Raglan's institute to see Nola. Frank leaves to calm Barton, leaving Candice in Ruth's care. While he is away, Ruth answers a phone call from Nola, who, recognizing her voice and believing her to be having an affair with Frank, insults her and angrily warns Ruth to stay away from her family. Meanwhile, Frank arrives to find Barton murdered by the same deformed dwarf-child, who dies after attempting to kill Frank.

An autopsy of the dwarf-child reveals a multitude of bizarre anatomical anomalies, primarily the absence of a [[navel]], indicating no known means of natural human birth. After the murders catch the attention of newspapers, Raglan reluctantly acknowledges that the deaths coincided with his sessions with Nola relating to their respective topics. He closes his institute and sends his patients to municipal care with the exception of Nola. Frank is alerted about the closure of the institute by Hartog.

Mike Trellan, one of Raglan's other patients, tells Frank that Nola is now Raglan's "queen bee" and in charge of some "disturbed children" in an attic. When Candice returns to school, two dwarf-children attack and kill Ruth in front of her class before absconding with Candice to the institute, with Frank in pursuit. Upon arrival, Raglan tells Frank the truth about the dwarf-children: they are the accidental product of Nola's psychoplasmic sessions; her rage about her abuse was so strong that she [[Parthenogenesis|parthenogenetically]] bore a brood of creatures resembling children who psychically respond and act on the targets of her rage, with Nola completely unaware of their actions. Realizing the brood are too dangerous to keep anymore, Raglan plans to venture into their quarters and rescue Candice, provided that Frank can keep Nola calm to avoid provoking the children.

Frank feigns rapprochement long enough for Raglan to collect Candice, but when he witnesses Nola give birth to another child through a psychoplasmically-induced external womb, she notices his disgust when she licks the child clean. The brood awakens and kills Raglan. Nola then threatens to kill Candice rather than lose her. The brood goes after Candice, who hides in a closet, but they begin to break through the door and try to grab her. In desperation, Frank strangles Nola to death, and the brood dies without its mother's psychic connection. Frank carries a traumatized Candice back to his car and the two depart. As the pair sit in silence, two small lesions—a germinal stage of the phenomenon experienced by Nola—appear on Candice's arm.

==Production==
===Screenplay===
{{quote box|bgcolor=#f5f5dc|width=20%|align=right|quote="''The Brood'' is my version of ''[[Kramer vs. Kramer]]'', but more realistic."|source=—Cronenberg commenting on his concept of the film{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=56}}}}

In retrospect, Cronenberg stated that he felt ''The Brood'' was "the most classic horror film I've done" in terms of structure.<ref name=bfi>{{cite web| last=Botting| first= Josephine|title=Why I love... ''The Brood''|work= bfi.org.uk| publisher= [[British Film Institute]]|date=March 17, 2017|url= https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/why-i-love-brood|access-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref> He conceived the screenplay in the aftermath of a bitter divorce which included a custody battle over their daughter.<ref name=bfi/>{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=52}} During his divorce, Cronenberg became aware of the drama ''[[Kramer vs. Kramer]]'' (also released in 1979),{{efn|''Kramer vs. Kramer'' was released theatrically in December 1979, after the release of ''The Brood'';<ref>{{cite web| work=[[The New York Times]]|url= https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/packages/html/movies/bestpictures/kramer-re.html?scp=1&sq=french%2520toast&st=cse|title= East Side Story|last=Canby|first=Vincent|author-link=Vincent Canby|date=December 19, 1979|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713115709/https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/packages/html/movies/bestpictures/kramer-re.html?scp=1&sq=french%2520toast&st=cse|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref> though Cronenberg stated that he intended ''The Brood'' to be his own version of ''Kramer vs. Kramer'',<ref name=bfi/> he would not have seen the film as it was still being shot in January 1979,<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/33822316/the_decatur_daily_review/|work=The Decatur Daily Review|location=Decatur, Illinois|title=Dustin Hoffman hopes to return to New York Stage|page=8|date=January 2, 1979|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> around the same time filming of ''The Brood'' took place. He may have been referring to its source novel by [[Avery Corman]].}} and was disillusioned by its optimistic depiction of a familial breakdown after a couple's separation.<ref name=bfi/> In response, he began writing the screenplay for ''The Brood'', aspiring to depict the strife between a divorced couple battling over their child.<ref name= bfi/>

===Casting===
In casting the roles of Frank and Nola Carveth, Cronenberg sought actors who were "vague facsimiles" of himself and his wife.<ref name= bfi/>{{sfn|Mathijs|2008|p=79}} Canadian actor Art Hindle was cast in the role of Frank.<ref name= birth>{{cite AV media|title=Birth Pains|year=2015|people= Eggar, Samantha; David, Pierre; Irwin, Mark; Board, John; Baker, Rick|publisher=[[The Criterion Collection]]|medium=[[Blu-ray]] documentary short}}</ref> There was difficulty in casting Nola Carveth. [[Samantha Eggar]], whom Cronenberg stated "looked a little like my ex-wife" and had a husband that looked like him, was selected.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=76}} Cronenberg met Eggar ten years later at the [[Sitges Film Festival]] where she told him that "''The Brood'' was the strangest and most repulsive film I've ever done".{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=84}} It was one of several Canadian films made by Eggar.<ref name="captive">{{cite magazine|magazine=Filmink|date=18 October 2025|access-date=18 October 2025|first=Stephen|last=Vagg|url=https://www.filmink.com.au/the-captive-and-captivating-samantha-eggar/|title=The captive and captivating Samantha Eggar}}</ref>

Oliver Reed was cast in the role of Hal Raglan, the psychotherapist who has kept Nola sequestered after her divorce from Frank.<ref name=bfi/> Reed accepted a reduced salary for the role.<ref>{{Cite news |date=1 August 1979 |title=Horror good for you |page=39 |work=[[Ottawa Journal]] |url= https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-ottawa-journal/33821345/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240613135929/https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-ottawa-journal/33821345/ |archive-date=13 June 2024 |via= Newspapers.com}}</ref> This marked the second time Eggar and Reed had starred in a film together, having previously co-starred in ''[[The Lady in the Car with Glasses and a Gun (1970 film)|The Lady in the Car with Glasses and a Gun]]'' (1970).<ref name=birth/> Additionally, Eggar and Reed had known one another personally, having grown up together in [[Bledlow]], England.<ref name=birth/> Eggar was impressed by Cronenberg's screenplay, and agreed to appear in the film, as she felt the role of Nola was "almost [[Shakespeare]]an...&nbsp;How could you turn this part down?"<ref name=birth/>

===Filming===
Les Productions Mutuelles Ltée and Elgin International Productions produced the film as The Brood Films. The film was shot in Toronto from 14 November to 21 December 1978, on a budget of $1.4 million ({{Inflation|CA|1,400,000|1978|fmt=eq}}) with $200,000 coming from the [[Telefilm Canada|Canadian Film Development Corporation]].{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=285}} The Kortright Centre for Conservation, just north of Toronto, was used as the location of the Somafree Institute. Additional photography was done in [[Mississauga]].

Eggar recalled the production crew being very small, with only around seven crew members in total while she was filming her sequences, many of them "academics and [[PhD]]s, standing there holding lights".<ref name=birth/> Her scenes were shot over a period of three days.<ref name=bfi/> To portray the brood of children, Cronenberg cast a group of child gymnasts from Toronto.<ref name=bfi/>

''The Brood'' was the first Cronenberg film to have an original soundtrack. Howard Shore has written the music for most of Cronenberg's subsequent films.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=99}}

==Release==
===Censorship===
''The Brood'' had cuts demanded for an R-rating for its theatrical release in the United States. Eggar conceived the idea of licking the new fetuses that her character Nola Carveth has spawned. "I just thought that when cats have their kittens or dogs have puppies (and I think at that time I had about 8 dogs), they lick them as soon as they're born. Lick, lick, lick, lick, lick...," Eggar said.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.terrortrap.com/interviews/samanthaeggar/|title=Collecting Life: An Interview with Actress Samantha Eggar - July 2014|publisher=The Terror Trap}}</ref>

However, when the climactic scene was censored, Cronenberg responded: "I had a long and loving close-up of Samantha licking the fetus [...] when the censors, those animals, cut it out, the result was that a lot of people thought she was eating her baby. That's much worse than I was suggesting."<ref name="coc">Chris Rodley (ed.), ''Cronenberg on Cronenberg'', Faber & Faber, 1997.</ref>

===Home media===
The film was released on [[VHS]] in 1982,{{sfn|Mathijs|2008|p=97}} and on [[DVD-Video|DVD]] in its original uncensored version by [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer]] on August 26, 2003.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.joblo.com/horror-movies/reviews/the-brood|work=[[JoBlo.com]]|title=''The Brood''|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713220835/https://www.joblo.com/horror-movies/reviews/the-brood|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref> [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]] subsequently released the film on DVD in the United Kingdom 2005.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/the-brood-v7238/releases|work=[[AllMovie]]|title=''The Brood'' (1979) Releases|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713221156/https://www.allmovie.com/movie/the-brood-v7238/releases|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref>

In mid-2013, [[the Criterion Collection]] added ''The Brood'', as well as ''[[Scanners (film)|Scanners]]'', to their selection of films available to [[Hulu]] and [[iTunes]] customers. The film was subsequently issued on DVD and [[Blu-ray]] by the Criterion Collection on October 13, 2015,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3353538/brood-mulholland-dr-getting-criterion-editions/|work=[[Bloody Disgusting]]|date=July 16, 2015|title='The Brood' And 'Mulholland Dr.' Getting Criterion Editions|last=Barkan|first=Jonathan|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713091342/https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3353538/brood-mulholland-dr-getting-criterion-editions/|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref> featuring a new [[2K resolution|2K scan]] of the original film elements.<ref name=pop/>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''The Brood'' was released as ''Chromosome 3'' in France and ''La Clinique de la terreur'' in Quebec. The film was distributed by [[New World Pictures]] and opened in the United States on 25 May 1979, Canada on 1 June, France on 10 October, and the United Kingdom on 13 March 1980. The French dub was released in Montreal on 14 March 1980.{{sfn|Turner|1987|p=285}} After its screenings in Toronto and [[Chicago]], ''The Brood'' grossed $685,000 over only a period of ten days between the two cities.<ref name=palmer/> By 1981, the film had grossed over $5 million.<ref name=dark/> Cronenberg was able to purchase a home using his earnings from the film.{{sfn|Rodley|1997|p=75}}

===Critical response===
While ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it "an extremely well made, if essentially unpleasant shocker",<ref>''Variety'', December 31, 1978.</ref> [[Leonard Maltin]] reviewed the film in two sentences: "Eggar eats her own afterbirth while midget clones beat grandparents and lovely young schoolteachers to death with mallets. It's a big, wide, wonderful world we live in!" and rated it an outright "BOMB".<ref>{{Cite book |last=Maltin |first=Leonard |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/Leonard_Maltin_s_TV_Movies/wgiiml0uTLwC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=%22Eggar+eats+her+own+afterbirth%22&pg=PA109&printsec=frontcover |title=Leonard Maltin's TV Movies |date=1984 |publisher=New American Library |isbn=978-0-451-13212-3 |page=109 |language=en|author-link=Leonard Maltin}}</ref> [[Roger Ebert]] called it "a bore" and "disgusting in ways that are not entertaining; as opposed, for example, to the great disgusting moments in ''[[Alien (1979 film)|Alien]]'' or ''[[Dawn of the Dead (1978 film)|Dawn of the Dead]]''", and concluded, "Are there really people who want to see reprehensible trash like this? I guess so. It's in its second week."<ref>{{cite web|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19790605/REVIEWS/906050301|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120718021626/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19790605/REVIEWS/906050301|archive-date=July 18, 2012|last=Ebert|first=Roger|author-link=Roger Ebert|date=June 5, 1979|title=The Brood |accessdate=November 7, 2011}}</ref> Writing for the ''[[Vancouver Sun]]'', Vaughn Palmer lambasted the film, referring to it as "mean, foul and witless...&nbsp;The people who made ''The Brood'' do not like people. They do not even appear to like themselves. They just like money."<ref name=palmer>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/33821316/the_vancouver_sun/|work=[[Vancouver Sun]]|location=Vancouver, British Columbia|last=Palmer|first=Vaughn|title=This movie is mean, foul and witless|date=September 1, 1979|via=Newspapers.com|page=19}}</ref> [[Kevin Thomas (film critic)|Kevin Thomas]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' praised the film as "well made" and "expertly acted," but criticized its depictions of violence, stating: "Perhaps Cronenberg means to make an extreme comment upon the irresponsibility of psychiatrists and parents, but ''The Brood'' is so totally sickening it's an irresponsible work itself."<ref>{{cite news|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|last=Thomas|first=Kevin|author-link=Kevin Thomas (film critic)|date=June 8, 1979|page=21|title=Tumors Beget Dwarves in 'Brood'|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''The Brood'' scored 82% from 33 reviews. The site's consensus reads: "''The Brood'' is a grotesque, squirming, hilariously shrill exploration of the bizarre and deadly side of motherhood".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/brood/|title=The Brood |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=October 21, 2025}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 63 out of 100, based on 9 critics, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Brood Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-brood/ |access-date=2026-02-10 |website=Metacritic |language=en}}</ref>

In ''[[Cult Movies (book)|Cult Movies]]'', [[Danny Peary]], who openly disapproves of ''[[Shivers (1975 film)|Shivers]]'' and ''[[Rabid (1977 film)|Rabid]]'', calls ''The Brood'' "Cronenberg's best film" because "we care about the characters", and, although he dislikes the ending, "an hour and a half of absorbing, solid cinema".<ref>Danny Peary, ''Cult Movies'', Dell Publishing, New York 1981.</ref> In his ''An Introduction to the American Horror Film'', critic [[Robin Wood (critic)|Robin Wood]] views ''The Brood'' as a reactionary work portraying feminine power as irrational and horrifying, and the dangerous attempts of Oliver Reed's character's psychoanalysis as an analogue to the dangers of trying to undo repression in society.<ref name="wood">Robin Wood, ''An Introduction to the American Horror Film'', in: Bill Nichols (ed.), ''Movies and Methods Volume II'', University of California Press, 1985.</ref>

''The Brood'' was listed #88 on the "[[Chicago Film Critics Association]]'s 100 Scariest Movies of All Time".<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.newspapers.com/clip/33821465/the_daily_herald/|work=The Daily Herald|location=Chicago, Illinois|title=Scary: Not all on list from horror genre|page=8|date=October 25, 2006|via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> In 2004, one of its sequences was voted #78 among the "[[100 Scariest Movie Moments]]" by the [[Bravo (US TV network)|Bravo Channel]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.filmsite.org/scariestscenes2.html|work=[[Filmsite.org]]|title=Greatest Scariest Movie Moments and Scenes|publisher=[[AMC Networks]]|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713220351/https://www.filmsite.org/scariestscenes2.html|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref>

==Analysis==
Cronenberg stated that it was his only film without humor.{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=56}} Child characters rarely appear in his films, with Cronenberg stating that having a child made it unbearable, but child characters are present in ''The Brood'' as "it was cathartic, I had to do it, but I have never done it since".{{sfn|Cronenberg|2006|p=58}}

Written in the aftermath of writer-director Cronenberg's divorce from his wife, ''The Brood'' has been noted by critics and film scholars for its prominent themes surrounding fears of parenthood, as well as corollary preoccupations with [[psychological repression|repression]] and the treatment of mental illness in women.<ref name=pop>{{cite web|work=[[PopMatters]]|title=Cronenberg's 'The Brood' Taps Into Some Fundamental, Primal Terror|last=McKnight|first=Brent|date=November 17, 2015|url=https://www.popmatters.com/the-brood-2495470024.html|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713105322/https://www.popmatters.com/the-brood-2495470024.html|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref>{{sfn|Creed|2007|pages=47–50}}

Film theorist [[Barbara Creed]] notes that Nola's [[parthenogenetic]] births are thematically "used to demonstrate the horrors of unbridled maternal power" in which a woman gives birth to "deformed manifestations of herself".{{sfn|Creed|2007|p=47}} Scholar Sarah Arnold similarly suggests that, despite Nola's apparent representation as a "bad mother" epitomizing "[[Barbara Creed|the monstrous feminine]]", ''The Brood'' "does not disseminate such images unproblematically, [and] instead questions these already (culturally and socially) pre-existing notions of the maternal and motherhood...&nbsp;the film fuses the concerns of the [[woman's film]] with that of the [[body horror]] film."{{sfn|Arnold|2013|p=84}}

[[Feminism|Feminist]] critic [[Carrie Rickey]] notes that, like many of Cronenberg's films, ''The Brood'' has been accused of presenting a [[misogyny|misogynistic]] representation of women.<ref name=rickey>{{cite web|url=https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3739-the-brood-separation-trials|work=[[The Criterion Collection]]|title=''The Brood'': Separation Trials|last=Rickey|first=Carrie|date=October 13, 2015|archive-url=https://archive.today/20190713110020/https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3739-the-brood-separation-trials|archive-date=July 13, 2019}}</ref> However, Rickey argues against this assertion, writing: "For me, Cronenberg's gynophobia is a nonissue. It's blaming the victim. After all, aren't we talking about movies where male scientists use women as guinea pigs and then are shocked, shocked when the test subjects become monstrous, voracious, etc.? Let me invoke the [[Jessica Rabbit]] defense: The women are not bad, they're just drawn that way. It's the male scientists who have inadvertently transformed them into men's worst nightmares."<ref name=rickey/>
