'''''Halloween II''''' (also known as simply '''''H2''''') is a 2009 American [[slasher film]], written and directed by [[Rob Zombie]]. It is the tenth installment in [[Halloween (franchise)#Films|the ''Halloween'' film series]] and the sequel to Zombie's [[Halloween (2007 film)|2007 remake]] of [[Halloween (1978 film)|''Halloween'']] (1978), though it is not a remake of the [[Halloween II (1981 film)|1981 sequel of the same name.]]<!--The theatrical release is 1 year. The Director's Cut is 2 years. Please do not change!--> Starring [[Malcolm McDowell]], [[Tyler Mane]], [[Sheri Moon Zombie]], [[Brad Dourif]], [[Danielle Harris]], and [[Scout Taylor-Compton]], the film follows [[Laurie Strode]] as she deals with the aftermath of the Haddonfield murders and her deteriorating relationship with fellow survivor [[Annie Brackett]], [[Samuel Loomis|Dr. Loomis]] trying to capitalize on those events with a new book, and [[Michael Myers (Halloween)|Michael Myers]] as he seeks to reunite with his sister on [[Halloween]] once more.

For ''Halloween II'', Zombie decided to focus more on the connection between Strode and Myers, and the idea that they share similar psychological problems. He wanted the sequel to be more realistic and more violent than the previous film, and to portray how the events of the first film affected the characters. Zombie also wanted to provide a glimpse into each character's psyche. [[Principal photography]] took place in [[Covington, Georgia]], which provided Zombie with the visual style he was looking for. Zombie had trouble finding a place to include [[John Carpenter]]'s original ''Halloween'' theme music, and it was only included in the final shot.

''Halloween II'' was the last film in the series made by [[Dimension Films]] before they lost the rights to the franchise in 2015.<ref name="wrap">{{Cite web |url=https://www.thewrap.com/halloween-rights-up-for-grabs-as-dimension-lets-michael-myers-slip-away/ |title=''Halloween'' Franchise Rights Up for Grabs |last=Sneider |first=Jeff |date=December 29, 2015 |website=TheWrap |access-date=March 29, 2019}}</ref> The film was released on August 28, 2009, to negative reviews from critics. On October 30, it was re-released in North America to coincide with the [[Halloween]] holiday weekend. The film did not do as well as the previous installment, grossing $40 million against its $15 million budget.<ref>{{Cite web |title='Halloween Kills' Just Reached A Surprising Box Office Milestone |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/scottmendelson/2021/11/29/halloween-kills-just-reached-a-surprising-box-office-milestone/?sh=25db78396e7a |access-date=October 30, 2022 |website=Forbes}}</ref> Zombie declined to return to film a further sequel,<ref name=ScreenRant-2009-07-27>{{cite web |url= https://screenrant.com/rob-zombie-halloween-3/ |title= Rob Zombie Says 'Never' to Halloween 3 |date= 27 July 2009 |website= Screen Rant }}</ref> ''Halloween 3D'', which was cancelled in 2012.<ref name=Collider-2012-03-08>{{cite web |url= https://collider.com/halloween-3d-release-date/ |title= HALLOWEEN 3D Removed from 2012 Release Schedule |website= Collider |date= 8 March 2012 |author= Adam Chitwood }}</ref><ref name=CB.com-2018-06-10>{{cite web |url= https://comicbook.com/horror/2018/06/10/halloween-movies-timeline-explained/ |title= The 'Halloween' Timeline Explained |author= Cameron Bonomolo |date= 9 June 2018 |publisher= Comic Book .com |access-date= 16 October 2018 |archive-date= 16 October 2018 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20181016165216/https://comicbook.com/horror/2018/06/10/halloween-movies-timeline-explained/ |url-status= dead }}</ref> The next installment of the franchise was a [[Halloween (2018 film)|sequel to the original film]], which was released in 2018.

==Plot==
In a flashback during his time at Smith's Grove Sanitarium, a young [[Michael Myers (Halloween)|Michael Myers]] is visited by his mother [[List of Halloween (franchise) characters#Deborah Myers|Deborah]] who gives him a white toy horse as a gift.

Fifteen years later, [[Laurie Strode]] is wandering around the town of Haddonfield in shock after having shot Michael.{{efn|As depicted in ''[[Halloween (2007 film)|Halloween]]'' (2007)}} [[List of Halloween (franchise) characters#Sheriff Leigh Brackett|Sheriff Lee Brackett]] finds Laurie and rushes her to the hospital. Meanwhile, paramedics pick up [[Annie Brackett]] and Michael's old psychiatrist [[Samuel Loomis|Dr. Sam Loomis]], who survived the attack. Michael's body is transported by another ambulance, but he mysteriously awakens and kills the paramedic after a traffic collision. Michael then appears at the hospital, murdering several nurses and a security guard in order to find Laurie, who escapes the facility. Michael chases her down and stabs her, and Laurie wakes up; the hospital sequence was a dream. 

It has been a year since Michael's killing spree. Laurie is living with the Bracketts; Michael is missing and presumed dead. While Laurie deals with her trauma in therapy, Dr. Loomis has turned the event into an opportunity to profit off another book. Meanwhile, Michael, who is still alive and living as a drifter, is having visions of his mother's ghost, instructing him to reunite with Laurie. Michael arrives at a farm, killing the owners and their dog before heading to Haddonfield, as Laurie is starting to have hallucinations. Meanwhile, Loomis goes on tour to promote his new book, only to be criticized by the public, who blame him for the actions of Michael and for exploiting the deaths of the victims.

When Loomis' book is released, Laurie discovers that she is actually Angel Myers, Michael's long-lost baby sister. She goes to a Halloween party with her co-workers, Mya and Harley, to escape how she is feeling. Michael appears at the party and kills Harley, then goes to the Brackett home where he fatally wounds Annie. As Laurie and Mya return to the house, they find a bloody Annie, who dies in Laurie's arms. Michael then kills Mya and comes after Laurie, who manages to quickly escape. Sheriff Brackett arrives home and finds his daughter dead. Laurie flags down a car, but Michael kills the driver and flips the vehicle over with Laurie still inside. Michael then takes the unconscious Laurie to an abandoned shack, where she is awakened by visions of the Myers' Mother, seen by both her and Michael.

The police discover Michael's location and surround the shack. Loomis arrives and goes inside to try to reason with Michael, but when he tries to save Laurie, Michael stabs him to death before being shot through the shack window by Brackett and impaled on a rake. Apparently released from her visions, Laurie walks over to Michael and stabs him with his own knife. The shack door opens and Laurie walks out wearing Michael's torn mask. Later on, Laurie sits in isolation in a psychiatric ward and grins as her mother's ghost approaches with the white horse.

==Production==

===Development===
{{quote box |quote=Don't feel hindered by any of the rules we've had in the past. I want this to be your vision and I want you to express that vision. |source=— Producer Malek Akkad speaking to writer/director Rob Zombie<ref name="Akkad"/> |width=40% |bgcolor=#F0F8FF}}

In 2008, at the 30 Years of Terror Convention, [[Halloween (franchise)|''Halloween'' franchise]] producer Malek Akkad confirmed that a sequel to [[Rob Zombie]]'s ''[[Halloween (2007 film)|Halloween]]'' (2007), a remake of [[John Carpenter]]'s [[Halloween (1978 film)|1978 film]], was in the works. Despite [[Julien Maury and Alexandre Bustillo]] being in negotiations to direct the sequel in November 2008,<ref>{{cite web|author=Kevin Powers|url=http://www.firstshowing.net/2008/11/03/more-details-on-the-sequel-to-rob-zombies-halloween/|title=More Details on the Sequel to Rob Zombie's Halloween|publisher=First Showing|work=Dead Central|date=November 3, 2008|access-date=November 4, 2008}}</ref> Zombie reprised as both director, writer, and producer.<ref>{{cite news|author=Michael Fleming|url=https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/zombie-making-halloween-sequel-1117997402/|title=Zombie making 'Halloween' sequel|publisher=[[Reed Business Information]]|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=December 15, 2008|access-date=December 16, 2008}}</ref>

In an interview, Zombie expressed how the exhaustion of creating the predecessor made him not want to come back for a sequel, but after a year of cooling down he was more open to the idea.<ref>{{cite news|author=Michael Fleming|url=https://variety.com/2008/film/markets-festivals/zombie-making-halloween-sequel-1117997402/|title=Zombie making 'Halloween' sequel|publisher=Reed Business Information|work=Variety|date=December 15, 2008|access-date=April 12, 2010}}</ref> He explained that with the sequel he was no longer bound by a sense of needing to retain any "[[John Carpenter]]-ness", as he "felt free to do whatever".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.iesb.net/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=7235:interview-rob-zombie-on-halloween-2&catid=44:interviews&Itemid=172|title=Interview: Rob Zombie on Halloween 2|publisher=IESB|author=Christina Radish|date=August 5, 2009|access-date=April 10, 2010}}</ref> Akkad said the original intention, when they believed Zombie was not returning, was to create a "normal sequel".<ref name="Akkad">{{cite web|author=Ryan Rotten|title=Set Interview: Halloween 2's Malek Akkad|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/horror/news/713437-set-interview-halloween-2s-malek-akkad|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=May 4, 2009|access-date=May 4, 2009}}</ref> Akkad and his [[Trancas International Films]] producing company hired various writers to come up with drafts for a new film, but none worked. Akkad, and [[Harvey Weinstein|Harvey]] and [[Bob Weinstein]] then turned to Bustillo and Muary, whose film ''[[Inside (2007 film)|Inside]]'' (2007) had been bought for distribution by [[the Weinstein Company]]. According to Akkad, the producers really wanted Zombie to return, as Akkad felt that there was something "lost in the translation" when the duo took over the project.<ref name="Akkad"/> After his work on the 2007 remake, Zombie had earned the trust of Akkad, who told him to ignore any rules they had set for him on the previous film. Akkad said that he wanted Zombie to move the franchise away from some of its established rules.<ref name="Akkad"/>

===Characters===
For the sequel, [[Tyler Mane]], [[Malcolm McDowell]], [[Scout Taylor-Compton]], [[Danielle Harris]], [[Sheri Moon Zombie]], and [[Brad Dourif]] returned to the roles of [[Michael Myers (Halloween)|Michael Myers]], [[Samuel Loomis]], [[Laurie Strode]], [[Annie Brackett]], Deborah Myers, and Sheriff Brackett, respectively. Although [[Daeg Faerch]], who portrayed a young Michael Myers in the 2007 remake, was initially signed on to reprise his role for ''Halloween II'' (then known as ''H2''), after filming briefly in Georgia as young Michael, he was later recast because he had grown taller.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.bloody-disgusting.com/news/15466 | author=Mr. Disgusting | title=A New Young Michael Myers Arrives in Haddonfield | publisher=Bloody-Disgusting | date=2009-03-03 | access-date=2009-03-05}}</ref> He was replaced by [[Chase Wright Vanek]]. Zombie had to recast the role, much to his own dismay, because Faerch's physical maturity did not fit what was in the script. Although Faerch is not in the sequel, the first trailer for ''Halloween II'' contained images of Faerch. Zombie pointed out that those images were test shots done and were not intended to be in either the trailer or the film.<ref name="Zombieint"/>

Taylor-Compton described her character as having "these bipolar moments",<ref name="Scoutint">{{cite web|author=Ryan Rotten|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=10171|title=Interview: The Dark Side of Laurie Strode|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=April 10, 2009|access-date=April 26, 2009}}</ref> where her emotions are spontaneously changing from points of happiness to agitation. The actress stated that Zombie wanted to see Laurie Strode travel into "these really dark places".<ref name="Scoutint"/> Taylor-Compton clarified that when the film starts Laurie is still not aware that Michael is her older brother, and as the film progresses more and more pieces of information are given to her and she does not know how to deal with them. The actress explained that the darkness brewing inside Laurie is manifested externally, generally through her physical appearance and the clothes she chooses to wear—Zombie characterized the look as "[[Grunge fashion|grungy]]".<ref name="Scoutint"/>

Instead of focusing on Michael, Zombie chose to look more at the psychological consequences on Laurie after the events of the remake. As Zombie explains, after Michael murdered her friends and family, Laurie became a "wreck", who continually sinks lower as the film moves forward.<ref>{{cite web|author=Scott Collura|url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/963/963622p3.html|title=Exclusive: The Shape of H2 (page 3)|publisher=[[News Corporation (1980–2013)|News Corporation]]|work=[[IGN]]|access-date=March 22, 2009}}</ref> Even Sheriff Brackett goes through changes. Brackett, who receives more screen time in this film, allows Laurie to move in with him and his daughter after the events of the first film. Zombie explained, "He's old, he's worn out, he's just this beat-down guy with these two girls he can't deal with."<ref>{{cite web|author=Ryan Rotten|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=11196|title=SDCC: Dourif to Have a Larger Role in Halloween II|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=July 25, 2009|access-date=July 26, 2009}}</ref> Zombie characterized Loomis in the sequel as more of a "sellout",<ref name="Kimmel">{{cite web|url=http://abc.go.com/watch/jimmy-kimmel-live/93521/232978/jimmy-kimmel-live-831|title=Jimmy Kimmel Live, August 31, 2009|access-date=September 7, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090905191249/http://abc.go.com/watch/jimmy-kimmel-live/93521/232978/jimmy-kimmel-live-831|archive-date=September 5, 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref> who exploits the memories of those who were killed by Michael in the 2007 film. Zombie explained that he tried to channel [[Vincent Bugliosi]], a lawyer who prosecuted [[Charles Manson]] and then wrote [[Helter Skelter (book)|a book about it]], into Loomis's character for the sequel, noting that he wanted Loomis to seem more "ridiculous" this time.<ref name="Kimmel"/> As for Michael Myers, the character is given almost an entirely new look for the film, which is being used, according to Taylor-Compton, to illustrate a new emotion for the character as he spends much of his time trying to hide himself.<ref name="Scoutint"/> Zombie said that of all of the characters that return in the sequel, Michael is the only one that does not change: "All the other characters are very different. Laurie; Loomis; they're having all kinds of problems in their life, but Michael just moves along. Michael is no different; he's exactly the same as he was ten years old and he killed everybody [...] He has no concept of the world around him, so he can never be affected by it."<ref name="Zombievid">{{cite web |date=August 26, 2009 |title=Shock Video: Rob Zombie talks ''Halloween II'' |url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=11530 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716060401/http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=11530 |archive-date=July 16, 2011 |access-date=August 27, 2009 |publisher=Shock Till You Drop}}</ref>

===Filming===
With a $15 million budget,<ref name="budget">{{cite news|author=Ben Fritz|url=http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/entertainmentnewsbuzz/2009/08/movie-projector-the-final-destination-halloween-ii-splitting-horror-audience.html|title=Movie projector: 'The Final Destination,' 'Halloween II' splitting horror audience|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=August 27, 2009|access-date=August 29, 2009}}</ref> production began on February 23, 2009, in Atlanta, Georgia.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=9138|title='Halloween 2' Seen Through New Eyes|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=January 9, 2009|access-date=January 11, 2009}}</ref> Zombie acknowledged that filming in Georgia provided certain tax breaks for the company, but the real reason he chose that location was because the other locations he was planning to use were still experiencing snowy weather. For him, Georgia's landscapes and locations provided the look that he wanted for his film.<ref name="Zombieint">{{cite web|author=Jeff Otto|url=https://www.bloody-disgusting.com/interview/560|title=H2 Set Visit and Exclusive Interview with Rob Zombie|publisher=Bloody-Disgusting, LLC|work=Bloody Disgusting|access-date=May 5, 2009|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090509082316/http://www.bloody-disgusting.com/interview/560|archive-date=May 9, 2009|df=mdy-all}}</ref> During production, Zombie described the sequel as being "Ultra gritty, ultra intense and very real"<ref name="1stpic">{{cite web|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=9682|title=The First Pic from Rob Zombie's H2|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=February 26, 2009|access-date=February 26, 2009}}</ref> and said that he was trying to create almost the exact opposite of what people would expect.<ref>{{cite web|author=Scott Collura|url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/963/963622p2.html|title=Exclusive: The Shape of H2 (page 2)|publisher=News Corporation|work=IGN|access-date=March 22, 2009}}</ref> Known for filming multiple sequences during production of his films, Zombie filmed an alternate ending to ''Halloween II''. In the alternate ending, Loomis and Michael crash through the shed the police have surrounded, and out into the open air. As Loomis grasps at Michael's mask, and pleads for him to stop, Michael stabs him in the stomach, telling him to "Die!". With Loomis injured and unconscious (it is not revealed whether he lived or died), the police open fire on Michael, killing him. Laurie, now completely insane, leaves the shack, picks up Michael's knife and walks over to Loomis' unconscious body. Against Brackett's orders, the police open fire on Laurie, apparently killing her too.<ref>{{cite web|author=Ryan Rotten|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=11580|title=What We Saw on the Set of Halloween II|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=August 30, 2009|access-date=September 1, 2009}}</ref>

===Music===
{{Further|Halloween II (2009 soundtrack){{!}}''Halloween II'' (2009 soundtrack)}}
For the sequel, Zombie only used Carpenter's original theme music in the final scene of the film, though the director admits that he and music composer [[Tyler Bates]] tried to find other places to include it. According to Zombie, Carpenter's music did not fit with what was happening in the film; whenever he or Bates would insert it into a scene it "just wouldn't feel right" to the director.<ref name="Zombievid"/> Zombie also used popular culture songs throughout the film, with "[[Nights in White Satin]]" appearing the most prominently. Zombie chose songs that he liked, and that would enhance a given scene within the film.<ref name="Zombievid"/> An official soundtrack for the film was released on August 25, 2009.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=11078|title=Full Halloween 2 Soundtrack Listing|publisher=Shock Till You Drop|date=July 15, 2009|access-date=July 15, 2009}}</ref> In addition, an album featuring the music of [[psychobilly]] band Captain Clegg and the Night Creatures was released in conjunction with ''Halloween II'' on August 28, 2009. Captain Clegg and the Night Creatures is a fictional band that appears in ''Halloween II''.<ref>{{cite web|author=James Zahn|url=http://www.shocktillyoudrop.com/news/topnews.php?id=10753|title=Halloween 2 Captain Clegg Show Poster|publisher=Shock Till You Drop!|date=June 5, 2009|access-date=April 8, 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|author=James Zahn|url=http://www.brutalashell.com/2009/07/interview-captain-clegg-the-nightcreatures/|title=Exclusive First Interview: Captain Clegg & The Night Creatures|publisher=Brutal As Hell|date=July 28, 2009|access-date=April 8, 2010|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090804195219/http://www.brutalashell.com/2009/07/interview-captain-clegg-the-nightcreatures/|archive-date=August 4, 2009|df=mdy-all}}</ref> [[Nan Vernon]], who recorded a new version of the song "[[Mr. Sandman]]" for the end credits of the 2007 remake, recorded a cover of "[[Love Hurts]]" for ''Halloween II''.<ref>{{cite web|first=Matt|last=Marcheschi|url=http://www.soundtrack.net/news/article/?id=1051|title=Rob Zombie's ''Halloween'' soundtrack to include vintage recordings from KISS, Alice Cooper, Rush, Peter Frampton, Nazareth, Blue Oyster Cult, BTO, and More|publisher=SoundtrackNet|date=August 17, 2007|access-date=September 5, 2009|archive-date=October 25, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081025103110/http://www.soundtrack.net/news/article/?id=1051|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Box office===
On its opening day, the film grossed an estimated $7,640,000,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=daily&id=halloween209.htm|title=Daily Box Office|publisher=IMDb.com, Inc|work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=August 30, 2009}}</ref> which is less than the $10,896,610 Zombie's 2007 remake pulled in during the same weekend of August.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=daily&id=halloween07.htm|title=Daily Box Office Totals|publisher=IMDb.com, Inc|work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=August 29, 2009}}</ref> By the end of its opening weekend, ''Halloween II'' had grossed $16,349,565.<ref name="overall">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=halloween209.htm|title=Overall Box Office|publisher=[[Internet Movie Database|IMDb.com, Inc]]|work=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=June 25, 2010}}</ref> That weekend earned more than the entire box office performances of ''[[Halloween III: Season of the Witch]]'' ($14,400,000), ''[[Halloween 5: The Revenge of Michael Myers]]'' ($11,642,254), and ''[[Halloween: The Curse of Michael Myers]]'' ($15,116,634), in unadjusted dollars.<ref name="Hallcom">{{cite web|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/franchises/chart/?id=halloween.htm|title=Halloween film series comparison|publisher=IMDb.com, Inc|work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=August 31, 2009}}</ref> The film dropped 64.9% in its second weekend, only grossing $5,745,206 and slipping from third to sixth place. Grossing just $2,114,486 in its third weekend, ''Halloween II'' dropped out of the box office top ten to fourteenth place.<ref name="weekend">{{cite web|url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?page=weekend&id=halloween209.htm|title=Weekend Grosses|publisher=IMDb.com, Inc|work=Box Office Mojo|access-date=September 15, 2009}}</ref> The re-release of the film was intended to take advantage of the Halloween holiday, but the film only brought in approximately $475,000.<ref name="LA"/> By the end of its theatrical run, ''Halloween II'' grossed a total of $33,392,973 in North America, and an additional $6,028,494 overseas for a worldwide total of $39,421,467.<ref name="overall"/> Compared to the other ''Halloween'' films, the 2009 sequel sits in fourth place, just behind the original ''Halloween''.<ref name="Hallcom"/>

===Critical reception===
Based on 85 reviews collected by [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Halloween II'' has an overall 25% approval rating from critics, with an [[Weighted mean|average]] score of 4.60/10. The consensus reads, "Zombie shows flashes of vision in the follow-up to his ''Halloween'' reboot, but they're smothered by mountains of gore and hackneyed, brutal violence."<ref name="rt">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/h2_halloween_2/|title=H2: Halloween II (2009)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date=June 7, 2026}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 35 out of 100, based on 17 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Halloween II (2009) |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/halloween-ii-2009/ |access-date=2024-07-08 |website=www.metacritic.com |language=en}}</ref>

Rob Nelson, of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'', felt the use of Deborah and the white horse was nothing more than "silly", and he disagreed with Zombie's choice to film ''Halloween II'' in 16mm film, as opposed to the wider format of 35mm that he used on his 2007 remake. Nelson also stated that the hospital scene was nothing more than a "butcher"-version of Carpenter's 1981 sequel, with the rest of the film feeling like it was rushed and "slapped together" at the last minute.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.variety.com/review/VE1117940908.html?categoryid=31&cs=1|author=Rob Nelson|publisher=Reed Business Information|work=Variety|title=''Halloween II'' review|date=August 28, 2009|access-date=April 12, 2010}}</ref> In contrast, ''[[Time Out (company)|Time Out]]'' believed the hospital scene at the start of the film "[bested the 1981 sequel] in just about every respect". ''Time Out'' stated that Compton's portrayal of Laurie Strode showed an "intense, nontrivializing dedication to the role" that kept interest, while the storyline of Dr. Loomis's egocentricity hinders the overall storyline. ''Time Out'' also said that Zombie hurt the film by trying to show how "violence lingers with, and perverts, all who are touched by it", but then undercut himself with "carnivalesque" violence.<ref>{{cite journal|url=http://newyork.timeout.com/articles/film/77981/halloween-ii-film-review |journal=[[Time Out (company)|Time Out]] |title=''Halloween II'' review |issue=727 |date=September 3–9, 2009 |access-date=April 12, 2010 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090911132906/http://newyork.timeout.com/articles/film/77981/halloween-ii-film-review |archive-date=September 11, 2009 }}</ref>

''[[The Boston Globe]]'''s Tom Russo had varied reactions to the film. Russo pointed out that Zombie attempted to be more inventive with ''Halloween II'', but only achieved mixed results for his efforts. Russo referred to the dream sequences of Deborah Myers and the white horse as "pretentiously silly", but agreed that the scenes did help to break up the standard genre violence and even went so far as to compare the sensation created by those scenes to "[[Tim Burton]] doing straight horror". In the end, Russo claimed that "only the most hardcore fans" would want the film series to continue.<ref>{{cite news |author=Tom Russo |date=August 29, 2009 |title='Halloween' sequel has few treats |url=http://archive.boston.com/ae/movies/articles/2009/08/29/halloween_sequel_has_few_treats/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090905155212/https://www.boston.com/ae/movies/articles/2009/08/29/halloween_sequel_has_few_treats/ |archive-date=September 5, 2009 |access-date=April 12, 2010 |work=[[The Boston Globe]] |url-status=live |publisher=The New York Times Company}}</ref> Joe Neumaier, of the ''[[Daily News (New York)|Daily News]]'', stated that Zombie has found himself with ''Halloween II''. Neumaier describes the film as a successful "'character-based' monster-flick". He described Zombie's use of music from the 1970s, like [[The Moody Blues]]' "[[Nights in White Satin]]" and [[10cc]]'s "[[The Things We Do for Love (song)|The Things We Do for Love]]", as "terrifically odd" throughout the film. Neumaier also said that the imagery of Deborah Myers and the "ethereal white horse" were a "nice visual relief" from Michael's violent attacks.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.nydailynews.com/entertainment/movies/2009/08/28/2009-08-28_halloween_ii_.html|work=[[Daily News (New York)|Daily News]]|title='Halloween II' scares up surprisingly good stab at redefining slasher genre by director Rob Zombie|date=August 28, 2009|access-date=April 11, 2010 | first=Joe | last=Neumaier}}</ref>

The [[British Film Institute]] was more positive, calling the film the best since the original, as well as the best horror film of 2009.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bfi.org.uk/lists/great-horror-film-from-every-year-from-1922-now|title=A great horror film for every year, from 1922 to now|date=October 28, 2022|website=BFI}}</ref>

==Cancelled sequel==
In 2009, Zombie declined to return to direct the sequel to ''Halloween II''.<ref name="ScreenRant-2009-07-27" /><ref name="CB.com-2018-06-10" /> On August 30 that year, the next film in the series, ''Halloween 3D'', was announced by the Weinstein Company and planned to be released in 2010, [[Retroactive continuity|retroactively establishing]] Laurie to have killed Loomis instead of Michael, with [[Todd Farmer]] and [[Patrick Lussier]] scribing.<ref>{{Cite web |last=White |first=James |date=2009-08-31 |title=Halloween 3D planned for 2010 |url=https://www.gamesradar.com/halloween-3d-planned-for-2010/ |access-date=2019-09-23 |website=[[GamesRadar]]}}</ref> The film was ultimately cancelled in 2012 after Bob and Harvey Weinstein decided to green light and prioritize ''[[Scream 4]]'' (2011) instead.{{citation needed|date=September 2019}} Dimension Films eventually lost the rights to the Halloween franchise.<ref name="wrap"/> In 2016, [[Blumhouse Productions]] acquired the rights to the series. The next installment, 2018's ''[[Halloween (2018 film)|Halloween]]'', served as a direct sequel to the original 1978 and effected a [[retroactive continuity|retcon]] of previous sequels; [[Universal Pictures]] distributed the film and its subsequent sequels.
