'''''Alice in Wonderland''''' is a 1951 <!-- Do not add "British-" (see talk page). Despite being set in Britain, the film was produced and developed by an American studio.-->American animated musical [[fantasy comedy film]] produced by [[Walt Disney Productions]] and released by [[RKO Pictures|RKO Radio Pictures]]. It is based on [[Lewis Carroll]]'s 1865 novel ''[[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland]]'' and its 1871 sequel ''[[Through the Looking-Glass]]''. The production was supervised by [[Ben Sharpsteen]], and was directed by [[Clyde Geronimi]], [[Wilfred Jackson]], and [[Hamilton Luske]]. With the voices of [[Ed Wynn]], [[Richard Haydn]], [[Sterling Holloway]], [[Jerry Colonna (entertainer)|Jerry Colonna]] and [[Kathryn Beaumont]], the film follows a young girl, [[Portrayals of Alice in Wonderland#Disney|Alice]], who falls down a rabbit hole and enters a nonsensical world, [[Wonderland (fictional country)|Wonderland]], which is ruled by the [[Queen of Hearts (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Queen of Hearts]], while encountering strange creatures, including the [[Hatter (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Mad Hatter]] and the [[Cheshire Cat]].

[[Walt Disney]] was supposed to make his first film ''Alice'' which was supposed to star [[Mary Pickford]] as Alice, but he chose not to do the film  and instead did ''[[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937 film)|Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs]]'' (1937). However, the idea was eventually revived in the 1940s, following the success of ''Snow White''. The film was originally intended to be a [[live-action animation|live-action/animated film]], but Disney decided it would be a fully animated feature film. During its production, many sequences adapted from Carroll's books were later omitted, such as [[Jabberwocky]], the [[White Knight (Through the Looking-Glass)|White Knight]], the [[Duchess (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Duchess]], [[Mock Turtle]] and the [[Gryphon (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Gryphon]].

''Alice in Wonderland'' premiered at the [[Leicester Square Theatre]] in London on July 26, 1951, and was released in New York City on July 28. The film was also shown on television as one of the first episodes of ''[[Walt Disney anthology television series|Disneyland]]''. It was initially considered a box-office failure, grossing $2.4 million domestically, and received mixed reviews from critics. However, its 1974 theatrical re-release achieved greater success, leading to additional reissues, merchandising, and home video releases as critical reception improved over time.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot length must be between 400 and 700 words. Current count is 512. Please do not add anything without removing something first or making sure it is absolutely necessary! -->
In a park in [[England]], a young girl named [[Alice (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Alice]] listens distractedly to her sister's history lesson. As she begins daydreaming of a nonsensical world, she spots a waistcoat-wearing [[White Rabbit]] passing by, who complains of being late. Alice chases him into a burrow and plummets down a deep hole. She sees the White Rabbit disappear into a tiny door and tries to follow, but the door's talking knob advises her to [[Resizing (fiction)|alter her size]] using a mysterious drink and food. Alice eventually manages to shrink and passes through the door's keyhole and into Wonderland. She meets several strange characters including the [[Dodo (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Dodo]] and [[Tweedledee and Tweedledum]], the latter of whom recount the tale of "[[The Walrus and the Carpenter]]".

Alice eventually finds the White Rabbit in his house, but before she can ask what he is late for, she is sent to fetch some gloves. She eats a cookie from a cookie box and grows into a giant again, trapping herself in the White Rabbit's house. The White Rabbit, the Dodo, and chimney sweep [[Bill the Lizard]] believe Alice to be a monster and plot to burn the house down. Alice escapes by eating a carrot from the White Rabbit's garden and shrinking down to the size of an insect. She meets and sings with some talking flowers, but they chase her away upon accusing her of being a weed. Alice is instructed by the hookah-smoking [[Caterpillar (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Caterpillar]] to eat a part of his mushroom grow back to her original size. Alice decides to keep the remaining pieces of the mushroom on hand.

Alice meets the [[Cheshire Cat]] who advises her to visit the [[Mad Hatter]], [[March Hare]] and [[Dormouse (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Dormouse]]. The three are hosting a mad tea party and celebrate Alice's "[[unbirthday]]", a day when it is not her birthday. The White Rabbit appears, but the March Hare and Mad Hatter destroy his pocket watch and throw him out of the party. Alice gives up on her pursuit of the White Rabbit and decides to go home, but gets lost in the Tulgey Wood. The Cheshire Cat appears and leads Alice into a giant hedge maze ruled by the tyrannical [[Queen of Hearts (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Queen of Hearts]] and her smaller husband, the [[King of Hearts (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|King of Hearts]]. The Queen executes anyone who enrages her, and invites Alice to a bizarre croquet match using flamingoes and hedgehogs as the equipment.

The Cheshire Cat appears again and pulls a trick on the Queen which she accuses Alice of doing, and Alice is put on trial. However, Alice eats the remains of the Caterpillar's mushroom and grows to an enormous height. Tired of Wonderland, Alice openly insults the Queen by scolding for her rash behavior and calling her a "fat, pompous, bad-tempered old tyrant" until she shrinks to her normal size and is forced to flee after the Queen orders her execution. Alice becomes pursued by most of Wonderland's characters until she finally reunites with the Doorknob. The Doorknob tells her she is having a dream, forcing Alice to wake herself up. Finally, Alice and her sister head home for tea.

Hall (art director)|David Hall]] joined the Disney studio and was immediately assigned to create the concept artwork for the film.{{sfn|Ghez|2016|page=170}} A [[leica reel]], featuring Hall's paintings,<ref name="ReflectionsOnAlice"/> was completed on September 20, 1939,{{sfn|Barrier|2007|page=148}} but Disney was not pleased; he felt that Hall's drawings resembled Tenniel's illustrations too closely, making them too difficult to animate, and that the overall tone of Perkins' script was too grotesque and dark.<ref name="ThroughtheKeyhole" /> Disney also expressed a bit of disinterest in the project,{{sfn|Allan|1999|page=214}} stating that "there would be any harm in letting this thing sit for a while. Everyone is stale now. You'll look at it again and maybe have another idea on it. That's the way it works for me. I still feel that we can stick close to ''Alice in Wonderland'' and make it look like it and feel like it, you know".<ref name="AliceNeverMade">{{cite web|last=Korkis|first=Jim|url=https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-disney-alice-in-wonderland-never-made/|title=The Disney "Alice in Wonderland" Never Made|website=Cartoon Research|date=July 24, 2020|access-date=November 7, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230328142126/https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-disney-alice-in-wonderland-never-made/|archive-date=March 28, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> By February 1940, the project was still in the development, albeit slowly,{{sfn|Barrier|1999|page=269}} with an additional story meeting held on April 2 of that year.{{sfn|Barrier|2007|page=179}} After finishing his work on ''[[Fantasia (1940 film)|Fantasia]]'', concept artist Gordon Legg created new inspirational sketches for the film, but eventually left the studio the following year.{{sfn|Allan|1999|page=171}} By the fall of 1940, the animation work on ''Alice in Wonderland'' was planned to be completed within the next two years,<ref>{{cite news|last=Harrisen|first=Paul|date=October 2, 1940|title=Disney Sets 7 Pictures|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=KOFRAAAAIBAJ&dq=wind+in+the+willows+disney&pg=PA8&article_id=5986,505327|work=[[The Vindicator (Ohio newspaper)|The Vindicator]]|access-date=November 12, 2023|via=[[Google News Archive]]|page=10}}</ref> but Disney himself was rather unenthusiastic about the project.<ref>{{cite news|date=September 5, 1940|title=WALT DISNEY NOW HAS 6 PRODUCTIONS 34 SHORTS, ON THE FIRE ALL AT ONCE|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=T8E0AAAAIBAJ&dq=alice+in+wonderland+disney&pg=PA4&article_id=933,5168202|work=[[The Lewiston Daily Sun]]|access-date=October 15, 2023|via=[[Google News Archive]]|page=4}}</ref>

Disney brought up ''Alice in Wonderland'' again at a meeting on April 8, 1941,{{sfn|Bloom|2003|page=197}} offering to produce it as an animated film starring a live-action actress,{{sfn|Allan|1999|page=214}} similar to his earlier ''Alice Comedies'' series;{{sfn|Gabler|2006|page=432}} at the same meeting, [[Gloria Jean]] was suggested for the role of Alice.<ref name="AliceNeverMade" />
In October of that year, given the box-office underperformance of ''[[Pinocchio (1940 film)|Pinocchio]]'' (1940) and ''Fantasia'' (1940), as well as the [[World War II]] cutting off the foreign cinema market,<ref>{{cite web|last=Solomon|first=Charles|url=http://disney.go.com/disneyatoz/familymuseum/exhibits/articles/studioatwar/index.html|title=The Disney Studio at War|website=[[Walt Disney Family Museum]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081028031741/http://disney.go.com/disneyatoz/familymuseum/exhibits/articles/studioatwar/index.html|archive-date=October 28, 2008|access-date=November 12, 2023|url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Joseph Rosenberg]] of [[Bank of America (1904–1998)|Bank of America]] issued an ultimatum, ordering Disney to restrict himself to producing animated shorts and to finish features already in production; no other feature film would begin work until they had been released and earned back their costs.{{sfn|Gabler|2006|page=376}} In response, the production of ''Alice in Wonderland'' was heavily scaled back and eventually shelved.<ref name="ReflectionsOnAlice"/>{{efn|Steven Watts, however, indicates that ''Alice in Wonderland'' was put on hold as early as in June 1941.{{sfn|Watts|2001|page=259}}}}

===Return to production===
Disney first attempted to revive ''Alice in Wonderland'' in mid-1943; new storyboards were developed throughout much of that year, but the project did not move forward.{{sfn|Barrier|1999|pages=372-373}} [[Ginger Rogers]] was briefly considered to portray the role of Alice on a [[Live-action animated film|live-action/animated]] feature, but she only voiced the character for a record album of the story which was released by [[Decca Records]] on October 21, 1944.<ref>{{cite web|last=Ehrbar|first=Greg|url=https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-disney-alice-that-never-was-except-on-records/|title=The Disney "Alice" That Never Was… Except on Records|website=Cartoon Research|date=September 4, 2018|access-date=April 8, 2024}}</ref> In the fall of 1945,{{sfn|Barrier|1999|page=392}} Disney hired British author [[Aldous Huxley]] to work on a [[Live-action animated film|live-action/animated]] feature, titled ''Alice and the Mysterious Mr. Carroll'', which would revolve around Lewis Carroll and [[Alice Liddell]] (who was the inspiration for Alice).{{sfn|Bloom|2003|page=195}} Huxley delivered a fourteen-page treatment on November 23, 1945, followed by the first draft of the script, written on December 5 of that year.<ref name="HuxleysVersion">{{cite web|last=Korkis|first=Jim|url=https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/aldous-huxleys-version-of-the-disney-alice/|title=Aldous Huxley's Version of the Disney "Alice"|website=Cartoon Research|date=October 9, 2020|access-date=November 12, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230506165111/https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/aldous-huxleys-version-of-the-disney-alice/|archive-date=May 6, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> He devised a story in which Carroll and Liddell were misunderstood and persecuted following the publication of ''Alice in Wonderland'', while stage actress [[Ellen Terry]] was sympathetic to both Carroll and Liddell, and [[Queen Victoria]] served as the [[deus ex machina]], validating Carroll due to her appreciation for the book.{{sfn|Barrier|1999|p=392}} Disney considered child actress [[Margaret O'Brien]] for the title role,{{sfn|Gabler|2006|p=459}} but he felt that Huxley's version was too literal an adaptation of Carroll's book.<ref name="ReflectionsOnAlice"/> [[Background artist]] [[Mary Blair]] submitted some concept drawings for ''Alice in Wonderland''. Blair's paintings moved away from Tenniel's detailed illustrations by taking a [[Modernism|modernist]] stance, using bold and unreal colors. Walt liked Blair's designs, and the script was re-written to focus on comedy, music, and the whimsical side of Carroll's books.<ref name="ReflectionsOnAlice"/> [[Lisa Davis (actress)|Lisa Davis]] (who later voiced Anita Radcliffe in ''[[One Hundred and One Dalmatians]]'') and [[Luana Patten]] were also considered for the role of Alice.<ref name="ReflectionsOnAlice"/><ref>{{cite interview|last=Braun|first=Amy|url=https://www.dvdizzy.com/lisadavis-interview.html|title=UltimateDisney.com's Interview with Lisa Davis, the voice and model for 101 Dalmatians' Anita Radcliffe|publisher=DVDizzy|date=March 4, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260308201717/https://dvdizzy.com/lisadavis-interview.html|archive-date=March 8, 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

However, Disney soon realized that he could only do justice to the book by making an all-animated feature and, in 1946, work began on ''Alice in Wonderland''.<ref name="ThroughtheKeyhole"/> With the film tentatively scheduled for release in 1950,{{sfn|Barrier|1999|p=394}} animation crews on ''Alice in Wonderland'' and ''[[Cinderella (1950 film)|Cinderella]]'' effectively competed against each other to see which film would finish first.{{sfn|Gabler|2006|p=459}} By early 1948, ''Cinderella'' had progressed further than ''Alice in Wonderland''.{{sfn|Barrier|1999|p=398}}

A legal dispute with [[Dallas Bower]]'s [[Alice in Wonderland (1949 film)|1949 film version]] was also under way.<ref>[https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-disney-alice-that-never-was-except-on-records/ The Disney "Alice" That Never Was...Except on Records|Cartoon Research]</ref><ref>[http://sensesofcinema.com/2018/alice-in-wonderland/lou-bunins-alice-wonderland/ Senses of Cinema]</ref> Disney sued to prevent release of the British version in the U.S., and the case was extensively covered in [[Time (magazine)|''Time'' magazine]].<ref name="Time">{{cite magazine|url=http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,889135,00.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100328183004/http://www.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,889135,00.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=March 28, 2010|title=Cinema: Battle of Wonderland|date=16 July 1951|magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]]|access-date=29 September 2009}}</ref> The company that released the British version accused Disney of trying to exploit their film by releasing its version at virtually the same time.<ref name="Time" />

===Writing===
The first story meetings on ''Alice in Wonderland'' were held as early as in December 1938.{{sfn|Barrier|2007|page=148}} Through various drafts of the script, many sequences that were present in Carroll's book drifted in and out of the story. However, Disney insisted that the scenes themselves keep close to those in the novel since most of its humor is in the writing.<ref name="ThroughtheKeyhole"/>

One [[Deleted scene|omitted scene]] from the 1939 treatment of the film occurred outside the [[Duchess (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Duchess]]' manor, where the [[List of minor characters in the Alice series#Fish Footman|Fish Footman]] is giving a message to the [[List of minor characters in the Alice series#Frog Footman|Frog Footman]] to take to the Duchess, saying that she is invited to play croquet with the Queen of Hearts. Alice overhears this and sneaks into the kitchen of the manor, where she finds [[List of minor characters in the Alice series#The Duchess's Cook|the Duchess' Cook]] maniacally cooking and the Duchess nursing her baby. The cook is spraying pepper all over the room, causing the Duchess and Alice to sneeze and the baby to cry. After a quick conversation between Alice and the Duchess, the hot-tempered Cook starts throwing pots and pans at the noisy baby. Alice rescues the baby, but as she leaves the house the baby turns into a pig and runs away.<ref>{{cite AV media notes|title=Alice in Wonderland: 60th Anniversary Edition - Deleted Scene: "The Pig and Pepper"|publisher=Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment|year=2011|type=Blu-Ray}}</ref> The scene was scrapped for pacing reasons.

[[File:Alice_in_Wonderland_(1951)_-_The_Walrus_and_the_Carpenter.png|right|thumb|200px|The Walrus and the Carpenter as seen in the film's trailer]]
Another scene that was deleted from a later draft occurred in Tulgey Wood, where Alice encountered what appeared to be a sinister-looking [[Jabberwocky|Jabberwock]] hiding in the dark, before revealing himself as a comical-looking dragon-like beast with bells and factory whistles on his head. A song, "Beware the Jabberwock", was also written, but the scene was scrapped in favor of the "[[The Walrus and the Carpenter|Walrus and the Carpenter]]" poem.<ref name="ThroughtheKeyhole"/> Out of a desire to keep the "[[Jabberwocky]]" poem in the film, it was made to replace an original song for the Cheshire Cat, "I'm Odd".

Another deleted scene in Tulgey Wood shows Alice consulting with [[White Knight (Through the Looking-Glass)|The White Knight]], who was meant to be somewhat a caricature of Walt Disney. Although Disney liked the scene, he felt it was better if Alice learned her lesson by herself, hence the song "Very Good Advice". The Trial scene at the end of the film was edited with Alice being accused of teasing the Queen during a game of croquet rather than the [[Knave of Hearts (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Knave of Hearts]] being accused for stealing tarts the Queen made.<ref name="ThroughtheKeyhole"/>

Other characters, such as [[Mock Turtle]] and the [[Gryphon (Alice's Adventures in Wonderland)|Gryphon]] were discarded for pacing reasons, though they would later appear alongside Alice in some commercials.

In an effort to retain some of Carroll's imaginative poems, Disney commissioned top songwriters to compose songs built around them for use in the film. Over 30 potential songs were written, and many of them were included in the film—some for only a few seconds—the greatest number of songs of any Disney film. In 1939, [[Frank Churchill]] was assigned to compose songs, and they were accompanied by a story reel featuring artwork from David S. Hall. Although none of his songs were used in the finished film, the melody for "Lobster Quadrille" was used for the song "[[Never Smile at a Crocodile]]" in ''[[Peter Pan (1953 film)|Peter Pan]]'' which came out two years after the film's release. When work on ''Alice'' resumed in 1946, [[Tin Pan Alley]] songwriters [[Mack David]], [[Al Hoffman]] and [[Jerry Livingston]] began composing songs for it after working on ''[[Cinderella (1950 film)|Cinderella]]''. However, the only song by the trio that made it into the film was "The Unbirthday Song".<ref name="Bohn">{{cite book|last=Bohn|first=James|title=Music In Disney's Animated Features: Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs to The Jungle Book|chapter=Alice in Wonderland and Peter Pan|publisher=[[University Press of Mississippi]]|isbn=978-1496812148|year=2017}}</ref>

While he was composing songs in New York, [[Sammy Fain]] had heard that the Disney studios wanted him to compose songs for ''Alice in Wonderland''. He also suggested lyricist [[Bob Hilliard]] as his collaborator.<ref>{{cite interview|title=Sammy Fain|work=Walt's People—Talking Disney with the Artists who Knew Him, Volume 12|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=PQ_nGPA2-qMC&q=peter%20pan%20sammy%20fain&pg=PT164|subject=Sammy Fain|interviewer=David Tietyen|date=August 22, 1978}}</ref> The two wrote two unused songs for the film, "Beyond the Laughing Sky" and "I'm Odd". The music for the former song was kept but the lyrics were changed, and it later became the title song for ''Peter Pan'', "The Second Star to the Right".<ref name="Bohn" /><ref>{{cite AV media notes|title=Deleted Songs: Beyond the Laughing Sky|others=Kathryn Beaumont|publisher=Walt Disney Home Entertainment|year=2004|type=DVD}}</ref> By April 1950, Fain and Hilliard had finished composing songs for the film.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=-fUDAAAAMBAJ&q=peter%20pan%20sammy%20fain%20billboard&pg=PA28|title=Music—As Written|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|volume=62|issue=15|date=April 15, 1950|access-date=June 26, 2018}}</ref>

The [[Alice in Wonderland (song)|title song]], composed by Sammy Fain, has become a jazz standard,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thesoundofvinyl.com/*/Soundtracks/Songs-From-Alice-in-Wonderland/5D6H0AST000|title=Songs From Alice in Wonderland|website=Sound of Vinyl|language=en|access-date=2018-11-12|archive-date=November 12, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181112101304/https://thesoundofvinyl.com/*/Soundtracks/Songs-From-Alice-in-Wonderland/5D6H0AST000|url-status=dead}}</ref> adapted by [[jazz]] pianist [[Dave Brubeck]] in 1952 and included on his 1957 Columbia album ''[[Dave Digs Disney]]''. Another notable jazz version is by [[Bill Evans]] on his classic 1961 album, ''[[Sunday at the Village Vanguard]]''.<ref>{{cite book| last=Levine| first=Mark| title=The Jazz Theory Book| url=https://books.google.com/books?id=iyNQpJ4oaMcC&pg=PT597| date=12 January 2011| publisher=O'Reilly Media, Inc.| isbn=978-1-4571-0145-8| page=597}}</ref>
The song, "In a World of My Own", is included on the [[Classic Disney: 60 Years of Musical Magic#Disc 5 (orange)|orange disc]] of ''[[Classic Disney: 60 Years of Musical Magic]]''.

=== Soundtrack and Camarata version ===
There was no soundtrack album available when the film was released in 1951. RCA Victor released a story album and single records with Kathryn Beaumont and several cast members that re-created the story, but it was not the soundtrack. In 1944, [[Decca Records]] had released a [[Ginger Rogers]] dramatization of Lewis Carroll's book with Disney cover art (perhaps tying in with earlier discussions of her being cast as a live-action Disney "Alice"),<ref>{{Cite web|title=The Disney "Alice" That Never Was… Except on Records {{!}}|url=https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-disney-alice-that-never-was-except-on-records/|access-date=2021-04-09|website=cartoonresearch.com}}</ref> Decca did indeed license the rights to release the 1951 Alice soundtrack from Disney, but later decided against it and never produced one. When Disney started its own record company, [[Disneyland Records]], in Spring 1956, it was found to be economically unfeasible at the time to take on the fees and other costs to produce a soundtrack album.<ref>{{Cite journal|last=Sadoff|first=Ronald H.|date=October 2007|title=Mouse Tracks: The Story of Walt Disney Records. By Tim Hollis and Greg Ehrbar. Jackson: The University Press of Mississippi, 2006. 221 pp. ISBN 1 57806 848 7 (paperback); ISBN 1 57806 849 5 (cloth)|url=http://dx.doi.org/10.1017/s026114300700342x|journal=Popular Music|volume=26|issue=3|pages=537–539|doi=10.1017/s026114300700342x|s2cid=162973106|issn=0261-1430|url-access=subscription}}</ref>

In 1957, [[Salvador Camarata|Tutti Camarata]] arranged and conducted an elaborate original production of the Alice score with [[Darlene Gillespie]], who had shown great promise among the ''[[The Mickey Mouse Club|Mickey Mouse Club]]'' cast as a singer. Camarata assembled a new orchestra and chorus (possibly with the cooperation of [[Norman Luboff]], as Betty Mulliner (Luboff) and choir member [[Thurl Ravenscroft]] can be heard) in the [[Capitol Studios|Capitol studios]] in Hollywood. The resulting album became one of the most influential and acclaimed studio versions of a score, garnering praise from within the industry as well as the public.<ref>{{Cite web|title=The #1 All-Time Greatest Cartoon Record Album Ever Made {{!}}|url=https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/the-1-all-time-greatest-cartoon-record-album-ever-made/|access-date=2021-04-09|website=cartoonresearch.com}}</ref> The original issue (WDL-4025), depicting Alice seated in a tree with characters beneath her, is highly collectible. The album was so popular it was reissued in 1959, 1963 and 1968 with different covers, including story albums with books and single records, all featuring music from this album, as well as translated versions of the Camarata ''Alice'' music for international recordings.

Selections from this album are still heard in the queue for the [[Alice in Wonderland (Disneyland attraction)|Alice In Wonderland]] dark ride at Disneyland in California and during the [[Storybook Land Canal Boats]] ride. Tokyo Disneyland incorporated musical arrangements from the Camarata version for live shows and CD releases.

To date, the only soundtrack material ever made available on vinyl records was released outside the United States. In the late nineties, over 45 years after the film's original release, a soundtrack album of ''Alice in Wonderland'' was finally released in the U.S. on [[Compact Disc|Audio CD]] by [[Walt Disney Records]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000001M4E |title=Walt Disney's Alice In Wonderland: Classic Soundtrack Series: Sammy Fain, Bob Hilliard |work=Amazon.com |access-date=July 13, 2010}}</ref>

== Release ==
''Alice in Wonderland''{{'s}} world premiere took place at the [[Leicester Square Theatre]] in [[London]] on July 26, 1951.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://d23.com/this-day/alice-in-wonderland-premieres-in-england/|title=Alice in Wonderland Premieres in England|date=July 26, 1951 |publisher=[[D23 (Disney)|D23]]|access-date=June 25, 2018}}</ref> During the film's initial theatrical run, the film was released as a [[double feature]] with the ''[[True-Life Adventures]]'' documentary short, ''[[Nature's Half Acre]]''.<ref name="NewYorkTimes" /> Following the film's initial lukewarm reception, it was never re-released theatrically in Disney's lifetime, instead being shown occasionally on television. ''Alice in Wonderland'' aired as the second episode of the ''[[Walt Disney anthology television series#Walt Disney's Disneyland|Walt Disney's Disneyland]]'' television series on [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]] on November 3, 1954,<ref name="Televisionland" /> in a severely edited version cut down to less than an hour.

Beginning in 1971, the film was screened in several sold-out venues at college campuses, becoming the most rented film in some cities. Then, in 1974, Disney gave ''Alice in Wonderland'' its first theatrical re-release. The company even promoted it as a film in tune with the "[[Psychedelic era|psychedelic times]]", using radio commercials featuring allusions to the song "[[White Rabbit (song)|White Rabbit]]" performed by [[Jefferson Airplane]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Harmetz|first=Aljean|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1974/04/21/archives/alice-returns-curiouser-and-curiouser-disneys-alice-in-wonderland.html|title='Alice Returns', Curiouser and Curiouser|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=April 22, 1974|access-date=June 25, 2018}}</ref> This release was so successful that it warranted a subsequent re-release in 1981.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.moviefone.com/2016/07/27/alice-in-wonderland-disney-facts/|title='Alice in Wonderland': 13 Things You (Probably) Didn't Know About the Disney Classic|publisher=[[Moviefone]]|access-date=January 29, 2020}}</ref> It was re-released in the UK on December 22, 1969 and on July 26, 1979.{{citation needed|reason=Please provide a source as to the fact that these re-releases happened.|date=July 2026}}

=== Marketing ===
Disney sought to use the new medium of [[television]] to help advertise ''Alice in Wonderland''. In March 1950, he spoke to his brother [[Roy O. Disney|Roy]] about launching a television program featuring the studio's animated shorts. Roy agreed, and later that summer they spoke to the [[Coca-Cola Company]] about sponsoring an hour-long Christmas broadcast featuring Disney hosting several cartoons and a scene from the upcoming film. The program became ''[[One Hour in Wonderland]]'', which was aired on [[NBC]] on Christmas Day 1950.{{sfn|Barrier|2007|p=228}} At the same time, a ten-minute featurette about the making of the film, ''Operation: Wonderland'', was produced and screened in theaters and on television stations. Additionally, Disney, Kathryn Beaumont, and Sterling Holloway appeared on ''[[The Fred Waring Show]]'' on March 18, 1951, to promote the film.<ref name="Televisionland">{{cite web|url=http://waltdisney.com/blog/alice-televisionland|title=Alice in Televisionland|publisher=[[The Walt Disney Family Museum]]|date=April 4, 2011|access-date=June 25, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180626030140/http://waltdisney.com/blog/alice-televisionland|archive-date=June 26, 2018|url-status=dead|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

=== Home media ===
''Alice in Wonderland'' was one of the first titles available for the [[Video rental shop|rental market]] on [[VHS]] and [[Betamax|Beta]] and for retail sale on RCA's short-lived CED Videodisc format. The film was released on October 15, 1981, on VHS, [[Capacitance Electronic Disc|CED Videodisc]], and [[Betamax]] for its 30th anniversary.{{citation needed|date=December 2023}} Five years later, it was re-issued in the "Wonderland Sale" promotion on May 28, 1986 on VHS, Betamax, and [[LaserDisc]] for its 35th anniversary. It was then re-promoted on July 12, 1991 for its 40th anniversary, surrounding the video re-issue of ''[[Robin Hood (1973 film)|Robin Hood]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Seideman|first=Tony|title=Prices Tumble as Disney Enters Its 'Wonderland' Campaign|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|date=April 12, 1986|volume=98|issue=15|page=52|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=7yQEAAAAMBAJ&q=alice%20in%20wonderland%20disney%20billboard&pg=PA52}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |url=https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Music/Billboard/90s/1991/BB-1991-05-18.pdf |title='Robin' To Perk Up Midsummer Nights |last=McCullagh |first=Jim |date=May 18, 1991 |magazine=Billboard |access-date=September 27, 2019 |page=78}}</ref> The film would later receive a third release on VHS and LaserDisc as part of the Walt Disney Masterpiece Collection on October 28, 1994, with the VHS cassette of the film being re-released multiple times: from March 3, 1995 to July 13, 1999.

In January 2000, Walt Disney Home Video launched the Gold Classic Collection, and then ''Alice in Wonderland'' was re-issued on VHS and DVD in the line on July 4, 2000.<ref>{{cite press release|url=https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Imagination+for+a+Lifetime+--+Disney+Titles+All+the+Time%3B+Walt+Disney...-a058460870|title=Imagination for a Lifetime -- Disney Titles All the Time; Walt Disney Home Video Debuts the "Gold Classic Collection"; An Animated Masterpiece Every Month in 2000.|location=Burbank, California|agency=[[Business Wire]]|date=January 6, 2000|access-date=June 25, 2018|archive-date=May 22, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180522180912/https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Imagination+for+a+Lifetime+--+Disney+Titles+All+the+Time%3B+Walt+Disney...-a058460870|url-status=dead}}</ref> The DVD contained the ''Operation: Wonderland'' featurette, several sing-a-long videos, a storybook, a trivia game, and its theatrical trailer.<ref>{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20000815083314/http://disney.go.com/disneyvideos/disneygold/html/aliceinwonderland.html|title=Alice in Wonderland &nbsp;— Disney Gold Collection|url=http://disney.go.com:80/disneyvideos/disneygold/html/aliceinwonderland.html|archive-date=August 15, 2000|access-date=June 25, 2018|publisher=[[Disney.com|Disney.go.com]]|url-status=live|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

A fully restored two-disc "Masterpiece Edition" DVD was released on January 27, 2004, including the full hour-long episode of the Disney television show with Kathryn Beaumont, [[Edgar Bergen]], Charlie McCarthy and Mortimer Snerd, [[Bobby Driscoll]] and others that promoted the film, computer games, deleted scenes, songs and related materials, and went into moratorium in January 2009. A year and two months later, Disney released a 2-disc special "Un-Anniversary" edition DVD on March 30, 2010 to promote [[Alice in Wonderland (2010 film)|the recent Tim Burton version]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://disneydvd.disney.go.com/alice-in-wonderland-special-unanniversary-edition.html |title=Official DVD website |work=Disney.Go.com |access-date=July 13, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100725215631/http://disneydvd.disney.go.com/alice-in-wonderland-special-unanniversary-edition.html |archive-date=July 25, 2010 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all}}</ref> The film was released in a Blu-ray and DVD set on February 1, 2011, to celebrate its 60th anniversary,<ref>{{cite web |url=https://highdefdiscnews.com/?p=49014#more-49014 |title=A Hi-Def sneak peek at Disney's planned 2011 Blu-ray titles |author=Justin Sluss |date=July 28, 2010 |publisher=highdefdiscnews.com |access-date=October 7, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100804014104/http://www.highdefdiscnews.com/?p=49014#more-49014 |archive-date=August 4, 2010 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all}}</ref> featuring a new HD restoration of the film and many bonus features. Disney re-released the film on Blu-ray and DVD on April 26, 2016, to celebrate the film's 65th anniversary (a reprint of the anniversary release from five years earlier).

The film was released on [[Disney+]] on November 12, 2019.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Alexander |first1=Julia |title=Disney+ launch lineup: Every movie and TV show available to stream on day one |url=https://www.theverge.com/2019/10/14/20913417/disney-plus-launch-lineup-marvel-star-wars-pixar-tv-shows-movies-simpsons-national-geographic |access-date=16 August 2020 |work=The Verge |date=14 October 2019 |language=en}}</ref>

On May 5, 2026, a [[Ultra HD Blu-ray|4K Ultra HD]] Blu-ray was released to celebrate the film's 75th anniversary with a restoration from a team that spent nine months completing a digital scan of the original nitrate successive exposure negatives, followed by extensive clean-up to address dust, warping, and age-related wear. Walt Disney Animation Research Library collaborated with Walt Disney Animation Studios veteran Michael Giaimo to ensure the restoration remained true to the filmmakers' original intent while reviewing every shot and refining color and luminance.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Latchem |first1=John |title=Disney Releasing 4K Restoration of Animated Classic 'Alice in Wonderland' May 5 |url=https://www.mediaplaynews.com/disney-releasing-4k-restoration-of-animated-classic-alice-in-wonderland-may-5/ |access-date=8 April 2026 |work=Media Play news |date=8 April 2026 |language=en}}</ref>

== Reception==
=== Box office ===
During its initial theatrical run, the film grossed $2.4 million in domestic rentals.<ref>{{cite magazine|title=The Top Box Office Hits of 1951|url=https://archive.org/stream/variety185-1952-01#page/n69/mode/2up/|magazine=Variety|page=70|date=January 2, 1952|access-date=June 25, 2018}}</ref> Because of the film's production budget of $3 million, the studio [[Write-off|wrote off]] a million-dollar loss.{{sfn|Barrier|2007|p=230}} During its theatrical re-release in 1974, the film grossed $3.5 million in domestic rentals.<ref>{{cite news|title=Big Rental Films of 1974|magazine=Variety|date=January 8, 1975|page=24}}</ref>

=== Critical reaction ===
Initial reviews for ''Alice in Wonderland'' were mixed,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/alice_in_wonderland_1951/reviews|title=Alice in Wonderland Reviews|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date=July 13, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Fowler |first=Matt |date=March 26, 2019 |title=The 25 Best Disney Animated Movies |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2019/03/26/the-25-best-disney-animated-movies |access-date=January 29, 2025 |work=[[IGN]] |quote=951's Alice in Wonderland wasn't received well by critics when it was first released, but has since been hailed a triumph of animation far ahead of its time.}}</ref> with particularly strong criticism coming from the [[British press]].<ref>{{Cite book |last=Mattis |first=Tyler |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Z_w6EQAAQBAJ&dq=alice+in+wonderland+1951+%22negative+reviews%22&pg=PA161 |title=Disney's Live-Action Movie Bombs, 1979-2019 |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |year=2024 |isbn=9781476696256 |location=United States |pages=161 |via=[[Google Books]]}}</ref> [[Bosley Crowther]], reviewing for ''[[The New York Times]]'', complimented that "...if you are not too particular about the images of Carroll and Tenniel, if you are high on Disney whimsey and if you'll take a somewhat slow, uneven pace, you should find this picture entertaining. Especially should it be for the kids, who are not so demanding of fidelity as are their moms and dads. A few of the episodes are dandy, such as the mad tea party and the caucus race; the music is tuneful and sugary and the color is excellent."<ref name="NewYorkTimes">{{cite news|last=Crowther|first=Bosley|title=THE SCREEN IN REVIEW; Disney's Cartoon Adaptation of 'Alice in Wonderland' Arrives at Criterion|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1951/07/30/archives/the-screen-in-review-disneys-cartoon-adaptation-of-alice-in.html|newspaper=The New York Times|page=12|date=July 30, 1951|access-date=January 6, 2019}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote that the film "has an earnest charm and a chimerical beauty that best shows off the Carroll fantasy. However, it has not been able to add any real heart or warmth, ingredients missing from the two tomes and which have always been an integral part of the previous Disney feature cartoons."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://archive.org/details/variety183-1951-07/page/n7|title='Alice in Wonderland' Enchanting Walt Disney interpretation of Lewis Carroll classic; bright b.o.|magazine=Variety|page=8|via=[[Internet Archive]]|date=July 4, 1951|access-date=January 6, 2019}}</ref>

Mae Tinee of the ''[[Chicago Tribune]]'' wrote that "While the Disney figures do resemble John Tenniel's famous sketches, they abound in energy but are utterly lacking in enchantment, and seem more closely related to [[Pluto (Disney)|Pluto]], the clumsy pup, than the products of Carroll's imagination. Youngsters probably will find it a likable cartoon, full of lively characters, with Alice's dream bedecked with just a touch of nightmare—those who cherish the old story as I have probably will be distinctly disappointed."<ref>{{cite news|last=Tinee|first=Mae|title=Finds 'Alice' More Disney Than Caroll|url=https://www.newspapers.com/newspage/372313868/|newspaper=Chicago Tribune|via=[[Newspapers.com]]|date=August 6, 1951|access-date=January 6, 2019|url-access=subscription}}</ref> ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' stated that "Judged simply as the latest in the long, popular line of Disney cartoons, ''Alice'' lacks a developed story line, which the studio's continuity experts, for all their freedom with scissors and paste, have been unable to put together out of the episodic books. Much of it is familiar stuff; Carroll's garden of live flowers prompts Disney to revive the style of his ''[[Silly Symphonies]]''. Yet there is plenty to delight youngsters, and there are flashes of cartooning ingenuity that should appeal to grownups."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=http://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,856899,00.html|title=Cinema: Battle of Wonderland III|magazine=Time|date=August 6, 1951|volume=58|issue=6|page=69|access-date=May 24, 2020|url-access=subscription|archive-date=June 26, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220626075041/http://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,856899,00.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> Writing for ''[[/Film]]'', Miyako Pleines says "Unlike the other Disney princesses before her, Alice seemed to have no real purpose (even if that purpose is simply to be a damsel in distress). People saw her as lacking ambition and drive, a lazy girl who daydreamed during her studies and wandered into a magical world."<ref name="AliceNotSuccess"/>

''Alice in Wonderland'' was met with great criticism from Carroll fans, as well as from [[Cinema of the United Kingdom|British film]] and [[Literary criticism|literary critics]], who accused Disney of "[[Americanization|Americanizing]]" a great work of [[English literature]].<ref>{{cite book|last=Thomas |first=Bob |year=1976 |title=Walt Disney: An American Original |location=New York |publisher=Hyperion Press |pages=220–221 |isbn=0-7868-6027-8}}</ref> Walt Disney was not surprised by the critical reception to ''Alice in Wonderland'' as his version of ''Alice'' was intended for large family audiences, not literary critics. Additionally, the film was met with a lukewarm response at the box office.<ref>{{cite web|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070810212331/http://disney.go.com/disneyatoz/familymuseum/exhibits/articles/aliceinwonderlandaftermath/index.html|url=http://disney.go.com/disneyatoz/familymuseum/exhibits/articles/aliceinwonderlandaftermath/index.html |title=Alice in Wonderland: The Aftermath|publisher=[[Disney.com|Disney.go.com]]|archive-date=August 10, 2007|access-date=June 25, 2018}}</ref> Additionally, he remarked that the film failed because it lacked heart.{{sfn|Gabler|2006|p=487}} In ''The Disney Films'', [[Leonard Maltin]] says that animator [[Ward Kimball]] felt the film failed because "it suffered from too many cooks—directors. Here was a case of five directors each trying to top the other guy and make his sequence the biggest and craziest in the show. This had a self-canceling effect on the final product."<ref>{{cite book|author-link=Leonard Maltin |last=Maltin |first=Leonard |year=1973 |title=The Disney Films |location=New York |publisher=Crown |page=103 |isbn=0-7868-8527-0}}</ref> Animators [[Frank Thomas (animator)|Frank Thomas]] and [[Ollie Johnston]] also considered the film a failure and stated on their website that they felt "none of us could fully capture the characters of Lewis Carrol's imagination."<ref>{{cite web|website=Frank and Ollie|title=Feature Films|url=http://frankandollie.com/Film_Features.html|access-date=August 2, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20051121221342/http://frankandollie.com/Film_Features.html|archive-date=November 21, 2005}}</ref>

''[[/Film]]'' reported that the film is "now considered to be a beloved Disney classic."<ref name="AliceNotSuccess">{{Cite web |last= Pleines |first= Miyako |date=February 22, 2022 |title=Why Disney's Alice In Wonderland Didn't Find Success Until The '70s|url= https://www.slashfilm.com/776359/why-disneys-alice-in-wonderland-didnt-find-success-until-the-70s/ |access-date=September 13, 2023 |website=[[/Film]] }}</ref> On the film aggregator website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Alice in Wonderland'' received an approval rating of {{RT data|score}} from {{RT data|count}} critical reviews with an average rating of {{RT data|average}}. The consensus states, "A good introduction to Lewis Carroll's classic, ''Alice in Wonderland'' boasts some of the Disney canon's most surreal and twisted images."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1029112-alice_in_wonderland|title=Alice in Wonderland|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]]|access-date={{RT data|access date}}}}{{RT data|edit}}</ref> Another film review aggregator, [[Metacritic]], which assigns a rating out of 100 based on top reviews from mainstream critics, calculated a score of 68 based on 10 reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/alice-in-wonderland-1951/ |title=Alice in Wonderland Reviews |publisher=[[Metacritic]] |access-date=September 14, 2023}}</ref>

==Legacy==
=== Stage version ===
''Alice in Wonderland'' has been condensed into a one-act stage version entitled, ''Alice in Wonderland, Jr''. The stage version is solely meant for middle and high school productions and includes the majority of the film's songs and others including ''[[Song of the South]]''{{'}}s "[[Zip-a-Dee-Doo-Dah]]", two new reprises of "I'm Late!", and three new numbers entitled "Ocean of Tears", "Simon Says", and "Who Are You?" respectively. This 60–80 minute version is licensed by [[Music Theatre International]] in the Broadway, Jr. Collection along with other Disney Theatrical shows such as ''[[Aladdin, Jr.|Disney's Aladdin, Jr.]]'', ''[[Mulan Jr.|Disney's Mulan, Jr.]]'', ''[[Beauty and the Beast (musical)|Beauty and the Beast]]'', Disney's ''[[High School Musical]]: On Stage!'', [[Elton John]] and [[Tim Rice]]'s ''[[Aida (musical)|Aida]]'', and many more. It was not available to license from 2018 to 2023,<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.mtishows.com/disneys-alice-in-wonderland-jr |title=Music Theatre International: Licensing Musical Theater Theatrical Performance Rights and Materials to Schools, Community and Professional Theatres since 1952 |access-date=July 27, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130829060059/http://www.mtishows.com/show_detail.asp?showid=000277 |archive-date=August 29, 2013 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all}}</ref> but is available again, now with some new songs from the 2010 film ''[[Alice in Wonderland (2010 film)|Alice in Wonderland]]'', as of 2024.<ref>{{cite web

=== Theme parks ===
[[File:Disneyland Alice 2012-06-30.jpg|250px|thumb|right|Alice at [[Disneyland]], 2012.]]
Costumed versions of Alice, The Mad Hatter, The White Rabbit, The Queen of Hearts, Tweedledum, and Tweedledee make regular appearances at the Disney theme parks and resorts, and other characters from the film (including the Walrus and the March Hare) have featured in the theme parks, although quite rarely. Disneyland features a [[Alice in Wonderland (Disneyland attraction)|ride-through visit to Wonderland]] on board a Caterpillar-shaped ride vehicle; this adventure is unique to Disneyland and has not been reproduced at Disney's other parks. More famously, five of the six Disneyland-style theme parks feature [[Mad Tea Party]], a [[teacups]] ride based on Disney's adaptation of ''Alice in Wonderland''. The [[Disneyland]] and [[Tokyo Disneyland]] versions of [[It's a Small World]] also featured Alice and White Rabbit in the England scene since 2009 (in Disneyland) and 2018 (in Tokyo Disneyland)''<ref>{{Cite web |date=April 17, 2024 |title=Every Disney Character That's Hidden In It's a Small World |url=https://www.msn.com/en-us/movies/news/every-disney-character-that-s-hidden-in-it-s-a-small-world/ar-BB1oavey?item=flightsprg-tipsubsc-v1a?season89c26d1ce19649ab8c6b1ea6904de419thu |website=[[MSN]]}}</ref>''.

''Alice in Wonderland'' is also frequently featured in many parades and shows in the Disney Theme Parks, including [[Main Street Electrical Parade|The Main Street Electrical Parade]], [[SpectroMagic]], ''[[Fantasmic!]]'', [[Main Street Electrical Parade|Dreamlights]], The Move It! Shake It! Celebrate It! Street Party and [[Walt Disney's Parade of Dreams]]. Tokyo Disneyland presented several live productions, including ''Alice's Wonderland Party'' and ''Alice's Wonderland Tales''. [[Disneyland]] Park features the world's only [[Alice in Wonderland (Disneyland attraction)|dark ride based on the film]] in addition to the Mad Tea Party teacup ride,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://disneyland.disney.go.com/attractions/?name=MadTeaPartyAttractionPage |title=Disneyland's Mad Tea Party Page |access-date=July 13, 2010| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100620214255/http://disneyland.disney.go.com/disneyland/en_US/parks/attractions/detail?name=MadTeaPartyAttractionPage| archive-date= June 20, 2010 |url-status= live}}</ref> and [[Disneyland Paris]] also contains a hedge maze called [[Alice's Curious Labyrinth]], which takes its inspiration from the film.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://parks.disneylandparis.co.uk/disneyland-park/lands/fantasyland/attractions/alices-curious-labyrinth.xhtml |title=Disneyland Paris' Alice's Curious Labyrinth Page |access-date=July 13, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090105234406/http://parks.disneylandparis.co.uk/disneyland-park/lands/fantasyland/attractions/alices-curious-labyrinth.xhtml |archive-date=January 5, 2009 |url-status=dead |df=mdy-all}}</ref> The now-defunct [[Mickey Mouse Revue]], shown at [[Walt Disney World]] and later at [[Tokyo Disneyland]], contained characters and scenes from the film.

On 25 May 2024, a limited-run stage show ''Alice and the Queen of Hearts: Back to Wonderland'' opened in the Theater of the Stars in [[Walt Disney Studios Park]] at [[Disneyland Paris]]. It will be presented until 29 September 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Alice & The Queen of Hearts Back to Wonderland |url=https://www.disneylandparis.com/en-ie/entertainment/walt-disney-studios-park/alice-back-to-wonderland-show/ |website=www.disneylandparis.com}}</ref>

=== Video games ===
{{More citations needed section|date=March 2024}}
In ''[[Disney's Villains' Revenge]]'', the Queen of Hearts is one of the villains who tries to turn the ending to her story to where she finally cuts off Alice's head.

''[[Mickey Mousecapade]]'' features various characters from the film. The Japanese version, in fact, is based very heavily on the film, with almost every reference in the game coming from the film.

A [[Alice in Wonderland (2000 video game)|video game version]] of the film was released on [[Game Boy Color]] by [[Nintendo|Nintendo of America]] on October 4, 2000, in North America.

The ''[[Kingdom Hearts]]'' video game series includes Wonderland as a playable world in the titles ''[[Kingdom Hearts (video game)|Kingdom Hearts]]'', ''[[Kingdom Hearts: Chain of Memories|Chain of Memories]]'', ''[[Kingdom Hearts 358/2 Days|358/2 Days]]'', ''[[Kingdom Hearts Coded|Coded]]'', and ''[[Kingdom Hearts χ]]''. In the games, Alice is one of seven "Princesses of Heart", a group of maidens who are a major part of the plot in the series. Other characters from the film that appear in the games include the Queen of Hearts and the Card Soldiers (also [[Boss (video games)|enemies]] in the games), Cheshire Cat, White Rabbit, Doorknob, Mad Hatter, March Hare, Tweedle Dee and Tweedle Dum, and the Caterpillar (this last in the non-official ''Kingdom Hearts V Cast'' game only).<ref>{{cite web|url=http://na.square-enix.com/games/kingdomhearts/ |title=Kingdom Hearts Official Page |access-date=July 13, 2010}}</ref>

In ''[[Toy Story 3 (video game)|Toy Story 3: The Video Game]]'', the Mad Hatter's hat is one of the hats you can have the townsfolk wear.

In ''[[Kinect Disneyland Adventures]]'', Alice, the Mad Hatter, the White Rabbit, and the Queen of Hearts make appearances, while the Caterpillar, the Cheshire Cat and the March Hare only appear in specific sections of the ''Alice in Wonderland'' attraction located in [[Fantasyland]]. The player can also acquire either Alice's blue dress and white pinafore apron or the Mad Hatter's olive green hat and mustard tailcoat with a blue bow tie as costumes to wear (depending on the [[Avatar (computing)|avatar]]'s gender) in several different stores located around the park.

In ''[[Disney Infinity (video game)|Disney Infinity]]'', there are Power Discs based on ''Alice in Wonderland''.

Several characters of the movie make appearances throughout the Epic Mickey-games. For example, the cards are seen throughout Mickeyjunk Mountain in the original ''[[Epic Mickey]]'', Alice appears as a statue carrying a projector screen in ''[[Epic Mickey 2]]'' and Alice, the Mad Hatter, and the Cheshire Cat appear as unlockable characters in ''[[Epic Mickey: Power of Illusion]]''.

Alice, the White Rabbit, the Mad Hatter, the March Hare, the Cheshire Cat, the Queen of Hearts and the Caterpillar appear as playable characters in ''[[Disney Magic Kingdoms]]'', along with some attractions based on locations of the film or real attractions from Disney Parks, as content to unlock for a limited time.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qWpzbzxU_K4 |title=Update 15: Alice in Wonderland {{!}} Livestream|publisher=[[YouTube]]|date=October 20, 2017}}</ref>

In ''[[Disney Mirrorverse]]'' Alice is playable.
