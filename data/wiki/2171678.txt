'''''Trouble Every Day''''' is a 2001 [[Erotic film|erotic]] [[horror film|horror]] film directed by [[Claire Denis]] and written by Denis and [[Jean-Pol Fargeau]]. It stars [[Vincent Gallo]], [[Tricia Vessey]], [[Béatrice Dalle]], [[Alex Descas]] and [[Marilu Marini]]. The film's [[Trouble Every Day (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] is provided by [[Tindersticks]]. The plot follows American scientist Shane Brown, who neglects his wife on their honeymoon to Paris to locate his former colleague Léo Semenau and his wife Coré, who holds cannibalistic urges.

Described as part of the [[New French Extremity]] film movement, the film caused controversy at its premiere at [[2001 Cannes Film Festival]] and polarized critics, with many booing or walking out during the screening.<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Gibbons |first1=Fiachra |last2=Jeffries |first2=Stuart |date=2001-05-14 |title=Cannes audience left open-mouthed |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2001/may/14/cannes2001.cannesfilmfestival |access-date=2025-02-03 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

==Plot==
An American couple, Dr. Shane Brown and his wife June, go to [[Paris]], ostensibly for their honeymoon. In reality, Shane has come to Paris to hunt down [[neuroscientist]] Dr. Léo Sémeneau and his wife, Coré, whom Shane once knew and was obsessed with. Despite having had a prolific career, Léo is now working as a [[general practitioner]] to keep a low profile. He locks Coré in their house every day, but she occasionally escapes and initiates sex with men before violently murdering them. To protect her, Léo buries the bodies.

Shane begins investigating Sémeneau's whereabouts. A doctor who once worked with Léo eventually gives Shane the couple's address, explaining that Coré is unwell. Meanwhile, two young men who have been casing the Sémeneau home break in, and one of them finds Coré in a boarded-up room. After she seduces him, they begin to have sex, but she violently bites him to death, ripping out his tongue with her teeth. When Shane arrives at the house, he discovers Coré covered in blood. She tries to bite him, but Shane is able to overpower her. As he strangles her, she drops a match, setting the house on fire. Shane leaves her to be consumed by the flames. Just after Shane departs, Léo arrives and witnesses the carnage and the dead Coré.

After Coré's death, Shane becomes strange and distant. While having sex with his wife, he stops and finishes by masturbating, then runs away from her and adopts a puppy. Finally, he returns to the hotel where he is honeymooning while his wife is away. He then proceeds to brutally rape a maid he has been observing throughout the film and bites her to death, then showers and washes the blood from his body. His wife enters and the couple agree to return home.

== Production ==

=== Development ===
The film's genesis dates back to 1989, when Claire Denis, on a short film in New York, met [[Vincent Gallo]]. At the request of a television channel, she considered a project for a horror genre film set at night in the city, starring Gallo. The project never came to fruition, but the idea of a [[Splatter film|gore film]] persisted in her mind. While filming ''[[Beau Travail]]'', she resumed work on the screenplay with [[Jean-Pol Fargeau]] and developed the writing for ''Trouble Every Day'', planning from the outset to entrust the lead roles to Vincent Gallo, thus realizing her ten-year-old idea, and Béatrice Dalle.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=July 3, 2001 |title=Claire Denis - Leçons de ténèbre |url=http://www.lesinrocks.com/cine/cinema-article/article/claire-denis-lecons-de-tenebre/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.lesinrocks.com%2Fcine%2Fcinema-article%2Farticle%2Fclaire-denis-lecons-de-tenebre%2F |archive-date=July 3, 2001 |website=Les Inrockuptibles}}</ref> Among the central elements that Denis put in the film were the descriptions of romantic relationships through the prism of predation,<ref>{{Cite web |date=August 6, 2001 |title=Human Predations |url=http://www.liberation.fr/tribune/0101382994-des-predations-humaines |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.liberation.fr%2Ftribune%2F0101382994-des-predations-humaines |archive-date=August 6, 2001 |website=Libération |language=fr}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=July 11, 2001 |title=Quit or "Trouble" |url=http://www.liberation.fr/culture/0101380588-quitte-ou-trouble |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.liberation.fr%2Fculture%2F0101380588-quitte-ou-trouble |archive-date=July 11, 2001 |website=Libération}}</ref> sexual desire, and the devouring part of the other touching on symbolic [[Vampire|vampirism]], as described in 19th century novels,<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=July 11, 2001 |title=How to always go further? |url=http://www.liberation.fr/culture/0101380570-comment-aller-toujours-plus-loin |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.liberation.fr%2Fculture%2F0101380570-comment-aller-toujours-plus-loin |archive-date=July 11, 2001 |access-date= |website=Libération}}</ref> the mysterious illnesses that strike certain people and the search for remedies (Denis evoked research on vaccines, medicinal plants, neurobiology and the study of behaviors, as well as certain works of [[Jean-Pierre Changeux]], whom she met), and the references to children's tales (stories of ogres and princesses).<ref name=":0" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=July 14, 2001 |title=Trouble Every Day |url=http://www.telerama.fr/cinema/12471-trouble_every_day.php |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.wikiwix.com/cache/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.telerama.fr%2Fcinema%2F12471-trouble_every_day.php |archive-date=July 14, 2001 |website=Télérama}}</ref><ref name=":1" />

From the first version of the script, consisting of a long text before its re-division into classic sections, Denis sought to create a permanent feeling of danger and anxiety.<ref name=":1" /> To do this, she drew inspiration from the works of Canadian photographer Jeff Wall, whose books she gave to [[Agnès Godard]], her director of photography, in order to create this sought-after atmosphere.

==Reception==
The film received mixed reviews from critics and generated controversy upon its screening at Cannes, with many booing and walking out, as well as two women in the audience fainting due to the film's violence, requiring an ambulance to be called.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Press |first=Liverpool University |date=2021-08-06 |title=Twenty years of Trouble Every Day (2001) |url=https://liverpooluniversitypress.blog/2021/08/06/twenty-years-of-trouble-every-day-2001/ |access-date=2025-02-04 |website=Liverpool University Press Blog |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Charlie Brownlee- Trouble Every Day (2001) Review: Claire Denis' Horror Romance is a Gory, Polarizing Portrait of Carnal Love and Desire {{!}} Aspect: Journal of Film and Screen Media |url=https://aspectfilmjournal.web.unc.edu/2023/01/charlie-brownlee-trouble-every-day/ |access-date=2025-02-04 |website=aspectfilmjournal.web.unc.edu}}</ref> On [[Rotten Tomatoes]] the film has a "rotten" approval rating of 58%, with an average rating of 6.4/10 based on 64 reviews. The site's consensus states: "An erotic thriller dulled by a messy narrative."<ref>{{cite web | url= https://rottentomatoes.com/m/trouble_every_day | title=Trouble Every Day | work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] | accessdate=10 July 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] the film has a weighted average score of 40 out of 100 based on reviews from 16 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web | url= https://metacritic.com/movie/trouble-every-day | title=Trouble Every Day | work= [[Metacritic]] | accessdate= 7 February 2015 }}</ref>

Derek Elley of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' described the film as "over-long, under-written and needlessly obscure instead of genuinely atmospheric."<ref>{{cite news |author=Elley |first=Derek |date=14 May 2001 |title=Trouble Every Day |url=https://variety.com/review/VE1117798064 |accessdate=15 February 2009 |work=Variety}}</ref> Chris Fujiwara of ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' was more positive, but concluded his review by calling the film "a success in some sense, but it's hard to like a film so cold and dead."<ref>{{cite news |author=Fujiwara |first=Chris |date=26 April 2002 |title=Out for blood? Art meets gore in 'Trouble Every Day' |url=http://www.boston.com/movies/display?display=movie&id=1332 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121023024914/http://www.boston.com/ae |url-status=dead |archive-date=23 October 2012 |accessdate=15 February 2009 |work=[[The Boston Globe]]}}</ref>

Later, the film developed a small cult following, who admire it for its themes of [[existentialism]] and its unique take on the [[Horror film|horror]] [[genre]] as well as gender roles. It was given an in depth analysis by ''[[Salon.com|Salon]]'' which looked at the intricacies of the film, particularly the metaphorical nature of the narrative.{{Citation needed|date=February 2025}} At Film Freak Central, Walter Chaw said, "Plaintive and sad, Claire Denis' ''Trouble Every Day'' is a rare combination of honesty, beauty, and maybe even genius."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Chaw |first=Walter |date=20 July 2022 |title=Trouble Every Day |url=http://www.filmfreakcentral.net/screenreviews/troubleeveryday.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070323204452/http://www.filmfreakcentral.net/screenreviews/troubleeveryday.htm |archive-date=2007-03-23 |access-date=2026-02-12 |website=Film Freak Central}}</ref> The film has been associated with the [[New French Extremity]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=ArtForum: Flesh & blood: sex and violence in recent French cinema |url=http://www.findarticles.com/p/articles/mi_m0268/is_6_42/ai_113389507 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040810004736/http://www.findarticles.com/p/articles/mi_m0268/is_6_42/ai_113389507 |archive-date=2004-08-10 |access-date=2026-08-24 |website=www.findarticles.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Anderson |first=Melissa |date=2020-07-10 |title=Blood Work: A Reevaluation of Trouble Every Day -
