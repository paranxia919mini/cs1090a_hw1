{{Nihongo foot|'''''The End of Evangelion'''''|新世紀エヴァンゲリオン劇場版 Air/まごころを、君に|Shin Seiki Evangerion Gekijō-ban Eā/Magokoro wo, Kimi ni|{{literal translation|New Century Evangelion Theatrical Edition: Air/Sincerely Yours}}|lead=yes|group=lower-alpha}} is a 1997 Japanese animated [[apocalyptic science fiction]] film directed by [[Hideaki Anno]] and [[Kazuya Tsurumaki]], written by Anno, and animated by [[Gainax]] and [[Production I.G]]. It serves as an ending to the anime television series ''[[Neon Genesis Evangelion]]'', which aired from 1995 to 1996. Due to time constraints, particularly Anno's indecision over how to conclude the series and the disastrous production schedule, the staff had little time to produce the final episodes of the original series. For ''The End of Evangelion'', Gainax revisited ideas that it had previously discarded due to lack of time, particularly for the [[Do You Love Me? (Neon Genesis Evangelion)|twenty-fifth episode]].

Taking place after [[The Beginning and the End, or "Knockin' on Heaven's Door"|the twenty-fourth episode of the series]], the film, formatted as two episodes, follows the paramilitary organization Nerv as they are attacked by the secret organization Seele, who seek to initiate a process named the Human Instrumentality Project on their own terms, while Nerv operations director [[Misato Katsuragi]] searches for [[Shinji Ikari]] to pilot his mecha [[Evangelion (mecha)|Evangelion]]. The series' voice actors reprised their roles, including [[Megumi Ogata]] as Shinji, [[Yūko Miyamura]] as [[Asuka Langley Soryu|Asuka]], and [[Megumi Hayashibara]] as [[Rei Ayanami|Rei]].

The film has been described by Anno and in official materials as both a remake of the last two episodes<ref name=DRNote/> and an alternative ending<ref name=TwoEndings/><ref name=RCBCommentary/><ref name=LD13/> to the original series, like a multi-ending video game.<ref name=BoogieNight>{{Cite AV media|people=[[Megumi Hayashibara|Hayashibara, Megumi]]; [[Hideaki Anno|Anno, Hideaki]]|date=April 14, 1996|title= 『林原めぐみの東京ヴギーナイト』 |trans-title=Megumi Hayashibara's Tokyo Boogie Night |language=ja|publisher=TBS Radio|medium=Radio broadcast|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260603124322/https://www14.big.or.jp/~ayase/info/evainfo1.html|access-date=August 7, 2026}}</ref> Shortly before the release, Anno and Gainax released another film, ''[[Neon Genesis Evangelion: Death & Rebirth]]''; the first segment, ''Death,'' summarizes the first twenty-four episodes of the series. The second segment, ''Rebirth'', previews ''The End of Evangelion''. In 1998, Gainax combined the overlapping films into ''Revival of Evangelion''. ''The End of Evangelion'' was a box-office success, grossing {{¥|2.47 billion}}. The film was honored at the [[Awards of the Japanese Academy]] and the [[Animation Kobe]], and won the 1997 ''[[Animage]]'' [[Anime Grand Prix]]. Critics praised its direction, editing, emotional power, and script, though some criticized its oblique religious symbolism and abstraction. Reviewers and publications have since ranked it among the [[list of films considered the best|greatest animated films of all time.]]<ref name="Hannah Collins">{{cite web |url=https://www.cbr.com/neon-genesis-evangelion-viewing-reading-order/ |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Best Viewing (and Reading) Order |author-first1=Hannah |author-last1=Collins |date=November 30, 2018|access-date=May 19, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233752/https://www.cbr.com/neon-genesis-evangelion-viewing-reading-order/ |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="timeoutny">{{cite news |date=April 16, 2014 |title=The 100 best animated movies: 70–61 |url=http://www.timeout.com/newyork/film/the-100-best-animated-movies-70-61 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233807/https://www.timeout.com/newyork/film/the-100-best-animated-movies |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |access-date=July 6, 2014 |newspaper=Time Out New York}}</ref><ref name="Collin-2025">{{Cite web |last=Collin |first=Robbie |date=2025-08-25 |title=The 50 best animated films of all time, ranked |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/0/best-animated-films/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250829004849/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/films/0/best-animated-films/ |archive-date=2025-08-29 |access-date=2025-08-31 |website=[[The Daily Telegraph]]}}</ref>

==Plot==

=== Episode 25': "Air" / "Love is destructive." ===
<!-- NOTE: The plot summary should be between 400-700 words. Please see WP:FILMPLOT or WP:PLOTSUM for more information. -->
Fourteen-year-old [[Shinji Ikari]] is the pilot of [[Evangelion (mecha)#Evangelion Unit-01|Evangelion Unit-01]], one of several giant cyborgs designed to fight hostile supernatural entities called [[Angels in Neon Genesis Evangelion|Angels]]. Shinji is distraught after having to kill his friend, [[Kaworu Nagisa]], who revealed himself as an Angel in human form. He visits his fellow pilot [[Asuka Langley Soryu]] in a hospital where she lies in a coma. Trying to shake her awake, he accidentally exposes her breasts and masturbates over her comatose body.

Nerv, the [[paramilitary]] organization that controls the Evangelions, is formally controlled by the secret committee Seele. Seele plans to initiate the Third Impact and the Human Instrumentality, an apocalyptic process to merge humanity into a [[group mind (science fiction)|single consciousness]]. Seele discovers that Shinji's father and commander of Nerv, [[Gendo Ikari]], intends to create his version of the Human Instrumentality Project to reunite with his deceased wife, [[Yui Ikari|Yui]], whose soul resides in Unit-01. After a failed attempt to hack Nerv, Seele dispatches the [[Japan Self-Defense Forces|Japan Strategic Self-Defense Forces]] to seize control of Nerv and execute its staff. Nerv Major [[Misato Katsuragi]] orders Asuka to be moved to [[Evangelion (mecha)#Evangelion Unit-02|Evangelion Unit-02]] and placed at the bottom of a lake. Misato wants Shinji, who is wallowing in self-hatred, to defend Nerv and rescues him from the invading troops, who mortally shoot her in the process. Before her death, Misato implores Shinji to pilot Unit-01 and kisses him. Shinji discovers Unit-01 has been immobilized in special [[bakelite]]. Nerv's head scientist [[Ritsuko Akagi]] attempts to initiate Nerv's headquarters self-destruction as an act of petty revenge against Gendo, but the Magi supercomputer Casper rejects her command, at which point Gendo kills her.

Gendo meets the Evangelion pilot [[Rei Ayanami]], who carries the soul of the Angel [[Angels in Neon Genesis Evangelion#Lilith|Lilith]]. Gendo possesses the body of the Angel [[Angels in Neon Genesis Evangelion#Adam|Adam]] in his hand, intending to combine it with Lilith to begin his own version of Third Impact. Meanwhile, Asuka overcomes her trauma upon learning that the soul of her deceased mother is inside Unit-02. She reactivates Unit-02 and destroys the military forces, including Seele's new [[Evangelion (mecha)#Mass Production Units (05-13)|mass-produced Evangelion units]]. However, the units reanimate after Unit-02's battery runs out and proceed to disembowel it. Unit-01 then breaks free and, with Shinji in the cockpit, ascends above Nerv Headquarters. Shinji sees Seele's units carrying the mutilated remains of Unit-02 and screams.

=== Episode 26': "Sincerely Yours" / One More Final: "I need you." ===
Rei betrays Gendo and takes Adam for herself. She merges with Lilith, who changes into a gigantic version of Rei. The Spear of Longinus extracts itself from the Moon, giving Seele the means to initiate the Third Impact via their scenario. The mass-produced units pull Unit-01 into the sky and crucify it, beginning the ritual to trigger the Third Impact. Lilith makes contact with Shinji. After several dreamlike contemplations, including strangling Asuka after she rejects his pleas for help, Shinji concludes he is alone and everyone in the world, including himself, should die. Lilith responds by initiating the Third Impact and dissolving human bodies into the [[primordial ooze|primordial fluid]] LCL, reforming their souls into a single consciousness.

In this new state, Shinji contemplates reality, individuality, and happiness. Despite his previous feelings, he eventually decides life is about experiencing pain as well as joy, leading him to reject the single consciousness with a hopeful vision involving Rei and Kaworu. His rejection causes the destruction of Lilith and the mass-produced units, setting the souls of humanity free. Yui's soul tells Shinji anyone can return to their physical body if they have the will, and they bid farewell, with Yui leaving Earth in Unit-01's body. Some time later, Shinji and Asuka lie on a beach near some tombstones, facing a sea stained red. Shinji catches a glimpse of an ethereal Rei and begins to strangle Asuka, but he stops when she caresses his face. Shinji breaks down in tears, while Asuka voices disgust.

After the first broadcast of the animated series ''[[Neon Genesis Evangelion]]'', its ending attracted considerable media attention in Japan.<ref name=Mainichi>{{cite news |first1=Kei |last1=Watanabe |first2=Daichi |last2=Nakagawa |first3=Tsunehiro |last3=Uno |title=Evangelion Special: From phenomenon to legacy |newspaper=Mainichi Times |url=http://www.accessmylibrary.com/article-1G1-145511765/evangelion-special-phenomenon-legacy.html |date=May 18, 2006 |access-date=September 8, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130927065311/http://www.accessmylibrary.com/article-1G1-145511765/evangelion-special-phenomenon-legacy.html |archive-date=September 27, 2013 |url-status=dead}}</ref> With the broadcast of the first episode, "[[Angel Attack]]", animation studio [[Gainax]] had only roughly completed twelve of the planned twenty-six episodes after nearly two years of production, forcing the studio to produce the remaining fourteen in about a third of that time.{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=94-96}} The increasingly demanding schedule caused the production system to collapse and animation quality to decline.<ref name="Tsurumaki"/> Gainax tried to achieve the greatest possible effect with the least possible effort{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|p=67}}<ref name=Omori>{{cite web|quote=庵野: いや、拾九話でみんなで作画して.|language=ja|access-date=15 June 2020|url=http://homepage3.nifty.com/mana/siryou4-4.htm|title=SFマガジン 1996年8月号 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161026185613/http://homepage3.nifty.com/mana/siryou4-4.htm|url-status=dead|archive-date=26 October 2016}}</ref> in the limited time remaining. [[Masayuki (animator)|Masayuki]] and [[Yoshiyuki Sadamoto]], for example, produced the [[The Beginning and the End, or "Knockin' on Heaven's Door"|twenty-fourth episode]] in three weeks amid a state of panic,{{sfn|June|pp=31-32}}<ref>{{cite book|title=ALL ABOUT 渚カヲル A CHILD OF THE EVANGELION|date=2008|publisher=Kadokawa Group Publishing|language=ja|p=149}}</ref> while the staff completed the final two episodes within a matter of days.<ref name=NewtypeJune96>{{cite magazine |title=EVA，再學 |magazine=Newtype |publisher=Kadokawa Shoten |date=June 1996|pages=10-15 |language=ja}}</ref> According to official information and staff statements, the time constraints and the series' director [[Hideaki Anno]]'s personal indecision<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.j-pop.com/anime/archive/feature/04_gal_999/otaking10.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20000126012803/http://www.j-pop.com/anime/archive/feature/04_gal_999/otaking10.html|archive-date=January 26, 2000|title=Return of the Otaking|publisher=J-pop.com|access-date=September 7, 2013}}</ref> led to the abandonment of the original script of the penultimate episode, "[[Do You Love Me? Gainax originally planned to release the separate feature film in mid-1997. Production of ''Rebirth'', however, fell behind schedule; the staff produced so much material that Gainax had to split it into two releases.{{sfn|Yoshihara|1998|pp=121-125}}<ref>{{cite web |url=http://j-pop.com/anime/archive/reviews/11_comp_graphics/picks0.html |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Death and Rebirth |author-first1=Carl Gustav |author-last1=Horn|access-date=August 14, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010219070024/http://j-pop.com/anime/archive/reviews/11_comp_graphics/picks0.html |archive-date=February 19, 2001 }}</ref> In addition, some staff members struggled with the demanding production schedule, prompting Gainax to postpone the new film indefinitely.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://johakyu.net/lib/2007/11/2007-11-13-000608.php |title=SFまんが道 第9回「貞本義行」篇: 序破急 |language=ja |access-date=May 6, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211217022900/http://johakyu.net/lib/2007/11/2007-11-13-000608.php |archive-date=December 17, 2021 }}</ref> In March 1997, Gainax released ''Death and Rebirth''. The film consists of two segments, running sixty and twenty-five minutes respectively. The first segment, {{nihongo|''Shi''|シ|}} or "Death", presents a montage of clips from the first twenty-four episodes of the series alongside previously unreleased sequences.<ref name=DRNote>{{cite book |date=1997 |title=Death & Rebirth Program Book |language=ja |chapter=Rebirth Production Note |publisher=Gainax|edition=Special }}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=50 |page=16 |publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref> The last segment, {{nihongo|''Shinsei''|新生}} ("Rebirth"), previews the new ending. It includes the first twenty minutes of the following installment and ends at the beginning of the confrontation between Eva-02 and the Eva Series. Gainax then announced a second concluding feature film. According to Carl Gustav Horn, editor of the English edition of the [[Neon Genesis Evangelion (manga)|''Evangelion'' manga]], Anno reportedly stated that the second film would present the same ending of the show from a different perspective.<ref name=EighthDay/><ref>{{cite book |title=The Complete Anime Guide: Japanese Animation Film Directory & Resource Guide|author-first1=Trish |author-last1=Ledoux|author2=Doug Ranney|author3= Fred Patten|location=Panama City|publisher=Tiger Mountain Press|page=129|year=1997|isbn=0-9649542-5-7}}</ref> The film has been described by official materials as both a remake<ref name=DRNote/> and an alternative ending<ref name=TwoEndings/><ref name=RCBCommentary/> to the original series finale. The series' official booklets also noted that the two are different endings that share the same themes and basic framework, comparing them to identical twins, "different people with the same genes".<ref name=LD13>{{cite book|language=ja|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Laserdisc Encyclopedia|volume=13|date=1998}}</ref> Hideaki Anno himself compared the two endings to a multi-ending video game.<ref name=BoogieNight/>

===Direction and development===
[[File:Godzilla Resurgence World Premiere Red Carpet- Higuchi Shinji (27989045433).jpg|thumb|upright=1.2|[[Shinji Higuchi]] collaborated with Anno on the storyboards of "Sincerely Yours".]]

After the series concluded, Anno entered a difficult psychological state, and his friend and anime director [[Hayao Miyazaki]] advised him to take a break.{{sfn|Takekuma|1997|p=179}} Anno followed his advice and rested for several months. Gainax officially began production of the film version of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' on November 8, 1996.{{sfn|Yoshihara|1998|p=100}} According to ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' producer Toshimichi Ōtsuki, the staff had started working on ''The End of Evangelion'' before the series finished airing and continued until the last minute to complete the second feature film on time.<ref>From the interview with Toshimichi Ōtsuki in {{cite news |author-first1=Kei |author-last1=Watanabe |date=May 6, 2006 |newspaper=[[Mainichi Shinbun]]|title=Evangelion Special: For producer Otsuki, success not always a bed of roses}}</ref> Unlike ''Death and Rebirth'', which received an extensive media campaign, ''The End of Evangelion'' received no special advertising campaign, and promotional activities remained minimal. The creators intended the film as a funeral with which to "bury" ''Neon Genesis Evangelion''.{{sfn|Yoshihara|1998|p=131}} Anno titled the film ''The End of Evangelion'' to represent the metaphorical death of the ''Evangelion'' project; he sought to bring the project to an end with his own hands rather than watch its popularity slowly fade away.<ref>{{cite book |title=マジック・ランチャー 魔法発射装置 |page=106|language=ja|date=June 1998|isbn=4-925140-00-0|author1=庵野秀明|author2=岩井俊二|publisher=デジタルハリウッド株式会社出版局 }}</ref>

Gainax initially proposed the title ''Evangelion: Rebirth 2'',<ref>{{cite book|title=[[Animage]]|publisher=[[Tokuma Shoten]]|date=April 1997|page=9|language=ja}}</ref> but changed it to ''The End of Evangelion'' during production.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ex.org/2.4/11-news.html |title=New briefs|access-date=April 21, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/19981202185145/http://www.ex.org/2.4/11-news.html |archive-date=December 2, 1998 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ex.org/2.3/10-news.html |title=New briefs|access-date=April 21, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070608092615/http://www.ex.org/2.3/10-news.html|archive-date=June 8, 2007 }}</ref> The filmmakers divided the feature into two segments, "Episode 25'" and "Episode 26'".{{sfn|Tavassi|2012|pp=274-275}} Like the television episodes, each segment carries two titles, one in Japanese and one in English. The staff initially titled the first segment {{nihongo|"The Door to Summer"|夏への扉|Natsu e no tobira}}, a reference to [[Robert A. Heinlein]]'s 1957 science fiction novel [[The Door into Summer|of the same name]].<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|page=504|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref><ref name="Tomo">{{cite book |date=1998 |title=エヴァ友の会 |volume=14 |language=ja}}</ref> The staff later opted for "Love is Destructive" and "Air", the latter carrying the double meaning of "atmosphere" and music.<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=3 |page=23|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref> The staff titled the second episode ''Magokoro wo, kimi ni'' ("Sincerely Yours"). Following a Gainax tradition of naming the final episode of a series after an existing story or feature film, "Sincerely Yours" pays tribute to the 1968 film ''[[Charly (1968 film)|Charly]]'', based on the novel ''[[Flowers for Algernon]]'' by [[Daniel Keyes]], which was distributed in Japan with the same title.<ref name=Tomo/><ref>{{cite book |title=Little boy: the arts of Japan's exploding subculture|url=https://archive.org/details/littleboyartsofj0000unse|author-link=Takashi Murakami |author-first1=Takashi|author-last1=Murakami|page=[https://archive.org/details/littleboyartsofj0000unse/page/129 129]|year=2005|publisher=Japan Society |isbn=0-300-10285-2}}</ref> The home-video editions of the series also included previews of "Air" and "Sincerely Yours" at the end of the corresponding original final episodes.<ref name="Platinum">Extra materials from the Platinum Edition DVDs.</ref> [[Kazuya Tsurumaki]], who had served as assistant director on the television series, directed "Air". Other Gainax staff members, including Masayuki and [[Shinji Higuchi]], contributed to the production and collaborated on the storyboards under Tsurumaki's direction.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=8|date=2009}}</ref> Anno directed "Sincerely Yours", while Higuchi and [[Jun'ichi Satō]] assisted him with the storyboards.<ref name=Production/><ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=13|date=2009}}</ref>

===Writing===
[[File:Yuko Miyamura by Gage Skidmore.jpg|thumb|left|upright=0.8|[[Yūko Miyamura]], [[Asuka Langley Soryu]]'s voice actress. Her performance influenced the writing of the final line.]]

Due to time constraints, the staff did not use the original script for the twenty-fifth episode of the series.<ref name=NewtypeJune96/> When Gainax decided to resume ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'', Anno based the script for "Air" on this unused material.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=10|date=2009}}</ref><ref name="Style2">{{cite web |url=http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama61.shtml |title=第61回 エヴァ雑記「第25話 Air」 |language=ja |access-date=May 7, 2017 |archive-date=June 5, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160605070323/http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama61.shtml |url-status=live }}</ref> The staff structured the episode to remain as close as possible to the ending they had originally intended.<ref>{{cite book |date=1998 |title=エヴァ友の会 |volume=13 |language=ja}}</ref> For the second episode, "Sincerely Yours", Anno added several narrative elements to the original script, further developing themes already introduced in the final episode of the series.<ref name="Production">{{cite book|year=1997|title=The End of Evangelion Theatrical Pamphlet|publisher=[[Gainax]]|language=ja|chapter=Production Note}}</ref> Unlike the television conclusion, which focuses on the protagonists' psychology, the film addresses the Human Instrumentality Project from an external perspective, while the series presents it through the main characters' introspection.{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=82}} Official materials stated that the story of ''Evangelion'' branches into two endings after the twenty-fourth episode<ref name=RCBCommentary/> and "the two stories each unfold differently and arrive at their own climaxes".<ref name=TwoEndings/> The staff's exhaustion influenced the tone of the script. Shinji's characterization had also changed since the twenty-fifth episode of the series, creating inconsistencies. The absence of the tension that had characterized the production of the original series also created difficulties for the staff; Kazuya Tsurumaki worked on the film while considering it unnecessary until the end.<ref name="Tsurumaki">{{cite book|year=1997|chapter=Kazuya Tsurumaki Interview|title=The End of Evangelion Theatrical Pamphlet|publisher=[[Gainax]]|language=ja}}</ref>

During production of the series and ''Death and Rebirth'', Anno asked anyone who worked on ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' to suggest possible ways to conclude the story; he relied particularly on Higuchi, Sadamoto, and [[Ikuto Yamashita]], the series' mecha designer.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.evaitalianfan.it/blog/2016/12/31/luomo-salvo-gainax/ |title=L'uomo che salvò Gainax |access-date=June 26, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170101015003/https://www.evaitalianfan.it/blog/2016/12/31/luomo-salvo-gainax/ |archive-date=January 1, 2017 |language=it}}</ref> One of Yamashita's proposals for the unrealized feature film<ref name=EC/> involved Eva-01 attacking the headquarters of Nerv's German branch, which possessed a weapon called the "Long Range Universal Invasion Cannon", or "Dead God Spine". In Yamashita's scenario, Evangelions would wage war in a world affected by mysterious rays of light capable of transforming people into [[werewolf]]-like creatures.<ref>{{cite book |date=1998 |first1= Kio |last1=Seiji |first2=Ikuto |last2= Yamashita |title=Sore Wo Nasumono: Neon Genesis Evangelion Concept Design Works |publisher=Kadokawa Shoten |language=ja |isbn=4-04-852908-0 |pages=74–75}}</ref> Wolfmen would surround Nerv's base, a sterilized stronghold; in a flashback, Shinji would transform into his Eva after exchanging bodies with it. The Evangelion pilots would also play a role in politics, while Nerv would regard Asuka with suspicion and Germany would consider her a traitor.<ref>{{cite book |date=1998 |first1= Kio |last1=Seiji |first2=Ikuto |last2= Yamashita |title=Sore Wo Nasumono: Neon Genesis Evangelion Concept Design Works |publisher=Kadokawa Shoten |language=ja |isbn=4-04-852908-0 |pages=76–77}}</ref> In one scene from Yamashita's script, Shinji would run alongside the werewolves, who acclaimed him as their king,<ref>{{cite book |date=1998 |first1= Kio |last1=Seiji |first2=Ikuto |last2= Yamashita |title=Sore Wo Nasumono: Neon Genesis Evangelion Concept Design Works |publisher=Kadokawa Shoten |language=ja |isbn=4-04-852908-0 |pages=78–79}}</ref> while Rei would kill a werewolf in cold blood and declare that she hated animals.<ref>{{cite book |date=1998 |first1= Kio |last1=Seiji |first2=Ikuto |last2= Yamashita |title=Sore Wo Nasumono: Neon Genesis Evangelion Concept Design Works |publisher=Kadokawa Shoten |language=ja |isbn=4-04-852908-0 |page=82}}</ref>

Gainax originally planned a giant battle as the show's ending{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=94-96}} with a final confrontation with the series' last twelve enemies, then called the ''Apostolos'', whose concept drew inspiration from the [[twelve Apostles]] of the [[Lamb of God]] in the [[Book of Revelation]].<ref name=ECv20p26>{{cite book|title=Evangelion Chronicle|volume=20|page=26|publisher=Sony Magazines|language=ja}}</ref> Gainax chose the Moon as the setting for the battle because of its symbolic association with Rei and the series' ending theme, "[[Fly Me to the Moon]]". The staff also planned to show a message written across the Moon's surface in the final scenes, in a sequence reminiscent of the ending of the [[OVA]] series ''[[Gunbuster]]''.{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=167-168}} Gainax ultimately abandoned most of these ideas during production; others resurfaced in the broadcast version, including the twelve ''Apostolos'', whose role informed the battle between Eva-02 and the Mass Production Models.<ref name=ECv20p26/> Additional changes occurred at the script stage; in one of the opening sequences, for example, Shinji would say goodbye to his two former classmates and friends, [[Kensuke Aida]] and [[Toji Suzuhara]], at their middle school in Tokyo-3, shortly before their sudden departure for Germany.<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|pages=506–509|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref> Echoing the final scene of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion''{{'s}} [[Fourth Children|seventeenth episode]], Toji would shoot a basketball while using a wheelchair.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama50.shtml |title=第50回 エヴァ雑記「第拾七話 四人目の適格者」 |language=ja |access-date=May 15, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233615/http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama50.shtml |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> In "Sincerely Yours", Lilith and the nine Eva Series would form a triangle-shaped pattern,<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|page=736|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref> which the final version replaced with a horizontal Tree of Life. Furthermore, Anno based the final sequence, in which Shinji strangles Asuka until she caresses his cheek, on an incident involving a female acquaintance of his. A man once strangled the woman, but as he was about to kill her, she caressed him. When he loosened his grip on her neck, she regained her cold demeanor and uttered the words that Asuka would have said to Shinji in the original script, {{nihongo|"I can't stand the idea of being killed by someone like you"|あんたなんかに殺されるのは真っ平よ|}}.<ref>{{cite book |title=井手功二のエヴァンゲリオンフォーエヴァー|chapter=VOICE OF EVANGELION |language=ja|date=September 1997|isbn=4-906613-24-1|author1=井手功二|publisher=アミューズブックス|p=80}}</ref>

===Voice cast===
For ''The End of Evangelion'', all the voice actors from the original animated series reprised their roles, except those whose characters the staff removed from the script during the writing process, such as Toji Suzuhara and Kensuke Aida.<ref>{{cite journal|journal=Animedia|publisher=Gakken|language=ja|date=October 1997|title=EVA新生!!}}</ref> After the final recording session, Anno asked the cast to record the film again from scratch, providing detailed instructions as they re-recorded almost every line.<ref name="Seiyuu">{{cite book|year=1997|title=The End of Evangelion Theatrical Pamphlet|chapter=声ノ出演|publisher=[[Gainax]]|language=ja}}</ref> Anno instructed the actors to perform as though they were acting in a live-action film, avoiding conventional anime voice-acting techniques. When he wanted to focus on the dialogue and emphasize the voice actors' performances, he framed the characters from behind, kept them off-screen, or used long shots, preventing facial expressions and lip movements from overly constraining the performances.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|pages=27–28|date=2009}}</ref> The cast also re-recorded the scenes from "Air" that had already appeared in "Rebirth".<ref>{{cite book|title=Newtype Complete 新世紀エヴァンゲリオン|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten|year=2005|language=ja|pages=100–101}}</ref>

As with the television series, Ogata felt comfortable with her role but found the screaming required during the recording sessions difficult.<ref name=Seiyuu/> While recording the film's final scene, in which Shinji strangles Asuka, Ogata physically strangled Asuka's voice actress, [[Yūko Miyamura]]. Miyamura struggled to produce realistic strangulation sounds and personally asked her colleague for help.<ref>{{cite interview |url=http://www.ravemagazine.com.au/content/view/23801/192 |title=Gold Coast Film Festival - Yuko Miyamura Interview |date=November 9, 2012 |access-date=October 11, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121119070329/http://www.ravemagazine.com.au/content/view/23801/192 |archive-date=November 19, 2012 |website=Rave Magazine|interviewer=Colin Fong|subject-last1=Miyamura|subject-first1=Yūko}}</ref> In her agitation, Ogata gripped Miyamura's neck too tightly, risking damage to her voice and nearly leaving her unable to deliver her remaining lines.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.akadot.com/story.php?id=31 |title=Rocking the Boat |date=April 27, 2001|access-date=October 11, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080623173408/http://www.akadot.com/story.php?id=31 |archive-date=June 23, 2008 }}</ref> Although the scene lasts only a few minutes, the actresses spent about ninety minutes recording it.<ref name="Usagi">{{cite web |url=http://usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970611.html |title=ゲルゲトショッキングセンター - 1997.06.11|access-date=April 24, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110804074603/http://usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970611.html |archive-date=August 4, 2011 }}</ref>
* Yūko Miyamura as [[Asuka Langley Soryu]]. In the original script, Asuka, immediately after Shinji strangles her, would deliver the final line, "I can't stand the idea of being killed by someone like you", in the coldest possible tone.<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|page=841|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=15|date=2009}}</ref> Dissatisfied with both the line and Miyamura's performance, Anno asked her to imagine a stranger capable of raping her at any time who instead masturbated while watching her sleep after sneaking into her room. He then asked what she would say if she woke up and realized what had happened. Disgusted by the scenario, Miyamura simply replied, {{nihongo|"How disgusting"|気持ち悪い|kimochi warui}}. Anno subsequently adopted the phrase as Asuka's final line.<ref>{{cite video |title=BSアニメ夜話 |date=March 28, 2005 |publisher=Nippon Hōsō Kyōkai |language=ja}}</ref>
* [[Megumi Hayashibara]] as [[Rei Ayanami]] and [[Yui Ikari]].<ref name=BVA/>
* [[Kotono Mitsuishi]] as Major [[Misato Katsuragi]]. Mitsuishi had difficulties during recordings because Misato experiences difficulty talking about her true feelings.<ref name=Seiyuu/><ref>{{cite journal |date=1997 |journal=Death & Rebirth Program Book |language=ja |title=出演者コメント |publisher=Gainax}}</ref>
* [[Fumihiko Tachiki]] as [[Gendo Ikari]]. Tachiki found it difficult to empathize with or understand the character. During the sessions, he received support from the staff, being directed step by step by Anno and the sound director.<ref name=Seiyuu/> The sessions for Gendo's recording lasted about two days.<ref name=Usagi/>
* [[Yuriko Yamaguchi (voice actress)|Yuriko Yamaguchi]] as [[Ritsuko Akagi]]. In the film, Ritsuko attempts to destroy Nerv headquarters, but her former lover Gendo Ikari shoots her dead. Just before firing, Ikari says, "Ritsuko Akagi, actually&nbsp;...{{sp}}". The film omits the final part of the line, leaving viewers to interpret what he says. Ritsuko replies, "You're a liar"; delivering the line required particular interpretative and acting skills from Yamaguchi. During the recording session, Anno noticed her emotional state and gave her a private clue. Yamaguchi stated, "with that one incredible hint, I, and Ritsuko Akagi, were utterly defeated".<ref name=Seiyuu/> The phrase in the original storyboards was supposed to be covered by the sound of an explosion.<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|page=667|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref>
* [[Akira Ishida]] as [[Kaworu Nagisa]]. Ishida said he found the role "very difficult", and felt "a lot of pressure" during the recording of the film version. His emotional tension grew exponentially when he learned that there would be two feature films, but he was satisfied with his performance.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970610.html|title=ゲルゲトショッキングセンター - 1997.06.10|publisher=Usagi|access-date=February 4, 2021|archive-date=November 22, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191122013842/http://www.usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970610.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Animation ===
Unlike the television series, which used [[16mm]] film, the production team shot the film in [[35 mm movie film|35mm film]].<ref>{{cite book |title=Data of Evangelion|publisher=Gainax|page=9|language=ja|year=2003}}</ref> The staff appointed [[Kazuchika Kise]], a member of [[Production I.G]] and art co-director of the television series, and [[Takeshi Honda (animator)|Takeshi Honda]], who had animated the [[A Cruel Angel's Thesis|opening theme song]] of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'', as animation and mecha design directors. Shunji Suzuki, Teishi Hiramatsu, and Anno served as art directors for the twenty-sixth episode. Suzuki and Hiramatsu directed the scenes, while Anno coordinated the battles between the mecha.<ref name=Production/> Tsurumaki also contributed to the animation, and the staff brought him in at the last minute to assist with the coloring process.<ref name=Tsurumaki/> Masayuki, assistant director of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' and director of "Death", co-produced the animation and worked on the storyboards and promotional posters. The staff also assigned him to supervise sequences involving water or waves, which animators generally considered among the most difficult elements to animate.<ref name=Production/>{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=90}} [[Makoto Kamiya]]<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2007-06-25/oshii-serve-up-female-tachigui-sequel-this-november |title=Oshii Serves Up Female Tachigui Sequel This November |date=June 25, 2007|access-date=May 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233633/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2007-06-25/oshii-serve-up-female-tachigui-sequel-this-november |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> served as special effects director. Hisaki Furukawa and [[Yō Yoshinari]] served as assistant animators, Harumi Takaboshi as colorist, Hiroshi Katō as art director, and Hisao Shirai as director of photography.<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=28 |page=24 |publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref>{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=128}} Takashi Hashimoto also joined the production as a key animator<ref>{{cite web|language=ja|url=http://www.style.fm/as/01_talk/hashimoto_t00_list.shtml|title=【橋本敬史　主要作品リスト】|publisher=Anime Style|access-date=August 8, 2026}}</ref> on the second half of "Sincerely Yours". Masayuki recruited Hashimoto with the promise of particularly spectacular action sequences; according to Hashimoto, however, the staff mainly assigned him mechanical and effects sequences from the final part of the film, including Unit-01's impalement and crucifixion, the Lance of Longinus stopping in front of Unit-01's throat, and several shots near the end of Instrumentality.<ref name="animestyle hashimoto04">{{cite web|url=http://www.style.fm/as/01_talk/hashimoto_t04.shtml|title=animator interview 橋本敬史 (4)『エヴァンゲリオン』と磯光雄ショック |trans-title=animator interview Takashi Hashimoto (4) "Evangelion" and Mitsuo Iso shock|language= ja|date= January 8, 2009|access-date= October 17, 2021|website=WEB Anime Style|publisher=Style}}</ref>

[[File:The End of Evangelion.jpg|thumb|left|upright=1.2|The Human Instrumentality scene, in which Lilith collects the souls of all humanity. Production I.G created the scene using computer graphics.]]

According to writers Víctor Sellés de Lucas and Manuel Hernández-Pérez, "Anno did not abandon most of the stylistic resources employed in the series, but he was more ambitious in the visualization of battles [in ''The End of Evangelion'']".<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Sellés de Lucas|first1=Victor|last2=Hernández-Pérez|first2=Manuel|title=Exegesis and authorial agency through Judeo-Christian iconography in Japanese anime: Neon Genesis Evangelion (1995-97) as an open work|journal=Imafronte|issue=31|pages=227|publisher=Universidad de Murcia|date=2024}}</ref> Gainax involved Production I.G, which had previously collaborated on several episodes of the original series.<ref name=Production/> The staff also sought to improve the quality of the "Air" footage already included in "Death". The filmmakers used [[computer graphics]] (CG) to add depth to the images and surpass the animation quality of the original television series. Omnibus Japan, which had previously worked on ''[[Patlabor 2: The Movie]]'' (1993) and [[Ghost in the Shell (1995 film)|''Ghost in the Shell'']] (1995), made the most significant contribution.<ref name=Production/> Most of the computer-generated sequences appear at the beginning of the Human Instrumentality and Third Impact scenes, in which cruciform lights cover the globe. The companies divided the work according to the technical requirements and stylistic choices of each scene. Production I.G handled scenes involving 3D computer graphics that traditional techniques could hardly achieve, such as the introductory shots of the nine Eva Series units, while Gainax developed scenes requiring 2D computer graphics, such as those depicting computer screens.<ref name=Production/> The sequences depicting the clash between Unit-02 and the Strategic Self-Defense Forces evoke the battle between the Angel [[List of Angels in Neon Genesis Evangelion#Sachiel|Sachiel]] and the United Nations forces in the first episode.{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=88}} Furthermore, the staff incorporated images from the original twenty-fifth episode in the film, including Eva-02 and Asuka at the bottom of a lake and the corpses of Ritsuko and Misato.{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=82}}<ref>{{cite web|language=ja|access-date=April 22, 2021|url=http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama58.shtml|title=第58回 エヴァ雑記「第弐拾伍話 終わる世界」|archive-date=December 11, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211211210731/http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama58.shtml|url-status=live}}</ref> They also inserted brief watercolor sequences during Instrumentality.<ref>{{cite book |title=Data of Evangelion|language=ja|year=2003|editor=Gainax}}</ref>

===Sound===
Director Anno commissioned Toru Noguchi, with whom he had previously worked on the television series ''[[Nadia: The Secret of Blue Water]]'', to create the sound effects. Anno asked Noguchi to pursue maximum realism and convey sensations that [[cel animation]] could not express. Noguchi paid particular attention to the sounds of lasers and AT Fields and to the background noise of cicadas, which he specially modulated to harmonize with the soundtrack. For the movements of the Evangelion units, Anno asked him to recreate the sound that bundles of wires would make when contracting like muscles, treating the units as living beings.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=25|date=2009}}</ref> He therefore avoided sounds commonly used in other mecha anime. Anno gave meticulous instructions, specifically requesting the sound of a [[World War II]] machine gun and the sounds of particular historical ships. For the Prog-Knife, the sound team reworked the metallic noise of a real cutter.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|pages=36–37|date=2009}}</ref> Makoto Sumiya, a recording engineer from Tokyo TV Center, assisted Noguchi in the process.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=40|date=2009}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
[[File:Tokyo Shinjuku Milano za.jpg|thumb|upright=0.8|The Shinjuku Milano-za, a cinema whose hall was filmed during the projection of ''Death & Rebirth'']]

For "Sincerely Yours", the staff decided to include live-action sequences representing Shinji's inner world;<ref>{{cite book |title=Data of Evangelion|publisher=Gainax|page=71|language=ja|year=2003}}</ref> these feature footage of street lamps, trains, graffiti, and the series' three main female voice actresses, Megumi Hayashibara, Kotono Mitsuishi, and Yūko Miyamura. A crew called the "Special Production Team" filmed the sequences and optically distorted the footage.<ref>{{cite book|date=July 2005 |title=AnimaniA |page=10 |language=de}}</ref> Anno wrote the script and worked with special effects director Shinji Higuchi, with whom he discussed ideas and compared opinions on every frame of the sequences.<ref name=Production/> To portray Shinji's world in harsher tones than the television ending, the filmmakers used multiple overlapping or reversed rhodium glasses and three-dimensional computer graphics.<ref name=Live/> One scene depicts a panorama of a city resembling Tokyo-3, which the staff created by integrating CGI elements.<ref name=EC>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=26 |pages=29–30 |publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref> The crew filmed other shots at the {{nihongo|Shinjuku Milano-za cinema|新宿ミラノ座||}}, located in the [[Shinjuku]] district of [[Tokyo]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://kai-you.net/article/10467 |title=新宿ミラノ座より愛を込めて 閉館イベントで『エヴァ』旧劇場版上映 |date=November 14, 2014 |language=ja |access-date=May 8, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180701083349/http://kai-you.net/article/10467 |archive-date=July 1, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.webdice.jp/topics/detail/4476/ |title=新宿ミラノ座最後の上映はエヴァ他全24作 |date=November 14, 2014 |language=ja |access-date=May 10, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233708/http://www.webdice.jp/topics/detail/4476/ |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref>

In the final cut, Gainax also added a shot of a cinema auditorium filled with viewers of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion: Death and Rebirth''.<ref name="Style">{{cite web |url=http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama62.shtml |title=第62回 エヴァ雑記「第26話 まごころを、君に」 |language=ja |access-date=May 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233624/http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama62.shtml |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> At the end of the segment, the staff inserted still images of graffiti on the walls of the Gainax Shop, doodles, and pseudo-emails purportedly written by fans of the series,<ref>From the official Japanese ''Renewal of Evangelion'' booklet.</ref> including the words {{nihongo|"Anno, I'll kill you!!"|庵野、殺す！！|}}.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/the-list/2015-03-21/.86129 |title=7 Things You Might Not Know About Hideaki Anno |author-first1=Lynzee |author-last1=Loveridge |date=March 21, 2015|access-date=May 13, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233627/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/the-list/2015-03-21/.86129 |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> The staff created the letters ''ad hoc'', drawing on emails that Gainax had received. Moreover, most of the fictional letters contain no criticism and instead praise the creators following the release of ''Death and Rebirth''.<ref>A translation of the fake letter can be read [https://evaotaku.tripod.com/mail.htm on EvaOtaku] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190329103612/http://evaotaku.tripod.com/mail.htm |date=March 29, 2019 }}.</ref> According to an official pamphlet for the film, the staff created the fictional emails to simulate hypothetical fan reactions and reflect on the relationships that develop "between a work and its admirers".<ref name=Live>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=14|date=2009}}</ref>

The authors originally planned a longer live-action segment with substantially different content from the final version.<ref name=Style/><ref>Extra contents from the Japanese Renewal Edition.</ref> The original segment centered on Asuka, who would wake up in an apartment after drinking and spending the night with Toji, with whom she would have a sexual relationship. Misato would live in the neighboring apartment, while Rei Ayanami, Asuka's rival in the original series, would work as her colleague and ''[[senpai]]''. In the alternative universe depicted in the live-action footage, Shinji would never have existed; while walking through the streets of Tokyo-2, however, Asuka would hear his voice calling her.<ref>{{cite book |title=Data of Evangelion|publisher=Gainax|pages=84–89|language=ja|year=2003}}</ref> In the live-action segment, director Anno voices Shinji instead of his original voice actress, Megumi Ogata.<ref>{{cite book |date=2021|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Blue Ray Ultimate Edition Encyclopedia|chapter=Special Contents 1995–2015}}</ref> The filmmakers later incorporated some of the unused footage into the film's trailers. During production, Anno decided to cut the segment; according to ''Evangelion Chronicle'' magazine, he made the decision after the production unexpectedly expanded into a two-film theatrical release.<ref name=EC/> The crew began filming on January 19, 1997, and continued for several days. They filmed pylons, bodies of water, and urban scenery representing Tokyo-2 in the snowy city of [[Matsumoto, Nagano|Matsumoto]], [[Nagano Prefecture]]. The crew also filmed in Kōfu and shot the movie-theater scene on March 14 during the premiere of ''Death and Rebirth''. On May 8, they filmed three dummies representing Rei, Asuka, and Misato on a street in a Japanese city. Filming concluded on May 10, when Anno and other crew members shot footage at a playground.<ref>From the official making of from the official Blu-ray edition of ''Evangelion''.</ref>

===Music===
{{See also|Music of Neon Genesis Evangelion#The End of Evangelion{{!}}''The End of Evangelion'' Original Soundtrack}}

Music composer [[Shirō Sagisu]], who had also written [[Music of Neon Genesis Evangelion|the music for the original series]], composed the soundtrack for ''The End of Evangelion''. The film also features music by classical composer [[Johann Sebastian Bach]]. The staff titled the twenty-fifth episode "Air" as a homage to the second movement of Orchestral Suite No. 3, known as "[[Air on the G String]]", which accompanies one of the episode's key scenes. The second half of the film also features the tenth movement of Bach's cantata "[[Herz und Mund und Tat und Leben]]".<ref name=Production/> Critics have compared the use of classical music during violent scenes to [[Stanley Kubrick]]'s works.<ref>{{cite journal|language=es|journal=Komikku|date=2018|url=https://archive.org/details/radicalamericako0000unse|issue=2|title=Bandas sonoras|author-first1=Martín |author-last1=Fernández Cruz|page=[https://archive.org/details/radicalamericako0000unse/page/17 17]|publisher=Freakshow Press}}</ref> In an interview, Anno was asked about similarities with Kubrick, but he claimed that his films did not influence him "that much".<ref>{{cite web|access-date=March 18, 2021|url=http://rickyreports.com/archives/annohideaki/|title=Evangelion Mastermind Anno Confesses Love for Anime|date=October 30, 2014|author-first1=Richard |author-last1=Rowland|archive-date=November 27, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201127075249/http://rickyreports.com/archives/annohideaki/|url-status=live}}</ref> Sagisu arranged and recorded a new version of "Air on the G String" for the film,<ref>{{cite book |date=2021|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Blue Ray Ultimate Edition Encyclopedia|chapter=世界の中心でアイを叫んだけもの}}</ref> while an existing recording of "[[Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring]]" by pianist [[Jan Panenka]] was used. The production commissioned two original songs for the film. The first one, "[[Thanatos: If I Can't Be Yours]]", based on a soundtrack from the original series, features [[gospel music]] singer Loren and artist Martin Lascelles, who performed under the stage name "Mash". Loren recorded the single in London in June 1997 under the supervision of Toshimichi Ōtsuki.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970609.html |title=ゲルゲトショッキングセンター - 1997.06.09|access-date=April 24, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120128172614/http://usagi.org/doi/seiyuu/radio/geruge/19970609.html |archive-date=January 28, 2012 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.anitama.cn/article/11756e7f78a2f22b |title=弹指一挥四十载、老骥伏枥今胜昔 |date=December 6, 2018 |language=zh |access-date=May 17, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190222101758/http://www.anitama.cn/article/11756e7f78a2f22b |archive-date=February 22, 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref> The film later uses the song for the credits between the two segments of ''The End of Evangelion''.<ref name=Production/>

Arianne performed the second song, "[[Komm, süsser Tod (Arianne song)|Komm, süsser Tod]]" ("Come, Sweet Death"), using English lyrics translated from lyrics by Anno. An official booklet about the ''Evangelion'' music noted that ''Mobile Suit Gundam III: Encounters in Space'', the third feature film in the ''[[Mobile Suit Gundam]]'' theatrical saga, had employed a similar device years earlier, with its director writing a song for the concluding film of an animated series.<ref name="Refrain">{{cite document |date=2003 |title=Refrain of Evangelion booklet |publisher=NBC Universal Entertainment Japan |language=ja}}</ref> The song shares its title with Bach's composition "[[Komm, süßer Tod, komm selge Ruh]]", while critics have compared its melody to that of "[[Hey Jude]]" by [[the Beatles]].<ref>{{cite web |author-last1=Botello |author-first1=Gabriella |date=June 25, 2019 |title=La historia detrás de 'Kommer, Süsser Tod', el himno de 'End of Evangelion' |trans-title=The story behind 'Kommer, Süsser Tod', the anthem from 'End of Evangelion' |url=https://www.fandango.lat/noticias/kommer-susser-tod-end-of-evangelion-netflix-39462 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200701232457/https://www.fandango.lat/noticias/kommer-susser-tod-end-of-evangelion-netflix-39462 |archive-date=July 1, 2020 |access-date=July 2, 2020 |language=es}}</ref> Sagisu attempted to rotate the guitar sound of "Komm, süsser Tod" across all surround channels. Anno asked him to compose the piece so that every element, from the harmonic progressions to the instrumental layering, would match the onscreen imagery.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=32|date=2009}}</ref> In "Sincerely Yours", the filmmakers used a Japanese song sung by preschool children in the playground scene. Producer Satsukawa and a special production team visited Haishima Municipal Kindergarten in [[Akishima]], Japan, which Mitsuhisa Ishikawa's daughter attended, to record the song.<ref name=Production/> The production team composed and recorded eighteen songs for ''The End of Evangelion''.<ref name=Production/> The staff initially intended to include a song titled "Everything You've Ever Dreamed" in the Human Instrumentality sequences, but discarded it during production and later included it on the album ''[[Music of Neon Genesis Evangelion#Refrain: The Songs Were Inspired by Evangelion|Refrain of Evangelion]]''.<ref name=Refrain/> The producers released the soundtrack across two albums in August and November 1997.<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=1 |pages=30–31|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref>

Like the original series, ''The End of Evangelion'' features numerous [[Psychology|psychological]], [[Philosophy|philosophical]], and [[Religion|religious]] themes and imagery, in a style that official materials describe as "pedantic".<ref name=RCBCommentary>{{cite journal|editor=Gainax|title=解説|journal=The End of Evangelion Program Book|year=1997|language=ja}}</ref> Anno incorporated various terms to create atmosphere and suggest a deeper meaning, allowing him, in his own words, to sound "intelligent".<ref>{{cite web |url=http://johakyu.net/lib/2007/07/2007-07-27-000550.php |title=世代をこえて共有するSF精神!: 序破急 |language=ja |access-date=May 16, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080329045234/http://johakyu.net/lib/2007/07/2007-07-27-000550.php |archive-date=March 29, 2008 }}</ref><ref name="Kanzaki">{{cite web |url=http://kanzaki.sub.jp/archives/000273.html |title=庵野秀明 in トップランナー【3】 |language=ja |access-date=April 23, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233621/http://kanzaki.sub.jp/archives/000273.html |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> Throughout the film, elements of [[Judaism]] and [[Christianity]] are mentioned, such as the [[number of the beast]],{{sfn|McDowell|2026|p=7}} 666,<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=17|pages=23–24|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=50|page=5|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref> the [[tree of life]],<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=12|page=21|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref> the [[Tree of the Sephiroth]], the Catholic [[stigmata]],<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=11|page=22|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref><ref>{{cite book|language=ja|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Laserdisc Encyclopedia|volume=14|date=1998}}</ref> the [[universal flood]], [[Noah's Ark]], the [[Genesis creation narrative]],<ref>{{cite journal|editor=Gainax|title=Note|journal=The End of Evangelion Program Book|year=1997|language=ja}}</ref> and the Book of Revelation.<ref name=Glossary>{{cite journal|editor=Gainax|title=用語集|journal=The End of Evangelion Program Book|year=1997|language=ja}}</ref> Writer Deniz Yenimazman also interpreted Shinji as a [[Messiah|messianic]] figure,<ref>{{cite book|editor-first1=Christian|editor-last1=Cotton|editor-first2=Andrew M.|editor-last2=Winters|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion and Philosophy|date=2022|publisher=Carus Books|isbn=978-1-63770-004-4|chapter=Apocalypse and Pharmakos}}</ref> while scholar Gavin McDowell likened the giant Lilith to "the androgynous, hermaphroditic Adam described in [[rabbinic literature]]".{{sfn|McDowell|2026|p=11}}

In the early stages of Instrumentality, the Eva Series forms a giant Sephirotic Tree in the sky,<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=5|page=25|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref> with each Eva assuming the role of a ''[[sephirah]]''.<ref name=Glossary/>{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=21}} During his metaphorical sacrifice, Shinji occupies the ''[[Tiferet]]'' ''sephirah''.{{sfn|Porori|2010|p=88}} ''Slant Magazine''{{'s}} Michael Peterson noted that [[Christian Kabbalah]] associates ''Tiferet'' with [[Christ]] on the cross and, consequently, with the point where the sacred meets the profane.<ref name=Peterson/> ''Anime Invasion'' magazine also noted that 16th-century Jewish mystical writings predicted a decline of the divine light in every human being, followed by the destruction of humanity in a catastrophic event.<ref>{{cite journal|journal=Anime Invasion|issue=3|date=2002|publisher=Wizard Entertainment|first=Jonathan|last=Clements|title=Touched By An Angel|p=28}}</ref> Eva-01 transforms into the Tree of Life in the following scenes, which an official pamphlet for the film connects to the inverted tree of the Kabbalah and a similar tree found in [[Hindu]] scriptures.<ref name=Glossary/> Seele calls this process the "Red Earth Purification Ceremony"; the name draws on theories concerning the etymology of Adam, according to which the name means "earth" or "red". The purification ceremony also references ''[[misogi]]'', a [[Shinto]] purification ritual.<ref name=Glossary/> During Instrumentality, the film also mentions the Jewish chamber of [[Guf]],{{sfn|McDowell|2026|p=10}} which Rei Ayanami opens during the Third Impact.{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|p=172}} In the original storyboard, the term indicates Lilith's stigmatas.<ref>{{cite book |title=新世紀エヴァンゲリオン 劇場版 絵コンテ集|publisher=[[Kadokawa Shoten]]|page=789|language=ja|year=1998|isbn=4-04-904290-8}}</ref> Eva-01 serves as a "medium", or ''[[yorishiro]]'', in the process. According to official materials, the Shinto term refers to an object that attracts divine spirits, known as ''[[kami]]'' in Japanese, or provides a medium through which they manifest.<ref name=Glossary/><ref>{{cite book |date=2021|title=Neon Genesis Evangelion Blue Ray Ultimate Edition Encyclopedia|chapter=One More Final: I Need You|quote=A medium is an object to which a diverse spirit is drawn or that shows the appearance of a divine spirit.}}</ref> Seele also describes Instrumentality as a [[rite of passage]]; ''Evangelion Chronicle'' magazine linked the term to [[Arnold van Gennep]]'s anthropological concept of the same name and to the sacrament of [[baptism]].<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=18 |page=22|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja}}</ref>

The film also references the [[psychoanalysis]] of [[Sigmund Freud]], including the dichotomy between [[Eros and Thanatos]]. According to critics, the characters remain caught between the sexual and life drive, or [[libido]], and the destructive, suicidal drive of [[destrudo]], a concept theorized by [[Edoardo Weiss]].<ref>{{cite book |title=Super/Heroes: From Hercules to Superman|author-first1=Wendy |author-last1=Haslem|author2=Angela Ndalianis Publishing|page=116|year=2007|publisher=New Academia |isbn=978-0-9777908-4-5}}</ref> According to ''Evangelion Chronicle'', an official encyclopedia about the series, the presence of destrudo in Shinji's mind is necessary to initiate Instrumentality.<ref>{{cite book |title=Evangelion Chronicle |volume=18|page=24|publisher=Sony Magazines |language=ja }}</ref> Critics have interpreted several scenes as representations of Shinji's sexual instincts, including those in which he sees Asuka in bed and Misato having sex with her boyfriend, [[Ryoji Kaji]]. They have similarly associated his death drive with scenes in which he destroys a sand pyramid, strangles Asuka, and sees childish drawings of dead animals.{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|pp=151-152}} According to writers Kazuhisa Fujie and Martin Foster, the lyrics of the film's two songs, "Thanatos" and "Komm, süsser Tod", emphasize the importance of this dialectic of drives to the protagonist's development.{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|p=149}} Fujie and Foster linked the Human Instrumentality Project, during which Shinji wishes for the death of all human beings, including himself, to Thanatos and destrudo. In the final scenes of ''The End of Evangelion'', Shinji rejects Instrumentality, while Yui tells him that all living beings can regain their human form if they possess the will to live, reaffirming the life drive.{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|p=159}} Critics also identified the psychological concept of the return to the womb in the core of Eva-01,<ref>{{cite book |title=Exploring the Limits of the Human Through Science Fiction|author-first1=Gerald Alva |author-last1=Miller Jr.|location=Basingstoke|publisher=Palgrave Macmillan|pages=91–92|year=2012|isbn=978-1-137-26285-1}}</ref> in the stigmata and on Lilith's forehead, and in the shape of the sandpit in which Shinji builds a sandcastle as a child.<ref name="Peterson">{{cite web |last=Peterson |first=Michael |date=August 29, 2008 |title=The Economy of Visual Language: Neon Genesis Evangelion |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/tv/the-economy-of-visual-language-neon-genesis-evangelion/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140725133718/http://www.slantmagazine.com/house/2008/08/the-economy-of-visual-language-neon-genesis-evangelion/ |archive-date=July 25, 2014 |access-date=April 22, 2020 |website=[[Slant Magazine]]}}</ref>

For the plot of ''The End of Evangelion'', the staff drew inspiration from Japanese works such as ''[[Devilman]]'' by [[Gō Nagai]], ''Ideon: Be Invoked'' by [[Yoshiyuki Tomino]],<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.wired.it/play/televisione/2019/06/21/opere-ispirato-neon-genesis-evangelion/ |title=5 opere che hanno ispirato Neon Genesis Evangelion |date=June 21, 2019 |access-date=April 23, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233638/https://www.wired.it/play/televisione/2019/06/21/opere-ispirato-neon-genesis-evangelion/ |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live|language=it}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://magmix.jp/post/15355/2 |title=富野由悠季監督のSF大作『イデオン』が残した、トラウマ級の伝説とは |date=June 27, 2019 |language=ja |access-date=April 23, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233738/https://magmix.jp/post/15355/2 |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> and the final chapter of the ''[[Space Runaway Ideon]]'' series, which depicts the extermination of humanity in a scenario similar to that of ''The End of Evangelion''. Critics have compared the massacre of Nerv personnel to the slaughter aboard the Solo Ship,<ref name=Apocalisse>{{cite journal|language=ja|date=February 1999|journal=Animage|title=新春精別企画 THE 終末|publisher=Tokuma Shoten}}</ref> the Tree of Life to the Ide, and the Third Impact to the final [[Armageddon]] caused by the Ideon.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.esquire.com/it/cultura/tv/a26020413/neon-genesis-evangelion-storia-recensione/ |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion, storia di un capolavoro |date=January 27, 2019 |access-date=May 19, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233621/https://www.esquire.com/it/cultura/tv/a26020413/neon-genesis-evangelion-storia-recensione/ |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live|language=it}}</ref> The final volume of Miyazaki's [[Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (manga)|''Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind'']] manga also influenced Anno.{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=97-103}} Critics and animation scholars have compared the underlying concept of Instrumentality to [[Cordwainer Smith]]'s [[Instrumentality of Mankind]]{{sfn|Stojnic|2021|p=36}} and the concepts of the [[noosphere]]{{sfn|Cordella|2017|loc=Gli hikikomori e i postumani}} and [[transhumanism]],{{sfn|Cordella|2017|loc=La mortalità, la guerra e l'apocalisse}} as well as to the theories of [[Jesuit]] philosopher [[Pierre Teilhard de Chardin]], who argued that the entire universe would ultimately unite and converge toward a single point, the [[Omega Point]].<ref>{{cite book|first=Claudio|last=Cordella|chapter=Emulazioni|title=Il volto di Ayanami. Simulacri e macchine pensanti tra Oriente e Occidente |publisher=Delos Digital|isbn= 978-88-254-1217-8 |date=2020|language=it}}</ref> Scholar Alba G. Torrents also compared the Human Instrumentality Project to [[Jacques Lacan]]'s [[psychoanalytic theory]], according to which the complete fulfillment of one's desires corresponds to death.<ref>{{cite journal|journal=Debats: Revista de cultura, poder i societat|issue=119|date=2013|language=es|title=La relación con lo materno en el cuerpo robótico en Neon Genesis Evangelion|publisher=Institució Alfons el Magnànim|page=64|first=Alba G.|last=Torrents}}</ref>

Takahashi Watanabe of ''[[Animage]]'' magazine noted that an image of a planetary [[syzygy (astronomy)|syzygy]] appears in the film's second half, paralleling a similar image in Kubrick's ''2001''.<ref name=Animage>{{cite journal|language=ja|title=大森望、森岡浩之対談|journal=Animage|date=September 1997|pages=18–20}}</ref> Critics have also interpreted other sequences as tributes to [[Karel |archive-date=November 25, 2009 }}</ref> ''Animage'' magazine described it as a ''shishōsetsu'' ([[I-novel]]), a form of Japanese [[confessional literature]].<ref name=Animage/> Anno himself described 7, 2021|archive-date=January 16, 2013}}</ref>{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=71-72}} He also described the final episode of the original series in particularly explicit terms, saying that it was "as if, instead of sperm, I had thrown a bucket of water" at the audience.{{sfn|Sanenari|1997|pp=77-78}} According to scholar Yuya Sato, the first half of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' employs elements ===Final scene===
[[File:2001-Sunrise.png|thumb|upright=1.1|left|The film's ending features an image of a planetary syzygy, which critics have compared to similar imagery in ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey (film)|2001: A Space Odyssey]]'' by [[Stanley Kubrick]].<ref name=Animage/>]]

In the final scene of ''The End of Evangelion'', Shinji awakens with Asuka on a deserted beach;{{sfn|Stojnic|2021|p=47}} Shinji strangles Asuka, who then caresses his cheek.<ref name="Ortega"/> He begins to cry, loosening his grip, as she says, "How disgusting", or "I feel sick". Scholars and anime critics<ref>{{cite web|access-date=2 July 2026 |author=Vrai Kaiser |date=6 January 2014 |language=en |title=Love and the Apocalypse: Asuka, Kaworu, and Gendered Expectations in Romance |url=https://vraikaiser.com/2014/01/06/love-and-the-apocalypse/}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |journal=[[Mechademia]] |volume=9 |title=Evangelion as Second Impact: Forever Changing That Which Never Was |date=2014 |publisher=University of Minnesota Press |doi=10.5749/mech.9.2014.0283 |first1=Andreu|last1= Ballús |first2=Alba |last2=Torrents|pp=291-292}}</ref> have interpreted the pair, who survive the Third Impact, as metaphorical counterparts to [[Adam]] and [[Eve]]<ref name="Ortega">{{cite journal |author-first1=Mariana |author-last1=Ortega |date=2007 |title=My Father, He Killed Me; My Mother, She Ate Me: Self, Desire, Engendering, and the Mother in "Neon Genesis Evangelion" |journal=Mechademia |publisher=University of Minnesota Press |location=Minneapolis |volume=2 |issue=10 |page=231|access-date=April 2, 2015 |doi=10.1353/mec.0.0010 |s2cid=120554645 |url=http://muse.jhu.edu/journals/mec/summary/v002/2.ortega.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304064627/http://muse.jhu.edu/journals/mec/summary/v002/2.ortega.html |archive-date=March 4, 2016 |url-status=live |issn=2152-6648|url-access=subscription }}</ref> and "the new primeval father and mother" of a new humanity.{{sfn|Stojnic|2021|p=51}} According to Hiroyuki Morioka, everyday life will begin again.<ref name=Animage/> Asuka's final line and Shinji's strangulation of her generated debate among fans of the series.<ref name=Animage/> ''[[AnimeNation]]'' interpreted the phrase "I feel sick" as expressing Asuka's realization that another human being, Shinji, exists independently of her. Asuka uses the same phrase in a scene from the director's cut of the twenty-second episode, "[[Don't Be]]"; disgusted by human contact, she refuses to share her space or existence with Shinji or Misato. According to ''AnimeNation'', Shinji strangles Asuka to prove to himself that she is a distinct being, [[Other (philosophy)|the Other]], while Asuka's final words may rework [[Jean-Paul Sartre]]'s statement, "[[Hell is other people]]".<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.animenation.net/blog/2003/01/27/ask-john-what-does-asukas-final-line-mean/ |title=Ask John: What Does Asuka's Final Line Mean?|access-date=April 22, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090309115813/http://www.animenation.net/blog/2003/01/27/ask-john-what-does-asukas-final-line-mean/ |archive-date=March 9, 2009 }}</ref> The series' official figurine game offers a similar interpretation::
<blockquote>Shinji renounced the world where all hearts had melted into one and accepted each other unconditionally. His desire to live with 'others'—other hearts that would sometimes reject him, even deny him. That is why the first thing he did after coming to his senses was to place his hands around Asuka's neck. To feel the existence of an 'other'. To confirm rejection and denial.<ref>''Evangelion Carddass Masters G'', card D-88, 気持ち悪い, [[Bandai]].</ref></blockquote>
According to the contemporary artist and writer [[Takashi Murakami]], Shinji, by attempting to kill Asuka, asks her for help and seeks her understanding. Asuka feels disgusted because she regards Shinji as pathetic and capable of communicating only with himself. Murakami thus interpreted her response as reflecting society's view of Shinji.<ref>{{cite book |title=Little Boy: The Arts Of Japan's Exploding Subculture|url=https://archive.org/details/littleboyartsofj0000unse|author-link=Takashi Murakami|author-first1=Takashi|author-last1=Murakami|publisher=Yale University Press|page=[https://archive.org/details/littleboyartsofj0000unse/page/130 130]-131|year=2005 |isbn=978-0-300-10285-7}}</ref> Anime critics have contrasted the scene with the optimistic tone of the series finale, interpreting Shinji's decision to stop strangling Asuka after he begins to cry as an expression of disgust toward himself. They have also argued that Asuka's final line reasserts the pride and selfishness that characterize her and that the words "I feel sick" suggest that neither character has changed or become a better person and that the film's events ultimately lack meaning.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/neon-genesis-evangelion/the-end-of-evangelion/.148874 |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The End of Evangelion |author-first1=Nick |author-last1=Creamer |date=July 11, 2019 |access-date=May 19, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233628/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/neon-genesis-evangelion/the-end-of-evangelion/.148874 |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://sea.ign.com/neon-genesis-evangelion-900656-ws/151206/feature/evangelion-neon-genesis-ending-explained |title=Evangelion Neon Genesis Ending Explained |author-first1=Vrai |author-last1=Kaiser |date=June 28, 2019 |access-date=May 22, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233632/https://sea.ign.com/neon-genesis-evangelion-900656-ws/151206/feature/evangelion-neon-genesis-ending-explained |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> Nozomi Omori compared the scene to [[J. G. Ballard]]'s collection of science fiction short stories ''[[The Terminal Beach]]''.<ref name=Animage/> Writer Kazuhisa Fujie interpreted the strangulation as a reference to the [[Freud]]ian concept of Thanatos, which pervades every moment of life, and to the impossibility of living alone, an idea reflected in the title "I need you". According to Fujie, Shinji loosens his grip and allows his libido toward the Other, Asuka, to prevail once more, thereby offering her his sincerity.{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|p=159}} Anime critic Vrai Kaiser argued that ''The End of Evangelion ''has gained an undeserved reputation as an act of "trolling" by Hideaki Anno toward the audience. According to Kaiser, the film actually suggests that imaginary realities can sometimes provide a necessary refuge, although they cannot sustain a person indefinitely; Asuka's final caress of Shinji's face represents the hope that, despite loneliness and the pain people inflict on one another, they can still show each other kindness.<ref>{{cite web|first=Vrai|last=Kaiser|url=https://vraikaiser.com/2014/09/01/writing-depressing-stories/|title=Do This, Not That: Writing Depressing Stories (Evangelion vs Lars Von Trier)|date=September 1, 2014|access-date=August 8, 2026}}</ref> Assistant director Tsurumaki offered a different interpretation of the sequence, saying: <blockquote>"Well, my personal view is, 'Do we really need to complement these troubles of the heart?' Regardless of whether or not we are complemented, have troubles, or find our answers, interpersonal relations exist, and the world goes on. I thought the last scene meant to say that life goes on, but I could be wrong".<ref name=Tsurumaki/></blockquote>

=== Critique of ''otaku'' ===
''The End of Evangelion'' continues the series' central theme, presenting a "story of communication".<ref name=Tsurumaki/> The feature film has been interpreted as a criticism of ''[[otaku]]''—obsessive fans of animation, video games, and comic books.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama49.shtml |title=第49回 エヴァ雑記「第拾六話 死に至る病、そして」 |language=ja |access-date=May 28, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181010182100/http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama49.shtml |archive-date=October 10, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.escapistmagazine.com/rebuild-thrice-upon-a-time-imagines-an-ending-beyond-neon-genesis-evangelion/|title=Thrice Upon a Time Imagines an Ending Beyond Neon Genesis Evangelion|access-date=January 12, 2022|author-first1=Darren |author-last1=Mooney|publisher=Escapist Magazine|date=September 6, 2021|archive-date=January 12, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220112234459/https://www.escapistmagazine.com/rebuild-thrice-upon-a-time-imagines-an-ending-beyond-neon-genesis-evangelion/|url-status=live}}</ref> Hideaki Anno, himself an ''otaku'', has criticized the subculture for what he considers its excessive isolation, self-referentiality, and introversion; after the end of the series, he criticized fans who immersed themselves in the Internet as a means of escaping reality, comparing their opinions to "graffiti in public toilets".{{sfn|Fujie|Foster|2004|p=163}} In 1996, Anno expressed disappointment at the reception of the original series, which he believed viewers had turned into a refuge from unpleasant aspects of reality.<ref>{{cite journal |date=July 1996|title=あんた、バカぁと、言われてみたい。（庵野秀明、宮村優子） |publisher=Tokuma Shoten |language=ja |journal=Animage}}</ref> Responding to subsequent criticism, he stated that, "My own feeling is all about the message I put at the beginning of the twenty-sixth episode. There is no fiction in that shot. It's real, and it's true".<ref>{{cite journal|language=ja|journal=Newtype|date=September 1997|title=庵野秀明が語るNOW & THEN|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten}}</ref> With ''The End of Evangelion'' he intended to "purify" viewers by prompting them to wake up and return to reality. He regarded this criticism, despite its confrontational appearance, as a form of "[[fan service]] that doesn't seem fan service", because it is still useful to viewers.<ref name="Kanzaki"/>{{sfn|Yoshihara|1998|p=115}} According to Kazuya Tsurumaki, ''Evangelion'' contains a message aimed at anime fans, including Anno himself. "In other words, it's useless for non-anime fans to watch it. If a person who can already live and communicate normally watches it, they won't learn anything.&nbsp;... Don't drag the past around. Find the next thing that interests you.&nbsp;... It's always better to let something that has finished [''Evangelion''] end".<ref name=Tsurumaki/>

Anime scholar Nicolle Lamerichs argued that ''End of Evangelion'' breaks the fourth wall and pushes its fans to reflect "on the meaning of the narrative, fan culture, and escapism".<ref>{{cite journal|last=Lamerichs|first=Nicole|date=2022|title=The Emotional Realism of Anime: Rewriting Characters and Affective Reception in ''Evangelion 3.0+1.0: Thrice Upon a Time''|journal=[[Mechademia]]|publisher=[[University of Minnesota Press]]|publication-place=Minneapolis|volume=15|issue=1|p=92|issn=1934-2489|eissn=2152-6648|jstor=48853842|lccn=2006215646|oclc=1376034838|s2cid=254824343|id={{Gale|A732431709}}}}</ref> Critics have interpreted Instrumentality, in which individual physical existence dissolves into an accommodating cosmic fusion, as a metaphor for the condition of ''otaku''.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama53.shtml |title=第53回 エヴァ雑記「第弐拾話 心のかたち 人のかたち」 |language=ja |access-date=May 28, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181010182123/http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/animesama53.shtml |archive-date=October 10, 2018 |url-status=live}}</ref> According to Japanese critic Manabu Tsuribe, the film ends when Shinji rejects Instrumentality and recognizes Asuka as a being separate from himself—the Other, whom consciousness can never completely internalize. Tsuribe interpreted Shinji as embodying the closed interiority characteristic of ''otaku'' mentality; in his reading, Asuka constitutes an ambiguous existence beyond Shinji's control, and her ambiguity mirrors that of ''Evangelion'' itself. Tsuribe also interpreted their violent and possessive relationship as an ironic criticism of the escapism of anime fans who retreat into their inner worlds and struggle to relate to other people.<ref name=Tsuribe>{{cite web |url=http://www001.upp.so-net.ne.jp/tsuribe/anime/critique/evae.html |title=Prison of Self-consciousness: an Essay on Evangelion|author-first1=Manabu |author-last1=Tsuribe |date=February 1999 |access-date=June 12, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233725/http://www001.upp.so-net.ne.jp/tsuribe/anime/critique/evae.html |archive-date=January 2, 2021 }}</ref> According to Nozomi Omori, the film concerns "living with anime" and can be interpreted as Anno's attempt to argue with himself. Omori and Tsuribe both compared ''The End of Evangelion'' to [[Mamoru Oshii]]'s ''[[Urusei Yatsura 2: Beautiful Dreamer]]'',<ref name=Animage/><ref name=Tsuribe/> in which the characters discover that their world is a dream shaped by Lum's wishes and ultimately return to an animated reality. By contrast, Omori argued that in ''Evangelion'', "after hearing 'I feel sick' one cannot return to the animated world" and instead finds oneself immediately thrust back into real life.<ref name=Animage/>

Anime critic Vrai Kaiser interpreted the opening scene with Shinji as a critique of the fetishization of female characters among male ''otaku'' audiences. Kaiser also viewed Shinji's violent behavior as an instance of "mental health toxicity becoming masculine violence".<ref>{{cite web|access-date=26 July 2026|url=https://www.animefeminist.com/podcast-chatty-af-105-neon-genesis-evangelion-watchalong-end-of-evangelion-final/|title=Chatty AF 105: Neon Genesis Evangelion Watchalong – End of Evangelion (with transcript)|publisher=Anime Feminist|website=animefeminist.com|date=December 21, 2019}}</ref> In the second part of The ''End of Evangelion'', Shinji discusses the nature of dreams with Rei Ayanami. During the live-action sequence, Ayanami tells him that a person cannot dream alone, since such a dream would merely constitute an alternative to reality, and accuses Shinji of using fantasy to deceive himself about reality. As Rei speaks, the film shows photographs of graffiti at Gainax's offices and images of fans at the Shinjuku Milano cinema, leading critics to interpret the dream as a metaphor for anime. The sequence also displays the caption {{nihongo|"Does it feel good?"|気持ち、いいの？|}};<ref name=Style/> according to an official film pamphlet, the question addresses both the fans and the staff of ''Neon Genesis Evangelion''.<ref>{{cite book |title=Neon Genesis Evangelion: The Feature Film - DTS Collector's Edition Booklet|publisher=Dynit Italia|page=15|date=2009|editor=Dynit Italia}}</ref> According to [[Yūichirō Oguro]], editor of materials for the series' home-video editions, the staff included the sequence to suggest that anime fans share Shinji's problems and to criticize those who seek refuge in fiction.<ref name="Oguro">{{cite web |url=http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama63.shtml |title=第63回 エヴァ雑記「纏め」１ |language=ja |access-date=May 7, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233632/http://style.fm/as/05_column/animesama63.shtml |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> According to Andrea Fontana, a writer and scholar of Japanese animation, the sequence allows ''otaku'' to "admire themselves, discuss themselves, just as Shinji did with himself", interpreting the scene as "a desirable rebirth for everyone".<ref>{{cite book |title=Anime. Storia dell'animazione giapponese 1984–2007|author-first1=Andrea |author-last1=Fontana|author2=Davide Tarò|location=Piombino|publisher=Il Foglio Letterario|page=103|year=2007|isbn=978-88-7606-160-8|ref=Fontana, Tarò}}</ref> Near the end of the film, Lilith, bearing Rei's appearance, disintegrates after Shinji rejects the cosmic fusion of Instrumentality. Oguro argued that Rei, the series' most popular character, represents ''Neon Genesis Evangelion''{{'s}} [[pleasure principle (psychology)|pleasure principle]] and interpreted her disintegration as marking the end of the ''otaku'' dream and a return to reality.<ref name="Style"/>

In 1997, ''Neon Genesis Evangelion'' stood at the center of national debate in Japan<ref name=Mainichi/> and became a [[social phenomenon]] after its ending. The controversy surrounding the original ending attracted large audiences to theaters, including viewers curious about the debate. The film also drew attention from newspapers and audiences outside the usual anime fanbase.<ref>{{cite journal |date=August 1997 |title=Two Big Anime Movies this Summer! |journal=Nikkei Entertainment |language=ja |access-date=October 31, 2015 |url=http://www.acsys.com/~tallman/rebirth_e.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010210204056/http://www.acsys.com/~tallman/rebirth_e.html |archive-date=February 10, 2001 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.excite.co.jp/news/article/E1408292536140/ |title=今夜「エヴァンゲリオン」旧劇場版地上波初放送、もう見られない幻の「シト新生」 |date=August 18, 2014 |language=ja |access-date=May 22, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210102233737/https://www.excite.co.jp/news/article/E1408292536140/ |archive-date=January 2, 2021 |url-status=live}}</ref> ''The End of Evangelion'' surpassed ''Death and Rebirth'' in attendance, attracting 400,000 viewers in the first three days of its release.<ref name=Playboy/> It ranked third at the Japanese box office that week, behind ''[[The Lost World: Jurassic Park]]'' and [[Hayao Miyazaki]]'s ''[[Princess Mononoke]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine|title=Japan Top 15|magazine=[[Screen International]]|date=August 1, 1997|page=27}}</ref> The movie earned approximately ¥1.5 billion in Japanese [[distributor rentals]], equivalent to about {{US$|12 million|long=no}} at the time, becoming the fourth-highest-grossing Japanese feature film of 1997.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2007-10-03/evangelion-1.0-is-now-top-grossing-eva-movie |title=Evangelion: 1.0 is Now Top Grossing Eva Movie |date=October 3, 2007|access-date=May 8, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190507145135/https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2007-10-03/evangelion-1.0-is-now-top-grossing-eva-movie |archive-date=May 7, 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref> Japanese media at the time highlighted the contrast between the film's poster and that of ''Princess Mononoke'', which similarly featured mature and violent themes; the poster for ''Princess Mononoke'' carried the tagline "Live!", while that of ''The End of Evangelion'' read "Wouldn't it be nice if everyone would just die?".<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cinematoday.jp/news/N0104479 |title=今夜金ロー！『もののけ姫』の誕生の舞台裏 |date=October 26, 2018 |language=ja |access-date=May 17, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191019043746/https://www.cinematoday.jp/news/N0104479 |archive-date=October 19, 2019 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.excite.co.jp/news/article/E1408896966616/ |title=ニュース トレンド レビュー 金曜ロードSHOW あの頃のエヴァ…今夜地上波初「THE END OF EVANGELION Air/まごころを、君に」 |date=August 25, 2014 |language=ja |access-date=May 22, 2020 |archive-date=December 2, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201202133622/https://www.excite.co.jp/news/article/E1408896966616/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The film generated considerable anticipation, with long lines forming in Japan for premiere tickets and attracting further media attention.<ref>{{cite journal|language=ja|title=7.18~7.19密着ドキュメント|journal=Animage|date=September 1997|pages=8–11}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal|language=ja|title=With NT 1/4|journal=Newtype|date=March 2010|page=33|publisher=Kadokawa Shoten}}</ref>
