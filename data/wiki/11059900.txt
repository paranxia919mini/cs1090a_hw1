'''''Sliver''''' is a 1993 American [[erotic thriller]] film starring [[Sharon Stone]], [[William Baldwin]], and [[Tom Berenger]]. It is based on the [[Ira Levin]] [[Sliver (novel)|novel of the same name]] about the mysterious occurrences in a privately owned [[New York City|New York]] high-rise [[sliver building]].<ref name="NYT" /> [[Phillip Noyce]] directed the film, from a screenplay by [[Joe Eszterhas]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Dretzka |first=Gary |date=October 26, 1997 |title=Beyond 'Sliver' |work=[[Chicago Tribune]] |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/1997/10/26/beyond-sliver/ |url-status=live |access-date=March 7, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120915101234/http://articles.chicagotribune.com/1997-10-26/news/9710260477_1_showgirls-jade-basic-instinct |archive-date=September 15, 2012}}</ref> Because of a major battle with the [[Motion Picture Association|MPAA]] (which originally gave the film an [[Motion Picture Association film rating system|NC-17]] rating), the filmmakers were forced to make extensive reshoots before release which necessitated changing the killer's identity.

The film was released theatrically on May 21, 1993, by [[Paramount Pictures]]. It topped the U.S. box office despite underperforming and was a huge hit in the international market. ''Sliver'', like many erotic thrillers of the time, found greater success in the [[home video]] market<ref>{{cite web|last=Willmore|first=Alison|date=April 8, 2022|title=Erotic Thrillers Owe Everything to Home Viewers|url=https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/erotic-thrillers-owe-everything-to-home-viewers.html|website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]|access-date=May 6, 2024|archive-date=April 12, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220412120925/https://www.vulture.com/2022/04/erotic-thrillers-owe-everything-to-home-viewers.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last=Hunt|first=Dennis|date=November 26, 1993|title=National Video Rentals : 'Sliver' Full of Rental Sex Appeal|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-26-ca-61079-story.html|newspaper=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=May 3, 2024|archive-date=May 3, 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240503031743/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-11-26-ca-61079-story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and was the 8th most rented film in the United States for 1994.<ref>{{cite web|date=December 29, 1994 |title=Billboard's year-end ranking of the top video sales and rentals between Dec. 4, 1993 and Nov. 26, 1994 |url=https://www.upi.com/Archives/1994/12/29/Billboards-year-end-ranking-of-the-top-video-sales-and-rentals-between-Dec-4-1993-and-Nov-26-1994/2631788677200/ |website=[[United Press International|UPI]]}}</ref>

==Plot==
Carly Norris, a beautiful book editor and divorcee in her mid-30s, moves into the exclusive [[New York City]] [[sliver building]] "113". She meets other tenants including Zeke Hawkins, a [[video game design]]er; Jack Landsford, a [[novelist]]; Vida Warren, a fashion model who moonlights as a [[call girl]]; and Gus Hale, a professor of [[videography]] at [[New York University]]. They tell Carly that she bears a striking resemblance to Naomi Singer, the previous tenant of her apartment who fell to her death from her balcony.

After running into Zeke numerous times, Carly invites him to her [[housewarming party]]. Soon afterwards, they begin a sexual relationship. Meanwhile, Jack starts stalking Carly and warning her about Zeke, who he says is "sick". He also points out that Zeke's deceased mother, a [[soap opera]] actress named Thea Manning, bears a resemblance to Carly. As Jack's behavior becomes more erratic, Gus dies in the shower under suspicious circumstances and Vida is murdered, with police suspicion falling on Jack for her death after Carly discovers him in the stairwell with her corpse.

Zeke reveals to Carly that he is the owner of 113, which he bought with his inheritance from his wealthy father. As owner of 113, Zeke has installed a comprehensive [[Closed-circuit television|video surveillance]] system throughout the building, allowing him to spy on all of the tenants from his own secret surveillance room. Through deduction and eventually one of Zeke's secret recordings, Carly learns that Jack killed Naomi in a crime of passion.

Jack was jealous of Zeke, who had sexual relations with both Naomi and Vida. Jack attacks Carly in her own apartment and she accidentally shoots him dead. Angry at Zeke for withholding evidence in Naomi's murder and jealous of his liaisons with Naomi and Vida, Carly destroys Zeke's surveillance room, tells him to "get a life", then leaves.

==Production==

===Location===
[[File:Morgan Court, New York City.jpg|thumb|Morgan Court, a sliver building at 211 Madison Ave in New York City, used as the filming location]]
In the film, the tall and narrow sliver building is located at 113 East 38th Street in [[Manhattan]], placing it at 38th Street and [[Park Avenue]]. The actual building used in the film is known as Morgan Court, located at 211 [[Madison Avenue]] in [[New York City|New York]], one block west and two blocks south of the fictional address.<ref name="AFI" /> The building has since become a condominium development. It was built in 1985 and has 32 floors. While the movie made use of the building's courtyard, the lobby was a Los Angeles film set.<ref name="AFI" />

===Original ending===
In the film's original ending Zeke Hawkins ([[William Baldwin]]), instead of Jack Landsford ([[Tom Berenger]]), turns out to be the killer. After the police assume Jack to be the murderer, Carly Norris ([[Sharon Stone]]) and Zeke burn the videotapes. Soon afterwards, they wed atop the Sliver building. On their honeymoon, they fly a helicopter over a [[Hawaii]]an [[volcano]] where Carly reveals that she knows he is the killer. She tells him she still has the tape of him murdering Gus in the shower and that "it's safe", implying she is willing to cover up his crimes and that she has found the excitement missing from her previous marriage. With their seatbelts off and Carly videotaping the scenery Zeke lowers the aircraft into the volcano as they both laugh gleefully. The scene then cuts to Zeke's surveillance room where the televisions display nothing but static. The end credits roll and leave the audience to decide whether they survive.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hicks |first=Chris |date=May 29, 1993 |title=WITHOUT 'SLIVER' OF A CLUE, MYSTERY ENDS POORLY |url=https://www.deseret.com/1993/5/30/19049162/without-sliver-of-a-clue-mystery-ends-poorly |access-date=July 1, 2022 |work=[[Deseret News]] |archive-date=July 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220701052251/https://www.deseret.com/1993/5/30/19049162/without-sliver-of-a-clue-mystery-ends-poorly |url-status=live }}</ref> The shooting of the final scene resulted in the crashing of the [[helicopter]].<ref name="AFI" /> After an investigation, the pilot's certificate was temporarily suspended.<ref name=NYT>{{cite web|work=[[The New York Times]]|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1993/05/30/movies/film-bucks-and-blondes-joe-eszterhas-lives-the-big-dream.html|title=FILM; Bucks and Blondes: Joe Eszterhas Lives The Big Dream|author-link=Maureen Dowd|author=Dowd, Maureen|date=May 30, 1993|access-date=February 18, 2017|archive-date=February 18, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170218145603/http://www.nytimes.com/1993/05/30/movies/film-bucks-and-blondes-joe-eszterhas-lives-the-big-dream.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Preview audiences disliked the idea of Carly turning immoral, so the ending was re-written and re-shot to the one used in the final release.<ref name="NYT" />

==Release and reception==
The film premiered on May 19, 1993, at [[Mann Theatres|Mann National Theatre]] in [[Westwood, Los Angeles|Westwood Village, Los Angeles]].<ref name = "AFI"/> It was released two days later, on May 21, and received negative reviews from critics. Review aggregate website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported that 21% of critics have given the film a positive review based on 29 reviews, with an average rating of 3.9/10. The site's critics consensus reads "''Sliver'' is an absurd erotic thriller with technobabble and posits prime Sharon Stone as a professional book nerd."<ref name="RT">{{cite web |title=Sliver (1993) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/sliver |access-date=February 27, 2025 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media|Fandango]] |archive-date=March 13, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240313021313/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/sliver |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 38 out of 100 based on 21 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web |title=Sliver Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/sliver |access-date=December 26, 2019 |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |archive-date=February 5, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220205182113/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/sliver |url-status=live }}</ref>

===MPAA ratings issues===
Director [[Phillip Noyce]] claimed he had to make 110 edits to the film in order to avoid an NC-17 rating by removing the display of male frontal nudity.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Cagle |first=Jess |date=May 21, 1993 |title=The troubled making of ''Sliver'' |url=https://ew.com/article/1993/05/21/troubled-making-sliver/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160309080159/https://ew.com/article/1993/05/21/troubled-making-sliver/ |archive-date=March 9, 2016 |access-date=July 1, 2022 |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |language=en}}</ref>

===Box office===
The film debuted at No. 1 at the box office making $12.1 million in 2,093 theaters.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Fox |first=David J. |date=May 24, 1993 |title=Stone Gets a 'Sliver' of Box Office but Not a Runaway |work=Los Angeles Times |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-05-24-ca-39255-story.html |url-status=live |access-date=March 7, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121104113645/https://articles.latimes.com/1993-05-24/entertainment/ca-39255_1_box-office |archive-date=November 4, 2012}}</ref> By the second week the box office taking dropped to No. 6.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Fox |first=David J. |date=June 1, 1993 |title=Sly's Back in Peak Form at Box Office |work=Los Angeles Times |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-06-01-ca-42182-story.html |url-status=live |access-date=March 7, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210514095029/https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-06-01-ca-42182-story.html |archive-date=May 14, 2021}}</ref> ''Sliver'' eventually grossed $36.3 million domestically<ref name="mojo">{{cite web|url=https://boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=sliver.htm|title=Sliver (1993) - Box Office Mojo|publisher=[[Internet Movie Database|IMDb]]|access-date=2014-04-11|archive-date=December 16, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181216210905/https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=sliver.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> and $87.6 million internationally to a total of $123.9 million worldwide.<ref name="ww" />

===Home media===
When originally released on [[VHS]], the film was released in both its theatrical R-rated version and an unrated version which restored the cuts made by the [[Motion Picture Association|MPAA]]. In March 2006, to coincide with the theatrical release of ''[[Basic Instinct 2]]'', which starred Stone, ''Sliver'' was released on [[DVD]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Sliver (Unrated Edition) |url=https://www.amazon.com/Sliver-Unrated-Sharon-Stone/dp/B000EQ5SRK/ |access-date=July 1, 2022 |website=Amazon |archive-date=August 19, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210819003827/https://www.amazon.com/Sliver-Unrated-Sharon-Stone/dp/B000EQ5SRK |url-status=live }}</ref> Only the unrated cut was made commercially available, but the R-rated cut was distributed for rental. Although the film was theatrically shown in the 2.35 [[aspect ratio (image)|aspect ratio]], the DVD features a matted, 2.10 [[aspect ratio (image)|aspect ratio]] transfer. In 2013, the R-rated cut was released on [[Blu-ray]], sourced from the same 2.10 aspect ratio HD master used for the DVD release.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Sliver Blu-ray |url=https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Sliver-Blu-ray/43913/ |access-date=July 1, 2022 |website=blu-ray.com |archive-date=July 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220701052251/https://www.blu-ray.com/movies/Sliver-Blu-ray/43913/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In 2024, [[Vinegar Syndrome]], under license from Paramount, announced an [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] release of the film as part of their annual [[Black Friday (shopping)|Black Friday]] sales event, featuring a new director-supervised 4K restoration of the unrated version presented in its original 2.35 theatrical aspect ratio. The alternative scenes from the R-rated cut are also included as a bonus.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Sliver |url=https://vinegarsyndrome.com/collections/frontpage/products/sliver |access-date=2024-11-29 |website=Vinegar Syndrome |language=en}}</ref>
