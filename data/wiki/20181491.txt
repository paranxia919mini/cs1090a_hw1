'''''Planet of the Apes''''' is a 2001 American [[science fiction film|science fiction]] [[Action film|action]] [[adventure film]] directed by [[Tim Burton]] from a screenplay by [[William Broyles Jr.]], [[Lawrence Konner]], and [[Mark Rosenthal (filmmaker)|Mark Rosenthal]]. The sixth installment in the [[Planet of the Apes|''Planet of the Apes'' film series]], it is loosely based on the 1963 novel [[Planet of the Apes (novel)|of the same name]] by [[Pierre Boulle]] and serves as a [[remake]] of the 1968 [[Planet of the Apes (1968 film)|film version]]. The film stars [[Mark Wahlberg]], [[Tim Roth]], [[Helena Bonham Carter]], [[Michael Clarke Duncan]], [[Kris Kristofferson]], [[Estella Warren]], and [[Paul Giamatti]]. It tells the story of astronaut Leo Davidson (Wahlberg) crash-landing on a planet inhabited by intelligent apes. The apes treat humans as slaves, but with the help of an ape named [[Ari (Planet of the Apes)|Ari]] (Bonham Carter), Leo starts a rebellion as he seeks to return.

Development for a ''Planet of the Apes'' remake started as far back as 1988 with [[Adam Rifkin]]. His project nearly reached the [[pre-production]] stage before being canceled. [[Terry Hayes]]'s script, titled ''Return of the Apes'', would have starred [[Arnold Schwarzenegger]], under the direction of [[Phillip Noyce]]. [[Oliver Stone]], [[Don Murphy]], and [[Jane Hamsher]] were set to produce. Creative differences ensued between Hayes and [[20th Century Fox]]. [[Chris Columbus (filmmaker)|Chris Columbus]], [[Sam Hamm]], [[James Cameron]], [[Peter Jackson]], and the [[Hughes brothers]] later became involved. With Broyles Jr.'s script, Burton was hired as director, and the film was put into active development. Konner and Rosenthal rewrote the script, and filming took place from November 2000 to April 2001.

''Planet of the Apes'' was released in the United States on July 27, 2001, by 20th Century Fox. The film received mixed reviews from critics, who criticized the screenplay and [[twist ending]], but praised [[Rick Baker (makeup artist)|Rick Baker]]'s [[prosthetic makeup]] designs, visual aspects, and musical score. It grossed $362.2 million worldwide, becoming the [[2001 in film|ninth-highest-grossing film of 2001]]. Despite its financial success, Fox chose to cancel the [[sequel]]. Ten years later, the film was [[reboot (fiction)|rebooted]] into film series in 2011 with the title, ''[[Rise of the Planet of the Apes]]''.

==Plot==
In 2029, aboard the space station ''Oberon'', Leo Davidson works closely with apes who are trained for space missions. His favorite is a chimpanzee named Pericles. With a deadly electromagnetic storm approaching the station, a small space pod piloted by Pericles is used to probe the storm. Pericles's pod heads into the storm and disappears. Leo takes a second pod to find Pericles, despite lacking authority to do so. Entering the storm, Leo loses contact with the ''Oberon'' and, in 5021 A.D., crashes on a planet called Ashlar. He learns that the world is ruled by intelligent apes who treat humans as slaves.

Leo meets a female chimpanzee named [[Ari (Planet of the Apes)|Ari]], who protests the mistreatment humans receive. Ari decides to buy Leo and a female slave named Daena to have them serve as servants in her father's house. Leo escapes his cage and frees other humans. Limbo, an orangutan trader, sees them but is taken prisoner to ensure his silence. The murderous [[General Thade]] and his gorilla junior, [[Colonel Attar]], march ape warriors to pursue the humans. Leo discovers Calima, the forbidden, holy temple of "Semos", the first ape, whom the apes revere as a God.

Calima turns out to be the remains of the ''Oberon'' which had crashed on the planet's surface and now looks ancient.{{Efn|The name Calima comes from the sign "'''CA'''ution '''LI'''ve ani'''MA'''ls", the relevant letters being the only ones not covered in dust.}} According to the computer logs, the station has been there for millennia. Leo realizes that when he entered the vortex, he was pushed forward in time, while the ''Oberon'', searching after him, was not, crashing on the planet long before he did.

The ''Oberon's'' log reveals that the apes on board, led by Semos, organized a mutiny and took over the vessel after it crashed. The human and ape survivors of the struggle left the ship, and their descendants are the people Leo has encountered since landing. The apes arrive and attack the humans who have gathered to see Leo, although he evens the odds when he uses the ''Oberon's'' fuel to blast the first wave of apes. The battle stops when a familiar vehicle descends from the sky, which Leo immediately identifies as the pod piloted by Pericles, who was pushed in time as Leo was and had just now found his way to the planet.{{Efn|The electromagnetic storm releases people from it in an opposite direction in time to their entrance because they were traveling backward not forward in time.}} When Pericles lands and opens the pod, the apes believe he is the returning Semos, and hostilities between humans and apes suddenly cease.

Pericles runs into the wreck of the ''Oberon'' and Leo runs after him, followed by General Thade. Thade and Leo fight until Thade is within the pilot's deck and Leo closes the automatic door, trapping Thade inside. Deciding to escape Ashlar and return to Earth, Leo gives Pericles to Ari, who promises to look after him. After saying farewell to Ari and Daena, Leo climbs aboard Pericles's undamaged pod and travels through the same electromagnetic storm.

Leo crashes in [[Washington, D.C.]], on [[Earth]] and looks up at what appears to be the [[Lincoln Memorial]], only to find that it is now a monument memorializing General Thade. A swarm of [[police]] officers, [[firefighter]]s, and [[news]] [[reporter]]s descend upon him, revealing all to be apes as Leo realizes that he is on ape-dominated Earth.

==Development==

===Late 1980s===
[[20th Century Fox]] president Craig Baumgarten was impressed with [[Adam Rifkin]]'s filmmaking with ''[[Never on Tuesday]]''. In 1988, Rifkin was brought in the studio to [[pitch (filmmaking)|pitch]] ideas for films. Rifkin, being a fan of the 1968 ''[[Planet of the Apes (1968 film)|Planet of the Apes]]'' felt it was best to continue the film series. "Having [[independent film]] experience, I promised I could write and direct a huge-looking film for a reasonable price and budget, like ''[[Aliens (film)|Aliens]]''."<ref name="Adam" /> Fox commissioned Rifkin to write what amounted to a sequel, "but not a sequel to [[Battle for the Planet of the Apes|the fifth film]], an alternate sequel to the first film".<ref name="Adam" /> He took influences from ''[[Spartacus (film)|Spartacus]]'', with the storyline being "the ape empire had reached its [[Roman Empire|Roman era]]. A descendant of Charlton Heston's character named Duke would eventually lead a human slave revolt against the oppressive Romanesque apes, led by General Izan. A real [[Sword-and-sandal|sword and sandal]] spectacular, monkey style. ''[[Gladiator (2000 film)|Gladiator]]'' did the same movie without the ape costumes."<ref name="Adam" />

Titled ''Return to the Planet of the Apes'', the project was put on fast track and almost entered [[pre-production]]. [[Rick Baker (makeup artist)|Rick Baker]] was hired to design the [[prosthetic makeup]] with [[Danny Elfman]] composing the [[film score]]. [[Tom Cruise]] and [[Charlie Sheen]] were in contention for the lead role. "I can't accurately describe in words the utter euphoria I felt knowing that I, Adam Rifkin, was going to be resurrecting the ''Planet of the Apes''. It all seemed too good to be true. I soon found out it was."<ref name="Adam" /> Days before the film was to commence pre-production, new [[studio executive]]s arrived at Fox, which caused creative differences between Rifkin and the studio.<ref name="Adam">{{cite book | author = David Hughes | title = Tales From Development Hell | publisher = [[Titan Books]] | date = March 2004 | location = [[London]] | pages = [https://archive.org/details/talesfromdevelop00davi/page/34 34&mdash;37] | isbn = 1-84023-691-4 | url-access = registration | url = https://archive.org/details/talesfromdevelop00davi/page/34 }}</ref> Rifkin was commissioned to rewrite the script through various drafts. The project was abandoned until [[Peter Jackson]] and [[Fran Walsh]] pitched their own idea, with the apes going through a [[Renaissance]]. In the story, the ape government becomes concerned over the new art works, the humans are revolting and the liberal apes shelter a half-human, half-ape from the gorillas. [[Roddy McDowall]] was enthusiastic about their proposal and agreed to play the [[Leonardo da Vinci]]-type character they had written for him. However, the executive Jackson spoke to was not a fan of the series and seemingly unaware of McDowall's involvement in the series, and Jackson turned his attention back to ''[[Heavenly Creatures]]''.<ref name="jackson">{{cite book|author=Brian Sibley|author-link=Brian Sibley|title=Peter Jackson: A Film-maker's Journey |publisher=Harpercollins |year=2006 |location=London |pages=236–40, 276, 324, 397 |isbn=0-00-717558-2}}</ref>

===Oliver Stone===
By 1993, Fox hired [[Don Murphy]] and [[Jane Hamsher]] as producers. [[Sam Raimi]] and [[Oliver Stone]] were being considered as possible directors,<ref name="Adam" /><ref name="Noyce" /> though Stone signed on as [[executive producer]]/co-writer with a $1 million salary.<ref name="Stone">Hughes, p.38-40</ref> On the storyline, Stone explained in December 1993, "It has the discovery of cryogenically frozen [[Vedic period|Vedic]] Apes who hold the secret numeric codes to the Bible that foretold the end of civilizations. It deals with past versus the future. My concept is that there's a code inscribed in the Bible that predicts all historical events. The apes were there at the beginning and figured it all out."<ref>{{cite magazine |author=Cindy Pearlman |url=https://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,308903,00.html |title=Monkey Business |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=1993-12-10 |access-date=2008-10-01 |archive-date=May 26, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130526104022/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,308903,00.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Stone brought [[Terry Hayes]] to write the screenplay entitled ''Return of the Apes''.<ref name="Stone" /> Set in the near future, a [[Plague (disease)|plague]] is making humans extinct. Geneticist Will Robinson discovers the plague is a genetic time bomb embedded in the [[Stone Age]]. He [[time travel]]s with a pregnant colleague named Billie Rae Diamond to a time when Palaeolithic humans were at war for the future of the planet with highly evolved apes. The apes' supreme commander is a gorilla named Drak. Robinson and Billie Rae discover a young human girl named Aiv (pronounced [[Eve]]) to be the next step in evolution. It is revealed that it was the apes that created the virus to destroy the human race. They protect her from the virus, thus ensuring the survival of the human race 102,000 years later. Billie Rae gives birth to a baby boy named [[Adam]].<ref name="Stone" />

Fox president [[Peter Chernin]] called ''Return of the Apes'' "one of the best scripts I ever read".<ref name="Stone" /> Chernin was hoping Hayes' script would create a franchise that included sequels, spin-off television shows and merchandise.<ref name="Stan" /> In March 1994, [[Arnold Schwarzenegger]] signed on as Will Robinson with the condition he had approval of director. [[Chuck Russell]] was considered as a possible director before [[Phillip Noyce]] was hired in January 1995, while pre-production was nearing commencement with a $100 million budget.<ref name="Noyce">{{cite magazine |author=Anne Thompson |author-link=Anne Thompson (film critic) |url=https://ew.com/article/1996/05/17/timeline-foxs-planet-apes-remake/ |title=The ''Apes'' of Wrath |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=1996-05-17 |access-date=December 24, 2025|archive-date=October 25, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081025070041/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,292533,00.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Stone first approached Rick Baker, who worked on Rifkin's failed remake, to design the prosthetic makeup, but eventually hired [[Stan Winston]].<ref name="Stan">{{cite magazine |author=Jeffrey Wells |url=https://ew.com/article/1994/12/23/another-remake-planet-apes/ |title=Monkey Business |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=1994-12-23 |access-date=2008-10-01 |archive-date=August 3, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140803070916/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,305006,00.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Ari">{{cite news |author=Scott Brake |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/034/034369p1.html |title=IGN FilmForce Takes You to ''Planet of the Apes''! |work=[[IGN]] |date=2001-02-27 |access-date=2008-10-02 |archive-date=December 7, 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081207180913/http://movies.ign.com/articles/034/034369p1.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

Fox became frustrated by the distance between their approach and Hayes' interpretation of Stone's ideas. As producer Don Murphy put it, "Terry wrote a ''[[Terminator (franchise)|Terminator]]'' and Fox wanted ''[[The Flintstones]]''".<ref name="Stone" /> Fox studio executive Dylan Sellers felt the script could be improved by comedy. "What if Robinson finds himself in Ape land and the Apes are trying to play [[baseball]]? But they're missing one element, like the [[pitcher]] or something." Sellers continued. "Robinson knows what they're missing and he shows them, and they all start playing."<ref name="Stone" /> Sellers refused to give up his baseball scene, and when Hayes turned in the next script, sans baseball, Sellers fired him. Dissatisfied with Sellers' decision to fire Hayes, Noyce left ''Return of the Apes'' in February 1995 to work on ''[[The Saint (1997 film)|The Saint]]''.<ref name="Noyce" /><ref name="Stone" />

===Columbus and Cameron===
Stone pursued other films of his own, Chernin was replaced by [[Thomas Rothman]], and Sellers ran a stop sign while intoxicated, slamming into a palm tree, killing his friend and Fox colleague Lewis Cherot, ultimately earning jail time.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Boyer |first=Edward J. |date=1996-12-05 |title=A Hollywood Story Written in Tears |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-12-05-me-5897-story.html |access-date=2025-07-23 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref> Meanwhile producers Murphy and Hamsher were paid off. "After they got rid of us, they brought on [[Chris Columbus (filmmaker)|Chris Columbus]]", Murphy stated. "Then I heard they did tests of apes [[skiing]], which didn't make much sense."<ref name="Murph">Hughes, p.41-43</ref> Stan Winston was still working on the makeup designs. Columbus brought [[Sam Hamm]], his co-writer on an unproduced ''[[Fantastic Four]]'' script, to write the screenplay. "We tried to do a story that was simultaneously an homage to the elements we liked from the five films, and would also incorporate a lot of material [from [[Pierre Boulle]]'s novel] that had been jettisoned from the earlier production," Hamm continued. "The first half of the script bore little resemblance to the book, but a lot of the stuff in the second half comes directly from it, or directly inspired by it."<ref name="Murph" />

Hamm's script had an ape astronaut from another planet crash-landing in [[New York Harbor]], launching a virus that will make human beings extinct. Dr. Susan Landis, who works for the [[Centers for Disease Control and Prevention]], and Alexander Troy, an [[Area 51]] scientist, use the ape's spacecraft to return to the virus' planet of origin, hoping to find an antidote. They find an urban environment where apes armed with heavy weapons hunt humans. The main villain was Lord Zaius; in contrast to [[List of Planet of the Apes characters|Dr. Zaius]], Lord Zaius was very cruel to the humans. Landis and Troy discover the antidote and return to Earth, only to find in their 74-year absence that apes have taken over the planet. "The [[Statue of Liberty]]'s once proud porcelain features have been crudely chiseled into the grotesque likeness of a great grinning ape".<ref name="Murph" />

Schwarzenegger remained attached, but Fox had mixed emotions with Hamm's script.<ref name="Murph" /> When Columbus dropped out in late 1995 to work on ''[[Jingle All the Way]]'', Fox offered the director's position to [[Roland Emmerich]] in January 1996.<ref name="Noyce" /> [[James Cameron]] was in talks during the filming of ''[[Titanic (1997 film)|Titanic]]'' as writer and producer. Cameron's version would have drawn elements from the original film and its sequel ''[[Beneath the Planet of the Apes]]''. Cameron approached [[Peter Hyams]] to direct the film, a choice that dissatisfied the studio.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://screenrant.com/planet-apes-james-cameron-arnold-schwarzenegger-plot-about/|title=What James Cameron's Planet Of The Apes Reboot Would Have Been About|first=Chrishaun|last=Baker|date=May 3, 2020|website=ScreenRant.com|access-date=July 15, 2026}}</ref> Also, following the financial and critical success of ''Titanic'', Cameron dropped out.<ref name="Murph" /> After learning about his previous involvement, Chernin and Rothman met with Peter Jackson to learn about his original Renaissance idea. Jackson turned down directing the film with Schwarzenegger and Cameron as his producer, recognizing they would probably conflict over the direction.<ref name="jackson" /> Schwarzenegger left to work on ''[[Eraser (film)|Eraser]]''.<ref name="Noyce" /> [[Michael Bay]] then turned down the director's position.<ref name="Murph" /> Jackson again turned down the project while facing the possible cancellation of ''[[The Lord of the Rings (film series)|The Lord of the Rings]]'' in 1998, because he was unenthusiastic following Roddy McDowall's death.<ref name="jackson" /> In mid-1999, the [[Hughes brothers]] were interested in directing but were committed to ''[[From Hell (film)|From Hell]]''.<ref name="Murph" />

===Pre-production===
In 1999, [[William Broyles Jr.]] turned down the chance to write the script, but decided to sign on "when I found out I could have an extensive amount of creative control". Fox projected the release date for July 2001, while Broyles sent the studio an outline and a chronicle of the fictional planet "Aschlar". Entitled ''The Visitor'' and billed as "episode one in the Chronicles of Aschlar",<ref name="Murph" /> Broyles' script caught the attention of director [[Tim Burton]], who signed on to direct the film in February 2000.<ref>{{cite news |author=Paul F. Duke |url=https://variety.com/2000/film/news/fox-goes-ape-for-burton-1117776621/ |title=Fox goes ''Ape'' for Burton |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=2000-02-22 |access-date=2008-09-30 |archive-date=October 24, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121024165708/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117776621 |url-status=live }}</ref> The film deviated from Burton's usual [[Gothic fiction|gothic]] films.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hall |first=Jacob |date=December 31, 2014 |title=Ranking the Films of Tim Burton |url=https://screencrush.com/ranking-tim-burton-movies/ |access-date=June 15, 2023 |website=[[ScreenCrush]] |archive-date=August 31, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220831065400/https://screencrush.com/ranking-tim-burton-movies/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=July 19, 2017 |title=You Blew It Up: All Planet of the Apes Movies Ranked |url=https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/planet-of-the-apes/you-blew-it-up-all-planet-of-the-apes-movies-ranke |access-date=June 15, 2023 |website=[[Paste (magazine)|Paste]] |archive-date=June 29, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220629100424/https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/planet-of-the-apes/you-blew-it-up-all-planet-of-the-apes-movies-ranke/ |url-status=live }}</ref> "I wasn't interested in doing a remake or a sequel of the original ''Planet of the Apes'' film," Burton said later. "But I was intrigued by the idea of revisiting that world. Like a lot of people, I was affected by the original film. I wanted to do a 're-imagining'."<ref name="Zan">Hughes, p.44-46</ref> [[Richard D. Zanuck]], who served as the president of the studio around the original film's release, signed on as producer in March.<ref>{{cite news |author=Paul F. Duke |url=https://variety.com/2000/film/news/zanuck-swings-back-to-apes-1117779695/ |title=Zanuck swings back to ''Apes'' |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=2000-03-21 |access-date=2008-09-30 |archive-date=October 24, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121024165756/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117779695 |url-status=live }}</ref> "This is a very emotional film for me. I [[Green-light|greenlighted]] the original ''Apes'' when I was the head of Fox in 1967."<ref>{{cite news |author=Army Archerd |author-link=Army Archerd |url=https://variety.com/2000/scene/people-news/zanucks-urge-polanski-to-return-to-u-s-1117863608/ |title=Zanucks urge Polanski to return to U.S. |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |date=2000-04-19 |access-date=2008-09-30 |archive-date=October 24, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121024165826/http://www.variety.com/article/VR1117863608 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Under Burton's direction, Broyles wrote another draft, but his script was projected at a $200 million budget. Fox wanted to cut it to $100 million.<ref name="Zan" /><ref name="Gems" /> In August 2000, two months before [[principal photography]], Fox brought [[Lawrence Konner]] and [[Mark Rosenthal (screenwriter)|Mark Rosenthal]] for rewrites.<ref name="Bro">{{cite book|author=Mark Salisbury, [[Tim Burton]] |title=Burton on Burton |publisher=[[Faber and Faber]] |year=2006 |location=[[London]] |pages=187–190 |isbn=0-571-22926-3}}</ref> Broyles "had a lot of respect with the work they [Konner and Rosenthal] did. And to think that given what I'd done and given what Tim wanted, they navigated the right course."<ref name="Zan" /> One of the considered endings had Leo Davidson crash-landing at [[Yankee Stadium]], witnessing apes playing baseball. Various alternatives were considered before the filmmakers decided on the final one.<ref name="ape" /> The production of ''Planet of the Apes'' was a difficult experience for Burton. This was largely contributed by Fox's adamant release date (July 2001), which meant that everything from pre-production to editing and visual effects work was rushed.<ref name="Zan" />

Konner and Rosenthal were rewriting the script even as sets were being constructed.<ref name="ape" /> Ari, [[Helena Bonham Carter]]'s character, was originally a princess. She was changed to "a Senator's daughter with a [[Social liberalism|liberal mentality]]".<ref>[[Helena Bonham Carter]], [[Colleen Atwood]], ''Ape Couture'', 2001, [[20th Century Fox]]</ref> One of the drafts had General Thade, [[Tim Roth]]'s character, as an [[Snowflake (gorilla)|albino gorilla]], but Burton felt chimpanzees were more frightening.<ref name="audio">[[Tim Burton]], DVD [[audio commentary]], 2001, [[20th Century Fox]]</ref> Limbo, [[Paul Giamatti]]'s character "was supposed to turn into a good guy. There was supposed to be this touching personal growth thing at the end," Giamatti reflected. "But Tim [Burton] and I both thought that was kind of lame so we decided to just leave him as a jerk into the end."<ref name="Paul" />

===Filming===
Burton wanted to begin filming in October 2000,<ref>{{cite news|author=Tim Ryan |title=Big Isle Lava Could Lure Film ''Visitor'' |work=[[Honolulu Star-Bulletin]] |date=2000-08-14 }}</ref> but it was pushed back to November 6, 2000, and ended in April 2001.<ref name="Gems" /><ref name="April" /> Filming for ''Planet of the Apes'' began at [[Lake Powell]], where parts of the original film were shot. Due to a local [[drought]], production crews had to pump in extra water.<ref name="audio" /> The film was mostly shot at [[Sony Pictures Studios]] in [[Culver City, California]], while other [[filming location]]s included [[Lava field|lava plain]]s in [[Hawaii]] and [[Trona Pinnacles]] at [[Ridgecrest, California|Ridgecrest]]. To preserve secrecy, the [[shooting script]] did not include the ending.<ref name="Bro" /><ref>{{cite news |author=KJB |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/034/034351p1.html |title=The Island of the Apes |work=[[IGN]] |date=2000-06-07 |access-date=2008-10-02 |archive-date=May 22, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110522135845/http://movies.ign.com/articles/034/034351p1.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Fox considered using [[computer-generated imagery]] to create the apes, but Burton insisted on using [[prosthetic makeup]] designed by [[Rick Baker (makeup artist)|Rick Baker]].<ref name="Gems" /> Baker was previously involved with Adam Rifkin's and Oliver Stone's unproduced remakes. Burton commented, "I have a relationship with both of them ([[Stan Winston|Winston]] and Baker), so that decision was hard," he says. "Stan worked on ''[[Edward Scissorhands]]'' and Baker did [[Martin Landau]]'s makeup [as [[Bela Lugosi|Béla Lugosi]] in ''[[Ed Wood (film)|Ed Wood]]'']".<ref name="sequel">{{cite magazine |author=Benjamin Svetkey |url=https://ew.com/article/2001/04/27/mark-wahlberg-stars-reimagined-planet-apes/ |title=Ape Crusaders |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=2001-04-27 |access-date=2008-10-01 |archive-date=March 5, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140305110944/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,281017,00.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

On his hiring, Baker explained, "[The original 1968 film] was such a landmark makeup picture, and I just had the hardest time turning it down. I'm a big makeup geek and a big ape geek, and I just thought, I really have to do this." Baker previously worked with designing ape makeup on ''[[King Kong (1976 film)|King Kong]]'', ''[[Greystoke: The Legend of Tarzan, Lord of the Apes]]'', ''[[Gorillas in the Mist]]'', and the 1998 remake of ''[[Mighty Joe Young (1998 film)|Mighty Joe Young]]''.<ref>{{cite magazine |author=Josh Wolk |url=https://ew.com/article/2000/12/20/meet-makeup-pro-behind-grinch-and-planet-apes/ |title=About Face |magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] |date=2000-12-18 |access-date=2008-10-01 |archive-date=May 26, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130526110041/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,92065,00.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The makeup took ranged from 2 to 4 & 1/2 hours to apply and an hour to remove. Burton explained, "it's like going to the [[dentist]] at two in the morning and having people poke at you for hours. Then you wear an ape costume until nine at night."<ref name="Gems">{{cite news|author=Richard Natale|url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-may-06-ca-59780-story.html|date=2001-05-06|title=Remaking, Not Aping, An Original|work=[[Los Angeles Times]]|access-date=2008-09-29|archive-date=August 22, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160822181633/http://articles.latimes.com/2001/may/06/entertainment/ca-59780|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="ape">Salisbury, Burton, p.191-202</ref> Burton was adamant that the apes should be substantially "more animal-like; flying through trees, climb walls, swing out of windows, and go ape shit when angry."<ref name="ape" /> For a month and a half before shooting started, the actors who portrayed apes attended "ape school" under the choreography of [[Terry Notary]]. [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM), [[Rhythm & Hues Studios]] (R&H) and [[Animal Logic]] were commissioned for the [[visual effects]] sequences. [[Rick Heinrichs]] served as the [[production designer]] and [[Colleen Atwood]] did [[costume design]].<ref>{{cite news |date=2000-12-24 |author=Amy Barrett |title=Going ''Ape'' |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/magazine/home/20001224mag-salientfacts.html |access-date=September 15, 2021 |archive-date=September 15, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210915063447/https://archive.nytimes.com/www.nytimes.com/library/magazine/home/20001224mag-salientfacts.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The helmets from ''[[Starship Troopers (film)|Starship Troopers]]'' were reused for the gorilla SWAT team in the final scene.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://whatculture.com/film/10-horror-movie-props-you-didnt-know-were-reused|title=10 Horror Movie Props You Didn't Know Were Reused|first=Andrew|last=Pollard|date=July 16, 2021|website=WhatCulture.com|access-date=July 15, 2026}}</ref>

To compose the [[film score]], Burton hired regular collaborator [[Danny Elfman]], who had previously been set as composer when [[Adam Rifkin]] was attached to do his own remake of the original back in 1989 before various filmmakers, including but eventually Burton himself, were attempted to do so later on.<ref name="Adam" /> Elfman noted that his work on ''Planet of the Apes'' contained more [[percussion instrument]]s than usual.<ref>[[Danny Elfman]], ''Chimp Symphony'', 2001, [[20th Century Fox]]</ref>

During filming, Roth held a grudge against Heston due to the latter's work with the [[National Rifle Association]]: "It was very difficult for me. On one level, there's the man and he's my [character's] dad. But on the other level, the whole NRA thing is what it is now. I'm so against it, very vocally so. But it was inappropriate for the workplace. If I'm going to talk to him, I'll talk to him outside the workplace. So it was just two guys in makeup doing a scene."<ref>{{cite web|last=Horn|first=Steven|title=INTERVIEW WITH TIM ROTH|date=1 August 2001|publisher=[[IGN]]|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/08/01/interview-with-tim-roth|access-date=11 August 2015|archive-date=June 4, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210604172908/https://www.ign.com/articles/2001/08/01/interview-with-tim-roth|url-status=live}}</ref>  Roth later claimed he would not have appeared in the film had he known he would be sharing a scene with Heston.<ref>{{cite web|title=Roth's Fury At "Monster" Heston|date=27 April 2003|publisher=[[Contactmusic.com]]|url=http://www.contactmusic.com/tim-roth/news/roth.s-fury-at-.monster.-heston|access-date=11 August 2015|archive-date=September 24, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150924125850/http://www.contactmusic.com/tim-roth/news/roth.s-fury-at-.monster.-heston|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Project APE ===
To help promote the release of ''Planet of the Apes'', [[20th Century Fox]] collaborated with [[Geocaching]]<ref>{{Cite web|title=20 years of Project A.P.E. – Official Blog|date=May 25, 2021 |url=https://www.geocaching.com/blog/2021/05/20-years-of-project-a-p-e/|access-date=2022-01-17|language=en-US|archive-date=January 17, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220117182950/https://www.geocaching.com/blog/2021/05/20-years-of-project-a-p-e/|url-status=live}}</ref> and released an [[Digital marketing|internet marketing]] campaign nicknamed "Project APE", that involved people going out into the real world. Geocaching was barely a year old at the time, and was just beginning to become more well known. The promotion's backstory, which actually had no connection to the movie, was that a group of renegade humans were placing artefacts (geocaches) around the globe in an effort to reveal an '''A'''lternate '''P'''rimate '''E'''volution. Over the course of several weeks in 2001, a cache containing props and memorabilia from the movie (prop [[blindfold]]s, prop knives, posters, trading cards and more)  was released every week. However, the cache's location was not given, but clues were given throughout the week that narrowed down the location until the cache's coordinates were released on a Friday. It was then a race to get to the cache, with the first person arriving at the location getting a pick of the goodies in the cache. The caches were large [[Ammunition box|ammo boxes]], with "Project APE" spray painted on the front. Fourteen caches were placed in a series of missions numbered 1–12 (one was "Special Movie" for the movie premiere in [[New York City|New York]] and there was a Mission 10a & 10b, with 10b being another cache with the [[London]] [[premiere]] tickets). Evidence points to a potential Mission 13, but no cache page has been found for it. Most of the caches did not last beyond a couple of finds, as most of them were muggled (stolen). Only two caches were active in 2021, with one located outside of [[Seattle|Seattle, Washington]], and the other in [[Brazil]].

===Critical response===
On review aggregation website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Planet of the Apes'' has an approval rating of 42% based on 201 reviews, with an average rating of 5.7/10. The site's critical consensus reads, "This remake of ''Planet of the Apes'' can't compare to the original in some critics' minds, but the striking visuals and B-movie charms may win you over."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1108704_planet_of_the_apes |title=Planet of the Apes (2001) |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=April 30, 2024 |archive-date=April 7, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200407214240/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1108704_planet_of_the_apes |url-status=live }}</ref> On [[Metacritic]] the film has an average score of 50 out of 100, based on 34 critics, indicating "mixed or average reviews".<ref>{{cite web |work=[[Metacritic]] |title=Planet of the Apes reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/video/titles/planetoftheapes |access-date=2010-04-17 |archive-date=July 4, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100704065853/http://www.metacritic.com/video/titles/planetoftheapes/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://m.cinemascore.com |archive-url=http://webarchive.loc.gov/all/20170916153548/https://m.cinemascore.com/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=September 16, 2017 |title=CinemaScore |work=cinemascore.com |access-date=August 3, 2021 }}</ref>

==Future==
===Cancelled sequel===
Fox stated that if ''Planet of the Apes'' was a financial success, then a [[sequel]] would be commissioned.<ref name="sequel" /> When asked whether he would be interested in working on a follow-up, director Tim Burton replied, "I'd rather jump out a window."<ref name="Zan" /> Mark Wahlberg and Helena Bonham Carter would have returned if Burton had decided to make another ''Apes'' film.<ref name="Mark">{{cite news |author=Steven Horn |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/301/301862p1.html |title=Interview with Mark Wahlberg |work=[[IGN]] |date=2001-07-31 |access-date=2008-10-02 |archive-date=January 13, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120113044113/http://movies.ign.com/articles/301/301862p1.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |author=Steven Horn |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/301/301761p1.html |title=Helena Bonham Carter Goes Ape |work=[[IGN]] |date=2001-07-26 |access-date=2008-10-02 |archive-date=January 13, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120113042806/http://movies.ign.com/articles/301/301761p1.html |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Paul Giamatti]] had been interested in reprising his role. "I think it'd be great to have apes driving cars, smoking cigars," Giamatti said. The film project went on hold when Cameron pulled out, but Fox Interactive remained confident a remake would progress eventually and continued with the game.<ref name=Adams>{{cite web |url= https://www.ign.com/articles/2000/02/10/igndc-monkeys-around-with-planet-of-the-apes-producer-cos-lazouras |title= IGNDC Monkeys Around with Planet of the Apes Producer Cos Lazouras |last1= Adams |first1= Tom |date= February 9, 2000 |website= www.ign.com |publisher= [[IGN]] |access-date= August 23, 2014 |archive-date= August 21, 2014 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20140821080803/http://www.ign.com/articles/2000/02/10/igndc-monkeys-around-with-planet-of-the-apes-producer-cos-lazouras |url-status= live }}</ref> Fox contracted French company [[Visiware]] as [[video game developer|developer]]; with the film on hold, the creators developed their own story inspired by Boulle's novel and the original films. Rental revenue to date (...) 40.8}}</ref> This [[THX]] certified two-disc DVD release features rare [[Nuon (DVD technology)|Nuon]] technology, which can only be used on Nuon-enhanced DVD players.<ref name="DigitalBits">{{cite web|url=http://archive.thedigitalbits.com/reviews2/planetoftheapes2001.html|title=DVD Review - Planet of the Apes: Special Edition (2001) - The Digital Bits|access-date=March 24, 2022|archive-date=March 24, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220324205743/http://archive.thedigitalbits.com/reviews2/planetoftheapes2001.html|url-status=live}}</ref> These Nuon features include viddies and various zoom points during the film.<ref name="DigitalBits" /> The first disc features audio commentaries with [[Tim Burton]] and [[Danny Elfman]], cast and crew profiles and enhanced viewing mode.<ref name="DigitalBits" /> It also contains a [[DTS, Inc.|DTS]] 5.1 audio track and DVD-ROM.<ref name="DigitalBits" /> On the second disc, there are extended scenes, an [[HBO]] special called ''The Making of Planet of the Apes'', behind-the-scenes footage, theatrical trailers, TV spots, previews for ''[[Moulin Rouge!]]'' and ''[[Dr. Dolittle 2]]'', posters and press kit, a music promo, screen tests, galleries, multi-angle featurettes and more.<ref name="DigitalBits" />

The film premiered on [[Blu-ray]] on February 13, 2007.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/dvd/news/18309-fox-announces-new-blu-ray-disc-titles|title=Fox Announces New Blu-ray Disc Titles|website=Comingsoon.net|date=January 8, 2007|access-date=July 15, 2026}}</ref>
