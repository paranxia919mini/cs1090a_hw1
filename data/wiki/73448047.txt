'''''Kokomo City''''' is a 2023 American [[documentary film|documentary]] film, directed, produced, and edited by [[D. Smith]]. It explores the lives of four Black [[transgender|trans]] women and their experiences as sex workers in New York and Georgia. [[Lena Waithe]] serves as an executive producer.

The film had its world premiere at the [[2023 Sundance Film Festival|Sundance Film Festival]] on January 21, 2023, and was released in theaters on July 28, 2023, by [[Magnolia Pictures]].

==Plot==
The film explores the lives of  [[Daniella Carter]], Dominique Silver, [[Koko Da Doll]], and Liyah Mitchell in New York and Georgia through interviews and re-enactments with actors.<ref name="Bugbee 2023"/> During the film, Carter, Silver, Doll, and Mitchell discuss their experiences as Black trans women and as [[sex worker]]s.<ref name="Gyarkye 2023"/><ref name="Rechtshaffen 2023"/> Smith also interviews Black men.<ref name="Gyarkye 2023"/>

==Production==
D. Smith had a successful career in the music industry, producing songs for [[Lil Wayne]], [[Ciara]], [[Keri Hilson]], [[Billy Porter (actor)|Billy Porter]] and [[André 3000]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2023/film/news/kokomo-city-director-d-smith-sundance-winner-1235505907/|title=Breakout Director D. Smith, Who 'Lost Everything' When She Transitioned, Just Conquered Sundance|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|first=Matt|last=Donnelly|date=January 30, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref> However, when she began to transition, she was essentially forced out of the industry, and ended up broke and homeless.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://outinjersey.net/homeless-after-the-music-business-rejected-her-as-trans-d-smith-is-back/|title=Homeless after the music business rejected her as trans, D. Smith is back|website=Out in Jersey|first=Chris|last=Azzopardi|date=February 15, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref> Smith had the idea for a documentary film revolving around sex work, after wondering what would happen if she had to turn to it to sustain herself, and those who had no other options.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ebony.com/filmmaker-d-smiths-kokomo-city-gives-transgender-women-a-real-voice/|title=Filmmaker D. Smith's Kokomo City Gives Transgender Women a Real Voice|website=[[Ebony (magazine)|Ebony]]|first=Delania|last=Dixon|date=January 20, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref> Smith was still homeless when she began working on the project, with a camera being purchased by a host of where she was once staying, and a laptop by a producer.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://businessdoceurope.com/berlinale-panorama-interview-kokomo-city-by-d-smith/|title=Berlinale Panorama interview: Kokomo City by D Smith|website=Business Doc Europe|first=Geoffrey|last=Macnab|date=February 22, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref>

To find subjects for the film, Smith turned to [[Instagram]] and [[YouTube]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://povmagazine.com/d-smith-talks-kokomo-city-and-trans-representation/|title=D. Smith Talks KOKOMO CITY and Trans Representation|website=POV|first=Pat|last=Mullen|date=January 21, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref>  Smith wanted her subjects to feel comfortable, telling them to talk as if they were talking to her and shooting at lower angles to make it look as if they were at a sleepover.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2023/03/kokomo-city-sundance-berlin-award-winning-documentary-magnolia-pictures-director-d-smith-subjects-daniella-carter-dominque-silver-interviews-1235275833/|title=Breakthrough Director D. Smith On Her Sundance And Berlin-Winning 'Kokomo City,' An Unfiltered View Of Black Trans Women|website=Deadline Hollywood|first=Matthew|last=Carey|date=March 1, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref> [[Lena Waithe]] and her producing partner, [[Rishi Rajani]] boarded the film as executive producers after having been blown away after viewing the film.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2023/film/features/lena-waithe-hillman-grad-sundance-mary-tyler-moore-the-chi-1235512276/|title=How Two Sundance-Winning Films Forecast the Future for Lena Waithe's Hillman Grad Production Company|website=Variety|first=Angelique|last=Jackson|date=February 3, 2023|access-date=April 2, 2023}}</ref>

==Reception==
{{RT prose|score=99|count=76|average=7.7|consensus=''Kokomo City'' is a rousing docu effort that illuminates trans lives within a world of adverse circumstance and invariable optimism.|access-date=April 18, 2024}}<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes|title=Kokomo City|id=kokomo_city|type=m|access-date=April 18, 2024}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a weighted average, assigned the film a score of 79 out of 100, based on 20 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/kokomo-city|title=''Kokomo City''|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=August 16, 2023}}</ref>

Peter Debruge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote, "This doc rocks, using music to set the tempo for its snappy mix of head-turning talking heads, tongue-in-cheek reenactments and outside-the-box supporting visuals."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Debruge |first=Peter |date=2023-01-27 |title='Kokomo City' Review: Trans Sex Workers Reframe Their Narrative in D. Smith's Raucous Doc |url=https://variety.com/2023/film/reviews/kokomo-city-review-d-smith-1235504198/ |access-date=2023-07-06 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Lovia Gyarkye of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' wrote, "these women share their experiences with familial rejection, masculine sexual anxiety and a retaliatory world threatened by the dissolution of the gender binary. Violence - both real and anticipated - is the most obvious thematic thread, but competing for space and attention is beauty."<ref name="Gyarkye 2023">{{Cite web |last=Gyarkye |first=Lovia |date=2023-02-16 |title='Kokomo City' Review: A Striking Kaleidoscopic Portrait of Black Trans Women |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/kokomo-city-review-trans-sex-workers-1235327058/ |access-date=2023-07-05 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |language=en-US}}</ref> Mey Rude of ''[[Out (magazine)|Out]]'' praised the film as an example of Black trans women "getting to be their unfiltered selves on-screen...and not hold anything back."<ref name="rude2023smith">{{cite web|last=Rude|first=Mey|url=https://www.out.com/out100/disruptors/d-smith|title=D. Smith|website=[[Out (magazine)|Out]]|access-date=November 20, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231027155608/https://www.out.com/out100/disruptors/d-smith|archive-date=October 27, 2023|url-status=live}}</ref>

In a review for the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', Michael Rechtshaffen writes, "At once bracingly candid and buoyantly energetic, the first feature by Grammy-winning trans singer-songwriter D. Smith clearly earns the trust of its personable quartet, who require little prodding to share their experiences and philosophies as they navigate decidedly nontraditional paths."<ref name="Rechtshaffen 2023">{{cite news |last1=Rechtshaffen |first1=Michael |title=Review: In 'Kokomo City,' trans sex workers, rowdy and risky, get a compassionate profile |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/movies/story/2023-08-04/kokomo-city-review-documentary-d-smith |access-date=18 October 2023 |work=[[Los Angeles Times]] |date=4 August 2023}}</ref> Wendy Ide wrote for a review in ''[[The Observer]]'', "For all the exaggerated winks in the music choices and provocative shots of beautifully lit buttocks, the film is an open and celebratory space in which the women can tell their stories. Some of them are hilarious, others bruising, all are painfully forthright about the all-too-real dangers they face."<ref name="Ide 2023" /> According to Teo Bugbee in ''[[The New York Times]]'', "Smith's approach grants respect to women who are often dehumanized, even in their most intimate settings."<ref name="Bugbee 2023">{{cite news |last1=Bugbee |first1=Teo |title='Kokomo City' Review: Dispatches From the Down Low |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2023/07/27/movies/kokomo-city-review.html |access-date=18 October 2023 |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=July 27, 2023}}</ref> Writing for Collider, Lisa Laman observed that 'Kokomo City' flourished as a work of subverting cinema norms by eschewing traditional documentary standards of othering marginalized voices on-screen, noting 'This is a movie where the personal stories of Mitchell, Carter, Silver, and Doll guide the feature along above all else. It’s a welcome departure from other documentaries about trans individuals and sex workers that largely concentrated on folks outside of those fields.' <ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20230825205522/https://collider.com/kokomo-city-documentary/ collider.com]</ref>

The film received the Panorama Audience Award at the 73rd Berlin International Film Festival<ref name=HR/> and also won the NEXT Audience and Innovator Awards at the 2023 Sundance Festival.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/sundance-2023-award-winners-list-1235311499/|title=Sundance: 'A Thousand and One,' Nikki Giovanni Doc Take Top Jury Prizes|website=Hollywood Reporter|date=January 27, 2023|first=Mia |last=Galuppo|access-date=April 21, 2023}}</ref>

==Koko Da Doll==
On April 18, 2023, Rasheeda Williams, better known as [[Koko Da Doll]], was found dead from a gunshot wound in [[Southwest Atlanta]]. Smith said Williams "will inspire generations to come and will never be forgotten." Other tributes came from her costars, one of the film's producers Harris Doran, and the Sundance Film Festival.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Jackson |first=Angelique |date=April 20, 2023 |title=''Kokomo City'' Documentary Star Koko da Doll Found Dead at 35 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |url=https://variety.com/2023/film/obituaries-people-news/koko-da-doll-dead-kokomo-city-documentary-1235590500/ |access-date=April 21, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/koko-da-doll-dead-kokomo-city-star-35-1235398868/|title=Koko Da Doll, Star of 'Kokomo City' Sundance Documentary, Found Dead in Atlanta|website=Hollywood Reporter|date=April 21, 2023|first1=Carly |last1=Thomas|first2=Etan |last2=Vlessing|access-date=April 21, 2023}}</ref> After her death, the film was dedicated to her.<ref name="Ide 2023">{{cite news |last1=Ide |first1=Wendy |title=Kokomo City review – the perilous, gutsy lives of four Black trans sex workers |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2023/aug/06/kokomo-city-review-the-perilous-gutsy-lives-of-four-black-trans-sex-workers |access-date=20 October 2023 |work=[[The Observer]] |date=6 August 2023}}</ref><ref name="Smith August 2023">{{cite news |last1=Smith |first1=D. |title='Kokomo City' Filmmaker D.
