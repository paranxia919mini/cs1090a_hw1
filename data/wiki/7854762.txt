'''''The House with Laughing Windows''''' ({{langx|it|La casa dalle finestre che ridono}}) is a 1976 Italian [[giallo]] [[Thriller film|thriller]] [[Horror film|horror]] film co-written and directed by [[Pupi Avati]]. The film was shot in Lido degli Scacchi in the [[Ferrara]] province of the [[Emilia-Romagna]] region in northern Italy.

== Plot ==
Stefano ([[Lino Capolicchio]]) arrives in a village of the [[Valli di Comacchio]] area where he has been employed to restore a [[fresco]] depicting what appears to be the martyrdom of [[Saint Sebastian]], which has been painted on a rotting wall of the local church by a mysterious, long-dead artist named Legnani. While temporarily taking up residence in the house that had been previously owned by the two sisters of the deceased painter, Stefano begins a romance with a new, beautiful schoolteacher, Francesca ([[Francesca Marciano]]), meanwhile learning from various townspeople that the painter had been a madman who had derived his art from real life. Specifically, Stefano learns that the artist – assisted by his two equally-insane sisters – had been a killer who brutally tortured people to death as inspiration for his horrific paintings – a practice that had likely been used for the very painting he is in process of restoring. As Stefano is discouraged for his task throughout the town, some of the villagers are brutally killed – including his employer – and he comes to suspect that their murderer is trying to deter him from discovering the full truth behind the artist and his ominous legacy within the sleepy community.

==Production==
{{expand section|date=December 2014}} 
The idea for ''The House With Laughing Windows'' came from a story director [[Pupi Avati]] had heard as a child involving the exhumation of a priest in the village of [[Sasso Marconi]].{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=159}} An early draft of the script was developed in the early 1970s under the title ''La luce dell'ultimo piano'' which was written by both Avati and Antonion Troisio.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=160}} It was produced by [[Antonio Cuomo]] who later backed out of the project.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=160}} Avati began working on the script again after the financial failure of his film ''[[House of Pleasure for Women]]'' and made several changes to the character and story.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=160}} The final story and screenplay was credited to Pupi Avati and Antonio Avati.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}}

''The House with Laughing Windows'' was the first film produced by director Pupi Avati's company A.M.A. Film.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}}{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=158}} The film was shot over five weeks between April and May 1976 in [[Comacchio]] and [[Minerbio]], [[Ferrara]], Italy.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}}{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=158}} Although the credits state Incir-De Paolis Studios in Rome, this was purely listed for bureaucratic reasons.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}}{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=158}}{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=162}}

==Release==
''The House with Laughing Windows'' was distributed theatrically in Italy by Euro International Films where on 16 August 1976.{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}} On its domestic release, the film grossed a total of 722,135,201 [[Italian lire]].{{sfn|Curti|2017|p=157}}

== Reception ==
{{expand section|date=December 2014}}
Mikel J. Koven, writing in ''La Dolce Morte'', argues that through the presence of the outsider Stefano, an art historian visiting the village who uncovers the long unsolved crime, the film explores a theme common to many [[Giallo|gialli]]: the shattering of the idyllic isolation of Italian village life by the forces of modernity.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Koven |first=Mikel J. |title=La dolce morte: vernacular cinema and the Italian giallo film |date=2006 |publisher=Scarecrow Press, Inc |year=2006 |isbn=978-0-8108-5870-1 |location=Lanham, [Maryland] Toronto, [Ontario] Oxford |pages=57}}</ref>

The film has been received well by contemporary critics. [[AllMovie]]'s review of the film was favorable, giving it a rating of 3 out of 5, and writing that "[though] fans of typical Italian horror films may find ''House with the Windows That Laugh'' [sic] lacking in the stylistic excesses of many of its contemporary companion pieces, it exceeds its contemporaries in almost every other area," and that it was "imbued with an overwhelming sense of dread that grows to an almost unbearable pitch."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://allmovie.com/movie/v145820 |title=House with Laughing Windows – Trailers, Reviews, Synopsis, Showtimes and Cast – AllMovie |last=Buchanan |first=Jason |work=[[AllRovi|AllMovie]] |access-date=29 July 2012}}</ref>

On review aggregate [[Rotten Tomatoes]], no score has yet been reached with only two critical ratings submitted.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/house_with_laughing_windows/|title=La Casa dalle finestre che ridono (The House of the Laughing Windows)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=10 December 2012}}</ref> The current ratings – both "fresh" — are from [[Emanuel Levy]] of emanuellevy.com, who gave the film a 3 out of 5, and Anton Bitel of Eye for Film, who gave the film a 4.5 out of 5, and wrote, "certainly a gripping [[giallo]], but also an intelligent allegory of post-war Italy's struggles to emerge from the [[Fascist]] outrages of its recent past."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.eyeforfilm.co.uk/review/the-house-with-laughing-windows-film-review-by-anton-bitel|title=The House With Laughing Windows|author=Anton Bitel|access-date=10 December 2012}}</ref>

== Availability and legacy ==

On 9 August 2011 ''[[Trailers From Hell!]]'' uploaded a video segment on the film to YouTube, in which [[Eli Roth]] discusses his feelings on its merits and the [[giallo]] genre, opining that it is "a great place to start [into giallo]" and "amongst the best" of the genre, ultimately comparing the film to the work of [[Dario Argento]], [[Mario Bava]], and [[Lucio Fulci]] — "the masters of the giallo."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gb559bhuJjc |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211213/gb559bhuJjc |archive-date=2021-12-13 |url-status=live|title=Eli Roth on THE HOUSE WITH THE LAUGHING WINDOWS|work=[[Trailers From Hell!]]|date=30 September 2013 |access-date=4 March 2016}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

''The House with Laughing Windows'' was released on [[DVD region code#1|Region 1]] DVD on 18 March 2003 through [[Paradox Entertainment]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.amazon.com/House-Laughing-Windows-Lino-Capolicchio/dp/B00008975Y/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1355191448&sr=8-1&keywords=the+house+with+the+laughing+windows|title=The House with Laughing Windows in Movies & TV|work=[[Amazon.com]]| date=11 March 2003 |access-date=10 December 2012}}</ref> On 5 January 2010 the film was released on [[DVD region code#2|Region 2]] DVD through Metrodome.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cduniverse.com/productinfo.asp?pid=8588917&style=movie|title=House With Laughing Windows DVD|work=[[CD Universe]]|access-date=10 December 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.tower.com/house-with-laughing-windows-dvd/wapi/107082265|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130205065737/http://www.tower.com/house-with-laughing-windows-dvd/wapi/107082265|url-status=dead|archive-date=5 February 2013|title=The House With Laughing Windows (Region 2) (Import) (DVD)|work=[[Tower.com]]|access-date=10 December 2012}}</ref>

It was screened as an outstanding example of its genre at the British Film Institute in November 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://whatson.bfi.org.uk/Online/default.asp?doWork::WScontent::loadArticle=Load&BOparam::WScontent::loadArticle::permalink=housewithlaughingwindows&BOparam::WScontent::loadArticle::context_id=|title=Buy cinema tickets for The House with Laughing Windows {{!}} BFI Southbank|website=BFI site|access-date=2019-11-29}}{{dead link|date=October 2023|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>
