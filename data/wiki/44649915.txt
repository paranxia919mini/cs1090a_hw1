In December 2014, Toho announced plans for a new domestic ''Godzilla'' film. Anno and Higuchi were announced as the directors in March 2015. Principal photography began in September 2015 and ended in October 2015. Inspiration for the film was drawn from the [[2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami|March 2011 earthquake, tsunami]], and [[Fukushima nuclear disaster|nuclear disaster]] in Japan.<ref name="box1"/> Production had a budget of {{JPY|1.3 billion}}, with advertising bringing the film's total budget to {{JPY|2.19 billion}}.{{sfn|''Kinema Junpo''|2017|p=36}}

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries are 400 to 700 words only. The current word count is 573 words. -->
In 2016, the [[Japan Coast Guard]] investigates an abandoned yacht in [[Tokyo Bay]]. The boat is soon destroyed, and the [[Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line]] is flooded with blood. After seeing a [[viral video]] of the incident, [[Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary]] Rando Yaguchi believes it was caused by a living creature, which is confirmed as news reports reveal its tail emerging from the ocean. Shortly thereafter, the creature moves inland, crawling through the [[Kamata, Tokyo|Kamata district]] and the [[Shinagawa]] area of [[Tokyo]], leaving a path of death and destruction during a disorganized and chaotic evacuation. The creature quickly evolves into a [[Bipedalism|bipedal form]], but overheats and returns to the sea.

The government officials focus on military strategy and civilian safety; Yaguchi is put in charge of a task force researching the creature. With high radiation readings from the creature's path, the task force realizes that it is energized by [[nuclear fission]]. The [[United States|U.S.]] sends a special envoy, Kayoco Anne Patterson, who reveals that Goro Maki, a disgraced, anti-nuclear [[zoology]] professor, studied mutations caused by radioactive contamination, predicting the appearance of the creature. Both American and Japanese scientific circles disbelieved Maki. The U.S. then prevented him from publicly disclosing his conclusions. The abandoned yacht in Tokyo Bay belonged to Maki, who left his research notes, jumbled into a code, on the boat before disappearing.

The creature—named "[[Shin Godzilla (character)|Godzilla]]" after Maki's research—reappears in its fourth form, twice its original size, making landfall near [[Kamakura]]. The [[Japan Self-Defense Forces]] mobilize but prove ineffective as Godzilla breaks through their defenses into Tokyo. The U.S. intervenes with a massively destructive airstrike plan, prompting the evacuation of civilians and government personnel. Godzilla is wounded with [[Massive Ordnance Penetrator|MOP]] "bunker-buster" bombs but responds with destructive atomic rays fired from its mouth and dorsal plates, destroying a helicopter carrying the prime minister, along with top government officials, and incinerating large swaths of Tokyo. Depleted of its energy, Godzilla goes into hibernation.

Yaguchi's team discovers that Godzilla's plates and blood work as a cooling system, theorizing that it could use a [[Coagulation|coagulating]] agent to freeze Godzilla. Analyzing tissue samples reveals that Godzilla is an ever-evolving creature capable of reproducing asexually. The [[United Nations]], aware of this, informs Japan that [[thermonuclear weapon]]s will be used against Godzilla should the Japanese fail to subdue it in a few days; evacuations are ordered in multiple prefectures in preparation. Unwilling to see [[Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki|nuclear weapons detonated in Japan]] again, Patterson uses her political connections to buy time for Yaguchi's team, in which the interim government has little faith.

Yaguchi's team manages to decipher Goro Maki's encoded research using origami. The team adjusts its plan and procures the means to conduct its deep-freeze plan with international support. Hours before the planned nuclear attack, Japan enacts the deep-freeze plan. Godzilla is provoked into expelling its atomic breath and energy against [[General Atomics MQ-1 Predator|Predator]] and [[General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper|Reaper]] drones. The team then detonates nearby buildings and sends unmanned trains loaded with explosives toward Godzilla's feet, subduing it, and enabling tankers full of coagulant to inject it into Godzilla's mouth. Many are killed, but Godzilla is eventually frozen solid.

In the aftermath, scientists discover that Godzilla's isotope has a [[half-life]] of twenty days, and that Tokyo can soon be rebuilt. The international community agrees to cancel the nuclear attack on the condition that, should Godzilla reawaken, an immediate thermonuclear strike will be executed. On Godzilla's tail, [[humanoid]] creatures appear frozen in the process of emerging.

==Themes==
Whereas the [[Godzilla (1954 film)|original ''Godzilla'' film]] was conceived as a metaphor for the [[atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki]] and the dangers of [[nuclear weapon]]s,<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2016/08/03/films/film-reviews/shin-godzilla-metaphorical-monster-returns/#.V_nKLCRsnIU|title='Shin Godzilla': The metaphorical monster returns|last=Schilling|first=Mark|date=August 3, 2016|work=[[Japan Times]]|access-date=October 8, 2016|archive-date=October 6, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161006041417/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2016/08/03/films/film-reviews/shin-godzilla-metaphorical-monster-returns/#.V_nKLCRsnIU|url-status=live}}</ref> ''Shin Godzilla'' drew inspiration from the 2011 [[Fukushima nuclear disaster]] and the [[2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami|Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami]]. Many critics noted similarities to those events.<ref name="box1"/> Mark Schilling of ''[[The Japan Times]]'' wrote that the Godzilla creature serves "as an ambulatory tsunami, earthquake and nuclear reactor, leaving radioactive contamination in his wake".<ref name=":0"/> Roland Kelts, the author of ''Japanamerica'', felt that the "mobilizing blue-suited civil servants and piles of broken planks and debris quite nakedly echo scenes of the aftermath of the great Tohoku earthquake, tsunami and nuclear disaster."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://thediplomat.com/2016/06/godzilla-resurgence-japan-reboots-its-most-iconic-monster/|title=Godzilla Resurgence: Japan Reboots Its Most Iconic Monster|last=DeHart|first=Jonathan|date=June 6, 2016|work=The Diplomat|access-date=October 8, 2016|archive-date=October 12, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161012093629/http://thediplomat.com/2016/06/godzilla-resurgence-japan-reboots-its-most-iconic-monster/|url-status=live}}</ref> Matt Alt of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' drew similar parallels with "the sight of blue-jumpsuited government spokesmen convening emergency press conferences ... [and] a stunned man quietly regarding mountains of debris, something that could have been lifted straight out of television footage of the hardest-hit regions up north. Even the sight of the radioactive monster's massive tail swishing over residential streets evokes memories of the fallout sent wafting over towns and cities in the course of Fukushima Daiichi's meltdown."<ref name="ny"/>

Robert Rath from Zam argued that ''Shin Godzilla'' is a [[satire]] of Japanese politics, and likened the protagonist Rando Yaguchi to the Fukushima plant manager [[Masao Yoshida (nuclear engineer)|Masao Yoshida]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.zam.com/article/977/godzilla-resurgence-is-actually-a-biting-political-satire|title=Godzilla Resurgence is actually a biting political satire|last=Rath|first=Robert|date=September 1, 2016|work=Zam|access-date=October 8, 2016|archive-date=October 10, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161010042309/http://www.zam.com/article/977/godzilla-resurgence-is-actually-a-biting-political-satire|url-status=dead}}</ref> William Tsutsui, author of ''Godzilla on My Mind'', wrote in the ''Arkansas Democrat-Gazette'' that "''Shin Godzilla'' leaves no doubt that the greatest threat to Japan comes not from without but from within, from a geriatric, fossilized government bureaucracy unable to act decisively or to stand up resolutely to foreign pressure."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2016/oct/07/shin-godzilla-20161007/?f=entertainment|title=Review: Shin Godzilla|last=Tsutsui|first=William|work=Arkansas Online|date=October 7, 2016|access-date=September 16, 2017|archive-date=September 12, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170912191639/http://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2016/oct/07/shin-godzilla-20161007/?f=entertainment|url-status=live}}</ref> In his review for ''[[Forbes]]'', Ollie Barder wrote that the film depicted the Japanese government's "complex and corpulent bureaucratic ways ... unable to deal with a crisis in any kind of efficient or fluid way", noting that the government members use the hierarchical system to protect their positions at the expense of citizens' lives.<ref name="barder"/> According to Schilling, the government officials, Self-Defense Forces officers and others working to defeat Godzilla are portrayed as hardworking and intelligent, despite "some initial bumbling".<ref name=":0"/>

Then-[[Prime Minister of Japan|prime minister]] [[Shinzō Abe]] spoke positively of the film's pro-nationalist themes, stating, "I think that [Godzilla's] popularity is rooted in the unwavering support that the public has for the Self-Defense Forces."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/asia_pacific/new-godzilla-film-imagines-a-strong-japan-pushing-back-against-the-us/2016/09/23/ddd7d5c4-7f70-11e6-ad0e-ab0d12c779b1_story.html|title=The new Godzilla film imagines a strong Japan pushing back against the U.S.|last=Fifield|first=Anna|newspaper=The Washington Post|date=September 23, 2016|access-date=November 21, 2017|archive-date=December 1, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201045841/https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/asia_pacific/new-godzilla-film-imagines-a-strong-japan-pushing-back-against-the-us/2016/09/23/ddd7d5c4-7f70-11e6-ad0e-ab0d12c779b1_story.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Development===
[[File:Mansai nomura godzilla.jpg|thumb|right|200px|A blend of practical effects and [[computer-generated imagery]] was considered (the former tested) for Godzilla; however, Toho settled on a completely CGI monster. Mansai Nomura portrayed Godzilla via motion capture.]]
In December 2014, Toho announced plans for a new ''Godzilla'' film for a 2016 release, stating, "This is very good timing after the success of [[Godzilla (2014 film)|the American version]] this year: if not now, then when? The licensing contract we have with [[Legendary Pictures|Legendary]] places no restrictions on us making domestic versions."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/japans-toho-produce-new-godzilla-754751|title=Japan's Toho to Produce New 'Godzilla' for 2016, First in 12 Years|last=Blair|first=Gavin |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=December 7, 2014|access-date=November 12, 2022|url-status=live|archive-date=November 12, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221112191720/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/japans-toho-produce-new-godzilla-754751/}}</ref> The new film would have no ties to Legendary's [[MonsterVerse]] and would serve as a reboot to Toho's series. Minami Ichikawa would serve as the film's production manager and Taiji Ueda as the film's project leader. Ueda confirmed that the screenplay was in development and filming had been planned for a summer 2015 shoot. Toho would additionally put together a project team, known as "Godzilla Conference" or "Godzi-con", to formulate future projects.<ref name="ar">{{cite web|url=http://www.augustragone.blogspot.com/2014/12/the-japanese-godzilla-will-rise-again.html|title=The "Japanese Godzilla" Will Rise Again! Toho had approached Anno in January 2013 to direct the reboot but Anno initially declined due to falling into [[Depression (mood)|depression]] after completing ''[[Evangelion: 3.0 You Can (Not) Redo]]'', stating, "A representative from Toho contacted me directly, saying, 'We'd like to direct a new Godzilla film.' At the time, I was still recovering from EVA 3.0, and right on the spot, flatly refused the offer, 'It's impossible. Even to begin work on [[Evangelion: 3.0+1.0|the next Eva]] is impossible.'" However, Toho's sincerity and his longtime friend and co-director, Shinji Higuchi, eventually convinced him to accept the offer in March 2013.<ref name="speak"/> Anno had also refused the offer due to a lack of confidence, stating, "I refused [the offer] since I didn't have confidence that I could exceed [[Godzilla (1954 film)|the first film]] or come close to equaling it. But I thought that if I were to come close even a little, I would have to do the same thing [as the first film]."<ref name="jp2"/> Mahiro Maeda provided the new design for Godzilla while Takayuki Takeya provided the [[maquette]].<ref name=jan3>{{cite web|url=http://augustragone.blogspot.com/2016/01/tohos-new-godzilla-resurgence-exposed.html|title=Toho's New Godzilla Resurgence Exposed Private Photos Taken at Toho Studios Leaked!|last=Ragone|first=August|work=The Good, the Bad, and Godzilla|date=January 3, 2016|access-date=March 1, 2016|archive-date=February 23, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160223211704/http://augustragone.blogspot.com/2016/01/tohos-new-godzilla-resurgence-exposed.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Director Higuchi stated that he intended to provide the "most terrifying Godzilla that Japan's cutting-edge special-effects movie-making can muster."<ref>{{cite news |work=[[ABC News (United States)|ABC News]] |agency=AP |first=Yuri |last=Kageyama |date=August 5, 2015 |title=AP Interview: Japan's 'Godzilla' Director Wants to Surprise |url=https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/wireStory/ap-interview-japans-godzilla-director-surprise-32891038 |access-date=June 28, 2020 |archive-date=August 8, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150808012358/http://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/wireStory/ap-interview-japans-godzilla-director-surprise-32891038? |url-status=live }}</ref> A variety of techniques such as puppets, animatronics, and digital effects were initially considered<ref name=jan3/> and an upper-body animatronic was produced but went unused after Toho decided to create a completely CG Godzilla. Principal photography began on September 1, 2015, with a large on-location film shoot at [[Kamata Station (Tokyo)|Kamata station]] in Tokyo.<ref>{{cite web|publisher=Scified.com|url=http://www.scified.com/site/godzillamovies/godzilla-2016-begins-production-working-title-revealed|title=Godzilla 2016 Begins Production, Working Title Revealed|access-date=September 2, 2015|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150902184944/http://www.scified.com/site/godzillamovies/godzilla-2016-begins-production-working-title-revealed|archive-date=September 2, 2015}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/123136/new-japanese-godzilla-movie-filming-this-weekend-in-tokyo/|title=New Japanese Godzilla Movie Filming This Weekend in Tokyo|last=Rigney|first=Todd|work=[[Dread Central]]|date=September 1, 2015|access-date=September 2, 2015|archive-date=September 2, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150902104647/http://www.dreadcentral.com/news/123136/new-japanese-godzilla-movie-filming-this-weekend-in-tokyo/|url-status=live}}</ref> It commenced on a budget of {{JPY|900 million}}, which later rose to {{JPY|1.3 billion}};{{sfn|''Kinema Junpo''|2017|p=36}} Anno noted that "originally, we couldn't make it with that budget".<ref name="AnnoInterview">{{cite web |url=https://www.sankei.com/article/20161126-IV4ASEQRUZNLFKM3LFNP3SPILI/ |title=【庵野秀明監督に聞く・動画付き】シン・ゴジラで重ねた無理とは？ 今はエヴァンゲリオン最新作に… |trans-title=[Interview with director Hideaki Anno (video included)] What is the impossibility that is piled up in ''Shin Godzilla''? ===Critical response===
''Shin Godzilla'' received critical acclaim in Japan,<ref name="acclaim">{{cite web|url=http://www.itechpost.com/articles/26656/20160831/shin-godzilla-receives-critical-acclaim-to-be-released-on-us-theaters-in-october.htm|title='Shin Godzilla' Receives Critical Acclaim, To Be Released On US Theaters In October|last=Dominguez|first=Anne|work=iTech Post|date=August 31, 2016|access-date=October 18, 2016|url-status=live|archive-date=January 1, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210101042207/https://www.itechpost.com/articles/26656/20160831/shin-godzilla-receives-critical-acclaim-to-be-released-on-us-theaters-in-october.htm}}</ref> while Western critics gave it a more mixed response.{{efn|name=Mixed}} The special effects and new depiction of Godzilla were praised, but the film was criticized for its long scenes, confusing dialogue, over-crowded characters and subplots.<ref name="box1" /> On [[review aggregator]] [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an [[approval rating]] of 87% based on 87 reviews, with an average rating of 6.70/10. The site's consensus reads: "''Godzilla: Resurgence'' offers a refreshingly low-fi – and altogether entertaining – return to the monster's classic creature-feature roots."<ref name="rottent">{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/godzilla_resurgence/|title=Godzilla Resurgence (Shin Godzilla) (2016)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=April 7, 2018|archive-date=November 27, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171127151907/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/godzilla_resurgence/|url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a score of 67 out of 100 based on 14 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/shin-godzilla-godzilla-resurgence | title=Shin Godzilla (Godzilla Resurgence) Reviews | work=[[Metacritic]] | publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] | access-date=April 21, 2018 | archive-date=April 6, 2018 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180406104914/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/shin-godzilla-godzilla-resurgence | url-status=live }}</ref>

Japanese pop culture site ''RO65'' called the film a "masterpiece of unprecedented filmmaking", and felt that the film retains a "strong respect for the fundamental message within Godzilla".<ref>[http://sp.ro69.jp/blog/cut/145901 『シン・ゴジラ』を観た。空前の傑作‼] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160814200027/http://sp.ro69.jp/blog/cut/145901 |date=August 14, 2016 }}.''sp.ro69.jp''</ref> ''Oricon Style'' praised directors Hideaki Anno and Shinji Higuchi for their realistic approach and the film's reality vs. idealism themes, calling it a "world class" ''Godzilla'' film.<ref name="sk">{{cite web|url=https://skreeonk.com/2016/07/26/the-first-japanese-reviews-for-shin-gojira-are-in-hailed-as-a-masterpiece/|title=The First Japanese Reviews For Shin 『シン・ゴジラ』最速レビュー！現代日本に現れた完全生物を媒介にする"現実 VS 理想"物語] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160722112744/http://www.oricon.co.jp/special/49146/ |date=July 22, 2016 }}.''oricon.co.jp''</ref> Cinema Today called the film a "thrilling experience" and a "masterpiece", feeling that the film was a return to form similar to 2004's ''[[Dawn of the Dead (2004 film)|Dawn of the Dead]]''.<ref name="sk"/><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cinematoday.jp/page/A0005055|title=おかえり、ゴジラ！|trans-title=Welcome Back, Godzilla!|date=July 7, 2016|website=Cinema Today|access-date=April 15, 2020|archive-date=September 29, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200929220605/https://www.cinematoday.jp/page/A0005055|url-status=live}}</ref> Kazuo Ozaki from Eiga.com praised the film as well, stating, "Hollywood, even with all its money, can't approach this kind of perfection"<ref name="jp2">{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2016/07/28/films/favorite-monster-returns-terrorize-japan-shin-godzilla/#.V5uvjTUWzIU|title=Our favorite monster returns to terrorize Japan in Shin Godzilla|last=Schilling|first=Mark|work=[[Japan Times]]|date=July 28, 2016|access-date=July 29, 2016|archive-date=July 31, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160731191547/http://www.japantimes.co.jp/culture/2016/07/28/films/favorite-monster-returns-terrorize-japan-shin-godzilla#.V5uvjTUWzIU|url-status=live}}</ref> while Koichi Irikura of Cinema Today called it a "birth of a masterpiece that boldly announces the revival of a Japanese Godzilla".<ref name="jp2"/> Brian Ashcraft from ''[[Kotaku]]'' felt the film was a "letdown", though he praised the film's special effects and social reflection of Japan, he criticized the film's depiction of the human characters, stating, "I wish the movie explored the relationships between the politicians and the researchers more instead of glossing over it" and concluded that "This isn't one of the best ''Godzilla'' films ever made, but it's certainly not one of the worst by any stretch, either. ''Godzilla: Resurgence'' is a series of compelling ideas in a so-so ''Godzilla'' movie".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://kotaku.com/godzilla-resurgence-the-kotaku-review-1784509330|title=Godzilla Resurgence Talks Too Much|last=Ashcraft|first=Brian|work=Kotaku|date=July 29, 2016|access-date=July 29, 2016|archive-date=July 29, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160729143932/http://kotaku.com/godzilla-resurgence-the-kotaku-review-1784509330|url-status=live}}</ref>

Ollie Barder from ''[[Forbes]]'' was surprised at "how good" the film was, praising Anno's references to visual tropes from [[Gainax]] properties such as ''[[Neon Genesis Evangelion]]'', though he was not completely fond of Godzilla's new design; he felt that the "googly" eyes made Godzilla look silly but that the design was more "organic and menacing" than previous incarnations and praised the film's depiction of Godzilla, stating, "I really liked the way Godzilla is handled in this new movie, as it feels a lot more like [[Giant God Warrior Appears in Tokyo|the God Soldier short]] that both Anno and Higuchi worked on" and concluded by stating that he "really enjoyed" the film and that it had a "far more coherent plot" than 2014's ''Godzilla''.<ref name="barder">{{cite web|url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/olliebarder/2016/08/16/godzilla-resurgence-review-surprisingly-good-and-very-much-classic-gainax/#9d85a0c6bf1a|title='Godzilla Resurgence' Review: Surprisingly Good And Very Much Classic Gainax|last=Barder|first=Ollie|work=[[Forbes]]|date=August 16, 2016|access-date=August 16, 2016|archive-date=August 16, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160816225241/http://www.forbes.com/sites/olliebarder/2016/08/16/godzilla-resurgence-review-surprisingly-good-and-very-much-classic-gainax/#9d85a0c6bf1a|url-status=live}}</ref> Marcus Goh from [[Yahoo]] felt that the film was a better reimagining than 2014's ''Godzilla'', though he criticized parts of Godzilla's design and the protagonists' plan to stop Godzilla. Goh gave the film a 3.1 score out of 5 and concluded that it "preserves the feel of ''Godzilla'' movies while updating it with modern responses".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://sports.yahoo.com/news/review-shin-godzilla-is-amazingly-cathartic-055402347.html|title=Review: Shin Godzilla" is amazingly cathartic|last=Goh|first=Marcus|work=[[Yahoo]]|date=August 21, 2016|access-date=August 22, 2016|archive-date=September 2, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160902061557/http://sports.yahoo.com/news/review-shin-godzilla-is-amazingly-cathartic-055402347.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Jay Hawkinson from [[Bloody Disgusting]] called the film a "very good ''Godzilla'' movie that teeters on greatness". However, he felt the film's drama "didn't always work" and that some of the English delivery felt "canned and often corny", particularly from Ishihara's character. Hawkinson praised the film's battle-scenes, Shiro Sagisu's score, and the film's homages to the franchise, and concluded that "''Shin Godzilla'' may be a reboot sans the rubber suit we've grown to love but it's unquestionably Godzilla".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/movie/3402102/review-godzilla-resurgence-shin-godzilla/|title=[Review] 'Godzilla: Resurgence' ('Shin Godzilla') Lights Up Tokyo in 4-D|last=Hawkinson|first=Jay|work=Bloody Disgusting|date=August 8, 2016|access-date=August 22, 2016|archive-date=August 23, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160823010119/http://bloody-disgusting.com/movie/3402102/review-godzilla-resurgence-shin-godzilla/|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[The Guardian|Guardian]]'' chief film critic [[Peter Bradshaw]] found Ishihara "slightly absurd" as an American "who bafflingly speaks English only with a strong and borderline unintelligible accent and comports herself with torpid model languor at all times".<ref>{{Cite web|date=August 11, 2017|title=Shin Godzilla review – Japan's great monster rises from the deep once more|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/aug/11/shin-godzilla-review-japans-great-monster-revived-once-more|access-date=December 21, 2020|website=the Guardian|language=en|archive-date=January 26, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210126014056/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/aug/11/shin-godzilla-review-japans-great-monster-revived-once-more|url-status=live}}</ref>

Elizabeth Kerr from ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' felt that Anno and Higuchi had done "the big guy justice" and "created a Godzilla for this era". While she felt that "all the telling (or reading) rather than showing reduces the story's overall impact" Kerr concluded that "there's an intangible quality to this Godzilla that Edwards (Emmerich doesn't count) never quite captured, and which is always welcome".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/godzilla-resurgence-film-review-923097|title='Godzilla Resurgence' ('Shin Gojira'): Film Review|last=Kerr|first=Elizabeth|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=August 26, 2016|access-date=August 26, 2016|archive-date=August 27, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160827040830/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/godzilla-resurgence-film-review-923097|url-status=live}}</ref> Matt Schley from ''[[Otaku USA]]'' called the film "A match made in kaiju-heaven", and praised Anno's directing: "It's also a reminder, after years in the ''Evangelion'' reboot woods, that Anno is one of Japan's most unique directorial voices in either animation or live-action filmmaking." Though he felt the special effects weren't as impressive as 2014's ''Godzilla'', Schley stated that the film's CG "gets the job done, though there are a couple questionable shots" and concluded by stating that "Hideaki Anno has achieved a successful resurgence for both the Big G and himself".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.otakuusamagazine.com/JPop/News1/Godzilla-Resurgence-a-Rebirth-for-Both-Godzilla-an-8058.aspx|title=Godzilla Resurgence a Rebirth for Both Godzilla and Hideaki Anno|last=Schley|first=Matt|work=Otaku USA|date=August 1, 2016|access-date=September 2, 2016|archive-date=August 20, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160820071646/http://otakuusamagazine.com/JPop/News1/Godzilla-Resurgence-a-Rebirth-for-Both-Godzilla-an-8058.aspx|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Other films and potential sequel ===
In July 2017, while attending the American fan convention [[G-Fest]], Higuchi confirmed that Toho could not produce another ''Godzilla'' film until after 2020.<ref>{{cite news |work=Bloody Disgusting |url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3447724/shin-godzilla-sequel-cannot-made-2020/ |first=John|last=Squires|title='Shin Godzilla' Sequel Cannot Be Made Until After 2020|date=July 18, 2017|access-date=July 18, 2017|url-status=live|archive-date=September 5, Entertainment]], who were producing their own [[MonsterVerse|''Godzilla'' films]], that restricts Toho from releasing their ''Godzilla'' films in the same year as Legendary's ''Godzilla'' In 2022, [[Hideaki Anno]] revealed that he wrote and submitted a proposal for a potential sequel, stating, "I wrote a proposal during the filming of ''Shin Godzilla'' on February 3, 2016, labeled ''Sequel Shin Godzilla Memo''. The primary working title for the project was {{nihongo|''Shin Godzilla Raids Again''|シン・ゴジラの逆襲|Shin Gojira no Gyakushu}} and with the assumption that Mr. Higuchi would direct, it was meant to be something like a Toho Champion Festival ''kaiju'' showdown. Since it was concocted to be released in 2018, the shortest time possible for a domestic-made ''Godzilla'' film to be released, it was a rough draft for people who wouldn't like ''Shin Godzilla'', an idea that wouldn't cost too much or take too long to create. I believed that it would be a bad idea to let it go to waste so I gave the proposal, draft, and visuals, as a gift to Mr. Higuchi and Toho, but since it was premature or whatever, among other reasons, after the film's release the discussion ended. While I did feel it was a waste, it's not something I could help since Toho decides what gets made."{{sfn|Anno|2022|p=73}}<ref>{{Cite web|title=Lost Project: Shin Godzilla Raids Again|url=https://www.tohokingdom.com/cutting_room/shin_godzilla_raids_again.html|access-date=October 11, 2024|website=Toho Kingdom|url-status=live|archive-date=October 12, 2024|archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20241012011023/https://www.tohokingdom.com/cutting_room/shin_godzilla_raids_again.html}}</ref>

In July 2025, [[Bloomberg News]] reported that a potential sequel to ''Shin Godzilla'' is amongst Toho's prospects.<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Kim |first=Sohee |date=July 17, 2025 |title=Godzilla Conquered Japan. Now Its Owner Plots a Global Takeover |url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-17/godzilla-studio-toho-plans-more-films-sequels-anime-and-jujutsu-kaisen |url-access=registration |access-date=July 17, 2025 |website=[[Bloomberg News]] |archive-date=July 26, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250726063353/https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-17/godzilla-studio-toho-plans-more-films-sequels-anime-and-jujutsu-kaisen |url-status=live }}</ref> At [[San Diego Comic-Con]] the following month, many attendees were eager to ask Higuchi about a potential sequel, but such questions became prohibited midway through his press roundtable interviews.<ref>{{Cite web |date=August 4, 2025 |title=San Diego Comic-Con 2025: Aliens and Activations|first=Jana|last=Monji|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/san-diego-comic-con-2025-aliens-and-activations |access-date=August 18, 2025 |website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|url-status=live|archive-date=September 6, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250906203835/https://www.rogerebert.com/festivals/san-diego-comic-con-2025-aliens-and-activations}}</ref> In an interview with ''The Direct'', Higuchi seemed disinterested in a sequel, stating: "Well, I think if I were to make a sequel, then that's going to mean I'm going to have a lot more work, and I prefer to just come to Comic-Con, buy some toys, and play. So I don't know what kind of themes I would put into a sequel."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Thompson |first=David |date=August 8, 2025 |title=''Shin Godzilla'' Director Breaks Silence on Possible Sequel (Exclusive) |url=https://thedirect.com/article/shin-godzilla-director-breaks-silence-on-possible-sequel-exclusive |access-date=August 18, 2025 |website=TheDirect.com |language=en|url-status=live|archive-date=September 6, 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250906204153/https://thedirect.com/article/shin-godzilla-director-breaks-silence-on-possible-sequel-exclusive}}</ref>

===Collaborative projects===
In 2016, Toho and [[Khara (studio)|Khara, Inc.]] collaborated on ''Godzilla vs. Evangelion'', a cross-over line of merchandise uniting ''Shin Godzilla'' and Anno's ''[[Neon Genesis Evangelion (franchise)|Neon Genesis Evangelion]]''.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.player.one/godzilla-vs-evangelion-merch-isnt-april-fools-joke-just-answer-our-prayers-523729|title=Godzilla vs Evangelion Merch Isn't An April Fools' Joke, Just The Answer To Our Prayers|first=Nd|last=Medina|work=Player One|date=April 1, 2016|access-date=March 7, 2022|url-status=live|archive-date=March 7, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220307182408/https://www.player.one/godzilla-vs-evangelion-merch-isnt-april-fools-joke-just-answer-our-prayers-523729}}</ref> In May 2019, [[Universal Studios Japan]] opened the ''Godzilla vs. Evangelion: The Real 4D'' attraction. It ran until August 2019.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3561388/godzilla-vs-evangelion-real-4d-experience-coming-universal-studios-japan-month/|title="Godzilla vs. Evangelion: The Real 4D" Experience Coming to Universal Studios Japan This Month!|first=John|last=Squires|work=Bloody Disgusting|date=May 15, 2019|access-date= October 9, 2020|url-status=live|archive-date= October 9, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201009233311/https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/3561388/godzilla-vs-evangelion-real-4d-experience-coming-universal-studios-japan-month/}}</ref> In February 2022, Toho, Khara, [[Toei Company]], and [[Tsuburaya Productions]] announced a collaborative project titled [[Shin Japan Heroes Universe]] for merchandise, special events and tie-ins. The project unites films that Anno had worked on that bear the [[katakana]] title "Shin" (シン): ''Shin Godzilla'', ''[[Evangelion: 3.0+1.0 Thrice Upon a Time]]'',{{efn|The film was released in Japan as シン・エヴァンゲリオン劇場版: 𝄂 (''Shin Evangelion Theatrical Edition: 𝄂'')}} ''[[Shin Ultraman]]'' and ''[[Shin Kamen Rider (film)|Shin Kamen Rider]]''.<ref name=":1">{{cite web|url=https://www.crunchyroll.com/anime-news/2022/02/13-1/godzilla-evangelion-ultraman-and-kamen-rider-to-collaborate-in-shin-japan-heroes-universe-project|title=Godzilla, Evangelion, Ultraman, and Kamen Rider to Collaborate in Shin Japan Heroes Universe Project|first=Mikikazu|last=Komatsu|work=[[Crunchyroll]]|date=February 13, 2022|access-date=March 7, 2022|archive-date=March 28, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220328011335/https://www.crunchyroll.com/anime-news/2022/02/13-1/godzilla-evangelion-ultraman-and-kamen-rider-to-collaborate-in-shin-japan-heroes-universe-project|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/hideaki-anno-launches-shin-japan-065553502.html|title=Hideaki Anno Launches "Shin Japan Heroes Universe" Project|first= Nicolaus|last=Li|work=Yahoo!Life|date=February 16, 2022|access-date=March 7, 2022|url-status=live|archive-date=March 7, 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220307184829/https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/hideaki-anno-launches-shin-japan-065553502.html}}</ref>
