'''''Near Dark''''' is a 1987 American [[neo-Western]]<ref name="cotter">{{cite web |last1=Cotter |first1=Padraig |title=Top 10 Action Scenes in Neo-Western Movies: From ''No Country for Old Men'' To ''Desperado'' |url=https://screenrant.com/neo-western-movie-action-sequences-ranking/ |website=[[ScreenRant]] |access-date=August 11, 2026 |date=October 21, 2025}}</ref> [[horror film]] co-written and directed by [[Kathryn Bigelow]] (in her solo directorial debut), and starring [[Adrian Pasdar]], [[Jenny Wright]], [[Bill Paxton]], [[Lance Henriksen]] and [[Jenette Goldstein]]. The plot follows a young man in a small [[Oklahoma]] town who becomes involved with a family of [[nomad]]ic American vampires.

Despite performing poorly at the box office, critic reviews were generally positive. Over the years, the film has gained a [[cult film|cult following]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Tobias |first=Scott |date=November 5, 2008 |title=The New Cult Canon: Near Dark |url=https://www.avclub.com/the-new-cult-canon-near-dark-1798215134 |website=[[The A.V. Club]] |publisher=Paste Media Group |access-date=July 18, 2024}}</ref>{{sfn|Mathijs|Mendik|2011|p=147}}

==Plot==
One night, Caleb Colton, a young man in a small [[Oklahoma]] town, meets an attractive young drifter named Mae. Just before sunrise, she bites him on the neck and runs off. The rising sun causes Caleb's flesh to smoke and burn. Mae arrives with a group of roaming vampires in an RV and takes him away. The most psychotic of the vampires, Severen, wants to kill Caleb but Mae reveals that she has already turned him. Their [[charisma]]tic leader, Jesse Hooker, reluctantly agrees to allow Caleb to remain with them for a week to see if he can learn to hunt and gain the group's trust. Caleb is unwilling to kill to feed, which alienates him from the others. To protect him, Mae kills for him and then has him drink from her wrist.

Jesse's group enters a bar and kills the occupants. They set the bar on fire and flee the scene. All except Mae want to kill Caleb after he endangers them by letting the only living occupant escape, but after Caleb endangers himself to help them escape their motel room during a daylight police raid, Jesse and the others are grateful and temporarily mollified. A camaraderie commences, with Caleb asking Jesse how old he is and Jesse responding that he "[[Confederate States of America|fought for the South]]" (during the [[American Civil War]]), making him about 150 years old (Severen had earlier suggested he and Jesse started the [[Great Chicago Fire]] of 1871).

Meanwhile, Caleb's father has been searching for Jesse's group. A child vampire in the group, Homer, meets Caleb's sister, Sarah, and wants to turn her into his companion, but Caleb objects. While the group argues, Caleb's father arrives and holds them at gunpoint, demanding that Sarah be released. Jesse taunts him into shooting him, then regurgitates the bullet before wrestling the gun away. In the confusion, Sarah opens a door, letting in the sunlight and forcing the vampires back. Burning, Caleb escapes with his family.

Caleb suggests they try giving him a [[blood transfusion]]. The transfusion unexpectedly reverses Caleb's transformation. That night, the vampires search for Caleb and Sarah. Mae distracts Caleb by trying to persuade him to return to her while the others kidnap his sister. Caleb discovers the kidnapping and his tires slashed but gives chase on horseback. When the horse shies and throws him, he is confronted by Severen. Caleb commandeers a [[Semi-trailer truck|tractor-trailer]] and runs Severen over. The injured vampire suddenly appears on the hood of the truck and manages to rip apart the wiring in the engine. Caleb [[jackknifing|jackknifes]] the vehicle and jumps out as the truck explodes, killing Severen. Seeking revenge, Jesse and his girlfriend, Diamondback, pursue him but are forced to escape in their car as dawn breaks.

Attempting to save Sarah, Mae breaks out of the back of the car with her. Mae's flesh begins to smoke as she is burned by the sun but she carries Sarah into Caleb's arms, taking refuge under his jacket. Homer attempts to follow, but as he runs he dies from exposure to the sun. Their sunproofing ruined, Jesse and Diamondback also begin to burn. They attempt to run Caleb and Sarah over but fail, dying as the car blows up. Mae awakens later, her burns now healed. She too has been given a transfusion and is cured. Mae says she is afraid, and Caleb pulls her into a hug to comfort her as the film ends.

==Production==
''Near Dark'' was part of a resurgence of American vampire films in the 1980s, along with the likes of ''[[Fright Night]]'', ''[[Once Bitten (1985 film)|Once Bitten]]'' (both 1985), ''[[Vamp (film)|Vamp]]'' (1986), ''[[The Lost Boys]]'' and ''[[The Monster Squad]]'' (both 1987).{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=6}} Director and co-writer Kathryn Bigelow wanted to film a Western movie; when she and fellow co-writer Eric Red found financial backing for a Western difficult to obtain, they decided to mix the Western genre with the then more popular vampire genre.{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=6}} The combination of the genres, which Bigelow and Red felt were ripe for revisionist interpretation, had been visited at least twice before on the big screen, with ''[[Curse of the Undead]]'' (1959) and ''[[Billy the Kid Versus Dracula]]'' (1966).

Bigelow knew (and later married) director [[James Cameron]], who directed ''[[Aliens (film)|Aliens]]'' (1986), a film that shares three cast members (Paxton, Goldstein and Henriksen) with ''Near Dark''. Actor [[Michael Biehn]] was offered the role of Jesse Hooker, but he rejected the role, because he found the script confusing. Lance Henriksen took over the role.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://lebeauleblog.com/2014/03/08/what-the-hell-happened-to-michael-biehn/ |title=''What the Hell Happened to Michael Biehn?'' – Article on the website ''Lebeau's Le Blog'' |access-date=June 9, 2019 |archive-date=October 1, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221001111548/https://lebeauleblog.com/2014/03/08/what-the-hell-happened-to-michael-biehn/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> A cinema seen in the background early in the film has ''Aliens'' on its marquee and Cameron played the man who "[[Finger (gesture)|flips off]]" Severen.

==Release==
''Near Dark'' premiered at the [[Toronto International Film Festival]] (TIFF) on September 12, 1987.{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=5}}

===Box office===
''Near Dark'' was released on October 2, 1987, in 262 theaters, grossing US$635,789 on its opening weekend. In its second week, the film's release widened to 429 screens.{{sfn|Abbott|2020|pp=7–8}} ''Near Dark'' went on to make $3.4 million, below its $5 million budget.<ref name=mojo/>

Bigelow has since attributed the film's lack of box-office success to its proximity to ''The Lost Boys''—which was released two months prior to ''Near Dark'', opening on a comparatively wider 1,542 screens—as well as it being an [[independent film]].{{sfn|Abbott|2020|pp=7–8}} She has stated that ''The Lost Boys'' was a "vampire movie made by [[Warner Bros.]] that had a tremendous amount of muscle behind it, distribution and marketing muscle behind it. And so we were just this little [...] whisper out there."{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=8}}

===Home media===
''Near Dark'' was released on [[VHS]] in 1988.{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=8}} By 1989, the film was released on [[LaserDisc]] by [[HBO Video]].{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=8}} In 2002, [[Anchor Bay Entertainment]] released the film on [[DVD]].{{sfn|Abbott|2020|p=8}}<ref>{{cite web |last=Bovberg |first=Jason |date=September 20, 2002 |title=Near Dark |url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/4630/near-dark/ |website=[[DVD Talk]] |publisher=[[Internet Brands]] |access-date=September 19, 2023}}</ref> This one is a [[THX]] certified two-disc release that features a [[DTS, Inc.|DTS]] 5.1 audio track, an audio commentary, a documentary, theatrical trailers, galleries and DVD-ROM.<ref>{{cite web |title=Near Dark |url=https://archive.thedigitalbits.com/reviews2/neardark.html |website=The Digital Bits |publisher=The Digital Bits, Inc. |access-date=September 19, 2023}}</ref> In November 2009, the film was released on [[Blu-ray]] by [[Lionsgate Home Entertainment]].{{sfn|Melton|2010|p=489}}

==Reception==
Part of a late 1980s revival of serious vampire depictions on the big screen, it received mostly positive reviews for its mix of the [[Western (genre)|Western]], [[Outlaw biker film|biker]] and [[Vampire film|vampire movie]] genres.{{sfn|Newman|1988|p=36}} On the [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Near Dark'' holds an 81% approval rating based on 79 critic reviews. The consensus reads: "''Near Dark'' is at once a creepy vampire film, a thrilling western, and a poignant family tale, with humor and scares in abundance."<ref name="rotten">{{cite web |title=Near Dark (1987) |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1014793_near_dark |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |access-date=January 16, 2026 }}</ref>

===Critical response===
In her review for ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Caryn James]] wrote, "Ms. Bigelow's too-studied compositions – Caleb in silhouette riding a horse toward the camera – clash with her unstudied approach to the characters' looks".<ref>{{cite news |last=James |first=Caryn |date=October 4, 1987 |title=''Near Dark'', a Tale of Vampires on the Road |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1987/10/04/movies/film-near-dark-a-tale-of-vampires-on-the-road.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |publisher=[[The New York Times Company]] |issn=0362-4331 |oclc=1645522 |access-date=September 30, 2008}}</ref> [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' was impressed by Bigelow's first foray into big budget films with the "hillbilly vampire" movie, describing it as "beautifully shot".<ref>{{cite news |last=Rosenbaum |first=Jonathan |title=Near Dark |url=http://onfilm.chicagoreader.com/movies/capsules/10298_NEAR_DARK |newspaper=[[Chicago Reader]] |publisher=Noisy Creek |issn=1096-6919 |oclc=1105307753 |access-date=February 4, 2009}}</ref> [[Hal Hinson]] of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' said the intermixing of vampire legends, westerns and biker movies has a result that is "both outrageous and poetic; it has extravagant, bloody thrills plus something else – something that comes close to genuine emotion".<ref>{{cite news |last=Hinson |first=Hal |date=May 5, 1988 |title='Near Dark' (R) |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-srv/style/longterm/movies/videos/neardarkrhinson_a13f83.htm |newspaper=[[The Washington Post]] |publisher=[[Graham Holdings|The Washington Post Company]] ({{small| now Nash Holdings}}) |issn=0190-8286 |oclc=2269358 |access-date=February 4, 2009}}</ref> In his review for ''[[The Globe and Mail]]'', [[Jay Scott]] wrote, "Bill Paxton as the undead sex symbol – is exceptional, but not exceptional enough to put across the cop-out that concludes the film".<ref>{{cite news |last=Scott |first=Jay |date=October 2, 1987 |title=Vampire myth spawns new terrors in seductive demons of ''Near Dark'' |newspaper=[[The Globe and Mail]] |publisher=[[The Woodbridge Company]] |issn=0319-0714 |oclc=61312660}}</ref>

Hoberman|Jim Hoberman]] would write in ''[[The Village Voice]]'' that "Bigelow has made bigger movies than [''Near Dark''], but none better," calling it "a poetic horror film that draws its power from the outlaw mythology of ''[[Bonnie and Clyde (film)|Bonnie and Clyde]]'' and ''[[Gun Crazy]]'' (or maybe the [[Manson Family]]), and its brooding loneliness from the western landscape. Nothing I’ve seen in the 20 years since
''Near Dark'' appeared has so successfully Americanized the myth of the vampire."<ref>{{cite news |last=Hoberman |first=Jim |date=June 19, 2007 |title=Hat Trick ==Cancelled remake==
A remake of the film was announced in October 2006 as a co-production between film companies [[Rogue Pictures]] and [[Platinum Dunes]], with [[Samuel Bayer]] attached to direct.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://variety.com/2006/film/markets-festivals/dunes-digs-up-rich-rogue-deal-1117951379/ |title=Dunes digs up rich Rogue deal |last=Fleming |first=Michael |date=October 5, 2006 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |issn=0042-2738 |oclc=60626328 |access-date=April 4, 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Weinberg |first=Scott |date=April 7, 2006 |title=Near Dark Remake ... Nears |url=http://news.moviefone.com/2006/04/07/near-dark-remake-nears/ |website=[[Moviefone]] |publisher=[[AOL]] ({{small|now Born in Cleveland LLC}}) |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180407120041/https://www.moviefone.com/2006/04/07/near-dark-remake-nears/ |archive-date=April 7, 2018 |url-status=dead |access-date=February 13, 2009}}</ref> In December 2008, [[Platinum Dunes]] producer [[Bradley Fuller]] stated that the project had been put on hold due to similarities in conception with ''[[Twilight (2008 film)|Twilight]]'' (2008), a film which also contained a romance between human and vampire characters.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Hewitt |first=Chris |date=December 12, 2008  |url=https://empireonline.com/news/story.asp?NID=23864 |title=Near Dark Remake Is Off |magazine=[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]] |publisher=[[Bauer Media Group]] |issn=0957-4948 |oclc=40516612 |archive-date=October 11, 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151011035852/http://www.empireonline.com/news/story.asp?NID=23864 |url-status=dead |access-date=January 15, 2014}}</ref>
