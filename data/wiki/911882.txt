'''''How to Marry a Millionaire''''' is a 1953 American [[romantic comedy]] film directed by [[Jean Negulesco]] and written and produced by [[Nunnally Johnson]]. The screenplay by Johnson was based on the plays ''The Greeks Had a Word for It'' (1930) by [[Zoe Akins]] and ''Loco'' (1946) by Dale Eunson and [[Katherine Albert]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=How to Marry a Millionaire (1953) |url=https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/50878 |website=[[AFI Catalog of Feature Films]] |publisher=[[American Film Institute]]}}</ref>

The film stars [[Marilyn Monroe]], [[Betty Grable]], and [[Lauren Bacall]] as three fashionable Manhattan models, along with [[David Wayne]], [[Rory Calhoun]], [[Cameron Mitchell (actor)|Cameron Mitchell]], and [[William Powell]] as their wealthy marks.

Produced and distributed by [[20th Century-Fox]], ''How to Marry a Millionaire'' was the studio's first film to be shot in the new [[CinemaScope]] widescreen sound process, although it was the second CinemaScope film released by Fox after the biblical [[epic film]] ''[[The Robe (film)|The Robe]]'' (also 1953). It was also the first color and CinemaScope film ever shown on prime-time network television (though [[Pan and scan|panned-and-scanned]]) when it was presented as the first film on NBC's ''[[NBC Saturday Night at the Movies|Saturday Night at the Movies]]'' on September 23, 1961.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Gomery|first1=Douglas|last2=Pafort-Overduin|first2=Clara|title=Movie History: A Survey|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=s0PP2Gm8xNcC|year=2011|publisher=Taylor & Francis|isbn=978-1-1368-3525-4|page=246|edition=2nd}}</ref>

The [[How to Marry a Millionaire (soundtrack)|soundtrack]] to ''How to Marry a Millionaire'' was released on CD by [[Film Score Monthly]] on March 15, 2001.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Alfred Newman / Cyril Mockridge – How To Marry A Millionaire (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) |url=https://www.discogs.com/release/3606163-Alfred-Newman-Cyril-Mockridge-How-To-Marry-A-Millionaire-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack?srsltid=AfmBOorXLGD9j6Hr0N-8XmjZzZBqEQRiwaMfeHgUK3iv-PrEd1haD17e |website=Discogs| date=March 2001 }}</ref>

==Plot==
[[Image:Marilyn Monroe, Betty Grable and Lauren Bacall in How to Marry a Millionaire trailer.jpg|thumb|upright=1.25|Monroe as Pola, Grable as Loco, and Bacall as Schatze]]
Resourceful Schatze Page, ditzy Loco Dempsey, and near-sighted Pola Debevoise are three women on a mission: each wants to marry a [[millionaire]]. To accomplish this task, they rent a luxurious [[Sutton Place, Manhattan|Sutton Place]] penthouse in New York City from Freddie Denmark (who is avoiding the Internal Revenue Service ([[Internal Revenue Service|IRS]]) by living in Europe) and together hatch a plot to court the city's elite. On the day they move in, Loco arrives with Tom Brookman, who had purchased her groceries for her because she "forgot her pocketbook". Tom shows interest in Schatze, but she dismisses him, stating that "The first rule is, gentlemen callers have got to wear a necktie" and, instead, sets her sights on the charming, classy, rich widower J.D. Hanley. While courting the older J.D., she initially fends off Tom's continued attempts to win her over. Eventually, she agrees to several dates, but after every date, she insists she never wants to see him again.

Meanwhile, Loco meets grumpy businessman Walter Brewster. He is married, but she agrees to accompany him to his lodge in [[Maine]], thinking it is a convention hall of the [[Benevolent and Protective Order of Elks|Elks Club]]. As Loco discovers her mistake, she comes down with the measles and is quarantined. Upon recovering, while Brewster is now bedridden with measles, she begins seeing the forest ranger, Eben Salem. When Salem indicates the huge expanse of forested land he is responsible for, saying "this is all mine", Loco mistakenly believes he is a wealthy landowner instead of a state employee overseeing acres of forestlands. Later discovering the truth, she is disappointed and tells Brewster about it on the drive back to New York.

[[Image:Alexander D'Arcy and Marilyn Monroe in How to Marry a Millionaire trailer.jpg|thumb|upright=1.25|Pola is romanced by a phony tycoon, played by [[Alexander D'Arcy]]]]
Pola is [[myopic]] but hates wearing glasses in the presence of men. She falls for a phony oil tycoon, J. Stewart Merrill, unaware that he is a crooked speculator. When she intends to take a plane from [[LaGuardia Airport]] to meet him in Atlantic City, she ends up on a plane to Kansas City. On the plane, she encounters Freddie Denmark again, having unknowingly met him when he had entered his apartment to retrieve documents to prove that his crooked accountant stole his money and left him in trouble with the IRS. Freddie also wears glasses and encourages Pola to wear hers as well. They fall quickly in love and get married.

[[Image:William Powell, Lauren Bacall, Betty Grable and Marilyn Monroe in How to Marry a Millionaire trailer.jpg|thumb|upright=1.25|[[William Powell]] as J.D. Hanley prepares to marry Schatze, with Loco and Pola as [[bridesmaid]]s]]
Loco and Pola are reunited with Schatze just before her wedding to J.D. Hanley. Schatze is unable to go through with the marriage and confesses to J.D. that she loves Tom. J.D. agrees to call off the ceremony. Tom is among the wedding guests and the two reconcile and marry. 

Afterwards, the three happy couples end up at a [[greasy spoon]] diner. Schatze jokingly asks Eben and Freddie about their financial prospects, which are slim. When she finally gets around to Tom, he casually admits a net worth of around $200 million, which no one takes seriously. He then calls for the check, and pulls out an enormous wad of money, paying with a $1,000 bill and telling the chef to keep the change. The three astonished women faint and crash to the floor, and the men drink a toast to their unconscious wives.

==Production==
[[File:Village Drive-In Ad - 4 August 1954, Santa Rosa, CA.jpg|thumb|160px|[[Drive-in theater|Drive-in]] advertisement from 1954]] 
[[Nunnally Johnson]], who adapted the screenplay from two different plays, produced the picture.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.classicmoviehub.com/facts-and-trivia/film/how-to-marry-a-millionaire-1953/ |title=Facts about "How to Marry a Millionaire" |website=Classic Movie Hub |access-date=4 December 2020}}</ref>

20th Century-Fox started production on ''The Robe'' before it began ''How to Marry a Millionaire''. Although the latter was completed first, the studio chose to present ''The Robe'' as its first CinemaScope picture in late September or early October 1953 because it felt the family-friendly ''The Robe'' would attract a larger audience to its new widescreen process.<ref>{{cite book |title=The Many Lives of Marilyn Monroe |last=Churchwell |first=Sarah |date=27 December 2005 |publisher=Picador |isbn=0-312-42565-1 |page=57}}</ref>

The film's cinematography was by Joseph MacDonald. The costume design was by [[William Travilla|Travilla]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/78631/how-to-marry-a-millionaire|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120807011527/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/78631/How-to-Marry-a-Millionaire/|url-status=dead|archive-date=August 7, 2012|title=How to Marry a Millionaire (1953): Cast & Crew|publisher=[[Turner Classic Movies]]|access-date=October 11, 2014}}</ref>

===Portrayal of New York===
Between scenes, the cinematography has some iconic color views of mid-20th century New York City: [[Rockefeller Center]], [[Central Park]], the [[Headquarters of the United Nations|United Nations Building]], and [[Brooklyn Bridge]] in the opening sequence following the credits. Other iconic views include the [[Empire State Building]], the lights of [[Times Square]] at night, the [[George Washington Bridge]], and the [[Statue of Liberty]].

===Music===
{{main|How to Marry a Millionaire (soundtrack)}}
A song extolling the virtues of New York follows the Gershwin-like music used for the title credits, after an elaborate five-minute pre-credit sequence showcasing a 70-piece orchestra conducted by [[Alfred Newman]] before the curtain goes up.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.tcm.turner.com/tcmdb/title/78631/How-to-Marry-a-Millionaire/ |title=How to Marry a Millionaire |publisher=Turner Classic Movies |access-date=January 18, 2020}}</ref>

The [[film score|score]] for ''How to Marry a Millionaire'' was one of the first recorded for film in [[stereo]]. It was composed and directed by Alfred Newman, with incidental music by [[Cyril J. Mockridge|Cyril Mockridge]], and orchestrated by [[Edward B. Powell]].<ref name=direction>{{cite magazine |url=http://www.filmscoremonthly.com/cds/detail.cfm/CDID/173/How-to-Marry-a-Millionaire/|title=How to Marry a Millionaire (1953): Track List|magazine=[[Film Score Monthly]] |access-date=October 13, 2014}}</ref> The album was released on CD by [[Film Score Monthly]] on March 15, 2001<ref name=sip>{{cite web |url=http://www.soundtrackinfo.com/tracks/howtomarryamillionaire/|title=How to Marry a Millionaire (1953)|publisher=soundtrackinfo.com |access-date=October 12, 2014}}</ref> as part of their series ''Golden Age Classics''.

The film's theatrical version begins with a nearly six-minute overture of Newman's symphonic piece "Street Scene", which he wrote in the style of [[George Gershwin]]. It is played on-screen by an 80-piece studio orchestra (billed as "The Twentieth Century Fox Symphony Orchestra") exploiting the film's widescreen format. Newman wrote the piece for the 1931 film [[Street Scene (film)|Street Scene]], which featured his first complete film score.

==Release==
''How to Marry a Millionaire'' premiered at the [[Saban Theatre#History|Fox Wilshire Theatre]] (now the [[Saban Theatre]]), in [[Beverly Hills, California]] on November 4, 1953.<ref>{{cite book|last=Schwarz|first=Ted|title=Marilyn Revealed: The Ambitious Life of an American Icon|date=2008|publisher=Taylor Trade Publications|isbn=978-1-589-79342-2|page=[https://archive.org/details/marilynrevealeda0000schw/page/390 390]|url-access=registration|url=https://archive.org/details/marilynrevealeda0000schw/page/390}}</ref> 

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film earned $8 million worldwide{{sfn|Solomon|1989|p=[https://books.google.com/books?id=WIZwZOz8LHsC&pg=PA89 89]}} and $7.5 million domestically, second that year only to ''[[The Robe (film)|The Robe]]''.<ref name=lev>{{cite book|last=Lev|first=Peter|title=Transforming the Screen, 1950-1959|year=2006|publisher=University of California Press|isbn=0-520-24966-6|page=118}}</ref> It was the [[1953 in film#Top grossing films (U.S.)|fourth highest-grossing film of 1953]], whereas Monroe's previous feature, ''[[Gentlemen Prefer Blondes (1953 film)|Gentlemen Prefer Blondes]]'', was [[1953 in film|seventh]].

===Critical reaction===
[[Bosley Crowther]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote "the substance is still insufficient for the vast spread of screen which CinemaScope throws across the front of the theatre, and the impression it leaves is that of nonsense from a few people in a great big hall."<ref>{{cite news |last1=Crowther |first1=Bosley |title=The Screen: Trio of Stars in CinemaScope |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1953/11/11/archives/the-screen-trio-of-stars-in-cinemascope-monroe-grable-bacall.html |access-date=17 July 2023 |work=The New York Times |page=37 |date=11 November 1953}}</ref> William Brogdon of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised the three female leads and wrote the film is "a solid comedy that would click even without the anamorphic filming," as well as Negulesco's direction which "builds a smart, entertaining show about three models on the prowl for millionaires."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Brogdon |first=William |url=https://archive.org/details/variety192-1953-11/page/n69/mode/1up |title=Film Reviews: How To Marry a Millionaire |magazine=Variety |page=6 |date=November 11, 1953 |access-date=May 21, 2026 |via=Internet Archive}}</ref> ''[[Harrison's Reports]]'' wrote the film should be "a box-office natural from all angles — presentation, story, cast, direction, production values, Technicolor photography and, above all, entertainment values that will register strongly with the great mass of movie-goers."<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://archive.org/details/harrisonsreports00harr_1/page/n207/mode/1up |title='How to Marry a Millionaire' with Marilyn Monroe, Betty Grable, Lauren Bacall, David Wayne, Rory Calhoun, Cameron Mitchell and William Powell |magazine=Harrison's Reports |page=180 |date=November 7, 1953 |access-date=May 21, 2026 |via=Internet Archive}}</ref> 

[[Philip K. Scheuer]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote: "Negulesco's direction is in faultless taste and the girls make an ultra decor for his Technicolored settings. I was pleased to note that Miss Monroe held her own, in comic ability, with her more experienced companions."<ref>{{cite news |last=Scheuer |first=Philip K. |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times-philip-k-scheuer/197999451/ |title='Millionaire' Eye-Filling Girl Show with Scenery |work=Los Angeles Times |at=Part III, p. 11 |date=November 5, 1953 |access-date=May 21, 2026 |via=Newspapers.com}} {{Open access}}</ref> Kate Cameron of the ''[[New York Daily News]]'' wrote the film "is a comical play, which would have been delightfully entertaining no matter how it had been photographed or projected. But, the CinemaScope treatment does enhance the production values of the picture enormously and when the action moves out of a stage-like setting, the backgrounds become startlingly realistic and, in some instances, breathtakingly beautiful."<ref>{{cite news |last=Cameron |first=Kate |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/daily-news/198005089/ |title=Dual Premiere For Comedy in CinemaScope |work=New York Daily News |page=17C |date=November 11, 1953 |access-date=May 21, 2026 |via=Newspapers.com}} {{Open access}}</ref> ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine wrote the film was "a light comedy devoted to a close inspection of three famous girls" and acknowledged the CinemaScope anamorphic process "can do the comic about as well as it can the epic."<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://time.com/archive/6797646/cinema-the-new-pictures-nov-23-1953/ |title=Cinema: The New Pictures |magazine=Time |date=November 23, 1953 |access-date=May 21, 2026}}</ref>

==Television adaptation==
In 1957, the film was adapted into a [[sitcom]] also titled ''[[How to Marry a Millionaire (TV series)|How to Marry a Millionaire]]''. It starred [[Barbara Eden]] (as Loco Jones), [[Merry Anders]] (Michelle "Mike" Page), [[Lori Nelson]] (Greta Lindquist) and as Nelson's later replacement, [[Lisa Gaye]] as Gwen Kirby. It aired in syndication for two seasons.

==Remake==
In 2000, [[20th Television|20th Century Fox Television]] produced a [[Television film|made-for-TV]] remake, ''[[How to Marry a Billionaire: A Christmas Tale]]''. It reversed the sex roles, and had three men looking to marry wealthy women. It starred [[John Stamos]], [[Joshua Malina]] and [[Shemar Moore]].

In 2007, [[Nicole Kidman]] bought the rights to ''How to Marry a Millionaire'' under her production company [[Blossom Films]], intending to produce and possibly star in a remake.<ref>[http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/content_display/news/e3ia76075fe469ef9a4c7607b47504d85bb Siegel, Tatiana. ''The Hollywood Reporter'' 2007-04-27]</ref>
