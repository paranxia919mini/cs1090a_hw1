'''''Fallen Leaves''''' ({{langx|fi|'''Kuolleet lehdet'''|lit=Dead Leaves}}) is a 2023 [[Romance film|romantic]] [[comedy-drama]] film written and directed by [[Aki Kaurismäki]].<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url=http://www.filmikamari.fi/pressit/tiedotteet.php?action=show&news_id=5377|title=Aki Kaurismäki ohjaa työläistrilogiansa neljännen osan|author=B-Plan Distributionin 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230609133340/https://www.filmikamari.fi/pressit/tiedotteet.php?action=show&news_id=5377|url-status=live}}</ref> It is Kaurismäki's 20th full-length film and a continuation of his ''Proletariat'' series, which was originally planned as a trilogy and already includes ''[[Shadows in Paradise (1986 film)|Shadows in Paradise]]'' (1986), ''[[Ariel (1988 film)|Ariel]]'' (1988), and ''[[The Match Factory Girl]]'' (1990).<ref>{{cite news|url=https://yle.fi/aihe/artikkeli/2017/09/14/koyhat-luuserit-paremman-maailman-haussa-kaurismaen-tyolaistrilogia-on|title=Köyhät luuserit paremman maailman haussa – Kaurismäen työläistrilogia on [[Alma Pöysti]] and [[Jussi Vatanen]] star in the main roles. The film is a co-production between Finland and Germany.<ref>{{cite web|last=Romney|first=Jonathan|url=https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/fallen-leaves-cannes-review/5182466.article|title='Fallen Leaves': Cannes Review|website=[[Screen Daily]]|date=22 May 2023|access-date=13 February 2025|url-access=limited|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230522181817/https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/fallen-leaves-cannes-review/5182466.article|archive-date=22 May 2023|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film premiered on 22 May 2023 at the [[2023 Cannes Film Festival|76th Cannes Film Festival]], and was selected to compete for the [[Palme d'Or]] in its main competition section, where it won the [[Jury Prize (Cannes Film Festival)|Jury Prize]]. It was released in Finland on 15 September 2023. It received critical acclaim and was named one of the top five international films of 2023 by the New York-based [[National Board of Review]]. The film was chosen as the [[List of Finnish submissions for the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Finnish entry]] for [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best International Feature Film]] at the [[96th Academy Awards]],<ref>{{cite web|url=https://yle.fi/a/74-20049424|title=Kuolleet lehdet on Suomen Oscar-ehdokas – Kaurismäki Ylelle: Mikään ei voisi kiinnostaa vähemmän|first=Janne|last=Sundqvist|publisher=[[Yle]]|date=13 September 2023|access-date=13 September 2023|language=fi|archive-date=8 November 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231108024543/https://yle.fi/a/74-20049424|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hs.fi/kulttuuri/art-2000009852449.html|title=Aki Kaurismäen Kuolleet lehdet valittiin Suomen Oscar-ehdokkaaksi – ohjaajan boikotti päättyi tällä erää|first=Sanna|last=Raita-aho|work=[[Helsingin Sanomat]]|date=13 September 2023|access-date=13 September 2023|language=fi|archive-date=25 September 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230925232611/https://www.hs.fi/kulttuuri/art-2000009852449.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and was one of the 15 finalist films in the December shortlist.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bergeson |first=Samantha |date=21 December 2023 |title=2024 Oscar Shortlists Unveiled: 'Barbie,' 'Poor Things,' 'Maestro,' and 'The Zone of Interest' Make the Cut |url=https://www.indiewire.com/awards/results/2024-oscar-shortlists-1234936672/ |access-date=22 December 2023 |website=[[IndieWire]] |archive-date=13 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240113023537/https://www.indiewire.com/awards/results/2024-oscar-shortlists-1234936672/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
Ansa{{efn|[[Finnish language|Finnish]] {{literal translation|trap}}.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Sims|first=David|date=17 November 2023|title=A Must-See Comedy About Miserable People Looking for Love|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2023/11/fallen-leaves-review/676030/|magazine=[[The Atlantic]]|access-date=3 December 2023|archive-date=4 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241004114647/https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/archive/2023/11/fallen-leaves-review/676030/|url-status=live}}</ref>}} is a single woman who lives in [[Helsinki]]. She works on a [[zero-hour contract]] in a supermarket, stocking the shelves. The equally lonely and [[Depression (mood)|depressed]] Holappa works as a [[Sandblasting|sandblaster]] and frequently drinks on the job. One night, co-worker and room-mate Huotari proposes they go to a [[karaoke bar]], and he reluctantly complies. There, the two drink heavily and Huotari sings for the audience. His singing is complimented by Liisa, who is drinking with her friend and co-worker Ansa. While Huotari tries to seduce Liisa and fails, Holappa and Ansa notice each other, but do not talk. Another night, while returning home, Ansa sees Holappa [[Blackout (drug-related amnesia)|drunk and unconscious]] at a bus stop. After checking that he is okay, she leaves just before he wakes up.

Ansa is eventually fired for taking expired food from the supermarket. In desperate need of money, she starts working as a dishwasher at a bar for a meagre [[Unreported employment|under-the-table salary]]. On her second day on the job, her boss is arrested for peddling drugs, and she is jobless once again, but runs into Holappa who buys her a coffee and a pastry. They later see ''[[The Dead Don't Die (2019 film)|The Dead Don't Die]]'' at the cinema and seem to hit it off. At the end of the date, she writes down her phone number for Holappa, but says she won't reveal her name until the next time they meet. While stopping to smoke a cigarette, he unknowingly drops and loses the paper from his pocket.

Ansa grows disappointed waiting for a phone call while Holappa has no way of tracking her down. Remembering that Ansa told him that she lives near the cinema, he repeatedly goes there and waits at the entrance for hours. He eventually sustains an injury at work, and is fired after failing a [[Breathalyzer|breathalyzer test]], which also gets him kicked out of the company dormitory. Ansa and Holappa eventually reunite outside the cinema one night, and Ansa invites him over for dinner. Everything seems to go well, until she catches him drinking from a hip flask in her flat. Because of the tragedies [[alcoholism]] has caused to her family, an angry Ansa confronts Holappa, who refuses to "take orders" and storms out. Without a home, he sleeps on benches at night and gets a job in construction, but is once again fired for drinking at work. Nonetheless, he continues to despondently drink at bars.

One day, while returning from her new [[ironworker]] job, Ansa saves a homeless dog by adopting it. At home, she washes the dog and treats it with affection. Missing Ansa, who also misses him, Holappa finds housing and pours his liquor bottles down the drain. He calls Ansa (who answers with her name, while he greets her with "it's me") to apologise, and they agree to meet up right away. On his way to her house, however, he is struck by a train.

Ansa waits for him at home, oblivious to the accident. One day while walking her dog, Ansa runs into Huotari who reveals that Holappa is in a [[coma]]. Huotari also asks for Liisa's number, despite knowing she thinks he is too old for her. Ansa gives him the number and goes to the hospital. Though she only knows Holappa's surname, Ansa cons the staff by pretending to be his "[[Religious sister|sister in faith]]" in order to see him. Each day while Holappa is in a coma, she reads and talks to him. Meanwhile, Liisa indicates that she and Huotari have begun seeing each other. 

One day, Holappa wakes up and leaves the hospital with Ansa and her dog. Holappa asks what the dog is called. She answers "[[Charlie Chaplin|Chaplin]]".

==Production==
''Fallen Leaves'' was produced by Sputnik Oy and [[Bufo (company)|Bufo]], and co-produced by the German company [[Pandora Film]]. The production also received the support of the [[Finnish Film Foundation]], [[Yle|Yle, the Finnish Broadcasting Company]], [[ZDF]]/[[Arte|ARTE]], Filmförderungsanstalt and Film-und Medienstiftung NRW.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.the-match-factory.com/catalogue/films/fallen-leaves.html|title=Fallen Leaves|publisher=The Match Factory|access-date=29 July 2023}}</ref>

Filming began in August 2022 in the [[Kallio]] district of Helsinki. [[Timo Salminen]] served as director of photography.<ref name=":1">{{cite news|url=https://www.aamuposti.fi/uutissuomalainen/4661952|title=Ohjaaja Aki Kaurismäki tekee uutta elokuvaa – "Sormeni ja alitajuntani kirjoittivat näköjään neljännen osan työläistrilogiaan"|newspaper=[[Aamuposti]]|date=10 June 2022|access-date=10 June 2022|language=fi|archive-date=24 April 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230424211810/https://www.aamuposti.fi/uutissuomalainen/4661952|url-status=live}}</ref> Production was done "in one take" and in Kaurismäki's typical style, according to Pöysti: "don't rehearse and don't read the script too much".<ref name="Nordisk Conference" />

The time period of the film is unclear, and it has been said to be set in an [[Parallel universes in fiction|alternative reality]].<ref name="Episodi">{{cite web|url=https://www.episodi.fi/elokuvat/kuolleet-lehdet/|title=Kuolleet lehdet – elokuvan arvostelu|last=Huhtala|first=Jussi|date=14 September 2023|website=Episodi.fi|language=fi|access-date=7 October 2023|archive-date=4 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241004114612/https://www.episodi.fi/elokuvat/kuolleet-lehdet/|url-status=live}}</ref> The wall calendar shown in the film shows autumn 2024, but the news narrated on the radio takes place in the early moments of the 2022 [[Russian invasion of Ukraine]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Kartastenpää|first=Tero|date=7 September 2023|title=Arvio: Viisi tähteä – Kuolleet lehdet on parasta Kaurismäkeä|url=https://suomenkuvalehti.fi/kulttuuri/arvio-viisi-tahtea-kuolleet-lehdet-on-parasta-kaurismakea/|newspaper=[[Suomen Kuvalehti]]|language=fi|access-date=7 October 2023|archive-date=4 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241004114547/https://suomenkuvalehti.fi/kulttuuri/arvio-viisi-tahtea-kuolleet-lehdet-on-parasta-kaurismakea/|url-status=live}}</ref> Tube radios, landline phones and old-fashioned trains are used in the world of the film, among other things.<ref name="Episodi"/> It also contains several references to films, including Jim Jarmusch's ''[[The Dead Don't Die (2019 film)|The Dead Don't Die]]'', David Lean's ''[[Brief Encounter]]'', and Charlie Chaplin's ''[[Limelight (1952 film)|Limelight]]''.<ref name="Nordisk Conference" /> According to [[Alma Pöysti]], the female lead, "the film is about lonely people with baggage, who meet later in life. It takes courage to fall in love later in life."<ref name="Nordisk Conference" /> It has been described as a [[tragicomedy]].<ref name="Nordisk Selection">{{cite web|url=https://nordiskfilmogtvfond.com/news/stories/cannes-film-festival-selects-aki-kaurismakis-fallen-leaves-for-main-competition|title=Cannes Film Festival selects Aki Kaurismäki's Fallen Leaves for main competition|last=Pham|first=Annika|publisher=Nordisk Film & TV Fond|date=13 April 2023|access-date=3 May 2023|archive-date=4 June 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230604103706/https://nordiskfilmogtvfond.com/news/stories/cannes-film-festival-selects-aki-kaurismakis-fallen-leaves-for-main-competition|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 97% based on 169 reviews. The website's consensus reads, "A quirky tale of star-crossed lovers, ''Fallen Leaves'' is a life-affirming gem from Finnish filmmaker Kaurismäki."<ref name="RT">{{cite Rotten Tomatoes|title=Fallen Leaves|type=m|id=fallen_leaves|access-date=26 February 2026}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted average]] score of 86 out of 100, based on 30 critic reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=fallen-leaves|type=movie|title=Fallen Leaves|access-date=12 February 2024}}</ref>

Reviewing the film following its Cannes premiere, [[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' wrote that the film is "romantic and sweet-natured, in a deadpan style that in no way undermines or ironises the emotions involved and with some sharp things to say about contemporary politics."<ref>{{cite news|last=Bradshaw|first=Peter|author-link=Peter Bradshaw|date=22 May 2023|title=Fallen Leaves review – deadpan Aki Kaurismäki comedy with springtime in its heart|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2023/may/22/fallen-leaves-review-deadpan-aki-kaurismaki-comedy-with-springtime-in-its-heart|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=24 August 2023|archive-date=4 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241004114547/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2023/may/22/fallen-leaves-review-deadpan-aki-kaurismaki-comedy-with-springtime-in-its-heart|url-status=live}}</ref> Glenn Kenny of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film four out of four stars, stating that "just an hour and twenty minutes long, the movie is a soulful romance that goes through conventional narrative paces."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/fallen-leaves-movie-review-2023|title=Fallen Leaves movie review & film summary (2023)|last=Kenny|first=Glenn|date=17 November 2023|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|access-date=15 December 2023|archive-date=15 December 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231215190722/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/fallen-leaves-movie-review-2023|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' called the film a "quiet masterpiece" and possibly Kaurismäki's greatest film.<ref name="Time Magazine 2023-11-17">{{cite news |last=Zacharek |first=Stephanie |url=https://time.com/6335626/fallen-leaves-review/ |title=Fallen Leaves Is a Quiet Masterpiece About Tenderness in a Cold World |work=Time Magazine |publisher=Time Magazine |date=17 November 2023 |accessdate=4 January 2024 |archive-date=4 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240104024653/https://time.com/6335626/fallen-leaves-review/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

American filmmaker [[Dean Fleischer Camp]] praised the film, saying "Kaurismäki tells sincere stories about marginalized peoples struggling for the basic stuff—food, shelter, dignity, love. But the films themselves are bone-dry comedies which encourage us to laugh at the protagonists' misfortunes, or at least at the absurdity of the world's indifference to them. And by depicting such sympathetic underdogs with such a coolly detached brush, Kaurismäki evokes in us, the audience, our very best. When the lights come up, I feel radicalized toward empathy and sincerity, ready to take up arms against the cynicism within myself and against the ironic posturing that is pop culture's favorite self-defensive crouch."<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Fleischer Camp|first=Dean|author-link=Dean Fleischer Camp|date=13 December 2023|title=16 Directors Praise 2023's Best Films: Alfonso Cuarón on 'Killers of the Flower Moon,' Jane Campion on 'Priscilla' and More|url=https://variety.com/lists/directors-best-films-2023/|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=1 January 2024|archive-date=4 October 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241004114610/https://variety.com/lists/directors-best-films-2023/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In August 2023, it was voted as the Best Film of the Year by the International Film Critics Federation [[Fipresci]].<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Hopewell|first=John|date=23 August 2023|title=Aki Kaurismaki's 'Fallen Leaves' Voted Best Film of the Past Year by International Film Critics Federation Fipresci|url=https://variety.com/2023/film/festivals/aki-kaurismaki-fallen-leaves-fipresci-1235702768/|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=23 August 2023|archive-date=23 August 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230823094405/https://variety.com/2023/film/festivals/aki-kaurismaki-fallen-leaves-fipresci-1235702768/|url-status=live}}</ref>
