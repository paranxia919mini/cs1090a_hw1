'''Charlestown''' is a [[Neighborhoods in Boston|neighborhood]] in [[Boston]], Massachusetts, United States, and the oldest settlement in modern-day Boston.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bostonredevelopmentauthority.org/neighborhoods/Neighborhoods.asp?action=ViewHood&HoodID=4 |title=Boston's Neighborhoods: Charlestown |year=2010 |publisher=Boston Redevelopment Authority (BRA) |access-date=August 17, 2010}}</ref> Also called '''Mishawum''' by the [[Massachusett]], it is located on a [[peninsula]] north of the [[Charles River]], across from downtown Boston, and also adjoins the [[Mystic River]] and [[Boston Harbor]] waterways. Charlestown was laid out in 1629 by engineer [[Thomas Graves (engineer)|Thomas Graves]], one of its earliest settlers, during the reign of [[Charles I of England]]. It was originally a separate town and the first capital of the [[Massachusetts Bay Colony]].

Charlestown became a city in 1848 and was annexed by Boston on January 5, 1874. With that, it also switched from [[Middlesex County, Massachusetts|Middlesex County]], to which it had belonged since 1643, to [[Suffolk County, Massachusetts|Suffolk County]]. It has had a substantial [[Irish Americans|Irish-American]] population since the migration of [[Irish people]] during the [[Great Irish Famine]] of the 1840s. Since the late 1980s, the neighborhood has changed dramatically because of its proximity to downtown and its colonial architecture. A mix of [[yuppie]] and [[Upper middle class|upper-middle-class]] [[gentrification]] has influenced much of the area, as it has in many of Boston's neighborhoods.

In the 21st century, Charlestown's diversity has expanded dramatically, along with growing rates of the very poor and very wealthy. Today Charlestown is a largely residential neighborhood, with much housing near the waterfront, overlooking the Boston skyline. Charlestown is home to many historic sites, hospitals and organizations, with access from the Orange Line [[Sullivan Square]] or Community College stops or the I-93 expressway.

== History ==
{{More citations needed section|date=September 2022}}
[[File:Bunker Hill Monument and Statue.JPG|thumb|upright|left|The Bunker Hill Monument and [[William Prescott]] Statue]]

Thomas and Jane Walford<ref>{{cite web|url=http://kristinhall.org/fambly/Walford/WalfordName.html|title=Walford Family Line|access-date=2009-05-04|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111005170609/http://kristinhall.org/fambly/Walford/WalfordName.html|archive-date=2011-10-05|url-status=dead}}</ref> were the original English settlers of the peninsula between the [[Charles (river, Massachusetts)|Charles]] and the [[Mystic River|Mystic]]. They were given a grant by Sir Robert Gorges, with whom they had settled at [[Wessagusset Colony|Wessagusset]] ([[Weymouth, Massachusetts|Weymouth]]) in September 1623 and arrived at what they called Mishawaum in 1624. [[John Endicott]], first governor of [[Massachusetts Bay Colony]], sent William, Richard and Ralph Sprague to Mishawaum to lay out a settlement. Thomas Walford, acting as an interpreter with the Massachusett Indians, negotiated with the local [[sachem]] [[Wonohaquaham]] for Endicott and his people to settle there. Although Walford had a virtual monopoly on the region's available [[fur trading|furs]], he welcomed the newcomers and helped them in any way he could, unaware that his Episcopalian religious beliefs would cause him to be banished from Massachusetts to [[Portsmouth, New Hampshire|Portsmouth]], [[New Hampshire]] within three years.

Originally a [[Puritan]] English city during the Colonial era, Charlestown proper was founded in 1628 and settled July 4, 1629, by [[Thomas Graves (engineer)|Thomas Graves]], [[Increase Nowell]], Simon Hoyt, the Rev. Francis Bright, and [[William Sprague (1609–1675)|the Spragues (Ralph, Richard, & William Sprague)]], among some 100 others who preceded the [[Great Migration (Puritan)|Great Migration]]. [[John Winthrop]]'s company stopped here for some time in 1630, before deciding to accept the invitation of [[William Blaxton]] to settle across the [[Charles River]] with him on the [[Shawmut Peninsula|Shawmut peninsula]]. This was the first act in the foundation of the city of [[Boston]].
[[File:John Winthrop Great House Charlestown Massachusetts.jpg|thumb|right|1629 site of Puritan leader [[John Winthrop]]'s "Great House" in City Square, uncovered during the [[Big Dig (Boston, Massachusetts)|Big Dig]]]]
[[File:Charlestown massachusetts and bunker hill between 1890 and 1910.jpg|thumb|right|Bird's-eye view of [[Boston]], Charlestown, and Bunker Hill, between 1890 and 1910]]
[[File:Charlestown from atop the Bunker Hill Monument 2025.jpg|thumb|A view facing South of Charlestown from atop the [[Bunker Hill Monument]] in 2025, with the [[Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Memorial Bridge|Zakim Bridge]] and [[West End, Boston]] in the distance ]]
The territory of Charlestown was initially quite large. From it, [[Woburn, Massachusetts|Woburn]] was separated in 1642,{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} [[Malden, Massachusetts|Malden]] in 1649 (including what is now [[Melrose, Massachusetts|Melrose]] and [[Everett, Massachusetts|Everett]])<ref>{{cite web |title=A Condensed History of Melrose |url=http://www.cityofmelrose.org/Interns%2007/a_condensed_history_of.htm |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090615120514/http://www.cityofmelrose.org/Interns%2007/a_condensed_history_of.htm |archive-date=2009-06-15 }} City of Melrose. Retrieved 2010-07-15</ref> and [[Stoneham, Massachusetts|Stoneham]] in 1725.<ref>{{cite book|title=A History of New England|volume=1|editor=R. H. Howard and Henry E. Crocker|page=202|year=1880|publisher=Crocker & Co.|location=Boston|url=http://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/m/mdp/pt?seq=228&view=image&size=100&id=mdp.39015014744588&u=1&num=202}}</ref> [[South Medford]], the land south of the Mystic River (now surrounded by Somerville), was known as "Mistick Field" and was transferred from Charlestown to [[Medford, Massachusetts|Medford]] in 1754. This grant also included the "Charlestown Wood Lots" (the Medford part of the Middlesex Fells), and part of what was at the time Woburn (now Winchester).<ref>{{cite book |last1=Brooks |first1=Charles |last2=Whitmore |first2=William Henry |title=History of the Town of Medford, Middlesex County, Massachusetts: From Its First Settlement, in 1630, to the Present Time, 1855 |url=https://archive.org/details/historytownmedf00broogoog |date=1855 |page=[https://archive.org/details/historytownmedf00broogoog/page/n54 5] |location=Boston |publisher=James M. Usher |access-date=August 30, 2017}}</ref> Other parts of Medford were transferred to Charlestown in 1811. Still-rural [[Somerville, Massachusetts|Somerville]] was split off in 1842 as Charlestown was urbanizing. [[Everett, Massachusetts|Everett]], [[Burlington, Massachusetts|Burlington]], [[Arlington, Massachusetts|Arlington]] and [[Cambridge, Massachusetts|Cambridge]] also acquired areas originally allocated to Charlestown.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=in-5Ueuwn0wC&q=intitle:History+intitle:of+intitle:Medford+intitle:Massachusetts|title=History of the Town of Medford, Middlesex County, Massachusetts: From Its First Settlement, in 1630, to the Present Time, 1855|first1=Charles|last1=Brooks|first2=William Henry|last2=Whitmore|date=21 July 2018|publisher=J.M. Usher|access-date=21 July 2018|via=Google Books}}</ref> [[Landfill]] operations eliminated the narrow ''Charlestown Neck'' that connected the northwest end of the ''Charlestown Peninsula'' to the mainland at [[Sullivan Square]].

On June 17, 1775, the Charlestown Peninsula was the site of the [[Battle of Bunker Hill]], named for a hill at the northwest end of the peninsula near [[Charlestown Neck]]. British troops unloaded at Moulton's Point{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} and much of the battle took place on [[Breed's Hill]], which overlooked the harbor from about 400 yards off the southern end of the peninsula. Charlestown, including its wharves and dockyards, was almost completely torched{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}} during the battle by British forces.{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} The town was not appreciably rebuilt until the end of hostilities but, in 1786, the first bridge across the [[Charles (river, Massachusetts)|Charles River]] connected Boston with Charlestown.{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} An {{convert|87|acre|adj=on}} [[Boston Navy Yard|Navy Yard]] was established in 1800; [[Charlestown State Prison]] opened in 1805.{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} The [[Bunker Hill Monument]] was erected between 1827 and 1843 using [[Quincy, Massachusetts|Quincy]] granite brought to the site by a combination of purpose-built railway and barge. Notable businesses included the [[Bunker Hill Breweries]] (1821) and [[Schrafft's]] candy company (1861).

The [[Charlestown Branch Railroad]] opened in 1840 to Sweet's Wharf. Tudor Wharf was the departure point for the ships of the [[Tudor Ice Company]].

Around the 1860s an influx of [[Irish people|Irish]] immigrants arrived in Charlestown. The area long remained an Irish and Catholic stronghold similar to [[South Boston]], [[Somerville, Massachusetts|Somerville]], and [[Dorchester, Massachusetts|Dorchester]], to the extent that the informal demonym "[[Charlestown Townies|Townie]]" continues to imply the working-class Irish, as opposed to newer immigrants.

During the [[American Civil War|Civil War]], over 26,000 men joined the [[Union Army]] and [[Union Navy|Navy]] at the [[Boston Navy Yard|Navy Yard]], which was also responsible for constructing some of the most famous vessels of the conflict: the ''[[USS Merrimack (1855)|Merrimack]]'', the ''[[USS Hartford (1858)|Hartford]]'', and the ''[[USS Monadnock (1863)|Monadnock]]''.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://archive.boston.com/news/local/massachusetts/specials/civil_war_trail/ |title=Exploring a New Freedom Trail for the Civil War |work=Boston Globe |date=2016 |author-last=Anderson |author-first=Rob |author2=Dave Butler |author3=Lauren Frohne |author4=Tom Giratikanon |author5=[[Monica Ulmanu]] |author6=Jason Williams |author7=Alan Wirzbicki}}</ref> Following the war, the city commissioned [[Martin Milmore]] to construct its [[Charlestown Civil War Memorial|civil war memorial]], dedicated in 1872{{sfnp|''EB''|1911}} and still standing in the community's Training Field.<ref>{{citation |contribution-url=http://siris-artinventories.si.edu/ipac20/ipac.jsp?uri=full=3100001~!297744~!13 |contribution=The Charlestown Civil War Memorial |title=Art Inventories Catalog |publisher=Smithsonian American Art Museum |location=[[Washington, DC|Washington]]}}</ref>

The city developed a water supply from the [[Mystic Lakes (Boston, Massachusetts)|Mystic Lakes]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mwra.state.ma.us/04water/html/hist1.htm|title=MWRA – Water System History|website=www.mwra.state.ma.us|access-date=21 July 2018}}</ref> and, on October 7, 1873, a vote was held to determine whether Charlestown should leave Middlesex County and join Boston as part of Suffolk County. Out of its 32,040 residents,{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}} 2,240 voted in support of the merger and 1,947 opposed. Boston residents also approved the question, 5,960–1,868.<ref>"The Result in Figures", ''[[The Boston Globe]]'', p. 5, October 8, 1873.</ref> Charlestown's separate city government was dissolved the next year.{{sfnp|''EB''|1878}}

During the early 1960s, the city initiated plans to demolish and redevelop sixty percent of the housing in Charlestown.<ref>[https://bpl.bibliocommons.com/item/show/1027820075_charlestown_district_study_5 "Charlestown District Study #5"], Boston City Planning Department, Comprehensive Planning Section, March 14, 1960, p. 1; [https://archive.org/details/charlestownpreli00bost "Charlestown Preliminary Sketch Plan"], Boston Redevelopment Authority, December 15, 1961, p. II-2.</ref> In 1963, the [[Boston Redevelopment Authority]] (BRA) held a town meeting to discuss their development plans with the community. The BRA's dealings with Boston's [[West End, Boston, Massachusetts|West End]] had created an atmosphere of distrust towards [[urban renewal]] in Boston, and Charlestown residents opposed the plan by an overwhelming majority. By 1965, the plan had been reduced to tearing down only eleven percent of the neighborhood, as well as the removal of the [[Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority#Streetcar subways and elevated rail|elevated rail]] tracks.<ref>Jones, Michael, ''The Slaughter of Cities: Urban Renewal and Ethnic Cleansing'' pp. 528–29, St. Augustine's Press, South end Indiana, 2004. {{ISBN|1-58731-775-3}}</ref>

Throughout the 1960s until the mid-1990s, Charlestown was infamous for its [[Irish Mob]] presence. Charlestown's [[McLaughlin Brothers]] were involved in a gang war with neighboring [[Somerville, Massachusetts|Somerville]]'s [[Winter Hill Gang]], during the [[Irish Mob Wars]] of the 1960s. In the late 1980s, however, Charlestown underwent a massive [[Yuppie]] gentrification process similar to that of the [[South End, Boston|South End]]. Drawn to its proximity to downtown and its colonial, red-brick, row-house housing stock, similar to that of [[Beacon Hill, Boston, Massachusetts|Beacon Hill]], many yuppie and upper-middle-class professionals moved to the neighborhood. In the late 1990s, additional gentrification took place, similar to that in neighboring Somerville.{{Citation needed|date=June 2008}} Today{{when|date=December 2023}} the neighborhood is a mix of yuppies, upper-middle-class and middle-class residents, housing projects, and a small working-class.

One of the oldest neighborhoods of Boston, Charlestown is home to the Bunker Hill Monument and historic Charlestown Navy Yard.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bostonredevelopmentauthority.org/neighborhoods/charlestown/at-a-glance|title=At a Glance {{!}} Boston Redevelopment Authority|last=Interactive|first=Boston|publisher=bostonredevelopmentauthority.org|access-date=2016-04-22}}</ref> Charlestown today is a mainly residential neighborhood with an institutional presence. Major institutions include [[Bunker Hill Community College]], Spaulding Rehabilitation Center, and a facility of Massachusetts General Hospital.  Many commercial ventures line the Mystic River along Medford Street and Terminal Street. The Navy Yard, home to {{USS|Constitution}}, is now part of the [[Boston National Historical Park]], which marks the southern edge of the neighborhood. The waterfront has two marinas, Constitution Marina and Shipyard Quarters Marina.

== Geography ==
[[File:BunkerHillBoats.jpg|thumb|left|[[Sailboat]]s [[Mooring (watercraft)|moored]] on the Charlestown side of the [[Charles River]] with [[Bunker Hill Monument]] in the distance]]
Charlestown is located north of downtown Boston on a peninsula extending southeastward between the [[Charles River]] and the [[Mystic River]].

''{{Vanchor|City Square}}'' in the southern part of Charlestown was the location of the city hall before annexation by Boston. It is also the terminus of the [[Charlestown Bridge]] and the former [[Warren Bridge]], and was formerly a stop on the [[Charlestown Elevated]]. The [[Central Artery]] was built between 1951 and 1954, routing elevated ramps through City Square. The Central Artery North Area (CANA) project moved these underground, into the City Square Tunnel, making way for a revitalized surface park.

The {{Convert|.17|acre|ha|adj=on}} Thompson Square is located at the confluence of Main Street, Dexter Row, Green Street, and Austin Street.<ref>{{Cite web|last=|first=|date=|title=COMMUNITY OPEN SPACE & RECREATION MISSION: THE NEIGHBORHOODS|url=https://www.cityofboston.gov/parks/pdfs/os3d.pdf|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060929204924/http://www.cityofboston.gov/parks/pdfs/os3d.pdf |archive-date=2006-09-29 |access-date=|website=}}</ref> [[Thompson Square station]] was formerly a stop on the [[Charlestown Elevated]].

A small portion of Charlestown is north of the Mystic River, containing the northern approach to the Malden Bridge and now the entrance to [[Encore Boston Harbor]] (which made jurisdiction over the casino a matter of dispute). This land was formerly a peninsula, but landfill has more completely connected it to the mainland of Everett.<ref>[https://www.digitalcommonwealth.org/search/commonwealth:9s161921m 1905 map]</ref> This boundary was part of the original 1649 separation of Malden from Charlestown, where the Penny Ferry<ref>Established in 1640 on land owned by the town, according to [https://archive.org/details/historyofcharles00froth <!-- quote=ferry. --> The History of Charlestown, Massachusetts By Richard Frothingham], page 95.</ref> operated at the time; the first bridge opened on the site in 1787.<ref>[https://www.necn.com/news/new-england/_NECN__Wynns_Everett__Or_Is_It_Boston___Casino_NECN-247427641.html Wynns Everett Or Is It Boston? Casino]</ref>

Two small hills mostly covered in residential buildings fill the interior of the neighborhood: Bunker Hill, which is more northerly, and Breed's Hill, which is the site of the [[Bunker Hill Monument]]. Bunker Hill was named after George Bunker, who had owned it during early settlement in the 1600s.<ref>[https://www.bunkerfamilyassn.org/bunker_hill.html Bunker Hill]</ref><ref>[https://www.bunkerfamilyassn.org/George_Bunker_Charlestown.pdf George Bunker of Charlestown, Massachusetts]</ref>

== Arts and culture ==
[[File:StM.jpg|thumb|right|Interior of [[St. Mary – St. Catherine of Siena Parish|St. Mary's Church]] (1887–1893)]]
[[File:Bunker hill monument.jpg|thumb|right|[[Bunker Hill Monument]]. Bunker Hill Day, [[Boston]], and Charlestown, between 1890 and 1901]]
[[File:USA-Winthrop Square.JPG|thumb|right|[[Winthrop Square (Charlestown, Boston)|Winthrop Square]]]]
[[File:USA-Soldiers Monument.JPG|thumb|right|[[Charlestown Civil War Memorial]]]]
Charlestown has many places of historical interest, some of which are included along the northern end of Boston's [[Freedom Trail]]. The Freedom Trail ends at the [[Bunker Hill Monument]] commemorating the famous [[Battle of Bunker Hill]], an early major battle in the [[American Revolutionary War]]. Launched in 1797, the [[USS Constitution|USS ''Constitution'']] is the oldest commissioned vessel in the US Navy and the oldest warship in the world still afloat. Today it is docked in the [[Charlestown Navy Yard]], today part of the National Park Service. Charlestown was also the location from which [[Paul Revere]] began his famous "midnight ride" before the [[Battles of Lexington and Concord]]. A restaurant opened in 1780 and still in operation, [[Warren Tavern]], claims to have been one of Revere's favorite taverns. Of Charlestown's churches, [[St. Mary – St. Catherine of Siena Parish|St. Mary's]] (1887–1893) is considered one of the masterpieces of [[Patrick Keely]]. In St. John's Episcopal Church, on Devens Street, the central stained glass above the altar is a notable work of William James McPherson, a Boston designer who later designed the stained glass for the Connecticut State Capitol.<ref>{{cite web|title=Connecticut State Capitol Facts|url=http://www.cga.ct.gov/capitoltours/capitolfacts.htm|access-date=31 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140425181602/http://www.cga.ct.gov/capitoltours/capitolfacts.htm|archive-date=25 April 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The [[Warren Tavern]] first opened in 1780.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.warrentavern.com/|title=Warren Tavern {{!}} A Historic Tavern in Charlestown|website=www.warrentavern.com|access-date=2016-04-15}}</ref> It is located at 2 Pleasant Street. The building was one of the first built after the [[Battle of Bunker Hill]]. The Tavern took its name from [[Joseph Warren]], American Patriot who played a key role in the American Revolution and was killed in the Battle of Bunker Hill. It was Warren who directed Paul Revere and William Dawes to send the message to Samuel Adams and John Hancock that the British were setting out to raid the town of Concord. Warren's friend Captain Eliphelet Newell decided to build a tavern named after his friend. George Washington visited the tavern when he came to Massachusetts to visit his friend Benjamin Frothingham.{{Citation needed|date=October 2017}} After the Tavern was closed in 1813, the building served other purposes, and then was saved in the 1970s. The Tavern was reopened in 1972.

The Constitution Yacht Charter is located on Boston Harbor.

According to the U.S. Census Bureau in its 2007–2011 report, the population of Charlestown is 16,685, comprising 7,843 males and 8,842 females. The largest age group is 25 to 29 years (14.6%), the second-largest is 30 to 34 (12.3%), and the third-largest is 35 to 39 (9.7%).

The majority of the population is white at 12,587 (75.4%). Minorities include Black or African at 1,227 (7.4%), Asian at 1,253 (7.5%), Hispanic or Latino at 1,227 (7.4%), and those of two or more races at 371 (2.2%). In recent years, the percentage of minorities living in Charlestown has increased from 4.9% of the population in 1990 to 23.5% in 2010. The population consists of 15.9% who are foreign born, 48.5% of whom are [[Naturalization|naturalized citizens]], and 51.5% who are not.

The median household income is $89,017, and the median family income is $100,725. The median income for whites is $103,652; that for Blacks or African Americans, $12,143; for Hispanics or Latinos, $30,833; for Asians, $61,875; and for others, $16,876.

Seventeen percent of the population and 37% of the children live below the [[Poverty in the United States#Recent poverty rate and guidelines|Federal Poverty Line]]. Of married couples, 32.4% are living in poverty with families. Of male householders with no wife present, 3.4% live in poverty; and of female householders with no husband, 64.2% live in poverty.

== Government and infrastructure ==
{{Main|Boston City Council|List of mayors of Boston}}

===== Public libraries =====
[[Boston Public Library]] operates the Charlestown Branch. The library first opened in the Warren Institution for Savings building on January 7, 1862. The library moved to a larger space in the new Charlestown City Hall in 1869. In 1913 the branch moved to the intersection of Monument Avenue and Monument Square, in proximity to the [[Bunker Hill Monument]]. The branch moved to its current location in 1970.<ref>"[http://www.bpl.org/branches/charlestown.htm Charlestown Branch Library] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130105184049/http://www.bpl.org/branches/charlestown.htm |date=January 5, 2013 }}." === Housing policy ===
{{Further|Boston Housing Authority}}
The Bunker Hill Public Housing has divided Bunker Hill Street into two Charlestowns. The housing development company Corcoran-SunCal plans to make changes and replace the 1,100 affordable units. "While preserving the affordable units, Corcoran-SunCal will also create approximately 1,700 additional market and moderate-rate units". This company will allow all current residents to move back into the housing complex. According to Project Manager Sarah Barnet, "by creating both affordable and market rate housing at the site the area will become a more thriving section of the neighborhood, a destination area for residents from all over a Charlestown and a high quality place for people to live".<ref>{{cite news |last=Staff |title=Housing Developer and Community Working to Make Project Successful |url=http://charlestownbridge.com/2016/03/25/housing-developer-and-community-working-to-make-project-successful/ |date=March 25, 2016 |newspaper=Charlestown Patriot-Bridge |access-date=March 1, 2017}}</ref>

== Transportation ==
The [[Interstate 93]] as the "Northern Expressway viaduct" travels roughly northwest–southeast and passes through the Sullivan Square area. The Interstate act as a boundary of Charlestown neighborhood with points heading west with only two roads heading westward: Cambridge Street in the north and Austin Street/Gilmore Bridge to the south. [[U.S. Route 1 in Massachusetts|US 1]] diverges with Interstate 93 at the [[Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Memorial Bridge]] where US 1 becomes a toll road and passes below [[Paul Revere Park]] and [[City Square]] before becoming the [[Tobin Bridge]] to the City of [[Chelsea, Massachusetts|Chelsea]] across the [[Mystic River]]. State routes passing through Charlestown include [[Massachusetts Route 38|38]] to the City of [[Somerville, Massachusetts|Somerville]] and [[Massachusetts Route 99|99]] to the City of [[Everett, Massachusetts|Everett]]. The sole city-owned road linking the neighborhood Charleston with Downtown Boston is North Washington Street to the Southwest.

According to the Census from 2010 to 2014, 53.7% of the population will drive to work and 30.0% will take some form of public transportation to get to their jobs and Charlestown is well served by public transportation as it is accessible by several forms of public transportation, including train, bus and ferry.

The train transportation is the [[Orange Line (MBTA)|MBTA Orange Line]], the [[Community College (MBTA station)|Community College station]], located near Bunker Hill Community College and serves the center of the town; and the [[Sullivan Square (MBTA station)|Sullivan Square station]], located on what was once a narrow neck of land referred to as the [[Charlestown Neck]].

Two bus lines serve Charlestown. Both routes start at Sullivan Square. and travel to the [[Financial District, Boston|Financial District]] of downtown Boston. The 93 bus goes from Sullivan Station, downtown via Bunker Hill Street and Haymarket Station.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mbta.com/schedules_and_maps/bus/routes/?route=93|title=93 – Bus – MBTA|first=Massachusetts Bay Transportation|last=Authority|website=www.mbta.com|access-date=21 July 2018}}</ref> The 92 bus runs from Assembly Square Mall, downtown via Sullivan Square Station, Main Street and Haymarket Station.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.mbta.com/schedules_and_maps/bus/routes/?route=92|title=92 – Bus – MBTA|first=Massachusetts Bay Transportation|last=Authority|website=www.mbta.com|access-date=21 July 2018}}</ref>

Charlestown is also accessible via the Charlestown Navy Yard Ferry Terminal where the MBTA operates a ferry between the Navy Yard and [[Long Wharf (Boston)|Long Wharf]] (near the [[New England Aquarium]]),<ref>MBTA, Ferry F4 Schedule[Long Wharf (Boston)]</ref> making this a popular choice among both commuters and tourists.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.celebrateboston.com/mbta/charlestown-long-wharf-ferry.htm|title=Charlestown Long Wharf Ferry Attraction|website=www.celebrateboston.com|access-date=21 July 2018}}</ref>
 
The [[Boston Harborwalk]] and the [[Freedom Trail]] pass through the neighborhood.
