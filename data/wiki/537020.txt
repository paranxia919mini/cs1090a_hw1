'''Charles Stuart Kaufman''' ({{IPAc-en|ˈ|k|ɔː|f|m|ə|n}}; born November 19, 1958) is an American filmmaker and novelist. His work is distinguished by [[postmodernist film|postmodernist]] and [[Surrealist cinema|surrealist]] storytelling, with many critics considering him an [[auteur]]. Getting his start as a television writer, Kaufman gained prominence for his collaborations with directors [[Spike Jonze]] and [[Michel Gondry]] on ''[[Being John Malkovich]]'' (1999), ''[[Adaptation (film)|Adaptation]]'' (2002), and ''[[Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind]]'' (2004), before going on to direct films himself with ''[[Synecdoche, New York]]'' (2008), ''[[Anomalisa]]'' (2015), and ''[[I'm Thinking of Ending Things]]'' (2020). In 2020, he released a novel, ''[[Antkind]]''.

==Early life and education==
Kaufman was born on November 19, 1958, in New York City to a [[American Jews|Jewish]] family,<ref>{{cite web|first=Stephen|last=Applebaum|url=https://www.thejc.com/its-not-easy-being-charlie-kaufman-yn53qrri|title=It's not easy being Charlie Kaufman|work=The Jewish Chronicle|date=May 7, 2009|access-date=April 27, 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|first=Howard|last=Feinstein|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2009/12/decade-charlie-kaufman-on-synecdoche-new-york-246078/|title=Decade: Charlie Kaufman on "Synecdoche, New York"|work=IndieWire|date=December 28, 2009|access-date=April 27, 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|first=Jason|last=Solomons|url=https://www.thejc.com/life/film/tradition-no-i-just-want-to-write-what-i-think-is-funny-aqzdnkwl|title=Tradition? No, I just want to write what I think is funny|work=The Jewish Chronicle|date=March 17, 2016|access-date=April 27, 2018}}</ref><ref name=TimesTribe>{{Cite news|first= Nate|last= Bloom|author-link= Nate Bloom  |title= The tribe at the Oscars, 2016  |newspaper=[[Times of Israel]]|date=February 25, 2016 |url=http://jewishstandard.timesofisrael.com/the-tribe-at-the-oscars-2016/  }}</ref> the son of Helen and Myron Kaufman.<ref name="google">{{cite book|title=The Philosophy of Charlie Kaufman|author=LaRocca, D.|date=2011|publisher=University Press of Kentucky|isbn=9780813133928|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=IorAJ5yVdFAC|page=3|access-date=October 15, 2014}}</ref> He grew up in [[Massapequa, New York]], before moving to [[West Hartford, Connecticut]].<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography">{{cite web|url=http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=31&Itemid=34 |title=Biography |publisher=BeingCharlieKaufman.com |access-date=August 28, 2012}}</ref> In high school, Kaufman was in the drama club, performing in numerous productions before landing the lead role in a production of ''[[Play It Again, Sam (play)|Play It Again, Sam]]'' during his senior year.<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography"/> After high school graduation, Kaufman attended [[Boston University]] before transferring to [[New York University]], where he studied film. While attending NYU, Kaufman met Paul Proch, with whom he wrote many unproduced scripts and plays.<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography"/>

==Career==
=== Early comedy work (1983–1997) ===
Between 1983 and 1984, Kaufman and Proch wrote comedic articles and spoofs for ''[[National Lampoon (magazine)|National Lampoon]]''. His work included parodies of [[Kurt Vonnegut]] and the [[X-Men]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php/scripts-writing/scripts-writing/other-writing |title=Scans of said articles |publisher=Beingcharliekaufman.com |access-date=December 4, 2017}}</ref> Kaufman and Proch tried to get their screenplays produced, sending them to many people in the film industry. The only response the two ever received was a supportive letter from [[Alan Arkin]] about their screenplay ''Purely Coincidental''.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://grimshaw.jeff.tripod.com/journeyintomadness.html|title=Journey Into Madness|archive-date=February 10, 2017|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170210182314/http://grimshaw.jeff.tripod.com/journeyintomadness.html}}</ref> In hope of finding a [[talent agent]], he wrote a portfolio of [[spec scripts]] based on television series such as ''[[Married... with Children]]'', ''[[Newhart]]'', ''[[It's Garry Shandling's Show]]'' and ''[[The Simpsons]]''. While pursuing his writing career, he began working odd jobs in [[customer service]] to support himself and his wife, Denise. During the late 1980s, Kaufman lived and worked in [[Minneapolis]], working at the ''[[Star Tribune]]'' circulation department for four and a half years, as well as the [[Minneapolis Institute of Art]].<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography" />

In 1991, one of Kaufman's spec scripts finally got attention, and he gained a talent agent. The agent suggested Kaufman move from [[Minneapolis]] to [[Los Angeles]] in search of more job prospects. He moved to Los Angeles alone for two months, interviewing for a variety of different television writing jobs. The only offer he got was a [[Comedy Central]] series titled ''Access America'', hosted by actor [[Fred Willard]], which coincidentally was being filmed in Minneapolis. Kaufman was prepared to accept the job and move back to Minneapolis until he was offered a job by [[David Mirkin]], writing for the second season of his and [[Chris Elliott]]'s sitcom ''[[Get a Life (American TV series)|Get a Life]]'', starring Elliott.<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography"/> He wrote two episodes of ''Get a Life'' before its cancellation in 1992. At first, Kaufman found the experience of working on a writing staff nerve-wracking and did not speak in the writer's room for the first few weeks. After his work was well received by other members of the staff, Kaufman overcame his timidity and became more amenable to showing others his work.

After ''Get a Life'''s cancellation, Kaufman tried to get work on series such as ''[[Seinfeld]]'', ''[[The Larry Sanders Show]]'', ''The Simpsons'' and ''[[Mr. Show]]'', but was not hired by any of them. He continued to work on other comedic series: Fox's sketch comedy show ''[[The Edge (TV series)|The Edge]]'', ''[[The Trouble with Larry]]'' and ''[[Ned and Stacey]]'', the last of which he also produced. The most notable series he worked on in this period was ''[[The Dana Carvey Show]]'', which featured writers and performers such as [[Louis C.K.]], [[Robert Smigel]], [[Greg Daniels]], [[Stephen Colbert]] and [[Steve Carell]]. On all these series, Kaufman struggled to keep his material from being adulterated or not produced at all, because of his unconventional writing and his quiet nature.

Kaufman also wrote some [[Television pilot|pilot]] scripts of his own, but none of them were produced.<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman Biography"/><ref name="salon.com"/> These included two pilots for [[Disney]], ''Depressed Roomies'' and ''Rambling Pants'' (the former a surrealist take on the "buddy sitcom" and the latter focused on the adventures of a time-travelling poet named Pants) and a pilot for [[HBO]], ''In Limbo'', a naturalistic look at a romantic relationship devoid of the usual tropes of [[romantic films]]. Before ''Being John Malkovich'' was released, he was hired to pitch and write scripts for film projects that were never produced. These unproduced projects included a script for an adaptation of [[Philip K. Dick]]'s novel ''[[A Scanner Darkly]]'', a pitch for a [[star vehicle]] for [[Don Rickles]] and [[Rodney Dangerfield]] in which they would play a washed-up, murderous [[comedy duo]] (an idea Kaufman used in ''[[Antkind]]''), and a pitch for a [[R rating (Motion Picture Association)|R-rated]] version of the TV series ''[[Gilligan's Island]]''.

=== Transition to films (1999–2004) ===
Kaufman first came to mainstream notice as the writer of ''[[Being John Malkovich]]'', directed by [[Spike Jonze]], earning an [[Academy Award]] nomination for his effort and winning a [[BAFTA]]. He wrote the script on spec in 1994, sending it to many companies and studios, all of which turned it down. The script eventually reached [[Francis Ford Coppola]], who passed it on to his then-son-in-law Jonze, who agreed to direct the film.<ref name="NYTimes">{{cite news |title=FILM; The Fun and Games of Living a Virtual Life |work=[[The New York Times]] |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1999/10/24/arts/film-the-fun-and-games-of-living-a-virtual-life.html |first=Peter |last=Kobel |date=October 24, 1999 |access-date=November 4, 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20101128172027/http://www.nytimes.com/1999/10/24/arts/film-the-fun-and-games-of-living-a-virtual-life.html |archive-date=November 28, 2010 |url-status=live}}</ref>

After the success of ''Being John Malkovich'', another of Kaufman's screenplays was produced: ''[[Human Nature (2001 film)|Human Nature]]'', which was directed by [[Michel Gondry]] and produced by Kaufman and Jonze. Kaufman and Jonze reunited yet again as the screenwriter and director of ''[[Adaptation (film)|Adaptation]]'', which earned him another Academy Award nomination and his second BAFTA. ''Adaptation'' featured a fictionalized version of Kaufman and his fictional twin brother, Donald, who is credited as writer of the film along with Kaufman. The idea came to Kaufman while attempting to adapt [[Susan Orlean]]'s book ''[[The Orchid Thief]]'' into film. Struggling with [[writer's block]], Kaufman turned the script into an exaggerated account of his struggles with adapting the screenplay.<ref name="brodesser-variety-1110">{{cite news|author=Claude Brodesser|url=https://variety.com/1999/film/news/scribe-revisiting-reality-1117757917/|title=Scribe revisiting reality|work=Variety |date=November 10, 1999|access-date=December 5, 2017}}</ref>

Kaufman wrote the screenplay for ''[[Confessions of a Dangerous Mind (film)|Confessions of a Dangerous Mind]]'', a biopic based on the "unauthorized autobiography" of [[Chuck Barris]], the creator of such popular game shows as ''[[The Dating Game]]'' and host of ''[[The Gong Show]]''. The film focuses on Barris's claim to have been a [[CIA]] [[hit man]]. It was [[George Clooney]]'s directorial debut. Kaufman criticized Clooney for making drastic alterations to the script without consulting him (instead, Clooney consulted Barris). Kaufman said in an interview with William Arnold: "The usual thing for a writer is to deliver a script and then disappear. That's not for me. I want to be involved from beginning to end. And these directors [Gondry and Jonze] know that, and respect it."<ref>{{cite news |title=Kaufman interviewed by William Arnold. |url=http://www.seattlepi.com/movies/165373_Kaufman19.html |access-date=May 19, 2007 | work=Seattle Post-Intelligencer |date=March 18, 2004}}</ref>

''[[Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind]]'', released in 2004, was Kaufman's second pairing with director [[Michel Gondry]]. Kaufman won his first [[Academy Award]] for [[Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay|Best Original Screenplay]] and third BAFTA for the film together with Gondry and French artist [[Pierre Bismuth]]. The trio also received the prestigious [[PEN American Center]] 2005 prize for screenplay for the film.<ref>{{cite web |title=PEN Center USA: 2005 Literary Awards Winners |url=http://penusa.org/go/awards/winners/2005-literary-awards-winners-published-in-2004/ |access-date=January 12, 2007 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20061125100255/http://penusa.org/go/awards/winners/2005-literary-awards-winners-published-in-2004/ <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date = November 25, 2006}}</ref> [[David Edelstein]] described the film in ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' as "''[[The Awful Truth]]'' turned inside-out by [[Philip K. Dick]], with nods to [[Samuel Beckett]], [[Chris Marker]], [[John Guare]]—the greatest dramatists of our modern fractured consciousness. But the weave is pure Kaufman."<ref>{{cite journal |author=David Edelstein |title=Forget Me Not: The genius of Charlie Kaufman's Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind |journal=Slate |date=March 19, 2004 |url=http://www.slate.com/articles/arts/movies/2004/03/forget_me_not.html |access-date=July 24, 2016}}</ref>

=== Directorial debuts and career struggles (2005–2014) ===

After agreeing to participate in [[Carter Burwell]]'s ''Theater of the New Ear'', a double bill "sound play", Kaufman wrote and directed the audio play ''Hope Leaves the Theater'', while the other play in the production, ''Sawbones'', was written and directed by the [[Coen Brothers]]. ''Hope Leaves the Theater'' follows a middle-aged woman, dissatisfied with her life and relationships, watching a play-within-the-play, and featured performances by [[Hope Davis]], [[Peter Dinklage]] and [[Meryl Streep]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Creative Screenwriting Magazine on Hope Leaves the Theater|url=http://www.creativescreenwriting.com/csdaily/picture/9_23_05_.html|archive-date=April 29, 2007|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070429153833/http://creativescreenwriting.com/csdaily/picture/9_23_05_.html}}</ref> ''Theater of the New Ear'' debuted in April 2005 at [[St. Ann's Warehouse]] in [[Brooklyn, New York]].<ref>{{cite web |title= The Body – Projects – Theater of the New Ear |url=http://www.carterburwell.com/tbi_project_pages/TONE.shtml|access-date=January 12, 2007 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20070107212928/http://www.carterburwell.com/tbi_project_pages/TONE.shtml <!-- Bot retrieved archive --> |archive-date = January 7, 2007}}</ref> Due to scheduling conflicts, later productions of ''Theater of the New Ear'' did not feature the Coens' play, replacing it with ''Anomalisa'', which Kaufman wrote under the pseudonym "'Francis Fregoli". ''Anomalisa'' centers on a man ([[David Thewlis]]) who perceives everyone in the world to be the same person ([[Tom Noonan]]) until he meets an exception ([[Jennifer Jason Leigh]]).<ref>{{cite web |title=Theater of the New Ear |url=http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php/theater-of-the-new-ear |access-date=July 30, 2015}}</ref>

Kaufman made his directorial film debut with the [[postmodern]] film ''[[Synecdoche, New York]]'', starring an ensemble cast led by [[Philip Seymour Hoffman]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Kaufman's Directorial Debut Lands Williams, Hoffman |url=https://www.moviefone.com/2006/08/11/kaufmans-directorial-debut-lands-williams-hoffman/ |access-date=December 5, 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171205194735/https://www.moviefone.com/2006/08/11/kaufmans-directorial-debut-lands-williams-hoffman/ |archive-date=December 5, 2017 |url-status=dead }}</ref> It premiered at the [[Cannes Film Festival]] in 2008. The idea for the film came when Kaufman and Spike Jonze were approached to direct a [[horror film]]. Rather than make a conventional horror film, the two agreed to have the film deal with things they found frightening, such as mortality and life's brevity.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://blog.worldmaker.net/2009/may/18/synecdoche-new-york-great-film-about-upcoming-zomb/ |title=Synecdoche, New York: A Great Film About the Upcoming Zombie Apocalypse? &#124; Blog &#124; WorldMaker.net |publisher=Blog.worldmaker.net |date=May 18, 2009 |access-date=November 27, 2012}}</ref> Kaufman decided to direct the film after Jonze left the project to direct ''[[Where the Wild Things Are (film)|Where the Wild Things Are]]'' instead. The film premiered at the [[2008 Cannes Film Festival]] where it split critics, with some calling it the best film of the year and others finding it pretentious.<ref>[https://www.metacritic.com/film/awards/2008/toptens.shtml] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090102205252/http://www.metacritic.com/film/awards/2008/toptens.shtml|date=January 2, 2009}}</ref>{{Failed verification|date=May 2025}}<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vulture.com/2009/01/vultures_critics_poll_the_comp.html |title=Vulture's Critics' Poll: The Complete Ballots |last=Kois |first=Dan |publisher=Vulture.com |date=January 5, 2009 |access-date=November 17, 2015}}</ref> In the years since its release, it has appeared on multiple lists ranking the best films of the 21st century.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/sep/13/100-best-films-movies-of-the-21st-century |title=The 100 best films of the 21st Century |work=The Guardian |date=September 13, 2019 |access-date=September 13, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films |title=The 21st Century's 100 greatest films |publisher=BBC |date=August 23, 2016 |access-date=September 8, 2016}}</ref> The film's poor box office resulted in Kaufman finding it difficult to gain funding for scripts to which he has attached himself as director.<ref name="auto">{{cite news|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/charlie-kaufman-explains-why-star-studded-frank-or-francis-fell-apart-says-it-could-still-happen-20151112|title=Charlie Kaufman Explains Why Star-Studded 'Frank Or Francis' Fell Apart, Says It Could Still Happen|work=Indiewire|first=Kevin|last=Jagernauth|date=November 12, 2015|access-date=December 9, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151230100855/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/charlie-kaufman-explains-why-star-studded-frank-or-francis-fell-apart-says-it-could-still-happen-20151112|archive-date=December 30, 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Kaufman was slated to write and direct a film with the working title ''Frank or Francis''. Few details have been confirmed about the plot, except that it is a [[musical comedy]] about [[Internet troll|internet anger culture]] and was set to star [[Jack Black]], Nicolas Cage, Steve Carell, [[Kevin Kline]], Catherine Keener, [[Paul Reubens]], [[Jacki Weaver]] and [[Elizabeth Banks]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1290:is-this-the-plot-of-qfrank-or-francisq&catid=73:frank-or-francis&Itemid=100004 |title=Is this the plot of "Frank or Francis"? |publisher=BeingCharlieKaufman.com |date=July 30, 2011 |access-date=August 28, 2012}}</ref> In July 2012, Black said that funding for the project had fallen through, as the studio was unsure about its chances for success after the financial failure of ''Synecdoche, New York''. Although the future of the project is not certain, Kaufman says "It could still happen. It would have to be reinvented, though. We had a whole cast and we were headed into pre-production. So, I'd have to get people back and who knows if they would be interested anymore. But at this point, we don't have any money, so that's a secondary concern."<ref name="auto"/>

Trying to make a return to television when the funding for ''Frank or Francis'' fell through, Kaufman sold a series to HBO in May 2012 with Catherine Keener attached to star, but the series did not get past the scripting stage.<ref name="IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN"/> Kaufman also directed and wrote a pilot for [[FX (TV Channel)|FX]] titled ''How and Why'' in 2014. The plot was described as being about a "man (played by [[John Hawkes (actor)|John Hawkes]]) who can explain how and why a nuclear reactor works but is clueless about life". Along with Hawkes, the pilot co-starred [[Michael Cera]], [[Sally Hawkins]], Catherine Keener and [[Tom Noonan]]. FX decided to not pick up the pilot.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2014/07/fxs-charlie-kaufman-pilot-not-going-forward-807417/|title=FX's Charlie Kaufman Pilot Not Going Forward|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|date=July 21, 2014}}</ref>

While struggling to get his directorial work made, Kaufman continued to work as a writer for hire, writing numerous unproduced screenplays. These included a satire set on a planet inhabited by everyone who ever lived, to be directed by Spike Jonze; an adaptation of [[George Saunders]]'s book ''[[CivilWarLand in Bad Decline]]'', to be directed by [[Ben Stiller]]; and an adaptation of [[Arthur Herzog]]'s novel ''IQ 83'', starring Steve Carell.<ref name="Dazed and Confused">{{cite web|url=https://www.dazeddigital.com/film-tv/article/50312/1/charlie-kaufman-im-thinking-of-ending-things-jessie-buckley-netflix-interview|title=Charlie Kaufman on his new film, a metaphysical time-hopping thriller|date=September 4, 2020 |publisher=Dazed and Confused}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2014/10/steve-carell-charlie-kaufman-i-q-83-viral-outbreaks-dumbs-down-world-857713/|title=Steve Carell, Charlie Kaufman Lock 'I.Q. 83'; Viral Outbreak Dumbs Down World|date=October 22, 2014|publisher=[[Deadline Hollywood]]}}</ref> He later attempted to develop his ''IQ 83'' screenplay into a limited series for [[HBO]], to no avail.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2020/film/features/charlie-kaufman-im-thinking-of-ending-things-1234748358/|title='I'm Thinking of Ending Things' Director Charlie Kaufman Isn't Trying to Mess With Your Mind|date=August 27, 2020|website=Variety|access-date= October 8, 2020}}</ref> He also did uncredited rewrites on films such as ''[[Kung Fu Panda 2]]'' and ''[[Ad Astra (film)|Ad Astra]]''.<ref name="Dazed and Confused"/> In April 2012, Kaufman was hired to adapt [[Patrick Ness]]'s ''[[Chaos Walking]]'' book series, of which he wrote the first draft before leaving the project. After multiple rewrites by other writers, the [[Chaos Walking (film)|film]] was released in 2021 with Kaufman uncredited.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.backstage.com/news/casting/tom-holland-and-daisy-ridley-starring-chaos-walking-will-shoot-canada/|title=Daisy Ridley-Starring 'Chaos Walking' will Shoot in Canada|date=May 30, 2017 |publisher=Backstage}}</ref>

=== Continued film work and debut novel (2015–present) ===
[[File:Charlie Kaufman & Duke Johnson 2015.jpg|thumb|left|Kaufman (left) and [[Duke Johnson (director)|Duke Johnson]] accepting the [[Grand Jury Prize (Venice Film Festival)|Grand Jury Prize]] for ''Anomalisa'' at the [[Venice Film Festival]] in 2015.]]
[[Dino Stamatopoulos]], a former colleague of Kaufman's from ''The Dana Carvey Show'', became interested in adapting Kaufman's ''[[Anomalisa]]'' play script into a [[stop motion]] [[animated]] film. With Kaufman's permission, Stamatopoulos turned to the [[crowd-funding]] website [[Kickstarter]] to fund the film. The Kickstarter page was set up in August 2012 and by the time funding had ended $406,237 was pledged.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/anomalisa/charlie-kaufmans-anomalisa|title=Charlie Kaufman's Anomalisa|website=Kickstarter|date=April 22, 2016 }}</ref> The rest of the $8 million budget was funded by the film's production company, Starburns Industries.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/anomalisa/charlie-kaufmans-anomalisa/posts/852303|title=Kickstart Update #39: Anomalisa Timeline|publisher=[[Kickstarter]]|author=Starburns Industries|access-date=June 11, 2018|date=May 22, 2014}}</ref> Kaufman co-directed the film with [[Duke Johnson (director)|Duke Johnson]], who had previous experience in stop motion filmmaking, and the original cast of the play production returned to reprise their roles. It had its world premiere at the [[Telluride Film Festival]] on September 4, 2015,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/article/telluride-film-festival-charlie-kaufmans-marvelously-strange-anomalisa-is-an-animated-identity-crisis-20150905|title=Telluride Review: Charlie Kaufman's Marvelously Strange 'Anomalisa' is An Animated Identity Crisis|publisher=Indiewire.com|first=Eric|last=Kohn|date=September 5, 2015|access-date=December 9, 2015}}</ref> receiving universal acclaim from critics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/anomalisa/|title=Anomalisa (2015)|work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|access-date=December 9, 2015}}</ref> The film went on to win the [[Grand Jury Prize (Venice Film Festival)|Grand Jury Prize]] at the [[Venice Film Festival]] and was nominated for an [[Academy Award]] for [[Academy Award for Best Animated Feature|Best Animated Feature]], but like ''Synecdoche'', it did poorly at the box office.<ref name="BOM">{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=anomalisa.htm |title=Anomalisa|work = [[Box Office Mojo]] |publisher=[[IMDb]] |access-date=February 21, 2016}}</ref>
[[File:Screen Shot of Charlie Kaufman.png|thumb|Kaufman at the 2020 [[Dublin International Film Festival]]]]
Kaufman's [[debut novel]], ''[[Antkind]]'', was released in July 2020. Kaufman said in 2016 that the novel was being written so as to be unfilmable, and is itself about "an [[Unfilmability|impossible movie]]".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php/recent-antkind-news/1676-charlie-s-novel-has-a-title-and-maybe-a-release-date-and-maybe-a-blurby-thing|title=Charlie's novel has a title! And maybe a release date? And a blurby thing.|date=August 24, 2024|website=Being Charlie Kaufman|access-date=April 4, 2019}}</ref> In ''Antkind,'' film critic B. Rosenberg attempts to recall a three-month long stop-motion animation film that perished in a fire. Believing the film to be his last chance at achieving the respect and admiration of his peers, B. embarks on an absurdist journey that explores [[comedy]], [[film theory]], [[philosophy]], and [[social commentary]] across ''Antkind''<nowiki/>'s 706 pages.<ref>{{Cite web|date=August 2, 2020|title=Antkind by Charlie Kaufman review – absurdism ad infinitum|url=http://www.theguardian.com/books/2020/aug/02/antkind-by-charlie-kaufman-review-absurdism-ad-infinitum|access-date=October 27, 2020|website=the Guardian|language=en}}</ref>

In January 2018, it was announced that Kaufman was working on writing and directing [[I'm Thinking of Ending Things|an adaptation]] of [[Iain Reid]]'s 2016 novel ''[[I'm Thinking of Ending Things (novel)|I'm Thinking of Ending Things]]''.<ref>{{Cite news| url= https://nationalpost.com/pmn/entertainment-pmn/books-entertainment-pmn/charlie-kaufman-adapting-novel-by-canadian-author-iain-reid-for-netflix| title= Charlie Kaufman Adapting Novel by Canadian Author Iain Reid for Netflix| date= January 25, 2018| website= The National Post| access-date= July 31, 2018}}</ref> In December 2018, it was announced that [[Brie Larson]] and [[Jesse Plemons]] were signed to co-star as the leads; the film was described as "the story of a woman's trip to a family farm that leads to an unexpected detour leaving her stranded, [and] a twisted mix of palpable tension, psychological frailty and sheer terror ensues."<ref>{{Cite web| url= https://collider.com/brie-larson-charlie-kaufman-im-thinking-of-ending-things/#images| title= Brie Larson to Star in Charlie Kaufman's Netflix Movie 'I'm Thinking of Ending Things'| date= December 4, 2018| website= [[Collider (website)|Collider]]| access-date= December 5, 2018}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web| url= https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/aquaman-2-is-in-the-works-and-more-movie-news/| title= AQUAMAN 2 IS IN THE WORKS, AND MORE MOVIE NEWS| date= December 7, 2018| website= [[Rotten Tomatoes]]| access-date= December 8, 2018}}</ref> The project began filming in March 2019, with [[Jessie Buckley]] having replaced Larson, and [[Toni Collette]] and [[David Thewlis]] joining the cast, and was released on Netflix in September 2020 to positive reviews.<ref>{{cite web| url= https://www.poughkeepsiejournal.com/story/entertainment/2019/03/21/brie-larson-im-thinking-of-ending-things-fishkill/3230051002/| title= Charlie Kaufman movie for Netflix filming in Fishkill, Hudson Valley| website= [[Poughkeepsie Journal]]| first= Jack| last= Howland| date= March 21, 2019| access-date= March 22, 2019}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web| url= https://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php/89-news/i-m-thinking-of-ending-things/1679-larson-out-toni-collette-jessie-buckley-and-david-thewlis-in-for-ending-things| title= Larson out, Toni Collette, Jessie Buckley and David Thewlis in for "Ending Things"| date= March 28, 2019| website= Being Charlie Kaufman| access-date= April 4, 2019| archive-date= April 4, 2019| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20190404182347/https://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php/89-news/i-m-thinking-of-ending-things/1679-larson-out-toni-collette-jessie-buckley-and-david-thewlis-in-for-ending-things| url-status= dead}}</ref>

In 2023, Kaufman directed his first short film, ''Jackals & Fireflies''. Based on a poem by Eva H.D., who also portrays the lead character, the film was shot entirely on a [[Samsung Galaxy S22]] [[smartphone]] by cinematographer Chayse Irvin.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Erbland |first=Kate |date=February 10, 2023 |title=Why Charlie Kaufman Made a Short About Human Connection on a Cell Phone |work=[[IndieWire]] |url=https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/charlie-kaufman-short-film-jackals-and-fireflies-1234807887/ |access-date=May 26, 2023}}</ref> Kaufman has written a script about dreams for [[Ryan Gosling]]'s production company and an adaptation of the novel ''[[The Memory Police]]'' that [[Reed Morano]] is slated to direct.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2020/film/features/charlie-kaufman-im-thinking-of-ending-things-1234748358/|title=''I'm Thinking of Ending Things'' Director Charlie Kaufman Isn't Trying to Mess With Your Mind|date=August 27, 2020|website=Variety|access-date= October 8, 2020}}</ref> In 2025, the script centered on dreams was revealed to be titled ''Later the War'', based on Iddo Gefen's short story "Debby’s Dream House". [[Channing Tatum]] (replacing [[Eddie Redmayne]]), [[Tessa Thompson]], and [[Patsy Ferran]] are slated to star, with Kaufman directing;<ref>{{cite web |last=Bergeson |first=Samantha |date=24 February 2025 |title=Charlie Kaufman to Write and Direct 'Later the War' Starring Eddie Redmayne and Tessa Thompson |work=[[IndieWire]] |url=https://www.indiewire.com/news/breaking-news/charlie-kaufman-direct-later-the-war-eddie-redmayne-tessa-thompson-1235098666/ |access-date=12 May 2025}}</ref> filming is set for 2027 in [[Cyprus]].<ref>{{cite web |url= https://deadline.com/2026/05/veterans-charlie-kaufman-reboot-later-war-tatum-thompson-1236881287/|title= Channing Tatum Joins Tessa Thompson & Patsy Ferran In Charlie Kaufman's 'Later The War' As Project Sets 2027 Cyprus Shoot & The Veterans Boards Sales – Cannes Market|date= May 5, 2026|access-date= May 6, 2026|first= Melanie|last= Goodfellow|work= Deadline Hollywood}}</ref>

==Themes and influences==
Kaufman's works explore such universal themes as [[identity crisis]], [[Death|mortality]], and the [[meaning of life]] through a [[Metaphysics|metaphysical]] or [[parapsychological]] framework. While his work resists labels, it is sometimes described as [[surrealist]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.indiewire.com/people/int_Kaufman_Charlie_991027.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20050924100331/http://www.indiewire.com/people/int_Kaufman_Charlie_991027.html|url-status=dead|title=Indie Wire interview.|archive-date=September 24, 2005}}</ref> He uses [[metafiction]] as a literary device, and sometimes includes fictionalized "facts" about his life in his work, notably ''Adaptation.'', ''Hope Leaves the Theater'', and ''Antkind''. [[Gender identity]] is also a recurrent theme in his work.<ref>{{Cite web|url= https://crookedmarquee.com/how-to-be-someone-else-transgender-themes-in-the-work-of-charlie-kaufman/|title=How to Be Someone Else: Transgender Themes in the Work of Charlie Kaufman|date=September 7, 2020|website=Crooked Marquee|language=en-US|access-date=October 3, 2020}}</ref> Non-human primates also recur in Kaufman's early work: in ''Being John Malkovich'', Lotte has a pet chimpanzee named Elijah; in ''Human Nature'', Puff was raised as a chimpanzee; in ''Confessions of a Dangerous Mind'', Penny dreams about a monkey; and in ''Adaptation'', the original [[deus ex machina]] was a Bigfoot-like "Swamp Ape".<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=22|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090328094907/http://www.beingcharliekaufman.com/index.php?option=com_docman&task=doc_download&gid=22|url-status=dead|title=Adaptation (Draft 2)|archive-date=March 28, 2009}}</ref>

Writers and directors Kaufman has named as his favorites, or as influences, include [[Franz Kafka]],<ref name="IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN">{{cite web|title=IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN|url=https://www.vulture.com/2015/12/charlie-kaufman-anomaslisa-c-v-r.html|website=Vulture.com|date=December 16, 2015|access-date=March 8, 2017}}</ref> [[Samuel Beckett]],<ref>{{cite web|title=Charlie Kaufman interview: Life's little dramas|url=http://www.scotsman.com/lifestyle/culture/charlie-kaufman-interview-life-s-little-dramas-1-1037351|website=Scotsman.com|access-date=March 8, 2017}}</ref> [[Eugene Ionesco]], [[Stanisław Lem]],<ref>{{cite web|title=Talking with the Kaufman About Pandas|url=http://www.thelmagazine.com/2008/10/talking-with-the-kaufman-about-pandas/|website=The L Magazine|date=October 22, 2008|access-date=March 8, 2017|archive-date=March 9, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170309073155/http://www.thelmagazine.com/2008/10/talking-with-the-kaufman-about-pandas/|url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Flannery O'Connor]],<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman">{{cite web|title=Being Charlie Kaufman|url=http://www.salon.com/1999/11/11/kaufman/|website=Salon|date=November 11, 1999|access-date=March 8, 2017}}</ref> [[Shirley Jackson]],<ref name="Being Charlie Kaufman"/> [[Philip K. Dick]],<ref name="auto1">{{Cite web|date=November 11, 1999|title=Being Charlie Kaufman|url=https://www.salon.com/1999/11/11/kaufman/|access-date=June 24, 2020|website=Salon|language=en}}</ref> [[Patricia Highsmith]],<ref name="auto1"/> [[Stephen Dixon (author)|Stephen Dixon]],<ref name="auto1" /> [[David Lynch]],<ref name="IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN"/> [[Lars von Trier]],<ref name="IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN"/> [[Roy Andersson]],<ref name="IN CONVERSATION: CHARLIE KAUFMAN"/> [[Woody Allen]],<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wh8MIOvolCU|title=The Filmmakers @KVIFF 2016: Charlie Kaufman|website=[[YouTube]]|date=July 9, 2016 }}</ref> and the [[Coen brothers]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Sternbergh|first=Adam|date=December 16, 2015|title=In Conversation: Charlie Kaufman|url=https://www.vulture.com/2015/12/charlie-kaufman-anomaslisa-c-v-r.html|access-date=June 24, 2020|website=Vulture|language=en-us}}</ref> He has also mentioned [[Tom Noonan]]'s ''[[What Happened Was...]]'' as a favorite of his.<ref>{{cite web |date=April 1, 2016 |title="Anomalisa" Star Reveals E-Mail Trail Sparked Close Bond To Hollywood Great Charlie Kaufman |url=https://www.sohh.com/anomalisa-star-reveals-e-mail-trail-sparked-close-bond-hollywood-great-charlie-kaufman/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230505120221/https://www.sohh.com/anomalisa-star-reveals-e-mail-trail-sparked-close-bond-hollywood-great-charlie-kaufman/ |archive-date=May 5, 2023 |access-date=2023-05-05}}</ref>

==Personal life==
{{As of|2020}}, Kaufman lives in [[Manhattan]],<ref name="New York Times">{{Cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2020/07/02/magazine/charlie-kaufman.html|title=This Profile of Charlie Kaufman Has Changed|first=Jon|last=Mooallem|newspaper=The New York Times|date=July 2, 2020}}</ref> having lived in [[Pasadena, California]], since 1998.<ref>{{Cite web|last=David|first=Mark|date=2019-04-22|title=Charlie Kaufman Lists Arts and Crafts Bungalow in Pasadena|url=https://variety.com/2019/dirt/real-estalker/charlie-kaufman-pasadena-house-1203194586/|access-date=2021-06-30|website=Variety|language=en-US}}</ref><ref name="salon.com">{{cite web |title=Salon.com Interview by Michael Sragow. |date=November 11, 1999 |url=http://www.salon.com/1999/11/11/kaufman/ |access-date=May 15, 2007}}</ref> He is married to Denise Monaghan,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Pener |first1=Degen |last2=Godley |first2=Chris |last3=Logan |first3=Bryan |date=2011-10-07 |title=Hollywood's Biggest Real Estate Deals of the Week: Matthew Perry, Ellen DeGeneres, Sacha Baron Cohen |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/gallery/hollywoods-biggest-real-estate-deals-245674/ |access-date=2024-04-16 |at=Thumbnail 13 |website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |language=en-US}}</ref> with whom he has a daughter, Anna.<ref name="New York Times" /><ref>{{Cite web|title=An interview with the artist Denise Monaghan, Pasadena, California|url=https://www.giovannabrunini.com/quixotic/2018/08/an-interview-with-the-artist-denise-monaghan-pasadena-california.html|access-date=2021-06-30|website=Quixotic}}</ref>
