'''''Hard Boiled''''' ({{zh|s=辣手神探|t=辣手神探|first=t, j|l=Hard Boiled Detective}})<ref name="Elder">{{harvnb|Elder|2005|p=xxviii}}</ref> is a 1992 [[Hong Kong action cinema|Hong Kong action film]] directed by [[John Woo]] from a screenplay by [[Gordon Chan]] and [[Barry Wong]], based on a story by Woo. The film stars [[Chow Yun-fat]] as Inspector "Tequila" Yuen, alongside [[Tony Leung Chiu-wai]] and [[Teresa Mo]]. It follows a hard-boiled [[police inspector]] whose pursuit of a violent [[Triad (organised crime)|Triad]] syndicate draws him into a dangerous operation involving an [[Undercover police|undercover officer]] working deep within the criminal organization.

The film was Woo's final [[Hong Kong film]] before his transition to [[Cinema of the United States|Hollywood]]. Conceived in response to criticism that his earlier works romanticized criminals, Woo aimed to create a police-centered action film inspired by the [[Dirty Harry (film series)|''Dirty Harry'']] series. Production was marked by extensive improvisation and ongoing script revisions, particularly following the death of co-writer [[Barry Wong]]. Several characters and narrative elements were introduced or reworked during filming, while earlier story concepts—including a subplot involving a child-targeting criminal—were abandoned.

''Hard Boiled'' was released theatrically in [[Hong Kong]] in 1992 to strong audience response. Although it did not match the domestic box-office success of Woo's ''[[A Better Tomorrow]]'' (1986), it outperformed ''[[The Killer (1989 film)|The Killer]]'' (1989) in the local market. Internationally, the film received widespread critical acclaim, with particular praise for its elaborate gunfight choreography, [[long take]]s, and kinetic visual style.

Over time, ''Hard Boiled'' has come to be regarded as a landmark of Hong Kong action cinema and one of the defining works of the [[heroic bloodshed]] genre. Its action sequences—especially the extended hospital shootout—are frequently cited by critics and filmmakers as among the most accomplished in the history of the genre. The film has since achieved [[cult film|cult status]] and has been recognized as a major influence on action cinema worldwide. In 2007, a sequel in the form of a [[video game]], ''[[Stranglehold (video game)|Stranglehold]]'', was released, with Chow Yun-fat reprising his role and Woo serving as creative director.

== Plot ==
In a Hong Kong [[teahouse]], [[Royal Hong Kong Police]] inspectors "Tequila" Yuen and Benny Mak surveil a group of [[Arms trafficking|gun smugglers]] while they are making a deal. When a rival gang ambushes the deal, a fierce [[shootout]] breaks out; the gangsters are defeated, but several police officers and civilians are wounded and Benny is killed. As revenge, Tequila executes the gangster who killed Benny rather than arrest him. He is reprimanded by Chief Superintendent Pang, who needed the executed gangster as a key witness. After a police funeral, Pang burns the personnel file of another smuggler Tequila killed, revealing him to be an [[Undercover operation|undercover cop]].

Meanwhile, Alan, an assassin working for Triad boss "Uncle" Hoi, murders one of Hoi's subordinates who had double-crossed them for a rival syndicate led by upstart Johnny Wong. Wong, who is looking to usurp the old Triad bosses through his control of the illicit arms trade, is impressed by Alan's skill and attempts to recruit him. Alan reluctantly accepts the offer, and Wong brings Alan to a raid on Hoi's warehouse as an [[initiation]], where many of Hoi's men are killed. Surrounded, Hoi lets Alan kill him to spare his surrendering men, but Alan kills them all anyway to please Wong. Tequila, who has been watching from cover, ambushes and defeats Wong's men, but is caught by Alan, who spares him. Tequila confronts Pang, demanding to know if Alan is an undercover cop. Pang refuses to say, but reveals the teahouse [[friendly fire]] killing to Tequila and warns him to stay away from the case.

Tequila tracks Alan to his sailboat and deduces he is undercover, but they are ambushed by the remnants of Hoi's gang. The pair fight off the attackers and Tequila flees just before Wong arrives, allowing Alan to keep his cover. Wong realizes that one of his lieutenants, Foxy, is a police [[informant]]. Wong's henchman, Mad Dog, beats Foxy before Alan is ordered to execute him with a shot to the chest, but before that he manages to place [[Lighter|cigar lighter]] in Foxy's chest pocket that saves his life. Foxy finds Tequila at a jazz bar and informs him that Wong's armory is hidden in a vault beneath the Maple  hospital. As Tequila takes Foxy to the hospital, Wong discovers that Foxy is alive and sends Alan to kill him, while also discreetly sending Mad Dog to monitor Alan. At the hospital, Alan confronts Tequila, demanding to know the whereabouts of the vault; while the two are distracted, Mad Dog kills Foxy.

Alan and Tequila discover Wong's vault, where they briefly skirmish with Mad Dog. As Pang, officer Teresa Chang, and other inspectors evacuate the hospital, Wong and his men attempt to gain leverage by taking the staff and patients hostage while indiscriminately shooting fleeing patients and responding police officers, irritating Mad Dog with his callousness. Alan and Tequila team up to rescue the hostages and battle Wong's men; meanwhile, Pang evacuates the lobby and takes command at the police perimeter, while Chang and the [[Special Duties Unit]] rescue trapped babies from the [[Maternity ward|maternity wing]]. As they fight their way through the hospital, Alan accidentally shoots a [[Plainclothes law enforcement|plainclothes officer]] and is overcome with guilt; Tequila consoles him by sharing his similar experience from the teahouse and encourages him to fight on.

The pair eventually confront Mad Dog again. While Tequila leaves to assist Chang and rescue one last baby, Alan and Mad Dog engage in a tense duel before ending in a standoff with a group of crippled patients between them. They allow the patients safe passage to leave, but Wong arrives and guns down the patients while trying to kill Alan, who escapes. Enraged, Mad Dog tries to kill Wong, but is gunned down by Wong when his pistol runs out of ammunition. Alan and Tequila kill the remaining gangsters and confront Wong, but he detonates bombs in the armory, setting the hospital ablaze and forcing Tequila to flee with the baby while Alan goes after Wong. As the hospital explodes, Wong drags Alan outside at gunpoint and forces Tequila to humiliate himself. Using this as a distraction, Alan wrestles for Wong's pistol and ends up shooting himself in the gut, surprising Wong enough for Tequila to fatally shoot Wong in the head, before collapsing.

Alan is revealed to have survived the ordeal. To protect Alan from the Triads, Pang and Tequila destroy Alan's personnel file and declare him dead, allowing him to leave Hong Kong to start a new life.

== Production ==

=== Development ===
<!-- development of the concept and script, as well as the securing of financing and producers -->
The film was originally developed in 1990.<ref name="commFox">{{cite AV media notes |title=Hard Boiled | orig-year = 1992 |others=John Woo |type=Commentary with John Woo and Terence Chang |publisher=Fox Lorber Films |year=2000}}</ref> After creating films which focused on the lives of gangsters, director [[John Woo]] wanted to make a film that glorified the police instead.<ref name="commDD">{{cite AV media notes |title=Hard Boiled | orig-year = 1992 |others=John Woo |type=Commentary with Bey Logan |publisher=Dragon Dynasty |year=2007}}</ref> Woo admired [[Clint Eastwood]]'s and [[Steve McQueen]]'s characters from their films ''[[Dirty Harry]]'' and ''[[Bullitt]]'' respectively, and wanted to make his own Hong Kong-style ''Dirty Harry'' police detective film.<ref name="WooDD">{{cite AV media |people=[[John Woo|Woo, John]] |date=2007 |title=A Baptism Of Fire: An Interview With Director John Woo |medium=DVD |publisher=Dragon Dynasty |location=Santa Monica, California, United States}}</ref> While creating this character, Woo was inspired by a police officer who was a strong-willed and tough member of the police force, as well as being an avid drummer. This led to Woo having Tequila's character be a musician as well as a cop.<ref name="WooDD" />

Before production started, Woo told his actors that he was not going to make the film as stylish as his previous films, but to have it be more of an "edgy thriller".<ref name="commDD" /> The role of Teresa Chang was originally made for actress [[Michelle Yeoh]], who had a long relationship with producer [[Terence Chang]].<ref name="commDD" /> After casting [[Teresa Mo]], the character of Teresa Chang was greatly re-written.<ref name="commDD" />
The film's initial story was about [[Tony Leung Chiu-Wai|Tony Leung]]'s character being a psychopath who would poison baby food.<ref name="WooDD" /> When Terence Chang was making connections to have Woo make films in the United States, he found people uninterested and disgusted with the theme of babies being poisoned. This halted production for a month to develop a new story.<ref name="commFox" /><ref name="ChangDD" /> Screenwriter [[Barry Wong]] was brought in to write a new story about Tony Leung's character being an undercover police officer. After writing the first part of the script, Wong went on a vacation in Germany, where he died, leaving the script unfinished.<ref name="ChangDD">{{cite AV media |people=[[Terence Chang|Chang, Terence]] |date=2007 |title=Partner in Crime: An Interview With Producer Terence Chang |medium=DVD |publisher=Dragon Dynasty |location=Santa Monica, California, United States}}</ref>

=== Filming ===
''Hard Boiled'' took 123 days to shoot in 1991.<ref name="Heard98">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=98}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Keely |first=Pete |title='Hard Target' at 25: John Woo on Fighting for Respect in Hollywood |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/hard-target-john-woo-clashing-jean-claude-van-damme-1137208/ |access-date=7 March 2024 |work=hollywoodreporter.com |date=24 August 2018 |language=en-US |quote=At the end of 1991 I was making my last Hong Kong film, Hard Boiled. |archive-date=1 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231201205536/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/hard-target-john-woo-clashing-jean-claude-van-damme-1137208/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last=Ebiri |first=Bilge |title=A LONG TALK JULY 3, 2023 'My Films Had So Much Anger' John Woo reflects on a career driven by action, ambition, and artistry. |url=https://www.vulture.com/2023/07/john-woo-on-face-off-mission-impossible-2-and-more.html |access-date=7 March 2024 |work=vulture.com |date=3 July 2023 |location=New York City |language=en-US |quote=In 1991, when I had just started shooting Hard Boiled, all of a sudden, I got a phone call from Oliver Stone. |archive-date=3 October 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231003225442/https://www.vulture.com/2023/07/john-woo-on-face-off-mission-impossible-2-and-more.html |url-status=live}}</ref> Although Woo told his cast that the film would be more gritty and not as stylish as his previous films, ''Hard Boiled'' became more stylish as the filming began.<ref name="commDD" /> The [[tea house]] sequence in the film was shot before the script was written.<ref name="WooDD" /> The crew found that the teahouse was going to be torn down and decided to film a scene there. Woo saw the staircase in the teahouse, and thought about a scene where a character would come shooting down gun smugglers while sliding down the banister.<ref name="WooDD" /> The teahouse sequence was shot in around a week's time and was choreographed by Woo and [[Philip Kwok]].<ref name="commFox" /><ref name="ChangDD" /><ref name="KwokDD" /> It was shot with interruptions from local triads in the area asking for protection money, and residents complaining about the noise.<ref name="commFox" /><ref name="ChangDD" />

[[File:John Woo Cannes 2005.jpg|upright|thumb|alt=A photo of a Chinese man wearing a black suit against an orange backdrop.|Director John Woo acted in ''Hard Boiled'' as a bartender who would give Chow Yun-fat's character advice. Woo's character was developed after filming had already started.]]
The script of the film went through several changes during filming. Due to the length of the film, scenes from a side-story involving the relationship between the characters Tequila and Teresa Chang were cut.<ref name="ChangDD" /> Another cut scene included Tequila playing clarinet over Benny's grave.<ref name="commDD" /> With these cuts, Chow Yun-fat felt his character was not very deep in comparison to Leung's character of Alan. To develop his character more, Chow asked John Woo to insert a mentor character in the film, which Woo himself would play; Chow felt that having Woo in this role would make Woo not cut out these scenes.<ref name="ChangDD" /> Philip Kwok's role of Mad Dog was not in the script and was created on the set. Kwok first worked with Woo on his film ''[[Once a Thief (1991 film)|Once a Thief]]'' and was asked to return to work on ''Hard Boiled''.<ref name="KwokDD">{{cite AV media |people=[[Philip Kwok|Kwok, Philip]] |date=2007 |title=Mad Dog Bites Again: An Interview With actor Philip Kwok |medium=DVD |publisher=Dragon Dynasty |location=Santa Monica, California, United States}}</ref> After reading the script, Woo felt that the character of Johnny Wong was not a strong enough physical threat. After seeing Kwok do several of the stunts while filming, Woo created the character of Mad Dog for him.<ref name="WooDD" /><ref name="commDD" />

The scenes in the hospital maternity ward and the warehouse were shot at a studio called "The Coca-Cola Factory", named for its former use as a [[Coca-Cola]] [[bottling plant]].<ref name="ChangDD" /><ref name="Heard101">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=101}}</ref> The hospital scenes took 40 days to shoot.<ref name="Fang44">{{harvnb|Fang|2004|p=44}}</ref> The hospital segment's location was chosen since they wanted to have an atypical location where gangs would hide their weapons.<ref name="ChangDD" /> While filming in the hospital, the windows were covered with blast shields to give the appearance of night time, which allowed the crew to film at any time during the day. The cast and crew stayed in the hospital for days, often losing track of time.<ref name="WooDD" /> After long hours of filming in the hospital, the crew became exhausted. This prompted the direction of one of the climax's action scenes, a lengthy shootout through the hospital's halls, to be a five-minute [[long take]], so as to shorten the time needed to film. To complete this, during a brief 20-second scene in the middle of the take inside an elevator, the crew quickly changed the set props and rigged the explosions and practical effects in time for the next scene to continue.<ref name="WooDD" /> While filming the hospital sequence, Tony Leung was injured when glass fragments went into his eyes, and he was hospitalized before returning after a week-long rest.<ref name="WooDD" /> Woo changed the ending of ''Hard Boiled'' after many members of the crew of the film felt that Leung's character should survive at the film's end.<ref name="WooDD" />

=== Post-production ===
Woo is a fan of jazz music and wanted a jazz-style soundtrack for ''Hard Boiled''.<ref name="WooDD" /> Woo had also previously wanted a singer to perform a jazz song and have Chow Yun-fat's character play saxophone in his previous film ''[[The Killer (1989 film)|The Killer]]''.<ref name="Heard78">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=78}}</ref> The producer for ''The Killer'', [[Tsui Hark]], rejected this idea for ''The Killer'', feeling that Hong Kong audiences did not enjoy and understand jazz music.<ref name="Heard78" /> The score heard in ''Hard Boiled'' was created by jazz musician [[Michael Gibbs (composer)|Michael Gibbs]]. During promotional screenings, the score for the film was different and was described as "very haunting music", but this score ultimately could not be used as the production crew could not acquire the rights to the music.<ref name="commDD" /> Other songs featured in the film, include "[[Hello (Lionel Richie song)|Hello]]" by [[Lionel Richie]] and the traditional song "[[Mona Lisa (Nat King Cole song)|Mona Lisa]]".<ref name="commDD" /> Woo chose these songs specifically for their lyrics to suggest that Tony was a sort of pen pal to Teresa.<ref>{{harvnb|Hall|1999|p=164}}</ref> All the characters in ''Hard Boiled'' had their voices dubbed by their own actors to save money. Woo stated this was convenient as he did not have to worry about setting up boom mics and other sound elements.<ref name="commFox" />

== Release ==

=== Theatrical run ===
''Hard Boiled'' was released on 16 April 1992 in Hong Kong.<ref name="hkfa" /> The film grossed HK$19,711,048 which was not as strong of a box office reception as Woo's ''[[A Better Tomorrow]]'' but was slightly better than the domestic gross of ''[[The Killer (1989 film)|The Killer]]''.<ref name="WooDD" /><ref name="commDD" /><ref name="hkfa">{{cite web |url=http://ipac.hkfa.lcsd.gov.hk/ipac/cclib/search/showBib.jsp?f=e&id=65537712064005 |title=Hong Kong Film Archive|access-date=4 September 2014|archive-date=4 September 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140904212809/http://ipac.hkfa.lcsd.gov.hk/ipac/cclib/search/showBib.jsp?f=e&id=65537712064005|url-status=dead}}</ref> On the film's initial release in Hong Kong it debuted at number 3 in the box office where it was beaten by [[Tsui Hark]]'s ''[[Once Upon a Time in China 2]]'' and the [[Stephen Chow]] film ''[[Fight Back to School II]]''.<ref name="ss-review">{{cite journal |title=Hard Boiled |last=Rayns |first=Tony|author-link=Tony Rayns |journal=[[Sight & Sound]] |location=London |volume=2 |issue=4 |date=August 1992 |pages=20–23}}</ref>

The North American premiere of ''Hard Boiled'' was in September 1992 at the [[Toronto International Film Festival]].<ref name="Heard105">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=105}}</ref> At the premiere, the audience response was very positive with people stomping their feet and yelling at the screen. This reception surprised producer Terence Chang who did not expect such a positive reaction.<ref name="ChangDD" /> It had a limited US release in June 1993, grossing US$71,858. In France, it was released the same month and sold 85,104 tickets.<ref>{{cite web |title=Lashou Shentan (Hard Boiled) |url=http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=4914 |website=JP's Box-Office |language=fr |access-date=30 April 2022 |archive-date=30 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220430093216/http://www.jpbox-office.com/fichfilm.php?id=4914 |url-status=live}}</ref> ''Hard Boiled'' received a wide release in the United Kingdom on 8 October 1993.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/film--the-dropdead-director-john-woo-makes-movies-with-guts-and-buckets-of-blood-kevin-jackson-talks-to-him-plus-jeremy-clarke-on-chow-yunfat-woos-favourite-leading-hardman-1509125.html |work=[[The Independent]] |title=The drop-dead director: John Woo makes movies with guts, and buckets of blood. For decades after going out of print on home media, the film was unavailable in Western territories due to rights issues with the film catalog of co-producer Golden Princess. Woo said in 2023 that the film, along with ''The Killer'', could not be re-released without the approval of the rights holders.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://collider.com/the-killer-hard-boiled-4k-releases-john-woo/ |work=[[Collider (website)|Collider]] |title=John Woo Explains Why 'The Killer' and 'Hard Boiled' Don't Have 4K Releases [Exclusive] |last=O'Rourke |first=Ryan |date=21 November 2023 |accessdate=9 October 2024|archive-date=5 December 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241205201301/https://collider.com/the-killer-hard-boiled-4k-releases-john-woo/|url-status=live}}</ref> In early 2025, [[Shout! Studios]] acquired the worldwide rights (excluding select Asian countries) to the 156-film Golden Princess film catalog, with intentions to make the films available via both physical media and streaming platforms.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ramachandran |first1=Naman |title=John Woo, Chow Yun-fat Classics Among 156-Film Golden Princess Library Acquired by Shout! Studios (EXCLUSIVE) |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/news/john-woo-chow-yun-fat-golden-princess-shout-studios-1236272850/ |website=Variety |access-date=26 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250113201036/https://variety.com/2025/film/news/john-woo-chow-yun-fat-golden-princess-shout-studios-1236272850/ |archive-date=13 January 2025 |date=13 January 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref> A 4K UHD edition of Hard Boiled was then released on 4 November 2025, quickly selling out.

== Reception ==
Initial reception to ''Hard Boiled'' was positive. Vincent Canby of ''[[The New York Times]]'' found it difficult to follow both the action scenes and the subtitles at the same time, but stated that "Mr. Woo does, in fact, seem to be a very brisk, talented director with a gift for the flashy effect and the bizarre confrontation."<ref>{{cite web |last=Canby |first=Vincent |title=Hard Boiled (1992) Review/Film; Blood, Fire And Death, Slow-w-wly |url=https://movies.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9F0CE2DC163DF93BA25755C0A965958260 |work=[[The New York Times]] |date=18 June 1993|access-date=8 August 2009}}</ref> A review in the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' stated that "With ''Hard Boiled'', John Woo shows himself to be the best director of contemporary action films anywhere."<ref name="Heard104">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=104}}</ref>
''[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]]'' spoke positively about the action scenes, noting the "epic shootouts that bookend Hard-Boiled, John Woo's blood-soaked Hong Kong gangster extravaganza, are wondrously staged, brilliantly photographed tableaux."<ref>{{cite journal |date=20 July 1993 |title=Blood, Bodies, Guns, Mobsters from Hong Kong |journal=[[The Philadelphia Inquirer]] |pages=E02 Public perception of the film has only improved over time; the review-aggregation website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] gives it a score of 92%, with an average rating of 7.8 out of 10, based on 71 reviews. The website's "Critic's Consensus" for the film reads, "Boasting impactful action as well as surprising emotional resonance, ''Hard Boiled'' is a powerful thriller that hits hard in more ways than one."<ref name="rottentomatoes">{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hard_boiled/ |title=Hard Boiled – Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=27 July 2023 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |archive-date=28 June 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230628022547/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/hard_boiled |url-status=live}}</ref> Film scholar Andy Klein wrote that the film is "almost a distillation of [Woo's] post-1986 work. Even if the plot is full of holes, and the emotional tug isn't quite as strong as in ''[[The Killer (1989 film)|The Killer]]'', the action sequences (nearly the whole movie) are among the greatest ever filmed".<ref name="Heard104">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=104}}</ref><ref name="Heard103">{{harvnb|Heard|1999|p=103}}</ref> Mark Salisbury of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire Magazine]]'' gave the film four stars out of five, calling it "Infinitely more exciting than a dozen ''[[Die Hard (film series)|Die Hards]]'', action cinema doesn't come any better than this." ==Accolades==
At the [[12th Hong Kong Film Awards]], David Wu and John Woo won the award for "Best Film Editing".<ref>{{Cite web |title=香港電影金像獎 |url=http://www.hkfaa.com/|access-date=27 December 2021 |website=香港電影金像獎|archive-date=30 August 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160830234550/http://www.hkfaa.com/|url-status=live}}</ref> Tony Leung was nominated for "Best Supporting Male Actor", but he refused the nomination on the grounds that he had a leading role in the film. His protest was supported by John Woo and Chow Yun-fat. This later led the [[Hong Kong Film Award]]s to change its nomination rules to allow for multiple leading roles from the same film.<ref>{{Cite web |title=5次金像奖影帝，竟拒绝男配提名，梁朝伟用演技征服评委_腾讯新闻 |url=https://new.qq.com/omn/20210605/20210605A049LE00.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211227041755/https://new.qq.com/omn/20210605/20210605A049LE00.html |archive-date=27 December 2021 |access-date=27 December 2021 |website=new.qq.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=吴宇森"暴力美学"最后绝唱：《无间道》前传，梁朝伟拒绝金像奖_腾讯新闻 |url=https://new.qq.com/omn/20200317/20200317A07LLL00.html|access-date=27 December 2021 |website=new.qq.com |date=17 March 2020|archive-date=27 December 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211227041756/https://new.qq.com/omn/20200317/20200317A07LLL00.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=你知道吗？梁朝伟这神作成就《无间道》原型，还让金像奖"丢脸"_腾讯新闻 |url=https://new.qq.com/omn/20210412/20210412A0BKAA00.html|access-date=27 December 2021 |website=new.qq.com |date=12 April 2021|archive-date=27 December 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211227041754/https://new.qq.com/omn/20210412/20210412A0BKAA00.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Video game ==
{{Main|Stranglehold (video game)}}

In 2007, [[Midway Games]] released the game [[Stranglehold (video game)|''Stranglehold'']]. The game's story and storyboards were made in collaboration with John Woo.<ref name="strangleholdDD">{{cite AV media |date=2007 |title=Stranglehold Preview |medium=DVD |publisher=Dragon Dynasty |location=Santa Monica, California, United States}}</ref> The game features the character Tequila from ''Hard Boiled'', who is travelling the globe in search of his kidnapped daughter.<ref>{{cite news |last=Leach |first=Gracie |title=John Woo Presents Stranglehold – Overview – allgame |work=[[Allgame]] |url=http://www.allgame.com/game.php?id=47731 | access-date = 8 August 2009 | archive-date = 14 November 2014 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20141114195414/http://www.allgame.com/game.php?id=47731 | url-status = dead}}</ref> In 2009 John Woo's production company Lion Rock Entertainment was reported to be developing a film version of the game, to be written by Jeremy Passmore and Andre Fabrizio.<ref>{{cite news |last=Parfitt |first=Orlando |title=IGN: Woo Making Hard Boiled 2 |url=http://movies.ign.com/articles/960/960356p1.html |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090312091006/http://movies.ign.com/articles/960/960356p1.html |archive-date=12 March 2009 |access-date=8 August 2009 |work=[[IGN]]}}</ref> Following the box-office disappointment of Woo's film ''[[The Crossing (2014 film)|The Crossing]]'', Woo and Chang disbanded Lion Rock Productions.<ref name="sd-mannix">{{cite web |url=http://www.screendaily.com/features/interviews/terence-chang-talks-china-market-challenges-and-new-ventures/5119574.article |work=Screen Daily |publisher=[[Screen International]] |title=Terence Chang talks China market challenges and new ventures |date=30 June 2017 |accessdate=10 July 2017 |last=Shackleton |first=Liz |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20170630174012/http://www.screendaily.com/features/interviews/terence-chang-talks-china-market-challenges-and-new-ventures/5119574.article |archivedate=30 June 2017}}</ref>
