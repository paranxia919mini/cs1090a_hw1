'''''Always''''' is a 1989 American [[romantic fantasy]] film directed and produced by [[Steven Spielberg]]. It is a [[remake]] of the 1943 [[romantic drama film|romantic drama]] ''[[A Guy Named Joe]]'', which was set during [[World War II]]. The main departure from the 1943 film is changing the setting from wartime to a modern [[aerial firefighting]] operation. However, it follows the same basic plot line: the spirit of a recently dead expert pilot mentors a newer pilot, while watching him fall in love with the girlfriend he left behind. 

The names of the four principal characters of the earlier film are all the same, except the Ted Randall character, who is called Ted Baker in the remake, and Pete's last name is Sandich instead of Sandidge.<ref>Evans 2000, p.97.</ref> The film stars [[Richard Dreyfuss]], [[Holly Hunter]], [[John Goodman]], [[Brad Johnson (actor, born 1959)|Brad Johnson]], and [[Audrey Hepburn]] in her final film role before her death in 1993.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/191/a-guy-named-joe|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120109091511/http://www.tcm.com/tcmdb/title/191/A-Guy-Named-Joe/|url-status=dead|archive-date=January 9, 2012|title=A Guy Named Joe|website=Turner Classic Movies}}</ref> ''Always'' was released in the United States by [[Universal Pictures]] on December 22, 1989, and received mixed reviews. The film was a mild success, earning $74 million worldwide on the budget of $31 million.

==Plot==
 <!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, the plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Pete Sandich is an [[aerial firefighting|aerial firefighter]] whose excessive risk-taking in the air deeply troubles his girlfriend, Dorinda Durston, a pilot who doubles as an air traffic controller for the firefighters. It also concerns Pete's best friend, Al Yackey, a fellow firefighter pilot.

After yet another risky and nearly fatal flight that Pete casually shrugs off, Al suggests he accept a safer job training firefighting pilots in Flat Rock, Colorado. He refuses until Dorinda tearfully confronts him, confessing her perpetual fear and anguish that he will be killed. Pete relents and tells her he will take the training job.

Pete flies one last mission, despite Dorinda's gloomy premonition. During the firebombing run, Al's engine catches fire and is about to explode. Pete makes a dangerously steep dive and skillfully douses Al's engine with a fire-retardant [[Phos-Chek|slurry]], saving him. As Pete struggles to regain control from the dive, he flies directly through the forest fire, igniting his engine and causing the aircraft to explode.

Pete strolls through a burned-out forest. Coming to a small clearing, he meets Hap, who explains Pete died in the explosion and now has a new purpose: As spirits did for him during his lifetime, he will provide ''Spiritus'' ("the divine breath") to guide others who will interpret his words as their thoughts.

Although time is non-linear from Pete's perspective, six months have elapsed in the real world and Al wants a grieving Dorinda to move past Pete's death. He takes her with him to Colorado to work at the flight school where Pete is to lead pilots who will be training to fight fires, one of whom is Ted Baker. More months pass and, to Pete's anguish, Ted falls in love with Dorinda as she begins emerging from her year-long mourning. Pete attempts to sabotage the budding romance, but Hap reminds him that his life ended; he was sent to inspire Ted but also to bid Dorinda farewell.

Ted, with Pete's inspiration, plans a dangerous rescue mission for trapped firefighters. Unable to bear another loss, Dorinda takes his aircraft to do the job herself. Pete, unseen by Dorinda, fails to dissuade her. However, with Pete's guidance, she can save the firefighters who are trapped on the ground. On the return flight, Pete tells Dorinda everything he wanted to say in life.

Dorinda makes an emergency [[water landing]] in a lake. As the sinking plane's cockpit floods, she seems reluctant to escape. Pete appears before her and, offering his hand, leads her to the surface. As Dorinda wades ashore, Pete releases her from his heart, allowing Ted to be in her life. Emerging from the lake alone, Dorinda walks back to the airbase where she accepts Ted and embraces him. Pete appears, smiles, and walks in the opposite direction to assume his place in the afterlife.

==Production==
[[Steven Spielberg]] confided that while making ''[[Jaws (film)|Jaws]]'' in 1974, he and [[Richard Dreyfuss]] had traded quips from ''A Guy Named Joe'', considered a "classic" war film, that they both wanted to remake.<ref name="Ebert" /> Originally intended to be an [[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM]] project, the film underwent a protracted 10-year gestation, with [[Tom Cruise]] reputedly being considered for the Ted Baker role.<ref>Freer 2001, p. 183.</ref> [[Dustin Hoffman]] was offered a role but turned it down.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Hattenstone |first=Simon |date=2012-12-14 |title=Dustin Hoffman: facing down my demons |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2012/dec/14/dustin-hoffman-interview-simon-hattenstone |access-date=2026-01-13 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

Dreyfuss had seen the 1943 melodrama "at least 35 times."<ref name="Ebert">{{Cite web |last=Ebert |first=Roger |date=December 22, 1989 |title=Always movie review & film summary (1989) {{!}} Roger Ebert |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/always-1989 |access-date=2026-01-13 |website=Chicago Sun-Times |language=en-US |via=RogerEbert.com}}</ref> For Spielberg, who recalled seeing it as a child late at night, "it was one of the films that inspired him to become a movie director,"<ref name="Ebert" /> creating an emotional connection to the times that his father, a wartime air force veteran had lived through.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Brantley |first=Lamar |date=2007 |title=Steven Spielberg as a Role Model |url=http://www.rolemodel.net/steven_spielberg.cfm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070705042416/http://www.rolemodel.net/steven_spielberg.cfm |archive-date=2007-07-05 |access-date=2026-01-13 |website=Rolemodel.net}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Scott |first=Matt |title=God of Filmmkaing Steven Spielberg Director of Minority Report |url=http://www.ambidextrouspics.com/html/steven_spielberg.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071212185115/http://www.ambidextrouspics.com/html/steven_spielberg.html |archive-date=2007-12-12 |access-date=2026-01-13 |website=www.ambidextrouspics.com}}</ref> The two friends quoted individual shots from the film to each other and when the opportunity arose, years later, were resolved to recreate the wartime fantasy.

Principal photography began on May 15, 1989; production took place in [[Western Montana|northwestern]] [[Montana]] in the [[Kootenai National Forest]], with some scenes filmed in and around [[Libby, Montana|Libby]]. Some 500 of its residents were recruited for the film as extras to act as wildland firefighters. The scenes where the plane flies over the lake at the beginning and lands in the lake at the end of the movie were filmed at [[Bull Lake, Montana|Bull Lake]], south of [[Troy, Montana|Troy]]. The scenes set in "Flat Rock, [[Colorado]]," were filmed at and around the [[Ephrata, Washington|Ephrata]] airport in [[eastern Washington]]. The scene where Pete and Hap are walking through the wheat field was filmed at [[Sprague, Washington|Sprague]], southwest of [[Spokane, Washington|Spokane]], where they spent two weeks filming in June. Footage of [[Yellowstone National Park]]'s [[Yellowstone fires of 1988|1988 fires]] was used for the fire sequences. Production wrapped in August 1989.

[[Audrey Hepburn]] appeared in ''Always'' in her last film role. Her cameo was an opportunity to raise money for her favorite cause; much of Hepburn's one million dollars plus salary was donated to [[UNICEF]].<ref>Walker 1997, p. 271.</ref><ref>Woodward 2010, pp. 361, 390.</ref>

===Aircraft used===
Two [[Douglas A-26 Invader]] fire bombers (Douglas B-26C Invader No.57]<ref>[http://registry.faa.gov/aircraftinquiry/NNumSQL.asp?NNumbertxt=9425Z&cmndfind.x=14&cmndfind.y=18 "N9425Z."] ''faa.gov''. Retrieved: December 5, 2009.</ref> and Douglas TB-26C Invader No. 59<ref>[http://registry.faa.gov/aircraftinquiry/NNumSQL.asp?NNumbertxt=4818Z&cmndfind.x=14&cmndfind.y=18 "N4818Z."] ''faa.gov''. Retrieved: December 5, 2009.</ref>) were prominently featured in ''Always''.<ref>Farmer 1990, p. 35.</ref> The flying for the film was performed by well-known film pilot [[Steve Hinton]] and Dennis Lynch, the owner of the A-26s. A combination of aerial photography, rear projection, and models was used to create the aerial sequences.<ref>Freer 2001, p. 186.</ref>

A number of other aircraft also appeared in ''Always'': [[Aeronca Champion|Aeronca 7AC Champion]], [[American Champion Decathlon|Bellanca 8KCAB Super Decathlon]], [[Beechcraft Model 18]], [[Cessna Skymaster|Cessna 337 Super Skymaster]], [[Cessna 340]], [[Consolidated PBY Catalina|Consolidated PBY-5A Catalina]], [[de Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter|de Havilland Canada DHC-6-300 Twin Otter]], [[Douglas C-54 Skymaster]], [[Fairchild C-119 Flying Boxcar|Fairchild C-119C Flying Boxcar]], [[McDonnell Douglas DC-10]] and [[North American B-25 Mitchell|North American B-25J Mitchell]]. Two helicopters were also seen: [[Bell 206]] JetRanger and [[Bell UH-1 Iroquois|Bell UH-1B Iroquois]].

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Always'' was released the same day as ''[[Tango & Cash]]'' and grossed $3.7 million in its opening weekend, finishing fifth behind ''[[Christmas Vacation]]'', ''Tango & Cash'', ''[[The War of the Roses (film)|The War of the Roses]]'', and ''[[Back to the Future Part II]]''. It went on to gross $43.9 million in the U.S. and $30.3 million in foreign territories, for a worldwide total of $74.1 million.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kurtz |first=Andy |date=February 5, 2007 |title=Directors Hall of Fame – Steven Spielberg |url=https://www.bullz-eye.com/2007/02/05/directors-hall-of-fame-steven-spielberg/ |access-date=2026-01-13 |website=bullz-eye.com |language=en-US}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]] the film holds an approval rating of 72% based on 29 reviews, with an average rating of 5.9/10. The website's critics consensus reads: "Its central romance takes occasional dives into excessive sentimentality, but ''Always'' otherwise flies high thanks to director Steven Spielberg's rousing feel for adventure."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1000725-always/?critic=columns |title= Always (1989) |website=Rotten Tomatoes |accessdate= August 24, 2026}}</ref> [[Metacritic]] assigned the film a weighted average score of 50 out of 100 based on 15 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/always |title=Always Reviews |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |access-date=February 28, 2022}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Home |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/ |access-date=2022-02-28 |website=[[CinemaScore]] |language=en-US}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' considered it "dated" and more of a "curiosity," calling it Spielberg's "weakest film since his comedy ''[[1941 (film)|1941]]''".<ref name="Ebert" /> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' gave it a more generous review: "''Always'' is a relatively small scale, engagingly casual, somewhat silly, but always entertaining fantasy."<ref>{{Cite magazine |date=December 31, 1988 |title=Always |url=https://variety.com/1988/film/reviews/always-3-1200428073/ |magazine=Variety}}</ref>

==Accolades==
''Always'' was nominated in 1991 for the [[Saturn Award]] as Best Fantasy Film, while Jerry Belson was nominated for the Best Writing category of the award at the Academy of Science Fiction, Fantasy & Horror Films (USA). Several critics have now considered the film as the progenitor of a new crop of "ghost" genre films, including ''[[Ghost (1990 film)|Ghost]]'' (1990).<ref>Jacobson, Colin. [http://www.dvdmg.com/always.shtml " 'Always' 1989 Review."] ''dvdmg.com''. Retrieved: December 5, 2009.</ref><ref>[https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1000725-always/ " 'Always' (1989)."] ''rottentomatoes.com''. Retrieved: December 5, 2009.</ref>

==Soundtrack==
* The character Pete Sandich, played by [[Richard Dreyfuss]], whistles "[[Garryowen (air)|Garryowen]]" and the theme to ''[[Leave It to Beaver]]''.
* "[[Smoke Gets in Your Eyes]]," performed live in the film by [[JD Souther]] and played later on tape by [[The Platters]], is what romantic couple Sandich and Durston refer to as "our song."
* The album was released in 1990 and featured tracks of the orchestral score of the film, composed and conducted by [[John Williams]] and performed by the [[Hollywood Studio Symphony]].
