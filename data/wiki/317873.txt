'''''Xanadu''''' is a 1980 American [[Musical film|musical]] [[fantasy film]] written by Richard Christian Danus and Marc Reid Rubel and directed by [[Robert Greenwald]]. Starring [[Olivia Newton-John]] along with [[Michael Beck]] and [[Gene Kelly]] in his final film role, the film features music by Newton-John, [[Electric Light Orchestra]], [[Cliff Richard]] and [[the Tubes]]. The title refers to the nightclub in the film, which takes its name from [[Shangdu|Xanadu]], the summer capital of [[Kublai Khan]]'s [[Yuan dynasty]] in China. The city itself appears in the 1816 poem ''[[Kubla Khan]]'' by [[Samuel Taylor Coleridge]], which is quoted by Newton-John's character in the film.

Released in the United States on August 8, 1980, by [[Universal Pictures]], the film was a critical and [[box-office bomb|box-office disappointment]], and (along with ''[[Can't Stop the Music]]'') inspired the creation of the [[Golden Raspberry Awards]] to recognize the worst films of the year. Despite this, the [[Xanadu (soundtrack)|soundtrack album]] became commercially successful worldwide and was certified double [[Music recording certification|platinum]] in the United States. The song "[[Magic (Olivia Newton-John song)|Magic]]" was a US chart-topper for Newton-John, and [[Xanadu (Olivia Newton-John and Electric Light Orchestra song)|the title track]] (performed by Newton-John and Electric Light Orchestra) reached number one in the United Kingdom and several other countries. The film has since become a [[cult following|cult classic]] for its mixture of 1980s music and culture, creative choreography, and special effects that were innovative for the time.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Chiu |first=David |title='Xanadu': Remembering The Cult Movie Musical's Amazing Soundtrack Album 40 Years Later |url=https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidchiu/2020/08/12/xanadu-remembering-the-cult-movie-musicals-amazing-soundtrack-album-40-years-later/#53da9a015fc4 |magazine=[[Forbes]] |date=August 12, 2020 |access-date=8 September 2020 |archive-date=March 2, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230302111535/https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidchiu/2020/08/12/xanadu-remembering-the-cult-movie-musicals-amazing-soundtrack-album-40-years-later/#53da9a015fc4 |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Plot==
Sonny Malone, a struggling self-employed illustrator residing in [[Los Angeles]], has failed to find passion in his art. He tears up one of his sketches and tosses the pieces out his studio window; the wind transports them toward a wall mural of nine dancing sisters that suddenly comes alive when the scraps touch the mural. The sisters fly across Earth, but one returns to roller skate through town, then deliberately slows upon encountering Sonny. She momentarily pauses to kiss him before skating away, confounding him.

Meekly returning to his old job of painting album-cover reproductions at AirFlo Records, Sonny is tasked by his tyrannical boss Simpson with painting a reproduction for a group called the 9 Sisters. The cover shows the mysterious woman whom Sonny had met in front of an abandoned [[Art Deco]]-style auditorium. The photographer tells Sonny that the woman was not supposed to be on the cover but suddenly appeared in a few of his shots. Returning to where he first encountered her, he instead encounters Danny McGuire, a former [[big band|big-band]] clarinet player and later orchestra leader turned construction mogul. During Sonny and Danny's conversation, the woman appears in person, causing Sonny to track her across town to the same auditorium, where she introduces herself as Kira but refuses to tell him anything more about herself.

Sonny and Danny befriend each other, with Danny detailing his romantic involvement in the 1940s with a singer who resembled Kira; her departure resulted in his own loss of creative passion. Kira encourages the duo to open a nightclub at the auditorium called Xanadu, and the two begin working together as partners. Sonny and Kira also gradually fall in love, culminating in a magically animated sequence. On the night before the club's opening, Kira confesses to Sonny that she is actually [[Terpsichore]], one of the [[Muses|Nine Muses of Olympus]]. She was sent to inspire the creation of Xanadu, but she cannot stay despite their mutual feelings, as she is forbidden from falling in love with mortals. Sonny becomes upset at the revelation, and Kira departs Earth having fulfilled her duty.

Danny tells Sonny to keep pursuing Kira, encouraging him not to abandon his dreams as Danny had done after his own muse left him. Sonny manages to enter Kira's home by roller skating into the Muses' mural. Inside the realm of the gods on [[Mount Helicon]], Sonny pleads with Kira's father [[Zeus]] to allow Kira to return to Earth, and Kira's sympathetic mother [[Mnemosyne]] attempts to influence Zeus. However, Zeus sends Sonny back to Earth. Kira then professes her feelings for Sonny, and Zeus ultimately relents, allowing her to be with Sonny for "maybe just one moment, or forever". Kira and the Muses perform at the grand opening of the Xanadu club before returning to their realm. Sonny is initially saddened by their departure, but after Danny orders a cocktail for him, he sees that the waitress who serves him resembles Kira, and begins a conversation with her.

==Production==
[[File:XanaduPanPacific1.JPG|thumb|center|500px|[[Pan-Pacific Auditorium]] transformed into "Xanadu" via special effects]]
Originally conceived as a relatively low-budget [[roller disco|roller-disco]] picture, a number of prominent performers joined the production, which evolved into a much larger project. However, roller skating was retained as a recurring theme, especially in the final scenes of the club's opening night. Earlier versions of the story established that Sonny Malone ([[Michael Beck]]) was the artist who created the mural from which the nine goddess sisters emerge. This provided a much stronger explanation for the [[Muses]]' interest in helping him achieve artistic success. However, continual rewrites and editing during production caused this plot point to be abandoned, except for one line spoken by Sonny as he laments his failure as a freelance artist: "I paint his van... I paint somebody else's mural..." This element of the plot was recycled and used in the later [[Xanadu (musical)|stage adaptation]]. The [[Marvel Comics]] adaptation published as ''[[Marvel Comics Super Special|Marvel Super Special]]'' #17<ref>{{Gcdb issue|id=34539|title=''Marvel Super Special'' #17}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|last= Friedt|first= Stephan|title= Marvel at the Movies: The House of Ideas' Hollywood Adaptations of the 1970s and 1980s|magazine= [[Back Issue!]]|issue= 89|page= 64|date= July 2016}}</ref> retained the more strongly emphasized connection between Sonny and the painting.

The plot of the 1947 film ''[[Down to Earth (1947 film)|Down to Earth]]'' was used as the basis for ''Xanadu''. In that film, [[Rita Hayworth]] plays [[Terpsichore]], with [[Larry Parks]] as stage producer Danny Miller. [[Gene Kelly]]'s character, Danny McGuire, previously appeared in ''[[Cover Girl (film)|Cover Girl]]'' (1944). Kelly retired from acting after the film's release and died in 1996.

[[Kenny Ortega]] and Jerry Trent served as choreographers. The [[Pan-Pacific Auditorium]] in [[Los Angeles]] was used for exterior shots of the nightclub. Sonny refers to the venue as "a dump" and the real Pan-Pacific Auditorium had fallen into disrepair. Danny remarks that "they used to have wrestling here," which was indeed true of the Pan-Pacific. The building was destroyed by fire a decade later. Xanadu's nightclub interior was built on Stage 4 of the [[Hollywood Center Studios]] beginning in 1979.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Xanadu Story chapter two |url=http://www.donosdump.com/TheXanaduStory/chapter02.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160813075457/http://www.donosdump.com/TheXanaduStory/chapter02.html |archive-date=August 13, 2016 |access-date=September 23, 2017 |url-status=usurped |website=Don-O's Dump}}</ref> [[Olivia Newton-John]] recalled problems with the script and the many story changes that occurred during filming.<ref name="ny" />

==Reception==
===Box office===
The film underperformed at the box office; it grossed only $23 million against a reported $20 million budget, a total that was insufficient to offset all related costs and return a profit.<ref name=Budget/>

===Critical response===
[[Universal Pictures|Universal]] canceled press screenings, suggesting that the studio was not confident in the film.<ref name=Varrev>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=August 13, 1980|page=23|title=Film Reviews: Xanadu|url=https://variety.com/1979/film/reviews/xanadu-1200424571/|access-date=January 12, 2020|archive-date=August 10, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200810145602/https://variety.com/1979/film/reviews/xanadu-1200424571/|url-status=live}}</ref> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it "a stupendously bad film whose only salvage is the music."<ref name=Varrev/> [[Roger Ebert]] awarded the film two stars out of four, describing the film as "a mushy and limp musical fantasy" with a confused story, redeemed only by Newton-John's "high spirits" and several strong scenes with Kelly. Ebert criticized the choreography, saying that "the dance numbers in this movie do not seem to have been conceived for film."<ref name="Ebert1980">{{cite news |last=Ebert |first=Roger |author-link=Roger Ebert |date=September 1, 1980 |title=Xanadu |newspaper=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |via=[[RogerEbert.com]] |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/xanadu-1980 |access-date=October 5, 2010 |archive-date=June 16, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130616013802/http://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/xanadu-1980 |url-status=live}}</ref> He noted that large dance scenes were not photographed well by cinematographer [[Victor J. Kemper]], who shot at eye level and failed to capture the larger patterns of dancers, with dancers in the background muddying the movement of the foreground.<ref name=Ebert1980/>

{{Rotten Tomatoes prose|33||46|Not even spandex and over-the-top musical numbers can save ''Xanadu'' from questionable acting, unimpressive effects, and a story unencumbered by logic.|ref=yes|access-date=August 30, 2025}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the {{#switch:  | episode = episode | series = series | season = season | film }} a score of 31 out of 100, based on 13 critics, indicating "generally unfavorable" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Xanadu'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/xanadu |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date= |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/xanadu|title=Xanadu|website=[[Metacritic]]|access-date=August 25, 2023|archive-date=June 25, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230625220452/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/xanadu|url-status=live}}</ref> The German television show ''Die schlechtesten Filme aller Zeiten'' (English: ''The Worst Movies of All Time'') featured the film in its third season. [[Janet Maslin]] wrote in her review: "Like ''[[The Wiz (film)|The Wiz]]''... ''Xanadu'' is desperately stylish without having any real style."<ref>{{cite news|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|author-link=Janet Maslin|first=Janet|last=Maslin|title=Miss Newton-John in 'Xanadu'|date=August 9, 1980|url=https://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9C00EFD81238F93AA3575BC0A966948260|access-date=March 3, 2017|archive-date=November 7, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171107113102/http://www.nytimes.com/movie/review?res=9C00EFD81238F93AA3575BC0A966948260}}</ref>

A double feature with ''Xanadu'' and another musical released several months earlier, ''[[Can't Stop the Music]]'', inspired [[John J.B. Wilson]] to create the [[Golden Raspberry Awards]] (or Razzies), an annual event recognizing the worst in cinema for a given year.<ref name="germain">{{cite news |last=Germain |first=David |title=25 Years of Razzing Hollywood's Stinkers |newspaper=[[South Florida Sun-Sentinel]] |agency=[[Associated Press]] |page=7D |date=February 26, 2005}}</ref> [[Robert Greenwald]] won the first [[Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Director]], and the film was nominated for six other awards at the [[1st Golden Raspberry Awards]].

==Legacy==
===Cult following===
Over the years, the film has developed a [[Cult film|cult audience]].<ref>{{cite book |editor-last1=Garraty |editor-first1=John A. |editor-last2=Carnes |editor-first2=Mark C. |author=American Council of Learned Societies |author-link=American Council of Learned Societies |title=American National Biography |publisher=[[Oxford University Press]] |volume=1 |page=312 |year=1999 |isbn=978-0-19-520635-7}}</ref>

[[Douglas Carter Beane]], who wrote the book for the musical based on the film, later called ''Xanadu'' "what happens when you let straight men near the musical... I blame [[cocaine]]. It's like people say, 'When you hear [[Ray Charles]] play, you can hear the heroin?' When you watch ''Xanadu'', you can see the cocaine up on the screen."<ref name="ny">{{cite magazine|url=https://nymag.com/arts/theater/features/32380/|magazine=[[New York (magazine)|New York]]|date=May 25, 2007|title=Springtime for 'Xanadu'|first=Michael|last=Martin|access-date=March 5, 2023|url-access=limited|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220521232818/https://nymag.com/arts/theater/features/32380/|archive-date=May 21, 2022|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2000, an unauthorized stage show titled ''Xanadu Live!'' was performed in Los Angeles, with actors speaking the film's dialogue and [[Lip sync|miming]] the songs.<ref name="ny"/>

===Stage musical===
{{Main|Xanadu (musical)}}

A $5 million [[Broadway theatre|Broadway]] musical adaptation of the same name began previews on May 23, 2007, and opened (with Newton-John and songwriter [[John Farrar]] in attendance) on July 10, 2007, starring [[Kerry Butler]] as Kira, [[Cheyenne Jackson]] as Sonny, and [[Tony Roberts (actor)|Tony Roberts]] as Danny. In the musical, Kira is the Muse [[Clio]], not Terpsichore. [[Jackie Hoffman]] and [[Mary Testa]] co-starred (in a plot twist new to the Broadway version) as evil Muse sisters [[Calliope]] and [[Melpomene|Malpomene]]. The show, which humorously parodied the plot of the film, was a surprise hit, receiving praise for its satirical approach, and was nominated for several [[Tony Awards]] at the [[62nd Tony Awards]].

The original cast recording was released in December 2007. The Broadway production closed on September 28, 2008, after 49 previews and 512 performances,<ref name="pbilltease">{{cite magazine |last=Gans |first=Andrew |url=http://www.playbill.com/news/article/105037.html |title=Xanadu Workshop – with Krakowski and Jackson – Presented Jan. 20–21 |magazine=[[Playbill]] |date=January 20, 2007 |access-date=January 29, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070218002133/http://www.playbill.com/news/article/105037.html |archive-date=February 18, 2007}}</ref> and a successful national tour followed.
