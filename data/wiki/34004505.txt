'''''Spring Breakers''''' is a 2013 American [[crime drama film]] written and directed by [[Harmony Korine]]. Starring [[James Franco]], [[Selena Gomez]], [[Vanessa Hudgens]], [[Ashley Benson]], [[Rachel Korine]], and [[Gucci Mane]], it follows four college-aged girls who go on [[spring break]] in [[St. Petersburg, Florida]] and descend into a world of drugs, crime, and violence after meeting an eccentric local drug dealer.<ref name="Rachel">{{cite web|date=November 4, Harmony Korine had developed the concept over several years prior to production, with fleeting ideas about the plot and what should transpire. His initial desire was to create a "[[Sensory perception|sensory]] film" that was more about feeling than action; he placed little importance on narrative or plot, the idea for which came later.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sbs.com.au/movies/article/2013/05/03/spring-breakers-harmony-korine-and-james-franco-interview|title=Spring Breakers: Harmony Korine and James Franco interview|work=[[Special Broadcasting Service|SBS]]|date=May 3, 2013|access-date=September 9, 2015|archive-date=June 3, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160603224014/http://www.sbs.com.au/movies/article/2013/05/03/spring-breakers-harmony-korine-and-james-franco-interview|url-status=live}}</ref> Once Korine developed the story's backbone, which takes place around the American spring break period, he traveled to Florida to write the screenplay. Production began in 2012. It is one of Korine's first theatrical works to receive a [[wide release]].<ref>{{Cite book|first=Eric|last=Kohn|title=Harmony Korine: Interviews|publisher=[[University Press of Mississippi]]|year=2014|isbn= 978-1-62674-348-9}}</ref>

''Spring Breakers'' was released on March 22, 2013, in the United States by [[A24]] and received generally positive reviews from critics, with praise for the acting and Debie's cinematography. It grossed $31 million worldwide on a $5 million budget and has been described as a [[Cult film|cult classic]].<ref name="midnight" /><ref>{{cite web|date=March 27, 2013|url=http://www.idsnews.com/news/weekend/story.aspx?id=91982|title=Spring Breakers: The newest cult classic|work=WEEKEND|publisher=IDS News|access-date=July 5, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130905170930/http://www.idsnews.com/news/weekend/story.aspx?id=91982|archive-date=September 5, 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|date=March 18, 2013|url=http://www.contactmusic.com/news/spring-breakers-trailer-selena-gomez_3560943|title=Is Record Breaking 'Spring Breakers' A Cult Movie In The Making?|work=Contactmusic.com|access-date=July 5, 2013|archive-date=January 21, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150121042101/http://www.contactmusic.com/news/spring-breakers-trailer-selena-gomez_3560943|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cinemablend.com/reviews/Spring-Breakers-6337.html|title=Spring Breakers Review|author=Sean O'Connell|work=CinemaBlend.com|date=March 19, 2013|access-date=July 5, 2013|archive-date=March 22, 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230322092350/https://www.cinemablend.com/reviews/Spring-Breakers-6337.html|url-status=live}}</ref> The film was selected to compete for the [[Golden Lion]] at the [[69th Venice International Film Festival]].<ref name="labiennale">{{cite web |url=http://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/festival/lineup/off-sel/venezia69/ |title=Venezia 69 |access-date=July 26, 2012 |work=labiennale |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120728223053/http://www.labiennale.org/en/cinema/festival/lineup/off-sel/venezia69 |archive-date=July 28, 2012 }}</ref> A sequel is in development.

==Plot==
<!-- Due to WP:FILMPLOT, the plot needs to be generic and CANNOT EXCEED 400-700 WORDS! The total word count is 505; DO NOT ADD UNLESS NECCESSARY! -->
College students Brit, Candy, and Cotty often spend their time partying while their friend Faith attends a religious youth group. As their classmates head to spring break, they are stuck behind because they don't have the money to travel. Desperate to make the trip, Brit and Candy get high on cocaine, don ski masks, and rob a local restaurant, using the threat of hammers and realistic-looking [[squirt gun]]s. They are assisted by Cotty, who drives (and later burns) the getaway car stolen from one of their professors. Cotty, Candy, and Brit divulge the details of their crime to a horrified Faith, who keeps quiet about it.

In [[St. Petersburg, Florida]], the girls attend wild beach parties fueled by alcohol, drugs, and sex. After a particularly wild party, all four are arrested for using narcotics. They spend the night in a [[holding cell]], but are bailed out by Alien, a rapper, drug hustler, and arms dealer. Alien charms Cotty, Candy, and Brit with his money and "bad boy" swagger, but Faith is extremely uncomfortable. Alien takes the girls to a local club frequented by gang members, where Faith becomes even more uneasy. Alien attempts to seduce Faith and convince her to stay with him, using equal parts menace, threats, and tenderness, but Faith leaves, begging the others to come with her. They refuse, and she returns to college alone.

Alien takes the remaining girls to a strip club owned by his rival and childhood best friend, Big Arch, who warns Alien to stop selling drugs in his territory. Alien takes the girls to his mansion, where he flaunts his drug money and cache of weapons, describing his life as the "[[American Dream]]". Brit and Candy grab two of his guns and threaten to kill him. Aroused, Alien fellates the guns and declares that he has fallen in love with the girls, claiming that they are his soulmates.

Alien arms the girls with pink ski masks and shotguns and takes them to his pool, where the girls ask him to play something inspiring on the piano. They sing [[Britney Spears]]' "[[Everytime]]", while a [[Montage (filmmaking)|montage]] plays of them performing several armed robberies. While in Alien's car, they are approached by Big Arch and another member of his gang who threaten them and execute a [[drive-by shooting]], wounding Cotty in the process. Alien promises to retaliate, but a traumatized Cotty comes to her senses and chooses to return to college. Brit and Candy stay behind and have [[Threesome|three-way]] sex with Alien in his pool. The three decide to take revenge on Big Arch. In a flashforward, the two girls call <!--to who?  -->home, promising to work harder and become better people.

In the present, the three take a motorboat to Big Arch's mansion. After they dock at the pier, Alien is immediately shot and killed by one of Big Arch's guards. Brit and Candy carry on, killing the rest of the members before confronting and killing Big Arch. The camera pans over the dead bodies of Big Arch's gang while the girls speak in a voice-over, first heard earlier in the film, describing the beach's beauty and musing that they have discovered who they truly are. Brit and Candy, silent and wearing pensive, ambiguous expressions, drive to college in Big Arch's [[Lamborghini]]. A final flashback shows the two girls kissing Alien's dead body before departing.

==Cast==
* [[James Franco]] as Alien, a rapper, drug hustler, and arms dealer who takes the girls under his wing.
* [[Selena Gomez]] as Faith, who tries to devote her life to Christianity but is also friends with party girls Brit, Candy and Cotty.
* [[Vanessa Hudgens]] as Candy, an irresponsible and uncaring college girl and Brit's best friend.
* [[Ashley Benson]] as Brit, a rebellious and danger-seeking college girl, also Candy's best friend.
* [[Rachel Korine]] as Cotty, a girl who likes to party but is more careful than her friends Candy and Brit.
* [[Gucci Mane]] as Archie, Alien's childhood best friend who has become a rival drug dealer.

Additionally, [[Heather Morris]], Ash Lendzion, and Emma Holzer appear as Faith's friends Bess, Forest, and Heather. [[Jeff Jarrett]] portrays a youth pastor, Russell Stuart appears as a DJ, and Houston-area sports writer [[John McClain]] appears as a judge.

=== Filming ===
The film was shot in March and April 2012 in and around [[St. Petersburg, Florida]]. The exterior shots and some interior shots of the college that Gomez and Benson attend were filmed at [[New College of Florida]]. Dormitory and classroom shots were filmed at [[Ringling College of Art and Design]].<ref name="interviewmag" /><ref>{{cite web | title='Spring Breakers' locations guide for locals | website=Tampa Bay Times | date=2013-03-21 | url=https://www.tampabay.com/things-to-do/movies/spring-breakers-locations-guide-for-locals/2110114/ | access-date=2023-04-19 | archive-date=April 19, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230419120658/https://www.tampabay.com/things-to-do/movies/spring-breakers-locations-guide-for-locals/2110114/ | url-status=live }}</ref> Korine announced in 2013 he planned to "remix" the film's unused footage and alternate takes into an entirely new cut.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Lee |first1=Chris |title=Harmony Korine on 'Spring Breakers': 'We're doing a remix' |url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-xpm-2013-jun-20-la-et-mn-korine-spring-breakers-remix-20130619-story.html |access-date=26 September 2019 |newspaper=Los Angeles Times |date=20 June 2013 |archive-date=September 26, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190926173601/https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/movies/la-xpm-2013-jun-20-la-et-mn-korine-spring-breakers-remix-20130619-story.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===
''Spring Breakers'' grossed $14,124,284 in North America and $17,600,000 in other countries for a worldwide total of $31,724,284.<ref name="BOM" /> In North America, the film opened to #6 in its first weekend with $4,858,944, behind ''[[The Croods]]'', ''[[Admission (film)|Admission]]'', ''[[The Call (2013 film)|The Call]]'', ''[[Oz the Great and Powerful]]'', and ''[[Olympus Has Fallen]]''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?yr=2013&wknd=12&p=.htm |title=Weekend Box Office Results for March 22–24, 2013 |publisher=IMDB |work=[[Box Office Mojo]] |date=March 25, 2013 |access-date=March 25, 2013 |archive-date=March 24, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130324215219/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/weekend/chart/?yr=2013&wknd=12&p=.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Critical response===
[[File:James Franco 2007 Spiderman 3 premiere.jpg|thumb|249x249px|The performance of [[James Franco]] earned praise from critics. A24 started a campaign to support him for a nomination for the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor]].]]
On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 67% based on 200 reviews, with an average rating of 6.6/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "''Spring Breakers'' blends stinging social commentary with bikini cheesecake and a bravura James Franco performance."<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/spring_breakers_2013/ |title=''Spring Breakers (2013)''|publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |work=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |access-date=September 7, 2024 |archive-date=November 17, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201117025557/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/spring_breakers_2013 |url-status=live }}</ref> At [[Metacritic]], which assigns a weighted mean rating from film critics, it received a rating score of 63 out of 100 based on 40 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/spring-breakers|title=''Spring Breakers''|work=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=December 8, 2014|archive-date=November 17, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201117025551/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/spring-breakers|url-status=live}}</ref>

Xan Brooks of ''[[The Guardian]]'' said the film is Korine's "most fully realised, purely satisfying feature film since ''[[Gummo]]''."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2012/sep/05/spring-breakers-review|title=Spring Breakers – review|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|first=Xan|last=Brooks|date=September 5, 2012|access-date=December 13, 2016|archive-date=October 14, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161014100714/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2012/sep/05/spring-breakers-review|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Emma Seligman]] of ''[[The Huffington Post]]'' described the film as "''[[Scarface (1983 film)|Scarface]]'' meets [[Britney Spears]]."<ref>{{cite news|url= https://www.huffingtonpost.com/emma-seligman/review-spring-breakers_b_1894384.html |title=REVIEW: 'Spring Breakers'|work=[[The Huffington Post]]|first=Emma|last=Seligman|date=September 18, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170107195045/https://www.huffingtonpost.com/emma-seligman/review-spring-breakers_b_1894384.html|archive-date=January 7, 2017}}</ref> Oliver Lyttlelton of ''[[IndieWire]]'' gave the film a B, stating that the film would be a future cult favorite for "[[midnight movie]]goers".<ref name="midnight">{{cite news|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/venice-review-harmony-korines-spring-breakers-is-a-surprisingly-semi-conventional-genre-flick-future-cult-favorite-20120904|title=Venice Review: Harmony Korine's 'Spring Breakers' Is A Semi-Conventional Genre Flick & Future Cult Favorite|work=[[IndieWire]]|first=Oliver|last=Lyttlelton|date=September 4, 2012|access-date=February 28, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130504105506/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/venice-review-harmony-korines-spring-breakers-is-a-surprisingly-semi-conventional-genre-flick-future-cult-favorite-20120904|archive-date=May 4, 2013}}</ref>

Guy Lodge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' gave it a negative review, saying, "this attractively fizzy pic may be a shock to the system for fans of teen queens Selena Gomez and Vanessa Hudgens, but remains pretty toothless titillation by its writer-helmer's standards."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.variety.com/review/VE1117948216/|title=Variety Reviews – Spring Breakers – Venice Film Festival Review|magazine=Variety|first=Guy|last=Lodge|date=September 5, 2012}}</ref> David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' noted that James Franco gives one of his more bizarre performances in his unpredictable career, saying "he's a cross between [[Bo Derek]] in ''[[10 (1979 film)|10]]'' and [[Richard Kiel]] in ''[[Moonraker (film)|Moonraker]]''."<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/spring-breakers-venice-review-368066|title=Spring Breakers: Venice Review|magazine=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |first=David|last=Rooney|date=September 5, 2012}}</ref>

Andrew Schenker of ''[[Slant Magazine]]'' gave the film 3 out of 4 stars.<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/review/spring-breakers|title=Spring Breakers - Film Review|magazine=[[Slant Magazine]]|first=Andrew|last=Schenker|date=March 12, 2013}}</ref> Jamie Dunn of ''[[The Skinny (magazine)|The Skinny]]'' gave it 4 out of 5 stars, saying: "If [[Michael Mann (director)|Michael Mann]] was to take a lot of hallucinogenics and shoot a [[Girls Gone Wild (franchise)|Girls Gone Wild]] video, it might look something like this."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.theskinny.co.uk/film/film_festivals/304162-spring_breakers|title=Spring Breakers - Film Review|publisher=[[The Skinny (magazine)|The Skinny]]|first=Jamie|last=Dunn|date=April 1, 2013|access-date=October 21, 2013|archive-date=October 21, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131021042234/http://www.theskinny.co.uk/film/film_festivals/304162-spring_breakers|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Richard Roeper]] of ''[[Rogerebert.com]]'' gave the film three-and-a-half stars out of four, praising the character of Alien and the film's sense of humor.<ref name="roeper">{{cite news|last=Roeper|first=Richard|date=March 20, 2013|title=Spring Breakers Movie Review|work=[[Rogerebert.com]]|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/spring-breakers-2013-1|access-date=2016-01-21|archive-date=November 2, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191102143234/https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/spring-breakers-2013-1|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Controversies ===

==== Riff Raff controversy ====
On February 15, 2012, Korine contacted rapper [[Riff Raff (rapper)|Riff Raff]] about appearing in an upcoming film, which later developed as ''Spring Breakers''.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Buyanovsky|first=Dan|date=July 19, 2013|title=RiFF RaFF Speaks On 'Spring Breakers' Lawsuit, What He'd Do With $10 Million|url=http://www.xxlmag.com/news/2013/07/riff-raff-speaks-on-spring-breakers-lawsuit-what-hed-do-with-10-million/|magazine=[[XXL (magazine)|XXL]]|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=July 23, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130723161126/http://www.xxlmag.com/news/2013/07/riff-raff-speaks-on-spring-breakers-lawsuit-what-hed-do-with-10-million/|url-status=live}}</ref> Once the trailer was released, there was speculation that the character Alien was based on Riff Raff.<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Long|first=Stephanie|date=March 28, 2012|title=James Franco to Play Riff Raff in Upcoming Harmony Korine Film|url=http://www.vibe.com/article/james-franco-play-riff-raff-upcoming-harmony-korine-film|magazine=[[Vibe (magazine)|Vibe]]}}</ref> According to Franco, Alien is based on the underground rap artist Dangeruss, who has a cameo in the film. "Of course, Harmony and I looked at some of Riff Raff's videos as inspiration, but he was one of a number of people we looked at. I would say the biggest influence on the role was this local, Florida rapper named Dangeruss. He's fairly unknown, but he was down there in the place, living the life, and he became the biggest model for me, and he's in the movie."<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Sebra|first=Matthew|date=May 1, 2012|title=Last Night...Talking Cornrows with James Franco|url=https://www.gq.com/style/blogs/the-gq-eye/2012/05/last-nighttalking-cornrows-with-james-franco.html|magazine=[[GQ]]|access-date=August 1, 2013}}</ref>

After much back-and-forth about the issue, Riff Raff announced in July 2013 that he was suing the creators of ''Spring Breakers'' for $10 million for "sampling" his life without his permission or a proper producer credit.<ref>{{cite magazine|author=C. Vernon Coleman|date=July 16, 2013|title=Riff Raff Is Suing 'Spring Breakers' Creators For $10 Million|url=http://www.xxlmag.com/news/2013/07/riff-raff-is-suing-spring-breakers-creators-for-10-million/|magazine=XXL|access-date=August 1, 2013|archive-date=July 21, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130721210630/http://www.xxlmag.com/news/2013/07/riff-raff-is-suing-spring-breakers-creators-for-10-million/|url-status=live}}</ref> However, a September 2013 search by ''[[LA Weekly]]'' for court documents resulted in no findings.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Weiss|first1=Jeff|title=Is Riff Raff Serious?|newspaper=LA Weekly|url=http://www.laweekly.com/2012-05-24/music/riff_raff_sodmg_mtv_diplo_mad_decent/|access-date=24 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120619072127/http://www.laweekly.com/2012-05-24/music/riff_raff_sodmg_mtv_diplo_mad_decent/|archive-date=June 19, 2012|ref=laweekly}}</ref>

==== Portrayal of women ====
''Spring Breakers'' has generated debate and controversy among critics, with some regarding the film as sexist due to its [[Sexual objectification|objectification]] of women, while others viewed the film as a [[Feminism|feminist]] or female-empowerment film. In regard to the former perspective, Heather Long in ''[[The Guardian]]'' suggests that the film "reinforces [[rape culture]]" and "turns young women into sex objects."<ref>{{cite news|last=Long|first=Heather|date=March 28, 2013|title=Spring Breakers isn't just a terrible movie, it reinforces rape culture|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|url=https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2013/mar/28/spring-breakers-movie-wild-girls-rape-culture|access-date=November 18, 2013|archive-date=October 23, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131023081257/http://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2013/mar/28/spring-breakers-movie-wild-girls-rape-culture|url-status=live}}</ref> Other reviewers state that it "pushes [[Alcoholic beverage|booze]]-and-[[bikini]] hedonism to the extreme,"<ref>{{cite news|last=Meyer|first=Carla|date=March 22, 2013|title=Movie review: James Franco only non-vacuous part of Spring Breakers'|newspaper=[[The Sacramento Bee]]|url=http://www.sacbee.com/2013/03/22/5279605/james-franco-only-non-vacuous.html|access-date=November 18, 2013|archive-date=October 23, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131023155118/http://www.sacbee.com/2013/03/22/5279605/james-franco-only-non-vacuous.html|url-status=dead}}</ref> as the "camera glides up, down, and around these women's bodies like a giant tongue".<ref>{{cite journal|last=Edelstein|first=David|date=March 15, 2013|title=Three New Films Examine What It Means When Girls Act Out|url=https://www.npr.org/2013/03/15/174322481/three-new-films-examine-what-it-means-when-girls-act-out|journal=[[NPR|National Public Radio]]|access-date=September 5, 2013|archive-date=December 4, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131204020417/http://www.npr.org/2013/03/15/174322481/three-new-films-examine-what-it-means-when-girls-act-out|url-status=live}}</ref> According to Josh Eells in ''[[Rolling Stone]]'', the film presents "a kind of girl-power camaraderie that could almost be called feminist," a result of the director's intent to "do the most radical work, but put it out in the most commercial way (...) to infiltrate the mainstream".<ref>{{cite magazine|last=Eells|first=Josh|date=March 15, 2013|title=Inside 'Spring Breakers,' the Most Debauched Movie of the Year|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/news/inside-spring-breakers-the-most-debauched-movie-of-the-year-20130315|magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]|access-date=November 18, 2013}}</ref> In his review of the film, [[Richard Roeper]] wrote: 
<blockquote>"Korine's camera is nearly an intrusive weapon as he lingers over the soft, limber bodies of Vanessa Hudgens, Selena Gomez, Ashley Benson, and his wife, Rachel Korine.... I think that's sort of the point. When a pre-med student on spring break loses her top, drinks to the point of passing out, and grabs a willing lugnut by the ears for six hours of anonymous fun, is she setting the woman's movement back 40 years, or taking charge of her life like any man would do at that age?"<ref name="roeper" /></blockquote>

==<span class="anchor" id="Spring Breakers: The Second Coming"></span>Sequel==
A [[sequel]], under the name of ''Spring Breakers: The Second Coming'', was announced on May 6, 2014, to have been written by [[Irvine Welsh]] and directed by [[Jonas Åkerlund]]. Welsh's script focused on a set of Spring Breakers coming into conflict with Christian extremists,<ref name="SB2">{{cite magazine |url=http://www.screendaily.com/5071351.article |title=Wild Bunch unveils full Cannes 2014 slate |last=Goodfellow |first=Melanie |date=May 6, 2014 |magazine=ScreenDaily |access-date=June 9, 2016}}</ref> and Wild Bunch originally slated the film to debut at the upcoming Cannes Film Festival. Upon the announcement, Franco released a statement saying that the sequel was "not being done with Harmony Korine or my consent" and that the producers were "capitalizing on that innovative film to make money on a weak sequel" and attempting to "make money off someone else's creativity".<ref name="SB2Franco">{{cite web |url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/james-franco-says-spring-breakers-sequel-will-be-a-terrible-film-that-makes-money-off-someone-elses-creativity-20140515 |title=James Franco Says 'Spring Breakers' Sequel Will Be "A Terrible Film" That Makes "Money Off Someone Else's Creativity" |last=Jagernauth |first=Kevin |date=May 15, 2014 |publisher=The Playlist |work=Indiewire |access-date=June 9, 2016 |archive-date=August 14, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140814015514/http://blogs.indiewire.com/theplaylist/james-franco-says-spring-breakers-sequel-will-be-a-terrible-film-that-makes-money-off-someone-elses-creativity-20140515 |url-status=live }}</ref>

By 2017, the project had been shelved in favor of a digital series for short-video platform Blackpills; the series never materialized, however, and Blackpills itself shut down after less than two years online. As a result, development on ''Spring Breakers: The Second Coming'' had restarted, with Harmony Korine, who would direct the 2019 film ''[[The Beach Bum]]'', being open to directing and writing the sequel.

In May 2025, a sequel, ''Spring Breakers: Salvation Mountain'', was officially announced, with [[Chris Hanley]] and Jordan Gertner of Muse Productions returning to produce. [[Matthew Bright]], director of the 1996 cult film [[Freeway (1996 film)|''Freeway'']]<ref>{{Citation |last=Bright |first=Matthew |title=Freeway |date=1997-09-03 |type=Crime, Drama, Thriller |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0116361/ |access-date=2025-08-05 |others=Reese Witherspoon, Kiefer Sutherland, Bokeem Woodbine |publisher=The Kushner-Locke Company, August Entertainment, Davis Films}}</ref>, will direct, while [[Bella Thorne]], [[Baby Ariel|Ariel Martin]], [[True Whitaker]], and [[Grace Van Dien]] were announced to join the cast.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ritman |first=Alex |date=May 14, 2025 |title='Spring Breakers 2' Set With New Cast: Bella Thorne, 'Stranger Things' Star Grace Van Dien and More |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/global/spring-breakers-2-bella-thorne-sequel-1236397224/ |access-date=June 3, 2025 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]}}</ref> However, in August 2026, Thorne would write and direct the sequel instead.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2026/08/bella-thorne-spring-breakers-2-tapped-write-direct-star-1237029133/|title=Bella Thorne Says ‘Spring Breakers 2’ Producers Tapped Her To Write, Direct & Star In Sequel After Seeing Directorial Debut|work=Deadline|last=Garner|first=Glenn|date=August 7, 2026|access-date=August 8, 2026}}</ref>
