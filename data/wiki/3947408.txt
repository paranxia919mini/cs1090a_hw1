'''''The Last Man on Earth''''' ({{langx|it|'''L'ultimo uomo della Terra'''}}) is a 1964 [[Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction|post-apocalyptic]] [[science fiction film|science fiction]] [[horror film]] directed by [[Sidney Salkow]] and [[Ubaldo Ragona]], based on the 1954 novel ''[[I Am Legend (novel)|I Am Legend]]'' by [[Richard Matheson]]. It stars [[Vincent Price]] and [[Franca Bettoia]], and was produced by [[Robert L. Lippert]]. The screenplay was written in part by Matheson, but he was dissatisfied with the result and chose to be credited under the alias "Logan Swanson".

The film was a co-production between the United States and Italy, and was filmed on-location in [[Rome]]. It was released in the United States by [[American International Pictures]]. In the 1980s, the film entered the [[public domain]].<ref>{{Cite book |last=Lampley |first=Jonathan Malcolm |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=bkeiwEZI4-cC&pg=PA98 |title=Women in the Horror Films of Vincent Price |date=2010 |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |isbn=978-0786457496 |location=Jefferson, North Carolina |page=98}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Calvert |first=Steve |date=7 September 2018 |title=Public Domain Movie: The Last Man on Earth (1964) |url=http://www.steve-calvert.co.uk/movie-last-man-earth-1964/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190127152722/http://www.steve-calvert.co.uk/movie-last-man-earth-1964/ |archive-date=January 27, 2019 |access-date=January 26, 2019 |website=A Passion For Horror}}</ref>

''The Last Man on Earth'' is the first of three adaptations of Matheson's novel, followed by ''[[The Omega Man]]'' (1971) and [[I Am Legend (film)|''I Am Legend'']] (2007).

==Plot==
[[File:The Last Man on Earth (1964) by Ubaldo Ragona and Sidney Salkow.webm | thumb|''The Last Man on Earth'' (full movie)]]
It is 1968, and Dr. Robert Morgan lives in a world where everyone else has been infected by a [[pandemic|plague]] that has turned them into [[undead]], [[Vampire|vampiric]] creatures that cannot stand sunlight, fear mirrors, and are repelled by [[garlic]]. They would kill Morgan if they could, but they are weak and unintelligent. Every day Morgan carries out the same routine: He wakes up, marks another day on the calendar, gathers his weapons, and then goes hunting for [[vampire]]s, killing as many as he can and then burning the bodies to prevent them from coming back. At night, he locks himself inside his house.

A [[Flashback (narrative)|flashback]] sequence explains that, in December 1965, Morgan's wife Virginia and daughter Kathy had succumbed to the plague before it was widely known by the public that the dead would return to life. Instead of taking his wife to the same public burn pit used to dispose of his daughter's corpse, Morgan buried her without the knowledge of the authorities. When his wife returned to his home and attacked him, Morgan became aware of the need to kill the plague victims with a wooden stake. Morgan hypothesizes that he is [[Immunity (medicine)|immune]] to the [[bacteria]] from a bite by an infected [[vampire bat]] when he was stationed in [[Panama]], which may have introduced a diluted form of the plague into his blood.

One day, a dog appears in the neighborhood. Desperate for companionship, Morgan chases after the dog, but does not catch it. Sometime later the dog appears, wounded, at Morgan's doorstep. He takes the dog into his home and treats its wounds, looking forward to having company for the first time in three years. He quickly discovers, however, that it, too, has become infected with the plague. Morgan starts burying the dog, which is now impaled with a wooden stake. He sinks further into depression and loneliness.

After burying the dog, Morgan spots a woman in the distance. The woman, Ruth, is terrified of Morgan at first sight and runs from him. Morgan convinces her to return to his home, but he is suspicious of her true nature. Ruth becomes ill when Morgan waves garlic in her face, but claims that she has a weak stomach. Morgan's suspicion that Ruth is infected is confirmed when he discovers her attempting to inject herself with a combination of blood and [[vaccine]] that holds the disease at bay. Ruth initially draws a gun on Morgan, but ultimately surrenders it to him. She tells him that she is part of a group of people like her&nbsp;— infected, but under treatment&nbsp;— and was sent to spy on Morgan. The vaccine allows the people to function normally with the drug in the bloodstream, but once it wears off, the infection takes over the body again. Ruth explains that her people are planning to rebuild society as they destroy the remaining humans, and that many of the vampires Morgan killed were still alive. Ruth desperately urges Morgan to flee, but he refuses.

While Ruth is asleep, Morgan transfuses his own blood into her. She is immediately cured, and Morgan sees hope that, together, they can cure the rest of her people. Moments later, however, Ruth's people attack. Morgan takes the gun and flees his home while the attackers kill the vampires gathered around Morgan's home. Ruth's people spot Morgan and chase him. He exchanges gunfire with them and picks up [[tear gas]] grenades from a police station armory along the way. While the tear gas delays his pursuers somewhat, Morgan is wounded by gunfire and retreats into a church. As he stands at the [[altar]], Morgan denounces his pursuers as "freaks" and one of his pursuers finally impales him with a thrown spear. In his final moments as Ruth cradles him, Morgan laments that his pursuers were afraid of him, then declares that he is the last true man on Earth, before bleeding out. As Ruth walks away from Morgan's body, she notices a baby crying and tries to assure the child that everyone is safe now.

==Production==
===Development===
Producer [[Anthony Hinds]] purchased the rights to Matheson's novel for [[Hammer Film Productions]]. Matheson wrote a script, and Hammer announced in 1958 that they would make it.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gabrish Conlan |first=Mark |date=July 29, 2018 |title=The Last Man on Earth |url=http://moviemagg.blogspot.com/2018/07/the-last-man-on-earth-associated.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190127153035/http://moviemagg.blogspot.com/2018/07/the-last-man-on-earth-associated.html |archive-date=January 27, 2019 |access-date=January 26, 2019 |website=Movie Magg}}</ref> However, [[Film censorship in the United Kingdom|British censors]] would not allow the film to be produced, so Hinds resold the script to American producer [[Robert L. Lippert]].

Lippert had wanted to make a "last man on Earth"-type film for a while. In the late 1950s, [[Charles Marquis Warren]] and Robert Stabler optioned a novel by science fiction writer [[George R. Stewart]] called ''[[Earth Abides]]''. [[Harry Spalding]], who worked for Lippert, said the release of ''[[The World, the Flesh and the Devil (1959 film)|The World, the Flesh and the Devil]]'' (1959) killed off plans for that project. Spalding then read Matheson's novel and suggested Lippert film that book instead.<ref name="harry">{{Cite book |last=Weaver |first=Tom |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=esCnTSqGtUYC&q=harry+spaling+writer&pg=PA320 |title=Double Feature Creature Attack: A Monster Merger of Two More Volumes of Classic Interviews |date=19 February 2003 |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |isbn=978-0786482153 |location=Jefferson, North Carolina |page=333}}</ref> The project was announced in August 1962.

Lippert originally told Matheson that [[Fritz Lang]] would direct the film, and Matheson thought that would be "wonderful". Eventually, however, [[Sidney Salkow]] was chosen to direct. Matheson made the follow-up comment: "Well, there's a bit of a drop."<ref name="tom">{{Cite book |last=Weaver |first=Tom |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wOsGOm3YwokC&q=%22last+man+on+earth%22+salkow+interview&pg=PA306 |title=Science Fiction Stars and Horror Heroes: Interviews with Actors, Directors, Producers and Writers of the 1940s through 1960s |date=2006 |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |isbn=978-0786428571 |location=Jefferson, North Carolina |page=306}}</ref>

===Filming===
To save money, the film was shot in [[black and white]] in [[Italy]] with a predominantly Italian cast and crew.<ref name="AIP">{{Cite book |last=McGee |first=Mark |title=Faster and Furiouser: The Revised and Fattened Fable of American International Pictures |date=1996 |publisher=[[McFarland & Company]] |isbn=978-0786401376 |location=Jefferson, North Carolina |pages=207–208}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last1=Brioni |first1=Simone |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=lOijDwAAQBAJ |title=Simone Brioni and Daniele Comberiati, Italian Science Fiction: The Other in Literature and Film. New York: Palgrave, 2019. |last2=Comberiati |first2=Daniele |date=18 July 2019 |publisher=Springer |isbn=9783030193263}}</ref> [[Vincent Price]] and child actress Christi Courtland were the only Americans among the cast. Typically for an Italian production of the time, the film was shot [[MOS (filmmaking)|MOS]] (without sync sound). The Italian cast recited their English dialogue [[Phonetics|phonetically]], and were later [[Dubbing|dubbed]] by English-speaking voice actors. [[Franca Bettoia]] was dubbed by [[Carolyn De Fonseca]].

Much of the film was shot on the futuristic architecture of the [[EUR, Rome|EUR]] district. Interiors were shot on sets at the [[Titanus]] studios. The climax was shot in [[San Pio X alla Balduina]], a Catholic church in [[Rome]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fuller |first=Peter |date=March 11, 2018 |title=The Last Man on Earth &#124; Touring the original Italian film locations in Rome |url=https://www.vincentpricelegacy.uk/the-last-man-on-earth-touring-the-original-italian-film-locations-in-rome/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211125204549/https://www.vincentpricelegacy.uk/the-last-man-on-earth-touring-the-original-italian-film-locations-in-rome/ |archive-date=November 25, 2021 |access-date=November 25, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Weinstock |first=Jeffrey |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=pSnwAAAAQBAJ&dq=%22the+last+man+on+earth+%22%22church%22&pg=PA61 |title=The Vampire Film: Undead Cinema |date=April 3, 2012 |publisher=Columbia University Press |isbn=9780231850032 |via=Google Books|page=61}}</ref><ref name="auto">{{Cite web |last=Macchi |first=David |date=September 22, 2019 |title=ROMAPEDIA: St. PIUS V |url=http://romapedia.blogspot.com/2019/09/st-pius-v_22.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211125204946/http://romapedia.blogspot.com/2019/09/st-pius-v_22.html |archive-date=November 25, 2021 |access-date=November 25, 2021}}</ref>

Matheson later said the film was the most faithful adaptation of his book, but he also called the result "inept" and used a pen name for his screenplay. He later said he thought [[Harrison Ford]] as star and [[George Miller (filmmaker)|George Miller]] as director would have been the ideal creative combination.<ref name="tom" />

== Release ==
The film was released in the United States by [[American International Pictures]] on May 6, 1964. A dubbed version was released in Rome in 1964.

== Home media ==
[[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer|MGM Home Video]], the current owners of the AIP film catalog, released a digitally remastered [[widescreen]] version of the film on [[DVD-Video|DVD]] in September 2005. In 2014, Scream Factory released Blu-ray version in the Vince Price Collection II, which included six other Vincent Price films.

==Reception==
Although the film was not considered a success upon its release, it later gained a more favorable reputation as a classic of the genre.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Hall |first=Phil |author-link=Phil Hall (US writer) |date=2006-04-21 |title=THE BOOTLEG FILES: "THE LAST MAN ON EARTH" Bootleg Files, Features, Uncategorized Film Threat |url=https://filmthreat.com/uncategorized/the-bootleg-files-the-last-man-on-earth/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201203133152/https://filmthreat.com/uncategorized/the-bootleg-files-the-last-man-on-earth/ |archive-date=December 3, 2020 |access-date=2026-03-21 |website=Film Threat |language=en-US}}</ref> As of February 2025, ''The Last Man on Earth'' holds an 80% rating at the film [[review aggregator]] Web site [[Rotten Tomatoes]] from 30 reviews.<ref>{{Cite web |title=''The Last Man on Earth'' reviews |url=https://rottentomatoes.com/m/1050388-last_man_on_earth/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200923063213/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/1050388-last_man_on_earth |archive-date=September 23, 2020 |access-date=January 26, 2019 |website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]] |publisher=[[Fandango Media]] |location=Los Angeles, California}}</ref> [[Phil Hall (US writer)|Phil Hall]] of ''[[Film Threat]]'' called ''The Last Man on Earth'' "the best Vincent Price movie ever made".<ref name=":0" />

Among the less favorable reviews, Steve Biodrowski of ''[[Cinefantastique]]'' felt the film was "hampered by an obviously low budget and some poorly recorded, post-production dubbing that creates an amateurish feel, undermining the power of its story",<ref name="cinefantastique">{{Cite web |last=Biodrowski |first=Steve |date=January 29, 2008 |title=The Last Man on Earth (1964) - Film Review |url=http://cinefantastiqueonline.com/2008/01/29/film-review-the-last-man-on-earth-1964/ |url-status=deviated |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090919184720/http://cinefantastiqueonline.com/2008/01/29/film-review-the-last-man-on-earth-1964/ |archive-date=September 19, 2009 |access-date=June 16, 2009 |website=Cinefantastique}}</ref> while [[Jonathan Rosenbaum]] of the ''[[Chicago Reader]]'' remarked, "Some would consider this version better than the 1971 [[remake]] with [[Charlton Heston]], ''[[The Omega Man]]'', but that isn't much of an achievement".<ref>{{Cite news |last=Rosenbaum |first=Jonathan |author-link=Jonathan Rosenbaum |date=December 10, 2007 |title=''The Last Man on Earth'' review |work=[[Chicago Reader]] |url=http://onfilm.chicagoreader.com/movies/capsules/5199_LAST_MAN_ON_EARTH |url-status=live |access-date=January 26, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171207232842/https://www.chicagoreader.com/chicago/the-last-man-on-earth/Film?oid=1054971 |archive-date=December 7, 2017}}</ref>

=== Creators' response ===
Among the film's creators, Price "had a certain fondness for the film" and felt it was better than ''The Omega Man''.<ref name="cinefantastique" />

Richard Matheson co-wrote the film's screenplay, but was unhappy with the results. To keep receiving [[Passive income|residual income]] from the film, though, he had to be credited, so he used the name "Logan Swanson", a combination of his wife's mother's maiden name and his mother's maiden name.<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.dvdtalk.com/dvdsavant/s1571pani.html |title=Midnight Movies Double Feature: Panic in Year Zero / The Last Man on Earth |date=2005 |publisher=[[Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer]] |place=Los Angeles, California |format=DVD |chapter=Richard Matheson Storyteller: The Last Man on Earth}}</ref> Matheson said: "I was disappointed in the film, even though they more or less followed my story. I think Vincent Price, whom I love in every one of his pictures that I wrote, was miscast. I also felt the direction was kind of poor. I just didn't care for it."<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Simmons |first=William P. |date=2004 |title=Reflections of a Storyteller: A Conversation with Richard Matheson |url=https://rodserling.com/the-cemetery-dace-interviews-richard-matheson/ |url-status=live |magazine=Cemetery Dance |location=Forest Hill, Maryland |publisher=Cemetery Dance Publications |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190826010918/https://rodserling.com/the-cemetery-dace-interviews-richard-matheson/ |archive-date=August 26, 2019 |access-date=March 21, 2026 |via=rodserling.com}}</ref>
