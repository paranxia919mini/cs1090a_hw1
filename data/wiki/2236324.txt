'''''Freaky Friday''''' is a 2003 American [[fantasy comedy]] film directed by [[Mark Waters]] and written by [[Heather Hach]] and [[Leslie Dixon]]. It is based on [[Freaky Friday|the 1972 novel]] by [[Mary Rodgers]] and marks the third film adaptation of the novel, as well as the third installment overall in the [[Freaky Friday (franchise)|''Freaky Friday'' franchise]]. The film stars [[Jamie Lee Curtis]] and [[Lindsay Lohan]] as a mother and daughter who inadvertently [[Body swap|switch bodies]] through the magic of Chinese [[fortune cookie]]s. The supporting cast includes [[Harold Gould]], [[Chad Michael Murray]], and [[Mark Harmon]].

''Freaky Friday'' was released theatrically by [[Walt Disney Pictures]] on August 6, 2003. It was a commercial success, grossing over $160 million worldwide against a production budget of $26 million. The film received largely positive reviews from critics, in particular for the performances of Lohan and Curtis, the latter being nominated for the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy]]. A sequel titled ''[[Freakier Friday]]'' was released in 2025, with Curtis, Lohan, Murray, and Harmon returning.

==Plot==
 <!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summary should be between 400 to 700 words. -->
Aspiring teenage musician Anna Coleman lives with her widowed [[psychiatrist]] mother Tess, younger brother Harry, and grandfather Alan. Tess is engaged to Ryan Volvo, whom Anna has not accepted, as she has not fully reconciled with her father's death three years earlier. At school, Anna deals with strict teachers, bullying from her former best friend Stacey Hinkhouse, and her crush on Jake, whom her mother disapproves of. Pink Slip, the band Anna plays lead guitar for, is scheduled to audition for a spot in [[Wango Tango]] at the [[House of Blues]], but Anna is prohibited from attending as the audition falls on the same night as Tess's wedding rehearsal. While dining at Pei-Pei's Chinese restaurant, the mother and daughter get into a heated argument, which is interrupted when Pei-Pei's mother gives them [[fortune cookie]]s. They are on separate sides of a bathroom door and read their fortunes aloud simultaneously before feeling an intense earthquake, to which the rest of the restaurant is oblivious.

The next morning, Anna and Tess wake up in each other's bodies. Tess goes to school as Anna and begins to fully understand her daughter's woes. She realizes that Anna's English teacher, Mr. Elton Bates, is targeting her out of spite because Tess rejected his prom invitation decades earlier. Tess intervenes, threatening to report him to the school board unless he ends his mistreatment of Anna. Meanwhile, Anna has difficulty handling her mother's clients and the stress of her upcoming wedding. The two return to the restaurant where Pei-Pei tells them that only showing selfless love towards each other will reverse the switch.

Anna attends Harry's parent-teacher conference, where she reads his composition about how much he actually admires Anna and decides to be nicer to him. Tess attempts to make amends with Stacey, but gets sent to detention when Stacey frames her for cheating on a test, finally demonstrating to Tess how cruel and mean-spirited Anna's former friend has become. When Jake notices her sneaking out of detention, he offers to help her finish the test. Tess realizes that she misjudged him, but ends up putting him off Anna after she vandalizes Stacey's test as revenge for bullying Anna by erasing most of the answers and writing "I'M STUPID!" on it. Meanwhile, Ryan surprises Tess with a talk show interview to discuss her latest book. As Anna herself has not read it, she instead goes into an amusing tirade about getting older. Tess and Jake watch the interview and while she is embarrassed, he is impressed. Anna later bumps into Jake at his job, and they bond over music.

At the rehearsal dinner, Anna's bandmates Maddie and Peg try to convince her to sneak off to the audition, but they are caught by security. Ryan gives Anna permission to go in hopes of winning her acceptance, and encourages Tess to support the band. Since Tess cannot play, Anna plays the guitar backstage while Tess mimes along. Finally realizing the full scope of Anna's musical talents, Tess promises to regard her daughter's ambitions and passions with more respect. Jake again becomes enamored with Anna upon seeing her perform. Back at the rehearsal dinner, Tess tells Anna to ask Ryan to postpone the wedding until the switch is reversed. Anna instead proposes a toast, finally accepting Ryan after understanding how happy he makes her mother. This triggers an earthquake within the venue, during which Anna and Tess return to their original bodies.

Tess and Ryan later marry, and Tess finally allows Anna to start dating Jake. Pink Slip performs during the reception. When Harry and Alan start arguing, Pei-Pei's mother gives them fortune cookies, but Pei-Pei tackles the pair before they can open them.

==Production==
===Development===
The concept for a remake of ''[[Freaky Friday (1976 film)|Freaky Friday]]'' (1976) originated when producer [[Andrew Gunn (film producer)|Andrew Gunn]] met with Buena Vista Motion Pictures Group president [[Nina Jacobson]] to discuss potential projects. Gunn proposed either a new adaptation of ''Freaky Friday'' or a reboot of [[Witch Mountain (franchise)|''Witch Mountain'']]. Though Jacobson was initially hesitant due to a [[Freaky Friday (1995 film)|1995 ABC remake]] of ''Freaky Friday'', she ultimately agreed. Gunn, [[The Walt Disney Company]] executive [[Kristin Burr]], and screenwriter [[Heather Hach]] began discussing potential ideas, eventually gaining support after the success of [[The Princess Diaries (film)|''The Princess Diaries'']] (2001), which proved that female-led, family-focused films could be commercially viable. Director [[Mark Waters]] joined the project after a meeting with Gunn and Burr, despite initial doubts about the quality of the script. He responded positively to the concept and had personal fondness for the original film.<ref name="OralHistory">{{cite news |last=Piña |first=Christy |date=August 6, 2023 |title="It Was the Perfect Time in History for the Movie": An Oral History of 'Freaky Friday' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/freaky-friday-oral-history-sequel-1235538406/ |access-date=August 6, 2023 |newspaper=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]}}</ref>

The early draft of the screenplay depicted a psychiatrist mother and a daughter who wrote for her school newspaper and aspired to interview [[Gwen Stefani]] at the [[House of Blues]]. Waters revised the characters into a straight-laced mother and a rebellious, alt-rock daughter, believing their contrast would make the premise more compelling.<ref name="OralHistory" /> [[Lindsay Lohan]]'s character was originally written as [[Goth subculture|goth]], but she felt the persona was too alienating and auditioned in a [[preppy]] style. The character was ultimately rewritten as a [[Grunge fashion|grunge]]-influenced teen.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Peretz |first=Yevgenia |date=February 2006 |title=Confessions of a Teenage Movie Queen |url=https://archive.vanityfair.com/article/2006/2/confessions-of-a-teenage-movie-queen |journal=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] |publisher=[[Condé Nast]] |issue=546 |page=120 |issn=0733-8899 |url-access=limited |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200325104605/https://archive.vanityfair.com/article/2006/2/confessions-of-a-teenage-movie-queen |archive-date=March 25, 2020 |access-date=March 25, 2020}}</ref> Lohan later stated that the character’s revised punk-leaning style and attitude were influenced by pop-punk singer [[Avril Lavigne]], whom she cited as a key reference for Anna Coleman’s look and persona.<ref>{{Cite Instagram |user=mtv |postid=DNMaj6mxfJ_ |date=2025 |title=Lindsay Lohan says Avril Lavigne inspired her Freaky Friday character |access-date=19 January 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Lindsay Lohan went through Avril Lavigne phase while shooting Freaky Friday |url=https://uk.news.yahoo.com/lindsay-lohan-going-avril-lavigne-083130079.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAFtzvrPu4bMNfnRH6nCj6hLi1ACf4MkL4Gc6nNoKAhZ1gPQ-lA2MMdKaU4Ce237cnVUIkKYLRPfX6ViRiyJBP4qaPR21pg5oBNxxj9WDVMTmYGWPSqmDeUOkgxlmVX5h7OER0xiuQ8_J0iOIBu0HQplArl-oW-Tx05hbwxc-FP0B |work=Yahoo News |date=9 June 2023}}</ref>

[[Amir Derakh]] served as the film's guitar consultant, coaching Jamie Lee Curtis and rehearsing with the fictional band members.<ref name="cosmo2023">{{cite news |last1=Kaplan |first1=Ilana |date=September 11, 2023 |title=Cosmo Exclusive: Your Favorite (Fictional) Band Pink Slip Might Reunite in the 'Freaky Friday' Sequel |url=https://www.cosmopolitan.com/entertainment/movies/a45085427/freaky-friday-pink-slip-oral-history/ |access-date=September 12, 2023 |newspaper=[[Cosmopolitan (magazine)|Cosmopolitan]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Gonzales |first1=Erica |date=August 6, 2023 |title=Twenty Years Later, 'Ultimate' From Freaky Friday Still Rocks |url=https://www.elle.com/culture/music/a44737628/freaky-friday-anniversary-ultimate-song/ |access-date=September 12, 2023 |newspaper=[[Elle (magazine)|Elle]]}}</ref> While Lohan trained for a year to learn the guitar, the final film used overdubbed performances by studio musicians.<ref name="nypost2003">{{cite web |last=Heller |first=Billy |date=August 2, 2003 |title=Teen for a Time: For 'Freaky Friday', Jamie Lee Curtis Asked an Expert – Her Daughter |url=https://nypost.com/2003/08/02/teen-for-a-time-for-freaky-friday-jamie-lee-curtis-asked-an-expert-her-daughter |access-date=March 25, 2020 |newspaper=[[New York Post]]}}</ref>

===Casting===
Initially, Andrew Gunn hoped to cast [[Jodie Foster]]—who played the daughter in the 1976 version—as the mother, but she declined.<ref name="The Ledger">{{cite news |last1=Kuklenski |first1=Valerie |date=August 13, 2003 |title=Disney's the King of Remakes |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1346&dat=20030813&id=VKEsAAAAIBAJ&pg=3682,651813 |access-date=March 25, 2020 |newspaper=[[The Ledger]]}}</ref> [[Annette Bening]] and [[Michelle Trachtenberg]] were cast as the leads, but both exited due to scheduling conflicts, with Bening reportedly anxious about the script. Curtis was ultimately cast four days before production began, after Jacobson recommended her based on her performance in ''[[True Lies]]'' (1994). Curtis initially nearly declined due to being a parent. Lohan's audition was reported to be underwhelming, but her prior success in [[The Parent Trap (1998 film)|''The Parent Trap'']] (1998) and strong chemistry with Curtis secured her the role. Other actresses considered for the role of Anna included [[Mischa Barton]], [[Kristen Stewart]], [[Mae Whitman]], [[Evan Rachel Wood]], [[Brie Larson]], [[Emmy Rossum]], [[Kristen Bell]], [[Shiri Appleby]], and [[Danielle Panabaker]].<ref name="cosmo2023" /><ref>{{cite magazine | title=How Lindsay Lohan and Jamie Lee Curtis' 22-year friendship fueled 'Freakier Friday' | magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]] | url=https://ew.com/freakier-friday-jamie-lee-curtis-lindsay-lohan-inside-sequel-cover-story-11722614 }}</ref>

[[Chad Michael Murray]] was cast as Jake based on his audition with Lohan, during which he deliberately aimed to make her feel the character's intended discomfort.<ref name="OralHistory" /> [[Jared Padalecki]] also auditioned for the part.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Reinstein |first1=Mara |date=August 7, 2023 |title=Chad Michael Murray Answers Every Question We Have About Freaky Friday |url=https://www.vulture.com/2023/08/chad-michael-murray-on-freaky-friday-and-lindsay-lohan.html |access-date=August 20, 2023 |website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]}}</ref> [[Rosalind Chao]] was cast as Pei-Pei and based her performance on her father, who ran a [[China|Chinese]] restaurant in [[Orange County, California]].<ref name="OralHistory" /> She later addressed criticism over her use of a Chinese accent, explaining it was modeled on her father's real speech pattern.<ref>{{cite magazine |date=March 22, 2024 |title=Rosalind Chao Is Ready to Fight About 3 Body Problem |url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/rosalind-chao-3-body-problem-interview |access-date=March 22, 2024 |magazine=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]}}</ref>

[[Kelly Osbourne]] was originally cast as Maddie but withdrew after her mother [[Sharon Osbourne|Sharon]]'s [[cancer]] diagnosis.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Baylis |first=Sheila Cosgrove |date=August 6, 2013 |title=Kelly Osbourne on Her Drug Battle: Mom Even Put Me in a Padded Cell |url=https://people.com/celebrity/kelly-osbourne-my-family-shares-its-battles/ |access-date=March 25, 2020 |magazine=[[People (magazine)|People]]}}</ref> She was replaced by [[Christina Vidal]].<ref>{{cite magazine |date=September 12, 2002 |title=Kelly Osbourne Won't Get 'Freaky' |url=http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/74244/kelly-osbourne-wont-get-freaky |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140707231202/http://www.billboard.com/articles/news/74244/kelly-osbourne-wont-get-freaky |archive-date=July 7, 2014 |access-date=March 25, 2020 |magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]}}</ref> Other actresses who auditioned for members of the fictional band Pink Slip included [[Raven-Symoné]], [[Naya Rivera]], [[Ashley Tisdale]], Gwen Stefani, [[Sophia Bush]], and [[Kat Dennings]].<ref name="cosmo2023" /> Vidal, Lohan, and [[Haley Hudson]] rehearsed at a [[North Hollywood, Los Angeles|North Hollywood]] studio in preparation for their roles.<ref name="OralHistory" /><ref>{{cite magazine |date=August 2, 2023 |title=Freaky Friday's Pink Slip: An Oral History of the Coolest Fictional Rock Band of the 2000s |url=https://www.teenvogue.com/story/freaky-friday-pink-slip-oral-history |access-date=August 27, 2023 |magazine=[[Teen Vogue]]}}</ref>

[[Tom Selleck]] was initially cast as Ryan, but left the project after Bening's departure.<ref>{{Cite news |title='Freaky' role is a perfect fit for Curtis |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/news/ct-xpm-2003-08-07-0308070006-story.html |access-date=November 23, 2021 |newspaper=[[Chicago Tribune]]}}</ref> [[Mark Harmon]] was later cast in the role. [[Ryan Malgarini]] was selected for the role of Harry after auditioning with his grandparents. He was the only child actor given a full script before auditioning and was chosen over [[Spencer Breslin]], [[Angus T. Jones]], and [[Daryl Sabara]].<ref name="OralHistory" />

[[Marc McClure]], who played Annabel's love interest in the 1976 film, appears in a cameo role as Boris the delivery man.<ref name="The Ledger" /> Additional cameos include Lindsay Lohan's mother, [[Dina Lohan]], and director Mark Waters.

==Reception==
===Box office===
In its opening weekend, the film grossed $22.2 million in 2,954 theaters, finishing second at the box office, behind ''[[S.W.A.T. (2003 film)|S.W.A.T.]]'' ($37.1 million).<ref>{{cite web|last=Linder|first=Brian|title=Weekend Box Office: S.W.A.T. Captures Top|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2003/08/11/weekend-box-office-swat-captures-top|publisher=IGN|access-date=May 31, 2025|date=August 11, 2003}}</ref> The film went on to gross $110.2 million in North America and $50.6 million in other territories for a total of $160.8 million.<ref name="BoxOfficeMojo">{{cite web|url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=freakyfriday03.htm|title=Freaky Friday (2003)|publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]|access-date=July 27, 2011}}</ref> The film was released in the United Kingdom on December 19, 2003, and opened at number four.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.saltypopcorn.co.uk/charts/box-office.php?chart=20031219|title=UK Weekend Box Office 19th December 2003 - 21st December 2003|publisher=www.saltypopcorn.co.uk|access-date=28 January 2024}}</ref>

===Critical response===
On [[Rotten Tomatoes]], ''Freaky Friday'' holds an 88% approval rating based on 160 reviews. The site's consensus reads, "Jamie Lee Curtis and Lindsay Lohan charm in Mark Waters' nicely pitched—and Disney's second—remake of the [[Freaky Friday (1976 film)|1976 hit]]".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Freaky Friday |publisher=Rotten Tomatoes |url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/freaky_friday |access-date=April 22, 2025}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], it has a score of 70 out of 100 based on 36 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Freaky Friday Reviews |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/freaky-friday/ |access-date=April 22, 2025 |publisher=Metacritic}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A−" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite web |title=FREAKY FRIDAY (2003) A- |url=https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220122629/https://www.cinemascore.com/publicsearch/index/title/ |archive-date=December 20, 2018 |publisher=[[CinemaScore]]}}</ref>

[[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' awarded ''Freaky Friday'' 3 out of 4 stars, praising the performances and chemistry between Curtis and Lohan. He wrote that "Curtis comes close" to [[Tom Hanks]]' performance in ''[[Big (film)|Big]]'' (1988) in her portrayal of a teenager in an adult body, and called Lohan's performance had "seriousness and intent focus beneath her teenage persona."<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Freaky Friday movie review & film summary (2003) |first=Roger |last=Ebert |url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/freaky-friday-2003 |access-date=April 22, 2025 |website=[[RogerEbert.com]]}}</ref> Lisa Schwarzbaum from ''[[Entertainment Weekly]]'' described Curtis's performance as "glorious", while [[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' considered it "some of her best work ever."<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Schwarzbaum |first=Lisa |date=July 31, 2003 |title=Freaky Friday (2003) |url=http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,467417~1~0~freakyfriday,00.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070518095807/http://www.ew.com/ew/article/0,,467417~1~0~freakyfriday,00.html |archive-date=May 18, 2007 |access-date=November 30, 2014 |magazine=Entertainment Weekly}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Scott |first=A. O. |date=August 6, 2003 |title=FILM REVIEW; Walking in Mom's Shoes With Mom's Feet, Too |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2003/08/06/movies/film-review-walking-in-mom-s-shoes-with-mom-s-feet-too.html |access-date=April 22, 2025 |newspaper=The New York Times |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' praised the film, calling it "a fleet and funny comedy" and "genuinely clever switched-identities romp".<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Leydon |first=Joe |date=July 21, 2003 |title=Freaky Friday |url=https://variety.com/2003/film/reviews/freaky-friday-2-1200540408/ |access-date=April 22, 2025 |magazine=Variety}}</ref> ''[[IGN]]'' also praises Curtis' performance, saying "Jamie Lee walks off with most of the laughs and crazy scenes". They also recommend the movie for kids, teenagers and adults.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Otto |first=Jeff |date=August 5, 2003 |title=Review of Freaky Friday |url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2003/08/05/review-of-freaky-friday |access-date=April 22, 2025 |website=IGN}}</ref>

''[[Slant Magazine]]''s Nick Schanger criticized the use of "Oriental mysticism", saying it contradicts the film's message ontolerance and understanding.<ref>{{cite web |last=Schager |first=Nick |date=August 2, 2003 |title=DVD Review: Freaky Friday |url=http://www.slantmagazine.com/film/review/freaky-friday |access-date=March 25, 2020 |magazine=Slant Magazine}}</ref> Ed Park of ''[[The Village Voice]]'' criticized the Chinese restaurant scene of being "strange racist bullshit," but says the premise is "enduringly appealing as it is absurd".<ref>{{cite news |last=Park |first=Ed |author-link=Ed Park |date=August 5, 2003 |title=Unsuper Freak |url=https://www.villagevoice.com/2003/08/05/unsuper-freak/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180915185620/https://www.villagevoice.com/2003/08/05/unsuper-freak/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=September 15, 2018 |access-date=June 22, 2021 |newspaper=[[The Village Voice]]}}</ref> 

[[Common Sense Media]] gave ''Freaky Friday'' 3 out of 5 stars, calling the Mother-daughter switch a "fun comedic chaos".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Movie & TV reviews for parents |title=Freaky Friday Movie Review |publisher=Common Sense Media |url=https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/freaky-friday |access-date=April 22, 2025}}</ref> ''Plugged In'', mentions the film "isn’t Oscar material or brilliant filmmaking, but it’s funny, generally clean and pro-family".<ref>{{Cite web |title=Freaky Friday |url=https://www.pluggedin.com/movie-reviews/freakyfriday/ |access-date=April 22, 2025 |website=Plugged In}}</ref>

In 2023, ''[[IndieWire]]'' named ''Freaky Friday'' the best [[body swap]] comedy ever made.<ref>{{cite web |date=December 6, 2023 |title=The Best Body Swap Comedies |url=https://www.indiewire.com/gallery/best-body-swap-comedy-movies/nine-lives-from-left-mr-fuzzypants-voice-kevin-spacey-jennifer-garner-2016-ph-takashi/ |access-date=December 6, 2023 |work=[[IndieWire]]}}</ref>

==Home media==
''Freaky Friday'' was released on [[DVD]] and [[VHS]] by [[Walt Disney Studios Home Entertainment|Walt Disney Home Entertainment]] on December 16, 2003. By the end of that month, it had sold approximately 4.97 million units, generating over $85.7 million in revenue.<ref>{{cite web |date=December 31, 2003 |title=Year End 2003 Top-selling titles (combined VHS and DVD) |url=https://variety.com/2003/biz/news/year-end-2003-top-selling-titles-combined-vhs-and-dvd-626828/ |access-date=June 8, 2024 |work=variety.com}}</ref> The film was later released on [[Blu-ray]] on March 27, 2018, as a Disney Movie Club exclusive. It became available for streaming on [[Disney+]] following the platform's launch on November 12, 2019.

In June 2023, Curtis revealed to fans at [[Disneyland]] that filming was set to happen in the following year.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/story/2023-06-26/jamie-lee-curtis-and-owen-wilson-worked-disneylands-haunted-mansion-ahead-of-movie/|title='Haunted Mansion' stars surprise Disney visitors, and Jamie Lee Curtis drops a 'Freaky' hint|website=[[Los Angeles Times]]|date=June 26, 2023|access-date=June 27, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/movies/jamie-lee-curtis-writers-strike-stalling-freaky-friday-sequel/|title=Jamie Lee Curtis says WGA strike stalling Freaky Friday sequel until 'writers get paid correctly'|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=July 10, 2023|access-date=July 23, 2023}}</ref>

In August 2023, the cast and crew were interviewed by ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' as the film celebrated its 20th anniversary, with producer Andrew Gunn teasing the sequel: "Trying to find an idea that also serviced Jamie and Lindsay but also worked in all these other characters, was one of the hardest things to do, but I'm telling you [the writer] just came up with this super funny, heartwarming idea," continuing, "I didn't know what a sequel would be 20 years ago, like it would be Harry switching with grandpa or something, but all of a sudden, 20 years later, it's like, 'Oh, actually, [a sequel] could work.' So, I think, time gives it more room to actually become something."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/freaky-friday-oral-history-sequel-1235538406/|title='Freaky Friday' Oral History: Cast and Crew Look Back, Ahead to Sequel|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=August 6, 2023|access-date=August 7, 2023}}</ref> Gunn later revealed they got a draft of a script for the sequel right before the [[2023 Writers Guild of America strike|writers' strike]], which again incorporates music and the fictional band Pink Slip "in a great way."<ref name="cosmo2023"/> On November 10, Curtis shared a reunion photo with Lohan on [[Instagram]] to celebrate the end of the [[2023 Hollywood labor disputes|SAG-AFTRA strike]] and tease the project's development.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.etonline.com/jamie-lee-curtis-reunites-with-lindsay-lohan-maybe-we-can-switch-places-in-the-future-214704|title=Jamie Lee Curtis Reunites With Lindsay Lohan: 'Maybe We Can Switch Places in the Future'|magazine=[[Entertainment Tonight]]|date=November 10, 2023|access-date=November 11, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/jamie-lee-curtis-lindsay-lohan-reunite-freaky-friday-sag-aftra-1234875727/|title=Jamie Lee Curtis and Lindsay Lohan Reunite, Tease 'Freaky Friday 2'|magazine=[[Rolling Stone (magazine)|Rolling Stone]]|date=November 11, 2023|access-date=November 11, 2023}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://z100.iheart.com/content/2023-11-11-surprise-freaky-friday-reunion-just-broke-the-internet/|title=Surprise 'Freaky Friday' Reunion Just Broke The Internet|publisher=[[WHTZ|Z100]]|date=November 11, 2023|access-date=November 11, 2023}}</ref>

In March 2024, Lohan confirmed the sequel was in the works.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://people.com/lindsay-lohan-excited-work-jamie-lee-curtis-freaky-friday-sequel-8604157|title=Lindsay Lohan Is 'Excited to Work with' Jamie Lee Curtis on Freaky Friday Sequel: 'We're Going to Have a Lot of Fun' (Exclusive)|magazine=[[People (American magazine)|People]]|date=March 4, 2024|access-date=March 8, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.accessonline.com/videos/lindsay-lohan-reveals-shes-working-on-script-for-freaky-friday-sequel-with-jamie-lee-curtis|title=Lindsay Lohan Reveals She's Working On Script For 'Freaky Friday' Sequel With Jamie Lee Curtis|website=[[Access Hollywood]]|date=March 5, 2024|access-date=March 8, 2024}}</ref> Later that month, ''The Hollywood Reporter'' announced [[Nisha Ganatra]] would be directing from a screenplay written by [[Jordan Weiss]], with filming set to begin for mid-2024 in Los 
Angeles and is scheduled to be released in 2025. Gunn was confirmed to produce again, along with [[Kristin Burr]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/freaky-friday-2-lindsay-lohan-jamie-lee-curtis-2-1235863059/|title='Freaky Friday 2' Is A Go With 'Welcome to Chippendales' Director (Exclusive)|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=March 29, 2024|access-date=March 29, 2024}}</ref> A few days later, a [[casting call]] revealed potential story details including that Tess and Anna would be body swapping with two teenage girls, the latter's daughter and her soon-to-be stepdaughter.<ref>{{Cite magazine|url=https://ew.com/freaky-friday-2-plot-details-lindsay-lohan-anna-daughter-8622872|title=Potential Freaky Friday 2 plot details reveal Lindsay Lohan, Jamie Lee Curtis body-swap with 2 teenage girls|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=April 1, 2024|access-date=April 3, 2024}}</ref> 

In June 2024, it was reported [[Julia Butters]] had joined the cast.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://variety.com/2024/film/news/julia-butters-freaky-friday-2-disney-1236029360/|title=Julia Butters Joins 'Freaky Friday 2' at Disney|newspaper=[[Variety (website)|Variety]]|date=June 7, 2024|access-date=June 8, 2024}}</ref> During that month, it was announced that filming had begun and that Harmon, Murray, Vidal Mitchell, Hudson, Soong, Tobolowsky, and Chao would all be reprising their roles with [[Sophia Hammons]] and [[Maitreyi Ramakrishnan]] joining the cast. The sequel, titled ''[[Freakier Friday]]'', is scheduled to be released on August 8, 2025.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2024/06/freaky-friday-2-adds-chad-michael-murray-stephen-tobolowsky-more-1235981629/|title=Sophia Hammons & Maitreyi Ramakrishnan Among Other New Additions To 'Freaky Friday 2' As Chad Michael Murray, Stephen Tobolowsky & More Are Set To Reprise; Theatrical Release Confirmed|publisher=Deadline Hollywood|date=June 24, 2024|access-date=June 24, 2024}}</ref>
