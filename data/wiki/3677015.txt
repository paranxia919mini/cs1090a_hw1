'''''Where the Wild Things Are''''' is a 2009 [[fantasy film|fantasy]] [[adventure film|adventure]] [[drama film]] directed by [[Spike Jonze]], who co-wrote the screenplay with [[Dave Eggers]]. Based on [[Maurice Sendak]]'s [[Where the Wild Things Are|1963 children's book]], it combines [[live-action]], [[Suitmation|performers in costumes]], [[animatronics]], and [[computer-generated imagery]] (CGI). The film stars [[Max Records]], [[Catherine Keener]], and [[Mark Ruffalo]], and the voices of [[Lauren Ambrose]], [[Chris Cooper]], [[James Gandolfini]], [[Catherine O'Hara]], and [[Forest Whitaker]]. The film centres on a young boy named Max who sails away to an island inhabited by creatures known as the "Wild Things", who declare him king.

In the early 1980s, [[The Walt Disney Company]] considered adapting the book with a blend of traditionally animated characters and [[computer generation|computer-generated]] environments, but development did not go past a test film to see how the animation hybridisation would result.<ref name="pixar-touch" /> In 1999,<ref name=":0" /> [[Universal Pictures|Universal Studios]] acquired rights to the book's adaptation and initially attempted to develop a computer-animated adaptation with Disney animator [[Eric Goldberg (animator)|Eric Goldberg]], but the CGI concept was replaced with a live-action one in 2003, Goldberg was dropped for Jonze, and work on the film was transferred to [[Warner Bros. Pictures]]. The film was produced by [[Jeph Loeb]], [[Gary Goetzman]], Sendak, John B. Carls, and [[Vincent Landay]], and made with an estimated budget of $100 million.<ref>{{Cite news| url=http://www.cnn.com/2009/SHOWBIZ/Movies/10/14/wild.things/index.html | work=CNN | title=Spike Jonze goes 'Where the Wild Things Are' - CNN.com | date=2009-10-14}}</ref> ''Where the Wild Things Are'' was a joint production between Australia, Germany, and the United States, and was filmed principally in [[Melbourne]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.filmedinmelbourne.com.au/wtwta/|title=Film Victoria - Where The Wild Things Are|work=Filmed in Melbourne|access-date= 23 March 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140323075820/http://www.filmedinmelbourne.com.au/wtwta/|archive-date=23 March 2014|url-status=dead}}</ref>

''Where the Wild Things Are'' was released on 16 October 2009, in the United States, on 3 December in Australia, and on 17 December in Germany. Despite concerns from within Warner Bros. and news outlets leading up to release over whether or not Jonze's approach to the film was [[suitable for children]], the film was met with positive reviews and appeared on many year-end top ten lists. However, the film was a financial failure, grossing just $100 million against an equal production budget. The film was released on DVD and Blu-ray on 2 March 2010.

==Plot==
Max is an emotional eight-year-old boy<ref>{{Cite web |title=WBshop.com - The Official Online Store of Warner Bros. Studios |url=http://warnerbros2009.warnerbros.com/assets/screenplays/wtwta_screenplay.pdf |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091229035446/http://warnerbros2009.warnerbros.com/assets/screenplays/wtwta_screenplay.pdf |archive-date=29 December 2009 |website=WarnerBros.com }}</ref> with an active imagination and divorced parents. After his older sister Claire refuses to intervene when her friends crush Max's [[snow fort]] with him inside during a snowball fight, Max furiously trashes her bedroom and destroys a frame that he made for her. Later, his mother Connie invites her boyfriend Adrian to dinner. Furious that she is not spending time with him, Max puts on a wolf costume and lashes out by biting her shoulder, before running away from home. At the edge of a river, Max finds a small boat that he boards.

Eventually, Max reaches an island inhabited by seven large monsters called Wild Things: Carol, Ira, Judith, Alexander, Douglas, KW, and the Bull. Carol is destroying the huts due to KW's departure, and Max attempts to join in, only to find himself facing the suspicious anger of the Wild Things. When they attempt to eat him, Max convinces them that he is a king with magical powers capable of bringing harmony to the group. Impressed, the Wild Things crown him as their new king, and after KW returns, Max declares a "wild rumpus" in which he and the Wild Things wreak havoc in the forest, before going to sleep in a pile.

The next day, Carol takes Max on a tour of the island and shows him a model that he built depicting what he wishes the island looked like. Inspired by this, Max orders the construction of an enormous fort. When KW brings her two [[owl]] friends Bob and Terry to the fort, a disagreement ensues as Carol believes that they are outsiders. To release their frustrations, Max divides the Wild Things into two teams for a dirt-clod fight. However, Alexander is accidentally injured during the game, and Carol scolds KW for jokingly stepping on his head, causing her to leave again.

Max finds Alexander alone in the fort, whereupon he reveals that although he knows that Max is not a powerful king, he warns him to keep it a secret from Carol. However, at night, Douglas reveals the truth when Carol has a meltdown over the state of the fort and Max's defeat to fulfill his duties as a king. Angered, Carol tears Douglas's arm off and chases Max into the forest, but KW saves him. After Carol leaves, KW explains to Max how difficult their lives are, with Carol's temper exacerbating them. Max realizes what his mother is going through and decides to leave the island.

Max returns to the destroyed remains of Carol's model island and makes a token of affection for him. He then apologizes to Carol and tells him of his intent of going home, but Carol responds with sadness. The other Wild Things escort Max to his boat. Upon finding Max's token, Carol arrives just in time to see Max sailing away and howls, and Max howls in return, along with the other Wild Things. Upon returning home, Max reconciles with his mother, who gives him dinner.

==Production==
===Development===
''Where the Wild Things Are'' started its development in the early 1980s, originally to be an animated feature by [[Walt Disney Productions]] that would have blended traditionally animated characters with computer-generated settings. Animators [[Glen Keane]] and [[John Lasseter]] (who later moved on to [[Pixar Animation Studios]]) had completed a test film to see how the animation hybridizing would work out, but the project proceeded no further.<ref name="pixar-touch" >{{Cite book  | url=https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780307265753 | title=The Pixar Touch | first=David A. | last=Price | isbn=978-0-307-26575-3 | publisher=Random House | year=2008 |page=[https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780307265753/page/53/mode/1up 53] |url-access=registration}}</ref>

Sendak and producer John B. Carls later formed Wild Things Productions in 1992, with the intent to produce adaptations of Sendak's properties. [[Universal Pictures]] and [[Playtone]] acquired rights to the book's adaptation in 1999, initially attempting to develop a live-action/CGI adaptation with [[Gore Verbinski]] attached to direct and [[Eric Warren Singer]] attached to write the screenplay.<ref name=":0">{{cite web|url= https://variety.com/1999/film/news/u-and-hanks-going-wild-1117491368/|title= U and Hanks going 'Wild'|access-date= February 4, 2022|date= February 16, 1999|work= Variety}}</ref> In 2001, Universal replaced the live-action/CGI concept with a fully computer-animated adaptation, with Disney animator [[Eric Goldberg (film director)|Eric Goldberg]] attached to direct.<ref>{{cite web |last=Fleming |first=Michael |date= August 6, 2001|title= U sings 'Wild Things'|url=https://variety.com/2001/film/news/u-sings-wild-things-1117850724/|access-date=February 5, 2022|work=Variety}}</ref> However, in 2003, they reverted to live-action, and Goldberg was replaced with Spike Jonze,<ref>{{cite news |last=Fleming |first=Michael |url=https://variety.com/2003/film/markets-festivals/spike-wild-man-for-u-1117894688/ |title=Spike 'Wild' man for U |work=Variety |date=October 27, 2003 |access-date=November 16, 2023}}</ref> who had previously collaborated with Sendak on an [[Harold and the Purple Crayon (film)|unproduced adaptation]] of ''[[Harold and the Purple Crayon]]'', which eventually was released in 2024.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/507839/shrek-the-third-screenwriter-adapting-harold-and-the-purple-crayon/|title=Shrek the Third Screenwriter Adapting Harold and the Purple Crayon?|date=2010-02-25|website=/Film|access-date=May 10, 2022}}</ref>

After years of interest from various producers, Sendak favoured Spike Jonze as director, noting he was "young, interesting and had a spark that none of the others had".<ref name="USATodayOct2009">{{cite news |last1=Bowles |first1=Scott |last2=Minzesheimer |first2=Bob |date=2009-10-15 |title=Spike Jonze bravely sails into 'Where the Wild Things Are' |newspaper=[[USA Today]] |url=https://www.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2009-10-15-wild-things-spike-jonze_N.htm |url-status=dead |access-date=2009-10-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091020093524/https://www.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2009-10-15-wild-things-spike-jonze_N.htm |archive-date=2009-10-20}}</ref> The film was originally set for release from [[Universal Pictures|Universal]]; however, disagreements between Universal and Sendak over Jonze's approach to the story led to a [[Turnaround (filmmaking)|turnaround]] arrangement where the film's production was transferred to [[Warner Bros. Pictures]].<ref name="Variety2006">{{Cite web |last=Snyder |first=Gabriel |date=2007-06-30 |title=Wild' ride for Warner |url=https://variety.com/2006/digital/features/wild-ride-for-warner-1117935686/ |access-date=2023-08-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150407115751/https://variety.com/2006/digital/features/wild-ride-for-warner-1117935686/ |archive-date=2015-04-07}}</ref>

In 2005, Jonze and [[Dave Eggers]] completed a 111-page screenplay, expanding the original thirteen-sentence story. During the process of writing the script, Jonze and Eggers would break in the afternoon so Spike could take an afternoon nap (just one example of the nontraditional approach they took to the writing process).<ref>https://www.gq.com/story/spike-jonze-dave-eggers-where-the-wild-things-are</ref> On 8 July 2006, production began open auditions for the role of Max.<ref>{{cite news | url=http://blogtown.portlandmercury.com/2006/06/re_roaring_terrible_roars_gnas.php | newspaper=[[The Portland Mercury]] | access-date=2009-10-17 | date=2006-06-30 | title=Re: Roaring Terrible Roars, Gnashing Terrible Teeth, Rolling Terrible Eyes, Showing Terrible Claws | first=Erik | last=Henriksen | url-status=dead | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080515145503/http://blogtown.portlandmercury.com/2006/06/re_roaring_terrible_roars_gnas.php | archive-date=2008-05-15 }}</ref> The process took months, but, eventually, Max Records was cast. [[Academy Award]]-winning make-up effects supervisor [[Howard Berger]] (''[[The Chronicles of Narnia (film series)|The Chronicles of Narnia]]'') turned down offers to work on the film four times. Although the book inspired him as a child to work in special effects, he felt filming it was a "horrible idea."<ref>{{Cite news|author=Mike Szymanski|title=Berger On Why He Said No To Wild Things|work=[[SCI FI Wire]]|date=2008-11-25|url=http://www.scifi.com/scifiwire/index.php?category=0&id=62433|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090412190804/http://www.scifi.com/scifiwire/index.php?category=0&id=62433|url-status=dead|archive-date=2009-04-12|access-date=2008-11-26}}</ref> Jonze then approached [[Steve Johnson (special effects artist)|Steve Johnson]] who had done initial designs and test suits, but ultimately [[Jim Henson's Creature Shop]] provided the animatronic suits for the Wild Things.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2009/10/where-the-wild-things-are-built-jim-hensons-creature-workshop/amp | title=Where the Wild Things Are Built: Jim Henson's Creature Shop | first=Julian | last=Sancton | magazine=Variety | date=17 October 2009 | accessdate=20 June 2021 }}</ref>

Jonze kept in close consultation with Sendak throughout the process, and the author approved creature designs created by Jim Henson's Creature Shop. To make the set a more comfortable environment for Max Records, Jonze encouraged the crew members to bring their children to the set. Some of them can be seen in the film's classroom scene.<ref name="where the wild things are documentary">{{cite video|title=Where the Wild Things Are documentary "The Kids Take Over the Picture"|medium=DVD documentary|date=2010}}</ref>

[[Michelle Williams (actress)|Michelle Williams]] was originally cast as the female Wild Thing KW only to leave the project after her voice "didn't match the original vision of how the Wild Thing should sound".<ref>{{cite magazine | last = Jankiewicz | first = Pat | date = 2009-10-16 | url = http://www.fangoria.com/fangoria-entertainment/the-monster-times/120-reviews/4317-where-the-wild-things-are.html | title = Where the Wild Things Are (Monster Times review) | magazine = Fangoria | access-date = 2009-10-16 | url-status = dead | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20091025051446/http://fangoria.com/fangoria-entertainment/the-monster-times/120-reviews/4317-where-the-wild-things-are.html | archive-date = 2009-10-25 }}</ref> She was replaced by [[Lauren Ambrose]], and filming continued.

===Filming and special effects===
[[Principal photography]] began in July 2006 at [[Docklands Studios Melbourne]] in [[Melbourne]], [[Australia]] and [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] in December 2006.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.mccs.net.au/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=18&Itemid=32|publisher=Melbourne Central City Studios|title=Credits|access-date=2009-10-16}}</ref><ref name="nytimes">{{Cite news|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2009/09/06/magazine/06jonze-t.html|title=Bringing 'Where the Wild Things Are' to the Screen|last=Knafo|first=Saki|date=September 2, 2009|newspaper=The New York Times|issn=0362-4331|access-date=August 30, 2021}}</ref>

In 2008, test footage was leaked onto the internet, garnering mixed reactions. Jonze called the footage "a very early test... to see if our SFX plan for the faces would work," but early fan outcry over the video, along with rumored "scared children" in test audiences, led Warner Bros. to delay the film's release for a year from its original 3 October 2008 release date. On 20 February 2008, speculation emerged that Warner Bros. was considering reshooting the entire film.<ref>{{Cite web | url = https://www.slashfilm.com/2008/02/20/spike-jonzes-where-the-wild-things-are-to-be-entirely-reshot/ | publisher =[[/Film]] | last = Stephenson | first = Hunter | date = 2008-02-20 | title = Spike Jonze's Where the Wild Things Are to Be Entirely Reshot?! | access-date = 2009-10-16 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20101009021219/http://www.slashfilm.com/2008/02/20/spike-jonzes-where-the-wild-things-are-to-be-entirely-reshot/ | archive-date = 9 October 2010 | url-status = dead }}</ref>

Then-WB president [[Alan F. Horn]] responded, "We've given him more money and, even more importantly, more time for him to work on the film. We'd like to find a common ground that represents Spike's vision but still offers a film that really delivers for a broad-based audience. No one wants to turn this into a bland, sanitized studio movie. This is a very special piece of material, and we're just trying to get it right." Producer Gary Goetzman followed, "We support Spike's vision. We're helping him make the vision he wants to make."<ref name="joblo">{{Cite web | url=https://www.joblo.com/index.php?id=spike-gets-final-cut | publisher=JoBlo.com | last=Barna | first=Daniel|date=2008-07-24 | title=Spike gets final cut | access-date=2009-10-16}}</ref>

At the end of 2008, Jonze teamed up with [[Framestore]] in London to complete his film and work with them to bring to life the performances through their animation and special effects team. Over the course of the next six months, Jonze spent time with the animators on the floor of the studio as they worked together to realize his intention for the performances that had started many years before with the voices, continued with the suit performances in Australia, and were completed in London's Soho. He also reshot several scenes and added three new ones, bringing the film's cost up from the original greenlit $75 million to $100 million.<ref>{{Cite web |date=12 October 2009 |first1=Anne |last1=Thompson |website=IndieWire.com |title=Where the Wild Things Are: Too Costly to Succeed? |url=https://www.indiewire.com/2009/10/where-the-wild-things-are-too-costly-to-succeed-239435/ }}</ref>

===Box office===
{{Anchor|Marketing}}

The film was released in North America in both conventional and [[IMAX]] cinemas on 16 October 2009.<ref>{{cite magazine|first=Nicole|last=Sperling|title='Where the Wild Things Are' gets long-awaited release date|url=http://hollywoodinsider.ew.com/2008/09/wild-things.html|magazine=[[Entertainment Weekly]]|date=2008-09-11|access-date=2008-09-12|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080912012059/http://hollywoodinsider.ew.com/2008/09/wild-things.html|archive-date=2008-09-12}}</ref> Early Friday box office estimates show the film earned about $32.7 million on its opening weekend in theaters.<ref>{{Cite news|first=Grady|last=Smith|title='Friday Estimates: Where The Wild Things Are On Top; Law Abiding Citizen Actually Does Well!|url=http://blog.theboxofficejunkie.com/2009/10/friday-estimates-where-wild-things-are.html|publisher=The Box Office Junkie|date=2008-09-11|access-date=2009-10-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091020022343/http://blog.theboxofficejunkie.com/2009/10/friday-estimates-where-wild-things-are.html|archive-date=20 October 2009|url-status=dead}}</ref> It grossed $77.2 million during its theatrical run in the U.S. and Canada, plus $22.8 million internationally. Overall, the studio took a loss as the final budget of the movie was estimated to be around $100 million.<ref>{{cite web|first=Grady|last=Smith|title='Friday Estimates: Where The Wild Things Are On Top; Law Abiding Citizen Actually Does Well!|url= https://www.boxofficemojo.com/article/ed3682206724/?ref_=bo_rl_ra |website=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref>

}}</ref> in [[Republic of Ireland|Ireland]] and the [[United Kingdom|UK]] on 11 December 2009;<ref name="IFCO" /> and in Germany on 17 December 2009.<ref>[http://www.movieworlds.com/filme/Wo_die_Wilden_Kerle_wohnen.php Wo die wilden Kerle wohnen – movieworlds.com].</ref>

===Critical response===
{{Anchor|Critics}}
On review website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film holds an approval rating of 73% based on 270 reviews, with an average rating of 7/10. ===Suitability for children===
[[Warner Bros.]] initially feared that the film was not family-friendly and would frighten children, but these fears were not shared by Jonze or Sendak.<ref>{{Cite news| url=https://www.theguardian.com/books/2009/oct/20/maurice-sendak-wild-things-hell | work=The Guardian | location=London | title=Maurice Sendak tells parents worried by Wild Things to 'go to hell' | first=Alison | last=Flood | date=2009-10-20}}</ref> Jonze refused to compromise,<ref>{{Cite news| url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2009/dec/05/spike-jonze-wild-things-are | work=The Guardian | location=London | title=Spike Jonze: 'I'm never going to compromise' | first=Steve | last=Rose | date=2009-12-05}}</ref> and [[Maurice Sendak]] said after having seen a completed cut of the film, "I've never seen a movie that looked or felt like this. And it's [Spike Jonze's] personal 'this.' And he's not afraid of himself. He's a real artist who lets it come through in the work. So he's touched me. He's touched me very much."<ref>{{Cite news | date = 2009-09-22 | last = Lee | first = Chris | title = When Spike met Maurice: Bringing 'Where the Wild Things Are' to the screen | url = http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/herocomplex/2009/09/when-spike-met-maurice-bringing-where-the-wild-things-are-to-the-screen.html | work = [[Los Angeles Times]] | access-date = 2009-10-16}}</ref> After seeing the finished product, a Warner Bros. executive stated of Jonze, "He's a perfectionist and just kept working on it, but now we know that at the end of the day he nailed it."<ref name="DH" />

===Video game===

A [[video game]] based on the film was released on 13 October 2009, for the [[PlayStation 3]], [[Xbox 360]], [[Wii]], and [[Nintendo DS]]. The former three were developed by [[Griptonite Games]], and the latter by [[WayForward]]. All were published by [[Warner Bros. Games]].

===Skateboards and limited edition shoes===

===Toys===
A series of collectible vinyl dolls of the Wild Things and Max was released by the Japanese company MediCom Toys. Other releases include an eight-inch articulated figure of Max in wolf costume and smaller-scale sets of the characters released under their [[Kubrick (toy)|Kubrick figure banner]].
