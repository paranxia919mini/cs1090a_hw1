'''''The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert''''' is a 1994 Australian [[road movie|road]] [[comedy film]] written and directed by [[Stephan Elliott]]. The plot follows two [[drag queen]]s (played by [[Hugo Weaving]] and [[Guy Pearce]]) and a [[transgender woman]] ([[Terence Stamp]]), as they journey across the [[Australian Outback]] on a tour bus named ''Priscilla'', along the way encountering various groups and individuals. The film was based upon the lives of three actual drag queens—Cindy Pastel, Strykermeyer, and Lady Bump—who were to play themselves but were later replaced with what the studio considered "[[Bankable star|bankable]]" actors.

''Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' premiered at the [[1994 Cannes Film Festival]], in the [[Un Certain Regard]] section. Upon its wide release, it became a surprise worldwide hit and its positive portrayal of [[LGBT]] individuals helped to introduce queer themes to a mainstream audience.<ref name=oregan/> It received predominantly positive reviews and won [[Academy Award for Best Costume Design|Best Costume Design]] at the [[67th Academy Awards]]. The film is one of only three films set in contemporary times to win the award for Best Costume Design. It became a [[cult film|cult classic]] both in Australia and abroad. The film provided the basis for a musical, ''[[Priscilla, Queen of the Desert (musical)|Priscilla, Queen of the Desert]]'', which opened in 2006 in Sydney. A sequel, ''[[Priscilla Queen of the Desert 2]]'', is, as of 2025, in the process of preparing to be filmed.

==Plot==
Anthony "Tick" Belrose, using the [[pseudonym]] of Mitzi Del Bra, is a [[Sydney]]-based [[drag queen]] who accepts an offer to perform his act at [[Lasseters Hotel Casino|Lasseters Hotel Casino Resort]] managed by his estranged wife Marion in [[Alice Springs]], in remote central Australia. After persuading his friends and fellow performers, Bernadette Bassenger, a recently bereaved [[Trans woman|transgender woman]], and Adam Whitely, a flamboyant and obnoxious younger drag queen who goes under the [[Drag (entertainment)|drag]] name Felicia Jollygoodfellow, to join him, the three set out for a four-week run at the casino in a large tour bus, which Adam christens "Priscilla, Queen of the Desert."

The trio proceed through remote lands bordering the [[Simpson Desert]] meeting a variety of characters. Early on, Tick reveals that Marion is actually his wife, as they never divorced, and that they are going there as a favor to her. They stop in [[Broken Hill]], where they are subjected to [[Homophobia|homophobic]]/[[Transphobia|transphobic]] abuse and [[Violence against LGBT people|violence]], including having their bus vandalized with homophobic and transphobic graffiti. 

After they leave the main highway to save time, their bus breaks down in the middle of the desert. Adam spends the whole day repainting the bus in lavender to cover the graffiti. After a homophobic couple turn up but leave them stranded, a group of friendly [[Aboriginal Australians]] with whom they perform at a [[corroboree]], help them get Priscilla to Bob, a middle-aged mechanic, in a small outback town. Bob is delighted to see drag performers in his small town and ultimately joins them for the remainder of the journey after his [[Filipinos|Filipina]] wife Cynthia leaves him.

Continuing their journey, they stop for repairs at the remote [[opal]]-mining town of [[Coober Pedy]], where Adam is attacked by a homophobic gang. Bob and Bernadette save him. Bernadette comforts the shaken Adam, allowing them to reach an understanding. As they near Alice Springs, the others come to terms with the secret of Tick's marriage and resolve their differences.

Upon arrival at the hotel, it is revealed that Tick and Marion also have an eight-year-old son, Benji, whom Tick has not seen for many years. Tick is nervous about exposing his son to his drag profession and anxious about revealing his [[homosexuality]]. He is surprised to discover that Benji already knows and is fully supportive. While Benji is playing with Adam, he reveals that Marion has previously been in a [[lesbian]] relationship.

Tick and Adam and the group go on a trip into the outback, where Marion insists that Benji go back to Sydney with them so that Tick can get to know him better. Tick and Benji are talking by the river, and Tick hesitantly talks to Benji about what he does for a living, to which Benji replies "Mum says you're the best in the business." Before returning to the hotel to finish out their run of shows, the three travellers fulfill a long-held dream of Adam's, to climb [[Kings Canyon (Northern Territory)|Kings Canyon (Watarrka)]] in full drag regalia.

When the others are loading the bus to return to Sydney, Bernadette announces she has decided to remain at the resort with Bob. Marion has arranged for her to manage the hotel’s entertainment at the hotel during Marion’s absence.

The film ends with Tick and Adam performing in the same club featured in the opening credits, with the crowd (including Benji working spotlight) cheering on encouragingly.

In a post-credits scene, the dummy attached to a kite that was released earlier in the film is revealed to have travelled to [[Tibet]] and landed in a [[Buddhist temple|temple]] where a monk recovers it.

==Production==
===Development===
The film was based on the lives of three [[Drag queen|drag queens]] who performed as Cindy Pastel (Ritchie Finger), Strykermeyer (Mark Fitzhugh), and Lady Bump (Stuart Garske).<ref name="The drag queens behind 'Priscilla'"/> [[Carlotta (performer)|Carlotta]] has also claimed to have inspired the film, in particular the character of Bernadette. Carlotta performed with [[Les Girls]] throughout her career, who are mentioned by name in ''Priscilla'' several times. In her autobiography she also recalls touring outback Australia in a bus with Les Girls and "props hanging out the back... way before ''Priscilla'' was ever thought of as a movie."<ref name="prue">{{cite book |last1=MacSween |first1=Prue |title=Carlotta: I'm not that kind of girl (as told to Prue MacSween) |date=2003 |publisher=Pan Macmillan |location=Sydney, NSW |isbn=073291194X |pages=166-167}}</ref>  

The original concept for ''Priscilla'' was to have Cindy Pastel, Strykermeyer and Lady Bump playing themselves, but this was scrapped once the film attracted a large budget, as it was decided "bankable" actors were needed. This ultimately led to the casting of three [[Heterosexuality|straight]] men with no background in [[Drag (entertainment)|drag]], and as ''The Age'' wrote, "the people upon whose lives the mega-hit was based were overlooked". The three original queens were profiled in the 1995 documentary, ''[[Ladies Please]]''.<ref name="The drag queens behind 'Priscilla'">{{cite news |last1=Tuohy |first1=Wendy |title=The drag queens behind 'Priscilla' |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-age-ladies-please/154942101/ |access-date=9 September 2024 |work=The Age |date=25 May 1995}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Tuohy |first1=Wendy |title=The drag queens behind 'Priscilla' (article continued) |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-age-ladies-please-continued/154943467/ |access-date=9 September 2024 |work=The Age |date=25 May 1995}}</ref>

''Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' had been conceived (mainly out of frustation) by filmmaker [[Stephan Elliott]] while he waited for a response from [[Phil Collins]] about his participation in the film ''[[Frauds (film)|Frauds]]''. Elliott, in an effort to distract himself, wrote a script in just 10 days, but one which felt at the time to be "tricky" to pull off. Rebel Penfold-Russell, Andrena Finlay and Stuart Quin (the co-founders of the production company Latent Image) pitched ''Priscilla'' to various financiers at the [[1991 Cannes Film Festival]], without success; however, before Cannes, they had taken the project to Sarah Radclyffe at [[Working Title Films|Working Title]],<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 05–06.</ref> who took the film's concept to PolyGram with Graeme Mason, who suggested Michael Hamlyn as the British co-producer and, with the backing of the Australian [[Film Finance Corporation]], were able to begin production of the film on a relatively modest budget of 2.7 million Australian dollars.{{cn|date=March 2025}}

Elliott and the film's eventual producers, Michael Hamlyn and Al Clark, agreed to work for $50,000 each, a relatively low fee for filmmakers at the time, while the lack of funding meant that the crew agreed to receive takings of the film's eventual profits in compensation for their low salaries.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 06–07 and 10.</ref> Due to the involvement of the Australian FFC, only one non-Australian actor was allowed to appear in the film, and Clark initially considered [[David Bowie]], whom he had known back in the 1980s, and later briefly thought of [[John Hurt]], although neither was available.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 14–16.</ref>

===Casting===
The idea of casting the actual drag queens upon whom the story was based was abandoned when the producers decided to seek out "bankable" straight actors without a background in drag instead.<ref name="The drag queens behind 'Priscilla'"/> In May 1993, after travelling around the [[Australian Outback]] searching for appropriate sites to film in, ''Priscilla''{{'}}s creators attended the Cannes Film Festival and Marche to advertise their project, hoping to capitalise on the selection of Elliot's first film ''Frauds'', which was "In Competition" at the festival and despite the fact that they had not yet confirmed any actors for the roles. Their primary choice for the role of Bernadette was [[Tony Curtis]], who read and approved of the script, but eventually became unavailable. They then approached [[John Cleese]], who was not interested.

For the part of Tick, they had initially wanted [[Rupert Everett]] and for Adam they wanted [[Jason Donovan]].<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 38–41.</ref> However, at a pre-production casting meeting held at Cannes, Everett and Donovan did not get on well with one another and were found to be openly hostile toward the production staff. In light of this, it was readily agreed that they would not be suitable for the parts<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]] pp. 52–55.</ref> and the search for their three leading men would resume. However, Donovan would go on to play Tick in the West End [[Priscilla, Queen of the Desert (musical)|musical adaptation of the film]].

After unsuccessfully lobbying [[Colin Firth]] to play the role of Tick, producers eventually awarded the part to [[Hugo Weaving]]. Initially considering [[Tim Curry]] for the part of Bernadette, they cast [[Terence Stamp]], who was initially anxious about the role because it was unlike anything that he had performed previously, although he eventually came on board with the concept.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 58–64.</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=Filmink|first=Stephen|last=Vagg|date=16 August 2025|access-date=16 August 2025|url=https://www.filmink.com.au/ten-connections-between-terence-stamp-and-australia/|title=Ten Connections between Terence Stamp and Australia}}</ref> Stamp himself suggested [[Bill Hunter (actor)|Bill Hunter]] for the role of Bob, who accepted the role without even reading the script or being told anything about the greater concept of the film other than the basic character description, while Australian actor [[Guy Pearce]] (who had previously appeared with Donovan in the Australian soap opera ''[[Neighbours]]'' in the late 1980s) was hired at the eleventh hour to portray the sassy but spirited Adam.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 64–65.</ref>

===Filming===
{{Quote box|width=246px|align=right|quote=It is striking what an effect the disguise of drag is having on [the actors'] personalities. It makes Guy [Pearce] flirtatious, combative and loud. It makes Terence [Stamp] withdrawn and watchful ("Hello sailor", he greets me warily with his back to the wall, looking like a fallen woman in a '50s melodrama.) It makes Hugo extraordinarily trashy.|source=Al Clark<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 73–74.</ref>}}
The Imperial Hotel in [[Erskineville]], [[Sydney]] was the filming location for the opening and closing scenes. The Imperial Hotel has hosted [[Drag show|drag shows]] since 1983, and continues to be an icon for [[LGBTQ culture in Sydney|Sydney's LGBT community]], with its restaurant renamed "Priscillas" in honour of the film.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-04-10|title=A Brief History of The Imperial Erskineville|url=https://imperialerskineville.com.au/blog/community/a-brief-history/|access-date=2021-10-30|website=Imperial|archive-date=30 October 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211030144447/https://imperialerskineville.com.au/blog/community/a-brief-history/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Many scenes, including one where Bernadette encounters a butch, bigoted woman named Shirley, were filmed at the [[outback]] town of [[Broken Hill]] in [[New South Wales]], largely in a hotel named Mario's Palace (now simply the [[Palace Hotel, Broken Hill|Palace Hotel]]), which Al Clark believed was "drag queen heaven".<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 24–25.</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/cult-film-article|title=Shrines of Obsession: The Real-World Locations of 11 Cult Films|last=Wadsworth|first=Kimberly|date=30 May 2014|website=Atlas Obscura|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190212224722/https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/cult-film-article|archive-date=12 February 2019|url-status=live|access-date=13 February 2019}}</ref> Some small scenes were filmed in the All Nations Hotel.

They also decided to film at [[Coober Pedy]], a remote rough-and-tumble [[opal]] mining town in south-central Australia which featured prominently in the film. The executive producer, Rebel Penfold-Russell, appears as the marathon runner.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. p. 31.</ref>

Initially, they tried to get permission to film upon [[Uluru|Uluṟu]], but this was rejected by organisations responsible for the monument, such as the Uluṟu Board of Management, as it would have been in violation of Indigenous Australian religious beliefs.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 13–14.</ref> Instead, the scene was filmed in [[Kings Canyon (Northern Territory)|Kings Canyon (Watarrka)]].<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 69–70.</ref> Dialogue from the scene was rewritten slightly to accommodate the new location.

===Post-production===
With filming over, the director and producers began editing the footage, repeatedly travelling to both London and to Los Angeles, which had then just been hit by the [[1994 Northridge earthquake]]. Scenes were deleted on the advice of early viewers to shorten the film.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. pp. 110–111.</ref>

==Release==
===Box office===
''The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' took $18,459,245 at the box office in Australia,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://film.vic.gov.au/resources/documents/AA4_Aust_Box_office_report.pdf|title=Australian Films at the Australian Box Office|publisher=Film Victoria|access-date=30 June 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110218045303/http://film.vic.gov.au/resources/documents/AA4_Aust_Box_office_report.pdf|archive-date=18 February 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> which is {{Inflation|AU|18429245|1994|fmt=eq|r=-3}}.{{Inflation-fn|AU}} It was the fifth [[List of highest-grossing films in Australia|highest-grossing Australian film]] in Australia of all time.<ref>{{cite magazine|magazine=[[Screen International]]|date=7 April 2000|page=27|title=Australia's top 10 domestic films|last=George|first=Sandy}}</ref>

Being an Australian film, not an American-produced Hollywood blockbuster, ''Priscilla'' was released as a minor commercial product in North America and other English-speaking nations.<ref>[[#O'R96|O'Regan 1996]]. p. 88.</ref>

Director Elliott noted that the audiences viewing the film in Australia, the United States, and France all reacted to it differently, going on to state that "At a screening we had for an Australian audience, they laughed at all the [[Australian English vocabulary|Aussieisms]]. The Americans laughed too, but at different jokes. There is a line where Tick says, 'Bernadette has [[MacArthur Park (song)|left her cake out in the rain]]...', the Americans laughed for ten minutes."<ref>[[#Eps94|Epstein 1994]]. p. 06.</ref> Tom O'Regan, a scholar of film studies, remarked that the film actually carried different meanings for members of different nationalities and subcultural groups, with [[LGBTQ people in the United States|LGBT Americans]] believing that the film was "the big one that will bring gay lifestyles into the mainstream", while Australians tended to "embrace it as just another successful Australian film".<ref>[[#O'R96|O'Regan 1996]]. p. 55.</ref>

===Critical reaction===
On Rotten Tomatoes, ''Priscilla'' has a 94% rating based on 47 reviews, with an average rating of 7.3/10; the consensus states: "While its premise is ripe for comedy – and it certainly delivers its fair share of laughs – ''Priscilla'' is also a surprisingly tender and thoughtful road movie with some outstanding performances."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/adventures_of_priscilla_queen_of_the_desert/ |title=''The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' |publisher=Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=4 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110629083811/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/adventures_of_priscilla_queen_of_the_desert/ |archive-date=29 June 2011 |url-status=live }}</ref> Metacritic gives a 70 out of 100 rating, based on 20 critics.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-adventures-of-priscilla-queen-of-the-desert | title=The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert reviews | publisher=[[Metacritic]] | access-date=2 July 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304104918/http://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-adventures-of-priscilla-queen-of-the-desert | archive-date=4 March 2016 | url-status=live }}</ref>

American film critic [[Roger Ebert]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' felt that Bernadette was the key part of the film, stating that "the real subject of the movie is not homosexuality, not drag queens, not showbiz, but simply the life of a middle-aged person trapped in a job that has become tiresome".<ref>{{cite news | url=http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=/19940826/REVIEWS/408260301/1023 | newspaper=Chicago Sun-Times | title=''The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' | first=Roger | last=Ebert | issue=129 | date=26 August 1994 | access-date=4 May 2011 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110606085300/http://rogerebert.suntimes.com/apps/pbcs.dll/article?AID=%2F19940826%2FREVIEWS%2F408260301%2F1023 | archive-date=6 June 2011 | url-status=live }}</ref> [[Janet Maslin]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' wrote "''The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert'' presents a defiant culture clash in generous, warmly entertaining ways."<ref name = "Ryll 2014">{{cite web |author=Alexander Ryll |year=2014 |title=Essential Gay Themed Films To Watch, The Adventures of Priscilla Queen of the Desert |publisher=Gay Essential |access-date=22 December 2014 |url=http://gay-themed-films.com/films-watch-adventures-priscilla-queen-desert/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150107142319/http://gay-themed-films.com/films-watch-adventures-priscilla-queen-desert/ |archive-date=7 January 2015 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' commented "In this roaringly comic and powerfully affecting road movie, Terence Stamp gives one of the year's best performances."<ref name="Ryll 2014"/> [[Kenneth Turan]] from the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' wrote "The comic pizazz and bawdy dazzle of this film's vision of gaudy drag performers trekking across the Australian outback certainly has a boisterous, addictive way about it."<ref name="Ryll 2014"/>

===Cultural impact and legacy===
[[File:Priscilla Queen of the Dessert drag queen homage on Fire Island.jpg|thumb|right|A drag queen homage to the film's costumes on [[Fire Island Pines]]]]
''Priscilla'', along with other contemporary Australian films ''[[Young Einstein]]'' (1988), ''[[Sweetie (1989 film)|Sweetie]]'' (1989), ''[[Strictly Ballroom]]'' (1992), and ''[[Muriel's Wedding]]'' (1994), provided Australian cinema with a reputation for "quirkiness", "eccentricity" and "individuality" across the world.<ref name=oregan>[[#O'R96|O'Regan 1996]]. p. 49.</ref> Both ''Priscilla'' and ''Muriel's Wedding'' (which had also featured a soundtrack containing [[ABBA]] songs) in particular became cult classics, not only in their native Australia, but also in the United Kingdom, where a wave of Australian influences, such as the [[soap opera]]s ''[[Neighbours]]'' and ''[[Home & Away]]'', had made their mark in the late 1980s and early 1990s.<ref>[[#Tur10|Turner 2010]]. p. 332–333.</ref>

In 1995, an American film, ''[[To Wong Foo, Thanks for Everything! Julie Newmar]]'', was released, featuring three drag queens who travel across the United States. According to Al Clark, the creators of ''Priscilla'' heard about the film while shooting theirs, and "for a moment [were] troubled" until they read the script of ''To Wong Foo'', when they decided that it was sufficiently different from ''Priscilla'' to not be a commercial and critical threat.<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. p. 88.</ref> ''To Wong Foo'' had a mixed critical response in comparison to ''Priscilla'',<ref>{{cite web |url=http://uk.rottentomatoes.com/m/to_wong_foo_thanks_for_everything_julie_newmar/ |title=''To Wong Foo, Thanks for Everything! Julie Newmar'' |publisher=Rotten Tomatoes |access-date=30 June 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091114210830/http://uk.rottentomatoes.com/m/to_wong_foo_thanks_for_everything_julie_newmar/ |archive-date=14 November 2009}}</ref> but was a box office success in North America<ref>{{cite web |date=1995-10-31 |title=To Wong Foo, Thanks for Everything, Julie Newmar (1995) |url=http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=towongfoo.htm |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140714153219/http://www.boxofficemojo.com/movies/?id=towongfoo.htm |archive-date=14 July 2014 |access-date=2016-08-19 |publisher=[[Box Office Mojo]]}}</ref> as it was a film from a major [[Hollywood studio]] and starred big-name actors.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Kumar |first=Naveen |date=2019-05-28 |title=How America Fell in Love With 'To Wong Foo' |url=https://www.them.us/story/to-wong-foo-camp |access-date=2022-08-29 |website=[[Them (website)|Them]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250716031359/https://www.them.us/story/to-wong-foo-camp|archive-date=2025-07-16}}</ref> Like ''Priscilla'', ''To Wong Foo'' has also enjoyed a [[cult following]].<ref name=":0" />

During the [[2000 Summer Olympics closing ceremony]], ''Priscilla'' was part of a parade of images of Australian popular culture. A 1980 [[Denning (bus manufacturer)|Denning]] (resembling the bus used in the film) featuring a giant steel [[stiletto heel]] which extended from and retracted into the roof – inspired by scenes from the film – paraded around the [[Stadium Australia|Olympic Stadium]]. The bus was accompanied by several stiletto heel [[tricycle]] floats and drag queens in big wigs in tribute to the film's international success and the local [[LGBTQ culture in Sydney|Sydney gay community]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.powerhousemuseum.com/collection/database/?irn=504130 |title=Shoe bike from Sydney Olympic Games closing ceremony |publisher=Powerhouse Museum Collection |access-date=30 June 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110629091956/http://www.powerhousemuseum.com/collection/database/?irn=504130 |archive-date=29 June 2011 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.nma.gov.au/collections-search/results?search=adv&ref=coll&collname=Colin+Dent+collection | title=Colin Dent collection | publisher=National Museum of Australia | location=Canberra | access-date=8 June 2011 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110405043257/http://www.nma.gov.au/collections-search/results?search=adv&ref=coll&collname=Colin+Dent+collection | archive-date=5 April 2011 | url-status=live }}</ref> The [[music video]] for [[Iggy Azalea]]'s 2013 single "[[Work (Iggy Azalea song)|Work]]" paid homage to scenes from the film.<ref name="MTV">{{cite web|author=Alexis, Nedeska|url=http://www.mtv.com/news/1703697/iggy-azalea-work-video-inspiration/|title=Iggy Azalea's 'Work' Video Inspired By Outkast|publisher=[[MTV News]]|date=14 March 2013|access-date=8 May 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151005225426/http://www.mtv.com/news/1703697/iggy-azalea-work-video-inspiration/|archive-date=5 October 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The bus used in the film, which was a 1976 [[Hino Motors|Hino Freighter]] from Japan, was rediscovered in 2019 at a property in [[Ewingar]], New South Wales. A campaign was launched by the [[History Trust of South Australia]], with the help of the [[Government of South Australia]] to restore the bus for eventual display in the [[National Motor Museum, Birdwood]]. {{as of|2024|4}} restoration works on the vehicle were taking place in [[Brisbane]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-04-12/bid-to-restore-priscilla-queen-of-the-desert-bus/103699136 |title=History Trust SA launches campaign to restore long-lost Priscilla, Queen of the Desert bus |publisher=ABC News|location=Australia|date=12 April 2024|access-date=21 April 2024}}</ref>

===Racism and sexism controversy===
The film has come under criticism for alleged [[Racism|racist]] and [[Sexism|sexist]] elements, particularly in the portrayal of the Filipina character, Cynthia.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Le Guellec-Minel |first1=Anne |title=Camping it out in the Never Never: Subverting Hegemonic Masculinity in The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert |journal=Revue LISA |date=4 September 2017 |volume=15 |issue=1 |doi=10.4000/lisa.9086 |doi-access=free }}</ref> Melba Marginson of the Centre for Filipino Concerns stated that Cynthia was portrayed as "a gold-digger, a prostitute, an entertainer whose expertise is popping out ping-pong balls from her sex-organ, a manic depressive, loud and vulgar. The worst stereotype of the Filipina." She argued that, by portraying Cynthia in this manner, the filmmakers were "violently kill[ing]" the dignity of Filipina women, something that she feared would lead to "more violence against us."<ref name="Cafarella, 1994">Cafarella, ''The Age'', 7 October 1994.</ref> An editor writing in ''The Age'' echoed these concerns, highlighting that "It is perhaps a pity that a film with a message of tolerance and acceptance for homosexuals should feel the need of what looks very much to us like a racist and sexist stereotype."<ref name="Cafarella, 1994"/> Similarly, in his study of bisexuality in cinema, Wayne M. Bryant argued that while it was "an excellent film", ''The Adventures of Priscilla'' was marred by "instances of gratuitous sexism."<ref>[[#Bry97|Bryant 1997]]. p. 108.</ref>

Producer Clark defended the film against these accusations, arguing that while Cynthia was a [[stereotype]], it was not the purpose of filmmakers to avoid the portrayal of "vulnerable characters" from specific minority backgrounds. He stated that she was "a misfit like the three protagonists are, and just about everybody else in the film is, and her presence is no more a statement about Filipino women than having three drag queens is a statement about Australian men."<ref name="Cafarella, 1994"/> [[Thomas O'Regan|Tom O'Regan]] noted that as a result of this controversy, the film gained "an ambiguous reputation."<ref>[[#O'R96|O'Regan 1996]]. p. 142.</ref>

==Soundtrack==
{{Main|The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert (soundtrack)}}

The film featured a soundtrack made up of pre-existing "[[Camp (style)|camp]] classics" ([[pop music]] songs that have a particular fanbase in the [[LGBT]] community). The original plan by the film's creators was to have a [[Kylie Minogue]] song in the finale, although it was later decided that an [[ABBA]] song would be more appropriate because its "tacky qualities" were "more timeless"<ref>[[#Cla94|Clark 1994]]. p. 34.</ref> (although in the musical adaptation, the character Adam performs a medley of Kylie Minogue songs atop Uluṟu). The film itself featured four main songs, which were performed by two or more of the drag queens as a part of their show within the film; "[[I've Never Been to Me]]" by [[Charlene (singer)|Charlene]], "[[I Will Survive]]" by [[Gloria Gaynor]], "[[Finally (CeCe Peniston song)|Finally]]" by [[CeCe Peniston]], and "[[Mamma Mia (ABBA song)|Mamma Mia]]" by ABBA. On 23 August 1994, [[Fontana Records|Fontana Island]] released the soundtrack on CD.<ref>[https://www.discogs.com/Various-The-Adventures-Of-Priscilla-Queen-Of-The-Desert-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack/master/97970 The Adventures Of Priscilla: Queen Of The Desert – Original Motion Picture Soundtrack] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201044130/https://www.discogs.com/Various-The-Adventures-Of-Priscilla-Queen-Of-The-Desert-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack/master/97970 |date=1 December 2017 }}.  [[Discogs]]</ref>

Original music for the soundtrack was composed by [[Guy Gross]], with choral and instrumental arrangements by [[Derek Williams (musician)|Derek Williams]], and released separately on CD.<ref>[https://www.discogs.com/Guy-Gross-The-Adventures-of-Priscilla-Queen-of-the-Desert-The-Priscilla-Companion-Dialogue-from-the-/release/11174361 The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert. The Priscilla Companion: Dialogue from the film & Original Music Score by Guy Gross] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201042509/https://www.discogs.com/Guy-Gross-The-Adventures-of-Priscilla-Queen-of-the-Desert-The-Priscilla-Companion-Dialogue-from-the-/release/11174361 |date=1 December 2017 }}. ==Sequel==
{{Main|Priscilla Queen of the Desert 2}}

In April 2024, Elliott announced that work on a sequel was underway, with the original main cast reprising their roles.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Saarinen |first=Nelli |date=21 April 2024 |title=Priscilla, Queen of the Desert sequel announced 30 years after beloved film's original release |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-04-21/priscilla-queen-of-the-desert-sequel/103750672 |publisher=ABC News|location=Australia}}</ref> After Stamp died on 17 August 2025, Elliott told ''[[The Guardian]]'': "[Stamp] agreed to do the sequel a few years ago and we've been particularly busy over the past year."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2025/aug/18/terence-stamp-remembered-by-priscilla-director-stephan-elliott|title=Terence Stamp remembered by Priscilla director Stephan Elliott: 'Those eyes turned everybody to jelly'|first1=Sian|last1=Cain|date=18 August 2025|work=The Guardian|access-date=19 August 2025}}</ref> In September 2025, Elliot revealed that, with the blessing of the cast and financiers, Stamp spent the final months of his life pre-filming all of his scenes with "a nine-camera array of the entire script".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Bamigboye |first1=Baz |title=Breaking Baz: Terence Stamp Pre-Shot ‘Priscilla Queen of the Desert 2’ Scenes Months Before He Died, Filmmaker Stephan Elliott Says Actor Had “Time Of His Life” |url=https://deadline.com/2025/09/terence-stamp-pre-shot-priscilla-queen-of-the-desert-2-1236563340/ |publisher=Deadline |access-date=28 September 2025}}</ref>

==Home media==
On 14 November 1995, the film was released on VHS. On 7 October 1997, it was released on DVD with a collectable trivia booklet.

In 2004, a 10th Anniversary Collector's Edition was released on DVD in Australia with the following special features: a feature-length [[audio commentary]] with writer/director [[Stephan Elliott]], three [[deleted scene]]s, two [[featurette]]s: "Behind the Bus: Priscilla with Her Pants Down" and ''[[Ladies Please]]'', cast and crew [[biographies]], the original Australian [[theatrical trailer]], US theatrical and teaser trailers, and a number of hidden features

In 2006, it was re-released on DVD in Australia with the following special features: a feature-length audio commentary with writer/director Elliott, "Birth of a Queen" (featurette), deleted scenes, tidbits from the Set, "The Bus from Blooperville" – Gag reel documentary, a photo gallery, and US theatrical and teaser trailers.

On 5 June 2007, it was re-released in the United States as the "Extra Frills Edition" DVD. This edition includes the same special features as the Australian 2006 re-release. On 7 June 2011, it was released for US [[Blu-ray]].

On August 24, 2024, it was announced that Imprint Films will release a 4K collector's edition of the film, restored from the 35mm print. It will host both new and legacy special features, including the 2015 documentary ''Between a Frock and a Hard Place'' that details the lasting impact of the film.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.blu-ray.com/news/?id=35089 | title=The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert 4K Blu-ray }}</ref>
