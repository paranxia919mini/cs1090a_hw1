'''''The Florida Project''''' is a 2017 American [[drama film]] directed by [[Sean Baker]], written by Baker and [[Chris Bergoch]], and starring [[Bria Vinaite]] in her film debut, [[Brooklynn Prince]], and [[Willem Dafoe]], with Valeria Cotto, Christopher Rivera, and [[Caleb Landry Jones]] in supporting roles. It was many of the cast members' first film appearance. The [[slice of life]] plot focuses on the summertime adventures of a six-year-old girl who lives with her unemployed single mother in a budget [[motel]] in [[Kissimmee, Florida]]. Their struggle to make ends meet and stave off homelessness takes place in a realistic environment near [[Walt Disney World]], which was code-named "The Florida Project" during its planning stages.<ref>{{cite web|title=VIDEO: First trailer for "The Florida Project" film highlights life at budget motel near Walt Disney World|access-date=August 27, 2018|url=http://www.insidethemagic.net/2017/08/video-first-trailer-florida-project-film-highlights-life-budget-motel-near-walt-disney-world/|first=Cristina|last=Sanza|publisher=JAK Schmidt|website=Inside the Magic|date=August 14, 2017}}</ref> It juxtaposes this with the local residents' less glamorous day-to-day lives and the children's joyful adventures as they explore and make the most of their surroundings while remaining blissfully ignorant of the hardships their adult caretakers face.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Hoffman |first=Jordan |date=2017-05-22 |title=The Florida Project review – poverty and joy in the shadow of the Magic Kingdom |language=en-GB |work=The Guardian |url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/may/22/the-florida-project-review-sean-baker-cannes-2017 |access-date=2023-07-06 |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

The film premiered in the [[Directors' Fortnight]] section of the [[2017 Cannes Film Festival]], and was released theatrically in the United States by [[A24]] on October 6, 2017. It was acclaimed by critics,<ref name="theguardian">{{Cite news |last1=Kermode |first1=Mark |date=2017-11-12 |title=The Florida Project review – thrillingly vibrant |language=en-GB |work=the Guardian |url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2017/nov/12/the-florida-project-review-sunshine-state-of-mind |access-date=2023-07-06 |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> who praised the performances and Baker's direction; Vinaite earned particular acclaim for her performance. Prince's work earned her a [[Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Young Performer]], while Dafoe was judged to have given "his finest performance in recent memory",<ref name="Hornaday">{{Cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/goingoutguide/movies/the-florida-project-bad-parenting-in-the-tawdry-shadow-of-disney-world/2017/10/11/b2675bf6-a933-11e7-92d1-58c702d2d975_story.html|title=Review {{!}} 'The Florida Project': Willem Dafoe delivers his finest performance in recent memory|newspaper=[[The Washington Post]]|first=Ann |last=Hornaday|date=October 11, 2017|access-date=November 28, 2017}}</ref> receiving Best Supporting Actor nods at the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Academy Awards]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Motion Picture|Golden Globes]], [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role|SAG Awards]], [[Critics' Choice Movie Awards|Critics Choice Awards]], and [[BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role|BAFTA Awards]].<ref name="goldenglobes">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2017/film/news/golden-globe-nominations-2018-nominees-full-list-1202634435/|title=Golden Globe Nominations: Complete List|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|date=December 11, 2017|access-date=December 11, 2017|first=Rebecca|last=Rubin}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-42618187|title=The Shape of Water leads Bafta nominations|work=[[BBC News]]|publisher=[[BBC]]|date=January 9, 2018 |access-date=January 9, 2018}}</ref>

Both the [[National Board of Review]] and the [[American Film Institute]] named ''The Florida Project'' one of the top ten films of the year.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.afi.com/afiawards/AFI-Awards-2017.aspx|title=AFI Awards 2017|publisher=[[American Film Institute]]|access-date=December 8, 2017}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.nationalboardofreview.org/2017/11/national-board-review-announces-2017-award-winners/ |title=National Board of Review Announces 2017 Award Winners |newspaper=[[National Board of Review]] |first1=Andy|last1=Gelb|first2=Shawn|last2=Purdy|first3=Rachael|last3=Trager|date=November 28, 2017 |access-date=December 7, 2017}}</ref>

==Plot==
Moonee, a six-year-old girl, lives with her young, single mother, Halley, at Magic Castle Inn and Suites, a budget [[motel]] in [[Kissimmee, Florida]], near [[Walt Disney World]]. Moonee spends most of her summer days unsupervised and making mischief with her downstairs neighbor, Scooty (whom Halley is supposed to watch while his mother, Ashley, works as a waitress at a diner), and Dicky, who lives at the nearby Futureland Inn. After Stacy, a new Futureland resident, catches the trio spitting on her car, Dicky is grounded for a week, and Moonee and Scooty meet and befriend Stacy's granddaughter, Jancey, who lives with Stacy.

Halley has recently lost her job as a [[stripper]] after refusing to have sex with clients, but this now affects her eligibility for [[Temporary Assistance for Needy Families|TANF]] benefits; she begins relying on food that Ashley obtains from work. Struggling to pay rent, Halley begins selling knockoff perfume to tourists in the parking lots of upscale hotels with Moonee's help. Meanwhile, Moonee and Scooty show Jancey around the neighborhood and teach her things, like how to get ice cream by begging for money. They regularly inconvenience Bobby, the Magic Castle's manager, once shutting off the motel's power. Despite this, he remains protective of them. Bobby's duties include preparing expense reports, ejecting drug dealers, and doing repairs; he sometimes enlists the help of his son, Jack, with whom he has a tenuous relationship.

After Dicky's family moves to [[New Orleans]], Scooty finds a lighter in a box that Dicky's family left behind; he, Moonee, and Jancey start a fire at an abandoned [[Condominium|condominium complex]]. Seeing the fire, Ashley figures out that Scooty was involved, and forbids him from hanging out with Moonee or Jancey; she also severs ties with Halley and Moonee.

Without the free food from Ashley, and with security guards beginning to bother her at the hotels, Halley's financial situation declines even further. She begins soliciting sex work online, keeping Moonee in the bathroom with loud music when she has a client over. When Halley steals a client's Disney World [[MagicBands]] to [[ticket resale|scalp]] them, he returns to demand them back. Bobby scares him off but applies restrictions on unregistered guests in Halley's room; he also warns her that he will evict her if she continues having clients over. In desperation, Halley approaches Ashley to apologize and ask for money. Ashley criticizes Halley for doing sex work and threatens to kill her if her son was ever exposed to Halley's prostitution; enraged, Halley viciously beats her in front of Scooty.

Soon afterward, [[Florida Department of Children and Families|DCF]] investigators show up and question Halley and Moonee separately about their lifestyle. In anticipation of another visit, Halley gives her [[Cannabis (drug)|weed]] to one of the housekeepers and has Moonee help clean their room. They go to a fancier hotel and have an extravagant meal, which Halley charges to a guest's room. When they return to Magic Castle, the investigators, having found evidence of Halley's sex work, are waiting with two police officers to take Moonee into [[foster care]] while they finish their inquiry. Not fully understanding what is happening, Moonee asks to say goodbye to Scooty, who lets slip that she is going to a new family. Upset, Moonee runs away from the investigators to bid Jancey goodbye. Seeing her friend's distress, Jancey grabs Moonee's hand and the two run away to Walt Disney World's [[Magic Kingdom]] [[Amusement park|theme park]].

==Production==
===Development===
Writer [[Chris Bergoch]] noticed a lot of children playing in motel parking lots while visiting his mother in [[Orlando, Florida]]. [[Sean Baker (filmmaker)|Sean Baker]] said he had always been inspired by the ''[[Our Gang]]'' films, because the characters "were actually living in poverty, but the focus was the joy of childhood, the humor that comes from watching and hearing children."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Feinberg|first=Scott|date=November 1, 2017|title=Savannah Film Fest: How 'The Little Rascals' Inspired 'The Florida Project'|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/race/savannah-film-fest-how-little-rascals-inspired-florida-project-1053827|access-date=April 12, 2021|website=The Hollywood Reporter|language=en}}</ref><ref name="theguardian"/>

In December 2017, producer Andrew Duncan stepped down from his role as financier of [[June Pictures]] after numerous allegations of [[sexual harassment]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Maddaus|first=Gene|date=2017-12-15|title='Florida Project' Producer Steps Down Amid Harassment Claims|url=https://variety.com/2017/film/news/andrew-duncan-florida-project-sexual-harassment-1202642007/|access-date=2021-12-12|website=Variety|language=en-US}}</ref> Baker said:<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Baum|first1=Gary Baum|last2=Masters|first2=Kim|date=2017-12-15|title='Florida Project' Producer Accused of Sexual Harassment by a Dozen Insiders|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/florida-project-producer-accused-sexual-harassment-by-a-dozen-insiders-1068287/|access-date=2021-12-12|website=The Hollywood Reporter|language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Fleming|first=Mike Jr.|date=2017-12-15|title=June Pictures Shakeup: Alex Saks Buys Out Financier Andrew Duncan Amid Allegation Cloud|url=https://deadline.com/2017/12/june-pictures-shakeup-alex-saks-financier-andrew-duncan-the-florida-project-1202227626/|access-date=2021-12-12|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|language=en-US}}</ref> <blockquote>While we did not witness nor have any knowledge of inappropriate behavior, we are of course deeply concerned about these allegations. I have been outspoken before and firmly believe that film sets and work environments absolutely must be safe spaces for everyone regardless of gender, age, race, or creed.</blockquote>

===Filming===
''The Florida Project'' was filmed in the summer of 2016 on location in [[Osceola County, Florida]],<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.orlandosentinel.com/classified/realestate/os-bz-florida-project-film-20171015-story.html|title='Florida Project' film portrays life in Kissimmee hotels|last=Shanklin|first=Mary|date=October 15, 2017|work=[[Orlando Sentinel]]|access-date=July 27, 2018}}</ref> including at the real Magic Castle Inn & Suites on [[U.S. Route 192|U.S. Highway 192]] in [[Kissimmee, Florida|Kissimmee]], about six miles from [[Walt Disney World]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2017/oct/15/in-the-shadow-of-disney-living-life-on-the-margins|title=In the shadow of Disney, living life on the margins|last=Luscombe|first=Richard|date=October 15, 2017|work=[[The Guardian]]|publisher=[[Guardian News and Media]]|access-date=July 27, 2018}}</ref>

Unlike Baker's [[Tangerine (film)|previous film]], which was shot with an iPhone, ''The Florida Project'' was filmed on [[35mm movie film|35mm film]], except for the final scene, which was shot without authorization in Disney World's [[Magic Kingdom]] park using an [[IPhone 6s|iPhone 6S Plus]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.papermag.com/the-florida-project-director-sean-baker-on-working-with-untrained-actos-and-secret-filming-in-disney-world-2499057069.html|title="The Florida Project" Director Sean Baker on Working with Untrained Actors and Secret Filming in Disney World|work=[[Paper (magazine)|Paper]]|date=October 20, 2017|access-date=October 22, 2017|first=Alexander|last=Hakimi}}</ref><ref name=":0">{{Cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/florida-project-ending-director-sean-baker-explains-meaning-how-he-did-it-1047215|title='The Florida Project': Director Sean Baker Explains How and Why He Shot That Ending|last=Lee|first=Ashley|date=October 11, 2017|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Prometheus Global Media]]|access-date=January 12, 2018}}</ref> To maintain secrecy, the shoot at the resort used a skeleton crew consisting of Baker, Bergoch, cinematographer [[Alexis Zabe]], acting coach and associate producer [[Samantha Quan]], actors Valeria Cotto and [[Brooklynn Prince]], and the girls' guardians.<ref name=":0" /> Baker intended the ending to be open to interpretation:<ref name=":0" /> <blockquote>We've been watching Moonee use her imagination and wonderment throughout the entire film to make the best of the situation she's in—she can't go to [[Disney's Animal Kingdom]], so she goes to the "safari" behind the motel and looks at cows; she goes to the abandoned condos because she can't go to the [[The Haunted Mansion|Haunted Mansion]]. And in the end, with this inevitable drama, this is me saying to the audience, if you want a happy ending, you're gonna have to go to that headspace of a kid because, here, that's the only way to achieve it.</blockquote>

Baker's sister, billed as "Stephonik Youth", was the film's [[production designer]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://deadline.com/2018/01/the-florida-project-stephonik-youth-production-design-interview-news-1202230046/|title='The Florida Project' Production Designer On Tracking Down Dreamy Pastel-Colored Locations For Dark Fairy Tale|last=Grobar|first=Matt|date=2018-01-01|work=Deadline|access-date=2018-06-26|language=en-US}}</ref> The film's signature [[Pastel (color)|pastel colors]] were already present at the location. When a rainbow or a group of [[sandhill cranes]] suddenly appeared, the cast and crew quickly improvised scenes that were highly praised for their [[cinéma vérité]] qualities.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sims |first=David |date=2017-12-23 |title=A Meaningful Chat With a Flock of Birds in 'The Florida Project' |url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2017/12/a-meaningful-chat-with-a-flock-of-birds-in-the-florida-project/549065/ |access-date=2024-07-24 |website=The Atlantic |language=en}}</ref>

==Release==
[[File:Willem Dafoe 2014 (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright|[[Willem Dafoe]]'s performance was critically acclaimed and nominated for several awards, including the [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor]] ]]
The film had its world premiere on May 22, 2017, in the [[Directors Fortnight]] section of the [[2017 Cannes Film Festival]],<ref name="Quinzaine">{{cite web |url=http://www.quinzaine-realisateurs.com/en/fortnight-2017/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170419004103/http://www.quinzaine-realisateurs.com/en/fortnight-2017/ |url-status=dead |archive-date=April 19, 2017 |title=Fortnight 2017: The 49th Directors' Fortnight Selection |access-date=April 20, 2017 |work=[[Directors Fortnight]] |publisher=Société des Réalisateurs de Films}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://variety.com/2017/film/news/directors-fortnight-lineup-juliette-binoche-gerard-depardieu-1202390797/ |title=Cannes: Juliette Binoche-Gerard Depardieu Drama to Kick Off Directors Fortnight |first=Elsa |last=Keslassy |date=April 19, 2016 |access-date=April 20, 2017 |work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]}}</ref> and shortly thereafter [[A24]] acquired the film's U.S. distribution rights, which had also sparked interest from [[Amazon Studios]], [[Neon (company)|Neon]], and [[Annapurna Pictures]], among others.<ref name=variety>{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2017/film/markets-festivals/sean-baker-cannes-1202445906/|title=A24 Buys Sean Baker's 'Florida Project' (EXCLUSIVE)|website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|first1=Ramin|last1=Seetoodeh|first2=Brent|last2=Lang|date=May 26, 2017|access-date=May 26, 2017}}</ref> Its [[limited theatrical release]] in the U.S. began on October 6, 2017.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2017/06/the-florida-project-sean-baker-cannes-title-opens-october-2017-1202111700/|title=Cannes Directors' Fortnight Title 'The Florida Project' Stakes Out October Date|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|publisher=[[Penske Business Media]]|first=Anthony|last=D'Alessandro|date=June 12, 2017|access-date=June 12, 2017}}</ref> [[Lionsgate Films|Lionsgate]] released the film on [[Blu-ray]], DVD, and download.

==Reception==
===Critical response===
''The Florida Project'' received critical acclaim upon its release, with particular praise for Baker's direction and the performances of Dafoe, Prince, and Vinaite. On [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], it has an approval rating of 96% based on 320 reviews, with an average rating of 8.8/10; the site's "critics consensus" reads: "''The Florida Project'' offers a colorfully empathetic look at an underrepresented part of the population that proves absorbing even as it raises sobering questions about modern America."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_florida_project|title= ''The Florida Project (2017)''|website=[[Rotten Tomatoes]]|publisher=[[Fandango Media]]|access-date=November 4, 2022}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a [[weighted average]] score of 92 out of 100 based on reviews from 44 critics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-florida-project|title=''The Florida Project''|website=[[Metacritic]]|publisher=[[CBS Interactive]]|access-date=December 19, 2017}}</ref>

Ann Hornaday of ''[[The Washington Post]]'' wrote, "Dafoe delivers his finest performance in recent memory, bringing to life a levelheaded, unsanctimonious character who offers a glimmer of hope and caring within a world markedly short on both."<ref name="Hornaday"/> [[Richard Roeper]] of the ''[[Chicago Sun-Times]]'' wrote, "It's film that'll make you wince at times, and you'll most likely not want to see twice, but seeing it once is an experience you'll not soon forget."<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://chicago.suntimes.com/entertainment/the-florida-project-ably-explores-gloomy-lives-under-sunny-skies/|title='The Florida Project' ably explores gloomy lives under sunny skies|work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]]|access-date=November 28, 2017|date=October 12, 2017|first=Richard|last=Roeper}}</ref>

In June 2025, ''The Florida Project'' ranked 74th on ''[[The New York Times]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century" and 70th on the "Readers' Choice" edition of the list.<ref>{{cite web |title=Readers Choose Their Top Movies of the 21st Century |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/readers-movies-21st-century.html |website=The New York Times |date=July 2, 2025 |access-date=2 July 2025}}</ref><ref name="Times">{{cite web | url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html | title=The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | work=The New York Times | date=June 23, 2025 }}</ref> In July 2025, it ranked 63rd on ''[[Rolling Stone]]''{{'}}s list of "The 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century".<ref>{{cite magazine | url=https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-lists/best-movies-of-the-21st-century-1235200512/ | title=100 Best Movies of the 21st Century | magazine=[[Rolling Stone]] | date=July 1, 2025 }}</ref>
