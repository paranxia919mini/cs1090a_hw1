'''''The Social Network''''' is a 2010 American [[Biographical drama|biographical]] [[drama film]] directed by [[David Fincher]] and written by [[Aaron Sorkin]]. Based on the 2009 book ''[[The Accidental Billionaires]]'' by [[Ben Mezrich]], the film portrays the founding of the [[Social network service|social networking]] website [[Facebook]]. It stars [[Jesse Eisenberg]] as Facebook CEO [[Mark Zuckerberg]], with [[Andrew Garfield]] as co-founder [[Eduardo Saverin]], [[Justin Timberlake]] as [[Sean Parker]], [[Armie Hammer]] as [[Cameron Winklevoss|Cameron]] and [[Tyler Winklevoss]], and [[Max Minghella]] as [[Divya Narendra]]. Neither Zuckerberg nor any other Facebook staff were involved with the project, although Saverin was a consultant for Mezrich's book.<ref name="NewYork">Harris, Mark. [https://nymag.com/movies/features/68319/ "Inventing Facebook"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201118143948/https://nymag.com/movies/features/68319/ |date=November 18, 2020}}. ''New York Magazine''. September 17, 2010. Retrieved December 5, 2019.</ref>

Production began when Sorkin signed on to write the screenplay. [[Principal photography]] began in October 2009 in [[Cambridge, Massachusetts]], and lasted until November. Additional scenes were shot in California, in the cities of [[Los Angeles]] and [[Pasadena, California|Pasadena]]. [[Trent Reznor]] and [[Atticus Ross]] of [[Nine Inch Nails]] composed the film's award-winning [[The Social Network (soundtrack)|score]], which was released on September 28, 2010.

The film premiered at the [[New York Film Festival]] on September 24, 2010, and was released theatrically in the United States on October 1, by [[Sony Pictures Releasing]] through its [[Columbia Pictures]] label. A major commercial success, the film grossed $225 million on a $40 million budget and was widely acclaimed by critics. It was named one of the best films of the year by 447 critics, and named the best by 110 critics, the most of any film that year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://criticstop10.com/best-movies-of-2010/|title=Best Movies of 2010 - CriticsTop10|publisher=Critics' Top 10|access-date=May 8, 2025}}</ref> It was also chosen by the [[National Board of Review]] as the [[National Board of Review Awards 2010#Top 10 Films|best film of 2010]]. At the [[83rd Academy Awards]], it received eight nominations, including for [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]], and [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]] for Eisenberg, and won for [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]], [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]], and [[Academy Award for Best Film Editing|Best Film Editing]]. It also received awards for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama|Best Motion Picture – Drama]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Director|Best Director]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Screenplay|Best Screenplay]], and [[Golden Globe Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]] at the [[68th Golden Globe Awards]].

== Plot ==
<!-- Please see WP:FILMPLOT before adding material. The word count for a plot is usually 400-700 words. -->
In intercut [[frame story|frame stories]], two ongoing depositions play out: in one, [[Mark Zuckerberg]] is being sued by his former friend [[Eduardo Saverin]], and in the other, he is sued by twins [[Cameron Winklevoss|Cameron]] and [[Tyler Winklevoss]].

On October 28, 2003, 19-year-old Zuckerberg, a [[Harvard University]] sophomore, is dumped by his girlfriend, Erica Albright. Returning to his dorm, Zuckerberg writes an insulting post about her on his [[LiveJournal]] blog. Zuckerberg creates a campus website called [[Facemash]] by hacking and downloading photos of female students from house [[face book|face books]], then allowing site visitors to rate their attractiveness. After traffic to the site crashes parts of Harvard's computer network, Zuckerberg is given six months of [[disciplinary probation|academic probation]].

Facemash's popularity attracts the attention of the Winklevoss twins and their business partner [[Divya Narendra]]. The trio invites Zuckerberg to work on [[Harvard Connection]], a social network exclusive to Harvard students focused on dating. Soon after, Zuckerberg approaches Saverin with an idea for <!--ORIGINAL NAME OF THE COMPANY-->"[[TheFacebook]]"<!--ORIGINAL NAME OF THE COMPANY-->, a [[Social networking service|social networking]] website that would be exclusive to [[Ivy League]] students. As its CFO, Saverin provides $1,000 in seed funding to allow Zuckerberg to build the website, which quickly becomes popular. When they learn of TheFacebook, the Winklevoss twins and Narendra are incensed, believing that Zuckerberg stole their idea while misleading them by stalling development on the Harvard Connection. They raise their complaint with Harvard President [[Larry Summers]], who is dismissive and sees no value in disciplinary action on TheFacebook or Zuckerberg.

Saverin and Zuckerberg meet fellow student Christy Lee, who asks them to "Facebook me," a phrase that impresses them. As TheFacebook grows in popularity, Zuckerberg expands the network to [[Yale University|Yale]], [[Columbia University|Columbia]], and [[Stanford University|Stanford]]. Saverin begins dating Lee, who arranges for him and Zuckerberg to meet [[Napster]] co-founder [[Sean Parker]]. Parker presents a "billion-dollar" vision for the company, impressing Zuckerberg. Saverin dismisses Parker as paranoid and delusional, except for his suggestion to rename TheFacebook to [[Facebook]]. Saverin deposits $18,000 into a new account as Zuckerberg relocates the company to [[Palo Alto]] on Parker's advice; Saverin remains in New York to work on business development while breaking up with Lee. Parker later moves into the house that Zuckerberg is using as a base of operations and becomes more involved with the company, much to Saverin's annoyance.

After narrowly losing in the 2004 [[Henley Royal Regatta]] for Harvard against the [[Hollandia Roeiclub]], the Winklevoss twins discover that, through Parker, Facebook has expanded to Europe with [[University of Oxford|Oxford]], [[University of Cambridge|Cambridge]] and [[London School of Economics|LSE]], and decide to sue the company for [[Intellectual property rights|intellectual property theft]]. Meanwhile, Saverin objects to Parker's decision-making for Facebook and freezes the company's bank account in the resulting dispute. He relents when Zuckerberg reveals that they have secured $500,000 from [[angel investor]] [[Peter Thiel]].

Saverin is invited to the company's new headquarters in [[San Francisco]] on the pretense of attending a business meeting and "millionth user party," but is enraged when he discovers that the new investment deal [[Stock dilution|dilutes]] his share of Facebook from 34% to 0.03%, without diluting the ownership percentage of any other owner. Furthermore, he is removed from the masthead as co-founder and CFO. He confronts Zuckerberg and Parker, vowing to sue as security removes him from the building. Shortly after, Parker is apprehended for cocaine possession with a minor at a party celebrating one million users. He attempts to blame Saverin, prompting Zuckerberg to cut ties with him.

Marylin Delpy, a junior lawyer for Zuckerberg, informs Zuckerberg that they will settle with Saverin since the sordid details of Facebook's founding and Zuckerberg's callous attitude will make him unsympathetic to a jury. Alone, Zuckerberg sends a Facebook friend request to Albright and repeatedly refreshes the page. Closing text reveals both cases were [[settled out of court]] and Zuckerberg became the world's youngest billionaire.

== Production ==
=== Screenplay ===
Screenwriter [[Aaron Sorkin]] said, "What attracted me to [the film project] had nothing to do with [[Facebook]]. The invention itself is as modern as it gets, but the story is as old as storytelling; the themes of friendship, loyalty, jealousy, class and power". He said he read an unfinished draft of [[Ben Mezrich]]'s ''[[The Accidental Billionaires]]'' when the publisher began "shopping it around" for a film adaptation. Sorkin added, "I was reading it and somewhere on page three I said yes. It was the fastest I said yes to anything ... They wanted me to start right away. Ben and I were kind of doing our research at the same time, sort of along parallel lines".<ref name="FaceValue">{{cite web|last=Callaghan|first=Dylan|title=Face Value|url=http://www.wga.org/content/default.aspx?id=4348|work=The Craft|publisher=[[Writers Guild of America West]]|access-date=August 14, 2012|date=October 1, 2010|archive-date=June 8, 2012|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120608190459/http://www.wga.org/content/default.aspx?id=4348|url-status=live}}</ref> In August 2008, Sorkin started a Facebook group for research purposes while he was working on the film. "I've just agreed to write a movie for [[Sony Pictures|Sony]] and producer [[Scott Rudin]] about how Facebook was invented. I figured a good first step in my preparation would be finding out what Facebook is, so I've started this page."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kois |first=Dan |date=2008-08-27 |title=CONFIRMED: Aaron Sorkin Creates Facebook Page, Writing Facebook Movie - Slideshow |url=https://www.vulture.com/2008/08/confirmed_aaron_sorkin_creates.html |access-date=2025-03-20 |website=Vulture |language=en |archive-date=September 24, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924175648/https://www.vulture.com/2008/08/confirmed_aaron_sorkin_creates.html |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Luft |first=Oliver |date=2008-08-28 |title=Aaron Sorkin to write Facebook movie |url=https://www.theguardian.com/media/2008/aug/28/facebook.television |access-date=2025-03-20 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> He emphasized the role of the competing narratives at a Q&A screening hosted by [[Myspace]]: "If the same story was behind the invention of MySpace or Friendster, I would have written that. Two separate lawsuits were brought against Facebook at roughly the same time. Rather than pick one and decide that's the truth, or pick one and say that's the sexist [''sic''], I like the idea that there are three conflicting stories."<ref>{{Cite web |date=2010-09-27 |title=Aaron Sorkin: Social Network is True. Also False. It's Complicated. |url=https://observer.com/2010/09/aaron-sorkin-emsocial-networkem-is-true-also-false-its-complicated/ |access-date=2025-03-20 |website=Observer |language=en-US |archive-date=September 24, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240924182249/https://observer.com/2010/09/aaron-sorkin-emsocial-networkem-is-true-also-false-its-complicated/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

According to Sorkin, Mezrich did not send him material from his book as he wrote it: "Two or three times we'd get together. I'd go to [[Boston]], or we'd meet in [[New York City|New York]] and kind of compare notes and share information, but I didn't see the book until he was done with it. By the time I saw the book, I was probably 80 percent done with the screenplay."<ref name="FaceValue"/> Sorkin elaborated:
{{Cquote|There's a lot of available research, and I also did a lot of first person research with a number of the people that were involved in the story. I can't go too deeply into that because most of the people did it on the condition of anonymity, but what I found was that two lawsuits were brought against Facebook at roughly the same time, that the defendant, plaintiffs, witnesses all came into a deposition room and swore under oath, and three different versions of the story were told. Instead of choosing one and deciding that's the truest one or choosing one and deciding that's the juiciest one, I decided to dramatize the idea that there were three different versions of the story being told. That's how I came up with the structure of the deposition room.<ref name="FaceValue"/>}}

==== Rowing production ====

David Fincher hired [[Loyola Marymount]] coach Dawn Reagan to help train Josh Pence and Armie Hammer.<ref name="Row2k">{{cite news |last=Boyne |first=Dan |title=Rowing and the Art of the Social Network – Part IV: The Actors Arrive |url=http://www.row2k.com/features/features.cfm?action=read&ID=482 |access-date=April 30, 2011 |newspaper=Row2k |date=October 12, 2010 |archive-date=November 18, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201118143943/https://www.row2k.com/features/482/Part-IV--The-Actors-Arrive/ |url-status=live}}</ref> While Hammer was new to the sport, Pence rowed previously at [[Dartmouth College]].<ref name="Row2k"/>

== Release ==
''The Social Network'' premiered at the [[New York Film Festival]] on September 24, 2010.<ref name="NYFF"/>

=== Box office ===
The film was released in theaters in the United States on the weekend of October 1–3, 2010. It debuted at No. 1, grossing $22.4 million in 2,771 theaters.<ref name="BoxOfficeMojo"/>  The film retained the top spot in its second weekend, dropping only 31.2%,<ref name="BoxOfficeMojo"/> breaking ''[[Inception]]''{{'}}s 32.0% record as the smallest second weekend drop for any number-one film of 2010, while being the third-smallest overall behind ''[[Secretariat (film)|Secretariat]]''{{'}}s 25.1% drop and [[Tooth Fairy (2010 film)|''Tooth Fairy'']]'s 28.6% drop. At the end of its theatrical run, the film grossed $97 million in the United States and $128 million in other territories for a worldwide total of $225 million.<ref name="BoxOfficeMojo"/>

=== Critical reception ===
[[File:Jesse Eisenberg - La red social - Madrid - 2.jpg|thumb|[[Jesse Eisenberg]] received critical acclaim for his performance as Mark Zuckerberg, earning an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor.]]
''The Social Network'' received widespread critical acclaim.  On review aggregator [[Rotten Tomatoes]], the film has an approval rating of 96% based on 335 reviews, with an average rating of 9/10. The website's critical consensus reads, "Impeccably scripted, beautifully directed, and filled with fine performances, ''The Social Network'' is a riveting, ambitious example of modern filmmaking at its finest."<ref>{{cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=the_social_network |type=m |title=The Social Network (2010) |access-date=October 3, 2025 |archive-date=November 5, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231105080452/https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the-social-network |url-status=live}}</ref> On [[Metacritic]], the film has a weighted average score of 95 out of 100, based on 42 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{cite Metacritic |id=the-social-network |type=movie |title=The Social Network |access-date=January 26, 2021 |archive-date=November 8, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231108035320/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/the-social-network/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "B+" on an A+ to F scale.<ref name="CinemaScore">{{cite web |url=https://m.cinemascore.com |title=CinemaScore |access-date=November 18, 2020 |archive-date=December 14, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191214005119/https://m.cinemascore.com/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

stupefyingly male environment at Harvard University in 2003".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/oct/14/the-social-network-review|title=The Social Network – review|date=October Tiffany wrote positive comments on Aaron Sorkin's screenplay, writing that his "reflex for writing witty, whiny men with outsized intellect and poorly disguised narcissism serves as an advantage instead of a handicap."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theverge.com/2017/3/17/14946570/the-social-network-facebook-mark-zuckerberg-president-of-the-world|title=In 2010, The Social Network was searing film's editing by [[Kirk Baxter]] and [[Angus Wall]] was also lauded by critics, leading to their win of the [[Academy Award for Best Film Editing]].<ref>{{cite [[Joe Morgenstern]] in ''[[The Wall Street Journal]]'' praised the film as exhilarating but noted: "The biographical part takes liberties with its subject. Aaron Sorkin based his screenplay on ... Nathan Heller of ''[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]'' wrote a negative review of the film, describing it as "rote and deeply mediocre" as well as "maddeningly generic", and said that, "Sorkin and Fincher's 2003 Harvard is a citadel of old money, regatta blazers, and (if I am not misreading the implication here) a Jewish underclass striving beneath the heel of a [[WASP]]-centric, socially draconian culture ... to get the university this wrong in this movie is no small matter."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2010/10/what-the-social-network-gets-wrong-about-harvard-and-facebook.html|title=What The Social Network gets wrong about Harvard—and Facebook.|first=Nathan|last=Heller|date=October 1, 2010|website=[[Slate (magazine)|Slate]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201114222042/https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2010/10/what-the-social-network-gets-wrong-about-harvard-and-facebook.html|archive-date=November 14, 2020|url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Social Network'' appeared on 78 film critics' top-ten lists of the best films of 2010, based on Metacritic's aggregation. Out of the critics, 22 ranked the film first, and 12 ranked the film second. Out of the films of 2010, ''The Social Network'' appeared on the most top-ten lists.<ref name="2010 Film Critic Top Ten Lists">{{cite web |url=http://features.metacritic.com/features/2010/film-critic-top-ten-lists |title=2010 Film Critic Top Ten Lists |access-date=January 6, 2011 |work=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[CBS Interactive]] |archive-date=January 11, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110111180332/http://features.metacritic.com/features/2010/film-critic-top-ten-lists/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=2010: The year in review|url=http://old.bfi.org.uk/sightandsound/polls/films-of-2010-intro.php|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120803004307/http://old.bfi.org.uk/sightandsound/polls/films-of-2010-intro.php|url-status=dead|archive-date=3 August 2012|website=BFI &#124; Sight & Sound|publisher=[[British Film Institute]]|access-date=7 February 2016|date=18 January 2012}}</ref> In 2016, ''The Social Network'' was voted the 27th-best film of the 21st century by the BBC, as voted on by 177 film critics from around the world.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films?ocid=twcul |title=The 21st Century's 100 Greatest Films |work=[[BBC]] |date=August 23, 2016 |publisher=[[British Broadcasting Corporation]] |access-date=September 4, 2016 |archive-date=August 23, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160823184121/http://www.bbc.com/culture/story/20160819-the-21st-centurys-100-greatest-films?ocid=twcul |url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2018, [[IndieWire]] writers ranked the script the fourth best American screenplay of the 21st century, with Michael Nordine arguing that "everything came together nearly perfectly on the film, thanks in large part to Aaron Sorkin's Oscar-winning screenplay. Its finds the loquacious scribe at his best, with all the verbal takedowns ... and rapid-fire back-and-forths we've come to expect (and, more often than not, love) from him. Sorkin's portrayal of Mark Zuckerberg was hardly flattering, but recent headlines suggest it may have been too sympathetic."<ref>{{Cite web|last1=Dry|first1=Jude|last2=O'Falt|first2=Chris|last3=Erbland|first3=Kate|last4=Kohn|first4=Eric|last5=Sharf|first5=Zack|last6=Marotta|first6=Jenna|last7=Thompson|first7=Anne|last8=Earl|first8=William|last9=Nordine|first9=Michael|last10=Ehrlich|first10=David|author-link7=Anne Thompson (film journalist)|date=April 20, 2018|title=The 25 Best American Screenplays of the 21st Century, From 'Eternal Sunshine' to 'Lady Bird'|url=https://www.indiewire.com/2018/04/best-screenplays-scripts-american-1201952200/|access-date=February 4, 2021|publisher=[[IndieWire]]|archive-date=January 19, 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210119174929/https://www.indiewire.com/2018/04/best-screenplays-scripts-american-1201952200/|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Home media ===
''The Social Network'' was released on [[DVD]] and [[Blu-ray]] on January 11, 2011 by [[Sony Pictures Home Entertainment]]. In its first week of release, DVD sales totaled $13,470,305 and it was the number-one-sold DVD of the week.<ref>{{cite the numbers |id=Social-Network-The-(2010) |title=The Social Network |access-date=February 11, 2021 |archive-date=November 18, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201118143959/https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Social-Network-The-(2010) |url-status=live}}</ref> The DVD includes an [[audio commentary]] with director David Fincher, and a second commentary with writer Aaron Sorkin and the cast. The Blu-ray and two-disc DVD releases include the commentaries, along with a feature-length documentary, ''How Did They Ever Make a Movie of Facebook?'', featurettes, ''Angus Wall, Kirk Baxter and Ren Klyce on Post'', ''Trent Reznor, Atticus Ross and David Fincher on the Score'', ''In the Hall of the Mountain King: Reznor's First Draft'', ''[[Swarmatron]]'', ''Jeff Cronenweth and David Fincher on the Visuals'', and a Ruby Skye VIP Room: Multi-Angle Scene Breakdown feature.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://homevideo.about.com/od/dvdr6/fr/The-Social-Network-DVD-Review-a.htm |title=DVD Pick: 'The Social Network' |last=Redwine |first=Ivana |publisher=About.com |access-date=February 1, 2011 |archive-date=November 18, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201118143942/https://www.thoughtco.com/mark-zuckerberg-biography-1991135 |url-status=live}}</ref> The film was released on [[Ultra HD Blu-ray]] in October 2021 as part of the Columbia Classics 4K Ultra HD Collection (Volume 2), featuring a new [[Dolby Atmos]] mix and upscaled [[Dolby Vision]]/[[HDR10]] transfer from the film's [[2K resolution|2K]] master.<ref>{{cite web |title=The Social Network - 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray (Columbia Classics Vol. 2) Ultra HD Review |url=https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/99770/thesocialnetwork4kultrahdbluraycolumbiaclassicsvol2.html |access-date=September 30, 2022 |publisher=High Def Digest |archive-date=September 30, 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220930211420/https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/99770/thesocialnetwork4kultrahdbluraycolumbiaclassicsvol2.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Accolades ==
{{Main|List of accolades received by The Social Network{{!}}List of accolades received by ''The Social Network''}}
''The Social Network'' won the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama|Best Motion Picture – Drama Golden Globe]] at the [[68th Golden Globe Awards]] on January 16, 2011.<ref name="HFPA">{{cite web |url=http://www.goldenglobes.org/nominations |title=HFPA – Nominations and Winners |access-date=January 16, 2011 |publisher=Golden Globes |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121022120719/http://www.goldenglobes.org/nominations/ |archive-date=October 22, 2012 |df=mdy-all}}</ref> The film also won the awards for [[Golden Globe Award for Best Director|Best Director]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Screenplay|Best Screenplay]], and Best Original Score, making it the film with the most wins of the night.<ref name="SUTGoldenGlobes">{{cite news |url=http://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/news/2011/jan/16/social-network-has-lead-at-globes-with-3-prizes/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160306035843/http://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/news/2011/jan/16/social-network-has-lead-at-globes-with-3-prizes/ |archive-date=March 6, 2016 |title='Social Network' has lead at Globes with 3 prizes |access-date=January 16, 2011 |author=Germain, David |work=[[Forbes]]|url-status=dead}}</ref>

The film was nominated for seven [[British Academy Film Awards]], including [[BAFTA Award for Best Film|Best Film]], [[BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role|Best Actor in a Leading Role]] (Eisenberg), [[BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role|Best Actor in a Supporting Role]] (Garfield), and [[Rising Star Award, BAFTA|Rising Star Award]] (Garfield). It won three for [[BAFTA Award for Best Editing|Best Editing]], [[BAFTA Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Adapted Screenplay]], and [[BAFTA Award for Best Direction|Best Direction]] on February 13, 2011.<ref name="BAFTA-Past-Winners&Nominees">{{cite web |url=http://www.bafta.org/awards/film/nominations/?year=2010 |title=BAFTA Past Winners and Nominees |access-date=February 14, 2011 |publisher=[[British Academy of Film and Television Arts|BAFTA]] |archive-date=December 23, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111223150238/http://www.bafta.org/awards/film/nominations/?year=2010 |url-status=live}}</ref>

''The Social Network'' was nominated for eight [[Academy Awards]]: [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]], [[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]], [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]], Best Film Editing, Best Original Score, [[Academy Award for Best Sound Mixing|Best Sound Mixing]], and [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]].<ref name="Oscars">{{cite web |url=http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/83/nominees.html |title=Winners and Nominees for the 83rd Academy Awards |access-date=March 24, 2011 |publisher=[[Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences]] |archive-date=September 4, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120904121804/http://www.oscars.org/awards/academyawards/83/nominees.html |url-status=live}}</ref> . Of these, it won three: Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Original Score, and Best Film Editing at the [[83rd Academy Awards]] on February 27, 2011.

The film won Best Picture from the [[National Board of Review]], [[National Society of Film Critics]], [[New York Film Critics Circle]], and [[Los Angeles Film Critics Association]], making it only the third film in history—after ''[[Schindler's List]]'' (1993) and ''[[L.A.
